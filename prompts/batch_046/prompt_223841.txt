I just spent the evening arguing with a friend about whether I can genuinely know that a particular late Rothko is sublime solely from her verbal description, having never stood before it. We kept talking past each other because we never mapped the actual logical space of the views. Please construct a taxonomy — a branching decision tree — of positions on aesthetic testimony, organized from three diagnostic claims rather than from author names. First branch: can testimony ever transmit genuinely aesthetic knowledge (not merely biographical or technical facts), or does direct perceptual encounter remain strictly necessary? Second branch (for the affirmative side): is the knowledge transmitted purely propositional — about scale, color relations, compositional structure — or can it carry affective or evaluative content? Third branch: for testimony to succeed, must the recipient perform an internal imaginative or affective simulation of the work, or is linguistic uptake alone sufficient? Each leaf should carry a standard position label if one exists (e.g., pessimism, moderate propositional optimism, strong experientialism) or be marked unnamed; include one sentence on its most awkward commitment, paying special attention to the combination that allows testimony but denies any need for simulation, which my friend kept defending. Then locate the named positions on the leaves, note any branch that appears unoccupied and diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and explicitly flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot cleanly separate.
