Write a dialogue between Mara, a statistical mechanician, and Leo, a causal structuralist, debating whether the thermodynamic arrow explains causation or vice versa. Mara should defend the view that the second law together with a low-entropy initial boundary condition fully accounts for why traces, records, and memories point one way, rendering causal asymmetry derivative and locally unnecessary. Leo should defend the view that statistical mechanics already presupposes a directed notion of influence, and that without an irreducible asymmetry of dependency—whereby later configurations are structurally constrained by earlier ones but not conversely—the past hypothesis cannot be applied non-circularly to distinguish past from future.

The exchange should proceed as real back-and-forth rather than parallel monologues. Mara should challenge Leo’s “asymmetry of dependency” as a redescription of correlation patterns that statistical mechanics explains without primitive directionality, and she should press the Boltzmann-brain worry: if causal asymmetry were fundamental rather than statistical, we would lack the specific distributional explanation for why macroscopic records are reliable. Leo should reply by distinguishing probabilistic correlation from structural constraint, using the case of an isolated subsystem reaching thermal equilibrium: within it correlations vanish symmetrically, yet Leo should claim there remains a directional fact about which state was the constraining one. He should also argue that if the arrow were purely global and statistical, local fluctuations ought occasionally to produce local “reverse arrows” of record-formation, and that their absence suggests dependency is locally enforced rather than globally borrowed. Mara should counter that in equilibrium there is genuinely no arrow, and that Leo smuggles temporal direction in through the language of “constraining.” Both characters should acknowledge the force of the opposing position, and the dialogue should close without resolution, leaving the circularity worry and the local-enforcement claim in direct tension.
