I am preparing a consistency audit for a discussion group on philosophy of mind and computation. By training I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I have been trying to formalize my intuitions about embedded rational agency, partly because the framing seems relevant to autonomous systems. Below is the set of commitments I have reached. I would like them audited, not lectured upon.

My commitments:
(1) Any agent physically implemented within the world it reasons about — an embedded agent — necessarily lacks a fully transparent model of its own decision procedure; there remains an unmodeled runtime layer analogous to the gap between source code and execution state.
(2) Despite (1), I hold that such an agent can rationally bind itself now to a specific future upgrade path or successor design, treating that commitment as normatively binding in the same manner as an external physical law.
(3) For an embedded agent, self-interest ought not to be delimited by a hardware boundary, but by the causal region it can influence without violating its own coherence conditions — that is, the set of states over which it can maintain consistent planning.
(4) Consequently, two embedded agents running identical source architectures on separate hardware, linked by a sustained shared protocol, are not metaphysically distinct in the manner of two biological persons; their preference structures should, in principle, be mergeable.
(5) Nevertheless, I wish to preserve a meaningful first-person indexical — asking where one is physically located — that tracks the actual physical location and execution trajectory of the computation, not merely its information content. Hence I deny that a perfect backup running elsewhere is literally me in the relevant sense.

Please evaluate which pairs are genuinely incompatible, which tensions are merely apparent, and rank the real conflicts by severity. For each, specify which commitment must be revised and what the least costly fix would entail. In particular: does (1) undermine the rationality of (2)? Do (3) and (4) collapse into a universalism that renders (5) unsustainable? Is there a hidden tension between (2) and (5) regarding whether binding commitments propagate to spatially separated copies? Also examine whether (1) and (3) only seem to conflict — perhaps the presence of an unmodeled runtime does not actually prevent defining coherence conditions from the outside — and clarify whether that dissolves on inspection. Finally, if I must make exactly one revision to restore consistency with minimal damage, which single commitment should I alter, and what does that cost me?
