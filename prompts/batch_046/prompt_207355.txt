I am a retired physicist who has recently turned to philosophy, and I sit on the advisory committee of our university's Centre for Philosophy and Religion. We must decide upon a proposal for a funded workshop that applies David Lewis's counterpart theory to the Christian doctrine of penal substitution. The organisers contend that if the suffering party is merely a counterpart across possible worlds rather than identical to the one who bears the moral debt, then punishment is duplicated rather than genuinely transferred, rendering the traditional formulation incoherent. Theologians on the committee object that this imports an alien metaphysics of identity and misrepresents the doctrine's internal logic. Please draft our decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—recommending a concrete course of action with reasons the organisers could actually respond to. The memo should engage seriously with the modal-logical challenge and with the theological objection, acknowledge the strongest consideration on the losing side, and be written in formal British English suitable for circulation before Thursday's meeting.
