I've been writing this paper for a seminar on Warring States philosophy and I keep getting stuck on the White Horse Dialogue — you know, that claim that a white horse is not a horse, from the School of Names (Mingjia). On one side people read Gongsun Long like he's running an early logic puzzle about predicates and universals: white picks out a specific form, horse picks out a general kind, so they dont map onto the same thing. On the other side people say thats completely anachronistic, that he's actually talking about how names (ming) organize social reality and the dialogue is a warning about bureaucratic classification — like, if you call something a white horse you've already changed its political status. We've hashed this out for months with the same passages and the same translations and literally nobody moves. Is this a real metaphysical disagreement about universals, a verbal fight over what meaning means in a classical Chinese context, or are we just asking different questions — one about logical form, one about hermeneutic context? I want a diagnosis that actually engages both readings, not a survey. And tell me: is there any distinction, textual evidence, or formal reconstruction that could break the deadlock, or is the question just ill-posed?
