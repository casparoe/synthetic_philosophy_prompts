I have been attempting to formalize the American Dream, Jantelagen, and the so-called tall poppy syndrome as three competing institutional answers to the same structural question: by what rule should a society allocate the positional but non-rival good of public esteem? The American Dream implements a meritocratic allocation; Jantelagen implements an egalitarian suppression of differential claims; and the tall poppy syndrome operates as the decentralized sanctioning mechanism that makes the Jantelagen rule self-enforcing. Since these three norms are therefore just parameter settings within a single recognition-allocation framework, evaluating them should not invoke incommensurable cultural values but only compare which parameter maximizes long-run social mobility. Given this framing, what normative justification remains for preferring Jantelagen or for treating tall-poppy dynamics as anything other than a suboptimal enforcement error—unless one mistakenly treats equality of outcome and equality of opportunity as independent goods rather than as competing allocations of the same esteem resource?
