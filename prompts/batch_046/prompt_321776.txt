I'm trying to nail down my position for a discussion group on Strawson and reactive attitudes and I'm worried I'm smuggling in commitments that don't actually fit together. Here's roughly where I stand, and I'd like an audit before I present it.

(1) Moral responsibility is grounded in reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, etc. — that respond to an agent's quality of will, not in some metaphysical property of being an uncaused cause. So I reject libertarian sourcehood. (2) For resentment to be fitting, the agent needs normative competance: they have to be able to understand that their action was wrong and be able to be moved by that understanding to act differently. If they genuinely couldn't grasp the wrongness or be motivated by it, blame is out of place. (3) Even if that normative competance was entirely shaped by deterministic causes — bad upbringing, genetics, thier environment — that doesn't undermine reactive attitudes. The causal origin of the capacity doesn't matter, only that it's present now.

(4) I don't think we should ever swap reactive attitudes for the objective attitude in ordinary interpersonal relationships. Treating someone like an object is only okay in clinical or institutional settings, not when we're dealing with them as fellow moral agents. (5) So determinism doesn't threaten responsibility; our practices never needed agents to be ultimate sources of thier actions. (6) But if someone's psychology is now fixed in a way that makes them permanently unreceptive to moral reasons — not just that they ignored reasons this time, but that they literally can't respond going forward — then resentment is inappropriate and the objective attitude is correct.

Do these hang together? I'm especially unsure about (2) and (5): does my "could have done otherwise" in (2) smuggle back the libertarian freedom I reject in (1) and (5)? And does (6) conflict with (3) — if determinism makes everyone's psychology causally fixed, am I accidentally giving everyone an excuse? Or am I missing an obvious tension between (4) and (6) that only looks bad? Can you rank the tensions by severity, say which ones only look like conflicts, and tell me the least costly revision if I want to keep the core Strawsonian picture? Don't just list them all as equally bad.
