Specify the desiderata that any adequate theory of stratified historical explanation must satisfy if it genuinely integrates the longue durée—geography, climate, and slow-moving social structures—with the explanatory reality of contingent events and individual agency. Do not summarize existing historiography; instead, provide the requirements list: the phenomena it must account for, the distinctions it must preserve, the empirical constraints it cannot violate, and the conceptual tensions it must resolve.

Address at least the following, with the motivating case or conceptual tension behind each:
(i) the causal autonomy of geoclimatic structures operating over centuries-long scales, without reducing discrete events to mere epiphenomena;
(ii) the irreducible explanatory contribution of short-duration events and individual decisions, without treating them as fully structurally overdetermined;
(iii) the distinction between structural constraint and structural determination;
(iv) consistency with the findings of historical climatology and archaeology regarding long-term patterns, without requiring that every event be predictable from climate or geographic data;
(v) the simultaneous rejection of methodological individualism (collapsing structures into aggregates of choice) and of structural determinism (collapsing choice into structural output);
(vi) the problem of structural persistence, in which institutions or social practices endure long after the geoclimatic conditions that originally shaped them have changed.

For each requirement, state whether it is non-negotiable for any theory claiming to integrate both temporal scales, or whether it reflects a camp-specific preference—such as the preference of Annales-style structuralism for explanatory primacy of the long term versus the preference of narrative or political historiography for the autonomy of the event.

Then identify which subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, particularly whether granting full causal autonomy to both slow structures and discrete events violates some principle of explanatory closure or parsimony. Flag which single requirement eliminates the largest number of popular synthetic accounts, and briefly indicate whether Braudel’s own tripartite division of historical time resolves the underlying tension or merely relocates it onto a classificatory scheme that avoids direct causal interaction.
