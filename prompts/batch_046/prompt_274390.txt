I'm a backend engineer at a mid-sized logistics company, and I was chatting with my friends over beers about something at work thats been eating at me. Management was thrilled: fewer errors, faster turnaround, all that. Maria didn't get laid off; they moved her into "strategic oversight," which mostly means sitting in meetings and staring at dashboards I built. Last week she told me she misses the old routine, not because she liked the drudge, but because at 5pm she used to know she had touched every item and fixed the little mismatches herself. Now she just watches green bars update and feels like she's floating outside the actual work. I honestly thought I was doing her a favor by cutting out the boring part, but she seems more disconnected than grateful.

One of my friends said work is just a contract for money, so if she's recieving the same paycheck for less grunt work she should count it as a win. But another friend brought up this idea I half-remember from a random blog post—how there's a difference between 'world-building' jobs and 'world-maintaining' jobs, and automation tends to erase the maintenance stuff because it looks invisible. Maria's old role was definitely maintaining: she talked to warehouse staff, caught errors before they snowballed, kept the physical flow honest. Now that pipeline is hidden inside my code, and she's been pulled up into a layer where nothing feels tangible. I'm wondering if I didn't just remove labor—I removed the part that made her feel embedded in something she actually understood.
