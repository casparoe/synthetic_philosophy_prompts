Every 48 hours technicians mist the surface to replace sublimated mass; within one year no original molecule remains. If the refrigeration system fails, the figure collapses into water and the insurer classifies the event as destruction of the artwork, not as transformation into a new object. Using the material-constitution framework (statue and clay), analyze (i) whether the work is identical to the ice aggregate present at any given moment, (ii) whether it survives complete molecular replacement, and (iii) whether the contract’s form/matter distinction resolves the metaphysical problem or only relocates it.

Student answer: The work is not identical to the present ice aggregate, because that aggregate could survive being melted and reshaped into a sphere, whereas Thermal Form #7 is destroyed if the thermal envelope is lost—precisely the modal divergence that distinguishes a statue from its clay. They share matter but differ in persistence conditions, so the relation is constitution, not identity.

Because constitution is symmetrical—the thermal envelope determines the ice’s formal boundaries just as the statue’s form determines the clay’s artistic boundaries—the contract’s distinction captures the metaphysics rather than imposing it. The work survives total replacement because identity is transitive: if W at t₁ is constituted by I₁ and W at t₂ by I₂, with I₁ and I₂ disjoint, then W is continuous across time, which proves W is not identical to either aggregate.

Still, this relocates the problem: we now need an ontology of the “thermal envelope.” If it is just the air-conditioning unit, then breaking the unit destroys the work, which is implausible; if it is an abstract form, then the work is constituted jointly by an abstract object and concrete ice, reproducing the original puzzle with an extra layer.
