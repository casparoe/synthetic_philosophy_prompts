You serve as an ecological-compliance referee for a regional land-use authority; every month you evaluate habitat-alteration proposals—removal of invasives, water diversion, controlled burns, rewilding—under the Net Sentient Welfare Balance (NSWB), which you must treat as your governing principle: a proposal is permissible only if the expected change in total sentient welfare, integrated over the affected lifespan of all sentient organisms and weighted by capacity for suffering or flourishing, is non-negative, with a precautionary tilt against introducing novel suffering whose magnitude is hard to bound. Your task is to turn NSWB into a reusable decision procedure that a referee with a biology background but no philosophy training can run in about thirty minutes per proposal, knowing that a director with ecological ethics training must sign off on any step that involves pure value judgment rather than empirical fact. Specify the ordered checklist of factual questions (population dynamics, sentience estimates, welfare-metric ranges, uncertainty bounds), the decision rule at each step—what counts as sufficient evidence to proceed, where the answer must be no and the proposal rejected outright, and exactly which distinctions must be escalated to the director because they are axiological rather than biological. Then apply the procedure to two proposals: A) removing an invasive shrub that has created dense cover for an overabundant rodent population suffering high starvation and predation, where removal would sharply reduce rodent numbers but stress survivors and reduce prey for local predators; B) diverting seasonal floodwater away from a grassland to prevent drowning of ground-nesting birds and aquatic invertebrates, where diversion would dry the wetland, eliminate the invertebrate population, and alter predator foraging. In at least one case the procedure should yield an answer you personally find unwelcome—identify which case and explain why NSWB logic forces that result despite your intuition. Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot do, for example resolving deep interspecies welfare comparisons or substituting for democratic value choices about which species matter most. Keep the procedure, both applications, and the limitation note within 550 words.
