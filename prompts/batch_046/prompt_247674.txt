I've been thinking about a particular skeptical configuration that shows up near Zhuangzi's butterfly dream but seems distinct from both it and from Cartesian dream skepticism. The setup involves two fully developed first-person narratives—say, being a human philosopher and being a butterfly—each with continuous memory, reasoning, and an internal sense of being awake. The skeptical pressure doesn't come from the possibility that one narrative is deceptive or illusory; rather, it comes from the absence of any non-circular criterion for deciding which narrative is the base reality and which is embedded in the other. Each framework has its own standards for coherence and wakefulness, so appeals to stability or consistency don't break the symmetry. This seems to target the privilege of a single autobiographical frame rather than the reliability of the senses or the persistence of identity across change. Is there an established philosophical term for this specific form of skepticism? If there is, how exactly does it differ from standard dream arguments and from Zhuangzi's original puzzle about transformation? If there isn't, can you name and define it precisely, and say where its boundaries lie—particularly regarding whether it requires actual symmetric experience or can operate with merely conceivable alternatives? Please take a definite position rather than surveying options, and answer in American English.
