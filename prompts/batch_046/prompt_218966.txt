i sit on the review board for the millbrook foundation's long-range capital comittee. we got this bizarre bequest proposal: the donor wants every dollar locked in a non-spending vault for 350 years, then released in full to whatever institutions exist in 2374. our charter mentions intergenerational fairness but doesn't specify how to weigh people who don't exist yet against current grantees. half the board thinks pure time preference is just irrational bias and we should reject the lock-up; the other half thinks discouting the future is unavoidable and maybe even part of what makes a point of view coherent rather than just a cognitive error. please write the short memo i'll circulate before thursday's vote: it should recommend a concrete decision—accept, reject, or renegotiate with specifc payout conditions—and take seriously the strongest argument for the opposite view. don't just restate standard economics; engage the idea that preferring the present might be tied to the conditions for meaningful agency at all.
