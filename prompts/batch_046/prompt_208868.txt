As a doctoral candidate working between philosophy of science and comparative philosophy of religion, I am preparing an opening piece for an interdisciplinary workshop on quantum interpretation and non-Western metaphysics. I want a poem that takes seriously Erwin Schrödinger’s Vedantic monism—specifically the claim that the multiplicity of conscious subjects is ultimately illusory—without falling into either the cliché of mystical oceanic dissolution or the reduction of his view to a loose analogy for quantum superposition. Instead, the poem should treat the unity of consciousness as arising from the same structural feature that troubled his physics: the impossibility of cleanly isolating the observer from the observed system, so that boundaries between subjects are not erased but shown to be entangled measurements within a single descriptive frame.

Please write a pantoum of six quatrains using the standard interlocking repetition scheme, in which the second and fourth lines of each quatrain become the first and third lines of the next. Add the constraint that every line must end with one of these six words, cycling in order: wave, boundary, mirror, entangled, ocean, one. Because the lines themselves repeat, the end-words will also recur and interweave, enacting the idea that what seems like a distinct subject is actually the same six elements rearranged—like states that cannot be factored into separate observers. The turning point should come in the fifth stanza, where the speaker realizes that one is not a count but a failure of division. The framing should be accurate enough that someone familiar with Schrödinger’s philosophical essays on consciousness and physics recognizes the argument, and coherent enough that someone unfamiliar can still follow it through the form alone. Do not resolve the tension into pure mysticism or pure physics; let the interweaving of lines stand in for the irreducible ambiguity between epistemology and ontology. No citations, bibliography, or source lists are needed—the aim is conceptual precision rendered structurally.
