ok so my advisor made me present this in our philosophy of death reading group and now i cant sleep. here's the chain my study partener threw at me:

(1) epicurus was basically right: death cant harm you bc theres no subject present when it happens. no experience, no harm.

(2) people try the deprivation reply — death robs you of future goods — but she says that fails too, since deprivation needs someone who actually gets robbed, and the dead person doesnt exist anymore, so theres literally no victim.

(3) so far standard. but then she extends it to suspended animation or long cryo. during suspension consciousness is gone, just like in death. so by (1) and (2) that gap isnt harmful and doesnt deprive anyone either.

(4) now compare two lives: life A is 80 yrs continuous consciousness. life B is 40 yrs awake, then 100 yrs suspended, then 40 yrs awake. B's subject gets 80 conscious years, same as A, spread over 180 chronological years.

(5) since the suspended gap contains no subject, B's subject isnt deprived of those 100 years — they never existed for them. so B is exactly as good as A for the person living it.

(6) but this means theres zero reason to prefer continuous existence over interrupted existance. if you can extend chronological lifespan via suspension without harming anyone, you should be indifferent between living straight through and taking long breaks.

(7) apply it to temporary unconsciousness: if im knocked out for a year, that year isnt a loss to me, and i should be indifferent between being awake and being knocked out. but clearly i care about not being knocked out, and saving me from a coma matters.

(8) so the argument proves that not only is death not bad, but all interruptions in consciousness are neutral, which wipes out the rational basis for preferring continuous life at all.
