me and two friends got into it over coffee about Wittgenstein's hinge epistemology — you know, the On Certainty stuff where some propositions are like the riverbed directing the flow of inquiry but can shift slowly. Maya said hinges, like the earth being old, aren't beliefs supported by evidence; they're background conditions that make doubt about other things possible at all. Leo said okay, but if hinges are totally outside the reason-giving game, then calling them "certain" is just smuggling dogmatism in the back door — you've labeled them special without explaining why they can't be wrong. Maya answered that Leo is treating hinges like hidden premises, but they're not premises, they're the axis the wheel of doubt turns around. Then Leo introduced this idea he called "hinge clustering": hinges never come alone, they come in interlocking clusters, and you can replace a whole cluster — like switching world-pictures — without ever doubting any single hinge in isolation. Maya said that actually proves her point, since cluster-replacement isn't rational revision but more like adopting a new form of life, and Leo is assuming there's some neutral standpoint outside all clusters. Leo said he's not assuming neutrality, just that if clusters are replaceable then hinges have the same logical status as big theories, and "hinge" is just a fancy name for "stuff we happen to not be questioning right now." They went back and forth like this for a while. Can you adjudicate their exchange move by move? Who's right about what, and is Leo's clustering idea coherent with Wittgenstein or does it break something? Also, what are they both missing about how hinge revision actually works? I definatly couldn't referee it.
