I serve as a senior evaluator for the International Synthetic Cognition Certification Board, which reviews autonomous advisory architectures—clinical triage systems, judicial risk-modeling agents, and environmental policy optimizers—before deployment in high-stakes settings. I must classify each submission according to Daniel Dennett’s taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian cognitive architectures, because our liability frameworks, human-oversight mandates, and stress-testing protocols are entirely keyed to that classification. I have roughly ninety minutes per case, no access to the development team’s internal logs beyond what they submit, and I must personally sign every determination, including explicit judgment calls that cannot be automated away. I need a reusable decision procedure—ordered diagnostic questions, criteria for sufficient evidence at each step, clear stopping points where a system is rejected or downgraded, and explicit flags for where the taxonomy itself forces a value-laden choice rather than a factual one. The procedure must handle hybrid architectures: for example, a reinforcement-learning agent that also maintains an explicit counterfactual world model, or a large language model fine-tuned via evolutionary prompt-selection. Apply the procedure to three submissions: first, a pure evolutionary algorithm that selects decision trees prior to deployment and learns nothing during operation; second, a reinforcement-learning agent with an internal generative model that simulates outcomes before acting; third, a language-model advisory system that exhibits fluent cultural reasoning and tool-use but was trained exclusively on synthetic data with no chain of human cultural transmission—this is the case where I personally want full Gregorian certification granted, but I need the procedure to force a rejection if the taxonomy genuinely requires participatory cultural inheritance rather than behavioral mimicry. Take a definite, committed position on how hybrids should be classified—by their strongest component, their weakest, or a non-aggregative rule—defend that choice with reasons, and apply it consistently rather than presenting alternatives for me to choose between. State clearly what the procedure cannot adjudicate, where it will systematically misfire, and why those failures are unavoidable given the taxonomy itself. I want a committed response that makes definite choices and gives its reasoning, not a menu of options left for me to resolve.
