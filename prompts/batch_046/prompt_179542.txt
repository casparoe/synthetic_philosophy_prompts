I just spent last night in a long, circular argument with a close friend about Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility. I defended the regress: true moral responsibility requires being causa sui, but that requires an infinite chain of prior selves creating themselves, which is impossible, so no one is ever truly responsible. My friend, who takes a reasons-responsive compatibilist line, countered that this demands an impossible, theologically loaded standard—no one needs to be an uncaused cause to be accountable; absence of coercion and responsiveness to reasons are sufficient. We kept repeating ourselves. She accused me of smuggling in the hidden premise that moral desert requires metaphysical self-creation; I accused her of changing the subject from desert to mere accountability practices. I want a committed diagnosis, not an even-handed survey. Is this disagreement substantive or verbal? Are we actually clashing over whether the concept of 'true moral responsibility' is coherent, or are we answering different questions—she about whether reactive-attitude practices survive determinism, I about whether anyone metaphysically deserves blame? Is there a hidden structural parallel here to the epistemic regress—foundationalism versus infinitism versus coherentism—so that my demand for a 'foundational' moral self is as optional as demanding foundational knowledge? Given your diagnosis, is there a conceptual distinction—say, between answerability and basic desert, or between causal and normative sourcehood—that could actually move the debate, or should I concede that we are facing bedrock intuitions about what 'being the source' must involve, with no argument or empirical finding capable of resolving it?
