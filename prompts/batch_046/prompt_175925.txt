I've come to think that Confucian role ethics gets misread as just a hierarchy of fixed duties—father commands, son obeys, ruler commands, subject obeys. But I think li is better understood as a kind of live negotiation protocol, where the roles (parent-child, ruler-subject, friend-friend) set the stage but shu—that reciprocity/empathy mechanism—constantly feeds back and reshapes what the role actually demands in context. So my position is that you can defend a non-authoritarian, even somewhat egalitarian Confucianism without ditching the role-structure entirely; the subordinate's moral perspective isn't external to the role, it actively calibrates it. What would actually count against this? Can it handle hard role conflicts, say being a loyal minister vs. a caring parent? And does it predict anything distinctive about moral development that rival theories dont?
