Exam question (graduate metaphysics):

F.H. Bradley argued that treating a relation as genuinely distinct from its relata generates an infinite regress: if R relates a and b, then R itself must be related to a and to b by further relations, and so on. First, reconstruct Bradley's regress precisely, distinguishing it clearly from his separate doctrine of internal relations. Second, explain Russell's response—specifically his claim that relations relate terms directly, without requiring further linking entities. Third, apply the problem to propositional unity: if a proposition is understood as a structured mental representation composed of constituent concepts plus a "binding operator" that unifies them into a single thought, does this reproduce Bradley's regress at the cognitive level? Assess whether Russell's theory of judgment—particularly his shift toward analyzing judgment as a multiple relation rather than as a single relation to a unified proposition—avoids the difficulty. Finally, consider whether a representationalist account of consciousness that borrows the neuroscience "binding problem" framework—for example, explaining feature-integration through temporal synchrony rather than through a unifying mental act—can solve propositional unity without invoking a relation that itself needs relating. Be clear about which philosopher holds which view, and do not confuse metaphysical claims about relations with psychological or neuroscientific claims about representation.

Student answer to be graded:

Bradley's regress shows that relations cannot be real. He says if R is between a and b, then you need R1 to link R with a, and R2 to link R with b, and this never stops. So Bradley concludes that all relations are unreal and only internal relations exist—meaning a relation is just part of the nature of its terms, not something extra. Russell disagreed and said relations are external, which means they just relate things without needing a bridge. Because of this, Russell solved the regress for propositions too. In his theory of judgment, Russell said the mind judges by standing in one big relation to all the parts, so there is no separate binding thing. This is basically the same as the binding problem in neuroscience, where the brain binds colors and shapes by firing neurons together in time. So temporal synchrony does the binding, and since it is just a physical process, there is no mental relation that needs another relation. Therefore Bradley's problem is solved both for the world and for thought, and we do not need any unsaturated concepts or Fregean solutions. The only error people make is thinking Russell kept propositions as single objects in his later work, but actually he moved to a multiple-relation view.
