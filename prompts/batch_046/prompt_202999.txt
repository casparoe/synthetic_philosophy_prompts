Title concept: Ghost Essences and Recursive Analogical Collapse

Central thesis: Hofstadter and Sander present analogy as moving cognition from surface similarities to abstract essences, which then become the building blocks of concepts. I argue that this movement is not unidirectional but recursive: extracted essences can themselves serve as surfaces for higher-order analogies. Most of the time this is harmless—recognizing that one social conflict is "like" another relies on just such recursion. However, I identify a pathological case I call second-order analogical collapse. This happens when an analogy maps between two domains that share a precise relational architecture but possess incommensurable ontological categories. The mind extracts an essence—what I term a ghost essence—that has full internal coherence as an abstract schema yet possesses no stable referential anchor in either domain. Unlike a simple false analogy, where surface resemblance misleads judgment, a ghost essence is structurally sound; the problem is that the concept it generates is ontologically homeless.

Illustrative case: The relational structure linking biological kinship (parental protection, dependency, inheritance of traits) is mapped onto corporate organizational hierarchies (executive oversight, subsidiary dependence, transmission of culture). The isomorphism is real and can guide reasoning—one might speak of a "parent company" or "corporate DNA"—but the ontological categories of biological reproduction and legal incorporation are incommensurable. The extracted essence, call it protective generational transmission, behaves like a genuine concept: it supports inferences, guides predictions, and resists simple dismissal. Yet it cannot be consistently anchored in biology without importing legal fictions, or in law without importing biological vitalism. It is therefore a ghost essence.
