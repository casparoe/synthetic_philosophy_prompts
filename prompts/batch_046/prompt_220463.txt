Design a 45-minute essay examination for an upper-level moral philosophy seminar on Scanlon's contractualism and the aggregation problem. The exam prompt must embed a novel conceptual framing—label it the “dyadic rejection constraint”—which stipulates that the reasonableness of rejecting a principle must be evaluated separately for each ordered pair consisting of one affected individual and the principle, without aggregating burdens or benefits across the group of affected parties. The question should require students to apply this constraint to the classic rescue scenario in which one rescuer can save either five strangers or one stranger, and to determine whether it allows Scanlon to maintain that saving the five is morally preferable without committing to a utilitarian aggregation of welfare. Crucially, the question must force students to confront whether the dyadic constraint generates intransitive rejection judgments when three or more parties are involved—for instance, if principle P is not rejectable by party A relative to B, not rejectable by B relative to C, but rejectable by A relative to C—thereby undermining the determinacy of contractualist reasoning. Do not request bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific page numbers. Provide: (1) the exam question itself, formulated so that it cannot be answered by summarizing Scanlon’s standard rescue-case discussion or by reciting the sense/reference-style labels of contractualism; (2) a model answer of approximately 600 words that correctly applies the dyadic constraint to both the binary and ternary cases, distinguishes burden-scaling from welfare-aggregation, and diagnoses the intransitivity risk; (3) a marking scheme of roughly 250 words that separates an A response—must apply the constraint to the rescue case, identify the intransitivity problem in the ternary case, and show why this is not merely a quantitative scaling issue—from a B response, which applies the binary case correctly but misses the ternary intransitivity or conflates the dyadic constraint with a simple pairwise comparison of welfare levels. Specify that the answer should be graded out of 30 marks, with 10 marks for accurate exposition of contractualist reasonable rejection, 12 marks for analysis of the dyadic constraint in rescue scenarios, and 8 marks for diagnosis of the determinacy problem arising from intransitivity.
