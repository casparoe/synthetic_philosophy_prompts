Write an essay defending a position in the debate over whether textual meaning is anchored by an author's recoverable intentional design or generated by the layered interpretive history of the work itself. Focus on the conceptual problem posed by a palimpsestic philosophical text—one composed through anonymous collaboration and successive annotation, such that no single consciousness can be identified as its originating author. Evaluate the case for originating intentionalism, which holds that meaning requires a reconstructible authorial purpose, against synthetic intentionalism, which holds that meaning can emerge from the structured interaction of textual layers even when original intent is irrecoverable. Address the central objection to each: that synthetic intentionalism risks collapsing into arbitrary reader-response, and that originating intentionalism renders genuinely collaborative or corrupted texts semantically void. Reach and justify a clear conclusion—whether one view is superior, whether they govern different textual categories, or whether a modified third position best resolves the conflict. Ground your argument in conceptual analysis rather than in appeals to specific scholars, texts, or bibliographic sources.
