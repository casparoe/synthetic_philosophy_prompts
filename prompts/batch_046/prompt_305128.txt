You serve on the Governance Reform Panel of the Trans-Alpine Regional Council, which must revise its charter defining membership in the regional demos for cross-border resource decisions. The Panel is weighing three criteria: (A) the "all-affected" principle, extending membership to anyone whose vital interests are shaped by Council actions; (B) the "all-subjected" principle, limiting it to those bound by Council enforcement mechanisms; and (C) a proposed "reciprocal-recognition" principle, granting membership only to parties who mutually acknowledge each other as co-legislators within a shared institutional framework—thereby excluding both external affected populations and internal residents who do not participate in that mutual acknowledgment.

The immediate case is a proposed reservoir that would alter water access for 40,000 residents in an adjacent non-member province, while within the jurisdiction approximately 8,000 long-term agricultural workers are subject to Council labor regulations but currently ineligible to vote. The Panel must recommend which criterion governs membership for ratifying this project, specify whether the vote should include the downstream province or the workers (or both, or neither), and provide an implementation timeline.

Draft the formal opinion for circulation before Thursday's session. It should present a concrete recommendation, justify it by applying your chosen criterion to both groups, and treat seriously the strongest argument for the rejected criterion—particularly the claim that democratic legitimacy requires including all affected vital interests regardless of institutional reciprocity.
