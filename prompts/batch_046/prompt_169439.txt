I spent my career in experimental neuroscience and have only recently turned to philosophy of action and ethics. I want a conceptual specification, not a literature review, of what an adequate theory distinguishing manipulation from legitimate persuasion must actually deliver, because the standard one-feature tests—autonomy infringement, rationality bypass, hidden intent—each seem to fail on cases I can construct from cognitive science.

Write the full desiderata: the conditions any adequate theory must satisfy to count as having drawn the boundary correctly. For each requirement, state the specific case or conceptual pressure behind it, not merely the abstract principle. The list should handle at least these pressures: (1) it must preserve the judgment that a physician’s frank, statistically accurate warning about surgical risks is legitimate persuasion, even when the framing deliberately exploits loss-aversion; (2) it must explain why a political message built from micro-targeted emotional priming feels manipulative, without implying that every emotional appeal is; (3) it must respect a distinction between altering a person’s first-order preferences and altering the cognitive architecture—attention, memory retrieval, or belief-fixation mechanisms—through which those preferences are formed or accessed; (4) it must address whether influence that operates through automatic, model-free habit pathways differs normatively from influence that engages explicit, model-based deliberation, and whether that neural-level distinction should do any philosophical work at all; (5) it must keep manipulation distinct from coercion, yet explain why manipulation is often more insidious precisely because it preserves the appearance of free choice; (6) it must remain consistent with empirical findings that people frequently confabulate reasons for beliefs induced by subliminal or subliminal-adjacent cues, without collapsing into the view that every unconscious influence is manipulative.

For each desideratum, indicate whether you take it to be non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference. Then flag any pair you believe is jointly unsatisfiable, with the reason. Keep the conceptual weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not on evaluating named theories—though in no more than one brief paragraph, sketch how an autonomy-centered account, a rationality-centered account, and a consent-centered account each fare against your list, identifying for each the single desideratum that does the most damage to it. I need no citations, no bibliography, and no reference to specific texts I should read; I want the specification itself.
