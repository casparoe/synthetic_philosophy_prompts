I have spent the last three months as the primary night caregiver for my father, who is losing his language and memory to a degenerative brain disease. In the exhaustion of these shifts, I have begun to notice something unsettling: when he is frightened and I calm him, the fear does not feel like it belongs to him and then is eased by me; it feels like one disturbance in a single field that happens to have two bodies in it. I have read enough of Schrödinger's later reflections—where he draws on Vedanta to argue that consciousness is singular, only appearing plural through the multiplicity of bodies and brains—to wonder whether this experience reveals something true about the structure of awareness, or whether it is only the cognitive blur of sleep deprivation and anticipatory grief. I want to know whether there is a coherent philosophical argument, independent of neurology or mysticism, for treating the dissolution of self-other boundaries I describe as evidence for a unitary consciousness rather than as a failure of psychological boundaries. Please begin with your bottom-line answer in the first sentence, then develop the reasoning with no preamble and without restating my situation.
