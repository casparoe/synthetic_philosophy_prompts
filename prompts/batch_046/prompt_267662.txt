(1) For any concept to have real content, there has to be a gap between applying it correctly and just feeling like you've applied it correctly.

(2) That gap needs some check that's independant of the mental state being checked—otherwise seeming-right just collapses into being-right.

(3) Take a genuinely private mental kind, call it S—maybe a specific after-image shade only I ever experiance.

(4) If I try to name S and keep a mental diary, later when I think 'that's S', there's no other person to correct me.

(5) Worse, there's no other mental state of mine that can serve as an independant check either, because every mental state I use to verify S is just as private and needs its own calibraton.

(6) So checking S launches an autophenomenological calibration loop with no base case: M1 checks M0, M2 checks M1, forever.

(7) Therefore S can't be grounded and the term is empty.

(12) Therefore I shouldn't be able to meaningfully think 'this hurts right now'.
