As a journalist who covers science and philosophy, I have adopted the standard post-Quinean view that the analytic-synthetic distinction is dead or at least too porous to do real work—no statement is fully immune to revision, and attempts to draw a sharp boundary collapse into circularity or arbitrariness. I want to know what would actually count against that settled position, not nostalgic appeals to intuition but concrete formal or empirical pressure points. Could results in two-dimensional modal semantics, the stability of certain conceptual roles across theory change, or specific failures of holistic translation rehabilitate a principled distinction? For each potential defeater, tell me whether it is a live threat or merely logically possible, whether a committed Quinean would concede damage or dismiss it by redescription, and rank them from fatal to awkward. I also want an honest judgment about whether the Quinean view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—adding escape clauses until nothing could refute it—and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a thesis of this kind.
