I'm presenting next month at a joint seminar in economic history and philosophy of social science, and I need a speaker's Q&A prep sheet rather than a general defense of my argument. My paper defends a conjunctural, or configurational, revision of Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis: I argue that the Protestant ethic was causally efficacious for the emergence of rational capitalism only as one element in a specific institutional configuration, specifically when it coincided with Roman-law property structures and autonomous urban corporate governance. On this reading, culture does not compete with material interests as an independent mental cause; rather, it operates like Weber's well-known metaphor of the switchman, redirecting institutional tracks that were already laid, but it can only do so where the tracks exist. The audience will be roughly ten economic historians—several of whom treat Tawney's and Hill's empirical critiques of Weber as largely settled—and six analytic philosophers of social science who worry that invoking values in causal explanations either collapses into individual psychology or becomes epiphenomenal to material conditions. Please list the eight objections I will actually face from this specific mix, ordered by how damaging they are to my argument. For each objection, phrase it in the vocabulary that audience would use—economic historians speaking in terms of selection bias, regional variance, and counterfactual evidence; philosophers speaking in terms of causal exclusion, supervenience, and ontological commitment—then provide a concise reply I could deliver in about two minutes, and flag precisely where that reply is weakest or concedes ground. Also tell me what I should concede explicitly in my opening remarks so it does not return as an attack, and identify the single question I am most hoping nobody asks—the one that forces me to defend the counterfactual about Catholic regions that possessed similar legal and urban institutions yet did not develop the same capitalist rationalization. For context, my sketch targets this: I claim that in regions with Protestant asceticism but without urban corporate autonomy—such as parts of rural Scotland—the outcome was intense religious discipline without economic rationalization, which is meant to show that culture requires the institutional switch to be causally productive rather than being sufficient on its own.
