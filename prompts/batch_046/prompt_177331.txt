I've been going back through Hofstadter's ant-colony dialogue — Aunt Hillary, the Anteater, the whole fugue thing — and comparing it with recient work on swarm cognition, and I can't figure out why the debate over whether an ant colony literally has a mind hasn't budged in years. The integrationists cite collective information processing and distributed decision-making; the reductionists say it's parallel algorithms with no unified subject. They mostly agree on the behavioral facts. So is this just verbal — different meanings of 'mind' — or is the hidden split actually about persistence conditions, since Aunt Hillary's ants die and get replaced constantly? Maybe the real disagreement isn't about cognition but about whether a mind can survive total part-replacement without breaking continuity. If that's the fault line, what would actually move anyone — a formal model, a conceptual distinction, or is it just bedrock intuition? Give me the diagnosis, not a survey.
