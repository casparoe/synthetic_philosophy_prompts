hey, im a retired biologist who got into philosophy late. been thinking about that wasp sphex and the sphexishness idea — mechanical rigidity vs being flexible. i tried to formalize an argument for my reading group and now it seems to prove free will is impossible for literally anything. heres the chain:

(1) free will requires being non-sphexish: you can adapt your response to the same situation for reasons, not just replay a fixed routine.
(2) any physical system that adapts does it through internal states that update according to some rule — learning rate, neural weights, feedback law, whatever.
(3) those update rules are themselves fixed; they dont change just because you want them to.
(4) so physical adaptability is just second-order sphexishness — youre mechanically rigid about being flexible.
(5) therefore no physical being can be genuinely non-sphexish.
(6) but if youre not governed by any rules or patterns at all, then your actions are pure noise.
(7) pure noise isnt free will.
(8) so free will is impossible for physical beings, and also impossible for anything non-physical.

every step feels okay, especially (3) and (6). i want to keep (1) and (5) if possible. where does this break? is there an equivocation between running a program and being law-governed? whats the cost of fixing it?
