I'm stabilizing a position I'm calling untethered normativity for a seminar defense, and I need a rigorous consistency audit of the package before I present it. Here are the commitments exactly as I hold them:

(1) Normative realism: there are objective reasons for action and evaluation that are not constituted by anyone's attitudes, conventions, or institutional practices.

(2) The reasoner-dependence constraint: for any fact to function as a reason for X, X must exist and be capable of being moved by it; reasons are essentially relational and require a relatum that is a reasoner.

(3) Free-floating rationales: normative truths—facts about what ought to obtain, or about comparative value—can be true in worlds that contain no minds at all; in a mindless world it could still be the case that suffering is bad and coherence is good.

(4) The transmission principle: when reasoners do come to exist, free-floating rationales become reasons for them not by being created de novo, but by being registered or picked up through rational capacities.

(5) Anti-reductionism with descriptive anchoring: reasons are not reducible to purely descriptive facts, yet they can be instantiated in descriptive structures—ecological equilibria, informational architectures, causal patterns—without being identical to them.

Please audit this set. Specifically:

- Identify which pairs are in genuine logical or motivational tension, and which only appear incompatible because of ambiguous terms.
- Rank the tensions by severity.
- For each genuine tension, specify the least costly revision and exactly what that revision sacrifices.
- Say whether there is a single change that resolves the most conflicts at once, and what it costs.
- Check whether the distinction between reasons (reasoner-relative) and rationales (free-floating) can be maintained consistently with (4) and (5), or whether it collapses under pressure.
- Do not treat all five commitments as equally problematic; some should come out largely unscathed, and you should say which ones.
