A colleague in my philosophy of science seminar summarized structural realism as follows: The view was developed to answer the pessimistic meta-induction. Epistemic structural realists claim that only mathematical structures survive theory change, so we should remain agnostic about the nature of underlying objects. Ontic structural realists go further, treating relations as ontologically primary and objects as derivative or eliminable, which dissolves the pessimistic meta-induction altogether because there is no enduring ontology of entities to be undermined. The colleague then concluded that this renders structural realism immune to standard realist objections, but at the cost of collapsing into a kind of Pythagoreanism in which physical reality is nothing but instantiated formal structure, leaving empirical discovery with no independent explanatory role.

This sounds mostly right, yet I suspect it conflates positions that defenders keep apart and overstates how cleanly the induction is dissolved. Could you audit the summary? Specifically, please separate (a) what is accurate or a standard gloss, (b) what subtly misrepresents either the epistemic or ontic version, (c) whether the claim about dissolving the induction applies equally to both, and (d) whether the Pythagoreanism charge genuinely follows or depends on an unstated premise about the relation between mathematics and empirical content.
