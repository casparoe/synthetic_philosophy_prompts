I am preparing a long-form essay on whether virtue ethics can survive the situationist critique without becoming conceptually unrecognizable, and before I publish I want a rigorous consistency check on the working framework I have assembled. Here is my current package: (1) Moral character is real, but only as domain-clustered dispositions—there is no trait of “honesty simpliciter,” only reliable behavioral patterns within specific ecological niches (financial dealings, intimate disclosure, professional obligations). (2) The situationist literature correctly demolishes the global-trait psychology assumed by classical virtue ethics, yet it does not show that dispositions are fictions; it shows they are context-sensitive and modular. (3) I adopt a threshold model: a virtue is present when an agent’s behavior in a relevant domain exceeds a baseline of ordinary situational variance, not when it is invariant across every context. (4) Reflective equilibrium in ethics must be empirically informed; if psychology demonstrates low cross-situational consistency, virtue theory should revise its ontology of character rather than dismiss the data. (5) Moral education should therefore prioritize “situation engineering”—the deliberate structuring of environments so that domain-clustered virtues are expressed—alongside traditional inner cultivation, and I take this to be continuous with Aristotelian practical wisdom rather than a betrayal of it. (6) At the same time, I want to preserve the Aristotelian claim that eudaimonia requires a unified, stable life-plan directed by coherent practical reason, which appears to presuppose some cross-domain integration of character. (7) Finally, I reject any moral realism that posits virtues as mind-independent properties of persons; I treat them as relational properties emerging from agent-environment interactions, yet I still maintain that character judgments can be objectively correct within a given ecological framework.

My question is whether these seven commitments actually hang together, or whether I am smuggling in incompatible pictures of the self. Specifically, is there a deep tension between (1) and (6) that forces me to abandon either the modular view or the Aristotelian unity requirement? Does (5) genuinely reconcile with (6), or does situation engineering collapse virtue into a form of environmental behaviorism that renders inner character redundant? Is (7) consistent with the normative force of (3) and (4), or does a relational ontology undermine the threshold model’s claim to objectivity? And is there an apparent contradiction—perhaps between (2) and (7)—that dissolves once the distinction between global-trait realism and relational objectivity is properly drawn?

Please audit this set: rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what must be revised or qualified, identify which tensions are only apparent, and state the least costly path back to consistency without presuming which commitment I would prefer to surrender. In your response, please say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you are least sure of.
