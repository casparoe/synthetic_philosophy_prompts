Consider a disagreement between two rational agents who observe the same empirical data regarding the scaling behavior and internal representations of large transformer-based systems, yet hold credences of 0.2 and 0.8 respectively for the claim that deceptive alignment will emerge in models trained with standard reinforcement learning from human feedback once they exceed a certain capability threshold. Rather than attributing this divergence to hidden evidence, I want you to evaluate whether it can be coherently explained by a failure of the common prior assumption in a domain where representational frameworks differ. Give me your own credence, expressed as a number between 0 and 1, that the divergence is primarily due to genuinely different prior distributions over the space of possible internal representations—rather than differences in likelihood weighting, ontological framing, or reasoning error. Decompose your reasoning into four parts: (a) the probability that formal differences in representing alignment—for instance, behavioral consistency versus mechanistic interpretability—count as prior differences rather than likelihood-model differences; (b) the probability that conditions analogous to Aumann agreement fail specifically because the agents lack common knowledge of translation rules between their representational frameworks; (c) the probability that at least one agent is committing a subtle reasoning error, such as base-rate neglect regarding deceptive behavior in non-biological optimization processes, that merely simulates a prior divergence; and (d) the probability that the empirical evidence is genuinely ambiguous in a way that makes high prior sensitivity rational rather than pathological. Assign a rough numerical credence to each part, specify how they aggregate—whether multiplicatively, as a weighted mixture, or through some other compositional rule—to produce your final figure, and identify the single sub-component to which your answer is most sensitive. Finally, state what specific finding, perhaps from formal epistemology or comparative studies of framework translation, would move that most-sensitive component by at least 0.2.
