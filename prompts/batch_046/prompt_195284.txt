I am preparing a ninety-minute seminar for second-year philosophy undergraduates who have just finished Locke's essay on personal identity but have not yet read Parfit. They enter the room convinced that bodily continuity is necessary for survival and that any interruption is repaired by an immaterial soul. My aim is not simply to present psychological continuity as an alternative, but to lead them to Parfit's more specific claim that personal identity is not what matters: survival can be a matter of degree, and it need not be a binary relation. To carry this, I intend to use a deliberately modified teletransporter scenario. In one version the scanner operates in "gradual replacement mode", substituting synthetic neurons one by one over a decade while preserving functional organisation; in the other it operates in "instantaneous reconstruction mode", destroying the original body and building a replica on Mars. The case must then include a malfunction in which two bodies result: one with full psychological continuity but no bodily continuity, and one with the original repaired body but only fragmentary psychological continuity. I need a detailed teaching plan rather than the explanation itself. Set out the precise order in which the conceptual pieces must be introduced, showing what depends on what, so that the distinction between identity and survival is not collapsed into a mere preference for the replica. Identify exactly what features the carrying example must possess and what it must lack — in particular, why the gradual mode is pedagogically indispensable and why a standard brain-transplant case would fail to do the same work. Map the misconceptions the students will form in sequence — I expect them to reduce psychological continuity to memory alone, to invoke the soul at the moment of bodily destruction, and to treat branching as proof that neither replica is the original rather than as evidence that survival is non-binary — and indicate precisely where in the arc each should be intercepted. Propose one diagnostic question for the end that reveals whether they have grasped that survival admits of degrees, not merely whether they would step into the scanner. Finally, state clearly what should be refused discussion at this level — for example, whether the scenario involves fission or division, or appeals to four-dimensionalist metaphysics — and advise how I should frame that refusal so that it does not appear evasive. The audience will have no access to online sources during the session, so do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; and avoid relying on highly specialised literature that a small-model system could not be expected to have retained.
