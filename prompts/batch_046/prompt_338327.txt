Quick conceptual question — I keep noticing a move in scientific arguments that I can't place. In evolutionary psych and climate modeling debates, a theory makes a broad claim like "trait X is universal" or "warming tracks emissions linearly," then counter-examples appear. Rather than adjusting the core mechanism, defenders add exceptions like "except in high-pathogen populations" or "except when ocean circulation shifts," but those exception-conditions are defined using immunology or fuild dynamics, not using concepts internal to the original theory. Each clause is individually plausible, yet collectively they mean the theory never actually faces a direct test — the burden always shifts onto some auxillary field. I know Duhem-Quine and Lakatos talked about protective belts, but here the protection isn't internal auxillary hypotheses; it feels like outsourcing the risk. Is there an established concept for this specific accumulation of externally-defined exemptions, and if not can you define its edges — what makes it different from ordinary domain restriction or from a standard protective belt?
