I was chatting with my friend over coffee about whether reasons have to match what we actually care about, and we got stuck on this weird middle ground — like, can there be a reason that's genuinely yours even if you don't feel it yet, but only if you've already kind of half-started doing the thing? I'm taking a course on practical reason and find the internal/external split a bit to tidy. So I want a taxonomy, like a decision tree or sorting quiz, that takes someone from a concrete case — imagine you see a stranger drop their bag and feel zero motivation to help, but still think 'they'd want me to' — and branches through questions about silent reasons, motivational residue, and whether external reasons need some transmision mechanism or can just sit there as brute normative facts. Each branch should split by what someone actually believes about the case, not by asking 'are you an internalist?' The leaves should include Williams-style internalism, Korsgaard-style constructivism, Parfit-style object-givism, and at least one unnamed hybrid. For each leaf, one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Also flag pairs the tree can't really separate, and note any empty slots in the logical space — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored? About 6-8 leaves total. No bibliography needed, I just want the instrument for a seminar handout.
