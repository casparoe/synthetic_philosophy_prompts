ok heres roughly where ive landed on knowledge/gettier stuff for a discussion group and i want a consistency check before i present.

(1) im convinced gettier cases demolish JTB — not just that its insufficient, but that justification isnt even necessary for knowledge.

(2) i think the right positive account is modal safety: p counts as knowledge only if in nearby worlds you wouldnt easily have believed p falsely.

(3) in the classic job/coins case — smith infers the job-getter has ten coins, but its actually smith himself who gets it and has the coins — theres definitely no knowledge. too fragile.

(4) in the disjunction case — smith believes jones owns a ford, so he infers jones owns a ford or brown is in barcelona, and brown actually happens to be there — i think smith DOES know the disjunction.

(5) the difference is that (3) has "intervening" luck where the world corrects the error, but (4) is just "environmental" luck that doesnt undermine the belief.

(6) i also think justification is purely a normative thing — epistemic blamelessness — not part of the metaphysics of knowledge. so patching JTB with a no-false-lemmas clause misses the point.

(7) and i hold that if a belief is safe, its knowledge regardless of false premises in its reasoning history.

go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly. for each real tension say what has to give and whats the cheapest fix. also flag any tensions that look real but dissolve once you look closer. rank them by severity, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems at once.
