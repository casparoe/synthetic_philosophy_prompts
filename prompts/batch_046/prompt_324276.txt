She uses it to suggest that if the labels are retrospective constructions, then there never was any real discontinuity in culture or science—only smooth change that we later chopped up for convenience. Before I respond in class, I want to understand exactly what that one-liner could be claiming. Could you separate the precise theses hiding inside it? Specifically: (1) that the vocabulary itself was coined long after the events; (2) that people alive at the time did not experience themselves as living through a named "rebirth" or "revolution"; (3) that quantitative measures—literacy rates, art production, scientific publication—show no special spike in those centuries compared to adjacent ones; (4) that these categories work for Western Europe but collapse when applied to East Asian or Islamic historiography, which uses entirely different divisions; and (5) that the labels smuggle in hidden value judgments—darkness, rebirth, progress—rather than describing neutral facts. For each thesis, say whether it is trivially true, genuinely disputable, or plainly false, and—crucially—whether accepting it actually supports her conclusion that no important break occurred, or whether it only attacks the naming convention. I also want to know: if Chinese historians do not divide their past into "Middle Ages" and "Renaissance," does that make the European scheme an arbitrary local convention, or can a regional taxonomy still track real regional changes? Please lay the readings out as a menu rather than picking one, and write the response in American English.
