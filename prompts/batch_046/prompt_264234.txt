Please describe the most important implications of the following hybrid ontological framework for the debate between Everettian, Copenhagen, and Bohmian interpretations of quantum mechanics. The framework assumes that the universal wavefunction evolves unitarily in a high-dimensional configuration space without collapse, generating decoherent branches as in Everett, but also posits that actual particle positions are governed by a Bohm-style guidance equation. However, it rejects both the quantum equilibrium hypothesis and the Copenhagen postulate of an irreducible classical-quantum divide. Instead, it imposes a single global constraint: the actual particle configuration of any macroscopic measuring apparatus must lie within the support of exactly one decoherent branch, with cross-branch correlations suppressed not by statistical equilibrium but by this selection rule.

Specifically, address: (1) how this modifies the standard Everettian problem of self-locating uncertainty and personal identity across branches; (2) whether the Bohmian guidance equation can still be coherently formulated without a privileged global foliation of spacetime once the equilibrium distribution is abandoned; and (3) what this implies for the Copenhagen claim that measurement outcomes require an external, classically described observer to actualize them. In each case, identify which structural feature of the standard interpretations is being preserved and which is being undermined, and explain whether the resulting view collapses back into one of the three canonical positions or remains genuinely distinct. Do not provide bibliographies or reading lists; engage directly with the structural logic of the interpretations.
