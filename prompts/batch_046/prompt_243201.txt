I am an undergraduate studying philosophy of mind, and I have adopted a strong discontinuity interpretation of Dennett's fourfold taxonomy—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. Specifically, I hold that Gregorian minds do not merely accumulate prior capacities but deploy a second-order predictive architecture, mediated by language and cultural transmission, that renders first-order trial-and-error mechanisms causally and explanatorily inert for human behavior. Consequently, I treat the taxonomy as discontinuous rather than cumulative: once this architecture is present, the lower kinds are superseded, not layered beneath.

What would genuinely count against this view? I am not asking for the familiar objection that language is continuous with animal signaling. Instead, I want to know which empirical findings (for example, in comparative cognition or developmental psychology), which conceptual distinctions (for instance, regarding representation, prediction, or cultural evolution), and which broader results about persistence or explanation in other domains it risks being undermined by. Which defeaters would defenders of the discontinuity thesis concede as damaging, and which would they reject as failing to grasp the architectural claim? Please rank these from fatal to merely awkward, and say whether the position is well insulated or genuinely exposed.

In your answer, please distinguish what is settled from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and avoid presenting a contested interpretation as if it were consensus. Please write in British English.
