Following a long and rather heated argument with a friend about consciousness, I want an audit of the summary she offered after reading a popular article on Ned Block’s distinction and the global neuronal workspace account. Her account ran roughly like this: Block holds that access consciousness is information made globally available to cognitive systems for reasoning, reporting, and behavioural control, which corresponds precisely to what the global workspace describes as ignition and widespread broadcast. Phenomenal consciousness, by contrast, is the raw subjective quality that can exist even when information never reaches the workspace. She illustrated this with blindsight, saying that because the subject can discriminate stimuli without freely reporting them, the information is present in the system but not globally broadcast; hence blindsight is a clear case of access consciousness without phenomenal consciousness. She then turned to Block’s overflow argument, citing Sperling’s partial-report experiments in which subjects appear to experience a grid of twelve letters but can report only about four. On her telling, this demonstrates that phenomenal consciousness can contain far more content than the workspace can broadcast, so phenomenal consciousness overflows access consciousness. From this she concluded that global workspace theory explains at best the access variety, and that phenomenal consciousness is therefore a non-functional, irreducible property that no broadcasting mechanism could ever capture.

Something about this chain feels too tidy, particularly the use of blindsight and the metaphysical conclusion drawn from the overflow case. I am inclined to think that the relationship between Block’s philosophical categories and the mechanistic details of workspace theory is more complicated, and that defenders of the global account do not simply concede that phenomenal consciousness is left unexplained. Could you tell me whether this is a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate, what is a common but misleading simplification, and what a defender of either Block’s view or the global workspace tradition would explicitly deny or qualify. Where the literature is genuinely divided, please distinguish settled points from contested interpretations, and do not present a disputed claim as if it were the consensus.
