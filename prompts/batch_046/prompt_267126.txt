Write a short story illustrating Alfred North Whitehead's conception of education as a rhythmic process moving through romance, precision, and generalization, rather than as linear accumulation of information. The protagonist should be a genuinely non-human learning system—perhaps a decentralized archival network or a synthetic ecological monitor—whose mode of cognition is structurally different from human consciousness. Show what the "romance" stage looks like for this entity (a period of loose, wonder-driven association), then the "precision" stage (a disciplined, restrictive analysis that risks deadening the initial insight), and finally a "generalization" stage that is not merely a summary but a surprising reintegration. The story should make the Whiteheadian rhythm visible without anthropomorphizing the learner, and it should suggest why premature precision—or premature generalization—would damage such a system's coherence.
