ok so i'm trying to figure out what i actually think about advance directives and whether your earlier self gets to boss around your later self when the later one is kinda... different? here are my gut verdicts on cases, and i can't get them to line up.

case 1: my mom's directive said if she gets alzheimers and doesn't recognize her kids, don't treat pneumonia aggressively, just let her go. now she has it, doesn't know us, but she laughs at cartoons and seems genuinely content. my verdict: honor the directive, withhold the antibiotics. she made the deal when she was the one who'd have to live through it.

case 2: my uncle said never keep him on a ventilator longer than two weeks, period. he went on one, woke up after three, and said he was grateful. my verdict: we should still have pulled it at two weeks. the waking up was luck, not a reason to violate the contract he made with himself.

case 3: my friend wrote that if she's ever in a vegetative state with no recovery chance, donate her organs even if it means ending support a day early. she ends up exactly there, but her partner is pregnant and wants one more day. my verdict: stick to the directive, take the organs. her earlier self's values shouldn't be held hostage by someone else's greif.

case 4: a guy refused all blood transfusions in his directive on religious grounds. then he got dementia, forgot his religion completely, and now asks for the transfusion that'd save him. my verdict: give him the transfusion. the person asking isn't the one who made the religious promise; the values that grounded the refusal are gone.

case 5: a woman said if she ever loses the ability to enjoy music — that's her whole reason for living — then no dialysis, no heroic stuff. now she has dementia, can't remember her family, but she still plays piano beautifully and clearly enjoys it. then her kidneys fail. my verdict: give her dialysis. the specific condition she named hasn't been met.

so far so good, but then there's case 6, the one i keep going back and forth on: my neighbor wrote that if he ever gets locked-in syndrome, keep him alive for exactly one year then withdraw care, no exceptions. now he's in it, it's been 11 months, and he blinks yes/no to communicate. he blinks "keep going" over and over. do we honor the one-year clause or his present blinks? part of me says the directive was precise for a reason; part says he's literally asking right now.

is there a coherent principle behind 1-5, and does it force me to pick a side in 6? or do i have to drop one of the earlier verdicts? also what would your principle say about a case i haven't listed — say, someone who directs that if she becomes severely demented she should be moved to a cheap facility to preserve her grandkids' inheritence, even if she protests later. now she's protesting but also doesn't understand what money is?
