In software verification people treat a failure mode that's unreachable in the abstract model as literally impossible in the real system, but security auditors usually won't defend against an attack unless it's happened before or can be clearly pictured. What view of conceivability as a guide to possibility does that split preseuppose? Is verification assuming that if we can't conceive a path in the model then there's no real-world path, while security assumes the opposite? And are those two stances consistent, or do they just mean different things by 'possible'?
