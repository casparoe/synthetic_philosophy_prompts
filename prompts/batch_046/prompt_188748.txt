So I've been noticing this weird pattern in online discussions and even in my grad seminar, and I dont have a clean word for it. Someone posts something genuinely ambiguous - like a joke that could be read as deadpan serious or as mocking the thing it sounds like its praising. Then a few people lock in one interpretation, and suddenly everyone else is not just agreeing with them but insisting they "saw it that way all along," or that the other reading is now obviously wrong, even though literally nothing in the original post changed except the sequence of replies. I see a parallel in citation chains: a study gets described in one review as supporting view X, and then later papers cite it using framing that only makes sense under X, gradually making the original study sound more decisive than its authors intended, just because the cascade of prior citations rewrote the context.

This feels different from people just hiding disagreement to fit in, because they genuinely seem to update their private understanding, not just suppress it. Its also not exactly the usual thing where people copy others because they assume others have better information. Here the cascade is reshaping interpretation itself. Is there an established name for this specific kind of interpretive cascade - where prior social readings dont just determine what people say, but actually change what they think they originally perceived? If not, can you define its edges precisely: how is it different from plain herding, from groupthink, and from just motivated reasoning? I'd like to know whether Im describing a real recognized phenomenon or just inventing a distinction.
