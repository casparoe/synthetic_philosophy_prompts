I am a doctoral student in sociology of religion and historical sociology, working on a dissertation about cultural explanation, and I am writing up my philosophical commitments for a reading-group session. I want an internal consistency audit before I present. Here is where I stand:

(a) I defend the general possibility of cultural causation in macro-history—something in the Weberian spirit—according to which value-systems are not merely epiphenomenal rationalizations of material conditions but can exercise independent causal influence on institutional development.

(b) I also hold that for a value to do genuine causal work it must satisfy what I call the motivational-translation condition: the value has to be internalized by agents in a way that actually restructures their practical reasoning and choice architecture, rather than simply correlating with behavior that is driven by other forces.

(c) At the same time, I think cultural causation can occur through what I term value-mediated selection environments: institutions can reward or penalize behaviors that align with a value-system regardless of whether the relevant agents have internalized that system, so that the value operates causally even when the motivational-translation condition is largely unmet.

(d) I accept methodological individualism for social explanation: any macro-level pattern must ultimately bottom out in explanations that reference individual actions, beliefs, and incentives, rather than treating cultures or institutions as agents with their own intentions.

(e) I reject teleological or functionalist explanations that treat values as if they literally “aim” at social outcomes; I think Weber’s talk of values “driving” capitalism is acceptable only as a heuristic, not as an ontologically serious causal claim.

(f) Finally, I agree with the historical critics who argue that Weber overstated the uniqueness of Calvinist predestination psychology—similar ascetic disciplines appear in Catholic and other traditions that did not produce capitalist rationalization—yet I maintain that this historical correction does not undermine the general claim in (a).

Please audit this package. Which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision that would restore consistency? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each specify which single commitment would have to be revised or qualified and what that revision would cost me philosophically. I want the answer written in American English, and I do not need bibliographies, reading lists, or citations—just the conceptual analysis.
