I've been poking at Dennet's multiple drafts idea since I left the lab and started reading philosophy in retirement. Here's an argument I cooked up that seems to blow up in my face. (1) The Cartesian theater is a myth: there's no single place in the brain where consciousness all comes together for a little viewer; instead there are just competing drafts being edited, with no final cut shown anywhere. (2) If there's no place where it all comes together, then there's no unified subject who is actually aware of anything — just scattered neural processes with no owner. (3) But scientific observation requires a unified observer who can consistently report what she sees and remembers. (4) So if the theater is really a myth, then there's literally nobody doing the observing in science — just brain processes spitting out sentences with no understander behind them. (5) That means scientific knowledge isn't actually known by anyone; it's just mechanical output with no real comprehension. (6) But that undermines Dennet's own theory, since he claims we understand consciousness precisely through scientific study. (7) So rejecting the theater ends up destroying the very reasoning used to reject it. (8) Therefore the Cartesian theater must exist after all, or science itself is impossible.

I definatly feel like (2) or maybe (4) is smuggling something in, but I can't name it. Which step should I give up if I want to keep the anti-theater view and also keep science intact? And what do I have to pay if I drop that step?
