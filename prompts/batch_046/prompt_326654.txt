Write a villanelle about Bertrand's paradox. Let the two mandatory refrains—unchanged verbatim in every recurrence—carry the principle of indifference, for example: "Every outcome weighs the same" and "The chord is drawn, the circle waits." Structure the five interior tercets so that each presents one of the three classic constructions: two random endpoints on the circumference (giving 1/3), parallel lines with distance from the center uniformly distributed (giving 1/2), and a midpoint chosen uniformly within the circle's area (giving 1/4). In each tercet, the surrounding lines should describe that specific parameterization precisely—radius versus chord, distance from center, uniform area—while the refrains sit unchanged, so that the reader feels the same principle of equal ignorance producing three different probabilities over the same geometric object. Use the opening and closing quatrains to frame the circle and its inscribed equilateral triangle as the shared reference, and let the closing quatrain bring both refrains together without resolving the contradiction: no appeal to pragmatism, subjectivity, or hidden variables, just the bare fact that continuous symmetry supplies no canonical measure. Keep the language exact enough that someone familiar with the paradox recognizes each construction immediately, but do not cite authors, texts, or historical attributions; the poem should stand entirely on its own conceptual structure.
