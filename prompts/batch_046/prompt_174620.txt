You've got this wierd split: in most of europe commercial surrogacy and paid egg donation are banned on the grounds that the body shouldn't be commodified, but in the US there's an open market where agencies charge different prices for eggs based on donor education, ethnicity, and even athletic record. At the same time, american contracts still frame it as a gift and make donors waive parental rights as if they're handing over a present, not selling tissue. The practice does X (market pricing with selection criteria) and Y (gift-language and inalienability waivers) at once. What theory of value or bodily integrity makes that combo coherant? Is it that some human capacities are incommensurable with cash except when mediated through individual choice, or is there a hidden distinction between selling reproductive labor and selling genetic material? Reconstruct the implicit view, check whether the european ban actually presupposes a different theory or just draws the line elsewhere, and say what a policy built on strict incommensurability — or on full commodification — would have to change about altruistic donation and selection screening.
