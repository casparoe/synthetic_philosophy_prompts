Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for a graduate seminar in Buddhist philosophy that sorts interpretive positions on Nāgārjuna's catuṣkoṭi, specifically as applied to the recursive claim that emptiness applies to itself. The tree must open with one entry question answerable without knowing Madhyamaka labels: for instance, whether the respondent treats the four corners—affirmation, negation, both, neither—as jointly exhaustive logical possibilities for any claim. Each subsequent branch node should pose a concrete commitment about how negation behaves in this recursive case—such as whether self-application produces a vicious regress, a stable fixed point, or a category mistake—rather than asking which school a respondent belongs to. The leaves must include: (i) the standard Madhyamaka stance that all four corners are negated without residue; (ii) a view that recursive self-negation dissolves into a non-conceptual insight rendering the taxonomy moot; (iii) a Svātantrika-style position preserving a conventionally valid, non-ultimate assertion at the final node; (iv) a Prāsaṅgika-style denial that any final node can be fixed; and (v) an orthogonal-domains interpretation—drawn from recent comparative work—that assigns the four corners to separate registers (ontological, epistemic, pragmatic, soteriological), so that emptiness-emptiness means different things in each and no contradiction arises. For every pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely distinguish, flag the overlap and say whether it is logical or merely pedagogical. Identify any answer-combination that yields an unoccupied leaf, and briefly diagnose whether the gap signals incoherence, unattractiveness, or an unexplored option. Limit the tree to six branch nodes or fewer, phrase every choice as answerable by someone unfamiliar with Sanskrit terminology, and do not request bibliographies, primary-text citations, or lists of secondary sources.
