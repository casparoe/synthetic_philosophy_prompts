hey, i tried writing some rough notes for a post about why human extinction is bad beyond just counting bodies. i read about this philosopher samuel scheffler and his afterlife conjecture from death and the afterlife — basically people care about humanity continuing after they die, not in a religious sense, but like knowing your projects get carried forward. i mixed it with some software analogies and added my own twist. can you comment?

draft essay / notes:

most people say extinction is bad because everyone dies. but scheffler argues there's another layer: a lot of our meaning comes from expecting a collective afterlife where other people keep going after we're gone — raising kids, maintaining culture, finishing long projects. if humanity ended right after you died, your life would feel different, less anchored, even if you personally survived until the end.

i want to map this onto open source. in software your commit only matters if the repo survives. if the whole project gets archived and deleted, all your contributions become dead bytes with no users. so is scheffler basically saying human meaning works like a pull request that needs future maintainers? that feels like a stretch, but maybe it captures the dependency.

heres my own twist, and im not sure its valid: maybe we're not actually valuing the far future in some deep metaphysical way. maybe we just satisfice. we care about the next two or three generations because thats the horizon we can actually imagine, and humanity continues forever is just a cheap cognitive shortcut — a bounded-rationality heuristic. if thats true, then extinction is bad mainly because it breaks our heuristic, not because there's a real dependency on infinite continuity.

also, what counts as the repo continuing? if we colonize mars or upload minds, does that preserve the afterlife? scheffler seems to assume biological continuity, but in cs terms a fork is still the codebase. i think this branching problem might be the weak point in my analogy, but i dont know enough philosophy to check.
