I'm working through whether Frankfurt cases actually undermine the principle of alternative possibilities, and Oedipus Rex keeps coming to mind as a kind of natural experiment. The plot is common knowledge: Oedipus learns he is fated to kill his father and marry his mother, flees Corinth to avoid it, strikes an old man at a crossroads in self-defense, then solves the Sphinx's riddle and takes Thebes and its queen—only to discover that the old man was Laius and the queen is Jocasta.

Read the play through Frankfurt's counterfactual-intervention framework and the PAP debate. Is Oedipus ever in a Frankfurt case? The oracle and the divine structure seem to function like a hidden controller that guarantees the outcome regardless of his first-order will, yet he acts without coercion—choosing to strike at the crossroads, choosing to investigate the plague. Do those moments count as Fischer and Ravizza's "flickers of freedom," salvaging responsibility because he genuinely could have done otherwise in the relevant sense, or are they illusory choices inside a deterministic mesh?

Take Frankfurt's hierarchical distinction seriously: Oedipus's first-order desires lead him to kill and marry, but his second-order volition—his horror at having done so—seems to reject them. Does the play map that hierarchy onto the structure of recognition and suffering, or does the mythic framework collapse the distinction? Be honest about misfires: the tragedy turns on epistemic failure—Oedipus doesn't know who he is—whereas Frankfurt cases bracket knowledge and focus on modal access. Does that make Sophocles a poor fit, or does it show that PAP debates miss something about responsibility that the play captures through ignorance rather than inability?

Give me a reading faithful in both directions: don't reduce the drama to an illustration of counterfactual intervention, and don't distort Frankfurt's conditions to make the oracle fit. Where does the philosophical lens illuminate a scene—especially the crossroads and the final recognition—and where does the text resist it? End by saying what the play adds to the debate that argument alone doesn't.
