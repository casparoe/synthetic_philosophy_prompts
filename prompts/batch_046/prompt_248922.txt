For my grad seminar on philopsophy of personhood, write out the list of desiderata that any relational theory — specifically one working in the ubuntu tradition but not just parroting it — has to meet if it's going to be adequate for the hard cases. I mean cases like: a person is exiled and all kinship ties are legally severed, but they still seem to have moral standing; or someone is in an asymmetrical care relation (parent/infant) where reciprocity is impossible; or the state withholds recognition and claims you're not a legal person, but your community still treats you as one.

For each desideratum say what case motivates it, whether it's non-negotiable or just a preference of the communitarian camp, and flag any pairs that seem jointly unsatisfiable. I'm especially interested in whether a theory can both (a) say personhood is genuinely constituted by relations and not just expressed through them, and (b) allow that personhood survives the breakdown of those relations without secretly smuggling in a liberal individualist core. That's the tension I want the list to expose.

Keep the weight on the criteria and what they cost, not on scoring existing theories. Dont need bibliographies or citations — I want to see if the list itself does the work.

Format:
- The hard cases (brief, ~150 words)
- The desiderata with motivations (~600 words)
- The tension map: which pairs conflict (~300 words)
- What's negotiable vs. non-negotiable (~200 words)
