I have been revisiting Douglas Hofstadter’s tripartite distinction from Gödel, Escher, Bach among frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages: roughly, messages that establish the interpretive frame within which a system is read; messages carried by the formal system itself in its direct operation; and messages that emerge only when the system becomes self-referential, producing what Hofstadter calls a “strange loop.” I work in programming-language semantics and am comfortable with the separation between object-language syntax, denotational semantics, meta-logical specifications, and reflective meta-programming (for example, the difference between a program’s internal judgments, its external soundness proof, and a self-interpreting compiler or reflective tower that treats its own syntax as data).

Please restate Hofstadter’s three-layer model in that vocabulary, one layer at a time. For frame messages, say whether they correspond to a meta-language specification, a type-system context, or something like a precondition that fixes interpretation; for outer messages, whether they map onto the object-language’s syntactic derivations, its semantic values, or the trace of computation; and for inner messages, whether they have any analogue in reflective evaluation, fixed-point combinators, or meta-circular interpreters, and if so, exactly where the analogy holds and where it breaks. Be explicit about what translates cleanly, what must be distorted to fit (for instance, if the informal, meaning-laden quality of a “strange loop” resists formalization as pure syntax manipulation), and what has no expression at all in programming-language semantics. Then deliver a residue report: does the untranslated remainder point to a genuine gap in formal semantics—something like an emergent, interpretive self-reference that syntax and semantics alone cannot capture—or does it suggest that Hofstadter’s “inner message” depends on an intuitive, pre-formal notion that dissolves once we require rigorous meta-language separation? I am grading this as an exercise in conceptual translation, so I want the failures of mapping treated as the most interesting part of the answer, not as shortcomings to be glossed over.
