I am trying to fix the evaluation standards for what counts as an adequate theory of philosophical intuition pumps — the mechanisms by which thought experiments persuade, mislead, or restructure intuitive judgment — before I assess any particular account. Write the full list of desiderata: the phenomena the theory must explain, the distinctions it must preserve, the results from cognitive science or logic with which it must be consistent, and the puzzles it must dissolve, with the motivating case or mechanism behind each requirement.

Cover at least the following four phenomena, treating them as test cases rather than mere illustrations: (1) framing-dependent reversals, as in the switch versus footbridge variants of the trolley scenario, where the normative verdict shifts despite invariant outcomes and agent intentions; (2) persistence pumps, such as the Ship of Theseus, where the intuition of identity or non-identity over time appears to track conventional practices of part-replacement rather than any mind-independent continuity condition; (3) modal-conceivability pumps, including the inverted-spectrum and philosophical-zombie arguments, which leverage first-person imaginative limits to license metaphysical conclusions about consciousness and possibility; and (4) a less standard case — the combinatorial-exhaustion pump drawn from modal-realist discussions of Borges’s library of Babel, where the intuition that every possible combination of books actually exists may conflate mathematical cardinality with genuine metaphysical possibility, and where the pump works by overwhelming the imagination with scale rather than by isolating a specific modal intuition.

For each desideratum, indicate whether it is non-negotiable (for example, explaining framing shifts without reducing them to noise that philosophy should simply correct away) or camp-specific (for example, preserving a sharp distinction between conceptual and empirical analysis in normative pumps). Then identify any subset of requirements that appears jointly unsatisfiable: in particular, can one theory simultaneously treat framing effects as cognitive heuristics or biases to be explained away, and also treat the pumped intuitions as authoritative data for conceptual analysis? Briefly score — without a table, and keeping the weight light — how a pure conceptual-analysis view, a heuristics-and-bias cognitive-science view, and a dual-process hybrid fare against the list, with particular attention to which single desideratum eliminates the most candidates. Keep the focus on the criteria themselves: I want to see which demands are genuine constraints on explanation and which are methodological prejudices of one camp.
