Please describe the most important implications of the Pasadena paradox—a St. Petersburg-like game in which rewards and penalties grow without bound and alternate in sign, so that the expectation is a conditionally convergent series lacking a unique value—for dominance reasoning when expected utility is undefined. Specifically, address whether the paradox forces dominance to be applied locally, path-by-path, rather than globally across outcomes, and how this bears on the distinction between ex ante rationality assessed state-by-state and ex post evaluation over realized histories. Discuss whether the case undermines the principle that a strategy dominated in every possible outcome should be rejected, or whether it instead reveals that global dominance presupposes a well-defined aggregation measure that is absent here. Finally, state what this implies for practical rationality under radical uncertainty, where payoff structures may be unbounded in opposing directions and no canonical expectation exists.
