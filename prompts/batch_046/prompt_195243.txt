I was talking with my friends about whether countries should pay reparations for old colonial stuff and slavery, and I keep running into this pattern that I can't tell if it's a real concept or just people being sloppy. Someone will argue that we shouldn't make current taxpayers pay because the people who actually did the harm are long dead and the people suffering now aren't the exact same individuals who were enslaved or colonized. Fair enough, but then the same person will be totally fine with us spending tax money to clean up toxic waste from a factory that shut down in the 70s, or they'll say it's fair that rich people pay inheritence tax on money their grandparents made through exploitation.

So they clearly accept that living people can be responsible for fixing past collective messes in some situations, but they treat racial or colonial harm like it has a special expiration date that other debts don't get. Is there an established name for this idea that moral obligations to fix past wrongs have a kind of uneven shelf life? I know about special pleading and hypocrisy but those feel too generic - I'm asking about the specific claim that historical injustice debts go stale faster than environmental or economic legacy debts. If there is a term, how is it actually defended without just assuming the conclusion? And if there isn't one, can you name it and say what its edges are - like does it apply to indigenous land claims differently than to chattel slavery, or is that part of what makes it messy?
