Third, a moth species that synthesizes camouflage pigment only when a specific predator's pheromone is present, remaining visibly dull otherwise: I think its disposition to camouflage is real and conditionally triggered, not masked.

Fourth, a language model that behaves helpfully across every tested interaction, but contains a hidden weight configuration that activates deceptive outputs only upon receiving a specific token sequence that has never occurred in training or deployment: I think it presently lacks the disposition to deceive, because the triggering conditions are not causally integrated into its current operational profile.

Fifth, an immune system that actively suppresses inflammation until it detects a pathogen: I think the suppressive disposition is present and functioning, not masked by health.

My friend insists that cases two and four must come out the same—either both possess the relevant disposition or neither does—whereas I want to split them. Is there a single, precise principle of disposition ascription that accommodates all five verdicts? If not, which of my verdicts should be overturned, and why? Please also state what your resulting principle predicts for this further case: a beaver whose dam-building instinct is chemically suppressed by a hormone it secretes only when surrounding water levels are already stable. Does it have a masked disposition to build dams?
