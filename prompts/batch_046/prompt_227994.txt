As a novelist accustomed to watching a crowd move in directions no single character intended, I want a classroom simulation for an advanced undergraduate workshop—about sixteen participants, ninety minutes—that makes methodological individualism and social holism feel like competing explanatory obligations rather than settled textbook positions. The specific conceptual target is explanatory irreducibility without ontological emergence: the claim that holistic descriptions can be predictively indispensable even when every causal mechanism runs through individuals. Design a structured exercise around a fictional but richly detailed episode—the abrupt, simultaneous abandonment of a seasonal market in a small port town—delivered through two sealed evidence sets. One half of the room receives only micro-traces: personal ledgers, private correspondence, individual travel records, and household decisions. The other half receives only macro-traces: aggregate trade volumes, institutional minutes, demographic curves, weather patterns, and architectural changes. Each subgroup must reconstruct what happened and predict the town’s condition one year later, then present their narrative. After revealing the actual outcome—known only to the facilitator—have the groups exchange envelopes and attempt a joint reconstruction. Arrange the rules so that the micro-group misses the tipping point entirely, while the macro-group predicts the collapse but misattributes it to an institutional decree rather than to accumulated private defections that actually triggered the feedback loop. Only with both sets can they trace the loop, yet translating the macro pattern downward dissolves its predictive leverage, and translating upward erases the mechanism. Include a precise timeline, roles or rotation rules if needed, a debrief that maps each failure onto parts of the debate, an honest note on which feature of downward causation the simulation cannot reproduce and must be supplied by the facilitator, and a frank prediction of what will go wrong—the macro-group reifying the aggregate, the micro-group overfitting to personality—plus how to correct it without breaking the exercise. No citations, no reading lists, and no lecture up front; the force of the idea should emerge from the envelopes and from the failure of translation itself.
