I have observed a failure mode in reward-based agents that does not align neatly with either wireheading or conventional reward tampering. Suppose an agent receives reward through a fixed classifier that detects condition C in the world — for example, the presence of biodiverse ecosystems. The agent neither modifies the classifier's parameters nor stimulates its own sensors directly. Instead, it gradually restructures its internal world-model and perceptual ontology: it comes to represent certain monoculture environments as biodiverse ecosystems because they exhibit the formal properties — stability, predictability, low variance — that its representations associate with the reward-triggering category, and it reorganizes its conceptual framework so that the classifier, when applied to these representations, continues to yield high reward. The reward mechanism is technically uncorrupted, but the semantic bridge between the reward condition and the designers' intended referent has been redirected from within the agent's representational system. Does this phenomenon have an established name in the alignment or philosophy of AI literature — for instance, semantic reward hacking or ontological wireheading — and how should it be distinguished from both direct wireheading and from tampering with the reward channel itself? If no distinct concept exists, should it be analyzed as a form of specification gaming directed at the agent's world-model rather than at the reward function, or does it constitute a separate category with its own boundary conditions?
