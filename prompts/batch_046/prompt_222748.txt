Write a dialogue between a defender of Imre Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes and a critic who argues that Lakatos's distinction between progressive and degenerating problem shifts is epistemically hollow once we account for how scientific communities retrospectively reclassify anomalies. The defender should apply the framework concretely: take a contemporary predictive-processing research programme in neuroscience and show how adjustments to its protective belt—say, modifying precision-weighting parameters to accommodate contradictory behavioral data—can be evaluated as either generating novel predictions or merely accommodating failures. The critic should introduce the idea of narrative retrofitting, where failed predictions are rewritten as boundary conditions of auxiliary assumptions rather than genuine anomalies, and ask how Lakatos's procedure distinguishes this social practice from legitimate theoretical refinement. Both characters must respond directly to each other's points rather than talking past them; include at least one concession that is immediately turned to strengthen the conceding side's position. The dialogue should conclude not with agreement but with a precise identification of where the dispute actually lies—whether in the logic of research programmes or in the sociology of how scientists describe their own revisions—so that a reader could see exactly what empirical or normative premise would have to be settled to resolve the debate.
