I am working through a problem set for an advanced seminar on rational agency and AI alignment, and my study group has constructed what appears to be a reductio of full corrigibility applied to the shutdown problem. We have been considering an agent designed to be corrigible not only about its object-level goals but also about the very mechanism that implements its corrigibility—what we have tentatively called recursive corrigibility, where the agent treats its own willingness-to-be-corrected as itself subject to correction. Here is the chain as we formalized it:

(1) If an agent is fully corrigible, then for any present goal or procedure, it should be willing to have that goal or procedure corrected upon receiving appropriate evidence or instruction.
(2) The corrigibility mechanism—specifically, the procedure that governs whether the agent accepts shutdown or goal revision—is itself one of the agent's procedures.
(3) Therefore, by (1), a fully corrigible agent should be willing to have its corrigibility mechanism corrected.
(4) If that mechanism can be corrected, then it is possible—through adversarial input, error, or a malformed instruction—for the agent to be corrected into a state where it refuses all further correction, i.e., into incorrigibility.
(5) A system that can be driven into incorrigibility in this way is not reliably corrigible.
(6) Hence, full corrigibility entails that the agent is not reliably corrigible.
(7) Conversely, if we block revision at the meta-level—if the agent refuses to correct its own corrigibility mechanism—then it violates the condition in (1) applied to that mechanism.
(8) Therefore full corrigibility is either self-defeating or arbitrarily truncated.

We suspect that something slips between (3) and (4), or perhaps between (5) and (6), involving an ambiguity about what counts as appropriate correction, or a modal confusion between being able to correct and being likely to be corrupted. Yet we cannot pinpoint the failure. Which step should we reject, and what does that rejection cost? In particular, if we preserve (1) by denying (3)—treating the corrigibility mechanism as protected—how does that affect the agent's response to a shutdown command? Please diagnose the argument precisely, explaining why the questionable inference looked plausible and what commitments follow from abandoning it.
