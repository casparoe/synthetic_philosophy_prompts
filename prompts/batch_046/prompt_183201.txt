I've been arguing with my roommate about whether asking 'why Britain and not China?' is even a legitimate historical question, and we're tired of talking past eachother. He's in a comparative history course and thinks the Great Divergence framing is basically sound if you just avoid being Eurocentric. I'm more skeptical — I think the Needham question already bakes in weird linear-progress assumptions, and comparing 'Britain' and 'China' as unitary actors is like comparing a startup to an entire supply chain. We want to write one of those adversarial colaboration statements, like philosophers do when they map out exactly where they agree and where they don't.

Can you draft the joint document for two camps? Camp one: the framing-is-valid people, who say comparative counterfactuals about industrialization are methodologically okay and that Britain vs Qing China is a well-defined contrast. Camp two: the framing-is-malformed people, who say the question presupposes teleological development, treats heterogeneous polities as single agents, and ignores that industrialization wasn't one package. We need a numbered section of agreed claims — stuff about actual economic histories, about what counterfactual reasoning requires in principle, about the heterogeneity within both regions — without citing specific papers. Then a disagreements table where each side's view is phrased in wording they'd actually accept. Then the cruxes: I think one is whether 'potential for self-sustaining growth' can be attributed to a region without circularity, and another is whether 'why not X?' implicitly frames X's path as failure. For each crux, propose a concrete analog case — like asking 'why did Rome fall and not the Han dynasty?' — and say what each camp predicts about whether that analog exposes the same framing problem or not. Also inject the distinction between explanatory counterfactuals and normative counterfactuals, since I think that's part of what's being confused. Keep it detailed and in the weeds, but don't ask for bibliographies or obscure citations — we're writing this for ourselves, not for a journal.
