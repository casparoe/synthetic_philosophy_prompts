I am a doctoral student in aesthetics and philosophy of mind, and I am seeking conceptual clarification on a problem that has arisen in my collaborative work with a researcher in computational musicology. She has built an interactive generative system that takes a single traditional folk melody and exposes a continuous, high-dimensional parameter space—continuous controls over interval tension, rhythmic displacement, modal mixture, and timbral density—allowing users to produce an enormous family of variations. She argues that because this space is vast and largely unexplored, any point selected within it should count as a genuinely original composition rather than a derivative copy of the source melody. I find this claim intuitively unsatisfying, yet I struggle to articulate the precise philosophical objection without simply appealing to an unexamined intuition about artistic originality.

The system also permits users to “lock” specific parameters—most commonly the melodic contour—while freely adjusting all others. My collaborator treats this locking mechanism as tracking a metaphysical distinction: the locked feature is the “essence” of the theme, while the unlocked dimensions are merely accidental ornamentation. This strikes me as suspiciously ad hoc, but I am unsure whether there is a principled way to designate certain musical properties as load-bearing for thematic identity without collapsing into an arbitrary essentialism about artworks.

My central question is whether there exists a rigorous philosophical framework—perhaps one that treats creativity as structured exploration within a conceptual space anchored by an original theme, or one that examines how thematic identity survives transformation—that can help me distinguish between (i) turning dials inside a pre-defined frame that preserves the original concept, and (ii) the kind of conceptual shift that produces a genuinely new thematic identity rather than a variation on the old one. Furthermore, I wonder whether the dimensionality or continuity of the parameter space has any independent bearing on the originality of the outputs, or whether questions of derivation and transformation depend entirely on how the frame itself is constituted rather than on how large its interior is.

I would particularly value an analysis of whether the intuition that some parameters are essential to a theme can be defended without begging the question against anti-essentialist accounts of artistic identity, and whether recognizing that true creativity might require altering the conceptual frame itself—rather than merely exploring its continuous interior—offers a more defensible account of what makes a variation genuinely transformative. I am looking for an application of relevant theoretical distinctions rather than generic practical advice about compositional practice.
