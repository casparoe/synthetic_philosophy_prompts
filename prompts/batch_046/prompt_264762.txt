I'm taking a philosophy of mind seminar and we got sidetracked arguing about Hofstadter's strange loop thing — how the self is just the symbol 'I' looping back on the neural patterns that made it. Here's where I land. A thermostat that reads temp and flips its heater has feedback but I say no strange loop — no symbol pointing back, just causation. A computer that logs 'I am running' and then reads that log to update its own code feels closer, but I still think it's not a real self because the symbol doesn't genuinely refference the pattern generating it; it's like a mirror that doesn't know it's a mirror. A human brain generating the thought 'I think' where the very neurons producing that thought are the ones being referred to — that feels like the real deal, a genuine loop. Now the hard ones. There's a corporation with charter documents describing its board rules, and the board uses those descriptions to rewrite the charter. I want to say there's a weak collective 'we' loop there, but I'm unsure. Meanwhile an AI that builds a perfect self-model but stores it offline so it never feeds back into processing: definitely not conscious, loop is open. But then there's the old book case — imagine every page describes the book's own binding and content, and publishers reprint it with updated pages based on that description.
