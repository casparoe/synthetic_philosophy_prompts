I've come to think societies have not just a permission to forget, like Rieff argues against the remembrance industry, but an actual positive duty to build forgetting into their institutions - call it mnemonic hygiene. I borrow Margalit's point that fixing people in past wrongs can be an ethical failure, but I push it further: democratic renewal needs structured amnesia, and AI archives violate this by making collective forgetting structurally impossible. I'm even tempted to say forgetting should be engineered into information architechture like noise in signal processing - epistemic entropy that stops path dependence. So play it straight: what counts against this view? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to awkward. Would perfect archives actually make societies work better? Is there a hidden contradiction in saying we must forget yet remember enough to know what we're forgetting? And be honest - have defenders already qualified this into unfalsifiability, say by claiming true forgetting only happens when moral identity is preserved? Is that insulation a feature or a bug? Give me the arguments and observations that should actually move someone like me, including whether anything like this has arguably already happened with digital memory.
