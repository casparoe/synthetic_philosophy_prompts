I've been thinking on my own about the handicap principle—the evolutionary idea that wasting resources can advertise quality because only the strongest can bear the cost. In biology it is clear enough: a peacock's tail is a burden that proves fitness. But in the way people present ideas, especially in writing and conversation, I keep noticing what looks like the opposite move. The people who seem most authoritative often do the least visible labor: they avoid footnotes, skip technical language, and present complex arguments as if they arrived effortlessly. Meanwhile people who feel uncertain tend to load their work with citations, qualifications, and jargon. This looks like a kind of reverse handicap, where the costly signal is not adding weight but deliberately removing the usual protective apparatus of scholarship.
