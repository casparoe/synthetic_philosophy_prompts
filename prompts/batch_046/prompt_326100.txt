You are a professional philosopher specializing in early modern metaphysics and the philosophy of economic institutions. Before answering, take a moment to brainstorm Berkeley's core immaterialist commitments—particularly his view that the existence of ordinary objects depends on being perceived, his conventionalist account of signs and value, and his denial that there are hidden material substrates behind observable phenomena—and consider how these commitments might be applied, or misapplied, to a modern institutional design.

Consider this institutional practice: A municipal government issues a digital currency called Notes. There are no physical tokens; each Note exists solely as an entry in a distributed public ledger. The ledger is maintained not by a central authority but by a rotating council of citizens who must actively observe (verify and record) each entry. The governing rule is that if a particular Note is not observed by at least three council members within a thirty-day period, the entry is not transferred elsewhere or physically destroyed; instead, it is simply no longer present in the register, and the associated purchasing power is said to have vanished. Advocates claim that economic value is not a hidden substance lying behind the token but consists entirely in the token being recognized as valuable by a community of perceivers. Critics respond that this confuses an epistemological condition on knowing value with an ontological condition on value's existence.

Your task is to analyze what philosophical commitments this practice presupposes. First, reconstruct the implicit theory of money and value at work: does the practice treat money as a substance, a relational sign, or an idea constituted by collective perception? Second, determine whether the practice is genuinely continuous with Berkeley's immaterialist view that being is constituted by being perceived, or whether it secretly relies on a representationalist picture in which the ledger merely tracks a pre-existing value that can be hidden or revealed. Third, examine internal consistency: does the clause that unobserved Notes vanish presuppose that there was something there to vanish—thereby contradicting strict immaterialism—or can it be read consistently? Fourth, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely happens to fit well with, noting any philosophical underdetermination. Finally, identify the single design feature that does the metaphysical work—without which the scheme collapses into a conventional digital currency with no interesting philosophical stakes—and explain what happens if that feature is removed, including how a defender of representationalism about economic value would attack the original design.
