Everyone explaining SSA vs SIA reaches for the urn analogy: one red ball, tons of blue, you draw red, so self-sampling says "wow, probably not that many blues" while self-indication says "my being here is evidence for a bigger urn." Bostrom uses it to license the presumptuous philosopher -- he bets the universe is tiny just because he exists. I want to audit how far this holds if we stop treating observers as discrete balls and start treating them as continuous observer-moments, plus we nest the urn inside a simulation hierachy where some urns don't run at all. Map the parts: urn, ball, draw, philosopher's bet. Then show where the mapping breaks with continuous streams and dead/nested urns. Which disanologies kill the presumptuous conclusion, and which ones are just decoration? Also, does SIA even apply per-moment or per-life here, and if the answer changes, was the analogy carrying the argument all along?
