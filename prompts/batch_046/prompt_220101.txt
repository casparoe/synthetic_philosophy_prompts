Audit the coherence of the following belief-set, which I hold about evolutionary reasoning, computation, and consciousness. I treat Darwinian selection and Turing-complete computation as parallel structural inversions: both generate functional success—adaptation or intelligent-seeming behavior—without representational comprehension inside the system. From this I draw conclusions about consciousness and explanation. Go through the commitments, identify which pairs genuinely conflict and with what severity, distinguish conflicts that only appear on the surface, and say what single revision would restore the most consistency. Do not lecture me on the views themselves; treat the set as given and trace the logical and motivational tensions.

My commitments:
1. Darwinian natural selection is the purest case of competence-without-comprehension: populations solve adaptive problems without representing them.
2. Turing's universal computation extends the same inversion—syntactic manipulation can yield behaviorally intelligent outputs without semantic understanding inside the system.
3. Jointly, these inversions establish that complex functional organization never requires an understanding agent in the causal chain; design and intelligence are emergent patterns, not preconditions.
4. Phenomenal consciousness is an exception: it is not a functional pattern but a form of comprehension itself—to be conscious is to understand-from-the-inside what an experience is like.
5. Therefore, evolutionary or computational explanations of consciousness fail in principle if they treat it as merely another adaptive competence or syntactic achievement.
6. Nevertheless, I maintain a fully naturalistic, evolutionary origin for consciousness: it arose through selection pressures on information integration, even though selection itself does not comprehend its products.
7. The explanatory gap is conceptual rather than ontological: it arises because phenomenal concepts are first-personal and cannot be intersubstituted with third-person physical-functional descriptions.
8. So I deny both dualism and standard functionalism: consciousness is real, evolved, and irreducible to competence-terms, with no contradiction—only a limit on explanatory models.
