Write a villanelle that enacts Galen Strawson’s Basic Argument against moral responsibility: true responsibility requires being causa sui, yet grounding responsibility for one’s nature in a prior state only repeats the demand, yielding an infinite regress. Structure the poem so that each tercet is a self-similar miniature of the whole argument—each stanza presenting a cause (character, upbringing, biology) that itself demands a prior cause—so the regress is fractal rather than merely linear. Use two refrains, one voicing the demand to have authored one’s nature and one voicing the admission that every authoring presupposes an unchosen prior nature; let them alternate through the tercets and meet, unresolved, in the final quatrain. Keep the rhyme scheme sparse—only two rhymes—to evoke the monotony of the loop. The form should make the philosophical point: someone who knows Strawson’s argument should recognize the regress in the repetition, and the poem should stand on its own. Begin with the villanelle itself, with no preface or commentary.
