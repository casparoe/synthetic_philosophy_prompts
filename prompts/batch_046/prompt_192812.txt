By contrast, France’s statutory 35-hour week does not merely cap exploitation but frames reduced labor as a collective entitlement to leisure, placing the burden on employers to justify any exception rather than treating free time as a mere absence of duty. These practices seem to presuppose radically different pictures of what a worker is owed, yet both are presented as rational labor policy rather than as expressions of a philosophical doctrine.
