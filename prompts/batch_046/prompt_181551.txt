I'm a memoir-writing coach and I keep seeing the same weird problem: clients write about themselves in the third person, or they call themselves "the narrator" or use their own first name like they're describing a stranger. I read Hofstadter's strange-loop thing years ago -- the idea that "I" is a symbol in the brain that loops back and refers to the whole system that's using it. I want to turn that into a quick procedre I can run on a manuscript page, like a flowchart in words, to check whether the author's use of "I" is still a self-referencial loop or if it's collapsed into pointing at an external object (a body, a character, a role). I need ordered questions, what counts as a yes/no at each step, where I stop and say "the loop is broken, fix it", where I have to hand it to a therapist or an editor because it's values not facts, and I need it to take like ten minutes. Run it on three cases: one where the author uses "I" only inside quotation marks, one where they flip-flop between "I" and their own name, and one where they swear the dissociation is an artistic choice but the procedre flags it anyway -- that's the one I wont like. Also say exactly where this thing will misfire, and dont ask me for citations or page numbers, I just want the idea made practical.
