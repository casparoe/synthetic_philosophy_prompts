so my grandma went into hospice about three weeks ago and i've been stuck on this weird moral math problem that i can't shake. like, can any amount of happiness actually balance out someone being in real agony, or is there a point where suffering just dominates everything else?

here are my gut verdicts, and some of them definitely don't fit together.

case one: one person gets 90 years of near-perfect bliss, but only if another person suffers the worst torture imaginable for one hour. i think that's not okay. the bliss doesn't 'buy' the torture, no matter how long it lasts.

case two: same bliss life, but the trade-off is that one other person lives those same 90 years with a constant mild migraine, maybe a 3/10. now i think... actually, maybe that is okay? the good life seems to outweigh there mild bad one?

case three: there's a world with one person suffering at 9/10 for decades, plus ten million people living decent happy lives at 7/10. if we could cure that one person's suffering but it would drop everyone else down to 6/10, i think we should do it. but if instead we just add a million more people at 8/10 without helping the sufferer, i think that doesn't make the world better. so suffering can be traded away sometimes but not diluted?

case four: we're deciding whether to have a kid. there's a 90% chance they live at 8/10, but 10% chance of five years at 9/10 suffering. my gut says don't have them. but if the risk was only five years at 4/10, then yes, have the kid. so there's some line?

case five: a cheap drug turns one person's 9/10 pain into 4/10, but 100 other people drop from 8/10 to 7/10 for life. i keep flip-floping. sometimes the suffering reduction feels like it should win, other times harming 100 people to help one feels wrong, like the scale should just be linear.

is there a single principle behind all this? maybe something about suffering being lexically worse above a threshold, and pleasure/pain not being fully commensurable? if so, which of my verdicts do i have to throw out? and what should i think about the case i'm genuinely stuck on: should we fund that drug if it also means ten extra people are born into lives at 5/10? i honestly don't know where i land on that one.
