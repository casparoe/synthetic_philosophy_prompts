I spent much of yesterday evening in a long and somewhat exhausting argument with a close friend about whether the left-right distinction still tracks anything coherent in contemporary politics, and we ended up testing her intuition by running through a list of historical movements and asking her to place each on one side, on the other, or outside the axis altogether. Her verdicts were strikingly consistent: she placed post-war Scandinavian social democracy on the left; Burkean traditional conservatism on the right; classical anarcho-syndicalism on the left; historical European fascism with its corporatist nationalism on the right; and the American civil-rights movement of the 1960s on the left. She insisted she was not using party labels or economic policy alone, but rather some deeper structural feature, though she struggled to articulate exactly what it was.

Now I want to see whether her pattern genuinely holds together or collapses under pressure. Consider a hypothetical political doctrine—let us call it Cosmopolitan Epistocracy—that advocates the complete abolition of national borders, the replacement of democratic voting by a global algorithmic council that allocates resources according to measured cognitive contribution, a rigid tiering of citizenship based on assessed problem-solving capacity, and an explicit rejection of local cultural privilege or familial partiality in the name of universal long-run human flourishing. The doctrine is aggressively universalist in scope, yet it institutionalizes a steep and explicit cognitive hierarchy and abolishes participatory equality altogether. Given her earlier placements, predict where she would situate Cosmopolitan Epistocracy: on the left, on the right, or outside the axis? Reconstruct at least two distinct rules that could jointly explain her five earlier verdicts—perhaps one tracking equality versus hierarchy, another tracking universalist versus particularist commitment, and possibly a third centered on democratic participation versus technocratic authority. For each rule, state precisely what it predicts about Cosmopolitan Epistocracy. Then give your best guess as to which rule she is actually running, with reasons, and identify the single further hypothetical case that would most cleanly distinguish these rules from one another if her verdict on Cosmopolitan Epistocracy turns out to be ambiguous or inconsistent with all of them.
