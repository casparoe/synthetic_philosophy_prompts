Next thursday I'm presenting at our dept colloqium a paper defending a somewhat unorthodox Sartre reading. I think bad faith in Being and Nothingness isn't mainly self-deception about freedom but an ontological misplacement: treating your social facticity — the waiter uniform, the professor title — as if it had being-in-itself (en-soi) rather than being a for-itself project you could always reinterpret. So bad faith isn't just a moral failure; it's structurally unavoidable in institutional life. The audience is mostly analytic philosophers of action who think Sartre is outdated, two continental grad students who love the waiter passage, and my advisor who thinks I'm overreading it. Give me the objections in the order they'll come, phrased in their vocabularies, with what I should concede, what I should hold, and the weakest point in my own position that I should defend rather than hope they miss. Include the question I'm secretly hoping nobody asks — whether this collapses Sartre into some kind of structural determinism.
