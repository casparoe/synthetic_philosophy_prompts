A Berkeleyan idealist rejects Premise 2, since for Berkeley to be is to be perceived, so hands depend on minds. Moore then notes that this contradicts idealism. As for skeptical scenarios, Moore’s proof targets metaphysical idealism rather than epistemological skepticism like the brain-in-a-vat hypothesis. Finally, Moore’s reliance on common sense does commit him to direct realism. He holds that we perceive physical objects directly, without mental intermediaries. This means he must reject representationalism entirely, because representationalism claims we are directly aware only of mental representations, which for Moore would reintroduce the veil of perception and collapse back into idealism. Therefore, Moore’s common-sense stance requires a naive direct realism that denies any cognitive mediation in perception.”
