Take the Iceland four-day workweek trial that ran from like 2015 to 2019 — the big government-worker one where they cut hours but kept full pay, and productivity mostly stayed flat or went up. Read it through Keynes's view of the good life in that 1930 essay, especially his split between solving the economic problem (absolute needs) and the permanent problem of using leisure wisely. I want a real mapping: what part of the trial is about the economic problem (the pay retention, the output metrics) versus what part touches the permanent problem (what people actually did with thier extra day, whether they reported deeper satisfaction or just less stress). Keynes also thought the love of money was basically a mental illness — does keeping full salary for less work support his view that people should decouple income from labor, or does it show thier self-worth is still tied to the paycheck? Then run it backwards: what does the trial say about Keynes? Does it confirm that cutting hours doesn't hurt productivity, or does it show he underestimated how much people need work for identity and social structure? Be honest about where his framework just doesn't apply — like, does the trial even address whether people became more artistic or philosophical, or did they just run errands?
