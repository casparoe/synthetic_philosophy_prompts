Here are my gut verdicts on some nonidentity-style cases — I'm trying to see if there's one principle behind them or if I'm just being inconsistnt.

1. A city builds a coal plant knowing it'll cause birth defects in people born decades later, but those specific people wouldn't exist without the economic boom. I say they can't claim they were wronged.

2. A couple delays conception by a month for career reasons; the child born (different DNA) has a disability. Same verdict: no wrong done.

3. But if they deliberately select an embryo they know will carry that disability, I say that's wrong — even though the kid wouldn't exist otherwise.

4. A government picks Policy A over B; A produces different people living decent but slightly worse lives than B's people. I feel uneasy but think nobody's wronged.

5. Same choice, but A's people suffer severe deprivation. Now I think there's a wrong, though I can't say who it's wrong to.

Is there a coherent principle — maybe about intent, modal proximity, or whether harm is person-affecting — that gets all five? If not, which verdict should I drop? And what does it predict for this case I'm stuck on: a museum curator picks an exhibit knowing it'll shift local social dynamics and thus determine exactly which couples meet and have kids, some of whom will live hard lives?
