I am preparing a handout for an advanced political philosophy seminar, and I want to move students past the standard introductory triad—Berlin's negative and positive liberty, plus republican freedom as non-domination—without asking them to memorize labels. Could you construct a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts actual positions according to the judgments people make about concrete cases? Begin with the root question: is liberty fundamentally the absence of something (interference, domination, or internal impediment), or the presence of something (autonomy, collective self-rule, capacity)? From there, branch on a second distinction that is often left implicit: whether a constraint counts against liberty only when traceable to an identifiable agent or institution, or also when it arises from dispersed structural patterns—market dynamics, algorithmic ranking, bureaucratic accumulation—that no one intends and that lack a clear dominator. Each internal node should be phrased as a question a student could answer from intuition or reasoning, not by recognizing a philosopher's name; for example, "If a social pattern reliably blocks your options but no individual or group designed it that way, is your liberty reduced?" The leaves should include familiar positions (negative liberty in its classic form; liberal positive liberty; Pettit-style republicanism; and a strict internalist autonomy view), plus at least two less commonly named combinations—such as a "structural negative liberty" that extends non-interference to systemic patterns, or a "diffuse domination" view that treats collective, unintended power relations as instances of domination. Where a logical combination of answers seems to have no defender, note it as "unoccupied" and briefly diagnose whether the gap is due to incoherence, obvious unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Please also flag any pair of named leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate—that is, positions that diverge in theoretical vocabulary but agree on every branch. The aim is a sorting instrument: students should be able to trace their own answers and land on a position they actually hold, rather than picking the label that sounds best. In your response, please clearly distinguish what is broadly settled in the literature—for instance, that republican liberty is conceptually distinct from negative liberty—from what remains genuinely contested, such as whether structural constraints without an identifiable dominator can constitute domination. Do not present any contested framing as the consensus view, and avoid any request for citations, bibliographies, or specific page references, since the handout is meant to stand on its own.
