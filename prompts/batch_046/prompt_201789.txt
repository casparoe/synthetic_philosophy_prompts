Write a villanelle about the Neolithic transition that treats the agricultural revolution not as a simple technological advance but as an accounting revolution: the shift from measuring well-being in leisure and autonomy—sustained in foraging societies by active, practiced egalitarianism and reverse-dominance social technologies—to measuring it in stored surplus, with the skeletal record of declining stature and rising dental pathology serving as structural markers of a new metabolic regime rather than incidental side effects. Use two refrains that must appear exactly as given: one tracking the cyclical, inescapable labor that grain demands ("The grain demands its season; the season demands the grain"), the other tracking the circular leisure that storage economies render invisible ("Leisure was the surplus we refused to store"). In the five tercets, build the forager's world of minimal material wants and deliberately maintained equality, then show how the visibility of accumulated grain made surplus defensible and therefore administrable, introducing hierarchy not as conquest but as the logistics of storage. The volta must occur in the final quatrain, where the two refrains converge and the speaker realizes that progress and affluence are not opposing descriptions but two accounting schemes applied to the same metabolic constraint: the body shrinks not because agriculture fails, but because it succeeds in extracting more labor per calorie than foraging ever required. The poem should be accurate enough that a specialist in prehistoric anthropology recognizes the argument—particularly the claim that hunter-gatherer egalitarianism was an enforced social practice, not a default absence of power—and should stand as a coherent poem for a reader who has never encountered the literature. Do not include citations, bibliographic references, reading lists, or named scholars.
