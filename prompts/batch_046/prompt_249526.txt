I am drafting a novel in which an aging philosophy lecturer, disillusioned with the clarity of ethical vocabulary, sets a final examination for his graduate seminar on Moritz Schlick's essay concerning the meaning of life. I want the question to turn on a tension that Schlick himself only gestures toward: if meaning is reconstructed as playful absorption rather than the achievement of purpose, then the pathology of modern labor is not overwork in the service of vocation but the systematic elimination of the very conditions for non-instrumental engagement. To prevent students from hiding behind summaries of logical empiricism, the prompt should embed an unfamiliar framing—asking them to test Schlick's distinction between Spiel and Arbeit against the phenomenon of karoshi and against Weber's analysis of vocational asceticism.

Please provide, for an upper-level seminar (90 minutes, approximately 2,500 words):

The essay question itself, requiring students to (a) reconstruct Schlick's claim that purpose-directed existence eliminates meaning rather than securing it; (b) articulate the technical sense in which play is self-rewarding without reducing to hedonism or aesthetic consumption; (c) distinguish this position precisely from Weber's secularized vocational calling; and (d) evaluate whether death from overwork undermines Schlick's framework or reveals its hidden dependency on a social distribution of leisure that late capitalism erodes.

A model answer of roughly 700 words demonstrating all four moves with conceptual precision, avoiding biographical summary or contextual remarks about the Vienna Circle.

A marking scheme separating an A response (all four moves present, with move d recognizing that Schlick's argument presupposes accessible conditions of leisure) from a B response (three moves correct but either conflates Spiel with generic enjoyment or fails to engage Weber's sociology) and a C response (describes Schlick's biography or the logical empiricist program without addressing the argument).

Finally, please indicate what would cause you to revise the adequacy of this model answer or the rubric—specifically, which interpretive correction regarding Schlick's concept of play, or which counter-argument linking labor conditions to the possibility of meaning, would lead you to change your assessment, and where the reasoning is most vulnerable to collapse.
