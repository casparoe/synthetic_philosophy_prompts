Solomonoff induction assigns prior probabilities to hypotheses in proportion to 2 raised to the negative Kolmogorov complexity, thereby grounding Occam's razor in algorithmic information theory rather than treating simplicity as a merely pragmatic bias. Consider a conceptual modification: the universal prior is restricted not merely to computable hypotheses, but to those whose predictions can be verified in time polynomial in the size of the observed data. Under this restriction, some hypotheses with very low Kolmogorov complexity become excluded or down-weighted because applying them requires intractable computation, while slightly more complex hypotheses remain fully weighted because they are efficiently checkable. Explain the conceptual tension this creates. Does the convergence result that justifies Solomonoff induction survive in a form that still supports Occam's razor as a normative principle about the probability of worlds, or does bounding verification complexity reduce simplicity to a contingent heuristic for resource-limited agents? In answering, clearly distinguish the normative claim that simpler descriptions correspond to more probable worlds from the instrumental claim that bounded reasoners should favor tractable predictions, and assess whether the modified prior collapses the two.
