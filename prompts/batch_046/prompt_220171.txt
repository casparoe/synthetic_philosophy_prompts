I am revising the assessment for my graduate seminar on eighteenth-century political economy, and I need a take-home essay examination that moves well beyond the standard textbook summary of Adam Smith’s views on empire and colonial administration. The course has already covered the familiar narrative—his attack on mercantilism, his argument that the monopoly of colonial trade harms both colony and metropole, and his well-known prediction that Britain might be economically healthier without direct dominion over its North American settlements. For this exam, however, I want students to confront a specific tension that introductory treatments usually flatten: Smith simultaneously praises the institutional simplicity of settler societies as approximating a natural system of liberty, yet he insists that maintaining territorial sovereignty over them is structurally irrational and constitutionally corrupting for the metropole. To make the assessment genuinely distinctive, please frame the essay question around what I will call an imperial opportunity-cost calculus—the idea that every unit of capital and labor diverted to distant garrisons, naval supply lines, and colonial administration represents not merely a fiscal expenditure but a misallocation that degrades the domestic division of labor and distorts the manufacturing base the empire claims to protect.

Please design a 48-hour take-home essay question suitable for advanced undergraduates or first-year graduate students, with an expected response of roughly 1,500 words. The question should require students to apply this calculus specifically to Smith’s contrasting treatments of the North American colonies and the East India Company’s territorial acquisitions in India. In particular, it should compel them to explain why Smith regards the American monopoly trade system as more damaging to long-term British economic health than direct colonial taxation ever could be, and why he treats the East India Company’s assumption of sovereign governing powers as a corruption of both commerce and public authority—without allowing them to answer by merely listing these conclusions. Alongside the question, provide a model answer of approximately 800 words that demonstrates the required analytical depth, and supply a marking scheme totaling 25 marks organized around five specific must-hit analytical moves, with the marks allocated to each move and a brief one-line note on the characteristic failure mode for each. The prompt should not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific editions or page numbers. If you draw on Smith’s texts, paraphrase his arguments without placing them in quotation marks.
