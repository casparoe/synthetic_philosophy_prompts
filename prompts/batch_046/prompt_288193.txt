The problem of Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition has been revisited repeatedly in metaphysics and the philosophy of language since Russell's classic criticism, yet the same moves keep reappearing: one side insists that relating a relation to its relata requires a further relation, generating an infinite regress, while the other treats the unity of a complex as primitive or as dissolved into the logical form of the proposition itself. More recently, some metaphysicians have tried to relocate the issue into debates about grounding, suggesting that a proposition's constituents are unified not by an added relation but by a non-relational determination relation that is not itself part of the content; others reply that this merely renames Bradley's 'relation of relations' without resolving the regress.

My working hypothesis is that the stall is not primarily about ontology but about mismatched questions: one group is asking what makes a proposition true or representational, another is asking what makes it one thing rather than a list, and both speak of 'unity' without distinguishing these tasks. Is that right, or is there a genuine substantive disagreement about the reality of relations or the structure of propositions hiding beneath?
