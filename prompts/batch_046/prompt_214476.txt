Construct a realistic, single-paragraph thought experiment set in an ordinary institutional environment—such as a clinical psychology lab or an agricultural research station—in which falsificationism classifies a research practice as genuinely scientific, while Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes classifies that same practice as pseudoscientific. The divergence must not come from an obviously ad hoc or degenerating protective belt; instead, the auxiliary hypotheses that shield the core theory from refutation must be structurally necessary to the practice's design, so that removing them would destroy the research program rather than merely weaken it. Present the scenario first, then provide the analysis: state the central predictive claim, identify the method by which it is tested, specify the relevant failure conditions or counterfactual outcomes that each theory treats as decisive, and show precisely why falsificationism delivers one verdict and Lakatos the other. Identify the single structural feature that does the work, explain what happens to the case if that feature is removed, and describe how a defender of Lakatos could attack the scenario by challenging the characterization of the auxiliary hypotheses. Throughout, clearly separate what is settled in the philosophy-of-science literature on demarcation from what is genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any disputed reading of Popper or Lakatos as the consensus position.
