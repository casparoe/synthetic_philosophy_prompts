I've been arguing with my flatmate about evidential cooperation in large worlds — you know, that idea where if there's loads of copies of you scattered across the multiverse, your choice gives you evidence about what they'll do. Anyway, I've got these verdicts on different setups and I just can't square them.

First, the classic scenario: you're instantiated on identical hardware in a million seperate universes, all deciding whether to chip in for some cosmic public good. I think you should contribute, because deciding to do so is strong evidence the others will too. That feels right. Then there's you versus a parallel version who went to a different school and has a totally different moral framework. I don't think you should adjust your behaviour for them — there's no evidential link, just coincidence. Now here's where it gets messy. Imagine fifty people who all took the same philosophy module and now reason about charitable giving in basically the same way. I think they should cooperate with each other, but only inside that cluster. Fine. But then: those fifty people all signed an explicit contract beforehand saying 'if you're in this group, donate regardless of reasoning'. Suddenly I feel like evidential cooperation is the wrong framing — they should just honour the contract, not treat each other as evidential signals. Yet isn't that structurally similar to the million-copies case? I can't see why my intuition flips.

There's also two AI systems running the same base algorithm, but one's memory was wiped with random noise so its reasoning path is corrupted. Even though the underlying architecture is identical, I think they shouldn't treat each other as partners. So it's not just about the architecture being the same — it's about the actual reasoning path being intact? And one more: suppose there are loads of copies, but each knows its own indexical location (like 'I'm copy #492') and they all use that indexical info differently. I feel like cooperation should weaken as the indexical knowledge diverges, even if the reasoning is otherwise the same. So what's the principle? Is it about reasoning-path similarity, or about the absence of explicit coordination, or something about indexical transparency? I want an account that captures why the million copies cooperate, why the contract group doesn't need evidential reasoning, why corrupted reasoning breaks the link, and why indexical divergence matters. Tell me which of my verdicts I'd have to drop to keep the principle simple. And what does it predict for a case I haven't mentioned — say, only two copies exist, but they both know the other is running a slightly modified version of the same reasoning code? Do they partially cooperate, or not at all?
