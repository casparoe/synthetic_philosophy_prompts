So ive been taking this philosophy of mind course and we got into mind uploading and that old ship of theseus thing applied to neurons. My friend was arguing that if you swap out your brain for silicon chips one at a time, slowly, you stay the same person the whole way — like replacing parts of a car. I said okay, but then what if you do it in chunks? Like replace 40% of your prefrontal cortex on monday, then another 40% on wednesday, leaving you with a half-biological half-chip brain in between. He said thats still gradual replacement, just faster, and as long as you dont lose consciousness youre fine. But then I pushed it further: what if you replace every other neuron, so your brain is literally checkerboarded with silicon and tissue, and then fill in the gaps a week later? At that point youre running on a fragmented substrate, but theres no moment where youre turned off. He got kind of annoyed and said im just playing with edge cases, but I think theres a real distinction here that people gloss over. They use gradual to mean both slow sequential and no total interruption, but those arent the same thing — one is about speed, the other is about functional continuity across gaps.

Is there an established term for this specific ambiguity, where an argument for substrate-independent identity relies on temporal continuity but secretly depends on an unstated threshold for how much simultaneous disruption is allowed? I know its adjacent to the Ship of Theseus and maybe split-brain discussions, but those usually deal with either physical parts or actual divided consciousness, not this weird middle zone where the system is technically continuous but structurally patchy. If theres no specific name, could you coin one and define its boundaries — especially how its different from plain equivocation on gradual and from standard continuity-of-consciousness arguments?
