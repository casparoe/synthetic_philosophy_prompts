I serve on my university's Joint Committee on Mental Health Funding and Research Ethics, and I must circulate a written opinion before Thursday's meeting on a pilot proposal that has become personally urgent since my younger sibling's recovery from addiction led me to doubt the value of "flourishing"-centered therapy metrics. The proposal—called the Tranquility-First Assessment Protocol, submitted jointly by a clinical psychologist and a philosopher of well-being—requests that our counseling center restructure all outcome evaluations so that only reductions in craving and suffering count as success, explicitly removing every metric that tracks positive states such as life satisfaction, creative self-actualization, or eudaimonic growth. Its defenders argue that measuring positive well-being covertly introduces new comparative cravings and that an institutional wellness program should aim solely at tranquility, defined strictly as the absence of craving and suffering, rather than at contentment or flourishing; its critics reply that this abandons students who seek counseling to pursue meaningful projects rather than merely to survive crisis, and that a university has a duty to foster growth, not merely to tranquilize distress.

The committee is divided between members who see the protocol as a philosophically rigorous correction to our current metrics and members who view it as an ethically dangerous contraction of care. I need a formal memo recommending a concrete course of action—approve the pilot with strict conditions, reject it outright, or approve a modified version—with a clear implementation timeline, such as a one-semester trial with mandatory safeguards. The memo must treat the strongest consideration on the losing side with genuine philosophical seriousness rather than dismissing it, and it should be accessible to committee members without philosophical training while engaging precisely with the conceptual distinction between tranquility and positive well-being that drives the dispute. It should not request bibliographies or external citations, but should stand on the reasoning alone.

Please draft this memo in an institutional voice, with a clear bottom-line recommendation and the reasoning supporting it, including an honest acknowledgment of what is sacrificed under that recommendation and how the proposed timeline addresses the risks raised by both sides.
