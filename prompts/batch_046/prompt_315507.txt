I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy. I sit on my firm's Automation and Workforce Impact Review Board, which must approve or reject proposals for deploying AI tools that significantly alter engineering labor. We have before us a proposal to roll out an autonomous pipeline that automates not only code generation but also the documentation of architectural rationale and the heuristic debugging patterns currently performed by senior engineers. The vendor and internal champions claim this will reduce required engineering hours by roughly twenty percent; management intends to approve the tool but maintain current product-release velocity, effectively absorbing the time savings as increased output rather than reduced working time. I recently encountered Marx's argument that machinery embodies past labor in a form that can dominate present living labor, and particularly his speculation that once social knowledge itself becomes a direct productive force embedded in automated systems, the appropriate social measure of wealth should shift from labor time to disposable free time rather than to greater surplus extraction. I want the board's memo to treat this framing seriously: it should recommend whether we approve the proposal outright, reject it, or approve it only if management commits to a concrete structural condition—such as a binding reduction in sprint commitments or a four-day-week pilot—rather than merely promising efficiency gains. The memo should be written as a formal institutional opinion I can circulate before Thursday's meeting. It needs a clear, actionable recommendation with a specific time frame or condition, reasoning that the engineering leadership can actually respond to rather than abstract metaphysics, and an honest, non-dismissive account of the strongest argument on the losing side—likely that treating this normative claim as a binding corporate constraint is an inappropriate overreach of an ethics board into business strategy, and that voluntary efficiency gains should be left to market dynamics. Please avoid any request for citations, bibliographies, or further reading; I need a standalone document. Write in a polished, formal register appropriate to an institutional review board.
