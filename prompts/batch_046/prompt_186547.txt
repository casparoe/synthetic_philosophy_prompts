Hey, I'm trying to work out an argument I cooked up about platonism and now I'm stuck in a loop. Here it is, step by step:

(1) If platonism is right, numbers exist outside space and time and don't causally interact with brains or anything else.
(2) But knowledge still needs some tracking relation — if the object were different or absent, the belief should shift accordingly.
(3) For abstract objects there's no counterfactual dependance, since they can't be absent or otherwise.
(4) So tracking fails for numbers specifically.
(5) Without tracking or causation we don't have real knowlege.
(6) So platonists can't actually know basic arithmetic.
(7) But they clearly claim to know that 2+2=4, and they build theories around it.
(8) That makes platonism self-defeating: it says we study things we can't possibly know about.
(9) Here's the part that feels like it proves too much. The same tracking failure hits necessary physical laws, logical tautologies, and even past events, since none of those can be counterfactualy different now.
(10) So if I reject (5) just to save platonism, I'm suddenly allowing knowlege of all kinds of causally inert stuff I was trying to keep out.
(11) If instead I keep (5) and ditch (1), I save consistency but lose the idea that math describes an independant reality.
(12) Where's the slip? Is it in (2) — maybe tracking doesn't apply to abstracta the same way? Or is there an equivocation between causal tracking in (1) and counterfactual tracking in (3)? I'd rather keep (1) and (7) if possible, but I want to understand the price.
