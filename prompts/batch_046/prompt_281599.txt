I am an undergraduate in a philosophy of social science seminar, and next week I am defending a paper that rehabilitates a restricted version of Max Weber’s Protestant ethic thesis while addressing the classic problem of explaining social outcomes with values. My core claim is that wertrational religious commitments do not produce capitalist practices as efficient psychological causes; instead, they alter local institutional payoff structures—by stigmatizing conspicuous consumption and legitimizing reinvestment—thereby functioning as selection filters that determine which economic behaviors survive competitive institutional selection. I invoke Weber’s distinction between wertrational and zweckrational action, but I combine it with a conceptual distinction drawn from philosophy of science between causal efficacy and causal relevance: values are causally relevant as stability conditions for a selection environment, not as direct drivers of individual choice. Please prepare me for the Q&A. The audience is the seminar group—analytic philosophers familiar with causal inference and mechanism literature, economic historians skeptical of unfalsifiable cultural narratives, and one faculty member who studies sociological mechanisms. They are firmly anti-teleological, alert to chronology and geographic counterexamples, and will recognize the thesis mainly through its standard empirical critiques. Order the objections by how likely and how damaging they are, phrase each in the vocabulary this audience would actually use, indicate for each whether I should concede part of it up front, provide a concise reply suited to a ten-minute defense, flag where each reply is weakest, and identify the single reply that must land for the proposal to survive. Include the objection I am secretly hoping nobody asks: that treating values as selection-filter conditions is incoherent because those values are themselves socially distributed, which seems to presuppose the very cultural explanation I am trying to avoid. If my framing illegitimately conflates Weber’s own conceptual apparatus with contemporary selection-language in a way that makes this defense task conceptually incoherent, or if I have misjudged the audience’s commitments, say so plainly rather than complying politely. Do not supply a bibliography, reading list, or requested citations; I need arguments I can deploy without reference materials.
