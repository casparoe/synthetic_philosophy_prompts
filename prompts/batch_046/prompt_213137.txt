I'm trying to isolate a recurring argumentative posture in debates over epiphenomenalism, specifically in the neighborhood of what is usually called the paradox of phenomenal judgment. Here is the pattern I keep noticing, described without the usual technical shorthand. A defender of the view that phenomenal properties are causally inert responds to the standard objection — that if consciousness does no causal work, then our judgments about consciousness are not caused by consciousness and therefore seem unreliable — by insisting that this is precisely correct: our introspective reports and verbal utterances are produced entirely by underlying neural mechanisms, and this causal disconnection in no way undermines the reality of the phenomenal properties themselves. That is a familiar move. But then, within the same discussion, the very same defender appeals to our direct, first-person access to phenomenal qualities as a reason those qualities resist reduction to physical descriptions. The access is treated as epistemically authoritative — as a kind of acquaintance that physical concepts lack — even though, according to the epiphenomenalist's own account, that acquaintance plays no causal role in producing the judgment that expresses it.

I am not asking for a survey of responses to the paradox, and I am not asking for bibliographic pointers. I want to know whether there is an established concept or label for this specific configuration: the simultaneous insistence that phenomenal judgments are causally disconnected from their objects (and therefore cannot be used to argue for causal efficacy) together with the claim that those same judgments enjoy a privileged epistemic status that seems to depend on being intimately, non-derivatively connected to their objects. If there is no precise term, I want the phenomenon carved out — what exactly distinguishes it from plain inconsistency, from strategic context-shifting between defensive and offensive postures, and from the broader tension between first-person authority and the causal closure of the physical — and, if appropriate, a coined label with explicit boundary conditions that separate it from nearby dialectical patterns such as special pleading or mere equivocation on "access." What are its edges, and what would have to change in the argument for the pattern to dissolve?
