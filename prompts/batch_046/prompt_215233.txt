Write a dialogue between a modal rationalist and a modal deflationist debating whether conceivability is a trustworthy guide to metaphysical possibility. Have them examine a specific case: a universe that is physically identical to ours in every empirically detectable respect, yet entirely lacks consciousness—not because of any physical absence, but because a necessary law linking physical organization to phenomenal properties holds in this world, a law that is genuinely epistemically opaque and can be neither confirmed nor denied by finite conceiving minds. The rationalist should argue that our ability to clearly conceive of such a world, with no hidden contradiction we can detect, provides defeasible reason for its metaphysical possibility. The deflationist should counter that this apparent conceivability only demonstrates the boundaries of our cognitive architecture, and that an undiscovered necessary connection or hidden incoherence could still render the scenario impossible, leaving conceivability with no direct modal authority. Both speakers should be equally rigorous, respond to the specific arguments just made rather than talking past each other, and maintain a genuine back-and-forth. Please separate what is settled from what remains genuinely contested among those familiar with modal epistemology, and do not present a disputed view as though it were the consensus.
