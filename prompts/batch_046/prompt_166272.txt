I've been arguing with a colleague about whether Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor should be read as making an empirical claim about human weakness or a structural claim about the architecture of happiness. To turn this into a workable dissertation prospectus—about eight pages, one semester, for a philosophy of religion fellowship—I want to propose a conceptual project rather than a literary one. The framing I need sharpened: treat the Inquisitor's three mechanisms—miracle as the outsourcing of causal explanation to an opaque power, mystery as the outsourcing of moral judgment to an inscrutable hierarchy, and authority as the outsourcing of practical decision to a unified will—not as religious temptations that corrupt an originally free will, but as three distinct modes of delegated cognition. On this reconstruction, freedom is not a positive capacity but a negative state defined by the refusal to delegate epistemic or practical authority; happiness, by contrast, requires at least one form of such delegation. The precise question is whether freedom and happiness are therefore structurally incompatible in the way the Inquisitor claims, or whether the argument collapses once we distinguish which mode of delegation is at stake.

The proposal should state exactly why standard readings—psychological versus theological—leave this open: they treat the Inquisitor's triad as a single package and therefore miss that one might reject delegation in the causal domain while accepting it in the practical domain, which would split the compatibility question into separate sub-questions. The method should be conceptual analysis of each delegation-mode, comparing the reconstructed Inquisitor with contemporary accounts of cognitive offloading but without requiring an empirical literature review or citation-heavy comparison; the deliverable should be either a taxonomy showing that freedom and happiness are compatible in some delegation-modes but not others, or a demonstration that the Inquisitor's argument depends on a hidden premise about the value of self-authorship that he never defends. Please include an honest assessment of what could make the project fail—such as finding that the three modes cannot be cleanly separated—or too large for one semester, and specify what result would count as success versus a dead end.
