Having spent three decades as an experimental physicist before turning, in retirement, to the conceptual foundations of social science and moral philosophy, I have become preoccupied with a question that sits at the intersection of empirical history and normative taxonomy: is the world becoming less violent? I do not want a bibliography or a survey of the literature—without web access one could only fabricate citations—but rather a rigorous conceptual essay that weighs the central arguments and reaches a justified conclusion. In particular, I want you to examine whether there is even a fact of the matter underlying this question, given that "violence" may be an essentially contested concept whose boundaries depend on prior normative commitments about agency, harm, and the proper unit of moral accounting.

Consider the standard narrative of a long-run decline in per-capita homicide and interstate warfare, and ask whether this should be treated as decisive evidence of moral progress. Then weigh it against the possibility that absolute counts tell a different story, especially if we extend the category of violence beyond intentional human-on-human acts to include structural or systemic harm. Here I want you to inject and examine a more specific tension: whether the exponential growth in the number of sentient beings subjected to industrial-scale harm—where intentionality is distributed across institutional structures rather than located in individual agents—should count in the same metric as historical warfare. Does including such cases render the concept of violence so heterogeneous that per-capita and absolute measures become incommensurable? You should also address whether normalization by human population alone is defensible, or whether a genuinely neutral metric would require weighting by some measure of sentience, thereby introducing discontinuities that make aggregate historical trends conceptually incoherent rather than merely ambiguous. Do not conclude with a non-committal balance: defend a specific position—for example, that there is a restricted fact of the matter confined to intentional interpersonal violence, or that the concept fragments into irreducible pluralism once we abandon anthropocentric framing. I am especially interested in whether your verdict depends on adopting a particular metaphysics of harm, or whether it can be sustained on purely conceptual and taxonomic grounds.
