Consider the following argument, drawn from the writings of Arthur Schopenhauer: all satisfaction is merely the temporary cessation of a prior lack or pain, so pleasure is essentially negative—like silence that exists only where noise has stopped—whereas suffering is positively real and constant, because desire and its inevitable frustration constitute the very structure of the will. He concludes that a life entirely free of suffering is not merely uncommon but conceptually impossible, since without the pressure of unsatisfied want there would be no motivation and therefore no life at all.

I am trying to understand what this metaphysical picture implies for a precise ethical distinction within suffering-focused ethics. Specifically, does Schopenhauer's claim that pleasure is ontologically parasitic on suffering entail that suffering enjoys lexical priority over pleasure in any normative calculus—so that no quantity of pleasure can justify any unit of suffering—or does it only support an asymmetry in which suffering is a positive evil and pleasure merely the absence of evil? Furthermore, if one evaluates outcomes within a risk-weighted expected-utility framework—where probabilities weight the disvalue of suffering—does Schopenhauer's ontology imply that suffering should be weighted exponentially rather than linearly, because suffering is the substantive reality of experience while pleasure is only its intermittent negation? Or can his metaphysics be reconciled with a gradated negative-utilitarian calculus that permits trade-offs?

Please help me understand the passage's bearing on these two questions: first, whether it forces a lexical or only a gradated interpretation of suffering's normative dominance; second, whether the ontological status Schopenhauer assigns to pleasure and suffering dictates a particular form—perhaps non-linear or threshold-based—of risk-weighted evaluation. I am especially unsure whether his view that satisfaction is always the ending of a pain commits him to the position that bringing into existence a being with a net-positive life, in which pleasures marginally outweigh sufferings, is still a moral harm, on the grounds that the sufferings are real positive entities while the pleasures are only borrowed absences.
