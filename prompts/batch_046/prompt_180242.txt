Construct a diagnostic decision tree for positions in the structural-realism debate, intended for students who accept that mature scientific theories are approximately true but that past theories often postulated entities that do not exist. The first branch should ask whether the structures preserved across theory change carry an inherent physical interpretation or are purely formal and mathematical. The second should ask whether those structures are relations that require relata, even if unknown, or can be genuinely free-standing relations in the radical ontic sense. The third should ask what continuity relation is required between successive theories: exact isomorphism of models, approximate isomorphism, or only a weaker categorical equivalence between model categories. The fourth should ask whether the view applies to all scientific domains or only to fundamental physics. Each leaf should name a position—epistemic structural realism, ontic structural realism, semi-realism, structural empiricism, or unoccupied—and state in one sentence its core claim together with its most awkward commitment. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, and identify any empty branch combinations, diagnosing whether each is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Phrase every node as a concrete question about scientific change—referring, for example, to the Fresnel-Maxwell transition or to quantum-classical correspondence—so that a respondent can answer without already knowing the label of the view it leads to. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
