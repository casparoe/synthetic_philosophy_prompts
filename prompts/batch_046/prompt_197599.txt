My working thesis is that the Landauer-based reply—according to which the demon must eventually erase its recorded measurements, dissipating at least kT ln 2 per bit and thereby preserving the second law—quietly presupposes a realist ontology of information rather than a purely relational one. Here is the outline of my argument as it stands.

First, the demon acquires information about molecular velocities and stores it in a physical memory. Second, to continue operating, that memory must be cleared, and this clearing is identified with a thermodynamically irreversible operation on the physical substrate. Third, the energy cost is derived from the reduction in the number of physical microstates corresponding to the erased bit. But if information is understood relationally—as holding only between a physical system and an interpreting agent, with no intrinsic informational properties in the substrate itself—then the change in the substrate during erasure is just one physical transformation among others. The link between that specific transformation and an increase in thermodynamic entropy seems to depend on treating the bit-value as an intrinsic feature of the substrate that constrains the available microstates. Without that realist commitment, it is unclear why the relational change in an agent's representational state should carry a necessary physical cost.
