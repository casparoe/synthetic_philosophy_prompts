As a retired microbiologist who has recently begun studying philosophy of history and philosophy of science, I find myself caught between two unsatisfactory extremes: deterministic narratives that treat pathogenic outbreaks, climatic shifts, and volcanic winters as mere background conditions to human affairs, and anthropocentric accounts that reserve all causal and explanatory agency for intentional human actors. I want a rigorous specification of what a philosophically adequate theory must deliver if it is to treat entities such as germs, climate regimes, or volcanic aerosols as genuine 'historical actors'—that is, as forces that shape historical trajectories without being reducible to either passive conditions or human proxies. Please construct the full list of desiderata such a theory must satisfy.

For each requirement, state the phenomenon it must explain—such as how a microbial epidemic can restructure labor economies without possessing intentions, or how a stratospheric aerosol veil can alter agricultural and demographic patterns without being an agent in the human sense—the conceptual distinctions it must preserve (causal contribution versus structural constraint versus agential efficacy; multi-causality versus environmental determinism; explanatory autonomy versus reduction to human response), the results from historical epidemiology, paleoclimatology, and volcanology it cannot contradict, and the conceptual pitfalls—particularly environmental determinism and its mirror-image, anthropocentric exceptionalism—it must avoid. Indicate which desiderata are non-negotiable and which represent camp-specific preferences (materialist, constructivist, intentionalist, etc.); identify any subset that is jointly unsatisfiable, for instance if granting genuine explanatory autonomy to non-human forces necessarily collapses the boundary between intentional action and physical causation; and specify which single requirement does the most damage to the most popular existing theories. Finally, propose one further hypothetical case—perhaps involving a synthetic pathogen with designed properties or a deliberate geoengineering intervention—that would best distinguish two competing frameworks that otherwise satisfy the list equally well. This is not a request for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I seek the evaluative framework itself, presented with the formality appropriate to a philosophy-of-science seminar, against which I can measure the accounts I am encountering.
