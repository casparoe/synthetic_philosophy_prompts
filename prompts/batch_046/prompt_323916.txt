As a science writer covering the philosophy of physics, I've been trying to understand the precise threat Bell's theorem poses to local realism. The reasoning I keep encountering is that, once we set aside superdeterminism and assume experimenters can freely choose measurement settings, reproducing quantum correlations requires actual faster-than-light signaling between distant detectors—because without such direct causal links, local hidden variables could not violate the CHSH bound. This seems to put quantum mechanics in direct tension with special relativity unless we abandon realism entirely. Am I correct to suspect that a subtle distinction is being missed here between non-local correlation and superluminal communication, or does the theorem genuinely force that dilemma?
