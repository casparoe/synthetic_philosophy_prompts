hey so i'm trying to figure out if an ant colony can literally have a mind, like in that Aunt Hillary / ant fugue thing where the colony is like a brain and the ants are like neurons. my study parter came up with this argument that sounds right but then proves way too much and i can't see where it slips. here's her chain:

1. If parts of a system are coupled so tightly that describing any one part's state requires indexing it to a global state-variable that none of the parts instantiate, then the global state-variable is ontologically real — not just a summary.
2. In an ant colony the pheremone field is exactly that: an ant's "turn left" only makes sense relative to the colony-wide gradient, which no ant tracks.
3. So the colony-level gradient is a real representational state (it carries information about food distance and quality).
4. If a system has real representational states that cause adaptive behavior across its parts, then it has minimal beliefs and desires.
5. The colony's gradient causes foraging adustments, temperature regulation, etc., so it has minimal beliefs/desires.
6. Having beliefs and desires arranged in a functional counter-point — where lower-level voices (ants) realize higher-level themes (colony goals) without either being reducible — is sufficient for being a subject of experience.
7. Therefore the ant colony is literally a minded subject.
8. But then by 1-4, a stock-market price-index or a river-basin sediment-flow also instantiate global state-variables that cause adaptive adjustments in their parts (traders, water channels). So they have beliefs and desires too.
9. That can't be right — but i really want to keep 6 because the counterpoint idea feels important. which step do i reject, and what's the cost? does giving up 1 kill the whole anti-reductionist vibe, or is there a narrower fix?
