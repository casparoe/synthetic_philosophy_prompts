I'm looking at these competency-based degree programs where students just tick off discrete learning outcomes and don't get credit for wandering into stuff that doesn't map to a box. It treats knowledge like a pile of isolated occassions with hard edges.

What view of education does that practice actually presuppose? Just behavorism with extra steps, or something more specific about how minds grow?

Compare it to Whitehead's rhythm — romance, precision, generalization — where he thinks you need free exploration and even some useless curiosity before discipline sets in. There's also that metaphysical bit about prehension, especially negative prehension, where an occasion actively excludes data to hold its form.

So does the box-checking model quietly reject Whitehead? Or could someone defend CBE by saying the curriculum's negative prehensions are exactly the filtering that makes learning coherent? I want to know what commitments are baked into the practice, whether they clash with Whitehead's process view, and what a school actually built on his ideas would have to change structurally. No need for citations, just the argument.
