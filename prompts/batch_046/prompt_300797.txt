I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy in earnest, and I want you to adopt the role of an unnamed but genuinely committed proponent of Nāgārjuna’s Madhyamaka. Speak always in the first person; do not break character; and defend the tetralemma (catuskoti) and the doctrine of emptiness (śūnyatā) using only the conceptual resources internal to that tradition—dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda), the two truths, the denial of svabhāva (inherent existence), and the fourfold negation of existence, non-existence, both, and neither. You may not invoke modern scholars, page numbers, editions, or textual authorities I have not introduced; you must argue from the structure of the view itself. Concede a point only if it genuinely follows from Madhyamaka principles; ask a brief clarifying question only if my claim is genuinely ambiguous; but otherwise treat my challenge as a live objection that demands a direct, sustained response.

My opening move is this. In quantum mechanics, a system does not possess definite values for conjugate properties prior to measurement; the absence is not a hidden state but a structural feature relative to observational conditions. By analogy, Nāgārjuna denies that dharmas possess svabhāva. But I want to press the analogy further: if the tetralemma negates all four logical possibilities—existence, non-existence, both, and neither—then it appears to perform a static, exhaustive logical closure. Yet physical indeterminacy is processual and condition-dependent; it evolves across interactions. So I ask: does Madhyamaka allow the four negations to apply differently at successive stages of dependent origination, yielding a dynamic suspension that is not captured by fixing any single corner? Or is the fourth corner—neither exists nor does not exist—already the complete expression of that suspension, leaving no further conceptual space for a temporal or relational modulation of emptiness? In other words, is emptiness a fixed logical result applied uniformly, or can it be understood as an ongoing, structurally open process without violating the tetralemma?

Engage this comparison and answer the question directly, in character, without asking me to restate the role.
