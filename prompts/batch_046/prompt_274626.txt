I'm working through Schopenhauer's account of music in The World as Will and Representation, particularly his thesis that music is not a representation of an Idea but a direct objectification of the Will itself, with bass lines corresponding to inorganic nature, harmony and inner voices to plant and animal life, and melody to human consciousness and its striving. From this I have built an argument that seems to drive his aesthetics into contradiction, and I want to locate the exact misstep.

(1) For Schopenhauer, the Will is absolutely one and indivisible; the plurality of phenomena and the grades of objectification—mineral, vegetative, animal, human—are merely phenomenal distinctions introduced by the intellect operating under the principle of sufficient reason.

(2) Music, however, is the direct copy of the Will unmediated by Ideas, yet it is structurally composed of simultaneous acoustic layers—bass, harmony, melody—that correspond precisely to those distinct grades.

(3) Because these layers are acoustically separable and can be attended to in isolation (one can listen only to the bass line, or only to the melody), music presents the grades of the Will as genuinely distinct, co-existing entities within a single auditory experience.

(4) But if the Will is directly present in music, and music presents these grades as separate, then direct experience of the Will itself includes separation and plurality—features that belong exclusively to the phenomenal world governed by space, time, and causality.

(5) Therefore either music is not a direct objectification of the Will (which abandons Schopenhauer's central claim about music's metaphysical privilege), or the principle of sufficient reason applies to the thing-in-itself (which collapses his transcendental idealism).

(6) Since Schopenhauer treats both (1) and the direct-objectification thesis as non-negotiable, the reasoning appears to show that his musical metaphysics is internally unstable.

Which premise or inference should I reject? I am inclined to preserve (1) and (2), since Schopenhauer's monism and his specific bass-harmony-melody correspondence are well established in his text, and I would rather not concede that music is merely representational. So I need to know whether the error lies in (3)—treating acoustic separability as ontological separation—or elsewhere, and what Schopenhauer must pay to correct it: perhaps admitting that direct Will-objectification can occur within the form of inner sense (time) and harmonic simultaneity without importing the full principle of sufficient reason, or perhaps conceding that melody, harmony, and bass are not separate grades at all but only analytical abstractions from an indivisible acoustic whole. Explain exactly where the argument overreaches and price the alternatives.
