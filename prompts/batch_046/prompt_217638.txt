I've been following the arguments about whether universities and public platforms should exclude speakers or groups that advocate for the exclusion of others, and I'm struck by how the conversation never seems to advance despite everyone claiming to care about tolerance. One side insists that unlimited tolerance must contain its own exception — that societies which tolerate intolerant movements will eventually be destroyed by them — while the other side insists that any institutional exclusion is already a betrayal of tolerance and that the proper remedy is always more speech, never less access. Both sides invoke what I understand to be called the paradox of tolerance, yet neither seems able to persuade the other, and the debate has the feeling of people talking past each other rather than disagreeing about a shared problem. I have started to think that the stall might come from a confusion between two different registers: tolerance as a personal moral virtue, which is about an individual's willingness to endure disagreement, and tolerance as an institutional design principle, which is about the rules governing shared spaces. There may also be a hidden disagreement about the nature of the harm involved — whether allowing a speaker to appear causes harm in a direct causal chain, or whether it causes a different kind of damage by altering what the institution itself represents and who can belong there.

Could you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is? Specifically, is it a verbal disagreement about the meaning of tolerance, a substantive disagreement about hidden empirical claims, a conflict between bedrock intuitions that neither side can argue for, or a case where both sides are answering different questions under the same word? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a distinction, a thought experiment, a change of framing — or should we conclude that no progress is possible here?
