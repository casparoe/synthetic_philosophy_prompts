I'm a compliance reviewer for a research consortium that funds autonomous systems, and lately every grant comes with a goverance diagram that looks like a bowl of spaghetti. I read about tangled hierarchies — like Escher's Drawing Hands where each hand draws the other — and this notion of an inviolate level, the one layer that's supposed to stay outside the loop and ground everything else. I want a step-by-step procedure my panel can run in about thirty minutes to decide whether a proposed oversight structure contains a real strange loop, or if it's just complicated but linear. For each step, say exactly what question to ask, what answer kills the proposal, what answer makes it pass, and where the procedure has to stop and hand the call to a person because it's a values judgment not a structural fact. Then run it on two hypothetical proposals: Proposal Alpha sets up Board A overseeing Board B overseeing Board A, with all three sharing a single veto clause; Proposal Beta installs a 'ground charter committee' that claims to be inviolate, but the charter's own amendment rules are voted on by the board the charter governs. Make sure one of these comes out in a way I'll probably hate — say, technically passing even though it smells wrong. Also flag the two or three places where the procedure is guaranteed to misfire. At the very end, put one line that starts with 'Verdict: ' and says whether this thing is actually deployable or just shuffles the hard call around.
