I'm teaching an interdisciplinary seminar on collective action and institutional failure, and my students keep reducing William Golding's Lord of the Flies to a simple allegory of Hardin's tragedy of the commons—overgrazing on a shared island—while ignoring the distinct problems of providing collective goods. I'd like to complicate that reading by bringing in the standard four-fold economic typology—private goods, club goods, common-pool resources, and pure public goods—and asking whether Golding's boys collapse precisely because they misclassify what kind of goods they are governing.

Read the novel through this framework. Treat the island's material resources (pigs, fruit, water) as rivalrous common-pool resources; the signal fire and shelter rules as pure public goods that are non-rivalrous but costly to maintain; and the conch shell not merely as a symbol of democracy but as a club good—excludable authority that is non-rivalrous in use. Does Ralph implicitly govern for public-good provision, while Jack treats the conch and the rules as open-access commons to be seized? Is the boys' social contract destroyed not mainly by scarcity, but by a category error—applying extraction logic appropriate for rivalrous resources to the non-rivalrous institutions needed to coordinate them?

Please take both the literary detail and the economic distinctions seriously: don't reduce Ralph or Jack to rational agents, but show where specific moments—Ralph's fading authority, Simon's solitary insight, the dissolution of the assembly, the final hunt—either confirm or resist the framing. In particular, address whether Golding suggests that small-scale societies can sustain self-governance without external enforcement only when they correctly identify whether a shared asset requires conservation, contribution, or exclusion rules. End by saying what the novel adds to the philosophical debate that an abstract model of commons failure misses, and where the literary representation forces the theory to account for something it otherwise ignores.
