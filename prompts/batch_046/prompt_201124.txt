There's Whig histroy — writing the past like it's all just marching toward whatever we think now — and I see a mirror image in science labs that doesn't have a proper name. Let's call it 'outcome-lock bias': once a protocol works, people retell the failed trials as if they were logically necessary stepping stones, and they quietly erase the alternative mechanisms that could have panned out but happened to loose the race. So compare Whig history proper (judging past thinkers by our present answers) with outcome-lock bias in retrospective science writing. Are they fueled by the same cognitive slip — maybe a human need for neat linear stories — or does the lab version carry an extra weight because failed experiments leave physical traces that have to be accounted for in publications? Also, which one is harder to correct once it gets locked into a textbook? I want the comparison to dig into the actual mechanics, not just say 'both are bad histroy'.
