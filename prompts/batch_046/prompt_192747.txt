what would actually count against this? i want more than the usual "but rational agents should choose anyway" reply. are there formal results—maybe in decision theory or computation—that show noise-based tie-breaking is unstable or irrational? do psychological studies of real indecission show people stuck with genuinely equal options, or is it always hidden preference conflicts? and if my view just says "it's all physics," does that make it unfalsifiable, or does it collapse into a trivial physicalism that dissolves every philosphy problem? rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about which ones a defender should concede versus dismiss. also, does this view risk being a feature—like explaining why algorithms never truly hang on equal inputs—rather than a bug?
