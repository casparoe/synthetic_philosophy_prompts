Rank the four positions below on the coherence of actual infinity, as illustrated by Hilbert’s hotel, ordering them from most defensible to least defensible and assigning each a score out of ten. For each position, provide a concise justification of no more than 150 words.

The four positions are:

1. The standard Cantorian stance: the hotel demonstrates that an actually infinite set can be placed in one-to-one correspondence with a proper subset, which is a feature rather than a contradiction, and thereby confirms the mathematical legitimacy of completed infinities.

2. The classical finitist stance: all infinity is potential; the hotel illicitly reifies an unending process into a completed totality, and the apparent paradox arises only from treating “all rooms” as if it named a definite, fully realised collection.

3. The narrative-framing objection: the hotel’s persuasive force depends on smuggling set-existence assumptions into an apparently innocent story about rooms and guests, so that what looks like an intuitive proof is actually circular reasoning disguised by concrete imagery.

4. The indexical-shift objection: “all rooms are full” functions like a context-sensitive indexical rather than denoting a completed totality; once we analyse the quantifier domain as shifting with each new assignment rather than fixed in advance, the paradox dissolves without either accepting or rejecting actual infinity outright.

In your response, explain briefly why you placed each position where you did, noting which objections you regard as carrying genuine philosophical weight and which depend on questionable linguistic or metaphysical presuppositions.
