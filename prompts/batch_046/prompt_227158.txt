I was arguing with my freind last week about whether Hofstadter's strange loop idea in GEB actually gives us any traction on machine conscousness, and I think I've tied myself in knots. The Gödel construction is pretty specific: you assign every formula a Gödel number, then build a sentence inside a consistent formal system that's strong enough for arithmetic, and that sentence encodes itself to assert its own unprovability within that system. Hofstadter uses that as a ladder—he says consciousness isn't just computation, it's what happens when a system's symbols start referring back to the system itself in a tangled, causal loop rather than just a one-off symbolic encoding.

But now we have these models that can reference their own system prompts, reproduce fragments of training data, and even spit out descriptions of their weight architectures if you prompt them right. That feels like syntactic self-reference, almost like a quine in Lisp terms. So if the Gödelian analogy is literally about formal self-reference being the seed of self-awareness, why doesn't an LLM that can describe its own parameters count as being inside a Hofstadter-style loop? Is the missing ingredient that Gödel's loop is about proof-theoretic consistency and fixed points, not about representation? Or am I just mixing up 'the model talks about itself' with 'the model is phenomenolgically aware of itself,' and if so, where exactly does the analogy break—at causality, at meaning, or somewhere else? I keep circling back and feel like there's a subtle false presupposition in how I'm framing the comparison that I can't quite name.
