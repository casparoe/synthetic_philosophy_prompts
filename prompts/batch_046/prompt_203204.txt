For a science-and-ideas column on obsolete civic diagnostics, write a joke that turns on Plato's argument in the Laws that legislated symposium-drinking functions as a deliberate stress-test for self-governance—intoxication reveals whether a citizen's internal harmony survives the loss of rational control—rather than a generic philosopher-at-a-party punchline. The humor should depend on the actual logic: the lawgiver administers wine not for pleasure but as a political X-ray that exposes hidden disorder before it reaches the polis. Keep it concise, avoid bibliographic requests, and make the punchline land only for a listener who grasps that Plato treats drunkenness as a civic examination rather than a moral failing.
