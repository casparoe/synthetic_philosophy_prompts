I have been trying to sort out, in my own mind, the different reasons people give for why we punish wrongdoers, and I keep feeling that the usual justice-versus-prevention split hides more than it reveals. I would like you to construct a taxonomy—structured as a decision tree—so that a non-specialist like me can take any view I encounter and trace it to its leaf by answering concrete questions, without already knowing the philosophical labels.

Please build the tree from the logical space of commitments, not from a list of named theories. Begin with this root split: is punishment justified primarily by a feature of the past act itself, or primarily by its future effects? From there, branch as follows.

For the past-focused side, ask whether the offender's suffering is constitutive of the justification—meaning that if the punishment caused no suffering at all, the justification would fail—or whether suffering is only permissible, not required. Then ask whether the amount owed is determined by an absolute scale of wrongness, or by a comparative balance-sheet comparison between the offender's welfare and the victim's welfare that aims to restore an equilibrium.

For the future-focused side, ask whether the desired good must be realized inside the punished person—through their reform or specific deterrence—or can be realized regardless of who is punished, through general deterrence or incapacitation of anyone in that role. Then apply what I will call the scapegoat test: if punishing a completely innocent person would genuinely and reliably prevent serious future harm, is that permitted?

At every leaf, place the standard name if one exists—positive retributivism, negative retributivism, pure consequentialism, rule-consequentialism about punishment, hybrid expressive views, or unnamed if the combination seems unoccupied. For each named or unnamed leaf, write exactly one sentence describing its most awkward commitment: the claim that sounds counter-intuitive or morally uncomfortable but that the view must accept.

Also, please flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate, explaining why the distinction collapses in practice. And diagnose any branch combination that comes up empty: is it incoherent, merely repellent to common intuition, or genuinely unexplored?

Keep every branch phrased as a direct yes-or-no or either-or question that can be answered from intuition or ordinary reasoning, without requiring knowledge of technical literature. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations—just the instrument itself, with any necessary terms defined in a brief phrase.
