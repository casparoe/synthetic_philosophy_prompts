For an advanced seminar in formal decision theory — about 16 students, 90 minutes, all familiar with conditioning and basic game theory but not necessarily with updateless frameworks — design an in-class activity that makes them feel the specific tension in updateless decision theory between treating a correlation as logical dependency versus empirical evidence. Do not use Newcomb’s problem or the prisoner's dilemma; instead, invent a multi-round group scenario in which each student privately commits to a reasoning policy at the start, then receives a message that is physically delivered like new data but is in fact deterministically computed from the policies of other students running the same algorithm. Arrange rules, roles, and a point-based payoff scheme so that students who revise their choice upon receiving the message do better in a local, causal sense but break a global consistency condition that earns a group bonus, while students who stick to their original policy preserve the global bonus but experience immediate local regret. Specify the timeline round by round, the materials needed (cards, envelopes, score sheets), how the facilitator should stage the delivery of the messages to make them feel like empirical updates, and how to assign the group-level reward without revealing the logical structure too early. Include a debrief that maps the moment of receiving the message onto the formal distinction between logical and causal conditioning, asks participants to identify when they treated the message as evidence about the world versus as a computation of their own prior commitment, and connects that identification back to the policy-level choice they made. Be explicit about what will predictably go wrong on the first run — for instance, students conflating the physical act of delivery with causal influence, or treating the group bonus as an afterthought rather than part of the decision problem — and how the facilitator should correct for it without giving away the framing. Finally, include an honest note on which feature of updatelessness — such as logical omniscience, the absence of self-doubt, or the global optimality condition — this physical classroom setup with partial information and social pressure cannot reproduce, and therefore must be supplied directly by the facilitator’s concluding framing.
