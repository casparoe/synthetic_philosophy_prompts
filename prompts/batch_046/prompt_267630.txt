I've been thinking about people with pain asymbolia—those who register injury but report no suffering, as if the alarm bell rings without the dread. From this I've formed the view that pain is bad only through its negative affective quality, not through the raw sensory signal itself. I imagine a treatment that preserves the protective warning but eliminates the emotional hurt, and it seems to me this would be an unqualified good for unavoidable chronic conditions. What would count against this position? I want an honest assessment of its exposure: which observations, arguments, or logical consequences should move me, sorted by how decisive they would be. For instance, does it matter if asymbolic patients still show unconscious behavioral avoidance? If removing the affective component also removes the motivation to protect the body? Or if there is a direct intrinsic badness in the sensation that doesn't depend on emotional response? I also want to know whether I have insulated the view into unfalsifiability by defining all harm as affective, and if so, whether that is a problem for a claim of this kind.
