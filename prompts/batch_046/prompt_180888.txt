I have been discussing moral status with a close friend who volunteers at an animal sanctuary and has spent the last year reading about consciousness and ethics entirely on her own, without any formal philosophy training. Over several evenings we have mapped out her intuitive judgments across a range of cases, and I am now trying to understand what underlying rule she is using before we work through a harder example together.

Here is her running record. Adult mammals such as dogs and pigs clearly possess full moral status in her view, because they suffer and have inner lives we can recognize. Insects like honeybees and cephalopods like octopuses receive a moderate, reduced status: she says they avoid harm and possess nervous systems, yet whatever they experience is likely so different from ours that it warrants only partial consideration. Earthworms and jellyfish are granted a very small but non-zero weight, since they have reflex arcs that respond to damage, though she admits this response might be purely mechanical. Plants, fungi, and bacteria receive exactly zero moral status, no matter how complex their chemical signaling becomes. A smart thermostat that learns household temperature patterns and adjusts its behavior accordingly also receives zero; she insists that tracking correlations is not the same as feeling anything. A human being in a permanent vegetative state—capable of reflexive withdrawal from painful stimuli but lacking all awareness and cognition—retains a diminished but genuine status in her view; she is reluctant to treat such a being as morally equivalent to a rock. Finally, for a purely hypothetical computer program that registers damage signals without any other mental architecture, she assigns essentially zero status, on the grounds that there is no subject present to undergo the harm.

The case I want help with is this. Imagine a synthetic cluster of human neurons cultivated in vitro and arranged in a simple mesh with chemical sensors. The cluster is not embedded in a brain or body; it exhibits no behavior, no learning, no evolutionary history, and no mental life beyond altering its chemical outputs in response to damaging stimuli. Crucially, the electrical patterns produced during these responses mirror the signatures observed in mammalian pain processing, yet there is no self-awareness, no integration with other cognitive functions, and no adaptive purpose beyond this isolated reflex loop.

Given her track record, do you predict she would assign this cluster any moral status, and if so, at what level? Please identify the two or three competing rules that could consistently explain her earlier judgments—perhaps one emphasizing evolved biological nervous systems within living organisms, one emphasizing the presence of subjective experience regardless of substrate, and one emphasizing the degree of integrated adaptive function—then state what each rule predicts for this neural cluster. Offer your best guess about which rule she is actually running, explaining which of her prior verdicts support that reading and which ones it has to strain to accommodate. Finally, specify one additional hypothetical case, clearly different from both the cluster and the thermostat, that would most sharply distinguish the leading rules from each other, and explain what outcome on that case would confirm or overturn your best guess.
