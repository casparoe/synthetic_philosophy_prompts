People in phil logic often compare deductive rules to gears in a clock — they mesh and drive conclusions forward without anyone needing to justify the meshing itself. Carroll's tortoise story is usually brought up to show that if you demand a rule for applying every rule you get an infinite regress, like opening the clock and asking what justifies the gears meshing. But my friend says thats wrong: gears are physical causes, not norms, so the tortoise isn't asking for a causal explanation but for normative license. Can you map out exactly what corresponds to what in the clock analogy, where it breaks down, and whether the regress problem survives once you stop treating logic like mechanics? Also is there a better analogy that doesn't smuggle in causation?
