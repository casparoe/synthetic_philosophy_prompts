A historian applies a Weber-inspired framework to judge whether cultural values are causally efficacious—independent of institutional and material conditions—in historical transitions. Her rulings: (1) the Protestant work ethic was efficacious in shaping capitalist labor discipline, not reducible to market structure; (2) Confucian examination values were not efficacious in building imperial bureaucracy, since state recruitment needs selected for the cultural form; (3) Romantic aesthetic categories were efficacious in reorganizing collective identity prior to state-building; (4) factory clock-time norms were not efficacious, being direct adaptations to machinery and wage contracts; (5) Mediterranean honor codes remain efficacious in violence patterns despite economic modernization, operating by their own symbolic logic. The new case: in regions with weak banking regulation, a historical tradition of communal risk-pooling predicts higher entrepreneurial investment; where regulation is strong, it predicts nothing. For consistency with her record, should she rule the tradition causally efficacious? Identify the closest analog precedent and say whether her track record genuinely underdetermines this case. Then lay out at least two competing principles that could organize all five verdicts, state what each predicts about the new case, give your best-guess ruling with the reasoning that favors it, and name the further case that would best distinguish which principle she is actually running. End with a line beginning "Verdict:" that states the recommended ruling, the closest analog, and whether the precedents underdetermine the case.
