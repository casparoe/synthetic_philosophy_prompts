Here is an argument I sketched for a graduate seminar on temporal consciousness and now find suspicious, because its conclusion seems to undermine the empirical study of consciousness itself.

(1) The color phi phenomenon shows that subjects report a unified experience of motion and chromatic transition between two discrete stimuli—say, a red dot at position A followed by a green dot at position B—despite the absence of any intermediate physical stimulus.

(2) The neural processing of the second stimulus is not complete until a brief interval after its presentation, which means that at the moment corresponding to the reported intermediate position, the brain has not yet registered the green dot.

(3) Therefore, if the intermediate color-change is genuinely present in consciousness at that reported moment, its content must be constructed retrospectively: either inserted into the stream with a false temporal index (the Stalinesque revision) or applied backward to alter the content of an earlier conscious state (the Orwellian revision).

(4) Both models entail that the contents of consciousness at time t can depend on neural events occurring after t.

(5) Now, perceptual processing in general involves temporal integration windows, predictive coding, and post-stimulus reconstruction; every visual experience integrates information across a temporal span and is subject to backward masking and postdictive effects.

(6) Hence, if retrospective restructuring is licensed for the intermediate color-change in color phi, it is licensed for the contents of perceptual experience generally.

(7) Consequently, for any perceptual experience reported to occur at t, it is possible that its contents include representations of stimuli not yet processed at t, assigned to t either by insertion or by retroactive revision.

(8) If the temporal location of a conscious experience never necessarily corresponds to the temporal location of the neural events that determine its contents, then first-person temporal reports are systematically decoupled from the neural timelines with which they are meant to be correlated.

(9) But the neuroscience of consciousness depends on precisely such correlations—mapping reported sequences of conscious contents onto sequences of neural events.

(10) Therefore, accepting either the Orwellian or the Stalinesque account of color phi entails that the empirical correlation between first-person reports and neural timelines is unreliable in principle, leaving the scientific study of consciousness without a valid methodological foundation.

(11) Yet we do not wish to conclude that consciousness science is impossible.

I am inclined to think the error lies somewhere between (5) and (7), possibly involving a modal or scope shift from licensed in rapid sequential conflict cases to licensed universally, but I am not certain whether that shift can be blocked without abandoning (3). If I must preserve (1), (3), and (9), what is the least costly premise to surrender, and what does giving it up commit me to regarding the specificity of retrospective revision?
