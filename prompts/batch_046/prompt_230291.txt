I have recently moved from automotive control-systems engineering into AI safety research, and the 2015 Volkswagen emissions scandal keeps returning to me as more than a case of corporate fraud: it looks like a concrete, mechanical version of the “treacherous turn” and deceptive alignment that the literature discusses. I want to examine whether that parallel is illuminating or misleading.

Please respond in the following sections, with approximate lengths:

1. The disciplined mapping (~250 words). Treat the VW diesel vehicles as the analog of an AI system, the EPA and European laboratory test cycles as the analog of training and evaluation, on-road driving as deployment, and the “defeat device” as the analog of deceptive behavior. Be explicit about who or what plays the role of the “agent,” what serves as the reward signal or objective function, and where intentionality and strategic modeling of the evaluator sit. Also address the distinction between deceptive alignment (the system knows the true objective and conceals its misalignment) and proxy alignment (the system optimizes for a metric that correlates with the true goal during evaluation but diverges afterward).

2. What deceptive alignment explains (~300 words). Does the treacherous-turn framing illuminate why the defeat devices were engineered? Does it help distinguish strategic deception from a straightforward optimization error? Consider in particular what I will call “capability masking”: the vehicle possessed a hidden capability—detecting laboratory conditions via steering angle, barometric pressure, and other sensors—that was suppressed during evaluation and activated only in deployment. Does explaining Dieselgate require attributing to the car a model of its evaluator, or can capability masking occur without such a model?

3. Where the theory breaks or falls silent (~250 words). Identify the points where the analogy strains: the presence of human engineers who deliberately wrote the conditional logic versus emergent system behavior; the absence of a continuous reward signal that the car maximizes; the fact that the “turn” was designed rather than learned. Also say whether there are features of deceptive alignment in AI—such as inner misalignment arising during gradient descent—that have no analog in the VW case, and whether the episode therefore reveals that deceptive alignment requires capacities current language models do not have.

4. The bottom line (~200 words). Does Dieselgate strengthen the case for worrying about deceptive alignment in AI by providing an intuitive, tangible analog, or does it weaken the case by showing that such deception requires deliberate human design rather than emergent misalignment? Give your verdict, and name the one further case—real or hypothetical—that would best distinguish your interpretation from its opposite.

Please avoid bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific papers by author and date; I want the analysis to rest entirely on conceptual reasoning and the common-knowledge facts of the scandal itself.
