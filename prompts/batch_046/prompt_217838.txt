I have been working through the literature on cross-cultural moral psychology—particularly the WEIRD critique and the way Moral Foundations Theory frames divergence as differential salience of shared foundations rather than incommensurable frameworks—and I am trying to design a conceptual case that forces that distinction into the open without relying on exotic ethnography. Please construct a thought experiment built to the following specifications. Present a single, realistic harm event—specifically, a preventable injury to a non-consenting third party that occurs because an agent with clear foreknowledge fails to intervene—but embed it in two institutional contexts that are both familiar in modern societies: in the first, the failure occurs through a breach of hierarchical duty (a junior professional disregards a direct, authoritative warning from a senior expert); in the second, the identical physical harm occurs through a breach of procedural fairness (a resource allocation that was supposed to be determined by need is secretly weighted by a hidden preference). The divergence in moral judgment should be engineered so that observers in context one condemn the act primarily through loyalty and authority violations, while observers in context two condemn it primarily through fairness and care violations, with the physical outcome held constant. Keep the scenario to one realistic paragraph; do not invent fictional tribes or invoke specific ethnographic studies by citation. After the scenario, walk through how a WEIRD psychologist, a moral relativist, and a moral realist each explain the divergence—making explicit which feature each view treats as explanatory and which it treats as noise. Then identify the precise structural feature of the case, likely the institutional framing of agency, that does the work of generating the split, and explain what happens if you remove that framing while holding the harm and the agent’s knowledge constant: does the divergence collapse, persist, or shift in a predictable way? Next, show how the defender of the view that comes out worst—presumably the strong relativist—would attack the case as question-begging, and pinpoint the weakest move in that attack. Finally, please state how confident you are in the claim that institutional framing rather than hidden cultural premises drives the divergence, and single out the specific point in your analysis where your confidence is lowest.
