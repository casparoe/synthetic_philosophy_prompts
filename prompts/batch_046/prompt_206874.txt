I have been trying to work out what I actually believe about the boundary of life by testing my reactions to concrete cases, and I would like to know whether there is a coherent principle behind them or whether some of my judgments have to be abandoned. Here are my verdicts. A dividing bacterium is clearly alive. A virus outside its host cell, metabolically inert and incapable of independent reproduction, is not alive. A flame that consumes oxygen, grows, and emits sparks that start new flames is still not alive. A growing salt crystal is not alive. A dormant bacterial endospore that shows no metabolic activity is alive, because it retains the organizational structure and potential to resume functioning. A synthetic minimal cell with an artificially designed genome that divides and metabolizes is alive. A lipid-bound protocell that maintains internal ion gradients, repairs its own membrane, and harvests chemical energy, but which contains no nucleic acids and has no mechanism for heredity or evolutionary adaptation, strikes me as alive, or at least closer to alive than the virus. By contrast, a self-replicating swarm of solar-powered robots that harvest raw materials, build copies of themselves, and alter their design over generations through embedded selection algorithms seems to me not alive, though it is more like life than the flame is. I keep going back and forth on whether the protocell and the robot swarm are genuinely different in kind, or whether my distinction merely tracks an unexamined prejudice in favor of carbon-based chemistry over silicon-based organization.

Is there one account of life—whether organizational, metabolic, evolutionary, or some hybrid—that delivers all of these verdicts? If not, which judgment should I overturn to achieve a simpler principle, and what does that principle then say about a case I have not mentioned: an engineered tissue culture that metabolizes and divides but was assembled de novo from non-living chemical precursors with no evolutionary history whatsoever? Please present your reasoning in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as one might write in a letter.
