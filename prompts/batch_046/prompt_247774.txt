You chair the exhibitions committee of a national civic-memory museum. Your committee approves or conditions displays about military defeats and failed political projects, and you must apply a reusable protocol rather than case-by-case intuition. Operationalize the following framework into a decision procedure the committee can run in a 90-minute meeting with non-specialist members; all judgments that cannot be eliminated must be marked for signed chair approval.

The framework holds that lost-cause narratives persist through three displacements: first, factual defeat is acknowledged but minimized in scale or agency; second, the defeat is recoded as a moral victory or necessary sacrifice; third, the narrative is abstracted into a structural alibi — a reusable pattern that explains present grievances or exclusions by analogy to the original loss, without requiring belief in any specific historical claim. The curatorial danger is not stage two (explicit myth) but stage three, where the myth has become invisible infrastructure.

Write the procedure as an ordered set of tests with clear decision nodes that distinguish stage-one acknowledgment, stage-two reframing, and stage-three abstraction in a proposed exhibit. Include specific criteria for identifying stage three, particularly through analogical mapping to present politics or identity claims. Apply the procedure to three cases: the post-1865 Lost Cause narrative of the American Confederacy; the first-century CE Masada siege reframed as collective martyrdom rather than surrender; and the 1940 Dunkirk evacuation reframed as an origin story of national character. Include at least one worked case where the procedure yields a result the proposing sponsors will reject — for example, approval only if the analogical infrastructure is dismantled, which may require stripping commemorative framing the sponsors intended. Flag the precise steps where expert historical judgment is unavoidable and must be documented, and state the failure modes where the procedure will misfire despite correct application. Keep the deliverable practical: a checklist, flow in words, and explicit limits.
