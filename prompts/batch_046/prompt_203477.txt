I'm presenting next week in our jurisprudence seminar a thesis that reframes the Hart–Devlin debate on legal moralism: rather than asking whether law should enforce shared morality or only prevent harm, I argue that both positions tacitly assume a representationalist model of legal norms, and that a performative, social-ontological account—where legal prohibition can constitute, not merely reflect, the moral bonds Devlin values—dissolves Hart's paternalism worry without collapsing into moral majoritarianism.
