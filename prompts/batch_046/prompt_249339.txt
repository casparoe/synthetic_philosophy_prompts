Write a deadpan satirical brochure of approximately 650 words for a fictional municipal department, the Bureau of Gentle Normalisation, adopting the voice of a relentlessly upbeat public-health administrator. The brochure should advertise its flagship initiative, the Reflexive Audit Programme, in which citizens voluntarily submit continuous biometric and emotional data for peer-normalisation, with the explicit claim that this produces—not restricts—genuine autonomy.

The satire must target Foucault’s analysis of disciplinary power and the power/knowledge nexus: demonstrate how surveillance is reframed as care, how comparison and measurement generate the norm rather than discover it, how the examination turns the self into an object of knowledge, and how resistance is co-opted as a feedback mechanism that strengthens the apparatus. Do not allow the voice to slip into liberal complaints about privacy invasion; instead, redefine privacy as “the interior space shaped by external norms” and autonomy as “the successful alignment of desire with the dataset.”

Structure the brochure as five short sections with bureaucratic headings: (1) Our Philosophy—Power as Productivity; (2) The Reflexive Audit—How We Measure; (3) Case Study—The Optimally Docile Subject; (4) Structured Resistance—Your Feedback Strengthens Us; (5) Enrolment—How to Begin. In section 3, invent a brief, anonymised case study of an employee who, after six months of continuous audit, reports that she no longer experiences conflict between her wishes and institutional requirements because her wishes have been “genuinely re-formed by the data.” In section 4, explain that complaining about the programme is itself valuable data that refines the norm, and that citizens are encouraged to submit formal “resistance reports” for analysis.

Keep the tone sustained, ironic, and legible: a reader should recognise that the bureaucrat genuinely believes every word, and should be able to identify exactly which Foucauldian claim is being exaggerated in each section, without the author winking or breaking character. Avoid citing specific texts, page numbers, or asking for a bibliography. Write in British English.
