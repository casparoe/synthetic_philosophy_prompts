hey, ive been chewing on dennett's brain-in-vat puzzle (the one where they extract your brain and wire it up, then ask if you're in the vat or back with the body) and my study group walked me into this reductio that feels wrong but i cant find the exit.

(1) if personal identity survives brain-transplantation, then what matters for being me is the functional dynamics of my brain, not my body.
(2) in dennett's setup there's a real ambiguity about whether "i" am with the brain in the vat or with the body in the operating room — and that ambiguity only makes sense if body-location isnt essential.
(3) now suppose we gradually replace every neuron with a silicon chip that preserves its functional role, in situ. by (1) i survive this.
(4) but we could also stream every chip-state to a remote server in real time, building a perfect functional duplicate.
(5) at the end there are two systems with identical dynamics, both continuous with the original me. identity is one-to-one, so i cant be both.
(6) therefore (1) is false: i dont survive brain-transplantation or replacement, because any principle that allows survival also allows branching.
(7) but then in dennett's original case, the vat-brain is just a copy and im dead, which totally contradicts the intuition he was trying to pump.
(8) so either we bite the bullet and accept branching (and reject one-one identity), or we reject (1) and say body-continuity is what matters after all.

the problem is i really want to keep (1) and (7) — i think dennett's right that he'd go with his brain, and branching feels like a reductio, not a solution. but then (6) looks valid. where's the slip? is there an equivocation between "survival" and "identity" hiding in step (5) or (6)? or does (2) misread the ambiguity as ontological when its just epistemic? if i have to choose, id rather keep (1) and (7) and pay whatever price, but i want to know exactly what im paying.
