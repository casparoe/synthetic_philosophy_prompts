As a software engineer without any formal training in philosophy, I have been trying to understand the debate around inside-view versus outside-view reasoning and its connection to epistemic modesty. In my work, the tension is concrete: when I estimate how long a complex feature will take, I have detailed knowledge of our legacy codebase, the specific skills of my teammates, and the hidden dependencies I have discovered—this is my inside view. At the same time, I can look at industry data showing that similar projects at comparable companies typically take twice as long as initial estimates predict—this is my outside view. The philosophical question that puzzles me is whether genuinely modest reasoning requires me to treat my private, first-person assessment as just one more data point in the reference class, or whether doing so actually violates a different kind of modesty—namely, the recognition that statistical aggregates often miss the causally relevant details that make my situation genuinely different.

Some thinkers maintain that epistemic modesty is essentially the discipline of distrusting one's own exceptionalism, which means the outside view should dominate whenever we are reasoning about outcomes that depend on human judgment. Others reply that this collapses the distinction between knowledge and guesswork: if I always override my specific causal understanding with a base rate, I am not being modest but rather abandoning the rational authority of my own reasoning. I am especially interested in whether there is a stable middle position—perhaps a domain-specific weighting scheme in which the inside view governs mechanistic explanations while the outside view governs temporal predictions—or whether any attempt to combine them secretly privileges the inside view, because the very decision about how to weight the two perspectives relies on the private judgment we were trying to constrain.

Write an essay that takes up this controversy. Weigh the arguments for privileging the outside view as the core of epistemic modesty against the arguments that modesty actually requires preserving the authority of the inside view in domains where causal specificity matters. Reach and defend a clear conclusion: either that a hybrid weighting scheme is philosophically defensible, or that one of the two views genuinely should dominate practical rationality. In justifying your verdict, address at least one subtle objection—such as the worry that selecting weights between the two views is itself an inside-view maneuver that undermines the supposed neutrality of the outside view.
