Take the Minneapolis City Council's 2020 pledge to disband the city's police department following the murder of George Floyd, and analyze it through Max Weber's concept of the state's monopoly on legitimate violence together with the conceptual distinctions among police reform, defunding, and abolition. Do not recount the electoral or legislative aftermath, which is common knowledge: focus on the conceptual mapping. How does Weber's framework classify a municipal government that attempts to withdraw its own monopoly rather than restructure it? Does the pledge represent a return of violence to the community, a redistribution to other state agencies, or an exposure of the fact that the monopoly was never uniformly exercised? What does the episode reveal about whether "defund" is a stable intermediate position—does it preserve the state's exclusive claim while starving its local exercise, or does it collapse into either reform or abolition once the monopoly is examined geographically? Finally, identify where Weber's theory is silent: for instance, does it account for neighborhoods that experience the monopoly not as protection but as occupation, and if not, what does that silence indicate about the limits of using monopoly theory to assess abolitionist demands?
