hey, i'm a phd student in philosophy of religion (mostly medieval jewish thought) and i'm stuck on an argument for my prospectus. it feels like it proves way too much but i can't find the exit.

the chain is built off maimonides' view in the guide part iii that animal sacrifice was never intrinsically valuable—just a divine accomodation to israel's idolotrous dependence on blood-ritual, like a moral prosthetic for a culture that couldn't wean itself off slaughter overnight.

(1) if a law is given purely as a concession to a contingent weakness (here: fixation on sacrificial worship) and not because it tracks an eternal rational good, then its normative force is indexed to the persistence of that weakness—call this the indexicality thesis.

(2) the weakness maimonides describes has disappeared, and modern ethics now treats animals as moral patients.

(3) so on maimonides' own reasoning, reviving sacrifice wouldn't be neutral omission but active indulgence of an obsolete cruelty, violating the very purpose of the original concession.

(4) but apply the indexicality thesis consistently: slavery regulations accomodate agrarian economies, purity codes respond to pre-scientific contagion fears, property laws assume tribal land tenure, and so on.

(5) once those conditioning weaknesses vanish, those laws should also sunset, leaving only the purely rational/eternal commands.

(6) yet even decalog prohibitions on theft or false witness depend partly on social-coordination needs in small tribal societies; if conditions change radically, even they risk expiration.

(7) therefore maimonides' concession theory, taken seriously, dissolves revealed religion into temporary scaffolding with no remaining halakhic structure.

this feels wrong—i want to keep (3), that reviving sacrifice is actually wrong on maimonidian grounds, without accepting (7). but every time i try to block the slide at (4) or (5) i seem to be special-pleading for the laws i happen to like.

which step should i reject? explain why it looked plausible and what giving it up costs—especially whether i can keep the idea that provisional revelation carries an implicit sunset clause without letting that clause swallow the whole system.
