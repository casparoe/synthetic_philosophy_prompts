My friend and I spent the better part of an evening arguing about the Rietdijk-Putnam argument and where it actually lands us, and I want to check whether the chain I built holds up or collapses under its own weight. Here is the argument as I laid it out. (1) Special relativity makes simultaneity frame-relative, so there is no universal "now" slice. (2) The Rietdijk-Putnam reasoning then says that if an event is real in one inertial frame, it is real in all of them, since ontological status should not depend on velocity. (3) From this, every event in the four-dimensional manifold exists with equal reality: the block universe. (4) If all temporal locations are equally real, then "present" functions exactly like "here" — a purely indexical relation with no metaphysical privilege. (5) Now, if my present mental states are not metaphysically privileged over my past or future states, then diachronic personal identity cannot be the persistence of one enduring subject through time; it must reduce to external relations (psychological continuity, causal connections) among four-dimensional "time-slices," just as two people at different places are distinct individuals related only externally. (6) But if the agent who performed an action yesterday is literally a different four-dimensional slice from me today, connected only by relations analogous to those between strangers, then there is no single subject who bears responsibility for past deeds. (7) This eliminates not only moral responsibility but the coherence of practical reasoning about the future, since the "I" who will reap the benefits of today's choice is not me but a related slice. (8) Because this conclusion undermines the very conditions for rational deliberation — including the deliberation that led to accepting the block universe — the argument seems to prove too much. Where does it go wrong? I am inclined to defend (4) and resist (5), but I want to know what rejecting (5) costs, and whether the problem actually lies elsewhere — perhaps in the slide from indexicality to the denial of endurance, or in treating temporal parts as metaphysically on par with spatially separated persons. Please respond in American English, and do not provide bibliographies or reading lists; I want a direct diagnosis of the argument's weakest link and the price of cutting it.
