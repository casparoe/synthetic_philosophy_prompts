I've been arguing with a friend about panpsychism and I think I've landed on a weird middle ground that I can't quite shake. Basically I accept there's something-it's-like to be fundamental physical entities, but I think the combination problem gets misframed if we assume macro-consciousness has to be built by aggregating micro-experiences like stacking bricks. Instead I want to defend closure-fusion: micro-phenomenal fields don't sum, they structurally interlock within causally integrated loops—think recurrent processing dynamics, but applied downward across micro-subjects—so the macro field is a single fused phenomenal boundary rather than a committee. This only occurs where there's bounded informational closure, which is why a human brain gets one fused field, a rock don't, and the universe as a whole doesn't either, since there's no global closure. I realize this sounds like I'm just renaming high-level integration, but I think it avoids both the aggregation problem and cosmopsychism without needing some special metaphysical glue.

What I'm actually committed to is that fusion requires not just correlation but a specific kind of top-down constraint on micro-phenomenal dynamics, so that the macro-subject's boundary is defined by where that constraint stops—it's own causal perimeter, basically. That means consciousness isn't an extra property that pops out; it's the form that interlocked micro-phenomenal dynamics take when they're enclosed in that loop. I know this risks sounding like I'm sneaking functionalism back in through the back door—if fusion tracks causal integration so tightly, why isn't consciousness just what complex integration does?—but I'd reply that integration is phenomenal all the way down, it's just that fusion is the binding relation, not an emergent bonus.

Which empirical results—say about split-brain patients, distributed artificial systems with recurrent loops, or cases where integration seems high but consciousness is absent—would be decisive? Which conceptual moves—maybe showing that fusion either collapses into standard emergentism or that causal closure can't do the work without being circular—would be fatal versus merely awkward? And be honest: have I already qualified this into unfalsifiability by tying fusion so closely to integration? If so, is that a problem for a metaphysical claim of this kind, or is insulation okay here? Rank the defeaters from knockout to cosmetic, say what a defender of this specific view (not panpsychism generally) would themselves concede, and tell me whether I'm actually risking anything or just describing integration in fancier language.
