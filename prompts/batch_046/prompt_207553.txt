Write the opening remarks—approximately five minutes when delivered—for a workshop called "Repetition Without Escape." The audience is evenly divided between philosophers familiar with Nietzsche's eternal recurrence and practicing musicians and choreographers who think about repetition and looped form but have no background in metaphysics. The workshop operates under a single structural rule drawn from the idea: every substantive claim made in the room must be one that its speaker could sincerely will to recur eternally in identical form. Use that rule to shape what you actually say in the remarks—filtering out certain conventional comforts about progress, closure, or unique destiny—while still welcoming both groups, explaining the constraint clearly enough for the artists, and avoiding any suggestion that the rule is a scientific cosmology rather than an ethical and formal discipline. Do not cite texts or authors; build the speech directly from the concept itself.
