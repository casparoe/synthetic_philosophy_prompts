Draft the joint statement that a Cliffordian evidentialist camp and a Jamesian permissivist camp could both sign after an adversarial collaboration on the ethics of belief. Rather than rehearse the standard textbook exchange, center the collaboration on institutional belief-formation: cases where a collective body must adopt a working stance before the evidence is conclusive, with moral stakes that include both the harm of error and the harm of delay, and where affected parties may be wronged either by false assent or by withheld judgment.

Structure the output as follows. First, a numbered agreed-claims section: what both sides accept about Clifford’s prohibition on belief lacking sufficient evidence; about James’s conditions for a genuine option; about whether belief is subject to direct voluntary control; and about whether collective belief differs structurally from individual belief. Second, a disagreements table with one row per crux: (i) whether practical acceptance for policy purposes can be separated from full doxastic belief; (ii) whether moral urgency or asymmetrical stakes ever justify lowering evidential thresholds; (iii) whether there is a principled asymmetry between the harm done by false belief and the harm done by suspended judgment. For each crux, state the precise formulation each camp accepts for its own view; specify the test case both agree bears on it—e.g., a public-health advisory issued on preliminary but non-conclusive data, with each side’s predicted evaluation recorded in advance; and note whether settling this crux would move either side. Third, identify any residual dispute that is merely verbal, especially around the scope of the term belief, and say exactly where the substantive disagreement remains once that is fixed.

Take a committed position throughout. Do not merely list options: state which crux is deepest, explain why, and say whether the collaboration reveals that one camp’s core commitment collapses into the other’s once verbal issues are cleared. Avoid citations, bibliographies, reading lists, and verbatim quotations.
