You chair the methodological review board of an interdisciplinary philosophy-of-mind journal that receives a steady stream of submissions arguing against physicalism via zombie-style conceivability premises. You need a reusable evaluation procedure—not a one-time judgment—that your board can apply to any such submission in about thirty minutes, without requiring members to reconstruct the metaphysics from scratch. Design the procedure as an ordered sequence of tests: first, logical coherence of the zombie scenario; second, physical-duplicate identity (whether every non-phenomenal property is preserved); third, classification of the conceivability claim into its precise type—positive, negative, or ideal—and whether the author supplies the corresponding mental exercise; fourth, the modal inference step, distinguishing epistemic from metaphysical possibility; and fifth, a consistency check against the relevant similarity relation that licenses the inference from conceivability to possibility. For each test, specify the passing criterion, the point at which the procedure halts with a clear rejection, and the point at which it must defer to human judgment because the dispute is about theoretical values rather than formal failure. The procedure must explicitly flag cases it cannot settle, including disputes over modal rationalism and the scope of physical duplication. Apply the procedure to three submissions: a standard global zombie argument; a regional zombie argument in which only a localized subsystem lacks phenomenal properties, where the procedure should reach a verdict you will find unsatisfactory—namely, that the local argument succeeds but fails to threaten global physicalism; and a submission that claims ideal conceivability but delivers only negative conceivability, where the procedure stops without resolving the underlying disagreement about whether modal reasoning requires positive imagination. Conclude by stating exactly what this operationalization cannot capture about the original philosophical debate, and identify which distinctions survive the translation into checklist form and which are necessarily flattened.
