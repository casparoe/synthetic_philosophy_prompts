Consider the proverb "When in Rome, do as the Romans do," which is routinely invoked to settle whether visitors should observe local conventions of tipping, queuing, and jaywalking even when those conventions carry no legal weight and conflict with the visitor's home norms. Rather than treating it as a single principle, disambiguate the precise philosophical claims it could express when applied specifically to these three practices—tipping, queuing, and jaywalking—particularly in cases where the visitor comes from a culture that treats service wages as embedded in prices (making tipping seem demeaning), enforces queuing loosely, or regards jaywalking as a minor coordination failure rather than a moral lapse. Lay out the following readings as a menu: (1) a descriptive coordination claim; (2) a claim about respect for local democratic self-legislation; (3) a moral universalist claim that local conventions generate obligations regardless of content; (4) a strong cultural-relativist claim that moral obligations are entirely constituted by local norms; (5) a harm-avoidance claim about not offending hosts or destabilizing expectations; (6) a virtue-ethical claim about adaptability and humility. For each reading, state whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible moral thesis, or false, and indicate whether it actually supports mandatory compliance for all three practices.

Then apply this taxonomy to each practice. For tipping, queuing, and jaywalking respectively, say which readings justify strict visitor compliance, which permit selective non-compliance, and which depend on hidden empirical premises—such as whether tipping functions as a genuine wage subsidy, whether queuing solves a high-visibility collective-action problem, or whether jaywalking in a given context creates risk rather than mere symbolic disorder. I am especially interested in whether a coherent intermediate position—call it "coordinated pluralism"—can be defended: one that treats queuing as carrying stronger moral weight than tipping or jaywalking because it addresses an immediate coordination failure with transparent mutual benefit, while allowing visitors to opt out of conventions that primarily signal social status or redistribute income in ways that conflict with the visitor's own framework of economic justice. Present your answer as a defended agenda of interpretations rather than a survey, and identify exactly where disagreements lie when someone uses this proverb to end debate rather than to open it.
