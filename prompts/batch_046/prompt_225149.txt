I am a sixth-form student preparing an extended essay on the collapse of moral norms. I have been studying the standard cases — chattel slavery and aristocratic duelling — and the competing explanations: that norms dissolve when rational arguments expose them as unjustified; that they dissolve when the material interests sustaining them shift; or that they dissolve when the honour codes that made them socially mandatory erode. Before I assess particular accounts, I want a rigorous, full specification of what an adequate theory of moral revolution must actually deliver.

Please set out the complete list of desiderata: the phenomena it must explain, the conceptual distinctions it must preserve, and the historical or sociological constraints it must respect, with the motivating case behind each. Specifically, the theory must handle not only slavery and duelling, but also the persistence of ritualised student sword-fighting (Mensur) in nineteenth-century German universities, where participants fought prescribed duels without personal grievance and long after legal prohibition, solely to demonstrate membership of an honour-bound fraternity; it must explain why this practice survived when its practical function had vanished, and whether its eventual decline supports an honour-based or an interest-based explanation. The theory must also distinguish sharply between a norm losing social prestige because its underlying honour economy collapses, and a norm losing enforcement capacity because the costs of punishment exceed the benefits, even though honour still attaches to compliance. It must preserve the distinction between a norm being abandoned altogether and being driven underground or restricted to isolated subcultures. Furthermore, it must be consistent with the historical pattern that some widespread norms — for instance, certain forms of public cruelty previously treated as spectacle — disappeared without any prominent philosophical argument being articulated at the time, and it must not collapse the difference between structural economic change and deliberate normative argument.

Please mark which of these requirements are genuinely non-negotiable for any theory worthy of the name, and which reflect the preferences of particular camps — for example, those who insist that rational moral argument is necessary for genuine normative change, versus those who allow purely structural or economic drivers. Then briefly indicate which single desideratum does the most damage to the largest number of popular theories — in particular, whether the requirement to explain honour-persistent-but-function-void practices such as Mensur is the decisive filter.

I do not require bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I am working without web access and wish to apply the framework using only reasoning and general knowledge. Please write in a formal, polished register, using British spelling and punctuation.
