Write an essay on whether compulsory voting, lowering the voting age to sixteen, and granting the franchise to long-term non-citizen residents can be justified by one coherent principle of democratic legitimacy, or whether they pull in incompatible directions. Weigh the case that democratic authority rests on affected interests, the case that it rests on autonomous consent to be governed, and the case that it requires the capacity for rational self-legislation. Examine whether compulsory voting can be reconciled with an autonomy-based justification; whether lowering the age undermines the rational-capacity rationale that might otherwise exclude non-citizens; and whether extending votes to residents without citizenship dissolves the boundary that makes democratic obligation binding rather than merely coercive. Reach and defend a specific verdict: either identify the unified principle that consistently supports all three reforms, explain which one or two must be rejected for coherence with the others, or argue that democratic legitimacy has no single inclusion principle and that these measures should be assessed independently on distinct normative grounds.
