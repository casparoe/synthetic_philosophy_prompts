Me and my study group got into it about consent after someone brought up this weird hypothetical. Two of us went back and forth -- Maya and Leo.

Maya said consent is only valid if you have what she called epistemic sovereignty over the framing, not just the facts. She meant if someone knows your private vulnerabilities and deliberately frames the choice using analogies calibrated to trigger your specific biases -- say they know you panic about missing deadlines and describe refusing treatment as like missing your last bus home -- then even if every word is literally true, they've violated your autonomy. Its deception about the decision landscape, not the facts.

Leo said thats impossible to apply. Every conversation frames things. If Maya's right, then no consent is ever valid because everyone picks analogies that suit there goals. The line should be false statements versus persuasion, and as long as you're competent, persuasion doesn't invalidate.

Maya came back: Leo's missing the difference between generic framing and targeted exploitation of a private vulnerability. If I know you have PTSD about hospitals and I deliberately use hospital imagery I know will trigger compliance, I'm not just persuading -- I'm weaponizing knowledge of your competence gaps.

Leo replied that she's just smuggling coercion in through psychology. Either there's actual coercion or there isn't. If we invalidate consent whenever someone uses psychological knowledge, then therapists and negotiators invalidate consent all the time. Persuasion is part of life.

They asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't call it. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right at each step -- not just who wins overall? Be committed: take actual positions, don't just list options. And say what both are missing, if anything.
