Write an essay on whether free-floating rationales—reasons that don't depend on any actual reasoner—can actually do normative work, or if they're just dead syntax once you remove the minds that hold them. I got into this argument with a friend after a seminar on moral realism where the speaker kept talking about reasons as if they were floating out there in logical space, and I kept thinking, okay but if every conscious being in the universe suddenly vanished, what happens to the reason don't torture? Is it still true, or does it just become a meaningless pattern?

I want you to take this seriously and not just wave your hands. Consider the view that rationality is like mathematics—existing whether anyone computes it—versus the view that reasons are essentially relational and need a bearer to have any force. To make it concrete, imagine a post-consciousness cosmos where all justification-structures are preserved in some inert physical substrate—like a crystal lattice encoding logical relations but with no interperter. Do those structures still generate obligations? Reach an actual conclusion and justify it, don't end with it depends.
