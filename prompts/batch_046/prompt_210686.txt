I'm putting together a seminar handout on the boundary problem — who actually belongs to the demos? I want a taxonomy in tree form, each branch labelled by the claim that splits it, built for students who know basic political theory but haven't memorised the literature.

Start with these four splits. One: is membership grounded in being subjected to coercive law, being affected by collective outcomes, or in some shared national life? Two: if you go with 'affected', does that include non-human animals with complex preferences, or is there a reciprocity condition — the ability to authorise rules — that excludes them? Three: what about future generations who don't exist yet but will inherit climate damage and debt? Are they members, represented interests only, or outside entirely? Four: there's a specific case I want included, the 'negative residents' — people born in the territory who have formally renounced citizenship, stay physically present and pay taxes, but claim that being counted in the demos violates their autonomy. Depending on split one, they might be in by subjection, out by consent, or in despite themselves.

Build the tree from the logical space: lay out the combinations these answers allow, then map named positions onto the leaves — all-subjected, standard all-affected, citizens-only, and any expanded view that takes in future people or animals. Note any empty combination, like maybe one that includes animals and future generations but excludes present non-citizen residents, and say briefly if it's incoherent, just ugly, or unexplored. Keep it to about seven leaves. Each leaf gets the position's name (or 'unnamed') and one sentence on its most awkward commitment — for instance, having to treat the negative residents as members against their will.

Also flag if your tree can't really seperate two positions that land together. Don't ask for a bibliography or citations. Phrase every branch as a yes/no or either/or question someone could answer without knowing the philosophical label. Use British spelling throughout (behaviour, centre, etc.).
