Below is a graduate-level exam question in statistical mechanics and the physics of information, followed by a student’s answer. You are grading in the capacity of a TA; do not reveal your own view on whether Maxwell’s demon is genuinely resolved.

Question: Maxwell’s demon sorts molecules by velocity using a gate, apparently lowering the gas’s entropy without work. Explain the respective roles of Szilard’s information-theoretic argument and Landauer’s principle in resolving the paradox. Specifically, address whether the unavoidable entropy cost is incurred during the demon’s acquisition of information about a molecule or during the erasure of the record of that acquisition. Then consider a demon equipped with an infinitely expandable physical memory that stores every measurement result but never resets or clears any bit. Could such a device operate indefinitely as a perpetual-motion machine of the second kind? In your answer, distinguish logical reversibility from thermodynamic reversibility, and be precise about how the system boundary should be drawn.

Student answer: Maxwell proposed a being that opens a gate between two chambers, letting fast molecules accumulate on one side and slow ones on the other, reducing total entropy with no work input. Szilard analyzed a one-molecule version and showed that acquiring information about the molecule’s speed necessarily produces an entropy increase of k ln 2, exactly canceling the gas’s decrease; this established that information is physical. Later, Landauer proved that the real cost is not in measuring but in erasing: resetting a physical bit dissipates at least kT ln 2 of heat. Bennett then demonstrated that measurement can be performed in a logically and thermodynamically reversible way, so Szilard’s measurement-cost argument is now obsolete and the modern resolution relies solely on Landauer’s erasure principle. Because erasure is the only unavoidable cost, a demon with infinite memory that never clears its records never pays the Landauer penalty. It can therefore sort molecules forever, driving the gas entropy downward without bound. The second law is preserved only if one arbitrarily expands the system boundary to include the demon’s ever-growing memory, treating the ordered bits in the record as an offsetting entropy increase. But mechanics imposes no constraint on boundary choice, so within the natural boundary of the gas alone the law is violated. Moreover, since each sorted molecule reduces the gas entropy by an amount scaling with ln(Vf/Vi), while any eventual erasure cost for N records scales as N kT ln 2, the dissipation upon resetting would necessarily exceed the accumulated reduction, confirming that only erasure—not measurement—restores consistency.
