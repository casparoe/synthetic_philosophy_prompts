In discussions influenced by Hofstadter’s GEB distinction among frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages, people often invoke the line “the interpretation is never inside the symbols” to reject the idea that a formal system could carry intrinsic semantic content. I recently heard it used to insist that any meaning must be supplied by an external framing interpreter, but I cannot tell what precise thesis is being asserted. Could you lay out the distinct readings of that slogan—specifically, whether it claims frame messages are causally necessary, conceptually necessary, or merely epistemically necessary for meaning; whether it denies that an inner message can exist without an actually tokened outer message, or only that it cannot be recognized; and whether the claim applies to all symbol-manipulating systems or only to artificial ones lacking evolutionary history? For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and indicate which ones actually warrant dismissing intrinsic formal meaning rather than merely complicating it. Present the answer as a disambiguation menu rather than a single interpretation.
