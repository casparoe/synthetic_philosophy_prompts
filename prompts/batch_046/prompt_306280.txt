A colleague walked me into this Gödelian argument about self-awareness and I cannot find the exit. (1) Gödel showed that any consistent formal system F rich enough for arithmetic can construct a sentence G_F that asserts of itself that it is not provable in F; if F is consistent, then G_F is true yet unprovable within F. (2) In GEB, Hofstadter proposes that a self-aware mind is analogously a formal system that embeds a model of itself: mental representations serve as formulas, inference rules as derivations, and self-reference is achieved through a strange loop in which the system describes its own structure. (3) So a consistent, self-aware mind M should be able to formulate the mental analog of G_M: the thought that this very thought is not derivable within M's own reasoning. (4) If M actually derives that thought, then by the analog of Gödel's reasoning the thought must be false, which makes M inconsistent; therefore, in any consistent M, that Gödelian self-thought is true but permanently underivable from within. (5) But then consistent self-awareness necessarily includes a blind spot: the mind can never genuinely recognize, from the inside, its own unprovability boundary. (6) Yet we do seem capable of contemplating our own cognitive limits—for instance, recognizing that there may be justified beliefs we cannot presently derive—which looks precisely like accessing the analog of G_M. (7) Hence either minds are not consistent formal systems, or self-awareness of this Gödelian kind is impossible, or the analogy between formal proof and mental derivation collapses. My colleague claims (7) forces us to abandon the formal-systems model of mind altogether, and that Hofstadter should have paid this price. I want to preserve some form of the strange-loop analogy, but I suspect something slips between (3) and (5)—perhaps an equivocation between syntactic provability and psychological accessibility, or a modal slide about what "can derive" means across object-level and meta-level reasoning. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost for explaining self-awareness through embedded self-models?
