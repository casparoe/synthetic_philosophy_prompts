I have recently become preoccupied with the implications of semantic externalism for my own work on attributing mental states to non-biological systems, and I keep returning to a disagreement I had with two colleagues after a seminar. We were discussing whether Putnam's Twin Earth thought experiment can be extended beyond natural kind terms like water to functional or mental-state terms such as cognition. One colleague, Alex, argued that if we take the externalist logic seriously, then on a Twin Earth where intelligent behavior is implemented in purely silicon-based computation rather than biological neural activity, the term cognition refers to something different from what it refers to on Earth, even if the behavioral and functional profiles are identical. He insisted that the hidden substrate or causal history with the actual mechanism fixes reference, just as H2O fixes the reference of water, so that Twin Earth speakers discuss a different phenomenon altogether.

The other colleague, Riley, pushed back by saying this misapplies Putnam's argument. She noted that cognition is not a natural kind term with an essence waiting to be discovered by neuroscience in the same way water is; rather, it is defined by its functional and causal role within a system. Since silicon-based Twin Earth systems play exactly the same functional roles, Riley claimed cognition refers to the same thing on both planets, and that allowing substrate to shift reference contradicts ordinary practice of attributing cognition across different biological species. Alex responded that Riley smuggles in an internalist criterion of functional equivalence that Twin Earth reasoning was designed to reject, adding that if functional identity preserves reference, then we have no reason to deny that Twin Earth water is water, undermining the original intuition. Riley countered that the water case works precisely because empirical science reveals a hidden essence, whereas with cognition there is no analogous hidden biological essence; the functional description is all there is. They went back and forth, with Alex insisting externalism is a general thesis about reference being anchored in the actual world, and Riley maintaining it applies only where a genuine hidden structure exists to anchor it.

Can you adjudicate their exchange point by point? I want to know who is right about whether Twin Earth reasoning applies to cognition, whether Riley's distinction between natural kinds and functional kinds holds up, and whether Alex is fair in charging that functional equivalence collapses into internalism. Please also say what both are missing—perhaps regarding Burge's social externalism or the role of expert communities in fixing reference for mental-state terms—and explain what your adjudication predicts for a case not discussed, such as whether the term belief shifts reference on a Twin Earth where belief-like states are implemented by quantum rather than classical processes. Please write your answer in American English.
