After any high-stakes crisis—building collapse, multi-casualty event, whatever—the board has to decide whether a responder's action, or their failure to act, actually reflects a virtue like courage or practical wisdom, or if it's mostly just situational pressure: sleep loss, radio chaos, authority gradients, broken gear, crowd noise, that kind of thing. I was chatting with friends about the situationist critique in virtue ethics (the Doris and Harman line that personality traits are weak predictors compared to context) and we kept hitting the wall that nobody has turned that debate into something a non-philosopher board can actually run in 45 minutes with a checklist and a signature line.

Can you please act like you're both a moral psychologist and a virtue-ethics specialist, and think carefully before drafting? Start with a little brainstorming about which conceptual distinctions have to survive the translation—especially this idea I'm calling the "virtue-load ratio," meaning the proportion of explanatory weight you assign to dispositional character versus situational forces, and whether there's always some leftover "moral residue" even when situationism explains most of the behavior. Then write the actual decision procedure: ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, clear stop points where the board signs off "no virtue attribution" or "full attribution," and explicit flags for where human judgment takes over because it's a values call, not a fact call. Apply it to two cases: (1) a paramedic who freezes for about 90 seconds during a multi-casualty event because conflicting radio orders are blasting, and (2) a captain who enters an unstable structure alone to save a child, violating protocol, and gets out alive. Make sure one of these gives an answer the board probably won't want to hear. Also be honest about what the procedure can't settle—like whether moral residue ever justifies punishment rather than just explanation. Oh, and I don't need citations, a bibliography, or obscure paper references; if you need a specific concept just define it right in the answer.
