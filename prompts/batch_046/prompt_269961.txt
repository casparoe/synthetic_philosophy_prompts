The audience is a philosophy of religion grad seminar — mostly people who think Ramsey was doing Wittgenstein-adjacent therapy and leaving us with nihilism, one historian of early analitic philosophy who actually knows the manuscript history of that epilogue and will nail me if I misrepresent the text, and two people in contemporary meaning-in-life work who think reducing the question to logic misses the phenomenolgy entirely. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come — the historian's archival nitpick first, then the phenomenology charge, then the this-collapses-into-pragmatism objection — each phrased like they'd actually say it, with what I should concede, what I should hold, and where my reply is weakest. Include the one I'm praying nobody asks: whether this makes Ramsey into a kind of decision-theorist he explicitly rejected. I sketched the bridge from degrees of belief to degrees of value-commitment but I know that's the shakiest part, so flag exactly where that bridge breaks under pressure and what I'd need to defend it.
