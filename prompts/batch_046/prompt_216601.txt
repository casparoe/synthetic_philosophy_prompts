I've been trying to lock down my epistemic commitments for a project on self-knowledge norms, and I want a consistency audit before I build further on them. Here is the package as it stands:

(1) I accept Williamson-style anti-luminosity: for any mental condition that comes in degrees, there exists a margin where one satisfies the condition but is not in a position to know that one does, so luminosity fails across the board for non-trivial states.

(2) I endorse a restricted KK principle: if S knows that p, and p is discursively articulable by S, then S is in a position to know that S knows p. I restrict this to conceptually structured knowledge and deny it applies to subpersonal processing or raw phenomenal feel.

(3) I hold that rational Bayesian updating requires credal transparency: at the point of updating, the agent must have first-person access to her own credence in the target proposition within a bounded error margin, or the update rule is not action-guiding.

(4) I maintain that phenomenal consciousness—e.g., feeling cold, seeing red—is epistemically inert: it makes no direct contribution to knowledge-that unless it is already subsumed under a conceptual articulation that falls within the scope of (2).

(5) I reject any Cartesian 'inner light' model; self-knowledge is reconstructed, fallible, and sensitive to framing effects, but I still hold that it is generally reliable for discursively structured beliefs.

The combinations I'm unsure about are (1) with (2), (2) with (3), and (3) with (4). More specifically: does applying anti-luminosity to knowledge-states themselves undermine the KK principle in (2), since knowing might be a graded mental condition? Does (3) smuggle luminosity back in specifically for credal states, and if so, does that conflict with (1) extended to degrees of belief? And is there a hidden tension between (4)'s claim about phenomenal inertness and (3)'s access requirement, on the assumption that credences have phenomenal accompaniments? I also wonder whether (1) and (5) are in tension—if self-knowledge is reconstructed and fallible, doesn't that align with anti-luminosity rather than conflict with it?

Please do not just flag every pair. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each specify the least costly revision and what it sacrifices—whether that means weakening KK, abandoning transparency, reconceiving phenomenal states, or accepting that anti-luminosity has a narrower domain than I assumed. Also identify the pairing that looks problematic but actually dissolves on inspection, explaining precisely where the appearance of conflict comes from and why it fails to bite. Finally, tell me whether there is a single revision that resolves the most serious tensions at once, or whether my package is only salvageable by giving up either the anti-luminosity commitment or the rationalist transparency norm. Be specific about which commitments pull in which directions.
