Question: Compare Dennetts and Caruso's positions on whether retributive punishment can be justified if we lack libertarian free will. In particular, explain how each thinker connects their view on free will to their stance on criminal justice, and say whether 'just deserts' can survive without metaphysical freedom.

Student answer:

Dennett is a compatibilist, so he thinks free will and determinism can coexist. Because people still have the kind of freedom that matters for morality, he thinks retributive punishment is fully justified: wrongdoers deserve to suffer in direct proportion to the severity of their crimes, and this desert doesn't depend on any spooky non-physical causation. Caruso, by contrast, is a free will sceptic who thinks determinism (and maybe indeterminism too) rules out the control needed for basic desert. So he rejects retributivism entirely and thinks we should replace prisons with therapeutic rehabilitation programmes that cure criminal behaviour like a disease. For Caruso, since nobody is truly responsible, the only reason to lock anyone up is if a court decides it would maximise social welfare, which makes his view basically a form of utilitarianism about crime.
