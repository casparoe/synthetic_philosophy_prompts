Write a deadpan bureaucratic compliance manual of approximately 600 words, issued in the flat procedural voice of a fictional Office of Epistemic Transparency. The manual implements the so-called Moorean Disclosure Protocol, under which every public statement of fact must be accompanied by an explicit first-person report of the speaker's doxastic state. Your satire should exaggerate the genuine philosophical tension that arises when a speaker asserts that some state of affairs holds while simultaneously disclaiming belief in it—Moore's paradox—by showing how the protocol renders ordinary institutional communication self-undermining. Invent concrete bureaucratic artifacts: standardized disclosure forms (for instance, a weather-report attestation and a tax-assessment disclaimer), penalty schedules for unaccompanied assertions, and training modules for civil servants who must learn to utter the required pairing without recognizing its absurdity. Keep the irony sustained and deadpan: do not explain the paradox, do not name philosophers, and do not break character. The humor should emerge from the accumulation of procedural detail and from the manual's unwitting commitment to the idea that assertion and belief-report are separable, regulable speech acts. Target the institutional fantasy that transparency can be achieved by mechanically decoupling what is said from what is believed. Avoid bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading.
