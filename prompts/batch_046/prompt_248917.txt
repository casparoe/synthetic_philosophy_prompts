I'm working through my intuitions about original versus derived intentionality and want to see whether they converge on one principle or whether I have to abandon some verdicts.

Here are my judgments on concrete cases.

1. A standard thermostat: its state correlates with temperature, but any aboutness seems purely derived from our interpretation.

2. The minimal two-register system Dennett discusses as a two-bitser, which shifts between states that an observer can read as tracking whether food is near or far: still derived, though the functional architecture makes the interpretation more constrained and less arbitrary than in the thermostat.

3. Searle in the Chinese Room, manipulating symbols by rulebook alone: the system as a whole lacks original intentionality; Searle retains his own original intentionality about English and about the rules, but the symbol-manipulation process itself adds none.

4. A large language model generating the sentence "I'm hungry" in a meal-related context, despite having no metabolism or biological substrate: derived, mediated through a vast training history that encodes human intentional patterns rather than through direct designer assignment.

5. A human with severe brain damage who no longer understands language but can press a "water" button driven by thirst: original intentionality remains present, because an internal biological need-state causally drives the behavior, even though the expressive vehicle is degraded.

6. An ant colony using pheromone trails with error-correction to recruit others to food sources: I'm genuinely split—part of me treats this as derived collective behavior, part thinks the functional tracking is robust enough to count as original intentionality at the superorganism level.

7. A synthetic neural organoid grown in vitro that metabolizes glucose and produces electrical patterns specifically correlated with glucose depletion, with no evolutionary history or consciousness: I lean toward original, since the state is coupled to an internal biological need-state, but I'm unsure because there is no selection history behind it.

What principle—perhaps centered on causal coupling to internal need-states, biological origin, evolutionary history, or the stability of interpretation—best captures verdicts 1 through 5 and resolves the tension in 6 and 7? Or must I drop one of those verdicts to keep the principle coherent? And what does the best principle predict for a hybrid case I haven't listed: an LLM embedded in a robotic body with sensors and actuators, where its outputs directly regulate energy intake, but all its internal representations were originally formed through text training?
