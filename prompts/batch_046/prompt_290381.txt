I should like an audit of the following package of commitments on rational belief, the lottery paradox, and the preface paradox. I do not know where the fault lines lie, and I am not attached to saving any particular plank.

(1) I hold a strict threshold view of belief: rational belief in p requires that one’s credence in p exceeds a fixed boundary, which I take to be invariant across domains—no separate bar for mathematics, history, or medicine.

(2) Both paradoxes are dissolved by denying that rational belief is closed under known logical entailment. It can be rational to believe, of each ticket in a fair thousand-ticket lottery, that it will lose; and it can be rational to believe, of each claim in a carefully researched monograph, that it is true—while in each case it is also rational to believe the corresponding existential or disjunctive claim that not all of them are true.

(3) I reject any deep bifurcation between theoretical belief and practical acceptance: if it is rational to believe p in a theoretical context, then it is rational to act upon p in the corresponding practical context, with no supplementary “practical threshold.”

(4) I think rational agents implicitly operate with an epistemic risk budget: over any large-scale intellectual project, the expected number of false beliefs should be kept within bounds compatible with the project’s integrity, and this budget should guide which propositions one endorses.

(5) I maintain that belief should be locally revision-independent: if one belief in a large set is shown false, rationality requires retaining belief in all the remaining members without downward adjustment, provided each still meets the threshold on its own evidence.

(6) I hold that stakes and practical costs never alter the belief-threshold itself; they should only affect the diligence with which one gathers evidence and updates credences prior to applying the threshold.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which are only apparently in tension, and what the cheapest revision would be for each genuine conflict. Order the tensions by severity, and for each state precisely what would have to give—whether a commitment must be abandoned, qualified, or relocated to a different level (e.g. from belief to assertion). In particular, I want to know whether (3) and (4) can coexist with (2), or whether the risk budget forces me either to raise the threshold for large projects (contrary to (1)) or to deny that I can believe every individual claim in the preface case. I also want to know whether (5) sits comfortably with (2) and (3), or whether revision independence commits me to something about practical stances that (3) cannot accommodate. Please answer in British English, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
