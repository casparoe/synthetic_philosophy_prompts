the outside view means you start with a statistical prior drawn from a reference class, ignoring internal details. for a modern continental state the right class includes rome, qing, and soviet union, but also maritime cases like britain and spain since all large empires share a lifecycle curve. the base rate mean is about 210 years with a wide spread, so if the state is currently 75 years old you subtract that from the mean to estimate roughly 135 years left, though modern bureaucratic decay metrics should lower that. kahneman showed that people suffer from the planning fallacy, which is why insiders always overestimate stability, so the outside view corrects by forcing the prior. you can include decay indicators only as adjustments, not replacements, but the real issue is quine's underdetermination: since any decay metric can be interpreted to fit either collapse or survival, the reference class selection is ultimately arbitrary and the forecast cant be rationally constrained.
