I am a professor of philosophy preparing a one-year dissertation-fellowship proposal in philosophy of biology, and I need your help sharpening the framing. In recent conversation with colleagues, I have become convinced that the standard opposition between etiological and causal-role accounts of biological function—where the former ties function to evolutionary history and the latter to present systemic contribution—rests on an unstated assumption that both the unit of selection and the temporal orientation are fixed. I want to investigate whether that assumption fails in microbiome-level cases: microbial traits that are actively maintained by host-level selection despite lacking any evolutionary history of selection within the microbial lineage itself. My working hypothesis is that if we apply the selection-for versus selection-of distinction at this multi-level setting, etiological accounts either generate contradictory function ascriptions—assigning a function to a microbial trait relative to the host system while denying it relative to the microbial lineage—or they collapse into causal-role accounts once the level is fixed. I need a concise, two-page proposal suitable for a fellowship committee that includes philosophers outside the philosophy of science. The proposal should state the precise question—whether clarifying selection-level ambiguity dissolves the etiological/causal-role debate or reveals it as a dispute about levels of selection disguised as a dispute about temporal direction—explain why current answers in the literature leave this open rather than settling it, describe the philosophical method (conceptual analysis of function ascriptions across selection levels, informed by existing biological descriptions of microbiome maintenance but not requiring original empirical research), specify what a successful deliverable looks like (for instance, a taxonomy of conflict-types that shows when one account must yield) and what counts as failure (if the distinction proves merely verbal or if both accounts converge regardless of level), and provide a realistic month-by-month timeline for twelve months. Please do not include a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations; I will compile those separately. The tone should be formal and accessible to non-specialists, and it should treat the strongest objection to my framing—namely, that microbiome cases are just standard multi-level selection problems with no special bearing on the function debate—with genuine seriousness rather than dismissing it.
