I've noticed this pattern in conversations about meaning and it bugs me that I dont have a clean lable for it. Someone says life has no built-in purpose, but then they dont just say "so make your own" — instead they insist that the best kind of meaning comes from throwing yourself into something you fully recognize could have been otherwise. Like, a musician who admits music doesnt matter cosmically, but says that makes her commitment to it more genuine, not less. Or someone who raises kids knowing that in a hundred years nobody will remember them, and claims that awareness is exactly what makes parenting meaningful rather than just biological programming. Its not the classic absurdist move where you smile despite the futility — these people seem to think the futility is part of the smile, like the meaning is generated in the gap between "this matters" and "there's no reason it should." Is there an established term for this specific structure — where meaning is allegedly deepened by the explicit recognition that the ground is missing? Or is it just a sloppy mix of existentialism and stoicism? And if there's no term, how should I think about its edges — is it distinct from just being authentic, or from ironic detachment?
