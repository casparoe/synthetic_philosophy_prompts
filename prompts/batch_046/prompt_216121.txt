I defend the halfer resolution to the Sleeping Beauty problem: upon awakening, Beauty should maintain her prior credence of 1/2 for heads, on the grounds that being awake is guaranteed in both outcomes and that treating the Monday and Tuesday awakenings as distinct epistemic samples improperly fragments a single persisting observer into duplicate data points. What would genuinely count against this position? Respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and include: an internal inconsistency in the conditioning rule I sketched; a parallel case in self-locating belief—say, in duplicated-laboratory or multiverse scenarios—where the same reasoning yields an implausible result; the strongest intuition that the thirder position captures about counting centered possibilities; a formal constraint that forces the 1/3 update under standard Bayesianism for centered worlds, stated without relying on named theorems you might misattribute; and an honest assessment of whether the halfer view can always be protected by redefining what counts as an independent sample, and if so whether that protection makes the view unfalsifiable in a damaging way.
