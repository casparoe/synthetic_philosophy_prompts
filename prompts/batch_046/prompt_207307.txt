I've been working through a problem about epistemic modesty in a reading group on rational belief, and I've built an argument that seems to collapse the inside-view/outside-view distinction from within. The steps feel individually plausible, but together they seem to force either global skepticism or a kind of special pleading, and I can't locate the escape hatch.

(1) Whenever I form a belief through reasoning, I can adopt the inside view—examining the specific inferences and evidence I have—or the outside view—consulting the base rate of error for people with my cognitive profile, training level, fatigue state, and known biases in this domain.

(2) Epistemic modesty requires that when these views conflict, I should weigh the outside view heavily, since I possess no special exemption from statistical patterns of human error.

(3) For any substantive belief, however, there exists some relevant outside-view statistic indicating that people in my reference class are frequently mistaken: expert disagreement is widespread, motivated reasoning is measurable, and even trained reasoners commit logical errors at non-trivial rates.

(4) So modesty consistently demands that I substantially downgrade confidence in whatever my inside view delivers.

(5) Apply this reflexively to the belief that epistemic modesty is itself correct: the outside view on philosophical reasoning about epistemic norms reveals high disagreement and poor inter-rater reliability among people with precisely my background.

(6) By (2)-(4), I ought therefore to downgrade my confidence in modesty itself.

(7) But weakening (2) removes the reason to prefer the outside view in other cases; the distinction then applies selectively, which requires an inside-view judgment about which domains are exempt.

(8) That exempting judgment is itself subject to (1)-(4), yielding either an infinite regress or an arbitrary stopping point.

(9) Consequently, the distinction either applies globally—undermining the principle that authorizes it—or not at all, leaving only global credulity or global suspension of belief.

(10) Therefore the inside-view/outside-view framework proves self-defeating.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? I want most to preserve (2) and (4), because they seem to capture a real constraint on not treating oneself as exceptional, but I need to know what that commitment costs me. If the flaw lies in (3), explain whether there are domains in which the outside view genuinely lacks purchase without my being an outlier. If it lies in (5)-(6), show how self-application can be blocked without special pleading. Please also say what would change your mind—whether a specific empirical finding about meta-cognitive calibration, or a formal model of self-trust, would shift your diagnosis of the weak step—and explicitly flag the point in your own reasoning that is most likely to fail.
