Rudolf Carnap died in 1970, long before governments began using formal, machine-checkable logical languages to structure municipal planning, welfare allocation, and transit policy. Imagine Carnap were alive today and asked to evaluate a proposed institutional framework—call it Logical Syntax Planning—in which every city-level administrative decision must be expressed in an artificially constructed, precisely defined syntax designed to eliminate metaphysical ambiguity and value-laden vagueness from policy discourse. Drawing on Carnap’s logical empiricism, his program for the rational reconstruction of scientific and everyday concepts, his physicalism, his sympathy for the unity of science, and his lesser-known but genuine interest in whether economic coordination could be expressed in formal, non-metaphysical terms, predict how he would assess this framework. Would he welcome it as the extension of philosophical clarification into governance, or would he resist it on the grounds that normative planning involves irreducible practical commitments that syntactic rules cannot capture? Reconstruct the tension in Carnap’s own work between logical reconstruction and the status of value judgments, and determine whether his likely stance would depend on treating planning as a purely technical problem or as one requiring ethical judgment that escapes formalization. Write your response in American English.
