As a retired evolutionary biologist who has only recently begun studying metaphysics in earnest, I have settled on the following five commitments and want a rigorous consistency audit before I present them. (1) I accept the standard Lewisian counterpart-theory framework: no particular exists in more than one possible world, and modal claims about me are made true by relations of qualitative similarity that hold between me and my counterparts elsewhere. (2) I reject haecceitism: there is no bare, non-qualitative "thisness" that could ground transworld identity. (3) I defend what I call serial genealogical essentialism: for any organism, continuous membership in its actual reproductive lineage is essential to being that particular thing; if the lineage diverges at conception, the resulting entity is neither me nor a counterpart of me, however physically similar. (4) I take specific biological counterfactuals—e.g., that this particular oak could have survived the blight—to be genuinely true, and I understand them as made true by counterparts that preserve the relevant essential properties. (5) I maintain that evolutionary scientists routinely and legitimately employ counterfactual reasoning (for instance, about adaptive radiation after mass extinction) without invoking possible-world semantics, and that a sound metaphysics of modality ought to respect that empirical autonomy.

Please examine these commitments pair by pair: identify which are in genuine logical or motivational tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each tension, say exactly what one commitment forces that another denies, and identify the least costly single revision that would restore consistency to the package. Finally, tell me whether the set can be salvaged intact without abandoning counterpart theory altogether, or whether (3) and (5) together make that impossible. Be precise about what each view actually commits me to.
