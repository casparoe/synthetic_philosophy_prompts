Write an essay on whether being religious actually makes people more moral, but skip the usual donation-stats and church-attendance correlations. First, unpack what "more moral" even means: is it about internal motives, rule-following reliability, or the kind of thick social trust that lets strangers cooperate without contracts? Then tackle this tension: highly secular societies (the Scandanavian model) score high on trust and low corruption, yet some philosophers argue this is just "moral freeloading" — secular ethics hasn't built its own engine for cooperation, it's just burning religious capital accumulated over centuries. Others claim secular institutions can run on "distributed reciprocity loops" where transparancy and mutual monitoring replace divine bookeeping. Don't cop out with "it depends on your definition." Reach and defend a real verdict: is secular high-trust genuinely self-sustaining, or is it running on borrowed fuel? Justify it.
