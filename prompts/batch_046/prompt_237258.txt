I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, but I'm auditing an undergraduate seminar on comparative ethics and have sketched an essay that applies Confucian role ethics to the ethics of open-source software maintenance. I want to know whether the conceptual framing holds together, so I'm asking for direct, committed feedback rather than a balanced survey of every possible view.

My core claim is that Confucian role ethics—particularly the interlocking obligations tied to roles such as parent-child, ruler-subject, and elder-younger—offers a more precise framework for understanding maintainer obligations in distributed software projects than either deontological rights-talk or aggregate consequentialism. Specifically, I argue that the Confucian principle of zhengming, or rectification of names, imposes an ethical demand that a maintainer's actual practices remain congruent with the relational title "maintainer." When a maintainer retains commit access and nominal authority while effectively abandoning active stewardship—what open-source communities sometimes call ghost maintainership—this produces what I term a zhengming rupture: the name no longer maps onto the role-constituted relationships (responsiveness, guidance, trust) that give the name its ethical content. I further suggest that repairing this rupture requires not merely a technical handover of repository rights, but a public acknowledgment of the changed relational status, analogous to how Confucian ritual (li) restores harmony after a disruption in social roles.

I'm particularly interested in whether this analogy between technical handover and ritual restoration is philosophically serious or just a stretched metaphor. I also want to know if I've mischaracterized zhengming—does it genuinely carry this relational-ontological weight in Confucian thought, or is it primarily a political-administrative tool for rulers? Finally, does my argument obscure important differences between hierarchical Confucian roles and the flatter, voluntary associations of open-source collaboration?

Please comment on the sketch's conceptual accuracy, its interest value, and especially on whether the "zhengming rupture" idea is defensible. I want a committed assessment: if you think the framing collapses, say so directly and explain exactly why; if you think it works but needs sharpening, specify precisely where and how, and take a definite position on whether the essay should pursue this line or abandon it.
