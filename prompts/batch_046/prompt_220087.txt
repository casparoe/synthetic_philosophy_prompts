I've been trying to formalize the value gap in Plato's Meno problem by treating justification as a depreciable epistemic asset governed by a doxastic amortization schedule—its worth is strictly its net present yield in subsequent true beliefs, with zero terminal value. On this model, knowledge becomes true belief bundled with an instrumental coefficient, and asking why knowledge exceeds true belief reduces to asking why an asset with compound returns outperforms its underlying commodity. This seems to dissolve the puzzle into a forecasting problem: we face a form of consequentialist cluelessness about epistemic goods because we cannot compute the full belief-tree that a given justification will generate. So is the Meno problem actually just a special case of that cluelessness—namely, our inability to amortize justification across an unknown future—or does this framing presuppose that the value of knowing is entirely reducible to amortized doxastic returns? If the latter, what kind of value survives once we strip away both intrinsic worth and instrumental yield?
