Assume Immanuel Kant were alive today and confronted with the modern distinction between lying and paltering—where paltering consists of technically truthful statements deliberately engineered to create false beliefs. How would he evaluate paltering's moral status relative to explicit lying? Would he treat it as falling under his prohibition on falsehood, or as a permissible evasion that respects the literal duty of truthfulness? Answer by drawing on Kant's actual commitments about the universalizability of maxims and the distinction between acting in accordance with duty and acting from duty, rather than on generic deontological slogans. In particular, consider whether a maxim of paltering could be universalized without contradiction, and whether its form differs essentially from the maxim of direct falsehood.
