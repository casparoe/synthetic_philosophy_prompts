I want you to take the empirical cluster around cross-cultural differences in folk morality—the WEIRD versus non-WEIRD distinction, the documented variation in how societies weight harm, fairness, loyalty, authority, and purity, the tight-versus-loose cultural dynamics, and the philosophical arguments about whether this variation undermines moral objectivity or merely reflects differential calibration of shared capacities—and restate the entire cluster in my terms. Treat moral judgments as inferences drawn under generative models of social coordination; treat cultural transmission as the propagation and refinement of shared priors; treat disagreement about moral objectivity as a disagreement over whether different groups approximate the same high-level latent structure governing normative interaction, or whether they run genuinely incommensurable generative models with no shared precision landscape. Preserve the philosophical stakes precisely: does divergence across cultures indicate systematic error relative to a single correct model, or does it represent locally optimal adjustments to different social ecologies?
