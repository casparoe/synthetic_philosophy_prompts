I recently retired from experimental physics and now serve on the Governance Committee of the International Consortium for Open Science. Our founding charter, ratified in 1998, guarantees unrestricted dissemination of findings. A dispute has arisen over Article 4: a member institution in a jurisdiction with strict export-control regulations argues that the phrase unrestricted dissemination should be evaluated in the present legal and technological context, which permits restricted access to dual-use data, whereas another member insists the term was fixed at ratification to mean immediate, unconditional publication regardless of subsequent regulatory change. A working group has proposed that the Committee adopt a formal two-dimensional semantic protocol for interpreting charter language, distinguishing the context of ratification, which fixes the linguistic character of normative terms, from the context of evaluation, which determines their current extension given changed conditions. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—adopt the protocol, reject it, or adopt it with conditions—together with a practical mechanism for resolving conflicts between character and content. The memo should explain specifically how this framework applies to Article 4, treat seriously the strongest objection that making charter obligations context-sensitive undermines the binding force of international agreements and invites strategic reinterpretation by member states, and state what principle the Committee would be committing itself to with each possible choice.
