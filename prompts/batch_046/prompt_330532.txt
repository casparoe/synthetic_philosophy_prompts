Assume the role of an expert philosopher of mind specializing in the metaphysics of consciousness and the philosophy of psychology. Before you evaluate any particular solution to the paradox of phenomenal judgment within an epiphenomenalist framework, please think carefully and begin with structured brainstorming: lay out the complete desiderata that any adequate theory must satisfy. Specifically, what must such a theory deliver regarding our judgments about phenomenal states—first-person present-tense reports, retrospective qualitative memories, comparative assessments between modalities, and judgments of phenomenal absence—given that phenomenal properties themselves lack causal efficacy?

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or distinction it protects: for example, why the theory must preserve the gap between phenomenal access and cognitive access, or why it must explain the reliability of introspective reports without invoking downward causation from consciousness to the physical substrate. Crucially, include a specific, somewhat unconventional constraint: the theory must also accommodate what we might call "silent phenomenal residues"—hypothetical cases in which a phenomenal state occurs but generates no corresponding judgment whatsoever, whether due to extreme dissociation, cognitive suppression, or total attentional collapse—without allowing opponents to dismiss such residues as proof that the state was never genuinely phenomenal. Evaluate whether accommodating these residues is a legitimate theoretical burden or an artificial requirement invented to burden epiphenomenalism.

Then, identify which pairs of desiderata appear jointly unsatisfiable—especially if one demands that phenomenal judgments be causally explained by the very states they report, while strict epiphenomenalism denies such causal links. Finally, briefly assess how three broad responses—the phenomenal concept strategy, a self-measurement model, and a deflationary eliminativist approach—fare against your list, but keep the primary weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; proceed entirely from conceptual analysis.
