I'm currently enrolled in a seminar on analogical reasoning and conceptual architecture, and we've been examining how meaning operates in severely constrained microdomains—those artificial problem spaces, like alphabetic pattern-completion tasks, where every feature is explicitly defined and nothing is hidden in background knowledge. In these settings, one might expect concepts to behave like rigid predicates with sharp boundaries; yet the literature on fluid concepts suggests that even here, analogical mapping depends on a kind of controlled semantic drift, where a notion like successor or opposite subtly shifts its extension depending on what other relations are active in the mapping. I am trying to understand the precise philosophical status of this slippage, particularly whether it is a bug in our representational scheme or an essential feature of analogical cognition.

To make this concrete, I have been working through a custom microdomain I designed for our discussion section: a system of geometric letterforms composed of line segments and loops, where analogies are drawn not by phonetic value but by topological properties—closure, connectivity, and nesting depth. In this space, the concept of closure applies clearly to the form corresponding to O, but its application to the form corresponding to C becomes ambiguous: geometrically C is open, yet in analogical contexts where phonetic rounding is salient, C can be mapped as the open counterpart to O in a way that borrows the functional closure of rounded articulation. The slippage seems unavoidable if the analogy is to succeed, but it also threatens to dissolve the concept into an arbitrary family resemblance. My question is not merely descriptive—how does Copycat-style reasoning accommodate this—but conceptual: is the fluidity located in the concept itself (as a graded or multidimensional structure), in the analogical mechanism (as a context-sensitive parameter adjustment), or in the representational substrate (as an emergent property of parallel constraint satisfaction)? I want a committed answer that takes one of these three loci seriously and explains what the other two must concede, particularly regarding whether conceptual identity can survive this kind of microdomain-specific deformation without collapsing into pure contextualism. Please do not simply survey the options; argue for one and show what costs the others.
