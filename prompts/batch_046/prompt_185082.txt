I'm writing on the historiography of twentieth-century totalitarianism and have noticed a conceptual slide: structuralist accounts treat Weimar's institutional collapse and the interwar economic crisis as making authoritarian nationalism inevitable, and from this premise many infer that Hitler was causally fungible—implying that Göring, or even a non-charismatic directorate, would have generated an essentially identical regime with the same racial architecture and expansionist logic. If structural conditions truly determine the outcome, doesn't that render the Great Man thesis not merely empirically mistaken, but conceptually incoherent? Or is the error mine—conflating structural inevitability with counterfactual robustness about specific institutional designs?
