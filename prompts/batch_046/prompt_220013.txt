I am writing a dissertation chapter on the political dimensions of logical empiricism, and I want to test the internal coherence of reconstructed Carnapian positions on economic planning. Please rank the following four interpretive stances—each attributing to Carnap a different attitude toward centralized versus decentralized resource allocation—into tiers (S-tier through D-tier) according to how well they cohere with three fixed commitments drawn from his philosophy: (1) the value-neutrality of empirical science, (2) the physicalist thesis that all scientific statements are translatable into the language of physics, and (3) the principle of linguistic tolerance, which denies that metaphysical disputes about the "correct" framework have cognitive meaning. The four stances are: (A) the strong Neurathian extension, which treats centralized planning as a direct institutional application of unified physicalist science; (B) the strict separationist view, which holds that Carnap's philosophy of science is entirely silent on institutional design, rendering planning a non-cognitive practical decision; (C) the pluralist-tolerance view, which argues that Carnap would permit competing quantitative-planning and market-coordination languages to operate side-by-side without philosophical adjudication; and (D) the crypto-normative view, which claims that Carnap's personal socialist commitments implicitly authorize planning despite his explicit denial that science can validate value judgments. For each tier, provide a one-sentence justification. Then, in two sentences, identify the specific tension between commitments (1) and (3) that makes the highest-ranked stance more stable than the lowest-ranked one.
