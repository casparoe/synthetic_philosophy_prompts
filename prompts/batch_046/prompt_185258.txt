I'm a doctoral student in the history and philosophy of technology, currently working through Marx's 1857-58 notebooks on machinery and fixed capital. In the portion often called the fragment on machines, Marx traces the development of the means of labor through successive metamorphoses, culminating not in isolated devices but in an automatic system of machinery. He describes this system as set in motion by an automaton — a moving power that moves itself — and suggests that machinery reaches its fullest development only when it becomes such an integrated system rather than a mere collection of tools. Alongside this, he notes that the worker's activity is reduced to that of a watchman or supervisor, overseeing a process that seems to operate independently of direct human labor.

I'm trying to understand a specific conceptual tension in this description. On one reading, Marx is advancing a substitution thesis: machinery is dead labor that replaces living labor in a linear causal chain, and the supervisory role is residual, confirming that surplus value still originates in living labor time. On another reading, which I want to test without importing anachronistic cybernetics, Marx describes something closer to a transformation thesis: the automatic system does not merely substitute for labor but restructures production into a self-referential loop in which fixed capital becomes the active subject, orienting its present operation toward its own future reproduction rather than toward an external input of labor. The automaton, on this view, is not just more efficient dead labor but a new form of capital that generates its own temporal horizon.

Could you help me determine whether the passage supports the transformation thesis, or whether the watchman figure and the emphasis on dead labor firmly constrain us to the substitution thesis? In particular, does Marx's claim that the automatic system represents the culmination of machinery imply that capital acquires an anticipatory or predictive structure — not in any modern cognitive sense, but in the sense that the system's operation is internally oriented toward reproducing its conditions of existence?

Please be explicit about what would change your mind: for instance, if the supervisory function is shown to be structurally indispensable rather than residually present, or if the self-moving character of the automaton can be read as purely mechanical causation with no self-referential dimension. Also, please identify where your reasoning is most likely to fail — for example, in projecting contemporary distinctions about closed-loop control onto a nineteenth-century account of factory mechanization, or in overreading the phrase about a moving power that moves itself.
