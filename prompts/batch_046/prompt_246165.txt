Compare the disquotationalist and prosententialist treatments of truth with respect to negative existential claims—specifically, assertions that deny the existence of entities for which the language possesses no corresponding positive predicate (for instance, that there are no round squares, or that caloric fluid does not exist). Structure your analysis around three questions. First, can each theory preserve the intuition that such denials are genuinely true rather than merely redundant, ungrounded, or expressively defective, given that there is no positive fact to disquote or prosententialize? Second, does either account implicitly commit its adherents to an ontology of negative facts, absences, or non-instantiations, or can both remain fully ontologically innocent? Third, trace the semantic ascent from the object-language denial to the meta-language attribution of truth—“it is true that there are no round squares”—and determine whether either theory succeeds in explaining this ascent without reintroducing a substantial, explanatory property of truth. To ground the comparison, construct a single worked example involving a scientific community that has abandoned a theoretical entity (use caloric fluid, or a precisely defined analog of your own construction), and show how a practitioner within each framework would articulate the truth of that rejection. Conclude by identifying which view offers the more coherent account of truth in cases of absent reference, and explicitly flag any juncture at which the disagreement between the two theories becomes merely verbal rather than substantive.
