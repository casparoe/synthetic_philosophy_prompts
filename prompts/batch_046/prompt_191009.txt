I'm writing from a long-running study group that meets to parse classic texts with the kind of precision usually reserved for technical papers. This week we're stuck on Descartes's Meditations, specifically on what the cogito actually secures. One member, who has been reading in the philosophy of language, argues that the move from thinking to existing establishes nothing more than the occurrence of a present mental event — that there is thinking happening now — and that Descartes illicitly promotes this into proof of a persisting thinking substance, res cogitans, via a grammatical sleight of hand. She treats the first-person formulation in the cogito on the model of an impersonal construction like Latin pluit, suggesting that the ego functions more like an essential indexical — irreducible to any description, in Perry's sense — which pins an occurrence to a present perspective without committing us to an enduring owner behind it. On her view, the first-personal structure of doubt is a feature of the event's phenomenology, not evidence of a substantial subject.

I countered that Descartes is explicit in defining himself as a thing that thinks, and that the move from occurrence to substance is not smuggled in but is part of the discovery itself. I claimed that doubting is an intentional act with a directed structure — you cannot have doubt without a doubter in a stronger sense than rain needs a rainer — and that this first-personal directedness gives us not just epistemic certainty about an occurrence, but metaphysical access to the kind of thing that performs such acts. She replied that this begs the question by assuming the very substantial ego the cogito is meant to prove, and that if we restrict ourselves to what is genuinely indubitable — the bare occurrence — then any further claim about substance exceeds the evidence.

Can you adjudicate this exchange move by move? Specifically: does her grammatical and impersonal-construction objection hold against the standard reading? Does my intentional-act reply actually establish substance, or only a functional role? Does her appeal to the essential indexical undermine the substantial interpretation, or does it merely redescribe it? And is there a missing distinction — perhaps between epistemic certainty that thinking occurs and metaphysical commitment to what kind of thing does it — that dissolves part of our disagreement without resolving it?
