I've been argueing with my friend Marcus over pizza about whether a government can be "okay" even if it's a dictatorship, as long as people are actually getting richer and safer. I'm taking an online course on politics and I find this "performance legitimacy" idea kind of slippery, but Marcus has really strong opinions. His verdicts so far: Singapore under Lee Kuan Yew, yes, totally legit — corruption near zero, everyone got richer, and the people jailed were mostly just annoying bloggers, not regular folks. Pinochet's Chile, definately not — GDP went up but they threw people out of helicopters and tortured thousands, so prosperity doesn't wash that away. Park Chung-hee's South Korea, yes — he crushed unions and jailed students, but he also turned a dirt-poor country into a manufacturing powerhouse, built schools everywhere, and the repression was aimed at communists and protesters, not random citizens. Rwanda under Kagame, yes — after the genocide he kept the peace, built roads and hospitals, kept corruption low; some journalists vanish, but ordinary people are safe and way better off than in neighboring democracies. And China today, he says mostly yes — lifting hundreds of millions out of poverty is huge, and while Xinjiang is horrific, he treats it as an exception, not the system for the average person.

Now Marcus is stuck on this other place. It's a real one-party country that went from dirt poor to middle-income in about 20 years. They built highways, electrified rural villages, and the bureaucracy is actually competent — the leader's family isn't looting like in other dictatorships. The prosperity is real, but it's mostly for the dominant ethnic group; the other 40% are kept out of universities and top government jobs. If you publicly criticize the leader, you get detained for 3-5 years in an administrative camp — no secret torture, just legal paperwork. But members of that minority group who protest sometimes just disappear; it's not genocide-scale, but about one in a hundred vanish each year into secret facilities. Thirty years ago, before this regime, there was a civil war that killed about a tenth of the population and there was basically no functioning economy.

What do you think Marcus would say — is this regime legit? I'm less interested in your own view than in the rule he's actually running, since his earlier answers fit more than one idea. Is it "widespread prosperity plus targeted, predictable repression equals legit"? Or "having a competent bureaucracy and not stealing matters more than voting"? Or "the alternative was so awful — civil war and famine — that delivering order and growth is enough no matter what"? Which of his earlier cases best splits these rules, and what further hypothetical case — maybe one with high growth but totally random, unpredictable terror, or one with perfect equality but stagnant poverty — would really tell them apart? Also, please answer in American English.
