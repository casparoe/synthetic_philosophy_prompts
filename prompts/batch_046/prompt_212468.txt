I'm working through some problems for my philosophy of physics seminar, and a fellow student walked me into an argument about Gödel's rotating universe that I can't seem to exit without giving up something I'd rather keep. The argument runs as follows. (1) Gödel's rotating universe contains closed timelike curves, which entail that there is no globally consistent temporal ordering—no objective fact about which events are earlier than others across the entire spacetime. (2) If there is no global temporal ordering, then the asymmetry required for causation—cause preceding effect—fails globally, even if local neighborhoods preserve it. (3) Moral responsibility, however, is essentially diachronic: an agent is responsible now for an action that was earlier performed by her, and this requires a linear trajectory of agency that can be traced without circularity. (4) So if global temporal ordering collapses, the diachronic structure that underwrites moral responsibility collapses with it, leaving only instantaneous, local attributions that cancel out over any closed loop. (5) Therefore, if we inhabit a Gödel-type universe, moral responsibility is globally illusory; and since cosmological observations do not conclusively rule out a rotating model, we should treat our own responsibility-attributions as locally useful fictions rather than globally true. (6) But this proves too much: it doesn't just undermine free will in rotating universes; it undermines any theory of moral desert that requires a linear past-to-future trajectory of agency, including most accounts of retributivism, karmic accounting, and even contractualist justification across generations. I want to keep (3) and reject the slide to (5), but I'm unsure whether the fault lies in (2)—that local causation can ground global responsibility despite the metric—or in a hidden equivocation between temporal ordering and causal ordering. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? In particular, does saving moral responsibility require denying that Gödel's metric really eliminates global time, or can I preserve the cosmological result while blocking its ethical extension by distinguishing the conditions for physics from the conditions for agency?
