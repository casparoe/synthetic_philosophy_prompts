I'm working through classic and contemporary intuition pumps in philosophy of mind and ethics—scenarios like the Chinese Room, the violinist analogy, and recent arguments about artificial consciousness—and I've noticed a recurring structural move that I don't have a word for. The pattern has two stages. First, the author engineers a boundary case sitting exactly where one of our ordinary concepts begins to break down: a system that behaves indistinguishably from a conscious human but lacks biological substrate, or an attachment that is total and non-consensual but serves a vital purpose. The reader is invited to deliver an intuitive verdict about that extreme limit—say, that there is no genuine understanding, or that no rights infringement occurs. Second, once that limit-judgment is secured, the author uses it as a fixed calibration point for the entire continuum closer to the center: if the perfect behavioral duplicate lacks consciousness, then a system that is only partially similar must lack it to an even greater degree; if total attachment is permissible, then any lesser attachment is automatically permissible too.

What makes this move questionable is that the intuition at the boundary is driven precisely by the extremity of the case—the total absence of biology, the total absence of autonomy—whereas in intermediate cases those absent features are only partly missing, and competing intuitions about gradual similarity or scaling rights should regain force. The pump freezes one response at the limit and slides it inward as if it were a linear measurement, even though the reasoning that justified the boundary judgment does not extend inward. I have seen this in discussions of fading qualia, in some formulations of the repugnant conclusion in population ethics, and in arguments that move from perfect simulation to partial simulation without independent argument.

Does this configuration have an established name in the literature on thought experiments, cognitive framing, or analogical reasoning? I know it is not standard framing, since the distortion is built into the scenario's architecture rather than its wording; it is not simple equivocation, because the concept being applied does not shift in meaning, only in domain; and it is not the familiar slippery slope, since there is no causal claim that one case produces the next—only an analogical calibration from a limit case. If there is a dedicated term, I want to know how it is distinguished from modal scope errors and from the fallacy of the continuum. If not, I would like a precise coinage with a clear definition, together with boundary conditions that show what belongs and what does not.
