I'm preparing for a discussion in my philosophy of science reading group, and I've been working through Karl Popper's assessment of evolutionary theory. From what I understand, Popper demonstrated that natural selection is essentially tautological—defining fitness as survival and survival as fitness—so that Darwinism fails his criterion of falsifiability and should be classified as metaphysical rather than empirical science. He held to this judgment consistently across his career, treating evolutionary explanations as useful heuristic narratives but denying them the status of proper scientific theories like those in physics.

Now, considering that contemporary evolutionary biology integrates quantitative genetics, experimentally testable hypotheses about selection pressures, and predictive phylogenetic modeling, I'm trying to determine whether Popper's tautology objection applies with equal force to modern synthetic theory. Is there a principled distinction, within Popper's own framework, between a theory that is unfalsifiable in logical structure and one that is merely resistant to quick empirical refutation—what we might call epistemically expensive rather than epistemically empty? Furthermore, I'm wondering whether a research programme that begins with a tautological core can ever genuinely transition into scientific status without first abandoning that core, or whether Popper's later acknowledgment of evolutionary biology's predictive successes requires us to say that he implicitly revised his demarcation criterion rather than merely applying it more generously. I would appreciate an explanation that disentangles these conceptual threads without simply rehearsing the standard narrative about Popper's recantation.
