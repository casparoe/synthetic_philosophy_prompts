Here is my opening challenge: a colleague in my research group has proposed what she calls a "closure-tracker" model—an artificial epistemic architecture designed to register precisely those propositions it does not yet know, updating its registry as knowledge is acquired. She applies this machinery to generate a self-referential proposition s, which asserts of itself that it is not registered by the tracker. If s is true, then by the system's own design it remains unregistered and therefore unknown; but if s is false, then the system registers it and thereby knows it, which renders s true after all. She argues that this loop is not an extraneous gadget but an operational instantiation of the very conjunction Fitch employs—namely, that an unknown truth conjoined with the claim of its own unknownness produces an unstable state. From this she concludes that the knowability principle is not merely paradoxical in the abstract but operationally incoherent for any system that can monitor its own ignorance. I have objected that she is importing a causal, computational mechanism into a logical argument about modal accessibility, yet she insists that Fitch's use of the knowability operator over conjunctions already presupposes just this kind of iterative self-monitoring. How do you adjudicate her move? Is the closure-tracker a legitimate extension of Fitch's reasoning, or does it conflate the grammar of epistemic attribution with the mechanics of information storage?
