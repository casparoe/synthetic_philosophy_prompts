Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for positions on epiphenomenalism, anchored to an under-mapped angle: the paradox of phenomenal judgment applied specifically to the sincere judgment that one lacks phenomenal consciousness altogether. The audience is an advanced undergraduate who grasps the basic paradox but not the specialist literature. Build the tree from logical commitments rather than from named theories, using concrete case-questions that can be answered without knowing any labels—e.g., whether a functionally identical zombie twin could sincerely, non-derivatively report that it has no inner experience, and whether such a report, if true, requires any causal efficacy of the absent phenomenal state.

The tree should have six to eight terminal leaves. The following four positions must appear, named only at the leaves:
- Self-stultification epiphenomenalism: all phenomenal judgments are causally disconnected from consciousness, so absence-judgments are as false or empty as presence-judgments.
- Phenomenal-concept strategy: special phenomenal concepts causally anchor judgments about presence, but absence-judgments lack such anchoring and are structurally unreliable.
- Functionalist deflationism: judgments about absence are structurally coupled to physical states and can be veridical, while judgments about presence are confabulated.
- Structural-mirroring view (inject this niche option): both presence and absence judgments are causally independent of phenomenal states yet structurally correlated with the underlying physical architecture, so absence-judgments can be true without consciousness playing any causal role.

For each unoccupied branch combination, briefly diagnose whether the gap is incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of positions that your diagnostic questions fail to cleanly separate, and say which question collapses them. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; the instrument should stand on its cases and branching logic alone.
