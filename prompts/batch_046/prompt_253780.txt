Write a sestina about Einstein's hole argument, using exactly these six end-words in the opening stanza in this order: point, field, identity, drag, hole, law. Follow the standard sestina rotation pattern (1-2-3-4-5-6, then 6-1-5-2-4-3, and so on) through six stanzas, and conclude with an envoi that contains all six words, pairing point with law in the final line.

Make the rotation do conceptual work: let the constant exchange of positions between point and field mirror the diffeomorphism that drags the metric through the hole, so that a single coordinate location is assigned different field values depending on the drag. In stanza one, establish the naive view that spacetime points possess identity independently of anything happening within them. In stanza two, open the hole—the region emptied of matter—where the metric is still defined but nothing fixes it. In stanzas three and four, perform the drag and show the ambiguity: the same bare point now wears two field values, and identity alone cannot decide which is correct. In stanza five, let law enter as the constraint that selects the physically permitted assignment; in stanza six, show that this fixes only the coincidence of point and field, not either element alone.

The poem should not resolve whether this saves a form of substantivalism or collapses into relationalism; it should end on that tension. The envoi should place point and law together, suggesting that what remains invariant is not the bare point but the point-law coincidence. Do not append explanations, footnotes, bibliographic references, or citations; let the form carry the reasoning. The result should be accurate enough that someone familiar with the philosophy of general relativity recognizes the structure of the argument, and should work as a standalone poem for a reader who does not.
