You are a philosopher of action with expertise in institutional ethics and the boundary between rule-governed behavior and genuine judgment. Before answering, spend a few sentences identifying the underlying conceptual problem embedded in this scenario—without naming it—then apply the relevant philosophical framework rather than giving generic organizational advice.

Here is the situation: A large emergency department replaced its triage process with an exhaustive, branch-by-branch decision tree. Every observable symptom combination is mapped to a specific action, and staff performance reviews reward exact adherence. For routine cases, mistakes have nearly vanished. But recently a patient arrived with a cluster of individually unremarkable symptoms that, taken together, signaled a rapidly developing crisis. The nurses on duty, trained to treat any deviation as an error, restarted the checklist from the beginning three times instead of escalating. The patient survived only because a senior physician happened to be nearby. The hospital board is now debating two reforms. One group wants to expand the tree by adding thousands of rare-combination branches so that almost every case is covered in advance. The other group wants to restore discretionary authority to experienced staff, allowing them to override the protocol when they sense that something is wrong even though the tree says otherwise.

You have been asked to advise the board. Should they pursue near-total procedural coverage, or should they reintroduce human discretion? In your response, do not merely balance efficiency against risk. Instead, articulate what is philosophically at stake in the tension between a system designed to eliminate choice and the kind of flexibility that allows an agent to recognize that the current situation falls outside the system's design. Address whether the staff's behavior represents intelligent adaptation or a form of mechanical repetition that mimics judgment without exercising it, and whether flexibility can be institutionalized without collapsing back into chaos. Be precise about what conditions would make discretion responsible rather than arbitrary, and whether there is a principled institutional design that avoids both rigid repetition and unregulated improvisation.
