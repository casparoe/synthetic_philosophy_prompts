After a long and somewhat exhausting argument with a close friend about whether panpsychism makes suffering physically ubiquitous, I am trying to determine where the actual conceptual work should go. She insists that the urgent priority is finding a non-circular physical criterion that distinguishes mere proto-phenomenal properties from states with negative valence in systems far simpler than brains—citing, in particular, certain non-equilibrium plasma configurations where decoherence asymmetry might, in principle, support a minimal form of integrated experience. I counter that this is premature: without first clarifying whether phenomenal properties can be coherently attributed to fundamental physical states at all, without collapsing into a full-blown ontology of consciousness, any attempt to localize suffering in stellar interiors or quantum fields risks being conceptually hollow.

Assume you are advising a small interdisciplinary working group—two philosophers of mind and one theoretical physicist—with three years of modest funding and no mandate for empirical experiments. They can pursue conceptual analysis, formal reconstruction of arguments, and framing papers, but they cannot run simulations or build detectors.

Given that constraint, which open questions or lines of work at the intersection of panpsychism, fundamental physics, and ethics should they prioritize, and in what order? I want a defended agenda rather than a survey: for each priority, explain why it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is specifically for this mix of philosophers and a physicist rather than for pure physicists or pure ethicists alone, and what kind of conceptual clarification over the next two to three years would cause you to reorder the list. Be explicit about the trade-offs between importance and tractability, identify what is overrated or should be deprioritized—especially the temptation to derive direct policy or moral prescriptions before the conceptual architecture is stable—and address whether the problem my friend raised about decoherence-linked valence deserves a place near the top or near the bottom.
