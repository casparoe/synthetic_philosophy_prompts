I've been arguing with my friends about where exactly the therapy/enhancement line should sit, and I'm realizing my gut instincts are all over the place. I'm not asking for the usual bioethics line about restoration to species-typical functioning, because that just kicks the can to "what counts as typical," and I want to see if there's a cleaner principle that actually does work. Here are my verdicts on actual cases. A cochlear implant for a deaf child: pure therapy, should be standard care. LASIK to fix severe myopia: therapy, totally fine. But LASIK for a pilot with 20/20 who wants 20/10: that feels like enhancement, though I don't think it should be banned, just... different ethically. Modafinil for diagnosed narcolepsy: therapy. Modafinil for a healthy grad student pulling an all-nighter: enhancement, and there's something unfair about it even if I can't pin it down. A prosthetic leg that restores normal walking after amputation: therapy. Carbon-fiber running blades for an amputee athlete: okay, but if an able-bodied runner used them to win a race that'd be cheating. Growth hormone for a kid with actual deficiency: therapy. For a kid who's just short but within two standard deviations: enhancement, and I think parents shouldn't do it. Vaccination: clearly preventive, not enhancement. Gene editing to remove a BRCA mutation: therapy. Gene editing embryos for a few extra IQ points: enhancement, and it feels wrong in a way I can't fully justify. The pair that really breaks me is steriod use: taking anabolic steriods to heal a torn ACL faster feels like legitimate medical therapy, but taking the exact same steriods to bulk up for aesthetics feels like pure enhancement that should be discouraged. Both move you away from your current biological baseline, yet I treat them totally differently. Is there one principle that saves all of these verdicts, or do I have to drop some? I'd especially like to know what your account predicts for a case I haven't considered: using an AI earpiece in real-time social situations to recall names, past conversations, and relevant facts, not because you have a memory disorder, but just to be more charismatic and network better. Is that therapy for social anxiety or enhancement of social capital? And please be honest about which of my intuitions your principle can't accomodate without becoming too ad-hoc.
