I spent my career in evolutionary biology studying genotype-phenotype mapping, and since retiring I have been trying to understand whether cultural values can be causes in the same empirical sense. I have constructed an argument about Weber's Protestant ethic thesis that seems to show cultural values cannot be causes at all, which strikes me as too strong. I would like you to tell me exactly where the reasoning slips.

(1) Weber presents the Protestant work ethic as a causal factor in modern capitalism: shared religious values shaped economic behavior in a way that explains a historical outcome.
(2) If values operate as empirical causes in the manner of biological factors, then societies with the same value-profile should produce the same economic phenotype unless an interfering environmental factor blocks the pathway.
(3) There are historical societies—certain Tokugawa merchant communities with ascetic, rationalized ethical codes, and some Confucian bureaucratic traditions—that possessed value-profiles structurally similar to the Protestant ethic, yet did not generate industrial capitalism, with no clearly identifiable interfering factor.
(4) A Weberian defender must therefore invoke an "institutional decoupling" mechanism: the same values can be present but decoupled from economic outcomes by surrounding institutions, just as genotype-phenotype decoupling occurs in evolutionary biology.
(5) But if institutional context can fully decouple values from outcomes, then values have no independent causal efficacy; they become causally inert, like neutral mutations with no phenotypic expression.
(6) Therefore either values are not decoupled—making Weber's thesis falsified by the counterexamples—or they are decoupled, rendering them causally powerless.
(7) Hence cultural values cannot be causes in any scientifically respectable sense.

This feels suspicious: I want to preserve (1) and (4), because I think culture matters and decoupling is real. But I cannot accept (7). Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Is there a hidden equivocation between causal independence and causal inertness, or does the analogy to genotype-phenotype mapping break down?
