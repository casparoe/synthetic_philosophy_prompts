If a Lewisian convention is sustained solely by mutual expectation and a conditional preference for conformity, then any agent who gradually deviates—say, by adopting a slightly modified linguistic form—should seem irrational: she still expects others to maintain the old pattern, so her behavior fails to coordinate. Given this, how can norms evolve endogenously rather than only through discrete external shocks? I have been wondering whether a threshold of tacit tolerance, below which micro-deviations are ignored and expectations update incrementally, can preserve rationality within Lewis's equilibrium conditions, or whether such incremental updating necessarily violates the common-knowledge structure required to stabilize conventions in the first place.
