hey so i've been thinking about tranquilism — the idea that literally only the absence of craving and suffering matters, and positive joy or achievement are just neutral byproducts.

here are my intuitions on some lives, and i can't tell wether i'm being consistent:

case 1: a monk in deep jhana who reports zero suffering, zero craving, but also describes the state as blank — not blissful, just absence. i think this life is going maximally well.

case 2: an artist with huge creative highs but constant anxiety about the next project. she suffers and craves, but produces beautiful work. i think her life is going worse than the monk's, despite the peaks.

case 3: someone takes a drug that wipes out all craving and suffering but also flattens their personality and relationships. they sit peacefully but don't recognize their family. i want to say this is worse, but by tranquilist lights it sounds perfect.

case 4: a society that trains children to never form desires, so they grow up peaceful but never pursue love, art, or knowledge. they live long, calm lives. is this culture promoting wellbeing?

my tentative principle is 'only negative mental states matter; everything else is instrumental at best'. but then case 3 breaks it — i care about recognition and relationships — and case 4 breaks it because i care about richness. but maybe those cares are just leftover cravings?

so is there one principle that saves 1 and 2 and also explains why 3 and 4 feel wrong without just adding 'except for human connection'? please state it precisely, with the case that motivates each clause, its verdict on a brain-dead patient on life support who feels nothing and wants nothing, and an honest list of which of my four verdicts it has to overturn. or if you think there are two competing principles, lay them out with the same details. also, can a culture genuinely 'choose' tranquilism if choice itself requires some desire?
