I have recently returned to the philosophy of space and time after encountering a physicist’s claim that general-relativistic frame-dragging finally dissolves Newton’s bucket argument. Compare the substantivalist interpretation of rotational inertia—where the curvature of the water surface indicates rotation with respect to an independently existing spacetime manifold—with a contemporary dynamical relationist position that denies substantival space but explains the same curvature through the water’s dynamical relation to distant cosmic matter. Specifically: does this relationist strategy genuinely eliminate absolute structure, or does it merely relocate that structure into the global boundary conditions of the matter distribution? And can the substantivalist reply without begging the question? In your comparison, treat this not as exegesis of Newton’s own views but as a live conceptual dispute: identify the precise point at which the disagreement shifts from semantics about “space” to an empirical claim about whether rotational effects can be derived purely from internal matter relations, and say which structural feature of each account does the explanatory work.
