Here is an argument I have been turning over since encountering XOR blackmail, and it now looks to me like it proves too much. (1) A rational agent should care only about outcomes that are counterfactually dependent on her act, not about outcomes that are merely evidentially correlated with it. (2) In counterfactual mugging, the predictor's payout is evidentially linked to my taking the cash, yet counterfactually fixed once the prediction is made; hence I should refuse. (3) XOR blackmail structures the mechanism so that the harm avoided is determined by a prior XOR gate over the blackmailer's randomisation, making my actual payment counterfactually independent of which victim is spared, regardless of branch. (4) Applying (1) to XOR blackmail therefore yields that I should never pay, even when the evidential expected utility strongly favours payment. (5) But (1) is just causal decision theory in full dress; if it governs XOR blackmail, it governs Newcomb's problem, transparent prisoner's dilemmas, and every predictor-linked case that evidential decision theory was built to capture. (6) So evidential reasoning is never licensed, which seems like a reductio rather than a feature. I want to keep (1) if I can, and I think (3) correctly describes the XOR structure, but I suspect I have smuggled in an equivocation between token-level and type-level counterfactual dependence. Which step should I reject, what does each rejection cost, and is there a principled way to let evidential reasoning survive in Newcomb-style cases without letting it back into XOR blackmail?
