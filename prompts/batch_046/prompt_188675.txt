The debate over W.V.O. Quine’s metaethical commitments has persisted for decades without resolution, despite broad agreement on the texts and philosophical context. One camp reads Quine as a moral naturalist who, in line with his physicalism and his rejection of the analytic-synthetic distinction, treats normative judgments as ultimately reducible to descriptive claims about human behavioral dispositions and evolutionary utility. The opposing camp reads him as a non-cognitivist who regards moral discourse as an expressive, pragmatic overlay on a scientific world-picture that contains no values in its ontology, analogous to his treatment of modal operators as useful but ontologically dispensable fictions. Both sides appeal to the same sparse remarks in Quine’s writings on ontology and language; both accept his holism, his behaviorism about meaning, and his insistence that science is the sole arbiter of reality; and both concede that Quine never produced a systematic ethics. Nevertheless, neither camp has moved.

My working theory is that the impasse is not primarily textual but a hidden disagreement about the scope of Quine’s criterion of ontological commitment—specifically, whether normative properties must be quantifiable over in the canonical formulation of science to count as real, or whether they can be accommodated as a pragmatic layer that guides inquiry without entering the ontology. To make this precise, I have been using a distinction between canonical notation commitment and pragmatic overlay commitment. But I am unsure whether this captures the real fault line. Perhaps the disagreement is instead about whether ethical naturalism must be reductionist, with Quine’s texts serving as a Rorschach test for prior commitments. Or perhaps the question itself is ill-posed: Quine may have had no unified metaethical view, and the expectation that he did is a category mistake.

I want an honest diagnosis rather than a survey. Is the disagreement verbal—different uses of “naturalism” or “ontological commitment”? Is it substantive—a disagreement about whether physicalism can accommodate norms without reduction? Is it a clash of bedrock intuitions about whether science can include values? Or is there a hidden formal question—for instance, about whether Quine’s criterion of ontological parsimony applies uniformly across domains—that neither side has made explicit? Given your diagnosis, what would actually advance the debate: the canonical-versus-pragmatic commitment distinction I sketched, a clarification of Quine’s pragmatism regarding normative language, or an argument that no resolution is possible because Quine deliberately avoided metaethics?
