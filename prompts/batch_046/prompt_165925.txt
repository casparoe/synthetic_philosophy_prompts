In an interdisciplinary reading group on long-term societal change, debate about whether the Eastern Mediterranean around 1200 BCE, the Classic Maya lowlands around 900 CE, and Easter Island represent genuine societal collapses or rapid restructuring has stalled despite broad agreement on the archaeological record. The discontinuists argue that when palace economies, writing systems, and long-distance exchange networks disappear, the institutional society that sustained them has collapsed, regardless of whether rural populations continue subsistence farming. The transformationists counter that Easter Island's settlement persisted long after deforestation, that Postclassic Maya polities re-emerged within centuries, and that labeling these episodes "collapse" confuses the dissolution of elite superstructures with societal death; they propose instead that we are observing institutional decoupling, in which central administration and monumental construction cease but local sociotechnical metabolism—kin networks, agricultural practice, craft traditions—remains largely intact. Both sides accept the same evidence: abandonment of ceremonial centers, regional demographic contraction in some zones, continuity of domestic architecture elsewhere, and sharply reduced monument production. They also agree that the term "collapse" carries normative weight and that complete societal disappearance is rare. Yet for over a year they have repeated the same comparative examples without convergence. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? A hidden empirical dispute about which indicators—demographic, institutional, or ecological—should dominate our assessment? A conceptual disagreement about whether collapse requires the breakdown of systemic feedback loops or merely the loss of a specific political form? Or are the two sides answering different questions—one about the fate of states, the other about cultural and economic continuity—under a single label? Given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a formal taxonomy that separates institutional decoupling from systemic metabolic failure, a comparative metric that applies uniformly across Bronze Age, Maya, and Easter Island cases, or is the impasse a sign that "collapse" names not one phenomenon but a family of processes that should not be unified?
