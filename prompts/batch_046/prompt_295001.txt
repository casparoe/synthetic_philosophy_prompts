people always say structural realism is like using a map: you dont need to know what the ground actually feels like or its intrinsic color, you just need the relational layout — roads, distances, topology — and thats all science gives us about reality. philosophers use it to defend the view against objections that we're missing the real nature of things, saying we're just keeping the floorplan and throwing out the furniture. i want a full audit of how far the map analogy holds. lay out the correspondance: what's the territory, the map, the cartographer, the legend, the scale? then the breaks: does science tell us anything about the 'paper' the map is printed on — the ontology of the relations themselves? now inject this twist: map projections like Mercator versus Robinson preserve different relations by distorting others. does structural realism have any ground for saying one scientific framework is closer to the true structure, or does the analogy push us toward saying any internally consistent projection is equally good? does that kill the realist bit? also, scientific theories work at wildly different resolutions — quantum fields versus evolutionary trees. does the map analogy smuggle in a single correct scale? tell me whether the analogy survives these disanalogies or whether it's been carrying the whole argument.
