Read Michel Gondry’s film Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind through the lens of the Orwellian versus Stalinesque models of retrospective revision in conscious experience, as these are standardly introduced via the colour-phi phenomenon and debates about the timing of awareness. In the film, Lacuna Inc. erases Joel’s memories of Clementine, yet during the procedure he attempts to conceal her within apparently unrelated memories, producing a non-linear, fragmentary stream in which earlier and later moments seem to interpenetrate. Does the film present this erasure as an Orwellian rewriting of Joel’s past experiences themselves — so that what he once lived through is genuinely altered — or rather as a Stalinesque airbrushing of the mental record, leaving an untouched substrate that resists access? Focus on the beach-house sequence and the final train-platform scene: do these suggest that emotional attachment can retroactively determine the content of an earlier conscious moment, analogously to how colour-phi subjects report seeing an intermediate colour before the movement that triggers it? And does the film’s structure — memories collapsing backward through time as they are destroyed — illuminate, or obscure, the claim that later neural events can revise the phenomenal content of earlier ones?

Please treat the philosophical distinction seriously rather than reducing the film to an illustration, and say where the film resists this reading. I am preparing this for a philosophy of mind seminar, so I want a reading that takes both the narrative and the theory seriously, and that concludes by saying what the film contributes to the debate that pure argument does not — for instance, whether the dramatisation of emotional resistance to erasure favours one model, or whether it reveals a difficulty shared by both.
