I retired from a biochem lab about three years back and have been reading philosphy in my spare time, mostly ethics and mind stuff. I'm stuck on hedonism and what actually ties all pleasures together.

Here are my gut calls on five situations. First, tasting a really well-made sourdough -- warm, physical, clear pleasure. Second, that click when your data finally fits the model after months of work; it's satisfying but doesn't feel like it's in my mouth or anywhere physical -- still pleasure, I think. Third, floating in a pool on a hot day with nothing to do; pleasant but diffuse and passive. Fourth, imagine a brain-computer interface that just feeds a mild, continuous good-feeling signal straight into the reward pathway, with no task or object attached -- I think that's pleasure too, maybe even the pure kind. Fifth, the little jolt you get when a dishonest competitor's grant gets rejectd; there's definitely positive feeling, but I want to call it not real pleasure, or at least not the kind that matters for well-being.

So my pattern is: 1 through 4 are in, 5 is out. But that feels messy. Cases 1 and 5 share intensity and a kind of bodily hit, yet I split them. Cases 2 and 4 share almost no felt quality, yet I keep both. Is there one principle that saves all four being pleasures while excluding 5? Or do I have to drop one of my verdicts? Also, what would it say about the long, backround satisfaction of keeping a garden for twenty years -- no peaks, just a low hum that's barely noticeable day to day?
