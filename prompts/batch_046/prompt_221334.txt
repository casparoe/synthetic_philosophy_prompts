So I'm trying to figure out what philosophical commitments this actually presupposes. Does laïcité only make sense if you think religious identity is a private preference that should be invisible in public, rather than a public good that deserves recognition? Or is it more about preventing domination, like making sure no kid feels pressured by majority religion? And what about the calendar inconsistency - does that show the practice secretly presupposes a specific cultural Christianity it won't admit, or is it just pragmatic? If you replaced the underlying theory with, say, a multicultural recognition model or a strict neutrality model, what parts of the French system would have to collapse and what could stay? I don't need citations or anything, just want to see if the practice is actually philosophically coherent or just a mishmash that happens to fit French history.
