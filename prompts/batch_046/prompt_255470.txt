I'm a novelist drafting a book about labor and rest across three timelines, and I'm trying to get my characters' moral instincts straight — not for plot, but so their arguments feel real. Here are my gut verdicts on cases, and I want to see what principle, if any, holds them together.

The 1936 French worker who strikes for paid holidays and a shorter week — he's right; leisure is a social right won through collective action. The Tokyo salaryman in the 80s who dies of karoshi after months of unpaid overtime — the company is wrong, but it's also wrong that anyone frames his exhuastion as a failure of will. The Weberian Protestant elder who tells a tired farmer that idle hands are the devil's workshop — wrong; that ethic confuses productivity with virtue. The freelancer who chooses 90-hour weeks to finish her manuscript — that's autonomous choice, not exploitation. But the gig worker who takes every shift because rent eats 70% of his income, leaving no time for his kid — wrong, even though he 'chooses' each shift. The trust-fund heir who never works and just travels — I don't think society owes him labor, but I also don't think he has a 'right' to leisure in the same thick moral sense as the striker.

The tension is between coercion and choice, but both the freelancer and the gig worker 'choose,' and they feel totally different. Meanwhile the heir breaks the coercion frame entirely. Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? Maybe something about whether rest restores one's capacity for autonomy, or whether labor is extracted under conditions of structual necessity? And what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on — a character who refuses a promotion that would double his salary but kill his weekends, telling his family they're being greedy? Is he asserting a right, or just being lazy?

Please tell me what principle captures the pattern, what it predicts for a case I haven't listed, and be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save. Also, say what would change your mind — which argument or consideration would lead you to overturn the principle, and where is your reasoning most likely to fail here?
