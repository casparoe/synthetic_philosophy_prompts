Hey, I'm a retired biologist — spent about 25 years in a lab studying enzyme kinetics — and since I stopped working I've been trying to read philosophy of biology on my own for fun. I'm probably in over my head, but I was arguing with my book club about what makes something a "function" and I cooked up this arguement that feels like it proves way too much. Maybe you can spot where I'm cheating.

1. Etiological accounts say a trait's proper function is fixed by what it was selected for in evolutionary history.
2. Causal-role accounts say it's fixed by what teh trait actually does right now that helps the system work.
3. Now picture a synthetic organism built from scratch in a lab — call it Synthos — with a heart-like pump engineered to move fluid around. No evolutionary ancestors at all.
4. By (1), that pump has no proper function, just an accidental effect.
5. By (2), it has the same proper function as any heart: circulating fluid.
6. So far that's just the standard disagreement, but here's where I think I drive it off a cliff. Take a natural heart that evolved for circulation but in one individual also accidentally detoxifies something because of a weird mutation.
7. The causal-role theorist might say detoxification is now part of its function since it contributes.
8. The etiological theorist says no, proper function is still circulation; detox is just a lucky side effect.
9. But if teh etiological theorist allows that distinction — proper function vs lucky side effect — then they need to explain why teh synthetic pump's circulation isn't also "just a lucky side effect" of its design.
10. After all, design intent is just another historical story, not different in kind from natural selection, so either every historical story (evolution or engineering) determines proper function and causal-role is always wrong, or current contribution determines it everywhere and evolutionary history never matters.
11. That can't be right, because both theories want to say they're partly right. So one of these steps has to be garbage.

Where am I going wrong? Is step 9 unfair? Or am I missing something obvious about how artifacts recieve functions? I feel like there might be a standard reply involving "derived" functions or designer intent, but I can't tell if that's actually principled or just hand-waving. Also, is there an actual name for this kind of "no evolutionary history" synthetic test case people use? I don't want a reading list, just the diagnosis and maybe the term if there is one.
