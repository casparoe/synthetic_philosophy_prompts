I heard a podcast summary of Jeffery conditonalization — the update rule for when your evidence is graded, like seeing a blurry photo or thinking "it probably rained." The host said you split the world into a partition, shift your credances over the cells based on experience, but lock the internal ratios inside each cell — that's rigidity. He claimed Jeffery proved this is really just Bayes' rule in disguise, as long as you treat your experience as a likelihood function over the partition. He also said Jeffery allowed the cells to overlap for vague evidence — he called it a "fuzzy partition" — and that rigidity prevents you from ever learning counterfactual dependencies, which is why decision theorists reject the rule completely. Is this fair? Can you separate what's accurate, what's a common simplification, and what Jeffery or his defenders would deny?
