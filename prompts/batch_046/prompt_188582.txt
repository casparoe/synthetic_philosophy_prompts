I am preparing a short research proposal—roughly one semester, about five thousand words—on an underexplored tension in Gödel's philosophical theology. The familiar picture treats his ontological argument for God and his private conviction that there is an afterlife as products of the same rationalist metaphysics: a plenitude of positive properties, modal realism about essence, and the idea that rational beings must survive bodily death because non-existence is a negative property incompatible with perfection. What has not been pressed is whether that afterlife conviction can survive a specifically Gödelian internal objection. If the reasoning capacities of a rational being can be modeled within a formal system rich enough to represent judgments about positive properties and personal identity, then that system's incompleteness—its inability to prove its own consistency—might generate a parallel limitation: the being could not fully justify, within the resources of that system, the claim that it persists after death, even if the claim is true. I want the proposal to do four things. First, formulate the tension precisely, not as a biographical curiosity but as a philosophical problem about the limits of rational self-certification. Second, explain why the standard separation of Gödel's logic from his theology makes this problem invisible. Third, lay out the method: a close reconstruction of the modal argument for personal survival implicit in his philosophical notebooks, paired with an explicit formal analogy to the second incompleteness result, followed by an assessment of whether the analogy collapses because mental properties are not arithmetizable or because personal identity is not a formalizable predicate. Fourth, define success (a clear verdict on whether the rationalist basis of Gödel's afterlife belief is undermined by its own logical framework) and failure (the formalization proves impossible or irrelevant), with a realistic month-by-month plan. Begin with the proposal text itself, offering no preface, no commentary, and no restatement of the question.
