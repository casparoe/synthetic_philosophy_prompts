I'm giving the opening remarks tomorrow at a workshop on Future Ethics—audience is split between longtermists who treat the year 3000 like an emergency and sociologists who think that's cultish. I want the talk to turn on the simulation argument, but specifically the ancestor-simulation version where we're replayed for study rather than created as descendants. The idea should do real work: if there's a decent chance our "deep future" is just a historical record being watched by people who already know the ending, then the usual longtermist fanaticism—treating present suffering as a rounding error against astronomical payoff—starts to look like misplaced certainty about a script we aren't writing. I want the speech to say this shouldn't kill concern, but should definitely dampen the kind of moral absolutism that makes the room roll its eyes.
