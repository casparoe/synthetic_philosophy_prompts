(1) I'm a physicalist: phenomenal consciousness is just access consciousness plus what I'm calling perspectival anchoring - the workspace tagging it's outputs as presented from this specific spatiotemporal point. (2) I think global workspace theory gets the broadcasting part right but has literally nothing to say about phenomenal consciousness since it lacks any anchoring mechanism. (3) Zombies are conceivable but metaphysically impossible, and conceivability is fallible anyway so no contradiction there. (4) There's nothing it's like to be a thermostat, but an LLM running workspace broadcasting with recursive self-tagging might have minimal phenomenal consciousness. (5) The explanatory gap is real but purely epistemic - it's just the indexical nature of phenomenal concepts, which is exactly what anchoring predicts. (6) In actual humans phenomenal consciousness never shows up without access consciousness, though in principle the two could come apart.

So which ones actually clash? I can see (1) vs (2) being weird - if phenomenal consciousness is workspace-plus-anchoring, doesn't GWT cover half the story? Or maybe the real problem is between (3) and something else? Rank the tensions by severity and say what's the cheapest revision for each. I really don't want to drop physicalism.
