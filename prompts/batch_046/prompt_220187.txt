After a career in experimental physics I have recently begun studying philosophy in earnest, and I find myself both attracted to and suspicious of the modern Stoic revival, approaching it with the instinct of a scientist who wants to know not merely whether an idea sounds wise but exactly what observation or argument would force him to abandon it. I have arrived at a particular interpretation of Epictetus’s dichotomy of control that I think diverges from both the metaphysical libertarians and the determinists who each try to recruit Stoicism for their camp: I hold that the dichotomy is not an ontological boundary separating internal free causes from external determined effects, but rather a pragmatic heuristic calibrated to what I call proximal authorship, by which I mean the class of outcomes for which one’s own deliberative processes constitute the most salient and immediately modifiable causal node, regardless of whether those processes are themselves fully determined. On this reading, the therapeutic power of Stoicism derives not from a claim that we are uncaused causes, but from the claim that emotional investment should be restricted to the zone of highest cognitive leverage—a zone that contemporary neuroscience suggests is real but narrower than intuition assumes. I want you to tell me, in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, boldface, or tables, what would genuinely count against this position: which empirical findings in cognitive science or clinical psychology might show that emotional well-being tracks beliefs about global causation rather than local leverage; whether there are passages in Epictetus or Marcus Aurelius that resist being read as pragmatic heuristics and instead commit them to a robust metaphysical dualism of internal and external; and whether my framing has been qualified into unfalsifiability, such that any failure of Stoic practice can be dismissed as misapplication rather than a defeat of the view. Please rank these defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish those that a committed defender of my pragmatic reading would themselves concede as damaging from those they would dismiss as missing the point, and conclude with an honest judgment about whether the position is genuinely exposed or whether its insulation has become a philosophical vice. Write this as a sustained letter, not as a structured report.
