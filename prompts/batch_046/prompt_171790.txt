I'm writing for the discussion board in my medieval philosophy course, where we've been working through Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism in the Tahāfut al-Falāsifa. I've noticed a recurring defensive maneuver that I don't have a name for, and I want to check whether it is an established diagnosis or just my own framing.

The pattern works like this. First, the occasionalist insists that fire burns cotton only because God creates the burning directly, denying fire any intrinsic causal power. Second, when asked why burning accompanies fire reliably rather than arbitrarily, the occasionalist appeals to a "customary course" (ʿādah) that God has established—suggesting there is a structured regularity in creation that makes the pairing predictable and non-arbitrary. At this stage, ʿādah sounds like it is doing real explanatory work: it accounts for the consistency of nature without requiring God to decide each instance in isolation. But third, if one presses that this regularity sounds like a secondary cause, the occasionalist strips ʿādah back to a bare correlation or occasion (sabab), insisting it has zero efficacy. Fourth, once that pressure subsides, the defender treats the customary course again as if it genuinely explains the pairing, rather than treating every burning as an isolated miracle.

So the move is not simply the standard occasion/efficient-cause distinction. It is an oscillation within the defense itself: loading explanatory weight onto the customary pairing to answer the regularity objection, then unloading it to avoid attributing causal power to creatures, then reloading it for predictive and explanatory purposes. Is there an established term for this specific oscillation—perhaps in the literature on Al-Ghazālī or in philosophy of causation? If not, how should we precisely characterize its structure: is it an equivocation on "explanation," a strategic ambiguity in the concept of ʿādah, or something closer to a motte-and-bailey? And what are its boundary conditions—how does it differ from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis that saves occasionalism by adding structure without attributing power?
