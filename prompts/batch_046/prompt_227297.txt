In a philosophy-of-science reading group discussing whether Bayesian confirmation theory can account for the evidential weight of archival astronomical observations—photometric plates taken in the 1950s that only recently became relevant to a 2023 dark-matter halo model—someone kept invoking the line "old evidence is no evidence" to dismiss the idea that those plates could confirm the new model. I need a precise disambiguation for the next session.

Please lay out, as a numbered menu, the distinct precise theses that slogan could express. Cover at least: (1) the formal Bayesian claim that if a datum E is already in the background knowledge K, then conditionalizing on E does not change the probability of hypothesis H; (2) a normative scientific-rationality claim that only predictions made before observing E can genuinely confirm H (a predictive-novelty condition); (3) a psychological claim that human belief revision is triggered only by surprising or newly encountered information, so old data cannot cause rational belief change; (4) a metaphysical or temporal-direction claim that evidential support runs only from present observation to future belief, not backward from past records; (5) a methodological-pragmatic claim that re-analyzing old archives is always epistemically suspicious, regardless of formal probabilities, because of selection effects or data-quality issues specific to legacy records; and (6) a more radical "no accommodation" thesis that any hypothesis constructed to fit already-known data receives zero confirmatory weight, even if the fit was not deliberately engineered.

For each reading, classify it as trivial, substantive and defensible, substantive but false, or conceptually confused, and briefly justify the classification.

Then, specifically for my colleague's use—dismissing 1950s plate data for a 2023 model—say which of these readings actually support that dismissal and which do not. In particular, distinguish the case where the astronomical community genuinely knew and incorporated those measurements into K long before 2023 from the case where the plates sat unexamined in an archive, unknown to the modelers, so that E was not in anyone's epistemic background when H was proposed. Note whether introducing non-rigid priors or allowing background knowledge to be partitioned by epistemic community rather than by global omniscience changes the verdict.

Finally, state which reading my colleague most likely intended, whether that reading is defensible, and whether it genuinely undermines the archival confirmation claim.

Verdict: summarize in one sentence which readings are live and whether the slogan succeeds in dismissing the archival evidence.
