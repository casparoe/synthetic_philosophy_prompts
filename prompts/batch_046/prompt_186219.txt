I run a small online forum for people who have never studied philosophy formally but keep arguing about what being free actually means. I would like you to design a sorting quiz—about eight concrete scenarios, each with two or three answer options—that places readers according to whether they mainly object to being physically prevented, to lacking the real means or capacity to act, or to living under another person's arbitrary control even when that control is never exercised. The cases should feel slightly strange and modern rather than like textbook examples: for instance, an algorithm that quietly hides job adverts from certain users without notifying them; an employment contract with a buried clause that makes resignation financially ruinous despite technically allowing it at any time; a community garden where one neighbour holds an unused veto over every planting decision; and a city where public transport is technically free but runs so rarely that only people with private vehicles can actually commute. Each question should ask what genuinely bothers the reader, phrased without using the technical labels negative, positive, or republican liberty. Then provide a scoring key that maps patterns of answers onto those three positions, built from the logical combinations rather than from a list of named theorists. Please also note any answer combinations that seem logically possible but do not match any recognised view—such as caring intensely about arbitrary power while being completely indifferent to whether people have the resources to do anything—with a brief diagnosis of whether that gap is probably incoherent, merely unpopular, or simply unexplored. Write the whole response in British English, keeping the tone as though you are helping a thoughtful friend build this instrument for a non-specialist audience.
