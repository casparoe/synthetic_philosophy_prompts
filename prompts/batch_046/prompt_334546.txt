Consider a hypothetical ontology in which all physical dynamics unfold within a substrate termed the careenium, characterized by continuous, non-representational state transitions that lack intrinsic semantic structure. Within this substrate, discrete representational entities—called simmballs—are realized as stable configurational patterns. Simmballs carry combinatorial content, can enter into recursive relations with other simmballs, and are often described as "pushing" the careenium into particular trajectories. The central question is whether this talk of pushing is merely metaphorical—simmballs being epiphenomenal descriptions of careenium behavior—or whether simmballs genuinely exert downward causal influence on the substrate that realizes them.

Please argue for the position that simmballs do causally push the careenium around, without invoking dualism, non-physical forces, or the claim that symbols possess intrinsic physical efficacy independent of their realization. Your argument should explicitly address three points. First, explain how a simmball's internal combinatorial structure can constrain the available state-space of the careenium in a way that is not reducible without remainder to the substrate's local physical laws alone. Second, defend the causal relevance of simmballs against the objection that every symbolic event already has a complete physical description in purely careenium terms, and therefore adding "the simmball caused it" is redundant. Third, respond specifically to the analogy that attributing causal power to a simmball is like attributing causal power to the average temperature of a gas: distinguish, within your framework, between descriptive redundancy and causal redundancy, and show why the simmball case falls on the causally effective side of that distinction. Keep the reasoning self-contained; do not invoke named authors, texts, or empirical studies.
