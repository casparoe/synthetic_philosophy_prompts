My surgeon is recommending removal of my appendix during an upcoming abdominal procedure. She argues it is vestigial: evolutionary selection shaped it for fiber digestion, modern diets have made that obsolete, and because I recently received a fecal microbiota transplant, my gut flora are now entirely donor-derived with no co-evolved symbiotic history to preserve. I counter that regardless of evolutionary origin, the appendix currently serves as a protected reservoir that repopulates my intestine with these transplanted bacteria after antibiotic courses, so excising it would destroy a functioning ecological backup. I am not seeking clinical advice, but conceptual clarity: is our disagreement merely semantic—about what we mean by the organ’s “purpose”—or does it expose a substantive philosophical dispute over whether biological function is determined by evolutionary history or by present causal contribution to the system? And does the fact that my microbiome was artificially replaced rather than inherited alter which framework should govern the judgment? How should I think through what is genuinely at stake?
