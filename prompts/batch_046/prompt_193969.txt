Write an essay addressing the following philosophical controversy: is it possible to defend a restricted version of the KK principle—that knowing p entails knowing that one knows p—against the anti-luminosity argument by drawing a sharp distinction between phenomenal luminosity and epistemic luminosity? Use the standard gradual-transition scenario—such as the imperceptible shift from feeling cold to feeling warm—as your central test case. Your essay should examine whether this distinction genuinely preserves self-knowledge for mental states or merely relocates the epistemic difficulty, assess whether it avoids collapsing into an implausible infallibilism, and reach a justified conclusion about whether any modified luminosity thesis remains philosophically viable.
