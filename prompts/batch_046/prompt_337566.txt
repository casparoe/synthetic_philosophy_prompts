I'm teaching a small grad seminar on Descartes — about 14 people, one 50 minute block. I want a classroom game that makes them feel the force of the cogito, but not the usual syllogism version they've seen a million times. Specifically, inject the framing that 'I think' works less like a hidden-premise inference and more like an indexical anchor you can't drop out of without leaving the conversation entirely. To keep it fresh, build the rules around a kind of bidding race: each player tries to commit to as little as possible — doubting every claim made by others — while still remaining in the dialog. Design rules, roles, materals, and a payoff scheme (no real money, just points or a silly prize) so they discover the floor themselves before anyone names it. They should bump into the fact that doubting one's own existence requires a speaker who hasn't already withdrawn. Include a debrief that maps moments in the game onto parts of the cogito, say what will predictibly go wrong the first run (probably people cheat with passive voice or generic pronouns), and be frank about which feature — maybe temporal continuity or substance dualism — the activity just can't reproduce and you'll have to supply yourself. Also, since I'm not totally sure this teaches the point rather than just feeling like a word game, tell me what argument or observation would change your mind, and flag where your reasoning is shakiest. Write casual, a typo or two is ok.
