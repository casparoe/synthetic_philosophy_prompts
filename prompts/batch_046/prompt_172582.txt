Below is a second-year examination question on Republic VI–VII, followed by a student’s short-answer response. I am grading it as an external examiner and want a point-by-point assessment; please do not state your own philosophical position.

Question: Compare Plato’s cave allegory (Republic VII) with the divided line (Republic VI). Explain how the four stages of cognition correspond to the four stages of the prisoner’s journey, identify which stage of the line corresponds to the prisoner’s first glimpse of the sun outside, and evaluate whether the return to the cave can be justified solely by the prisoner’s knowledge of the Form of the Good or whether Plato requires an additional premise about political obligation.

Student answer:
The divided line distinguishes eikasia, pistis, dianoia and noesis. In the cave, the prisoner begins in eikasia with the shadows, then reaches pistis upon seeing the puppets and the fire inside. Once outside, he first sees reflections in water and then trees and stars; these visible objects belong to dianoia, since they are grasped through reasoning about what lies beyond immediate appearance, before he finally reaches noesis and apprehends the Form of the Good, symbolised by the sun. The sun is therefore grasped by noesis, as it is the direct analogue of the Good itself. The return is justified because the philosopher alone knows justice through the Good, and Plato’s principle that each part must perform its proper function makes ruling structurally necessary; without it, the city remains in doxa, treating images as Forms in a dimmed state rather than as copies of physical things that themselves participate in the Forms.
