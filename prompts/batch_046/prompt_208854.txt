I am advising a small interdisciplinary research group—comprising three philosophers and one logician—who have secured two years of dedicated funding to examine the conceptual architecture of transcendental reasoning. Rather than a broad literature review, I need a rigorously defended agenda: which three or four open questions in this domain should command the majority of their collective effort, and which lines of inquiry ought to be treated as secondary, derivative, or premature?

The framing that has emerged in our preliminary discussions centres on a distinction that is often implicit but rarely scrutinised directly. On one hand stand ambitious transcendental arguments, which purport to derive substantive metaphysical or normative conclusions—such as the necessary existence of other minds, the objectivity of value, or the binding authority of logical norms—from conditions alleged to be unavoidable for any possible judgment or experience. On the other hand stand modest variants, which claim only to establish structural or conceptual conditions for coherence, without reaching beyond the framework of experience itself. The niche conceptual problem we wish to place at the centre of the agenda is whether this ambitious-modest boundary remains stable once one allows that the relevant "conditions" might not be static structures but dynamically revisable norms—what might be termed adaptive transcendental conditions—and whether formal tools drawn from non-monotonic reasoning can illuminate, or instead undermine, the anti-sceptical force traditionally claimed for such arguments.

Given this orientation, I ask for a prioritised ranking of the open questions that genuinely matter for this group's work. In particular: first, does the ambitious-modest distinction collapse under adaptive or non-monotonic reconstruction, or can it be reformulated to survive? Second, can transcendental reasoning justify intersubjective norms without circularly presupposing the very linguistic or social practices it seeks to ground? Third, which of these questions is most tractable for a team lacking specialised training in formal physics or empirical psychology, and which answers would carry the greatest downstream consequences for adjacent debates in epistemology and normative ethics?

Please defend your ordering explicitly. I want the criteria made transparent—whether upstream dependency on other questions, tractability within a two-year window, relative neglect within contemporary analytic philosophy, or potential to reshape related fields—and I want to know where limited resources would be misallocated. This is not a request for a bibliography, citation list, or survey of named scholars; it is a request for an agenda with reasoning.
