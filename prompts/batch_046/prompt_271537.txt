For a dissertation chapter on the ethics of posthumous representation, I am trying to stabilize the following package of commitments, and I need an audit before I present it.

(1) I accept a narrative-contract model of posthumous obligation: the dead are not Epicurean subjects of harm, but there is a non-instrumental wrong in corrupting the socially constituted arc of meaning they were constructing with their community—especially when a dramatization imports anachronistic moral frameworks or attributes decisive public actions incompatible with the best archival evidence.

(2) At the same time, I hold that the primary evaluative norm for biopic and historical fiction is hermeneutic rather than archival: the work succeeds ethically and aesthetically when it renders the subject’s choices intelligible to present audiences, and this often requires chronological compression, composite figures, invented dialogue, or the translation of historical moral categories into contemporary ones.

(3) For figures whose lives are structurally under-documented—particularly those erased by colonial, patriarchal, or class-based archival practices—I think imaginative reconstruction of interior life, moral framework, and social context is not merely permitted but ethically required, because leaving them silent perpetuates the very erasure that produced the sparse record.

(4) I also maintain that the standard of justification for deviation should scale inversely with documentary density, but that it applies uniformly across the inner/outer boundary: inventing an unrecorded private doubt corrupts the narrative contract no less than inventing an unrecorded public betrayal, and there is no special moral license for psychological speculation.

(5) Finally, I deny that filmmakers bear any special epistemic responsibility to audiences: if a viewer mistakes dramatic license for biography, the error belongs to the viewer’s failure of genre literacy, not to the artist.

Please examine these for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be? For each real tension, rank it by severity, specify what has to give and at what cost, and identify the cheapest fix. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most conflicts, and whether that revision is one I should actually make or whether it costs too much of what I care about. Be specific about where the usual motivations for (1) and (2) pull in opposite directions, and about how (3) interacts with (4) once the apparent conflict between them dissolves.
