My opening move: Consider a nested-shell universe—two concentric, massive, hollow spherical shells in an otherwise empty asymptotically flat spacetime. By the Lense-Thirring effect, the rotating outer shell drags inertial frames in the gap, so the water should exhibit a parabolic surface even though it is not rotating relative to the distant stars, while a second bucket placed inside the stationary inner shell—where frame-dragging is negligible—remains flat despite being enclosed by rotating machinery.
