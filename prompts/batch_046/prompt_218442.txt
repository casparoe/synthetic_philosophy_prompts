i've been thinking about Bostrom's unilateralist's curse — the thing where if anyone can act on their own judgment about some big collective risk, the people with the worst estimates end up causing the harm because there's no coordination forcing them to stop. I'm trying to apply this to a weird scenario: a decentralized open-source AI project where any contributor can push a "safety patch" that restricts how the system answers philosophical or conceptual prompts, with no central gatekeeper. Some contributors think letting the model discuss ethics freely is extremely risky, others think the risk is near zero and over-filtering kills usefulness. Given that each person can unilaterally merge their own restrictions, what are the most important implications of the curse for how this kind of project should organize its governance?

I especially want the parts about where individual judgment diverges wildly even among people with similar technical backgrounds — and whether the curse gets worse when the domain is fuzzy, like "philosophical reasoning," compared to something concrete like chemical synthesis instructions. I was discussing this with friends after a course on tech governance, and someone said open-source is naturally self-correcting, but that feels wrong to me. Don't need citations or a bibliography, just the concrete structural implications, especially the ones that wouldn't apply if everyone shared the same risk model.
