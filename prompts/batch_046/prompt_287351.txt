I serve on the review board for our university's Foundational Physics and Philosophy of Science grant program. We have received a proposal titled "Point-Identity Locking for Numerical Relativity: Resolving the Hole Ambiguity In Silico." The applicants—an interdisciplinary team of relativists and metaphysicians—propose to implement a persistent labeling scheme for grid points in numerical simulations of black-hole mergers. Their claim is that without such labels, the simulation's output is underdetermined in exactly the manner Einstein's hole argument describes: any diffeomorphism applied inside a region that avoids the initial data slice produces an observationally equivalent but point-distinct solution, so the computer cannot uniquely determine which point is which across iterations. They argue that adding a non-dynamical point-ID layer—essentially a substantivalist tagging system—restores determinacy and makes the simulation scientifically tractable, even if it violates pure diffeomorphism invariance at the computational level.

Half the board sees this as a legitimate methodological fix: simulations already rely on coordinate choices, and explicit tagging is just making that choice transparent. The other half argues that the proposal mistakes a feature of the formalism for a bug—Einstein's argument was precisely that such point-identity has no physical meaning, so baking it into the ontology of the simulation commits the category error of treating mathematical artifacts as physical entities. They worry that funding this would endorse a metaphysically confused framework and set a bad precedent for how our program treats the boundary between physics and philosophy.

Please draft a decision memo for the board—approve, approve with conditions, or reject—giving clear reasoning that addresses both the scientific utility of deterministic labeling and the philosophical objection rooted in the hole argument. The memo should include an honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side, and should recommend specific conditions or safeguards if the decision is not an outright rejection. Do not include a bibliography or suggested reading list; the board needs actionable reasoning, not references.
