Assume Albert Einstein—working from the economic and political commitments he articulated in his late-1940s essay on socialism, including his critique of unplanned capitalist production, his defense of a democratically governed planned economy, and his explicit warning that centralized bureaucracy risked becoming an authoritarian caste—were alive today and had studied the current debates over fully automated luxury communism, AI-driven algorithmic resource allocation, and decentralized autonomous organizations as mechanisms of economic coordination. How would he likely assess whether these technologies resolve the contradiction he identified between productive efficiency and democratic control, or whether they merely displace economic anarchy into an opaque, algorithmic form? In particular, address: first, whether AI-driven planning could satisfy his requirement for rational economic coordination without reproducing the bureaucratic dangers he feared; second, whether governance through decentralized autonomous organizations offers a genuine democratic alternative to both market and state planning, or instead collapses into a new plutocracy masked by technical neutrality; and third, what stance he might take on universal basic services funded by automated productivity, given his views on labor, human dignity, and the purpose of economic life. Do not treat Einstein as a mere precursor to any present-day ideology; instead, use the internal tensions in his own position—between scientific rationalism, democratic socialism, and institutional skepticism—to construct a view that is historically grounded yet not reducible to contemporary orthodoxies in either socialist or techno-libertarian discourse.
