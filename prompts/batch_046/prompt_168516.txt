I want to step back and ask what any adequate theory of non-human environmental causation in history actually has to deliver before I assess particular frameworks, whether they draw on historical sociology, actor-network approaches, or environmental history.

It must preserve space for differential human agential response—societies that adapt, resist, or collapse under apparently identical stress—so that causal contribution never becomes historical destiny. It must accommodate feedback loops in which human practices alter the environmental factor itself, breaking any pure one-way dependency. It must avoid attributing intentionality or strategic purpose to non-human forces, yet allow for something stronger than mere background conditioning—call it directional structural causality—without anthropomorphizing pathogens, atmospheric patterns, or magma systems. I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations; I want the specification of adequacy itself, because that is where the conceptual work lies.
