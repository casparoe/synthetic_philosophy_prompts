Write a villanelle — nineteen lines arranged in five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains (A1 and A2) and an ABA rhyme scheme — that treats democratic constitutional precommitment as a Ulysses contract. The first refrain, voiced by the present constituent power, should articulate the act of binding a future electorate through entrenchment clauses and supermajority rules; the second refrain, voiced by that future electorate, should articulate encountering those ropes as either protection or usurpation. Use the alternating structure so that each tercet advances the argument: the first four tercets should build the case for binding (the siren call of momentary majorities, the need for a mast, the knot tied by amendment procedures), and the volta should occur in the fourth tercet with the recognition that the future self is not merely tempted but genuinely other — a problem of democratic continuity rather than weakness of will. The final quatrain should bring both refrains together without resolving whether the ropes hold or are justly cut. Incorporate precise constitutional imagery — sunset provisions, judicial review as the knot, the supermajority as wax in the ears — rather than relying solely on nautical metaphor, though the mast and ropes should remain the central conceit. The poem should be philosophically accurate enough that a reader familiar with precommitment theory recognizes the tension between intertemporal rationality and democratic legitimacy, and should remain a coherent poem for a reader without that background. Do not resolve the paradox in the closing lines; do not provide a bibliography or cite specific authors; let the form’s compulsory repetition suggest that every generation re-enacts the same bind.
