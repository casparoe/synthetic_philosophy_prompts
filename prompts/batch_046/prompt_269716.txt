For my A-level philosophy coursework I am developing a position on non-human causes in history and should like a consistency audit before I finalise my essay. Please reply in British English. Here are my commitments.

(1) Pathogens, long-term climate shifts, and major volcanic eruptions ought to be analysed as genuine historical actors — entities that initiate causal sequences and reshape societies — rather than treated as passive background conditions.
(2) I reject environmental determinism in every form: I hold that human societies always retain the capacity for creative institutional and cultural adaptation, so ecological or geological pressures never mechanically dictate historical outcomes.
(3) Attributing agency to these forces does not entail attributing consciousness, intention, or moral accountability; agency here is structural and relational, not psychological.
(4) I think that recognising these entities as co-actors actually strengthens anti-determinism, because overdetermination — the intersection of microbial, climatic, volcanic, and human causal streams — breaks any single-line causal explanation.
(5) Nevertheless, I hold that when an outcome — for instance, the collapse of a settlement — is genuinely co-produced by a non-intentional volcanic winter or epidemic alongside human policy choices, assigning retrospective political responsibility to that society's leaders becomes conceptually unstable, since those leaders were not the sole or principal authors.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are in genuine tension, and which only appear to conflict but dissolve once the commitments are properly understood? Rank the real tensions by severity. For each, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision — giving up or substantially modifying one commitment — would clean up the most inconsistencies, and whether that cost is worth paying.
