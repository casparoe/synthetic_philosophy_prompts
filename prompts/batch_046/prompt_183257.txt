hey, I'm drafting a short essay for a philosophy-of-mind seminar on Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model and I'd like some comments. The usual take is that creativity is just turning knobs on a conceptual space (tempo, harmony, irony, etc.) and seeing what lands. I want to argue that's wrong in a specific way: the knobs are coupled like pendulums, not independent dials. Twist the 'irony' knob and the 'sincerity' axis doesnt stay put — it wobbles. So there's no fixed landscape you're searching over; the coordinate system itself deforms as you move.

My outline:
1. Recap the model (concept spaces with adjustable dimensions).
2. The misreading: creativity as search across a static landscape.
3. The coupling claim: dimensions are interdependent, so evaluation criteria shift with each adjustment.
4. Anti-reductionist punch: you can't break a creative act into independent feature-changes because the meaning of each feature depends on the current state of the others.
5. A worry: am I just describing context-sensitive evaluation? And is there any cognitive-science backing for literal coupling, or is this pure metaphor?

I'm especialy unsure about point 4 — does it actually distinguish Hofstadter from standard constraint-relaxation accounts, or am I just relabeling? And I dont want to sound like I'm claiming the brain literally has coupled pendulums.

Can you comment on accuracy, whether the distinction is interesting or muddy, and whether the anti-reductionist move holds up? No bibliography needed — just straight feedback. End with a one-sentence summary of your overall take.
