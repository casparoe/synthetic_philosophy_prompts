Design a final exam essay question for an upper-level philosophy of history seminar, answerable in 45 minutes and expecting about 500 words. The question should ask whether the macro-contrastive framing Why Britain and not China—central to Great Divergence debates and implicit in responses to the Needham question—is philosophically well-posed. Require students to go beyond summarizing Pomeranz, Landes, or standard economic-history narratives, and instead to examine the logic of civilizational-scale contrastive explanation: specifically, whether such questions presuppose an illicit determinism, whether they suffer from causal overdetermination when applied to whole societies, and whether reversing the explanatory burden (treating Chinese non-industrialization rather than British industrialization as the phenomenon needing explanation) exposes a hidden modal asymmetry in how counterfactual branches are weighted. Provide the exam question itself, a model answer, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points totaling 25 marks. For each point, state the marks, the specific analytical move a top answer must make, and a one-line note on the characteristic mistake that should cost marks. Do not include reading lists, citations, or bibliographic references.
