I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I am drafting a proposal for a one-semester term paper of approximately 6,000 words in epistemology. I want to examine whether Robert Nozick’s tracking conditions for knowledge—specifically the sensitivity and adherence requirements—are genuinely incompatible with epistemic closure, or whether they can accommodate a restricted closure principle that applies only to a specific class of entailments.

My precise research question is this: within Nozick’s counterfactual framework, can we distinguish “method-preserving” logical entailments, in which the belief-forming method that tracks the premise also tracks the conclusion, from “method-breaking” entailments, in which the derivation severs that counterfactual link? If so, does tracking support closure for the former while legitimately blocking it for the latter—particularly in sceptical scenarios—without collapsing into a generic reliabilist account?

The existing literature leaves this open because defenders of tracking typically reject closure globally or defend it with ad hoc qualifications, whereas closure advocates tend to abandon tracking altogether. Neither camp systematically asks whether the tracking apparatus itself contains the resources to discriminate among entailment types according to whether the original counterfactual stability is preserved.

My approach will be conceptual and taxonomic rather than historical or purely formal-logical. I will construct a framework that classifies entailments—logical, causal, and modal—by testing whether the derivation of the conclusion relies on the same counterfactual dependencies that secured knowledge of the premise. I will use intuitive case studies to assess whether a restricted closure principle can be stated precisely and defended against standard counterexamples, such as knowing one has hands but not knowing one is not a brain in a vat.

A successful result would be a clear, principled criterion for when tracking permits closure and when it blocks it, together with an honest assessment of whether the criterion is genuinely motivated or merely stipulative. The project fails if the distinction collapses into an unmotivated list of exceptions, or if preserving closure requires weakening the tracking conditions to the point where they lose their distinctive anti-luck character.

Given the one-semester scope, I will restrict the discussion to propositional knowledge of empirical claims and exclude higher-order knowledge, group epistemology, and detailed formal modal logic. I ask that the proposal state the question precisely, explain why current positions do not settle it, describe the conceptual method and why it is preferable to purely historical or formal-logical alternatives, specify what a result would look like and what would count as failure, and provide a realistic scope assessment.
