Prepare a one-year dissertation prospectus addressing Karl Popper's critique that historicism is impossible because social systems are open and shaped by the growth of knowledge itself. The existing literature mostly either repeats Popper's open-system objection or attempts to rescue historicism through statistical regularities in economics and demography. I want to explore a middle position neither side has developed: whether there can be conditionally closed regularities—structural patterns that hold only within bounded domains and only during periods when epistemic feedback is locally suppressed—that survive Popper's objections because they do not claim universality, yet still do predictive work that pure trend-statements cannot. The prospectus should state the precise research question, explain why standard Popperian and historicist positions leave this intermediate space open, propose a conceptual method (not an empirical study) for testing whether such regularities can be coherently formulated without collapsing into either universal laws or mere trends, specify a realistic month-by-month plan for twelve months, define what result would count as success and what would count as failure, and avoid any bibliography or citation list. Begin immediately with the prospectus's central claim about this intermediate position and its feasibility within one year, then present the reasoning, method, and timeline with no preface or restatement of this prompt.
