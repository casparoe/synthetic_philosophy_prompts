I have been thinking about freedom on my own and want to check whether my views actually cohere, because I am preparing to discuss them with friends who will press me. Here is roughly where I have landed.

First, I take the heart of freedom to be the absence of deliberate interference: if no person, law, or institution is actively preventing you from doing something, then in the basic sense you are free to do it. Whether you have the money, strength, or skill to do it strikes me as a separate matter of resources or ability, not liberty itself.

Second, I do think there is a deeper layer. Even when no one ever stops you, being under another person's arbitrary power feels like a real loss of freedom. I imagine a servant with a kind master who never issues a command: the servant still seems unfree in a way that a free laborer is not.

Third, I think this deeper layer only applies when the power is genuinely arbitrary. If authority is bound by clear, fair rules that everyone accepts, then being subject to it is different from being subject to a person's whim.

Fourth, I hold that resources, health, and education are not liberty but welfare or opportunity. A poor person facing no legal barriers is free, just poor; giving them money changes their welfare, not their freedom.

Fifth, despite that, I think extreme poverty can make someone genuinely unfree. If you have no shelter, no food security, and essentially no meaningful choices, that feels like coercion or imprisonment, not merely like lacking tools.

Sixth, I think the form of government does not determine freedom. A democracy that constantly restricts people seems less free than an authoritarian state that mostly leaves citizens alone, and a monarchy with strong legal limits on the ruler seems just as free as a republic.

Seventh, I have been testing these ideas against a scenario I invented. Imagine a society where every road has a toll, and the toll collectors are private citizens acting entirely within the law. The law says anyone may travel, but only the wealthy can pay. To me, the poor people in this society are genuinely unfree to travel, yet I also want to say that no one is arbitrarily exercising power over them and that no legal barrier blocks them.

My request is for an audit of these seven points. I want to know which pairs are in real tension, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled, and for each genuine tension how severe it is and what the least costly revision would be. Please rank the tensions rather than treating them all as equally serious. I am particularly unsure whether my second point conflicts with my sixth, since an authoritarian state that leaves people alone still holds arbitrary power, and whether my fourth point can survive my fifth and seventh together. Please respond in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, written as you would in a letter or email.
