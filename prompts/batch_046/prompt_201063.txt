Your argument should defend the specific claim that this reconceptualization transforms the rational structure of risk assessment—namely, that mitigating s-risks should be treated as lexically prior to standard expected-value optimization for positive outcomes, not because suffering outweighs happiness in some simple scalar asymmetry, but because preserving the coherence of normative topology is a precondition for any subsequent value calculus to be intelligible. Please address two objections in detail: first, that this framing illegitimately anthropomorphizes cosmic-scale processes by importing concepts of moral agency where they do not apply; and second, that even if granted, the view collapses into conventional risk aversion with an arbitrarily high coefficient rather than yielding a genuinely distinct decision rule. Construct your case from first principles in decision theory, moral philosophy, and the philosophy of risk, without appealing to specific bibliographic authorities or reading lists.
