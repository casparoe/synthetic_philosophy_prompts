As a retired experimental physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of technology and ethics, I would like you to work through the following problem with care. Please adopt the role of a professional philosopher specializing in the ethics of technological change and applied epistemology. Before constructing your answer, proceed in stages: first, briefly brainstorm the central tension between accelerating technological capability and the institutional or cognitive structures needed to govern it; second, reconstruct the concern expressed in John von Neumann's 1955 Fortune essay Can We Survive Technology, namely that rapid technological growth in a finite, politically fragmented world risks outpacing humanity's organizational and moral capacity to manage it; third, develop and defend the specific argument I outline below.

I ask you to argue for what I will call the principle of epistemic discontinuity: societies ought to embed within large-scale technological systems a designed conceptual expiration—what might be termed a conceptual sunset clause—so that any technology whose safe and justified operation depends on a particular scientific model or ethical framework automatically enters a controlled decommissioning protocol when that framework is substantially revised or abandoned. The aim is to prevent orphaned infrastructure, that is, physical systems that continue to operate long after the epistemic and moral conditions that originally authorized their creation have disappeared.

Please defend this principle as not merely compatible with von Neumann's diagnosis but as a philosophical consequence of it, given his emphasis on the danger of technologies surviving the organizational intelligence required to steer them. Address at least two serious objections: first, that mandatory conceptual obsolescence would suppress beneficial innovation and waste accumulated resources; second, that von Neumann's own outlook favored adaptive governance and continuous institutional learning rather than pre-planned technological discontinuity. Be precise about whether you are attributing this principle to von Neumann himself or only deriving it from his reasoning. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; rely solely on conceptual analysis and the framework described above.
