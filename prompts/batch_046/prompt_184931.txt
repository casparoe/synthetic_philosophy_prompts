Imagine an agent who maximizes expected logarithmic utility—the Kelly criterion—updates her beliefs correctly between rounds, and at each step stakes exactly the Kelly fraction on every available gamble. Now suppose an adversary does not deceive her about probabilities, but adaptively selects, from a menu of genuinely available bets, the particular gamble she will face next, conditioning that choice on her current capital and belief state. Over an infinite sequence, the adversary can arrange matters so that her capital converges to zero with probability approaching one, even though she never accepts a negative-expectation wager, never violates transitivity, and never departs from the Kelly rule at any individual decision point.
