Following a recent period of personal political disillusionment — particularly observing members of my own family adopt opposing party labels whilst agreeing on nearly every concrete policy — I have begun to doubt whether "left" and "right" denote anything more substantial than temporary coalitions of grievance. I wish to examine this systematically with a small reading group I convene: eight adults, meeting for a single ninety-minute session, none professional philosophers, though all accustomed to structured argument.

Please design a sorting taxonomy — specifically a decision tree with branching questions — that guides a participant from concrete commitments to a named (or unnamed) position on whether the left-right distinction is real and, if so, what holds each side together. The tree must not ask anyone "are you left or right?" or "do you value equality?"; instead, every node must present a concrete scenario or claim upon which positions genuinely diverge, answerable without prior knowledge of philosophical labels.

The branches should turn upon at least these dimensions: whether the distinction tracks a stable moral-psychological cleavage or is purely a product of local institutional incentives; whether it survives translation across cultures with different economic bases; whether internal coherence on each side requires suppressing contradictions visible only at the level of individual belief; and one further dimension drawn from my own recent framing — that left and right might be better understood as opposing temporal orientations toward inherited symbolic capital (preservation versus deliberate liquidation), rather than as opposing attitudes to hierarchy per se.

Each leaf should carry a position name — for example, "rhetorical fiction", "multi-dimensional cluster", "evolutionary cleavage", "institutional coordination device", or "unnamed" — and, in one sentence, its most awkward commitment: the belief or consequence that defenders of that leaf must accept but typically avoid stating.

Please also flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate given only these questions, and explain briefly what further distinction would be required to disentangle them.

Write the response in British English, and do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific texts that a participant without web access could not evaluate; the taxonomy should be fully self-contained.
