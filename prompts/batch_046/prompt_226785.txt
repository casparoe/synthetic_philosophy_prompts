Here's an argument my friend cookd up about Arendt and political lying that ends in a weird place and I cant tell if it's just sloppy or actually hits something.

(1) Arendt says politics depends on a shared factual world held together by collective testimonal; destroy the facts and you destroy the space where opinion can happen.
(2) She also thinks factual truth is fragile because it relies on witnesses, and modern bureacracies can manufacture 'facts' by controlling archives and repeating narratives.
(3) Now, any democratic election involves framing—choosing which stats to highlight, which histories to invoke, which voices count as credible witnesses.
(4) By Arendt's own standard, framing is just a slower form of archive-manipulation: it doesnt deny specific events but reshapes the testimonal landscape so the shared reality shifts.
(5) So if Arendt is right that organized lying kills politics, then democratic politics—which is nothing but organized framing—is also self-destructive.
(6) The only way out is to say framing isnt lying, but then her boundary between legitimate persuasion and destructive fabrication collapses.
(7) So either Arendt's view makes all democratic rhetoric illegitimate, or her distinction between lying and framing is arbitrary.

Where's the slip? I want to keep (1) and (2) if I can, but I'm not sure (3) is fair. Is there a hidden move between framing and fabrication that saves her?
