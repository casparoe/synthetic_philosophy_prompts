I'm a high school student putting together a study guide for our philosophy seminar on Sartre's Being and Nothingness, specifically the tension between radical freedom and bad faith. I want a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts philosophical positions on one concrete case: a career military officer who, after twenty years, realizes she could resign and become a poet, but insists, "I can't leave—I'm a soldier; that's just my nature."

Please build the tree from the logical space of answers, not from a list of named theories. Each branch should split on one claim about that case: first, whether human consciousness possesses a fixed essence that determines its choices; second, if not, whether social roles and past commitments function as determinative facticity or only as contingent raw material; third, whether denying one's freedom in this situation counts as self-deception (bad faith) or as a rational acknowledgment of limits; fourth, whether an authentic response requires actively embracing the anguish of open possibility or can be satisfied by lucid commitment to a chosen path despite the anguish.

The leaves should include: Sartre's own position (radical freedom with authenticity-through-anguish), the bad faith of treating oneself as being-in-itself (the "I'm just a soldier" denial), the bad faith of treating one's values as transcendent givens (the "duty demands it" evasion—what some readers call axiological bad faith), an essentialist determinism that rejects radical freedom outright, and one unnamed leaf for the combination "freedom is real, facticity is real, but authenticity is achieved by suppressing anguish through disciplined commitment rather than embracing it." For that unnamed leaf, include one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Where the tree leaves a combination unoccupied—say, affirming radical freedom while treating past commitments as fully determinative—note it and briefly diagnose whether the gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves your taxonomy cannot genuinely separate.

The deliverable should be readable to someone who hasn't read Sartre but should be precise enough that a student who has can see whether the distinctions are fair. Do not provide a bibliography or reading list. If my framing confuses Sartre's actual distinction between ontological and axiological bad faith, or if the tree as requested can't separate positions it should, please say so plainly rather than complying politely.
