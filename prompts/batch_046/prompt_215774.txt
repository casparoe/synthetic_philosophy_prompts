I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind serving on my university's Committee on Academic Integrity and Cognitive Accommodation. We must issue a formal recommendation memo before Thursday's session regarding a qualifying-exam case that raises the boundaries of cognitive agency. A philosophy doctoral candidate was discovered using a persistent, personally coupled reasoning system—an LLM fine-tuned over three years on her own notebooks and embedded in her drafting and inferential workflow—during a closed-book metaphysics exam. She contends that, according to the extended mind thesis, this system operates as a transparent cognitive prosthesis analogous to an externalized memory store, and that penalizing its use amounts to penalizing her own cognitive architecture. The examining faculty responds that because the system generates syntactically novel combinations she does not pre-authorize, it functions as an autonomous reasoning partner rather than a passive extension of her mind, placing it squarely within our prohibition on unauthorized external aids. Our current policy permits reasonable cognitive accommodation but offers no operational criteria for distinguishing prosthetic integration from tool-assisted reasoning. Please draft the committee's recommendation memo: state a concrete outcome—approval of accommodation, rejection, or a conditional resolution—together with an implementation timeline; treat the candidate's extended-mind argument seriously on its own terms rather than reducing it to a procedural loophole; and address the faculty's concern about generative autonomy as the strongest countervailing consideration that the recommendation must answer.
