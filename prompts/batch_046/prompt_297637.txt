After three decades studying microbial ecology and taxonomy in both field and laboratory settings, I have recently turned to philosophy of science, hoping to understand whether scientific classifications genuinely carve nature at its joints or are merely useful instruments. I am unsatisfied with sweeping essentialist claims that ignore the operational flexibility of microbial categories, and equally with conventionalist dismissals that seem unable to explain the predictive success of chemical taxonomy. Before I commit further study to essentialism, homeostatic property cluster theory, or domain-sensitive pluralism, I want the criteria of adequacy made explicit.

Write the desiderata that any theory of natural kinds must satisfy. The account should be tested against cases spanning different ontological regimes: chemical elements with precise atomic structure; biological species complicated by horizontal gene transfer, ring speciation, and hybridization; psychiatric categories such as major depressive disorder that lack clear biomarkers; and engineered or boundary-blurring entities—including minimal synthetic cells and viral quasispecies—that challenge reproduction-based or metabolism-based definitions of life. For each case, state what the theory must get right—whether it can accommodate graded membership, support law-like inductive generalizations, or distinguish genuinely discovered kinds from pragmatically constructed ones. The theory must also resolve specific puzzles: how to treat cross-cutting classifications in microbiomes (for instance, gene-family groupings versus ecological-functional niches) without imposing a false hierarchical unity; whether kindhood is uniform across physics, biology, and medicine or genuinely plural; and how to preserve the intuition that some classifications are mind-independent without requiring rigid essences in domains where boundaries are experimentally manipulated. Provide the motivating consideration behind each requirement, flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—especially any conflict between demanding universal essential properties and accommodating the fluid, operationally defined boundaries of synthetic and viral life—and briefly indicate how essentialism, the homeostatic property cluster view, and a pluralist conventionalism fare against your list. Please state how confident you are in each assessment, and identify the specific desideratum, tension, or comparative judgment about which you are least certain.
