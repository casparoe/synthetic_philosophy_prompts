I'm putting together notes for a philosophy of mind seminar and want a consistency check before I present. My commitments are roughly these:

(a) The extended mind thesis is about right for tightly coupled tools — say, a notebook you've used for decades to actually work through problems, not just store them.
(b) Moral responsibility only tracks mental states you can consciously access and stand behind; if a state is buried in an external tool and you've lost access, it's not ethically on you.
(c) Epistemic credit works differently — you can genuinely know things via reliable external processes even if you cant recall them right now.
(d) I want to block cognitive bloat, so not every object near you counts as part of your mind.
(e) If my old diary has a false belief I wrote years ago and totally forgot, I'm not epistemically blameworthy for it; but if I'd formed the exact same false belief in my head yesterday, I am.

Do these actually hang together? Which ones genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only look like tensions, and whats the least painful way to restore consistency? Rank them by severity please — don't just list them all as equally bad.
