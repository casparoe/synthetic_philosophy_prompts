Read the 1999 film The Matrix through the lens of al-Ghazālī's occasionalism and his denial of necessary causal connection. In the film, Neo discovers that the physical world is a computer simulation maintained by machines; within it, the regularities we call natural laws are engineered outputs rather than intrinsic powers of objects. Al-Ghazālī argues in his critique of the falāsifa that fire does not burn cotton by any nature of its own, but that God directly creates the burning at each moment of contact, with apparent causation being only habitual concomitance. Treat the Matrix simulation as an occasionalist ontology made technological: the code is the direct source of every event, and objects within the simulation possess no genuine secondary causal powers. Where, then, does human agency fit? Does the film's resolution, in which Neo can bend the rules of the simulation, ultimately vindicate the philosophers' belief in real causal powers inhering in agents, or does it confirm the occasionalist view that all effective power belongs to the single source maintaining the order?
