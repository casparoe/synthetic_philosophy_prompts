Please construct a joke—suitable for an audience that understands this distinction—in which an aquaculture welfare inspector applies the philosophical problem of other minds in an absurdly literal fashion during an audit of a lobster or crab facility. The punchline should turn on the mismatch between the behavioural criteria used to justify legal protection (withdrawal reflexes, avoidance learning) and the inspector's insistence that these are merely mechanistic outputs lacking phenomenal backing, leaving the farm manager in a paradox where compliance with welfare law requires assuming sentience that the inspector's own sceptical framework denies. Write the response in British English, keep the tone polished but genuinely funny to those familiar with the debate, and avoid any anthropomorphising sentimentality; the comedy should reside in the conceptual contradiction itself.
