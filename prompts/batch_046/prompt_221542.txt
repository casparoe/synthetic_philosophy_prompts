Consider a theory that aims to settle, for any agent and any particular belief, whether that agent ought to privilege her inside view—the direct reasoning, evidence, and arguments she can access from a first-person perspective—or instead defer to an outside view derived from statistical base rates about people with her cognitive profile, the track record of her belief-forming methods, or aggregate forecasting performance. Write the full list of desiderata that such a theory must satisfy to count as adequate, with the motivating case or conceptual tension behind each requirement.

For each desideratum, specify what it demands: which patterns of belief revision it must license (for example, cases where the inside view delivers a precise, structured argument while the outside view reports only that people like her are usually wrong about this domain), which distinctions it must preserve (such as that between believing P and believing that one is unreliable about P), and which biological or psychological results it cannot contradict. Flag which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which reflect the prejudices of particular camps—such as whether determinism about belief-formation is presupposed, or whether empirical equivalence with naive Bayesian updating is required.

Pay special attention to recursive self-application: if the theory instructs the agent to distrust her reasoning about whether to distrust her reasoning, does this generate a vicious regress, a mandatory suspension of judgment, or a stable fixed point? Identify any subset of desiderata that may be jointly unsatisfiable, particularly those involving bootstrapped modesty, where adopting the outside view seems to require using the very reasoning processes whose reliability the outside view undermines.

Finally, without making this the main deliverable, briefly indicate how a pure inside-view rationalism, a pure base-rate deference model, and a hybrid calibrationist approach that weights inside-view confidence against outside-view statistics fare against your list—just enough to show where each contender fails or succeeds on the criteria. Keep the weight on the specification itself: the conceptual work should lie in determining which demands are legitimate and whether they can all be met together.
