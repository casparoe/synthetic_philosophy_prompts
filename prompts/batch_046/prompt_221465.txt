The adversarial variant actually strengthens the modesty defence. If the filler acts after seeing my choice, then symmetric credances are clearly wrong—the adversary will stack the urn against me, so avoiding it is strategic sense, not ambiguity aversion. This reveals that in the standard Ellsberg case, agents were implicitly running maximin reasoning all along: they treat ambiguity as worst-case risk without admitting it. Savage's axioms don't apply here because they presuppose state-independent utility, but in adversarial settings preferences depend on the adversary's knowledge state. So the agent isn't incoherent; she's just using a decision rule that accounts for hidden intentions. The real mistake in the modesty defence is thinking ambiguity is about missing probabilities. Actually it's about facing an opponent whose intentions are unknown, which means standard Bayesian updating can't capture the situation. Once you see this, ambiguity aversion looks perfectly rational, not a failure of coherence.
