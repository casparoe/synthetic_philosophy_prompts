I am writing this immediately after a long, draining argument with a close friend over whether formalizing the Anthropocene is an act of scientific description or institutional power, and specifically over who holds the authority to declare when human impact “counts” in deep time—the geologists, the international commissions, or the sediment itself. You should adopt the persona of a professional philosopher of science with expertise in institutional epistemology and a practiced hand at deadpan satire. Before you begin writing, please take a moment to think carefully and brainstorm in a short internal outline: identify the real procedural mechanisms of epoch-definition (working groups, golden-spike votes, stratigraphic charts, the ICS framework) and the conceptual tensions they carry (the mismatch between human-scale history and geological time, the politics of selecting a single global boundary marker, the silent marginalization of non-Western geological archives). Then compose a satirical op-ed of approximately 600 words, written in the earnestly administrative voice of “Dr. E. Vance,” a fictional senior secretary of the International Subcommission on Quaternary Stratigraphy. The piece should defend the proposal to date the Anthropocene’s base not to 1950—the well-known plutonium and plastic horizon—but to Tuesday, 23 March 1952, the date of the first full-yield thermonuclear test, on the explicit bureaucratic grounds that it simplifies fiscal-year reporting and avoids the awkwardness of a round-number anniversary. The satire must exaggerate genuine features of stratigraphic practice—fetishization of a single global Golden Spike, procedural gatekeeping by small expert committees, the pretense of neutrality in selecting a human-centered marker—without inventing false institutional structures or naming any living individual. Maintain sustained irony throughout: every sentence should read as sincere administrative prose, with the absurdity emerging only through accumulation and logical extension. The target is the discourse and practice of epoch-definition, not any particular person. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or requests for further sources. Write so that a reader familiar with the Anthropocene Working Group debates recognizes the exaggeration as fair, and a reader unfamiliar with them can still follow the bureaucratic logic being mocked.
