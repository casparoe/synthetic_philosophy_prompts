I defend a strong version of institutional path dependence applied specifically to normative frameworks, not merely to technologies or policies. The claim is that once a society initially frames political obligation in a particular conceptual register—say, contractual consent rather than reciprocal status—that framing functions as an attractor state for all subsequent rational deliberation. Even fully informed, uncoerced agents attempting to justify or reform their institutions will converge back onto commitments internal to that initial register. Consequently, I hold that had early modern European societies adopted status-based reciprocity as their primary normative vocabulary, centuries of philosophical debate and institutional experimentation would not have produced anything recognizably like rights-based constitutionalism; reasoning itself would have been channeled into a different stable equilibrium.
