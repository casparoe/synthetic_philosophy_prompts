I serve on the Democratic Legitimacy Review Panel for Riverside County's proposed Charter Amendment 47, and I am asking you to help draft the formal opinion I will circulate before next Tuesday's vote. The amendment would redefine the electorate for a single binding referendum on the county's fifty-year water and watershed management plan: in addition to registered citizens over eighteen, it would grant votes to long-term resident non-citizens present for at least five years, to future-generation guardians selected by sortition from the current citizenry to represent unborn stakeholders, and to ecological trustees appointed to represent the watershed itself as an affected party. I discussed this with my rationalist study group over the weekend, and we kept circling back to the same tension—if democratic legitimacy requires that those affected by a decision have a say in it, then the citizen-only franchise is clearly underinclusive for long-horizon ecological governance; but if legitimacy requires that decision-makers can be held collectively responsible by a bounded community of equals, then expanding the demos this far may dissolve democratic reciprocity into an incoherent pluralism of voices with no shared accountability.

Please act as a senior political philosopher and institutional design expert with experience in constitutional review. Before drafting, take a few minutes to brainstorm the strongest version of both the affected-interests argument and the bounded-reciprocity objection, paying particular attention to how each handles representation of entities that are not present, not human, or not yet born. Then produce the panel's decision memo: recommend whether to approve the amendment, reject it, or approve it with specific structural conditions—such as weighted voting rules, sunset clauses, or exclusion of particular stakeholder categories. Your memo should present a clear, actionable bottom line with reasoning the county council could actually respond to, including feasibility concerns, risks of strategic manipulation in trustee selection, and whether partial inclusion strikes a more defensible balance. Treat the strongest consideration on the losing side with genuine intellectual honesty rather than straw-manning it, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I need reasoning the panel can evaluate on its merits alone.
