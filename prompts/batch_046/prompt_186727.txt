I have come to accept a particular reconstruction of Augustine's ethics of speech: that the absolute prohibition on lying articulated across De Mendacio and Contra Mendacium is not best understood as a deontological rule governing false utterances, but as an ontological claim about the relation between language, being, and God. On this reading, mendacium is the deliberate misuse of a signum that fractures the speaker's orientation toward Veritas; the eight-grade taxonomy in De Mendacio is therefore a hierarchy of spiritual corruption rather than a scale of social deception, with religious falsehood at the apex and the jocose lie—uttered without intent to deceive—at the base. I further draw from this a permissive corollary: because Augustine requires only the alignment of the speaker's interior intent with truth, not that the communicative effect prevent the hearer from forming false beliefs, strategic ambiguity—speaking truly but arranging words so as to mislead—does not count as lying and is therefore permitted in cases of protective deception, in a manner that modern Kantians would reject. What would count against this reconstruction? I want the textual evidence, conceptual distinctions, and rival interpretations sorted by how decisive they would be, from fatal to merely awkward. Specifically: do Augustine's treatments of the Hebrew midwives and Rahab condemn the false utterance itself or only the absence of charitable intent? Does his formal definition of mendacium require deceptive intent, false content, or both, and can it be satisfied by strategic ambiguity? Is the signum framework anachronistically imported from De Doctrina Christiana? Does this reading collapse Augustine into the casuists he attacked in the Priscillianist controversy? I want to know what committed defenders of this ontological interpretation would themselves concede as damaging, what predictions it makes—about silence, mental reservation, or the limits of charitable speech—that rival deontological readings do not, and whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability. If it has, assess whether that insulation is a defect for an interpretive thesis of this kind.
