In researching evolutionary aesthetics for my next novel, I have been tracking an evolutionary biologist’s informal judgments about whether particular phenomena are genuine handicap signals rather than strategic displays or direct adaptations. Her rulings so far: the peacock’s train is a classic case; gazelle stotting is not, being a direct pursuit-deterrent rather than a wasteful quality advertisement; visible human philanthropy is, because resources are genuinely burned for status; the metabolically expensive and predation-risky song of a nightingale is; and the enlargement of the human brain is not, since its energy cost is repaid directly in cognition. For the novel I have invented a literary culture in which authors deliberately publish only unfinished fragments, knowing that this limits audience and income, but also demonstrating that they could have completed the work; the practice correlates strongly with prestige, yet the fragments themselves are genuinely less accessible than finished books. Would she classify this fragmentary practice as costly signaling? Which of her earlier verdicts is the closest analog? Identify the competing rules that fit her track record, state what each predicts about the fragments, and say which ruling she would actually make and why. Please indicate how confident you are in this prediction and single out the point about which you are least sure.
