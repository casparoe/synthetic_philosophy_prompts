I am a biologist with extensive familiarity with macroevolutionary dynamics—fitness landscapes, adaptive radiation, exaptation, evolutionary stasis, niche construction, drift, and Red Queen dynamics—but only a layperson's exposure to philosophy of science. I want you to take three distinct positions on whether philosophy makes progress and translate each entirely into that evolutionary vocabulary, as precisely and charitably as you can. The first position holds that philosophy accumulates true propositions in a cumulative fashion analogous to scientific knowledge-building. The second holds that philosophical progress consists mainly in conceptual clarification—refining distinctions, dissolving pseudo-problems, and achieving greater internal coherence within conceptual schemes—without necessarily adding new factual discoveries. The third holds that philosophy addresses perennial questions that resist definitive resolution, so its progress is measured not by convergence on answers but by increasing argumentative sophistication and the mapping of logical space. For each position, construct the evolutionary analog: cumulative knowledge should be rendered in terms of adaptive radiation into stable niches or climbing fitness peaks; conceptual clarification should be rendered through exaptation, niche construction, or the reshaping of selective environments; and problem-solving stasis should be rendered through evolutionary stasis, drift, or Red Queen dynamics in which running is required merely to stay in place. Do not smuggle philosophical assumptions into the biological terms under cover of translation; if a philosophical claim has no obvious evolutionary counterpart, say so explicitly rather than forcing a fit.

Then provide the residue report. For each of the three translations, specify which elements map cleanly onto evolutionary concepts, which survive only with distortion—for instance, whether treating philosophical "truth" as evolutionary "fitness" implicitly imports a pragmatic or instrumentalist theory of truth that the original position may reject—and which central philosophical notions, such as a priori justification, normative evaluation of arguments, or the very idea that a pseudo-problem can be dissolved rather than solved, have no expression at all in evolutionary vocabulary. Conclude by assessing whether the untranslatable residue points to a genuine limitation in evolutionary metaphors for understanding philosophical work, or whether it instead reveals that certain philosophical claims about progress were always less substantive than they seemed, relying on distinctions that collapse once we insist on a rigorous external vocabulary. I am not asking for citations, author attributions, or a reading list; I want only the translation and the honest accounting of what did not survive it.
