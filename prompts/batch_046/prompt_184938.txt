There's this proposal floating around in some urban planning circles where you fill half a city council with people drawn by lot, but the other half stays elected, and here's the twist: the lot members recieve triple pay and they alone can block any budget line touching housing or transit. Meanwhile the elected ones propose anything but need the lot bloc's sign-off. What theory of democratic legitimacy does that split presuppose? Does it assume random selection confers some special kind of impartiality voting can't, or is it more about insulating domains from political pressure? And if representation should track affected interests, does weighting the lottery toward renters (which the proposal also does) fix the bias or just swap one for another? I'm trying to figure out if the practice is even internally consistent or if it's smuggling in different ideas about equality and authority that don't actually fit together.
