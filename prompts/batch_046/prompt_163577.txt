She further contends that Arendt's distinction between rational truth, which carries its own coercive force, and factual truth, which is contingent and therefore uniquely vulnerable, explains why scientific consensus can be politically destabilized without anyone technically uttering a falsehood. I want the interview to press her well beyond a summary of these claims.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that treat her position with precision and avoid generic biographical openings. I specifically want at least one question on whether Arendt's distinction between factual and rational truth can survive cases where scientific facts—say, climate measurements—are contested not by denying data but by reframing the institutional authority that certifies it; one on whether Arendt's observation that lying is structurally self-defeating applies to collective actors like intelligence agencies or state bureaucracies rather than to individual liars; and one on her claim that the remedy for political lying is not greater transparency but the strengthening of a non-bureaucratic public realm—specifically, what institutional form such a remedy could take without itself becoming another administrative apparatus. For each of these three, include a sharp follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, anticipating her tendency to deflect by saying that this merely illustrates Arendt's broader point about world-alienation applied to language. Finally, include one question about what historical or institutional evidence could genuinely weaken her position rather than being interpreted as further confirmation of bureaucratic dissolution, since she often treats counterexamples this way. The questions should be phrased as I might ask them—formally but with the directness appropriate to a student moderator—and should not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
