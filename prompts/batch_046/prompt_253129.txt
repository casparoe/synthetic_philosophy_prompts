You are advising a three-year research program with one philosopher of mind—no empirical lab access, no grant for data collection—tasked with advancing open questions that arise from the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: the long-standing challenge that sensory pleasures (warmth, taste, relief) and so-called higher pleasures (intellectual satisfaction, aesthetic absorption, moral contentment) appear to share no common phenomenological quality, which threatens hedonism’s core claim that pleasure is a single, uniform good. Do not survey the debate; instead, prioritize which of the following three live lines of work should command the bulk of effort, defend the ordering with explicit criteria (downstream dependency for normative ethics and well-being policy, tractability without empirical methods, and whether settlement would actually change how welfare is aggregated), and specify what evidence or philosophical argument would cause you to revise the ranking.

The three lines are: (A) the thin qualitative core project—whether every pleasure retains a minimal phenomenal signature (e.g., a specific valence orientation plus felt intensity) sufficient for normative aggregation, even if the thick qualitative character differs radically across modalities; (B) the revisionary turn—whether heterogeneity forces phenomenal hedonism to collapse into an attitudinal or desire-based account of welfare, and whether such a revision eliminates the distinctive normative pull of pleasure altogether or merely rebrands preference-satisfaction; and (C) what I will call the binding problem for composite pleasure—whether the heterogeneity objection operates only at the macro-level of activity-types, and whether a philosophically reconstructed account of momentary cross-modal affective binding (without reliance on neuroscientific data) can restore unity at the micro-level of experience.

For each line, specify: its upstream or downstream relation to the others; what a genuinely tractable philosophical result would look like within the time and resource constraints; whether resolving it alters practical well-being metrics in policy or is largely an internal correction to hedonist theory; and what argument or conceptual distinction would change your view of its importance. Conclude by identifying the single question among these three that, if settled, would most diminish the urgency of pursuing the remaining two, and defend why that settlement is load-bearing rather than cosmetic.
