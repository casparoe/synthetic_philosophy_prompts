I am constructing the intellectual biography of a historian of economic culture for a novel, and I need his judgments to be rigorously consistent. In the chapters so far, he has ruled on four explanations that invoke values as independent causes. First, he accepted that Calvinist predestination anxiety caused the ascetic restructuring of economic rationality in early modern Northern Europe, but insisted that the mechanism was psychological internalization rather than institutional constraint alone. Second, he rejected the claim that Confucian filial harmony caused Qing stagnation, arguing that the values were institutionally flexible and that state fiscal and geopolitical conditions were sufficient. Third, he accepted that Islamic waqf norms caused long-term capital immobilization in land, yet he treated this as a case where religious law embedded values in enforceable institutional rules, not as an individual ethos transforming profit motives. Fourth, he rejected the argument that South Korean Protestant growth in the 1970s caused the industrial miracle, judging the correlation spurious and the values largely post-hoc legitimation for state-led development policy.

The new case in the manuscript concerns the so-called effective altruist and longtermist culture among Silicon Valley founders and venture capitalists since roughly 2015, with its emphasis on quantifying moral impact across future generations and its coincidence with a marked shift toward high-risk, multi-decade investments in artificial intelligence and biotechnology that explicitly disregard quarterly returns. The historian must decide whether this cultural value system is a genuine cause of the altered capital allocation, or whether material and structural conditions—regulatory arbitrage, tax-advantaged endowments, network monopolies—are sufficient and the ethos is merely decorative.

Please predict his verdict, identify the closest analog among his four precedents, and state honestly whether the precedents genuinely underdetermine the case. If they do, articulate the two competing principles he could be applying—one that requires values to restructure economic rationality through deep psychological internalization, and one that requires values to be embedded in formal or informal institutional rules that constrain choice—say what each principle predicts for this Silicon Valley case, and name a further hypothetical case that would force a choice between them. Then, please indicate what specific argument or piece of evidence would change your prediction about his verdict, and identify where your reasoning is most likely to fail—whether in overestimating the coherence of his implicit theory, in misclassifying the institutional structure of modern venture capital, or elsewhere.
