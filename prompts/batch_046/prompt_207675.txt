I am a high school student currently enrolled in an introductory philosophy course, and I have spent several weeks studying supertasks—particularly Thomson's lamp and the question of whether an infinite sequence of discrete actions can possess a well-defined completion state. For a creative assignment, I have constructed a variation that I have not encountered in any standard textbook: imagine an automated lamp aboard a research probe falling radially toward a Schwarzschild black hole. Because of gravitational time dilation, an observer at infinity sees the lamp's toggle intervals shrink exponentially and the entire infinite sequence appear to conclude in a finite span of coordinate time, while instruments aboard the probe register only finitely many toggles before reaching the singularity. I would like you to write a short story that uses this setting to dramatize the conflict between the external and internal descriptions of the lamp's final state.

Please present your story as exactly five scenes, numbered one through five, with no introductory or concluding paragraphs outside the list. Each scene should be rendered in one or two concise sentences that combine narrative action with the conceptual problem, and together the five scenes should build toward an unresolved tension about whether the lamp is ultimately on or off at the limit.
