In my philosophy of mind reading group we've been stuck for months on Dennet's four creature kinds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorain. Everyone agrees on the taxonomy and on the usual examples (worms, rats with hippocampal maps, chimps with internal models, humans with language and tools). But we split hard on whether the Popperian/Gregorian boundry marks a real qualitative jump or just a smooth gradient of cognitive complexity we've reified for neatness. One camp says Gregorian minds are basically Popperian minds that picked up cultural scaffolding; the other says there's a distinct architectural shift once a creature can treat its own representations as objects of revision via external symbols, not just run internal simulations. We've read the same literature, referenced the same crow-tool and rat-place-cell work, and nobody moves. Is this disagreement actually about hidden empirical criteria for 'representational decoupling', a verbal dispute over the word 'kind', or two different questions — evolutionary novelty versus architectural requirements — shoved under one heading? And given your diagnosis, is there a conceptual distinction or a hypothetical case that could break the stalemate, or is this just bedrock intuition about qualitative jumps with no progress to expect?
