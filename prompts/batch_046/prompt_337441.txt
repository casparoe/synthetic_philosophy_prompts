I am writing for a seminar on intertemporal rationality, and I have come to defend a qualified version of pure time preference: that discounting future goods is rational only if future temporal stages stand in a weaker unity relation to the present stage than co-temporal stages do, specifically within a branching presentist framework in which the present is ontologically privileged and future branches lack the direct causal-phenomenal integration required for full prudential concern. What would count against this position? Please also indicate which objections a committed defender would concede as genuinely damaging versus which they would dismiss as resting on rival metaphysical assumptions, and whether further insulating qualifications risk rendering the view unfalsifiable. Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
