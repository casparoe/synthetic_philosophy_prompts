Compare the common prior assumption in Aumann's agreement theorem with the common-knowledge-of-rationality premise that standard presentations pair with it. Which of the two is actually doing the epistemic labor that forces agreement, and could either be relaxed without destroying the result? Then apply this comparison to a hard philosophical case: two transparent, rational peers—one a modal realist, one a modal nominalist—who share all the same evidence regarding a particular modal argument but arrive at opposite probabilities. Does their disagreement show that the common prior has genuinely failed, or that common knowledge of rationality was never present? And does the case suggest that Aumann-style agreement is a meaningful normative ideal for real metaphysical disagreement, or only a formal artifact of shared probabilistic frameworks?
