I'm a retired chemist who got into philosophy of science after leaving the lab. I've been reading about Kepler lately and I'm stuck on something that feels like a pattern, but I don't have the word for it. He calculated planetary orbits with this incredible geometric precision, yet he also cast horoscopes and talked about celestial bodies having souls or influences. Now, I know people say he did astrology for money or because everyone did then, but what strikes me is that he seemed to think the mathematical exactness of the orbits was actually a point in favor of the astrological view, not against it. Like, the more precisely you can predict where Mars will be, the more meaningful its position becomes for human affairs. I see a shadow of the same thing today: someone builds an incredibly precise statistical model of human behavior, and instead of saying 'this replaces the old explanations', they say 'see, it's so precise that it proves there's a human element the model can't capture' -- but they don't throw out the model, they just treat its rigor as evidence for something beyond it. Is there an established name for this move? I don't think it's just putting beliefs in seperate boxes, because in those cases the boxes don't talk to each other; here the precision in one box is being used as support for the other. Does this phenomenom have a specific concept, and if not, how exactly should I distinguish it from plain hypocrisy or cognitive dissonance? I only need the carving, not a bibliography.
