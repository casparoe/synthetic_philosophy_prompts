I've been trying to map out my view on conceptual slippage in microdomains for a seminar I'm running on fluid concepts, and I want a consistency check before I present it to the group next week. The specific case I'm obsessed with lately is what I'm calling "shadow curation" — when people repost and lightly annotate digital artifacts in ways that copy the form of museum curation but strip away the institutional authority and provenance tracking. Here is roughly where I've landed, though I might be missing something obvious: (1) I think conceptual identity is fixed mostly by inferencial role rather than reference or origin; (2) I think when "curation" migrated from museums to social media and then into these shadow practices, it retained enough core inferential connections to count as the same concept, just stretched thin; (3) I think in shadow curation, the concept of "authorship" gets copied over but sheds its responsibility conditions, so it isn't authorship anymore but a kind of fluid copycat that borrows the shape without the normative weight; (4) I think there's a hard line — if a concept loses more than about half its original inferential links, it's not conceptual slippage but homonymy, and the old concept is just dead; (5) I think Western IP frameworks misclassify shadow curation as derivative work because they apply a legal concept outside its proper microdomain, where the rules of transformation are totally different from art-world contexts.

I'm pretty sure there's a real tension between (2) and (4) — if curation can stretch that far without breaking, why does authorship snap immediately when it loses responsibility? — and I also worry that (3) sneaks in a normative judgment about "responsibility" that (1) can't actually ground since (1) is supposed to be purely descriptive about conceptual structure. There's also this nagging feeling that (5) presupposes microdomains have clear borders that (2) seems to deny, since concepts apparently flow across them freely. Can you audit this package for me?
