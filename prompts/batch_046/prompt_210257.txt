You advise a regulatory body that must determine whether citizens' choices under behavioural nudges reveal stable preference orderings or only framing effects. Translate the philosophical distinction between revealed preference—where utility is merely an index of choice consistency—and mental-state conceptions of welfare into a reusable procedure for a compliance officer with no training in economics or philosophy, who must apply it repeatedly under time pressure and sign off each decision for audit.

Set out an ordered checklist or flowchart in words, with decision nodes for: (i) whether the choice set has changed; (ii) whether the same option is selected under reversed framing; (iii) whether the observed pattern satisfies the Weak Axiom of Revealed Preference across three observations; and (iv) whether inconsistencies can be explained by information updates rather than preference shifts. Include three worked cases—one certifying a revealed preference, one returning 'indeterminate—judgement required', and one producing a verdict the unit will resist (a stable-looking pattern that the theory denies reveals true welfare). At each stage, flag where human judgement is irreducible and where the procedure is bound to misfire. Write in British English.
