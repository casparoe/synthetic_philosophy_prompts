I've been in a philosophy of action semenar all semester where we've been stuck on Strawson's reactive attitudes thing. Every week someone says compatabilism wins because moral responsibility is just about whether people are appropriate targets of resentment and gratitude, not about metaphysical free will. Then someone else says that's just changing the subject — you're explaining why we *feel* blame, not whether blame is *deserved* — and then we loop back to whether determinism undermines desert. We've been going in circles for months and nobody has conceded a single point, and the same three objections keep coming up like clockwork.

Lately I've been wondering if part of the stall is that both sides are treating reactive attitudes as purely backward-looking — resentment about what you did, gratitude for what you did. But there's this other category I don't see anyone talking about: prospective reactive attitudes, like when you preemptively hold someone accountable for a role they're about to take, or when you feel anticipatory distrust toward a system before it fails. If Strawson's framework is really about the interpersonal demands we make on each other, shouldn't these forward-looking attitudes matter just as much? The compatabilists in my group seem to treat them as just predictions, while the desert-theorists treat them as illegitimate because there's no past act to ground them. So maybe the debate isn't about free will at all but about whether moral responsibility is essentially retrospective, and we're arguing past each other because one side is doing psychology and the other is doing normative metaphysics without admitting it.

So here's what I want: is this stall a real substantive disagreement about the metaphysics of agency, or is it mostly verbal — with reactive attitude meaning an emotional response to one side and a normative status to the other? Or are they actually answering different questions, one about the conditions of blame and one about its justification? And if my hunch about the missing prospective dimension is right, would adding that actually move anything, or is it just another layer on the same deadlock?
