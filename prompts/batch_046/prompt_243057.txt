In debates about literary meaning, intentionalists often invoke a conversational analogy: interpreting a text is like interpreting a speaker's utterance in face-to-face dialogue. On this picture, the author corresponds to the speaker, the text to the utterance separated in time and space, the historical and linguistic context to the conversational setting, and the reader to a listener who recovers meaning by attributing rational intentions via cooperative principles drawn from pragmatics. Anti-intentionalists have resisted this by treating a poem not as a personal statement but as a dramatic object—more like a stage play than a letter—so that psychological origin is irrelevant to semantic value. I want a rigorous audit of how far the conversation analogy actually holds. First, provide an explicit correspondence table mapping speaker, utterance, context, listener, and cooperative principle onto their textual counterparts. Second, identify the disanalogies that bear on the intentionalist conclusion: texts often lack a singular conscious author (consider collectively redacted religious or legal codes), the communicative channel is broken and only material traces survive, and readers frequently adopt non-cooperative stances—what some theorists call suspicious or reparative reading—that deliberately suspend charity. Third, examine whether the analogy smuggles in contested assumptions—individualist psychology, face-to-face reciprocity, a singular voice—that fail for institutionally authored, anonymously compiled, or long-circulating works. Fourth, say whether the intentionalist conclusion survives once these breaks are visible, or whether a better analogy (perhaps legal contract, archaeological artifact, or collaborative design) does more work with less baggage. Throughout, separate what is broadly settled—such as that authorial intention plays some role in at least certain interpretive practices—from what remains genuinely contested—such as whether intention is normatively decisive, whether anti-intentionalism is self-defeating, and whether collective or anonymous texts can bear recoverable intentions at all. Do not present either camp's position as the consensus.
