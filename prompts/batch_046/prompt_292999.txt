Write a poem of exactly five quatrains—unrhymed blank-verse stanzas of four lines each—exploring pure time preference and the ethics of discounting future welfare. Render the conceptual tension precisely: take seriously the economist’s claim that future goods should be discounted only for risk, growth, and uncertainty, not for temporal location itself, and take equally seriously the philosophical objection that treating future harm as less weighty solely because it is distant is arbitrary, structurally parallel to spatial moral neglect, and hard to justify without collapsing into a weakness of imagination. Let a refrain close each stanza in a modified form, tracking how a constant exponential factor applies uniformly regardless of the scale of suffering or benefit at stake. Between the third and fourth stanzas, include a volta in which the speaker notices this parallel with spatial distance and realizes it threatens to dissolve the boundary between rational forecasting and mere moral myopia. Do not resolve the tension into either a call to action or a resigned acceptance of inevitability; let the refrain and the structural turn carry the conceptual burden. Present the result as exactly five stanzas, with no lines, title, or commentary outside them, and shape each stanza as one or two grammatically complete sentences distributed across the four lines.
