I'm on the cultural funding board for our mid-sized city and we just got this wild proposal from an arts collective called Mirror-Stage. They want to build an immersive biometric theater where the audience wears sensors that feed back into the performance in real time — heart-rate and galvanic skin responce get aggregated and the actors shift the emotional arc live. The collective claims this creates genuine communal sympthy because everyone is literally sharing physiological states.

But I'm convinced this is just Rousseau's nightmare about spectacle updated for the algorithm age: artificial, mediated feeling replacing authentic civic virtue. My co-chair thinks I'm being a Luddite and that Rousseau was only complaining about 18th-century Parisian salons, not sensor arrays. The board needs a recommendation by Friday on whether to approve the pilot grant. Please draft the memo I'll circulate: it should recommend a concrete course of action with conditions or a time frame, take the Rousseauian corruption objection seriously — especially whether biometric co-regulation fixes the problem or just makes perfomed emotion more efficient — and honestly address the strongest reason to fund them anyway. Don't just say art is good for community cohesion; I need an actual yes/no with reasoning the collective could respond to.
