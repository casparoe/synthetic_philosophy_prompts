it says:

"The classic money-pump argument shows that anyone with cyclical preferences can be exploited. Say you prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A, and you'll pay a penny to switch. A bookie can charge you to go from C back to A, then A to B, then B to C, and you're back at C but broke. This proves intransitive preferences violate diachronic rationality—rationality over time just means not being pumpable. The only fix is transitive preferences with a single utility function. Critics say this assumes you're dumb enough to keep trading, but that's wrong: the vulnerability is in the preference structure, not your psychology. So expected utility theory is really just the formal version of this pump argument."

i'm suspicious about two things. first, it sounds like it's treating diachronic rationality as if it's just the absence of cycles across time, but isn't there a difference between having intransitive preferences at one moment vs. genuinely changing your mind between trades? second, the leap from "you're exploitable" to "EU theory formalizes this" feels huge—does the pump argument actually motivate the full axiomatic setup, or just the transitivity axiom?

can you audit this? go through the summary and tell me what's accurate, what's subtly misrepresenting the argument (like conflating synchronic intransitivity with diachronic failure), and what a defender of cyclical preferences—say, someone who thinks preferences can rationally shift with context—would object to. also, does the handout's conclusion that EU is just formalizing the pump actually hold up?
