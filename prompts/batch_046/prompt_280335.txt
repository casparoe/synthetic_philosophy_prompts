In popular tech-ethics discourse, critics routinely invoke an analogy between algorithmic platforms and Bentham's panopticon as interpreted by Foucault: users inhabit cells of perpetual potential visibility; the platform's data architecture functions as the central inspection tower; because users never know precisely when they are being observed and evaluated, they internalize surveillance and become self-censoring, docile subjects. This analogy is used to license the conclusion that platform design necessarily produces disciplined conformity, and that the appropriate remedy is therefore to dismantle or radically limit the architecture of visibility itself.

Your task is to audit how far this analogy actually holds.
