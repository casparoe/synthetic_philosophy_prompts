After a long argument with a friend about whether honor, dignity, and victimhood cultures can be morally ranked, I want an internal consistency check before I defend my position further. Here is my current package:

(1) Moral universalism is correct: ethical norms are not culture-bound, so asking whether one cultural form is morally superior to another is legitimate, not a category error.

(2) Dignity culture represents clear moral progress over honor culture, because it replaces externally validated, status-dependent shame with an internalized conception of equal, inalienable worth.

(3) Victimhood culture is not an accidental corruption of dignity culture but its logical extension: once every harm is construed as a rights-violation and every person is assigned infinite moral weight, the accumulation and display of grievances becomes the dominant mechanism for securing status.

(4) The proper criterion for comparing cultures is their capacity to sustain autonomy and cooperative reciprocity. By this standard, honor culture outperforms victimhood culture—honor demands reciprocal obligation—though dignity culture remains superior to both.

(5) I reject purely consequentialist evaluation of cultures, yet I maintain that long-term social stability and the aggregate well-being of members are relevant, if not decisive, indicators of a culture's moral health.

(6) Victimhood culture is morally inferior precisely because it severs the link between personal agency and moral standing; nevertheless, recognizing structural victimization is essential to justice, and denying victim-status to genuinely oppressed groups constitutes a grave moral failure.

(7) A culture should also be assessed by the character traits it systematically cultivates. I regard courage and loyalty—central to honor culture—as more morally weighty than the sensitivity and advocacy characteristic of victimhood culture, which is why I rank honor above victimhood. At the same time, I believe honor culture's in-group loyalty is incompatible with justice for strangers, which is why dignity culture, not honor, occupies the top position.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each conflict? For each genuine tension, specify whether it is logical, motivational, or evidential, rank them by severity, and identify the least costly revision—whether modifying (3), softening (6), abandoning (5), or adjusting (7)—together with what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package. Finally, tell me which single revision, if I must make exactly one, would restore the greatest overall coherence, and describe what the cleaned-up position looks like.
