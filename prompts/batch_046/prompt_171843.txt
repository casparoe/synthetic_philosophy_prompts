You serve on the Institutional Ethics and Safety Board of the Meridian Neuroadaptive Research Consortium, and the board must render a decision by the end of March on whether to renew funding for Project Resonance, a Phase-II closed-loop neuromodulation trial for severe, treatment-resistant behavioral addiction. Rather than altering external contingencies or employing cognitive restructuring, the protocol uses real-time feedback to suppress predictive-error signaling in the ventral striatum precisely when the patient anticipates the addictive reward, thereby decoupling subjective satisfaction from actual dopaminergic reinforcement. The investigators describe this as reward-channel isolation: patients report durable well-being and reduced craving, yet advisory behavioral economists warn that the intervention structurally parallels wireheading—the agent’s internal utility register is satisfied without any corresponding improvement in world-state or in the underlying preference architecture. The clinical team responds that conventional therapies, including selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors and exposure-based extinction, already modify internal reward weightings, and that suppressing a maladaptive prediction error is therapeutically indistinguishable from correcting a miscalibrated belief. The board’s own charter mandates that funded interventions preserve agentic coherence, defined not as the static preservation of pre-treatment preferences but as the patient’s sustained capacity for reflective endorsement of their motivational architecture over time; however, the charter offers no operational metric for coherence, leaving the board to determine whether the protocol satisfies or undermines it.

Please draft the formal written opinion you will circulate before the March session. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approve with binding conditions, suspend pending an independent longitudinal study, or reject outright—together with a specific timeline for implementation or review. The reasoning must engage the philosophical distinction between altering an internal reward mechanism and altering the world that generates rewards, treat the wireheading objection in its strongest form rather than dismissing it by analogy, and examine whether agentic coherence supplies a decisive criterion or merely relocates the normative dispute. You should also include an honest, substantive treatment of the strongest consideration favoring the course of action you do not recommend, and address what the board would be committing itself to, in principle, if it adopts your proposed standard for future reward-modulation protocols.
