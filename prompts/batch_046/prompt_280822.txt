Construct a realistic, one-paragraph scenario set in a hospital ethics committee of five members — two senior clinicians, one bioethicist, two patient advocates — deciding whether to approve a conventional gene-therapy protocol for a rare paediatric condition. Engineer the case so that it displays a discursive dilemma modified by two features: one premise involves a graded probabilistic judgement rather than a binary fact, and members carry differential epistemic weights according to their professional roles. Specify the individual judgements on each of the three premises and on the direct conclusion vote, arranged so that premise-based aggregation (with weights) recommends approval, conclusion-based majority aggregation recommends rejection, and a distance-minimising rule — selecting the judgement set closest to unanimous agreement — sides with rejection. Then demonstrate step by step why each aggregation method delivers the verdict assigned to it. Identify the precise structural feature — the interaction between the weighted probabilistic premise and binary conclusion voting — that makes the divergence occur, and explain whether removing either the weighting or the probabilistic form collapses the case into an ordinary discursive dilemma. Finally, sketch how a defender of premise-based aggregation would object to invoking distance-minimisation in this setting, and state how confident you are in your analysis, singling out the point about which you are least certain.
