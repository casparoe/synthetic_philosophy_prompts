In my philosophy of quantitative social science seminar, our professor has offered these verdicts on whether various trends show the world is becoming less violent and whether there is a fact of the matter: the post-1945 collapse in interstate battle deaths per capita is a genuine reduction, not an artifact; the global fall in homicide rates in both per-capita and absolute terms is likewise real; the rise in reported intimate-partner violence in Nordic countries with stronger gender-equality institutions carries no fact of the matter, being driven by reporting infrastructure; the near-global abolition of legal chattel slavery is a clear reduction in direct violence, though economic coercion replaced it; and the US carceral expansion since the 1970s despite falling crime rejects any “less violent” reading, since the state now inflicts direct harm on more people in absolute terms while per-capita metrics obscure institutional growth.

Physical harm per student is near zero, but the absolute number of students subjected to algorithmic risk-scoring and preemptive psychological harm has grown by orders of magnitude, with emerging documentation of severe anxiety and exclusionary effects.
