I'm on this small municipal board that reviews commemorative plaques and memorial language for old state harms — forced relocations, land seizures, labor camps, stuff like that. We meet twice a month, none of us are philosophers, and we keep getting proposals that ask the town to "acknowledge collective guilt." I want a reusable decision procedure built out of Jaspers' four guilt types — criminal, political, moral, metaphysical — but turned into a flowchart my collegues can actually run. For each type: the single question to ask, what answer kills the proposal, what answer lets it through but forces specific wording, and the exact point where it's a values call not a fact call so I flag it for a vote. The procedure has to deal with two hard cases: the town government is technically continuous but every resident is new, and current people benefit structurally without having caused the harm. Then apply it to three proposals: (1) a plaque stating the town bears criminal guilt for an 1890 eviction; (2) a garden memorial saying present residents share moral guilt for living on stolen land; (3) a school-renaming that claims metaphysical guilt "for being part of humanity that failed." Include one result the proposers will hate. Also flag where the procedure misfires — especially when institutional continuity gets read as personal blame — and be honest about what it just can't settle. Keep it practical, no bibliography needed, just the checklist and the worked cases.
