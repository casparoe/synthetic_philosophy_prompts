hey so i'm trying to sort out my thoughts for a metaphysics of propositions seminar and want to check if my package actually hangs together before i present. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) bradley's regress is a real problem, not a pseudo-problem — if relations are entities on a par with their relata you get an infinite hierarchy of relations relating relations, which is nuts.

(2) i still think propositions are structured entities with real constituents: subjects, predicates, and the relations connecting them.

(3) i solve unity by saying the relation inside a proposition isn't just another constituent — it's the binding act that holds the other parts together, and it doesn't need further binding because that's literally its job.

(4) world-facts work the same way: relations bind objects into facts, but they aren't extra objects that themselves need binding.

(5) i reject multigrade-predicate and internal-relation fixes, since they can't explain how the exact same relation holds between totally different pairs.

(6) lately i've wondered if vague cases mess this up — like when it's genuinely indeterminite whether this is still a heap. my idea is that the binding relation itself might be imprecise or loose, so constituents are only partly tied together, which explains the lack of sharp boundary without putting fuzziness into the world.

does this cohere? specifically, does (1) undermine (2)-(4) once you admit that saying relations bind in the world still sounds like treating them as entities with a job? and does (6) clash with (3)'s idea that binding is primitive and all-or-nothing? there's also probably an apparent tension between (2) and (3) — if the relation isn't a constituent, how can it be part of the proposition's structure? — that i'm hoping dissolves on closer look.

please audit it: rank the real tensions by severity, say which ones only look bad, and tell me the least costly revision. don't just list everything as equally messed up — i want to know what actually has to give and what i can keep.
