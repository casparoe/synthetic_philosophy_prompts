Please explain the conceptual distinction between counterpart-theoretic accounts of modal predication and transworld-identity accounts, focusing specifically on the problem of accidental intrinsics—contingent properties such as having a particular height or scar. I am particularly interested in how each view handles an object that is qualitatively identical in all intrinsic respects to an actual object, yet stands in none of the same external relations: it has no parents, no siblings, and no causal history linking it to the actual world. On the counterpart view, does qualitative identity alone make it a counterpart? On the transworld-identity view, could it still be the very same object despite lacking those relational properties? Furthermore, discuss whether expanding the counterpart-relation to include weighted extrinsic similarities—rather than purely intrinsic ones—risks collapsing counterpart theory into a disguised version of transworld identity, or whether there remains a principled metaphysical boundary between the two positions even under such an expanded similarity metric.
