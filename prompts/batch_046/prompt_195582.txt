I was arguing with my roommate about whether pain is actually bad, or if its just the wanting-it-to-stop part that sucks. We got onto those asymbolia cases where people report intense hurt but dont flinch or take meds—like they recieve the signal but it doesnt register as something to avoid. The usual philosophical move is to split pain into sensory and affective dimensions, then say only the affective part carries the badness. Fair enough. But then I started noticing in the papers Im skimming that whenever a weird counterexample shows up—say, an asymbolic patient who rates his pain 10/10 but smiles and asks for more, or someone with congenital insensitivity who seeks burning sensations and calls them warm and interesting—philosophers rarely concede that pain might not be uniformly bad. Instead they add another conceptual fence: first its badness tracks aversive motivation, not raw sensation; then when motivation is present but detached—like the patient wants the burn for spiritual reasons—suddenly theres representational badness versus practical badness, or intrinsic versus instrumental unpleasantness. Each distinction seems reasonable on its own, but stacked together they make the core claim pain is bad basically unfalsifiable, because any case that looks like badless-pain or painless-badness gets reclassified outside the target category.

Im trying to figure out if theres a specific name for this kind of slow accumulation of individually defensible distinctions that collectively protect an intuition. I know Popper talked about immunizing strategies and theres the idea of an auxilliary hypothesis, but those feel too broad—this is more like conceptual gerrymandering inside one debate. Does it have an established label in philosophy of science or philosophy of mind? And if not, how should I draw its edges: does it need to be intentional protection, or can it happen accidentally? Whats the difference between this and just legitimately refining a concept? I dont want a bare name dumped on me; I want to know exactly what the phenomenon is, what it isnt, and where it tips from reasonable precision into question-begging.
