For our graduate seminar in metaontology, please compose a dialogue between two equally sharp interlocutors—Dr. Elena Vasquez, who defends a position she terms “register pluralism,” and Dr. Marcus Chen, a realist about institutional ontology—on whether disputes over the existence of social kinds (for instance, currency or legal status) are merely verbal. Vasquez should argue that assertions such as “the euro exists” and “the euro does not exist” can both be correct, but only relative to distinct conceptual registers (practical-economic versus physical-ontological), and that the appearance of contradiction arises from failing to index the existential quantifier. Chen should counter that this framing obscures a substantive metaphysical question—whether social kinds possess distinctive modal persistence conditions, specifically whether they survive total replacement of their material substrate—that is not reducible to descriptive framing, and he should press this via a “substrate-replacement test.” Their exchange should feature direct rebuttals to actual points raised, with neither reduced to a strawman. In your response, please include a brief reflexive note stating which specific argument or empirical observation would lead you to revise your assessment of whether the dispute is verbal, and identify the point in your reasoning most susceptible to failure.
