I sit on the city's Public Memory Commission and we need a decision memo by Friday. The museum is proposing a permanent exibit about the 1968 Riverside Corridor demolition — the urban-renewal project that tore through the working-class neighborhood. There's a three-way split on how the exibit should explain what happened.

The staff historians want a covering-law frame: federal funding formulas, demographic pressure curves, statistical patterns of displacement, economic models. The community board wants a pure narrative: Mayor Halloway's specific choices, the Riverside Tenants Union protests, the contingent failure of the court challenge, the letters families wrote. Then there's this third proposal from our digital curator — a toggle interface where visitors switch between statistical and narrative modes. But she admits the algorithm always weights the statistical layer heavier because verification is easier, so in practice the narrative gets buried.

We have to recommend approval, rejection, or approval-with-conditions — and it needs to be a real recommendation, not just "here are the options." I want you to take the strongest argument on the losing side seriously — the community people are right that a covering-law account can turn real victims into statistical noise — but then definately defend your pick. Don't just lay out pros and cons; pick one and stick with it.

Please don't include a bibliography, reading list, or citations — we need something actionable for Thursday's meeting, not a literature review. Write it like an actual memo: clear recommendation at the top, then reasoning, then the honest treatment of the strongest objection. And please, this should be a committed response: take a definite position with reasons, don't leave the final call to me. There might be a typo or two in my message; ignore them and just draft the memo.
