For a long-form feature I am writing on why the biological category of "species" is fraying at the edges of synthetic biology and microbial genetics, I need a self-contained classification instrument—something my readers can use to discover which philosophical stance they actually hold without first knowing the technical labels.

Please construct a decision tree built from logical commitments rather than from a roster of famous biologists. The tree should begin with this concrete puzzle case: a research team engineers a self-replicating microbial consortium whose members exchange genetic material horizontally among three donor lineages, none of which can reproduce sexually with one another, and the resulting population occupies an ecological niche previously filled by one donor but now performs metabolic functions borrowed from all three. Call this the chimeric consortium.

Design the tree with exactly four branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete yes/no or either/or question that a reader could answer without knowing which philosophical position it leads to. The four dividing lines must cover, in whatever logical order you judge best, the following issues:

1. Whether membership in a species requires reproductive isolation, shared evolutionary history (monophyly), or neither of these conditions.
2. Whether widespread horizontal gene transfer among microbes destroys the reality of discrete lineages altogether, or merely complicates the tracing of them.
3. Whether the engineered, chimeric origin of the consortium places it outside natural-kind taxonomy, or whether functional integration into an ecological role is sufficient for species membership regardless of origin.
4. Whether the fact that no single criterion applies uniformly across sexual eukaryotes, asexual microbes, and synthetic organisms makes pluralism inevitable, or whether it demonstrates that "species" is a pragmatic fiction lacking metaphysical backbone.

Each leaf should carry either an established philosophical label—such as Biological Species Concept, Phylogenetic Species Concept, Ecological Species Concept, Species Pluralism, or Species Nominalism—or a precise descriptive name for a combination of answers that has no standard label. Where a combination of answers leaves the logical space apparently empty, flag it in one sentence and indicate whether the gap is best explained by internal contradiction, unattractiveness, or mere neglect in the literature.

The instrument should not cite authors, provide bibliographies, invoke obscure technical debates that a general science reader could not follow, or ask for further reading. It should be entirely self-contained: a reader should be able to trace honest answers through the four branches and arrive at a named or precisely described philosophical position.
