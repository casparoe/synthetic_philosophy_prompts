I hold the strong diachronic-coherence view that any agent vulnerable to an intertemporal money pump—where a sequence of locally rational trades, each evaluated by the agent’s current utility function, results in a net loss relative to the agent’s initial preferences—thereby demonstrates a failure of practical rationality rather than legitimate preference evolution. On this view, even if the shifts driving the pump arise from genuine learning, maturation, or adaptive response to changed circumstances, the susceptibility itself reveals a lack of the structural unity required for rational agency; coherence is not merely instrumental but constitutive of it.
