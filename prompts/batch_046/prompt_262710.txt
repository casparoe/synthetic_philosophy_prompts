I've come round to the idea that Hegel doesn't just put up with war as a necessary evil — he treats it as a kind of ethical shock-therapy for civil society, the moment where private interest gets dragged back into awareness of the universal so the state doesn't rot into pure economic administration. I've been pushing this a bit further: if that's right, then a state that permanently outsources its violence to contracters or autonomus drone systems, leaving citizens with no actual collective moment of risk, ought to suffer a specific kind of internal decay — not just political apathy but a fragementation of ethical life where Sittlichkeit collapses into mere legalism and market coordination. What would count against this? Not the standard pacifist line. I want to know which patterns in modern democracies with heavy PMC use or remote warfare would actually falsify the link. Which bits of Hegel's own logic — say the gap between the state's internal health and the individual's sacrifice — could be turned round to break the outsourcing-corruption connection? And what would a committed defender concede, maybe that other collective risks like climate collapse can play the same shock-therapy role, versus what they'd flat-out deny? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me whether the position is actually exposed here or if it's been insulated by enough qualifications that nothing can really touch it.
