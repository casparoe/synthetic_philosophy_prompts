In a graduate seminar on artificial cognition, participants keep invoking the phrase competence without comprehension—first to note that Darwinian natural selection produces exquisitely adapted organs without any designer grasping optics or immunology, and then, by analogy, to argue that a large language model can produce fluent, accurate outputs without grasping semantics. Others immediately object that Darwin’s inversion was about the absence of a conscious designer, not about the absence of functional representation in the organism itself, and that Turing’s computational framing actually presupposes precise symbol manipulation rather than its absence. I want to disentangle the precise theses that slogan could be advancing. Please lay out, as a menu with verdicts, the distinct readings: the trivial mechanistic claim that correct outputs can be generated without introspective access; the stronger anti-representationalist claim that functional success never requires internal models or causal understanding; the evolutionary-analogy claim that biological adaptation licenses dismissing understanding as a criterion for artificial systems; and any other substantive or false interpretations hiding in the phrase. For each reading, indicate whether it is a platitude, whether it genuinely follows from Darwin’s reasoning about selection or from Turing’s reasoning about computation, and whether it actually supports the use made of it in the LLM debate. Write in American English, present the readings as a numbered or bulleted list I can bring to the seminar, and do not provide a bibliography or citation list.
