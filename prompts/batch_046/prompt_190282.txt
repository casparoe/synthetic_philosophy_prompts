I'm trying to work out what a theory of democratic membership actually has to deliver, and I keep feeling like people just jump to all affected interests or birthright citizenship without saying what job the theory is even supposed to do. So can you lay out the full spec?

Here's what I mean. Any account of who belongs to the demos has to handle the obvious cases - the long-term resident alien who pays taxes, the citizen living abroad for decades, the child who will inherit the decision - but also some weirder ones I'm stuck on. Like people with e-residency who vote in a country's digital elections but have never set foot there, or climate refugees who don't exist as a defined population yet but will be governed by carbon policies made today, or seasonal agricultural workers who are physically present for harvest but legally absent the rest of the year. And then there's the distinction between having a say and having equal say, or between being afffected because you're impacted versus being a member with actual standing.

So: write the desiderata. What cases must it classify correctly? Which distinctions - affectedness vs membership, presence vs citizenship, temporary vs permanent - must it preserve, and which ones is it okay to blur? Are there non-negotiable constraints from democratic theory itself, like not letting outsiders override local self-rule, or from moral equality? And flag any pair of requirements that probably can't both be satisfied - I have a hunch that include everyone affected by a decision and preserve democratic autonomy of bounded communities are in tension, but I want to see if there's a deeper conflict hidden in there.

Then, just briefly, how do the usual candidates do? Not a full scorecard, just: the all affected interests view, the territorial-residence view, and maybe something like a stakeholder contract view that ties membership to sustained recipricol obligation. Which desiderata do they each fail?

Keep it practical - I'm not looking for a bibliography, just the conceptual architecture. And I know I'm mixing up demos and demoi wrong somewhere, don't correct me unless it actually breaks the argument.
