I was discussing American exceptionalism with a friend last night, and we seemed to be operating with different understandings of what it means for the thesis to be false. I had been treating exceptionalism as a unified claim with three interlocking parts: a historical thesis that American political and social development followed a genuinely singular path; a normative thesis that this singularity generates special obligations or privileges for the United States in international affairs; and an ideological thesis that citizens continue to orient their political identity around this sense of special destiny. My friend, drawing on comparative history, argued that the first part has been decisively undermined—American development is better explained by settler-colonial dynamics, capitalist consolidation, and transnational legal borrowing than by any unique genius—and therefore insisted that holding onto exceptionalism in any form is intellectually dishonest. She seemed to assume that the normative and ideological dimensions are necessarily parasitic on the historical one: if the empirical uniqueness claim collapses, then the moral and cultural structures built upon it must also be abandoned, or at least radically revised.

This left me wondering whether her inference is valid, or whether I am the one committing a category error. Is it actually the case that debunking the historical uniqueness thesis automatically undermines the normative claim that Americans ought to preserve certain constitutional traditions with special care, or the descriptive claim that exceptionalism operates as a functional ideology regardless of its empirical warrant? I find myself wanting to ask how one should rationally update across these three registers—historical, normative, and ideological—when only the first has been challenged. Must the defender of exceptionalism concede that the concept is now empty, or is there a defensible version that substitutes distinctiveness for uniqueness, or that severs descriptive genealogy from prescriptive commitment? And if such a version survives, does it still count as exceptionalism, or has it been transformed into something so modest that the original dispute has dissolved?
