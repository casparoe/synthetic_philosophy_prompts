ok so i had this heart scare last month (false alarm but it shook me) and now im trying to figure out what i actually think about death, since its suddenly not abstract. heres roughly where ive landed:

1. i think the epicurean argument is right: when youre dead theres no subject there to experience anything, so death itself isnt bad for you. harm needs a recipient.
2. i also buy the symmetry thing — we dont mourn not being alive in 1850, so dreading not being alive in 2150 seems like a mistake. treat both directions the same.
3. but i also feel like dying young is genuinely tragic because it robs you of goods youd have recieved. if my friend died at thirty she lost decades that were thier, not just ours.
4. i hold that harm requires the subject to exsist at the time the harm occurs — you cant hurt someone who isnt there.
5. i think its rational to fear death, but only the dying part: pain, loosing control, the end of projects. not fear of being dead, since thats not a state youre in.
6. and i want to say "her death was bad for her" literally, not just "bad for us." it describes her life trajectory, not just our grief.

do these hang together? i feel like 3 and 4 pull against each other hard, and 1 with 6 feel off too, but maybe 2 and 3 are the real clash? im honestly not sure where the damage is worst.

please take a committed stance: rank the tensions by severity, say which ones are only apparent and dissolve on inspection, and tell me exactly what has to give with the least damage to the overall picture. dont lay out a buffet of options — pick the package that works and defend it. also, where am i sneaking in an asymmetry im not entitled to?
