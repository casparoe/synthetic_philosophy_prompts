Two grad students were arguing in my office yesturday about whether an ant colony could have a mind, basically playing with the Aunt Hillary / ant fugue idea. (1) Maya said no, because consciousness needs a single integrated perspecive, and the colony is just thousands of separate agents with no shared point of view—like saying a crowd is one person because they cheer together. (2) Leo said that's just biological chauvinism; if you look at the functional architechture, the colony processes information, solves problems, and regulates itself, so denying it a mind is like denying a computer consciousness just because it's silicon. (3) Maya pushed back that functionalism still requires the right kind of causal integration, and pheromone trails are too local and too slow to bind into a global workspace—there's no 'it' that it's like to be the colony, just a lot of 'it's like to be this ant.' (4) Leo then introduced this weird hypothetical: imagine the colony evolved synchronous oscillation patterns in pheromone release that temporarily lock distant sectors into a unified response state—call it a fugue state—where information is globally available for about thirty seconds. He said if that happens, then there's a transient unified perspective, and if we reject that, we're just drawing the line at whatever biology we don't like. (5) Maya said even then it's just a very complicated reflex loop, not a subject; the oscillation has no persistence, no memory across states, and no self-model. Please go through each move and say who's right, where they're talking past each other, and whether Leo's fugue-state injection actually changes the argument or just moves the goalposts.
