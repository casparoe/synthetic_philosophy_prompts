I am a PhD candidate in philosophy working on minimalist theories of well-being. For a chapter on the extension conditions of tranquilism—the view that well-being consists solely in the absence of conscious craving and suffering—I have sketched five amendments that attempt to handle intertemporal, social, and non-biological cases without importing hedonistic or perfectionist values. I would like you to evaluate them conceptually, without bibliographic references.

The amendments are:

A. Static individual tranquilism: well-being is determined moment-by-moment by the absence of craving and suffering in a single conscious subject, with no aggregation across time.

B. Temporal-integrative tranquilism: well-being is the life-integral of tranquil intervals, permitting brief episodes of suffering if they instrumentally extend subsequent tranquility.

C. Intersubjective tranquilism: a tranquil state contributes to well-being only when it is intersubjectively recognized; unacknowledged solitary tranquility carries no weight.

D. Asymmetric weight tranquilism: suffering carries direct negative well-being weight, while craving carries only derivative weight mediated through its propensity to generate suffering.

E. Synthetic tranquilism: the framework extends to artificial systems exhibiting functional analogues of craving (e.g., persistent reward-deficit loops) and suffering (e.g., non-terminating error-state persistence), treating the absence of these analogues as well-being-bearing.

Please rank these five amendments (A–E) into three tiers—Top Tier, Middle Tier, Lower Tier—according to how well they preserve the core tranquilist commitment that only the absence of craving and suffering determines well-being, specifically judging by (i) resistance to smuggling in non-experiential values (achievement, social recognition, temporal unity, functional design) and (ii) internal conceptual coherence. For each amendment, provide a one-sentence placement justification.

Then, indicate which single amendment, if treated as the canonical form of tranquilism, would be most exposed to the adaptive-preferences objection—that subjects might adapt to deprivation and report absence of craving despite objective harm—and explain in two to three sentences why that vulnerability arises precisely because of the amendment's distinctive feature.
