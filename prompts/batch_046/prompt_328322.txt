Write a one-page research proposal (approximately 600 words) for an undergraduate philosophy term paper on the conditions for valid consent. Focus specifically on whether deceptive framing—structuring true information through interface designs that systematically impede rational uptake—should be classified as a disclosure failure, a form of coercion, or an undermining of the subject's competence. Explain why standard tripartite accounts (which treat deception, coercion, and incompetence as conceptually independent vitiating conditions) fail to settle this classification problem. Propose an approach that compares a graded, domain-specific model of competence against a binary-threshold model when applied to cases of structured epistemic manipulation. Specify the possible conclusions—that framing collapses into deception, operates as a novel coercive mechanism, or reveals competence to be relationally defined by the informational environment—and indicate which conceptual considerations would decide among them. Conclude with an honest assessment of whether this project is tractable within one semester, what a partial result would look like, and what would count as failure. Do not request a bibliography, reading list, or specific citations; refer to theoretical positions and arguments only in general terms.
