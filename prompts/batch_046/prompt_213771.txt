ok so ive been thinking about this ever since i started mentoring kids at the community center last fall. i genuinely like hanging out with them, but i also realize part of why i keep showing up is that it makes me feel like im not a total waste of space. now im stuck on whether my actions actually have moral worth, especially with all that talk about whether reasons need to be motivationally transparent to even count as ours.

heres an argument that feels like it collapses everything:

(1) an act only has moral worth if its done for the right reason, not just because it happens to help someone.
(2) the right reason cant be self-interested, otherwise its just prudence dressed up as morality.
(3) but if i examine any reason i might cite -- i care about their wellbeing, i want justice, i feel solidarity -- each one either traces back to my own emotional payoff or to evolutionary programming that serves my genes. and if a reason isnt motivationally transparent -- if i cant fully endorse it knowing its causal origin -- then it isnt really my reason at all.
(4) so every actual psychological cause of my helping is self-interested or alien at the root, which means none of them qualify as the right reason.
(5) therefore none of my mentoring has moral worth, and by the same token almost nobody's good deeds do.
(6) but we clearly do think some people act with genuine moral worth and others are just performing.
(7) so (1) is wrong and acting for the right reasons cant be a condition on moral worth after all.

where does the slip happen? i want to hold onto (1) and (6) together if i can. is (3) doing hidden work by assuming that a reasons origin in biology or psychology makes it the wrong kind of reason? or is the mistake in (4) -- conflating the causal story with the justifying reason? please be honest about how sure you are and tell me which part of your answer youre least confident in.
