The debate about German vs American folk morality -- whether Germans genuinely treat rule-following and Ordnung as moral goods in themselves, while Americans rank individual conscience above collective harmony -- has been going in circles for years. Sociologists cite traffic norms and recycling compliance, philosophers invoke rationalist tradition vs romantic individualism, and noone moves. I think part of the stall is that one side measures morality by institutional reliability and the other by heroic dissent, so they're tracking different virtues and calling both 'morality'. But there might also be a hidden empirical disagreement about whether people in each culture feel personal guilt or social shame when breaking rules. Diagnose what's actually happening: is it verbal, about the word 'morality'; substantive, about moral psychology; or are we just answering two different questions -- social coordination vs personal integrity -- under one heading? What would count as progress, and is asking 'which has the better of it' comparing incommensurable goods?
