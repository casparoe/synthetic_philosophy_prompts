I am writing a short paper for my course on nineteenth-century moral philosophy, and I want to make sure I understand the mechanism behind Nietzsche's account of slave morality correctly. From what I have read, Nietzsche describes ressentiment as a biologically maladaptive emotional complex that caused the weak to invent good and evil as a reactive inversion of aristocratic good and bad, with the result that this inverted value system spread like a cultural pathogen despite reducing the vitality of the populations that adopted it. If we take this literally as an evolutionary claim, then the persistence of slave morality across millennia looks like a classic group-selection paradox: a trait that harms individual fitness and group competitiveness should not stabilize. Should I read Nietzsche as implicitly endorsing a Lamarckian or non-Darwinian model of cultural inheritance to explain how this maladaptive system survived, or is there a different way to understand his historical narrative that avoids treating moral change as a biological adaptation problem?
