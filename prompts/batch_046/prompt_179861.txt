Ask students to evaluate whether this tension can be resolved by reading the concession not as mere psychological accommodation but as an epistemic scaffold: a controlled redirection that preserves the formal structure of worship (submission, mediation, material expression) while gradually substituting its object, even though it requires worshippers to operate within false beliefs during the transitional period. The question should require students to determine whether this scaffold reading saves Maimonides' rationalism or instead commits him to an implicit, and perhaps unacknowledged, theory of pedagogical deception.

Please provide: (1) the exam question itself, clearly worded and self-contained; (2) a model answer of approximately 600 words that must include five specific moves—(a) a precise reconstruction of the concessionary argument; (b) an articulation of the conflict with the Law's perfection thesis; (c) an analysis of the scaffold reading's resolution strategy; (d) a critical evaluation of whether that strategy succeeds or collapses into deception; and (e) a brief comparison with one rival interpretation (for instance, the symbolic-education or intrinsic-value reading) to demonstrate command of the interpretive landscape; (3) a marking scheme that allocates points out of 25, assigns specific weights to each of the five moves (suggest 4 marks each) plus 5 marks for coherence and argumentative rigor, and distinguishes an A-range answer (all five moves sharply articulated, with the deception problem explicitly confronted and the rival reading accurately characterized) from a B-range answer (accurate summary of the concession thesis but treating the tension as superficial, missing the deception problem entirely, or failing to compare rival readings). Do not include any request for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists in the exam prompt; the question must be fully answerable from conceptual knowledge of the text and the philosophical issues alone.
