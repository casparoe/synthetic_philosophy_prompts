For a workshop on consciousness and self-reference I am drafting a position paper and need an internal consistency audit before circulating it. Here is my working profile: I reject the Cartesian theater—there is no central stage or homuncular observer to whom experience is displayed. I accept Dennett's multiple drafts model in broad outline: there is no single canonical narrative of experience, only parallel distributed processes competing for behavioral dominance. I hold that phenomenal consciousness is real but ontologically unremarkable—what-it-is-likeness is a mode of presentation of physical-functional states, not a primitive property. I think the hard problem is largely a pseudo-problem generated by the conceptual isolation of phenomenal concepts, though I also maintain that a genuine, purely epistemic explanatory gap persists between third-person functional descriptions and first-person experience. I believe first-person authority is real but limited: it derives not from privileged introspective access to an inner show, but from the direct availability of certain drafts for verbal report and action-guidance. I insist there is a determinate fact about whether I am conscious right now, even though no inner witness adjudicates among drafts. Finally, I treat the unity of consciousness as a post-hoc narrative construction, yet I posit a pre-narrative mechanism—call it indexical tagging—by which drafts are bound with a self-reference that explains felt unity without requiring any observer.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe is each real conflict? For each tension, specify whether it arises from incompatible motivations or from direct logical entailments, identify the cheapest revision that would resolve it and what that revision costs, and rank the tensions by severity. Then tell me which single change to this profile would eliminate the most tension overall, and whether making that change is advisable or whether it undermines a commitment I should keep. Do not lecture on consciousness generally: treat this specific profile as the object, trace exactly what it commits me to, and do not assume I am prepared to abandon any particular item in advance.
