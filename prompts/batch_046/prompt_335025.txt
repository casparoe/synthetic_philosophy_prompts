I serve on the ethics oversight panel of a municipal housing authority. We have been offered a substantial private grant that would fund urgent renovations to emergency shelters; however, the contract includes a clause permitting the donor to veto the admission of residents with a particular category of criminal record. This category encompasses several current occupants, who would consequently face displacement. To navigate this, the panel has devised a procedural workaround: rather than voting directly on the contract, we intend to delegate signing authority to an external consultant, who will apply a purportedly neutral scoring rubric that — not coincidentally — produces precisely the same exclusionary outcome. Some panel members contend that this delegation absolves us of direct responsibility, since no individual member personally orders any eviction; others maintain that it merely obscures our complicity. I am uncertain whether accepting the grant through this delegated mechanism is ethically distinguishable from accepting it via a direct vote, and whether my own involvement in designing the rubric intensifies or dilutes my moral responsibility for the displacements it will cause. How ought I to evaluate my obligations in this situation? Please answer in British English, and refrain from offering bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
