draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between indirect-normativity theorists and direct-deliberation-skeptiks about whether rationality is a property of actual minds or only of counterfactual idealized versions. the indirect side holds that norms apply to whatever survives full-information, logically-coherent reflection, even if that idealized self is pretty distant from the actual agent. the direct side holds thats just describing a different person and smuggling direct norms in through the back door.

produce a document both could sign. start with a numbered agreed-claims section: what they both accept about the formal structure of counterfactual reasoning, about what idealization involves (information access, logical coherance, maybe value revision), and about where the dispute is not merely verbal. then a disagreements table: each row states one sides precise view in wording that side accepts, with the other sides rejection in neutral language. then list the cruxes — i think the big ones are the modal-anchoring problem (does the idealized self retain identity with the actual agent?), the guidance problem (can indirect norms actually direct present action without collapsing into direct ones?), and the sovereignty problem (how far can values shift during idealization before the agent loses epistemic authority over the result?). for each crux, include a concrete case or argument both sides agree is relevant — for example, an agent whose idealized version rejects risk-aversion she actually holds, or a scenario where full information reveals a hidden preference that contradicts her current commitments — and record in advance what each camp predicts that case demonstrates.

write it like theyre actually putting their names to it, not like youre picking a winner. keep it casual; a typo or two is fine. dont ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists — the point is to see if the disagreement can be made exact without leaning on who said what in the literature.
