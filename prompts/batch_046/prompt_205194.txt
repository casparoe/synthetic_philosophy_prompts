I am writing for a course on social ontology and norm evolution, and I need to translate David Lewis's account of convention into a practical audit protocol for a platform-governance office. The office receives weekly reports of recurring behavioral regularities in an online community—such as users adopting a specific emoji chain to signal disagreement, or federated nodes consistently routing messages through one hub despite redundant paths—and must determine whether each regularity is a Lewisian convention, whether it is arbitrary, and whether redesign is feasible without coordination failure. Please design a reusable step-by-step procedure: list the ordered questions the office should ask, specify what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, indicate where the procedure stops with a clear negative, where it stops with a positive but no-redesign verdict, and where it must hand off to human judgment because the issue is normative rather than factual. The procedure must explicitly handle three hard cases: patterns sustained primarily by algorithmic recommendation rather than mutual expectation; patterns that function as conventions within a subgroup but not across the entire user base; and patterns that solve a coordination problem but appear non-arbitrary due to functional or technical constraints. Then apply the procedure to three proposed cases: (1) the emoji-chain disagreement signal; (2) the federated routing-hub preference; and (3) a proposed three-day delay in direct-message responses adopted as a politeness norm, with the platform considering enforcing immediate delivery notifications. Conclude by stating where the procedure is likely to misfire and what categories of decision it is structurally unable to resolve.
