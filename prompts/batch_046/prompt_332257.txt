I'm a novelist fluent in narrative architecture—character arcs, dramatic obligation, genre conventions, and the logic of plot resolution. For a conceptual project, restate Confucian role ethics in that vocabulary alone. Focus on the claim that moral identity is constituted by relational roles—parent and child, ruler and subject, elder and younger sibling—and that virtue lies in performing these roles with appropriate feeling and ritual precision. Translate this into terms of narrative function, relational plot contracts, and genre-bound duty. Then report the residue precisely: which elements translate cleanly, which require distortion to preserve dramatic coherence, and which—such as a role-transcending universal compassion—have no narrative equivalent at all. Conclude by judging whether that residue exposes a limitation in thinking about morality through fiction, or whether it marks a genuinely non-dramatizable core within Confucian ethics that any novelistic treatment must either betray or remain silent about.
