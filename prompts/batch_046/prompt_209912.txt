Please argue that Weber’s Protestant Ethic thesis is best reconstructed as an argument about explanatory framing rather than about efficient causation: specifically, that ascetic Protestant values did not push capitalist institutions into being through direct psychological motivation, but functioned instead as an ecological filter that made the logic of rationalized accumulation sustainable while rendering competing value-rational orientations—such as aristocratic status consumption or guild-based craft honor—practically invisible to actors. Defend the view that, on this reading, culture explains historical outcomes not by entering into a causal chain alongside material factors, but by constricting the space of viable value-rational orientations within which instrumental-rational institutions could stabilize. In developing this position, address the objection that treating values as background conditions of institutional possibility rather than as independent variables makes Weber’s claim unfalsifiable or tautological, and explain why this reconstrual actually strengthens rather than weakens the idea that culture can be part of a genuine explanation without collapsing into either idealism or materialist reductionism.
