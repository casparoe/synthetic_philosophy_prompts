I'm on the board of our county historical society and we just recieved funding to redo the permanent exibit after last spring's flood wrecked the old gallery. I keep arguing with the other board members about whether we organize everything around big events and local heros, or around the slow stuff - river geography, climate patterns, the soil economics that made the town exist in the first place. I need a reusable decision procedure my team can run on any proposed display item: a flowchart in words with questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer, where it stops with a hard no, and where it's a values call that needs the director's sign-off because the facts don't settle it. Ground it in Braudel's framework of geographical, social, and event durations - not just vibes - and keep it usable by people who aren't academics and meet once a week. Apply it to three proposals: the original 1892 town charter signed by our founder, a chart of river flood cycles over two centuries, and personal letters from a factory worker during the 1923 strike. Make sure one result - probably the charter - gives us an answer we'll hate, like splitting it across both wings or putting it in the slow-structures section. Also say clearly what the procedure can't do, especially with family-memory artifacts or pure art pieces. No bibliographies or reading lists, I just need to actually run this.
