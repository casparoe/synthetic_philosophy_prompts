I'm preparing a feature on democratic institutional design for a science-and-ideas magazine. Write an essay of no more than 450 words addressing the following controversy: should a modern democratic state replace its upper legislative chamber with a body selected entirely by sortition—random selection with demographic quotas—that holds only a suspensive veto (the power to delay, not block, substantive legislation) but retains exclusive binding authority over procedural rules and constitutional maintenance? Weigh the claim that this hybrid captures the deliberative and epistemic advantages of lottocracy without undermining electoral accountability, against the objection that giving non-elected citizens binding procedural power violates political equality, and that a merely suspensive veto risks reducing the chamber to an ornamental delay. Reach and defend a specific verdict—do not end with a non-committal balance. State your thesis clearly in the opening paragraph, develop the opposing arguments in the middle, and restate your justified conclusion in the final paragraph.
