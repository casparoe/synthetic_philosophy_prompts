question: if a perfect forgery is indistinguishable from the original and is displayed for decades, does the original still have more value? discuss with referance to ideas about authenticity and reproduction.

my answer:

i'd say yes, but the reason changes. benjamin's idea of aura is about an artworks unique presence in time and space, but with digital files now that feels old. actually nelson goodman argued that in autographic arts like painting, history of production matters, so even a perfect copy is a forgery. i think this is right, but i want to add my own idea called reverse provenance: if a copy is shown in museums for fifty years and everyone thinks its real, then it becomes the reference point. the actual original, if found later, is now the weird outlier that doesnt match public memory.

goodman also said aesthetic value is just in the visual experience, so the copy and original should be equally good to look at. the difference is only artistic or ethical — the original connects to the painters hand. but i think goodman mixes this up. he cant have it both ways: if history matters for authenticity, it should also affect how we value the experience, not just be an add-on.

for digital art there isnt really one original file, just loads of copies, so reverse provenance applies even stronger — the version everyone shares is effectively the real one. this shows that value isnt in the object but in the social story around it.
