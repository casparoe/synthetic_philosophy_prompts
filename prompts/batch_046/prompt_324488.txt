My roommate summed up Popper's verisimilitude idea like this: science makes progress by getting closer to the truth, and Popper measured that by counting a theory's true consequences minus its false ones. But since every false theory has infinite false logical consequences, you can't actually compare two false theories to see which is closer. Popper realized this and more or less abandoned the concept. Then philosophers Miller and Tichy showed its impossible in principle—given any false theory you can always construct another false one thats closer just by adjusting the logical weights—so scientific realists today talk about predictive success or explanatory power instead of truth-likeness. Is this summary fair? Which parts are accurate, which are misrepresentations or overstatements, and does the conclusion that realists dropped verisimilitude actually follow?
