I'm a high school student preparing a philosophy presentation on aging and biotechnology. I have been reading Cicero's essay on old age, in which he argues that bodily decline is not miserable if one cultivates mental vigor and accepts natural limits rather than fearing death. Today researchers are developing senolytic drugs that clear senescent cells and partial epigenetic reprogramming—using modified Yamanaka factors—to revert tissue age without full dedifferentiation. If Cicero were alive now and understood these specific interventions, would he likely regard them as a rational extension of self-care compatible with his view, or as a modern version of the excessive attachment to youth he criticizes? I am particularly interested in whether reversing biological age would, in his framework, undermine the moral purpose and wisdom he assigns specifically to old age, or whether acceptance of mortality could still be maintained even if the body is partially rewound.
