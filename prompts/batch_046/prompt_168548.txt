I've been running a small philosophy of language group and we've been stuck on Bradley's regress for like three months now. We all agree on the basic setup: a proposition has to be unified, if you analyse it into parts plus a relating relation, that relation needs to be tied back into the parts, and then you're off to the races. Some people in the group say Russell basically killed this with his theory of judgment where the relation isn't just another term but does the actual relating work. Others say that's just hand-waving, you're still left explaining how the mind or the world actually binds things without just positing a magical glue. And then there's this one person who keeps bringing up whether the problem even gets started if you reject the idea that relations are ever internal to their terms in the first place, which sounds right but also feels like changing the subject. I can't tell if we're arguing about metaphysics, about what counts as an explanation, or whether we're just using unity in different ways. Is this a real substantive deadlock or mostly a verbal mess? And if its the former, what kind of distinction or formal result would actually move anyone?
