My confusion runs like this. Hacking’s looping-effect argument shows that once people are sorted into psychiatric categories—autism spectrum conditions, dissociative disorders, or even schizophrenia—they often internalize the classification, altering their self-narrative and symptom presentation in ways that feed back into clinical observation and diagnostic practice. If this feedback is inherent to any “human kind,” then it seems categorical diagnosis must be epistemically self-undermining over time: the more successfully a DSM category is applied in one cohort, the more it transforms the target population, rendering the original operational criteria obsolete for the next cohort. That would imply longitudinal psychiatric epidemiology relying on stable categories should be impossible, and that only biomarker-based neuroscience—which avoids interactive classification entirely—could produce genuine psychiatric knowledge. Yet clinical research clearly continues to run multi-decade cohort studies using DSM-derived categories, and psychiatrists do not generally treat diagnosis as mere self-fulfilling prophecy. So am I wrong to think looping is necessarily destabilizing? Is there a hidden assumption that looping applies uniformly across all mental disorders—including conditions like schizophrenia—or is there a principled reason categorical diagnosis remains valid even when the classified kind is genuinely interactive? I feel like I am conflating the inevitability of looping with the impossibility of stable taxonomy, but I cannot see where the slide happens.
