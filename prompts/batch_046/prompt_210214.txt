I'm preparing interview questions for a philosophy podcast episode with an unnamed defender of lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism who also advances a strong incommensurability thesis she terms evaluative dimensionalism: suffering and pleasure are not merely asymmetrical in moral weight but categorically distinct in kind, so that once suffering crosses a defined lexical boundary, any quantity of pleasure becomes not outweighed but conceptually inadmissible within the same moral calculus. I need eight to ten rigorous, non-generic questions that press precisely on the interaction between the lexical threshold and this dimensionalist claim, rather than asking her to restate negative utilitarianism broadly. For each substantive question, provide an anticipated standard reply and a targeted follow-up designed to make retreat into generic asymmetry arguments progressively harder. Specifically, include: one question probing whether her dimensionalism is doing independent philosophical work or whether the lexical priority alone could secure her conclusions without the incommensurability apparatus; one about worlds with zero suffering and zero pleasure versus worlds with minimal sub-threshold suffering paired with extraordinary non-hedonic flourishing—such as deep intellectual achievement or aesthetic creation—and whether her view forces her to prefer the former; one examining what phenomenological or empirical finding could, in principle, count against her denial of a common metric between suffering and pleasure; and one closing question about whether her position implies that moral agents have a lexical duty to eliminate suffering before they may legitimately pursue any other goods, and what that means for personal projects of meaning. The tone should be formal and suitable for an audience versed in normative ethics but not necessarily specialists in axiology; do not request citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
