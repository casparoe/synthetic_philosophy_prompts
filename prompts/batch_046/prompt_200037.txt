I hold a version of Benatar’s asymmetry that extends beyond the standard antinatalist framing: rather than treating the absence of pleasure as merely “not bad” because there is no subject to suffer deprivation, I treat pleasure and pain as asymmetrical in their modal conditions of value—pleasure must be instantiated in an actual subject to generate value, whereas pain generates disvalue both in its presence and in the avoidance of its presence, regardless of whether a counterfactual subject exists to “benefit” from that avoidance. From this I derive that any decision to create sentient beings introduces an asymmetrical moral risk: potential pleasure is contingent on actualization and carries no pre-actual value, while potential pain is a disvalue that is not canceled by non-actualization, which makes non-creation the rational default unless some compensating reason survives this structural imbalance. I frame this not just as an ethical claim but as a decision-theoretic observation about bets with asymmetric payoff structures under modal uncertainty.
