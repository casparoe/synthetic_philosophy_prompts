I am advising my sister on whether to relocate for a position in an industry I once studied closely. Several years ago I examined market data and concluded that the sector faces sustained contraction, yet I never shared this assessment with her. Now I actively encourage the move, and I have configured my information environment—news filters, conversation topics, even which reports I ask colleagues to summarize—to exclude updates about that sector. When she asks directly whether I have hidden concerns, I sincerely reply that I do not. A close friend argues that this is conceptually incoherent: either I still hold the belief that the sector is risky, in which case I am knowingly misleading both her and myself, or I have genuinely revised my view, in which case my deliberate information avoidance is merely irrational anxiety with no deceptive element. Is it philosophically possible for a person to maintain a belief in one mental compartment while sincerely disavowing it in another, such that shielding oneself from evidence is not a sign of hidden knowledge but part of how the disavowal is sustained? Or does this kind of concealment necessarily require that I still know the truth I am hiding, making my reassurance to her impossible to render sincerely?
