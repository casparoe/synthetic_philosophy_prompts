Two philosophers of science, speaking at a seminar I attended, argued over whether Daniel Dennett’s three stances—physical, design, and intentional—can be legitimately extended to what they called orphan patterns: functional regularities in decentralized systems that are neither engineered by a designer nor representable by any single agent. They focused on a specific case. In a large open-source cryptography project, contributors repeatedly revised one module in ways that, taken together, neutralized an attack vector none of them had identified. The coordination was robust—small perturbations in individual goals still yielded convergence on the secure configuration—but there was no collective mind and no original specification of the outcome.

(1) The first speaker claimed that this forces at least a modified design stance. We must treat the pattern as purpose-driven, she said, as if it were designed for security, because its counterfactual robustness and predictive utility depend on attributing a functional end-state that is not reducible to the physical dynamics of each contributor’s actions. Without that attribution, she argued, we miss why the pattern persists across different local motivations.

(2) The second speaker replied that this is a category mistake. Either there is an actual designer, in which case the intentional stance applies, or there are only physical interactions governed by protocol rules, in which case the design stance is merely a predictive heuristic for complex dynamics and explains nothing that the physical stance could not in principle cover. He added that meteorological systems also show counterfactual robustness—storms form reliably—without anyone invoking a design stance for weather.

(3) The first speaker countered that the weather analogy fails because storms lack goal-directed plasticity: if atmospheric conditions shift slightly, the storm does not adjust to preserve a functional outcome, whereas the contributor network does. She argued that this plasticity licenses treating the functional pattern as autonomous from both individual minds and brute physics.

(4) The second speaker said that goal-directed plasticity is just a redescription of feedback loops in the physical dynamics; attributing autonomy to the pattern is reification. He concluded that unless the first speaker can specify what predictive or explanatory work the design stance does that is not already done by a sufficiently detailed physical-level account plus local intentional stances applied to each contributor, the orphan pattern adds nothing but mystification.

Please adjudicate the four moves in turn: rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify the rating. Then say who, if anyone, has the stronger position on whether a genuinely free-floating design stance is warranted here, and identify what either side is missing—particularly whether there is a principled way to distinguish patterns that genuinely support a stance from those that merely tolerate a heuristic redescription.
