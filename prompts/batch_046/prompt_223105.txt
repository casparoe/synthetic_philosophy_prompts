I'm drafting the final examination for an advanced undergraduate seminar on seventeenth-century moral and political philosophy. Please compose one essay question that students should be able to answer in ninety minutes, aimed at testing whether they can do more than summarize Pascal's well-known remarks that justice without force is powerless and force without justice is tyrannical. The question should require students to reconstruct Pascal's underlying distinction between the order of bodies and the order of charity, and then apply that framework to the following institutional scenario: a constitutional democracy enforces a distributive policy that a substantial minority considers substantively unjust, where the enforcement is procedurally legitimate but relies ultimately on coercive power. Students must determine whether Pascal's account implies that the minority owes obedience because of conventional justice, because of sustaining force, or because of a failure to recognize the separation of orders, and they must defend whether this result amounts to political quietism or leaves conceptual room for reform. Provide the exam question itself, a model answer of approximately 650 to 750 words, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points totaling 25 marks. For each point, state the mark allocation and include a one-line description of the typical failure mode—such as collapsing Pascal's conventionalism into Hobbesian social-contract theory, treating the order of charity as supplying direct political prescriptions, or ignoring the theological premise that makes the separation of orders necessary rather than contingent.
