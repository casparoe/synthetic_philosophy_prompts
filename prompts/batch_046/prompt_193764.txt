I am outlining an essay on Schopenhauer's claim, in The World as Will and Representation, that music is not a copy of Platonic Ideas but a direct objectification of the Will. I want to press a tension in this account. Schopenhauer distinguishes melody—which expresses the Will's restless striving—from harmony, which expresses the grades of its objectification. I argue that this distinction actually assigns representational roles to musical form, which seems to contradict his insistence that music is wholly non-representational. I also want to ask whether his doctrine of genius, defined as the capacity to perceive Ideas, can accommodate musical genius, since music apparently bypasses Ideas altogether and accesses the Will directly. Please comment on these notes. Specifically: (1) Does Schopenhauer's melody/harmony distinction genuinely commit him to representational content, or can it be read as purely formal? (2) Is there a consistent way to understand artistic genius in music within his epistemology, or does music require a different kind of cognition? (3) Where might my reading overstate the conflict with his broader metaphysics? Evaluate the coherence of the essay's central claim and suggest how to sharpen it without distorting his view of the Will.
