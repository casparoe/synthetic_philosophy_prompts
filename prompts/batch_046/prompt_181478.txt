Hey, I'm a novelist working on a story about a woman who was blind from birth and then gets her sight back. I keep running into this thing that feels like it's right next to Molyneux's problem but not quite the same thing. So here's the pattern. In version one, she touches a cube and a sphere, then sees them for the first time—classic setup, can she match them? That's the usual problem. But in version two, she touches a cube, then sees a cube, but she can't tell that the visual cube and the tactile cube are literally the same object in front of her unless she's been trained to link them first. She knows both are cubes, she can navigate both spaces, but there's this gap where the two experiences stay in seperate tracks—like her brain hasn't fused them into one thing she can point to and say that is it, from both sides. And version three: she hears a voice and sees lips moving, and same issue—she knows both are speech, but she doesn't immediately bind them as one event happening to one person. Is there an established name for this specific failure of cross-modal binding, as opposed to the standard Molyneux question about recognizing shape? If philosophers have talked about it, what's the term and how exactly does it differ from the shape-recognition problem? If not, how should we draw the line between them?
