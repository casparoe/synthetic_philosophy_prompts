If John Maynard Keynes—who in his Treatise on Probability treated probability as a logical relation between premises and conclusion, introduced the idea of the “weight” of an argument, and who in The General Theory sharply distinguished calculable risk from true uncertainty—were alive today and asked to evaluate modern AI safety forecasting that assigns precise numerical probabilities to unprecedented catastrophic outcomes (say, a specific percentage chance that a frontier model causes irreversible societal harm within ten years), how would he likely analyze such practices? Would he regard them as legitimate extensions of probabilistic reasoning to new domains, or as category mistakes that apply risk calculus where only radical uncertainty obtains? And in what ways might his distinction between the “weight” and the “probability” of an argument illuminate the epistemic status of predictions about technologies that possess no historical frequency distribution?
