I've been working through a variant of constitutive panpsychism that treats macro-conscious subjects not as aggregates of micro-experiences but as stable informational attractors in a high-dimensional relational dynamics among micro-phenomenal fields. On this view, phenomenal binding is analogous to synchronization in coupled oscillators rather than to part-whole composition, which lets me sidestep the classic decombination problem without committing to cosmopsychism. I want to stress-test this. What would actually count against it? I'm not looking for the standard dismissal that the combination problem is unsolved. Rather: which neuropsychological findings (say, about split-brain integration or anesthesia-induced dissolution of attractor dynamics), which formal constraints from information-theoretic measures of integration, and which metaphysical commitments about the ontology of relations it forces on me, would be decisive? Which of these would a defender of Russellian monism or cosmopsychism concede as damaging, and which would they dismiss as misframed? Rank the defeaters from fatal—the view collapses into a rival or violates a constraint I accept—to merely awkward—requires an ad hoc qualification. In particular, does the attractor framing risk insulating itself from empirical falsification, and is that insulation a bug or a feature?
