A podcast transcript summarises Avicenna’s flying-man thought experiment as follows. Imagine a fully formed adult suspended in a void, deprived of all sensory contact — not even feeling his own limbs — and with no prior memories or experiences. Even so, the speaker claims, he would immediately affirm his own existence. The host takes this to show that self-awareness is entirely non-representational and independent of any bodily state: it is a direct intuition of one’s self (dhāt) as a bare substance. From this, the host concludes that Avicenna is advancing a form of substance dualism essentially identical to Descartes’s cogito, in which the soul is a complete substance (jawhar) that requires the body only as an instrument for external action, not for its being or for self-knowledge. The host adds that this directly refutes modern embodied-cognition accounts that tie self-awareness to proprioception or sensory integration, since Avicenna has shown that none of these are necessary for the soul’s awareness of itself.
