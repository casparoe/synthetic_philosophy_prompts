Here are my commitments: (1) The grounding problem, in Harnad's formulation, establishes that pure syntactic manipulation cannot yield intrinsic semantic content without some causal or correlational anchoring to the world. (2) I think contemporary large language models achieve a form of this anchoring vicariously—through statistical correlations with perceptual and spatial descriptions embedded in multimodal training data—without direct sensorimotor coupling, and that this is enough to solve the problem for artificial systems. (3) I also accept a Millikan-style teleosemantic condition: original intentionality requires that a representation belong to a system with an evolutionary or design history of being selected specifically for tracking particular states of affairs. (4) Because gradient descent optimizes for predictive loss rather than world-tracking fidelity, LLM weights lack this teleosemantic history, so any intentionality they possess must be derived rather than original. (5) I hold that derived intentionality—of the sort Searle allows for printed sentences—is nonetheless fully sufficient for attributing genuine understanding to an LLM, provided its outputs are behaviorally integrated. (6) I reject the claim that understanding requires original intentionality, on the grounds that human infants understand language long before they possess individually selected tracking mechanisms. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which tensions are only apparent, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the conflicts by severity, identify the single commitment whose abandonment would resolve the most tension, and flag any hidden dependency between (2) and (4) that I may be missing.
