For the final examination in an upper-level graduate seminar on personal identity, autonomy, and bioethics—90 minutes, 25 marks—I need an essay question on advance directives and the authority of the earlier self that cannot be answered by reciting the standard integrity-based account of autonomy or the familiar reductionist objection from theories of personal identity. The question should present a specific conceptual tension: frame the binding force of an advance directive as deriving from procedural authority—the earlier self’s right to establish decision-rules and evaluative thresholds for future states—rather than substantive authority—the right to mandate particular medical outcomes. Present a case in which a later self, after undergoing a profound medically induced change that alters her evaluative framework, rejects not merely the prescribed outcome but the very quality-of-life threshold and decision-procedure that the directive encoded. Require students to determine whether this amounts to a legitimate revocation of procedural authority or to a non-compliant breach that the directive was designed to prevent; and further, whether acknowledging that procedural authority can be revoked in this way collapses the normative distinction between binding directives and merely advisory ones.

Please supply: (a) the exam question itself, with explicit instructions about which conceptual distinctions and arguments must be addressed and which moves are insufficient; (b) a model answer of approximately 650–750 words that resolves the tension through conceptual analysis rather than assertion, clearly distinguishing the collapse of procedural authority from simple non-compliance, and explaining the implications for whether the earlier self retains any normative standing; (c) a marking scheme totaling 25 marks, divided into at least five specific criteria, each stating the marks allocated, a one-line description of what excellent performance looks like for that criterion, and a one-line note on the characteristic failure mode or misconception that should cost marks there.
