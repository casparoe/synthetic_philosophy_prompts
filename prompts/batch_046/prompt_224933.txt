I'm on the advisory board for the Mid-Atlantic Interstate Council on Environmental Governance — we're basically a volunteer commitee that reviews pilot programs for cross-border regulation, though half the members are retired lawyers and the other half are county planners who mostly argue about parking. Last Tuesday my co-chair forwarded me this weird proposal from a coalition of county executives who want to hand air-quality monitoring up to the federal EPA but keep a local veto over any standard that's stricter than the federal floor. They're calling it "reverse subsidiarity" and they want our council to endorse it as a model charter for other regions. I was discussing it with friends over coffee and one of them said it sounds like Swiss cantons ceding powers upward, but I think that's wrong — cantons don't usually do that voluntarily for monitoring tasks. Anyway, we need a formal memo by next Friday recommending whether to approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions this upward-delegation mechanism, and the reasoning needs to actually engage the philosophical tension with the EU's Article 5 TEU principle, the German Länder's constitutional role, and the US anti-commandeering line from Printz and New York v. US. You are an expert in comparative constitutional law and political philosophy, so please think carefully and start with some brief brainstorming on how each system treats the direction of authority. Draft the memo in British English — use "programme", "centre", "labour", etc. — with a clear bottom line and an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, so the county executives can actually respond to it rather than just seeing doctrine restated. Also, please say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you are least sure of. I really need this to read like a working document, not a seminar paper.
