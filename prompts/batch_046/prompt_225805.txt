I run a philosophy seminar on rationality and action — about 14 students, mostly grad with a few seniors, one 70-minute session. Design a classroom game that makes them feel the absent-minded driver problem without me just drawing the decision tree on the board. To keep it from being the same example they've seen a million times, inject a niche framing: autonomous delivery pods that suffer a "recall drift" glitch at specific geofenced waypoints, where the pod forgets its original routing plan and defaults to local optimization. Students should play both the ex-ante dispatcher (who picks the route) and the in-the-moment pod (who acts at the waypoint), with seperate payoff cards so the incentives split.

The rules should push them into picking a path they know they'll botch at the node, and they shouldn't realize the trap until after they've committed. Include roles, card rules, a point system with no real cash, and a timeline. Then a debrief that maps each round onto the classic driver problem, says what will predictably go wrong the first time (probably students trying to write side-contracts to "patch" the glitch), and is honest about what feature — likely the normative pull of the ex-ante plan — the game can't actually reproduce and I have to supply in discussion. Write it casual, like you're emailing a colleague. A typo or two is totally fine.
