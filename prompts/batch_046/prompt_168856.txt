ok so I retired from experimental physics a couple years ago and got obsessed with metaethics, but I'm still thinking like a scientist. Here's roughly where I've landed - I want a consistency check before I present this at a local philosophy meetup:

1. Moral realism: thier are objective moral facts out there, mind-independent, like physical laws exist whether anyone measures them or not.

2. Our moral beliefs come mostly from evolutionary and cultural programming - they track survival and group cohesion, not truth. Same reason our eyes evolved for navigating, not for seeing atoms.

3. Still, reflective equilibrium is the right method. We take those raw intuitions and revise them with reasoning, just like science takes noisy data and builds theories.

4. Normative properties (ought, wrong, good) can't be reduced to natural ones. There's a real is-ought gap that's ontological, not just epistemic. They're emergent in a strong sense, like consciousness, not like temperature.

5. But I think anything real has to sit in the causal/natural order. If moral facts exist, they should in principle leave traces detectable by improved science - neuroscience, sociology, whatever - otherwise they're ghost properties.

6. Widespread moral disagreement doesn't prove there's no fact. Physics had competing frameworks for centuries and eventually converged.

So do these hang together? I think 4 and 5 might be fighting, and maybe 2 and 3 since I'm building equilibrium on debunked intuitions. But I'm not sure if 1 and 2 are actually in tension or just look like it because of the evolutionary story. I want you to be committed about this: rank the tensions by severity, say for each pair what the cheapest revision is and exactly what it costs you, then tell me which single change cleans up the most problems overall. Don't lay out a menu and leave me hanging - definatly pick a position and defend it.
