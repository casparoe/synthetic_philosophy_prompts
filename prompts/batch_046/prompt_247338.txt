I defend a particular interpretation of Weber's Protestant ethic thesis that treats ascetic Protestantism not as a direct psychological driver of capitalist accumulation but as what I call a constraint-dissolving frame: a system of collective representations that did not motivate economic rationalization by supplying new desires but by dissolving the moral and religious prohibitions that had previously made sustained, methodical accumulation appear sinful, thereby restructuring the causal field within which material interests could operate without generating cognitive dissonance. On this view, values are causes not in the manner of physical forces that push agents toward outcomes but as conditions that alter the space of permissible reasons and prohibitions, so that explaining modern capitalism requires citing the ethic not as an independent variable competing with economic interests but as a transformation of the conditions under which those interests could be rationally pursued. Tell me what would genuinely count against this position—not merely the familiar objections that correlation is not causation or that capitalism had multiple geographical origins, but the deeper defeaters: which historical observations about capital accumulation in non-Protestant contexts or in periods prior to the Reformation it cannot accommodate; which conceptual difficulties arise when one claims that meanings restructure causal fields without themselves being mental states of individual agents; and which results in the philosophy of social science, particularly concerning the status of reasons-explanations and the problem of overdetermination in historical narratives, threaten to undermine it. I want these ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an honest assessment of which ones a defender of the frame-constitutive reading could absorb through qualification without rendering the thesis unfalsifiable, and whether such insulation is itself a defect for an explanatory claim of this kind. Please also address whether the view risks collapsing into the trivial observation that cultural change always accompanies economic transformation, and if so whether that collapse is damaging to a position that aims to explain rather than merely describe. Present your answer as continuous prose only—no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables—written in the manner of a letter or extended email.
