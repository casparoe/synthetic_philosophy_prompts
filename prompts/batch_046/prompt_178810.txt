The Rietdijk–Putnam argument uses special-relativistic simultaneity relations to show that for observers in relative motion, whether a distant event is past or future is frame-dependent; if neither frame is metaphysically privileged, then all such events must be equally real, yielding a four-dimensional block universe. Decades of replies—denying that determinacy implies reality, invoking a hidden privileged present, or charging that the argument conflates coordinate descriptions with ontological commitment—have not settled the dispute, and the literature now largely repeats the same moves. My working theory is that the stall is not about physics or temporal passage but about an unstated methodological rule: whether ontological commitments drawn from a theory must be invariant under every symmetry transformation of that theory, a constraint neither side has defended or rejected explicitly.
