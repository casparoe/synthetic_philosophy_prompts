I'm the senior commissioning editor at an international affairs quarterly that publishes long-form crisis analyses, and we've recently instituted an editorial policy requiring every submitted manuscript to declare—explicitly—whether its underlying historical ontology is cyclical or linear. We've found that readers systematically misread cyclical arguments—whether they invoke Polybius's anacyclosis of constitutional forms, Ibn Khaldun's erosion and renewal of group solidarity, Spengler's morphological life-cycles of cultures with irreversible winter phases, or Toynbee's rhythms of challenge and response—either as deterministic predictions or as mere pessimistic metaphors, which distorts the author's actual causal claims. I need a reusable decision procedure I can apply in about fifteen minutes to each of roughly twelve submissions per week, with no philosophical training required, that translates these four thinkers' core distinctions into concrete, ordered tests run on the argument's structure rather than on its bibliography or citation pattern. The deliverable should be a flowchart in words: first a vocabulary screen, then a mechanism test, then a teleology check, with explicit markers for the judgment calls I cannot eliminate—especially where a manuscript claims both cyclical pattern and human agency capable of breaking the cycle. I need it to include worked applications to three hypothetical submissions: one framing recent AI governance failures as an analog to Polybian constitutional decay; one framing climate adaptation as a linear rupture that demands unprecedented institutions; and one—this is the case I expect to dislike—that synthesizes Spenglerian civilizational winter with Toynbeean creative response in a way that should be flagged as ontologically incoherent or at least forced into revision. The procedure must state honestly what it cannot do, such as adjudicate the empirical truth of the historical claim or detect hidden linear-progress assumptions masked by cyclical metaphors, and it must specify what evidence or argument would change my classification of that hybrid case. Finally, please include a brief note identifying the step—likely the boundary between Toynbee's genuinely regenerative cycles and linear-progress narratives that borrow cyclical language—where the procedure's reasoning is weakest and most prone to misfire.
