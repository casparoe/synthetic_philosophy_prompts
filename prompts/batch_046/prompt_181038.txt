I am a doctoral student in philosophy of physics, currently writing a methodological appendix for my dissertation on structured disagreement in consciousness studies. In a recent graduate seminar on non-biological moral patienthood, my reading group reached an impasse over whether constitutive panpsychism carries ethical obligations toward fundamental physical processes. Rather than continuing to argue past one another, we have agreed—at least in principle—to produce an adversarial-collaboration document that both sides could sign. I need you to draft that document.

The two camps are these. Camp One, the Fundamental-Valence Extensionists, maintains that if phenomenal properties are genuinely fundamental, then some minimal form of valence is also fundamental, and that aggregation across quantum fields or spacetime regions could yield widespread, low-intensity suffering even without brains or nervous systems; they take seriously a hypothetical I will call the vacuum-fluctuation suffering hypothesis, in which transient, non-integrated micro-experiences with negative valence arise from dark-energy fluctuations and accumulate cosmologically. Camp Two, the Organizational-Emergence Restrictionists, accepts that phenomenal properties might be distributed at a fundamental level in some metaphysically thin sense, but denies that valence can exist without a specific organizational architecture—such as integrated information above a macroscopic threshold, or biological homeostatic loops—because phenomenal unity and affective quality require binding mechanisms that decoherence destroys at the fundamental scale; they therefore regard the vacuum-fluctuation scenario not as an empirical risk but as a category error.

Please produce the joint statement. First, provide a numbered section of agreed claims about panpsychism, the physics of decoherence and quantum coherence, and the conditions under which moral patienthood is usually ascribed, phrased so that both camps could endorse them without reservation. Second, present a disagreements table with two columns—one phrased in wording the Extensionists would accept, one in wording the Restrictionists would accept—specifying exactly where they part ways on whether organizational integration is necessary for negative valence, and whether phenomenal density without phenomenal unity can carry ethical weight. Third, identify the cruxes; I take the central ones to be (a) whether phenomenal binding can occur without macroscopic, decoherence-resistant structures, and (b) whether negative valence is conceptually separable from integrated phenomenal unity. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it—for example, a rotating Bose-Einstein condensate sustaining long-range quantum coherence versus an identical system undergoing rapid environmental decoherence—together with each camp’s explicit prediction about what that case demonstrates. Fourth, say whether any portion of the dispute is merely verbal—perhaps an equivocation on “fundamental suffering”—identify which portion, and explain why. Finally, because this is a methodological exercise rather than a definitive adjudication, please state explicitly how confident you are in the framing of these cruxes and in the claim that part of the disagreement is verbal; also single out the specific point about which you are least certain—whether it concerns the coherence conditions required for valence, the applicability of integrated-information measures to quantum systems, or another matter—and briefly explain why that uncertainty persists.
