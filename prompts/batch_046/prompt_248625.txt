I am taking a graduate course on formal epistemology and the limits of rational self-reference, and we have spent several weeks on the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents—the structural constraint, derived from Löb's theorem, that prevents a sufficiently expressive rational agent from consistently assigning full credibility to all of its own future reasoning outputs without encountering something structurally analogous to Gödel's second incompleteness result. Rather than asking for a standard survey, I want to think about where the genuinely live work lies, so I want to inject a framing that seems underexplored in the literature I have encountered: the problem of distributed Löbian tension, in which a network of agents with partial but asymmetric transparency into one another's reasoning architectures attempts to maintain collective epistemic self-trust without collapsing into either universal skepticism or circular mutual endorsement. Given this framing, which open questions or lines of work around the Löbian obstacle are most upstream—meaning that resolving them would restructure how we understand the obstacle's scope—versus which are largely downstream applications? I want a defended prioritization among three candidate directions. First, whether the obstacle can be localized to specific subclasses of an agent's outputs, allowing rational partial self-trust without global consistency failure, and whether this localization survives once we move from single-agent to distributed settings. Second, whether the obstacle represents a substantive epistemic limitation or is largely an artifact of particular formalizations of belief, proof predicate, and modal logic—such that changing the formal machinery dissolves or relocates the problem rather than solving it. Third, whether the constraint propagates differently in multi-agent or distributed reasoning systems where no single node possesses the full collective proof trace, and whether collective self-trust requires conditions stronger or weaker than those that apply to individual agents. For each direction, defend its priority in terms of tractability for philosophical rather than purely technical research, identify which other questions depend on its resolution, and specify what kind of result—formal, conceptual, or applied—would cause you to reorder the priorities. I am not asking for a bibliography or a reading list; I want an agenda that fixes where limited research effort should actually go, together with the criteria—dependency, tractability, and downstream impact—you are using to rank them, and an honest account of which lines you would deprioritize because they depend on one of the above being settled first or because their answer would not change practice regardless.
