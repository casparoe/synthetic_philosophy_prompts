In Scanlon-style contractualism the aggregation problem has been deadlocked for years: Scanlon insists you can't pool minor harms across many people to justify a severe harm to one, critics roll out rescue cases and numbers, defenders reply that aggregation just isn't a moral reason. Everyone knows the moves and nobody budges. Is this disagreement actually about what 'reasonable rejection' means — a hidden semantic fight — or are both sides answering different questions, one about justification and one about practical decision-making? Or is it just bedrock intuitions about whether five lives outweigh one? I want a real diagnosis of the stall, not another survey of the positions, and an honest take on whether a new distinction or formal framing could break it, or if we're just stuck with our starting points.
