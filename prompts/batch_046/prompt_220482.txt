I've mostly bought into origin essentialism -- the idea that if a different sperm had fertilized the egg, that'd be a different person, not me. But I want to stress-test it properly. What would actually count against it? I'm not asking for the standard fission intution pump; I want the hard cases. Like, mitochondrial replacement therapy creates someone with genetic material from three people -- does that fracture the essential link to specific gametes? Or take embryo splitting: if one zygote becomes twins, do both have the same essential origin, and does that cause problems? Also, what metaphysical commitments about persistance does the view need that might clash with how we think about organisms in biology? Rank the defeaters: fatal, awkward-but-survivable, and ones where the essentialist just bites the bullet. And be honest -- has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that actually a problem for a claim like this?
