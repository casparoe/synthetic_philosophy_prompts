I have just come away from a three-hour argument with a friend about Karl Popper’s problem of verisimilitude, and neither of us convinced the other. Write an essay that enters this dispute at the level we left it. The core controversy is whether verisimilitude can do any genuine philosophical work in distinguishing scientific progress from mere instrumental success, given that we know every current theory is false. My friend’s position is that any formal or informal measure of “truthlikeness” necessarily presupposes knowledge of the complete true theory, which makes the concept circular and useless for actual appraisal of living science; I tried to defend the idea that we can rank false theories by comparative explanatory depth, problem-solving range, and resistance to refutation without claiming access to final truth. Take up both sides with real rigor. In particular, address the further objection that verisimilitude collapses into a comparison of predictive accuracy, thereby surrendering its realist ambitions and becoming redundant with standard instrumentalist accounts of theory choice. Do not end on a non-committal note: reach and justify a definite verdict on whether verisimilitude remains a viable conceptual tool for evaluating theories we acknowledge to be false, or whether it should be abandoned. Your reasoning should be accessible to someone familiar with Popper’s general philosophy of science but should not depend on naming, describing, or comparing specific post-1980 formal measures of truthlikeness; avoid citations, bibliographies, and appeals to authority. Make the argument turn on the internal logic of the concept itself.
