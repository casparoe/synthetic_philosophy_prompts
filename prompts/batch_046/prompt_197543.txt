I'm writing a handout for a grad seminar on AI ethics — about 12 people, mostly phil and CS crosovers. I want a taxonomy, like a decision tree with branch labels, that sorts positions on honesty and deception in LLMs and agentic systems. Third branch: is honesty best understood negatively, as absence of optimization against the listener's belief-formation, or positively, as active disclosure of uncertainty, provenance, and internal conflict?

Every leaf should carry a name if there's a real camp — behavioral-functionalism, representationalist deception, transparency-maximalism, whatever — or be marked "unnamed" if the combo seems empty, plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Note any dead branches and say whether they're incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag pairs of leaves your tree can't really separate — maybe representationalists who allow simulated beliefs and transparency advocates end up demanding identical auditing protocols in practice?

Write each node as a concrete claim or case someone could answer without already knowing the label it leads to. Don't ask "are you a behaviorist?" Inject one niche distinction that isn't in every textbook — maybe something about "epistemic rent-seeking by the model" or "provenance tracking as a separate moral category from mere truth-telling" — so the tree isn't just regurgitating standard AI-safety taxonomy. No bibliography, no "see Smith 2024" — half the room can't access paywalled journals anyway. There might be a typo or two in this prompt, that's fine.
