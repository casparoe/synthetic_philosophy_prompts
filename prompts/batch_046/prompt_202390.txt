For a graduate seminar on metaphysical arguments for the divine, I am constructing a diagnostic instrument that maps where a student actually stands on the relationship between the kalam cosmological argument and the contingency argument, rather than asking them to choose a label from a list. The difficulty with standard surveys is that they treat temporal finitude and modal contingency as separate exhibits, without probing whether a philosopher regards them as logically independent, as mutually supporting, or as redundant once one is granted. I want a decision tree—starting from the shared premise that contingent beings require an explanation for why they exist—whose branches are concrete metaphysical commitments that split the logical space before the standard names are introduced.

The first node should ask whether that required explanation must take the form of a temporal origin or first cause, or whether it can be satisfied by an atemporal sustaining ground. For those who reject the temporal requirement, the next split should turn on whether an actually infinite past is metaphysically compatible with every contingent thing having an explanation: specifically, distinguish the view that an infinite regress is always “explanatorily open” (some member remains unexplained) from the view that the infinite whole can achieve “explanatory closure” without circularity, allowing the series itself to be fully accounted for. Further branches should separate positions by whether the necessary being is conceived as timeless, everlasting through infinite time, or causally prior to time altogether; and by whether the argument’s conclusion is interpreted as a personal agent, an impersonal ground of being, or a formal principle of sufficient reason instantiated in reality. Each leaf should carry either a recognized name—such as the classical kalam position, the Leibnizian contingency argument, the hybrid view that derives temporal finitude from modal contingency, or the separabilist stance that denies any entailment between the two—or be marked “unnamed,” accompanied by one sentence identifying its most awkward commitment, for example that an infinite past is fully explained despite every segment having a cause, or that a timeless ground can be causally efficacious without being temporally prior. Please also flag any pairs of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely distinguish—where two positions agree on every branch but differ only in emphasis or vocabulary rather than substance—and diagnose whether any unoccupied combinations in the logical space are incoherent, merely unexplored, or defensible only under auxiliary assumptions not shared by the other branches. The instrument should contain between six and ten leaves, be answerable without consulting texts, and avoid asking for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
