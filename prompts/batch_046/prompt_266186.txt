Write a short story about a translator tasked with rendering a 16th-century French poem into English under strict formal constraints: she must preserve not only the rhyme scheme and meter, but also an acrostic hidden in the original's line-initial letters that encodes a syllogism about determinism. Midway through, she discovers that English phonetics make it impossible to maintain both the surface meaning and the acrostic's logical form; preserving one forces a reversal of the other. The narrative should treat her choice—whether to save the propositional argument or the formal architecture—as a case study in whether an author's "real" intent lies in what the poem says or in the structural constraints that made that saying possible.
