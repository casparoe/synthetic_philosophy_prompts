hey so im a retired biologist who got into philosophy late and ive been trying to sort out when something is really a tragedy of the commons versus just a public goods headache. here are my gut verdicts and they dont line up clean.

1. classic overgrazed pasture — herders add cattle, grass dies. clear tragedy.
2. free public radio, everyone listens, almost nobody donates. i feel like this is different; my listening doesnt use up the signal, so its more of a supply failure, not a commons collapse.
3. our old shared ultracentrifuge: people booked 30 min slots, always ran over, left rotors dirty. next user lost half their slot cleaning. definite tragedy.
4. an open database of failed chemistry experiments anyone can search and upload to. people free ride on searching, rarely contribute. it stays small but doesnt collapse. i call this public goods failure, not tragedy.
5. but then a shared slack channel for our research consortium where people dump half-baked ideas. once noise got high, the senior folks left and good ideas stopped surfacing. i want to call this a tragedy too, even though nothing physical gets used up.
6. community seed bank for heirloom crops: people withdraw seeds and deposit new ones. if withdrawals outpace deposits it collapses. tragedy, but with a feedback loop.

the tension is between 2 and 5 — both involve free riding, but in one the good survives and in the other it dies. and 4 vs 5 are both information goods but i split them. is there one principle that saves all six, or which verdict do i have to toss? also what does the best account predict for a case i havent listed: a city water supply where residents draw unlimited but can also vote on conservation taxes? please be honest about which of my calls it cant save.
