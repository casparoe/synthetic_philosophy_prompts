I'm trying to pin down my view on mind uploading and gradual replacement for a discussion group, and I'd like a consistency check—not a general lecture, but an audit of how these hang together. Here is my current package:

(1) I'm a functionalist about propositional attitudes: beliefs and desires are constituted by causal/functional roles, and substrate doesn't matter for those.
(2) I'm less sure about phenomenal consciousness; I think a unified stream of experience may depend on real-time causal integration—something like dynamic binding—rather than on static information layout alone.
(3) I take personal survival to be a matter of psychological continuity (memory, personality, rational agency), not bodily continuity.
(4) I accept the gradual-replacement intuition: swap neurons one-by-one for silicon equivalents and I survive the whole sequence, with no hidden point where I die and a duplicate takes over.
(5) I deny that destructive scan-and-emulate preserves me: the original is destroyed, and the running simulation is at best a psychological successor lacking the continuous causal integration that sustained my stream.
(6) I think the gradual case involves no metaphysically sharp cutoff—continuity comes in degrees—but for practical and ethical purposes we treat the endpoint as the same subject.
(7) I reject Parfit's "closest continuer" account, mainly because branching shows one-one identity is not what matters, yet I still hold that in non-branching cases there's a determinate fact about whether I survive.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what the cheapest revision to each tension would be. I have a hunch that (3) and (5) sit uneasily with (1), and that (4) and (6) might seem to conflict but don't; I'm not signaling which commitment I'd drop. Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say exactly what has to give.
