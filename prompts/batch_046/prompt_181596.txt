Please explain the conceptual knot at the heart of the epiphenomenalist's paradox of phenomenal judgment, but do so under an explicitly non-standard framing: suppose that phenomenal concepts are not formed through direct qualitative acquaintance, as on the familiar acquaintance model, but are instead structurally indexed—that is, they pick out phenomenal properties exclusively through those properties' structural-functional relations to other mental states, with no intrinsic qualitative transparency. On this assumption, reconstruct the paradox with precision: does the causal inertness of phenomenal properties still block the formation of true judgments about them? If the paradox survives, locate exactly where the explanatory gap relocates—whether to causal explanation, semantic access, or conceptual emptiness. If it dissolves, specify whether the dissolution renders phenomenal judgments epistemically tractable but metaphysically hollow, or whether it collapses the phenomenal-functional distinction altogether. Identify which premises of the standard paradox depend essentially on the acquaintance model and which remain intact under structural indexing.
