The other camp — the Contextualists — says anekāntavāda is fundamentally an ethical-epistemic practice rooted in ahiṃsā (non-violence in cognition), that the seven modes are pedagogical heuristics not formal logic, and that formalizing them either makes the system trivial or inconsistent. They want a joint statement they could both actually sign.

Write it as two sections: first, a numbered list of agreed claims about anekāntavāda, syādvāda, the distinction between non-absolutism and relativism, and what the doctrine actually requires — no vague platitudes, just precise points both sides accept.
