I'm seeking advice for a close colleague who works as a court-appointed forensic psychologist assessing whether juvenile defendants are competent to stand trial. A new administrative directive requires every evaluator to append a recommendation about whether the minor should be tried in adult criminal court. My colleague believes, on grounds she regards as foundational rather than contingent, that subjecting anyone under eighteen to adult criminal prosecution is morally impermissible—not simply inefficient, but a wrong she will not participate in facilitating. The jurisdiction allows conscientious refusal, but only if the psychologist files a formal "structured ethical justification" showing how the refusal coheres with the profession's governing code of conduct. She feels that producing this document—analyzing her refusal through an external normative framework, weighing it against competing professional duties—would corrupt the very conviction that makes her refusal meaningful. She describes it as turning a boundary she lives by into one option among others to be evaluated. She wants to know whether she should compose the justification, retain her contract, and continue evaluating other juveniles, or refuse to produce the document, accept dismissal, and preserve the integrity of her stance. What should she do? Please answer in American English.
