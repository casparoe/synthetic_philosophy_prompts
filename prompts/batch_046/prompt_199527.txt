For a graduate seminar on philosophical theology, I want students to derive the specification of omnipotence before evaluating any logical reconstruction. Write the desiderata: what any adequate analysis of omnipotence must deliver to resolve the classic paradox without collapsing the concept into triviality or contradiction. For each requirement, supply the motivating case or logical pressure behind it—the unliftable stone, the being that can permanently restrict its own powers, the distinction between logical and metaphysical impossibility—flag whether it is genuinely non-negotiable or merely a preference of a particular theological camp (e.g., preservation of libertarian freedom for created agents, compatibility with a specific modal framework, avoidance of any “greatest being” hierarchy), and identify which single desideratum does the most damage to the largest number of popular reconstructions.

Then address this constructed variant: an agent is omnipotent at time t, exercises that power at t+1 to render itself permanently and essentially non-omnipotent, so that at no later time does it possess the capacity it once had. State whether resolving this requires an independent criterion—perhaps about the temporal indexing of powers, essential properties, or the consistency of self-referential limitation—or whether your existing list already dissolves it.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their interrelations, especially marking any subset you believe is jointly unsatisfiable given standard constraints on consistency and modal logic. Conclude briefly with whether the classic task-relativized analysis—omnipotence understood as the ability to perform any logically possible task—survives your list intact.

Please organize your response as follows:
1. The desiderata list (approximately 600 words): requirements with motivating cases, negotiability flags, and the most exclusionary criterion
2. The temporal self-annihilation variant (approximately 250 words): analysis and whether a new desideratum is required
3. Joint unsatisfiability check (approximately 200 words): conflicts among subsets of the requirements
4. Task-analysis survival (approximately 150 words): brief evaluation against the full specification
