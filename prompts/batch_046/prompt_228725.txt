I work in predictive processing and active inference, and I am fluent in the vocabulary of hierarchical generative models, precision-weighted prediction errors, free-energy minimization, and the treatment of beliefs as priors optimized against sensory input. Take Wittgenstein's account of religious belief — specifically his claim that religious language is not a failed empirical hypothesis but operates within a form of life that sets the conditions of sense rather than being tested within them, that religious commitment involves being captured by a picture rather than assenting to a proposition, and that doubt and justification have different grammars inside and outside the religious framework — and restate it systematically in my vocabulary. Treat forms of life as the highest-level priors in a hierarchical model, religious propositions as predictions whose precision is weighted so heavily that no sensory prediction error can revise them, and the distinction between genuine faith and superstition as a difference in how precision is allocated across levels or in whether the model can still generate actionable predictions in other domains. For each element — the non-empirical character of religious commitment, the idea that religious belief is not irrational but governed by its own rules, and the claim that moving outside the religious framework changes what counts as a reason — say what translates cleanly, what must be distorted to fit (for example, treating normative capture as a parameter setting), and what has no expression at all in predictive-processing terms. Then report the residue: is there anything essential to Wittgenstein's view, particularly regarding the non-cognitive, non-instrumental dimension of belief and the idea that religious and empirical commitments are incommensurable rather than merely high-precision, that simply cannot be expressed without smuggling in assumptions foreign to active inference? And does that absence indicate that my framework lacks resources to represent framework-level commitments that are not subject to error correction, or does it suggest that Wittgenstein's distinction between empirical and religious belief is less substantive than it appears once formalized this way? I want the translation to be honest — do not let the vocabulary of precision and prediction error do work that Wittgenstein assigned to grammar and form of life — and I want the residue treated as the most interesting part of the answer.
