Institutional frameworks for military service in liberal democracies often recognize conscientious-objector status for those who reject all war on universal moral or religious grounds, while withholding comparable exemption from citizens who refuse participation only in a particular conflict they deem unjust. What philosophical commitments regarding the authority of individual conscience over state commands, the architecture of moral reasons, and the distinction between categorical and contingent moral judgments does that boundary presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory of civic duty and moral responsibility that renders the distinction coherent, ask whether maintaining it requires a specific view about the objectivity or overriding weight of moral obligations, and specify what changes to the institutional design would be required if the underlying theory were replaced by one that treated selective moral refusal and absolute pacifism symmetrically.
