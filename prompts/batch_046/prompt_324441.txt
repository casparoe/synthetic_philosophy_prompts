I'm in a philosophy of biology seminar, and before we compare competing accounts of what a species is, I want a full specification of what any adequate theory must deliver. Write the desiderata for a theory of biological species. For each requirement, state the motivating case or distinction and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference tied to a particular camp.

The list should include: (1) handling reproductive isolation and gene flow in sexual organisms—including hybrid zones and ring species—without either dissolving boundaries into continua or imposing arbitrary cut-offs; (2) applying consistently across domains of life, especially to asexual organisms and to prokaryotes where horizontal gene transfer makes reproductive isolation moot, and saying whether domain-generalism is required or pluralism is permissible; (3) respecting evolutionary dynamics, so that species are treated as lineages with their own evolutionary fates rather than as static types or arbitrary slices of variation; (4) relating to phylogenetic systematics, specifically whether species must be monophyletic and what the theory should say about paraphyletic grades (e.g., ancestral lineages that persist while giving rise to descendant lineages); (5) distinguishing the status of being a species from questions of degree or kind of status—such as ecological role, conservation priority, or taxonomic rank—without collapsing these distinctions; (6) accommodating the dual use of 'species' as a rank in Linnaean taxonomy and as a counting unit in biodiversity metrics and conservation law, without equivocation; (7) avoiding essentialism, so that membership does not depend on shared necessary and sufficient intrinsic properties; and (8) accounting for cryptic species—reproductively isolated lineages that are morphologically and ecologically indistinguishable—without making morphology or niche occupation definitional.

For each desideratum, give the consideration behind it. Then identify any subset that is jointly unsatisfiable and explain the tension—for example, between strict monophyly across all life domains and accommodating widespread hybridization, or between a single universal criterion and the need for domain-specific treatment. Also flag which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the most popular accounts.

Finally, briefly score three broad approaches against the list: an account centered on reproductive isolation, an account centered on phylogenetic lineage distinctness, and an account centered on ecological niche occupation. Keep the scoring concise; the criteria themselves are the main deliverable.
