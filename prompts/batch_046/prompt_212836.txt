Exam question: Judy Benjamin is initially 50/50 between Red Territory and Blue Territory. She receives a fully credible report consisting of two indicative conditionals: (C1) if she is in Red Territory, then she is in the mountains; and (C2) if she is in Blue Territory, then she is in the valley. She then receives direct, certain observational evidence that she is in the mountains. Analyze how she should update her credence in being in Red Territory. In particular, address (i) whether receiving the conditional report should have shifted her unconditional credence in Red before she observed her location, and (ii) whether the final update upon observing the mountains should be treated as standard Bayesian conditionalization or as Jeffrey conditioning on a partition.

Student answer: Judy starts with P(Red) = P(Blue) = 0.5. By Stalnaker’s thesis, learning a conditional A → C should align her credence in the conditional with P(C|A). Applying this, she accepts C1 and C2, but this concerns only her conditional beliefs; her unconditional distribution over territories remains unchanged, so P(Red) stays at 0.5. Next, she observes that she is in the mountains. This calls for Jeffrey conditioning on the partition {Mountains, Valley}, with the weight on Mountains going to 1 and on Valley to 0. This yields P(Red | Mountains) proportional to P(Red ∧ Mountains) = 0.5, while P(Blue ∧ Mountains) = 0 because Blue implies Valley. Hence her final credence in Red is 1. One might think that accepting C1 allows modus ponens once she observes Mountains, but indicative conditionals do not license modus ponens, so that route is blocked; the Jeffrey update is the correct mechanism.
