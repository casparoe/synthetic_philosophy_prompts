I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I am trying to resolve a design dispute about a minimal embedded sensor I have built for an industrial climate-control system. The hardware is severely constrained: it contains exactly two binary registers, yielding four discrete voltage patterns that shift deterministically in response to direct thermal and pressure inputs. In our internal documentation we map two of these patterns to conditions we call critical overheating and excessive energy waste, but the circuitry itself contains no symbols or representations—only physical state transitions. My co-founder argues that because every semantic content assigned to these voltage patterns originates in our engineering documentation and in the interpretations of downstream users, the device possesses no intrinsic tracking relation to the world; at best, it borrows meaning from us, much like printed words borrow meaning from readers. I see it differently: since the register transitions are causally coupled to environmental conditions through an immediate feedback loop with no intermediate interpreter, I believe the module is tracking those conditions in its own right, not merely serving as a vessel for derived significance. The disagreement has become practically urgent because our system logs every state change to an immutable distributed ledger, and our liability contract distinguishes between autonomous primary measurements and interpreted secondary reports. My co-founder cites a philosophical distinction he describes as original versus derived intentionality, and he specifically invokes a minimal-system thought experiment he calls a two-bitser—a device with only two internal states—to argue that even the smallest causally coupled tracker lacks genuine aboutness and that all of its content is necessarily conferred from outside. I am not persuaded that architectural minimalism alone dissolves intrinsic tracking. Could you help me identify the precise philosophical structure underlying our disagreement? In particular, is there a principled reason to regard my two-register module as genuinely tracking environmental conditions on its own, or does every bit of its semantic content necessarily depend on the engineers and users who assigned the labels? And if the distinction between original and derived tracking is valid, does it turn on the complexity of the internal architecture, or is the fact that my system has exactly two bits of state precisely what makes the case philosophically sharp?
