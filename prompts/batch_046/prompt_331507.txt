Write a sestina on the socialist calculation debate using the six end-words price, local, compute, shadow, converge, and loss. Frame Lange’s trial-and-error board as a distributed algorithm that sends shadow-price messages downward each round, hoping producers will converge on equilibrium without owning the capital; let the sestina’s circular repetition enact that iterative loop. Use the form to show that every convergence on a price vector requires the loss of local, unquantified knowledge—the residue Hayek argued can never be compressed into a message. In the envoi, place Lange’s claim that the center can compute and Hayek’s reply that computation is not the problem in adjacent lines without resolving them. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows the debate recognizes both positions, and the structural return of the end-words should mirror the unending chase after a price that never fully captures what it prices.
