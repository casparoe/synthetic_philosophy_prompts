I'm serving on our university's synthetic biology oversight commitee and we have to decide whether a new lab-built protocell counts as a "living organism" under our containment rules. The thing replicates through an artificial ribozyme loop and keeps its internal pH stable, but it lacks any lipid membrane and doesn't take in energy from outside — it just runs down its chemical gradient until it stops. Our policy manual defines living entities as "self-maintaining systems with bounded metabolism," which seems to cover normal bacteria but leaves this in a gray zone. Some members want to apply the NASA working definition — metabolism, reproduction, evolution — and say it fails the metabolism part; others cite that newer process-philosophy view where life is less about having parts and more about maintaining a continuous organizational flow over time, which this system arguably does. I was discussing with my friend in philosophy and he thinks the board is conflating ontology with regulation, but I'm not sure that helps us vote.

I need a formal memo recommending approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions for classifying it under living-organism protocols. The memo should propose concrete criterias the lab can actually check, treat the strongest argument on the losing side fairly, and avoid just listing philosophers — this is a decision document, not a literature review. Write it in American English please, and sorry if there's a typo or two since I'm typing fast between meetings.
