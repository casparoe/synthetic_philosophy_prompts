Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains (A1 and A2) and rhyme scheme A b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 A2—on Simone Weil’s critique of political parties, particularly her argument that parties function not as associations of citizens but as machines for manufacturing collective passions that suppress independent moral judgment. The speaker should be a party member who gradually discovers that the enthusiasm they experience is not their own but an imposed affect, and that the demand for solidarity has replaced their capacity to discern justice from injustice.

Use the villanelle’s repeating lines structurally: one refrain should be a collective party declaration or slogan that grows more hollow or coercive with each recurrence; the other should be the speaker’s original private moral judgment, which becomes progressively buried, distorted, or silenced. The turn—placed at the transition from tercets to quatrain—should mark the speaker’s recognition that genuine justice requires the ability to dissent from the collective, a capacity that party membership structurally eliminates. Do not resolve the tension: the closing quatrain must present both refrains together, unreconciled, leaving open whether individual conscience can survive institutional belonging.

The poem should render Weil’s position with enough precision that a reader familiar with her essay on party abolition recognizes the specific mechanism she describes—parties generating collective passion, substituting loyalty for thought, and making members incapable of justice—rather than treating it as generic anti-partisanship. At the same time, inject a conceptual twist not usually emphasized in standard summaries: frame the party’s corruption of judgment as a problem of epistemic closure that operates not through false beliefs but through the replacement of first-person deliberation by a collective affect that feels like one’s own. Keep the language precise, avoid sermonizing, and let the formal repetition of the villanelle enact the mechanical repetition it describes.
