I'm preparing a position paper for a philosophy of physics seminar on Einstein's hole argument, and I want to defend what strikes me as the strongest available dissolution: not merely that the argument fails because bare manifold points lack independent physical identity, but that determinism in general relativity must be formulated exclusively over diffeomorphism equivalence classes of metrics. On this view, asking whether a metric evolves differently "inside a hole" is already to misapply determinism at the level of coordinate representations rather than gauge-invariant physical states.

Play it straight: what would have to be true for this stronger dissolution to collapse? For each potential failure point — a revived substantivalism about spacetime points individuated by primitive thisness or by non-dynamical relational structures; a reformulation of determinism that requires uniqueness at the representational level; or an interpretation of general covariance that treats diffeomorphisms as physical symmetries with empirical content rather than descriptive redundancy — say what the formalism or the ontology would need to look like, and distinguish routes that are live in current work (for instance, discrete spacetime or causal-set approaches that might restore point-like ontology) from those that are merely logically possible. I am especially interested in whether this position insulates itself by definitional fiat: does defining determinism over equivalence classes just rename the problem away, and is that a theoretical virtue or a methodological vice? Finally, indicate whether any observation or theoretical development — perhaps involving quantum correlations in regions with non-trivial topology — could genuinely resurrect the hole argument in a form that even this dissolution cannot absorb, or whether the view is genuinely well-insulated and, if so, whether that insulation is a strength or a weakness.
