i keep running into this at the think-tank i work with: we review development proposals that claim some local cultural value — say, a particular honor code or attitude toward risk — is causally driving an economic outcome like low entrepreneurship or high savings, in a kind of Weberian 'spirit of capitalism' move but applied to regions that have nothing to do with Protestantism. The reviewers split every time: some say it's a legitimate cultural explanation via elective affinity, others say it's just correlation with institutions or post-hoc rationalization. I want a reusable review procedure, grounded in Weber's actual argument about how values become causes — specifically the distinction between value-relevance and value-judgement, and the idea that culture works through elective affinity with material conditions rather than direct determination — that a non-sociologist reviewer can apply offline in the field with no library access. It should have a clear sequence of tests (institutional mediation check, counterfactual substitution for the value, mechanism tracing through intermediate practices), worked examples of one proposal where culture genuinely passes and one where it looks causal but fails, and explicit flags for the steps where human judgement can't be eliminated. Please include the case the reviewers won't like — where the procedure rejects a culturally intuitive explanation — and be honest about what Weber's framework just can't settle, especially when distinguishing a value's causal role from its rationalization requires interpretation that the procedure can't automate.
