Please argue for the following position. Begin with Putnam's Twin Earth case for natural-kind terms: Oscar and Twin Oscar share identical narrow psychological states, yet 'water' refers to H2O on Earth and XYZ on Twin Earth, suggesting that mental content is partly fixed by the environment. Now extend this to hybrid demonstrative-descriptive terms—expressions like 'this clear liquid, which I have always called water'—where a speaker's mental file includes both a perceptual-demonstrative pointer and a historical chain linking the term to its original baptism. If that mental file embeds an indexical component tracking the causal origin of the baptism event, then Oscar and Twin Oscar do not actually share identical narrow contents: their files differ in the historical-indexical parameter that anchors the demonstrative to either H2O or XYZ. From this, defend the claim that semantic externalism about natural kinds fails to demonstrate that narrow psychological states are insufficient for mental content; instead, it reveals that the narrow states were never genuinely identical, because any competent speaker's conceptual file for such a term already contains a hidden historical index. Argue that this collapses the externalist victory into a form of historical internalism, and explain why treating demonstrative anchors as transparent—rather than as parts of the mental representation—creates only the illusion that the environment alone fixes content.
