I have noticed a recurring structure in cases where someone seems to deceive themselves without ever telling themselves a direct falsehood. The person adopts a goal with clear, strict success conditions, and privately records outcomes against those conditions. Over time, however, they publicly evaluate themselves using gradually relaxed criteria, treating each relaxation as an exception warranted by context. Crucially, they do not discard the private record; they preserve it as uninterpreted data or notes for later review, never allowing it to correct the public assessment. When others point out the discrepancy, the person does not deny the private record but insists it lacks the contextual framing needed for proper judgment. This is not simple lying to others, since the public account is sincerely held, and it is not ordinary suppression, since the counter-evidence remains accessible to the agent. Is there an established term for this particular configuration — especially the split between a preserved but quarantined evidential record and a sincerely maintained, criterion-shifting self-evaluation? How does it differ from compartmentalization, bad faith, or motivated reasoning? If it has no standard name, how should we draw its boundaries: for instance, does it require that the private record remain genuinely accessible, or could it work with a fully unconscious tracking mechanism?
