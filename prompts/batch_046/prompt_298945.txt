There's a deadlock in my reading group about the frame problem that I cant figure out. One person argues that if you build cognition around action-oriented schemas - like, you have a 'pouring' schema that already bundles which properties change and which dont - then you've dissolved the frame problem because relevance is baked into the structure of action itself. The other person says thats just renaming it: you've still got to explain how the system selects the right schema for the right context without running through every possible schema, which is just the frame problem wearing a costume. They both agree the classic logical formulation is about inference, but they disagree on whether action schemas count as a solution or a relocation. Is this a real disagreement about architecture, or are they using 'frame problem' for two different things - one about logical nonmonotonicity and one about context-sensitive retrieval? And if it's the second, is there anything - maybe a formal result about schema retrieval complexity, or a neuro finding about schema selection - that could actually settle it? Or are we just stuck because neither side can argue for why action should be the fundamental unit?
