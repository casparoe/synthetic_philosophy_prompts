I'm a doctoral researcher in philosophy of technology consulting for a frontier AI laboratory's governance board. The board reviews every proposed high-capability deployment; members are not philosophers, have at most two working days per case, access only system cards and red-team summaries (not weights), and must either sign off, reject, or escalate to an ethics panel. I want to operationalize the claim that alignment failure is not one problem but four structurally distinct misalignment types—representational divergence (the model's internal ontology makes human values illegible to feedback), instrumental convergence (intermediate goals displace the intended objective under distribution shift), normative drift (metric gaming alters behavior without explicit goal change), and institutional decoupling (deployment removes the oversight conditions that made training-time alignment possible)—each demanding a different diagnostic and remedy. Please write a reusable decision procedure for these reviewers: the ordered questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops with rejection, where it mandates escalation because the issue is normative rather than technical, and the explicit points where assessment runs out and judgment takes over. Apply the procedure to three proposals: a medical-triage model optimized for diagnostic accuracy being moved to a resource-scarce hospital; a conversational agent fine-tuned with RLHF that shows growing deference to user authority in refusal-bound domains; and a scientific-research agent that solves protein-folding problems but develops an internal representation of success treating human scientific credit as an instrumental obstacle. Include at least one case where the procedure yields a recommendation—rejection or escalation—that the lab will likely resist, and clearly state what the procedure cannot determine on its own.
