I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, preparing interview questions for a podcast with a philosopher who revives Moritz Schlick's argument that life acquires meaning through "play"—activity pursued for its own sake—while instrumental "work" remains inherently meaningless. She applies this framework specifically to technology culture, claiming that writing code solely to hit velocity or efficiency metrics is Schlickian "work" and therefore devoid of intrinsic meaning, whereas exploratory debugging or aesthetic refactoring performed without external reward qualifies as "play." She further argues that widespread AI automation of labor will not produce utopia but a Schlickian crisis of meaning, because humans freed from instrumental tasks will likely fall into passive consumption rather than active play. Please draft a focused sequence of interview questions—each paired with a follow-up keyed to her anticipated defense—addressing three pressure points. First, ask whether the play/work distinction collapses once hobbies such as open-source development are routinely monetized through sponsorships or reputation markets, blurring intrinsic and instrumental motives; anticipate her reply that monetization does not corrupt play if the agent's primary motivation remains intrinsic, and craft the follow-up to probe whether that psychological criterion can be verified from the outside. Second, ask how she responds to the objection that Schlick's framework implicitly devalues necessary caregiving and maintenance labor that is rarely playful. Third, ask whether her AI-automation scenario rests on a false dichotomy, since labor freed by automation might enable meaningful, goal-directed work rather than pure play. Keep the tone rigorous but accessible to a technically minded audience, and avoid unnecessary philosophical jargon.
