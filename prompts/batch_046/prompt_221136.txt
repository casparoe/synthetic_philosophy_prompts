I was discussing with my friends after our philosophy of religion course and we got stuck on what feels like a new twist on the omnipotence paradox, though maybe it's just an old one in disguise. If omnipotence is defined task-based — being able to perform any task that isn't loigcally contradictory — then there should be a task like "create a being that can perform a task that the original being cannot perform." That task doesn't seem logically contradictory on its face; it's just a comparative power claim, not a square circle. So an omnipotent being should be able to perform it, but then there's a task the original being can't do, which contradicts omnipotence. Theologians usually respond by saying omnipotence doesn't include logically impossible tasks, but describing this comparative task doesn't involve a formal contradiction in logic — it's more like saying "draw a line longer than the longest possible line," which sounds conceptually confused but not syntactically contradictory. So is the mistake thinking that "more powerful than omnipotent" is logically possible? Or is there a hidden contradiction I'm not seeing?

I feel like the error is somewhere between task-definition and maximal properties, but I can't pinpoint it. Maybe I'm smuggling in an assumption that power is always scalable like height, when omnipotence is supposed to be a ceiling? Anyway, where exactly does this reasoning slip, and what does fixing it cost us in terms of how we understand divine power?
