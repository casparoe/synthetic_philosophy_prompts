I have recently begun volunteering with a wildlife rehabilitation center, and watching injured raptors recover through instinct alone—without any understanding of anatomy or intention to heal—has made Darwin's inversion suddenly personal. At the same time, I have been debugging a simple pattern-matching script that performs its task correctly despite my inability to articulate how it does so, which brought Turing's parallel inversion home: a competence that runs ahead of comprehension. I want to close a small gathering with a poem that holds these two inversions together without collapsing them into a single metaphor.

Please write a poem of exactly sixteen lines, divided into two stanzas of eight lines each, in iambic pentameter. The first stanza should present the traditional reasoning order—comprehension preceding and directing competence—using imagery of human craft, design, or deliberate instruction; its rhyme scheme should be ABABCDCD. The second stanza should present Darwin's and Turing's inversions: natural selection producing adaptive function without foresight or understanding, and computation producing correct behavior without semantic comprehension. Its rhyme scheme should literally reverse that of the first stanza (DCDCBABA), so that the formal structure enacts the conceptual inversion. Between the stanzas, the human agent or first-person voice should disappear; the turn should occur at the boundary, not within a stanza.

Do not name Darwin or Turing in the poem, and do not include bibliographic references, citations, or reading suggestions. The concepts should be recognizable to someone familiar with the philosophy of biology and philosophy of mind, but rendered with enough precision that a specialist would not wince—specifically, avoid suggesting that natural selection has intentions, and avoid suggesting that computation requires consciousness. Leave the tension open: competence without comprehension should appear not as a failure but as a strange, productive inversion. The poem should stand on its own for listeners who do not know the theories, yet reward those who do.
