I'm on our university's student governance reform board, and we've been asked to decide wether to approve a pilot scheme for the student union elections next term. The proposal is that students can trade votes on specific ballot items — not candidates, just issues like funding allocations — using an internal token system. So if you don't care about the sports centre renovation but care deeply about mental health funding, you could swap your vote weight on the first for extra influence on the second. The claim is that this respects preference intensity and makes the union more responsive.

But half the board thinks this is basically vote selling dressed up in digital tokens, and that it corrupts democratic equality — one person one vote becomes however many tokens someone can accumulate through trading. The other half says it's just preference aggregation, no different from coalition-building, and that banning it is paternalistic.

We need a memo for the full senate recommending approval with safeguards, rejection, or a conditional one-term pilot. I want it to take seriously the idea that some goods shouldn't be commodified even if efficency goes up — not just dismiss it as old-fashioned. But I also want an honest engagement with the efficiency argument. The recommendation needs to be concrete, with a clear time frame — say whether to run a single term pilot or kill it outright — and it should acknowledge the strongest point on the side that loses.

Could you draft this for me? Please write in British English, and don't worry about formal citations — just reason it through properly. There's probably a typo or two above, sorry.
