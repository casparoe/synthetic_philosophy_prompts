In debates over Sen's minimal liberalism, a familiar move is to analogize the assignment of decisive rights to installing separate thermostats on a single central HVAC loop in a shared house: each resident legitimately governs their own comfort setting, but the loop is physically coupled, so one person's liberal adjustment forces thermal conditions on the other. Proponents use this to argue that Sen's paradox — where the prude ranks neither-reading > self-reading > other-reading, the libertine ranks self-reading > other-reading > neither-reading, and minimal liberalism gives each authority over whether they themselves read, generating a cycle that blocks Pareto-superior outcomes — is not a deep tension but an engineering error. If the domains were genuinely separated (private apartments with independent systems), the conflict would disappear, so liberalism and Pareto efficiency are reconcilable once we stop forcing coupled choices into a single social state space.
