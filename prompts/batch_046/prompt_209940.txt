I want your decomposed credence in the claim that Avicenna's flying-man thought experiment establishes a genuinely non-corporeal mode of self-awareness sufficient to ground the substantial independence of the rational soul from the body. Decompose it into: (a) the probability that a subject could maintain first-person self-awareness in the total absence of exteroceptive and proprioceptive input; (b) the probability that such awareness, if sustained, is best explained by direct acquaintance with a non-physical substance rather than by a formal self-model or a retained neural trace of prior embodiment; (c) the probability that the thought experiment's isolation conditions can be coherently specified without tacit reliance on embodied spatial framing or sensory memory; and (d) any further factor you judge essential to the inference. For each factor, give a numerical credence and a single line of justification, then show how they combine. Identify the factor in which you have the least confidence, and specify whether structured first-person reports from prolonged sensory-isolation conditions could plausibly revise it, or whether such reports are necessarily contaminated by residual bodily awareness and therefore conceptually incapable of testing the claim.
