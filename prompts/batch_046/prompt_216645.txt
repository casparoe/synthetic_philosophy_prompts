Draft the joint statement that a conceptual-impossibility camp and a psychological-reality camp on self-deception could both sign. Camp A maintains that intentional self-deception is conceptually incoherent: deception requires one party to know a proposition and conceal it from another who lacks it, and identifying deceiver with deceived eliminates the knowledge asymmetry that defines deception, leaving at most self-induced error. Camp B maintains that self-deception is coherent and common, operating not through explicit contradictory belief but through partitioned cognition, motivated inattention, or graded credence management, so that an agent can genuinely aim to sustain a false view in one cognitive subsystem while knowing better in another. Produce: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about belief, intention, deception conditions, and the structure of agency; (2) the exact disagreements in neutral wording, with each camp's formulation accepted for its own position; (3) the cruxes, especially whether the self must be unitary for deception to be defined and whether probabilistic belief representations dissolve the contradiction; and (4) for each crux, a concrete hypothetical scenario both sides agree tests it, with each camp's predicted description of the agent's mental state. State explicitly if any portion of the disagreement is verbal rather than substantive. Do not include citations, reading lists, or references to specific authors.
