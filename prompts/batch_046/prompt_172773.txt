Here are four commitments I have come to hold about suffering, pleasure, and evaluative comparison, and I want to know whether they form a jointly tenable package or whether some pull against others in ways I have not seen.

(1) I accept a lexical-threshold version of negative utilitarianism: there is some kind or magnitude of suffering that no amount of pleasure can outweigh, but below that threshold, pleasure and pain are fully intersubstitutable on a single scale.

(2) I maintain that if such a threshold exists, it must be anchored to a qualitative discontinuity in the experience itself—something like the irreversible dissolution of rational agency—rather than to an arbitrarily high quantitative intensity. Otherwise the boundary looks like a conventional cut on an otherwise smooth hedonic curve.

(3) At the same time, I regard pleasure and pain as ontologically homogeneous: they are opposite directions of one hedonic vector, comparable in principle across all contexts in the way temperature is comparable, with no irreducible plural dimensions.

(4) I reject any pluralist or domain-segregated theory of value; I want every evaluative comparison to be mediated by a single metric, not partitioned into incommensurable spheres.

My request is an audit, not an exposition. Specifically: does (3) undermine (2)'s claim that the threshold is qualitative rather than quantitative? Does (1) require a form of incommensurability between suffering and pleasure that (4) forbids? Is there a hidden tension in trying to ground a non-arbitrary discontinuity within a vector that is supposed to be continuous and unitary?

Order the tensions by severity. For each, say what must give—whether (2)'s qualitative anchor collapses into quantitative convention, whether (4) must admit a partial pluralism, or whether (1) itself must be abandoned. Also identify any apparent tension that dissolves on inspection. Conclude by stating what the cheapest revision is and what the package becomes—ordinary smooth-scale negative utilitarianism, value pluralism, or something else—once consistency is restored.
