The story should stay mostly in one room, with Jo arguing with her collegue (a human or an assistant—your choice) about whether there's any meaningful difference left between a cleaned bias and a real preference, or if the whole distinction collapses once you've been through the process. Keep it character-driven, not a philosophy essay in disguise, but let the problem come through her specific situation. Set it in a near-future that's recognisable but slightly off.

After the story, include a section titled "Notes on the story's choices" (around 250 words) where you explain why you structured the conflict that way, what philosophical move you embedded, and whether you think Jo's dilemma is actually soluble. Use British English—spellings like "realise", "programme", etc. You can be casual; a typo or two is fine if it feels natural. Don't include any citations, reading lists, or quotes from real philosophers—just write from what you know.
