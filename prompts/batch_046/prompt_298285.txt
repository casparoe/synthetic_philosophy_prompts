I just spent the better part of an evening arguing with a close friend about whether historical writing can ever be genuinely objective. We circled Ranke’s aspiration to present events exactly as they occurred, Carr’s insistence that historians select and shape facts long before they reach the page, and Hayden White’s argument that all historiography is structured by underlying narrative tropes—romance, tragedy, comedy, satire—that do more work than the archive ever could. By the end we agreed that if a university tried to institutionalize this disagreement, the result would be some kind of absurd bureaucratic audit. I want you to write that audit.

Write a satirical parody, roughly 650 words, in the deadpan voice of an official university administrative memo announcing a mandatory “Narrative Objectivity Audit” (NOA) for all doctoral dissertations in history. Format it with numbered sections, bullet points, and a sidebar rubric. The memo should present an invented quantitative framework—call it the “Integrated Historiographical Compliance Index”—that assigns scores for Rankean source purity, Carr’s fact-selection transparency, and White’s tropological disclosure. The satire must exaggerate real features of these three positions rather than inventing strawmen: for example, the audit should genuinely try to measure “emplotment mode” as if it were a methodological choice rather than a structural condition, should treat Carr’s problem of selection as a correctable procedural error, and should assume Ranke’s archival ideal can be achieved through stricter documentation rules. Do not state the objections explicitly; let them emerge from the bureaucratic logic itself. Before drafting, spend a moment outlining which specific feature of the audit corresponds to which historiographical problem, so the parody remains internally consistent and fair.

You are a professional philosopher of history with expertise in historiographical theory and institutional rhetoric. Think carefully about the target—the practice of reducing conceptual debates about historical knowledge to administrative metrics—and make sure the humor comes from that reduction, not from mocking any individual thinker. Write in American English. Begin with the memo text itself, with no preface, no framing commentary, and no restatement of this request.
