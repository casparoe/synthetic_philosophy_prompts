my friend alex and my coworker jordan got into it over lunch about whether a delivery drone's navigation system literally 'believes' its internal map is wrong when it reroutes. alex said sure—if treating it as having beliefs predicts its behavior better than treating it as just weights and sensors, then the intentional stance applies and we're done. jordan said thats too quick: with humans we only attribute beliefs because there's counterfactual stability—if you believed X you'd do X across a bunch of nearby situations—but this drone just updates its state mechanically whenever the lidar feed changes, so there's no real modal robustness. alex came back that robustness is a spectrum, not all-or-nothing: if the camera was occluded the drone wouldn't update its map-state, so the 'belief' is causally integrated with other parts of the system. jordan said thats just good engineering, not psychology; calling it belief sneaks in normative expectations about rationality that circuits don't have. alex said we're not sneaking in norms, we're using the exact same predictive shortcut we use for a thermostat. jordan replied that thermostats are precisely the case where we avoid belief-talk and just mention set-points, so alex is overreaching by treating a control loop like an agent. neither would budge. can you adjudicate this move by move—rate each step and say who's closer to right? also, what are they both missing, especially about systems that have no self-model but still seem to track the world?
