Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a concluding quatrain, with two refrains and two rhymes—staging the dispute between presentism and eternalism as an argument about the existence conditions for cross-temporal relations. Assign the first refrain, “Only what is now is real,” to the presentist voice, and the second refrain, “What was and what will be are real as well,” to the eternalist block. Let the compulsive return of these lines enact the metaphysical deadlock: each repetition should occur in a context that presses whether the presentist can ground truths about the past—say, that some present object bears the relation of being older than a past object, or that a present event was caused by an earlier one—without admitting the existence of past relata, and whether the eternalist can preserve the asymmetry of causation and the openness of the future if every time is equally real. Use the tercets to build the tension that presentism threatens to make change mysterious—if existence is confined to the present slice, in what robust sense does anything genuinely become—while using the quatrain to turn sharply, like a volta, to the question of whether treating “now” as an indexical analogous to “here” dissolves the ontological privilege of the present or merely relocates it. Do not resolve the tension into a sermon about impermanence or a shrug about the circle of life; instead, let the rhyme scheme lock the two refrains into unavoidable proximity so that the poem ends with the standoff intact. Render the conceptual stakes with enough precision that a metaphysician of time would recognize the truthmaker problem for presentism and the problem of temporal passage for eternalism, without invoking any named author, specific publication, or bibliographic reference.
