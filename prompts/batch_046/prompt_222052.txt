I've been arguing with a colleague in an aesthetics seminar about whether the moral conditions of an artwork's production can be structurally inseparable from its aesthetic form without being representationally present in the work itself. To fix the idea concretely, imagine a large-scale orchestral composition—call it a palimpsestic symphony—whose harmonic architecture and temporal pacing were generated through a compositional process that necessarily relied on the creator's sustained, deliberate deception of vulnerable collaborators, yet the finished score contains no thematic reference to deception, betrayal, or harm, and listeners without any biographical knowledge consistently report profound and stable aesthetic satisfaction. The deception is not illustrated; it functions as an invisible causal scaffolding for the precise structural features that produce the aesthetic effect, so that altering the production history without altering the score seems conceptually impossible.

Given that framing, I want your actual credence—not a range, but a specific number between 0 and 1—that such a work can still be genuinely good art, understood here as possessing substantial positive aesthetic value that is not merely instrumental or ironic. Please decompose the reasoning into explicit terms: first, the probability that aesthetic value is in principle separable from the ethical conditions of production (an autonomist factor); second, the probability that causal-structural inseparability undermines appreciation in a way that mere representational absence does not (a scaffolding-corruption factor); third, the probability that the audience's ignorance of the production history is aesthetically or ethically irrelevant rather than contingently exculpatory; and fourth, any remaining factor you think decisive—perhaps whether "good art" implicitly requires that the work be available to uncorrupted appreciative engagement. Assign each term a rough probability with one line of justification, show how they combine into your final number, and identify which term that number is most sensitive to. Then specify what kind of variant case—say, one where the deceptive scaffolding is partially audible in the score's dissonances, or one where the harm caused was trivial—would most move your credence and in which direction.
