State your actual credence, on a scale from 0 to 1, that the gender-equality paradox — stronger occupational gender segregation in societies with more extensive gender-egalitarian norms and economic development — is best explained primarily by the liberation of culturally mediated preferences rather than by residual biological predispositions or by hidden structural injustices that survive formal equality of opportunity. Do not reply that it depends on framing; provide a specific number and decompose it.

Break the reasoning into four explicit terms: (a) the empirical robustness of the paradox after distinguishing horizontal segregation (choice of field) from vertical segregation (rank and compensation) and after controlling for measurement artifacts; assign a probability that the pattern is real and not spurious. (b) The conditional probability that, if the pattern is real, its dominant driver is unconstrained preference expression rather than residual structural sorting, occupational prestige gradients, or unintended policy effects. (c) The conditional probability that biological sex differences contribute negligibly to cross-national variance in horizontal career choice, given widespread formal equality. (d) The normative weight you place on the premise that justice in career outcomes demands proportional representation across fields rather than merely equal opportunity and non-discrimination.

For each term, give a rough number and a one-line justification. Then show approximately how they combine into your final credence. Identify which term is least certain and would move your overall number the most if revised, and describe — without citing specific studies — what kind of cross-national evidence or conceptual clarification would cause that revision.
