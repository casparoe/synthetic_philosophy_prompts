I was arguing with a friend over coffee about whether determinism still matters if we can never actually predict the weather past a few days. He pulled out the old pool-table analogy: the universe is basically like a giant billiards game where every ball's path is fixed by the laws of motion, so in principle if you knew the exact start positions and velocities you could compute every future bounce. He said chaos theory just means our measurments are sloppy, not that determinism fails, and that the pool-table picture proves determinism is still true even if it's useless for actual forecasts. I pushed back and mentioned I'd heard about something called the KAM theorem which apparently shows some deterministic orbits stay stable forever even when you nudge them, plus that in real chaos the error grows exponentially rather than linearly. He said I'm mixing up practical limits with metaphysical ones and that I'm overreading a physics result.

Can you lay out what exactly maps onto what in the pool-table analogy — which features of real billiards carry over to physical determinism and which don't, where the mapping breaks especially around sensitivity and measurement error, whether the KAM result actually undermines the chaos story or just qualifies it, and whether his conclusion — that determinism survives intact but prediction dies — actually holds up once all the disanalogies are on the table? Also, if the pool table turns out to be a bad picture, is there a better everyday analogy that captures both determinism and the exponential blow-up of error without making it sound like hidden randomness?
