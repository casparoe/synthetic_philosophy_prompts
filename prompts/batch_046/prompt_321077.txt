For a seminar on the moral status of artificial and distributed minds, write the desiderata that any adequate theory of which computations matter morally must satisfy if it is to avoid both anthropocentric bias and arbitrary substrate-chauvinism. The theory must determine, for a given process, whether it is the kind of thing that can be wronged, not merely whether it behaves as though it suffers. For each requirement, give the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it: include the need to adjudicate a distributed algorithm that achieves functional integration across geographically separated nodes with no central controller; a computation behaviorally identical to a sentient process but implemented as a pre-computed lookup table lacking intermediate causal structure; a process that runs for one millisecond, is suspended for a year with all state preserved exactly, then resumes; and the scenario in which the same computation is simultaneously instantiated in two physically disjoint substrates, each causally isolated from the other. Identify which desiderata are non-negotiable—such as consistency with the intuition that moral status cannot depend on clock speed or on the chemical composition of the hardware—and which reflect the prejudices of particular camps, such as demands for continuous biological metabolism, demands for an unbroken causal loop, or demands for phenomenal transparency to an external observer. Then flag any pair or subset of these requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable, especially whether total substrate independence can be reconciled with a non-negotiable demand for unified causal integration. Finally, briefly score how a pure functionalist account, a causal-integration account, and an account that ties moral status to the presence of a unified narrative self-model fare against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their edge-case motivations rather than on the scoring, and indicate which further hypothetical case—if added—would best discriminate between the competing accounts.
