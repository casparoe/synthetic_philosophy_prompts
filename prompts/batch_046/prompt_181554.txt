I am a retired ethologist who, since leaving the laboratory, have been working through the philosophy of action and free will. I am applying for a six-month independent research fellowship, and I need a two-page proposal turning a half-formed idea into a tractable project. My suspicion is that the free-will debate has been asking a structurally wrong question: it treats determinism as a uniform property of actions, whereas comparative biology distinguishes sharply different kinds of mechanical rigidity. The sphex wasp’s nest-sealing ritual—repeated with identical movements even when the egg is removed—exemplifies what I will call sphexishness: a fixed action pattern lacking error-correction or contextual modulation. But other deterministic biological processes, such as reflex arcs with gain control or neural circuits with feedback loops, are equally deterministic yet flexibly adaptive. I want to ask whether constructing a taxonomy of rigidity drawn from these biological cases can show that what matters for agency is not determinism per se but the presence or absence of particular control architectures, and whether this dissolves or sharpens standard puzzles like Frankfurt-style manipulation arguments.

Please write the proposal with the following elements. First, state the precise question: can sphexishness serve as a model for one specific type of behavioral rigidity, and does distinguishing this type from other deterministic-but-flexible mechanisms reframe the conditions for free action? Second, explain why existing compatibilist, libertarian, and hard-determinist positions leave this open: they typically contrast “mechanical” with “free” without asking which deterministic architectures preclude the flexibility relevant to agency. Third, describe the method and timeline over six months: I will construct a three-part taxonomy of rigidity (sphexish repetition, deterministic reflex with feedback, deterministic plastic circuit); apply it to two standard philosophical cases—Frankfurt’s hierarchical control scenario and a manipulation argument—to see whether the taxonomy reveals conflations between sphexish control and other deterministic controls; and then argue either for a revised framing of agency around control architecture rather than determinism, or for a negative result showing the taxonomy collapses back into the standard determinism/indeterminism distinction. Fourth, specify the deliverable and failure conditions: success would be a conceptual framework that philosophers of action can use to classify manipulation cases more finely; failure would be if every biological distinction maps cleanly onto existing philosophical categories with no remainder, or if applying the taxonomy to human action requires speculative neuroscience that exceeds the project’s conceptual scope. Fifth, give an honest scope assessment: six months permits conceptual taxonomy and application to existing arguments, but not empirical study or formal modeling. Do not include a bibliography, literature review by citation, or reading list—I will supply references. Finally, please say explicitly what would change your mind: identify the specific argument or consideration that would convince you that the distinction between sphexish rigidity and other deterministic processes is philosophically irrelevant to free will, and flag where in your reasoning you are most likely to fail.
