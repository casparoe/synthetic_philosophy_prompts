Compare Buddhist anatta with Parfit's reductionist view of personal identity—both reject a permanent self, but one comes from meditative practice and the other from teleportation thought experiments. Here's a weird case to stress-test them: suppose someone's mental processes are slowly copied into two independant substrates that never recieve feedback from each other, like a functional split-brain without the surgery. Parfit might say psychological continuity just splits into two relations; anatta might say there's never been an owner to split in the first place. But which view actually explains why the original person should—or shouldn't—care about both copies? Push on whether either framework smuggles in a hidden 'owner' when it talks about concern for future experience, or if they genuinely avoid that. Don't just summarize the usual differences; dig into this specific duplication scenario and see where they actually conflict.
