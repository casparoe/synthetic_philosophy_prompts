Write a poem of exactly eighteen lines arranged in six tercets, using the form of a simultaneous double sestina: Voice A (rest frame) and Voice B (constant velocity v) alternate tercets, both rotating through the same six line-ending words—light, mirror, now, slice, return, stand—so that each voice completes one full rotation over its three tercets. Both voices describe the same four spacetime events—emission from a source, reflection at a distant mirror, return to the source, and a distant clock-strike—but because simultaneity is frame-relative, Voice B pairs the word "now" with different partner-words in its rotation than Voice A does, never aligning it with the same event. Use no rhyme beyond the repeated end-words; in the first three tercets each voice should complete its cycle with "now" landing in a different structural position. Conclude with a shared couplet (outside the tercet form) in which both voices speak one line together, stating that the disagreement is about slice-assignment, not about existence. The poem should render the Rietdijk–Putnam reasoning accurately: special relativity makes any global "present" observer-dependent, so presentism lacks a unique slice and only a four-dimensional block remains, with all events equally real. Do not cite authors, texts, or dates, and do not include a bibliography or reading list; the formal mismatch of the two rotations should itself show why there is no invariant "now."
