I am auditing my own epistemic commitments after a recent psychiatric crisis—specifically, a prolonged episode of severe depersonalization and subsequent treatment—has made hinge epistemology personally urgent rather than merely academic. I have come to hold the following views, and I need to know whether they form a consistent package or whether I am smuggling in incompatible motivations.

(1) I accept the Wittgensteinian picture developed in On Certainty: hinge propositions—such as that I have continuous bodily existence, that the external world is not a recent fabrication, or that I have not always been on the moon—are not themselves items of knowledge or justified belief. They are the fixed framework conditions within which justification and doubt operate, lying outside the space of giving and asking for reasons.

(2) Because of my recent experience, in which basic bodily hinges genuinely collapsed for me—where I could not take for granted my own physical continuity—I now think that hinges can be locally and temporarily destabilized for an individual without that individual being globally irrational or delusional, provided the destabilization is recognized as abnormal and is not treated as a discovery.

(3) I hold that the correct response to such destabilization is not evidential but practical and therapeutic: one restores hinges by re-engaging in embodied action and social practice, not by accumulating proof or argument.

(4) I also think that which propositions function as hinges is form-of-life relative; there is no universal, culture-independent inventory, since different practical contexts make different things fixed.

(5) Despite (4), within any given stable form of life I want to preserve a genuinely graded distinction between hinge-level commitments and ordinary empirical beliefs, with the latter being more or less well-supported in a non-binary fashion.

(6) To determine, in my own case, which beliefs should be assigned hinge-status and which should be treated as open to revision, I rely on reflective equilibrium—adjusting particular judgments and general methodological principles until they achieve coherence.

(7) I reject evolutionary debunking arguments directed at hinge commitments on the ground that hinges are not cognitive mechanisms aimed at truth-tracking and therefore cannot be debunked by showing unreliable origins; at the same time, I think evolutionary anthropology can legitimately explain why certain hinges—belief in other minds, in physical continuity—are widespread across human societies.

Which of these commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and what is the least costly revision that would restore consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging, and specify for each whether commitment (1), (3), (4), (6), or (7) has to give or be qualified. I am not asking for a general exposition of hinge epistemology; I want an audit of this particular set.
