In studying Wittgenstein's On Certainty, I have become stuck on why the literature about hinge propositions never seems to reach agreement. One group treats hinges as non-propositional framing conditions that make justification possible without being justified themselves; another treats them as a special class of arational knowledge or entitlement; and a third insists that Wittgenstein was not advancing an epistemological thesis at all but dissolving a pseudo-problem about doubt. Despite extensive exchange, these positions appear immovable. Is this stalemate a substantive disagreement about whether certainty is a cognitive state, a verbal conflict over how knowledge and belief are grammatically deployed, or a case of three different inquiries—logical structure, phenomenology of doubt, and therapeutic clarification—being conducted under one heading? And if your diagnosis is correct, what kind of move—an analytical distinction, an empirical observation about belief-fixation, or a reframing of the original question—would actually count as progress rather than repetition?
