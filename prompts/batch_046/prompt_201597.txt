I'm taking a course on historical explanation and philosophy of science, and I've noticed a recurring argumentative pattern in discussions about institutional and technological persistence that I don't have a precise word for. Consider debates over why certain suboptimal standards remain dominant—perhaps a particular input layout that persisted despite technically superior alternatives, or an administrative protocol that survives despite well-documented inefficiencies. The explanation usually begins with a contingent branching story: an early, somewhat arbitrary adoption that generated increasing returns and gradually locked in the standard. Yet when I press the counterfactual—whether a genuinely different initial condition could have yielded a permanently better equilibrium—the same interlocutors often retreat to structural inevitability, insisting that deeper functional or economic pressures would have produced an isomorphic outcome regardless of the branch point. The contingent narrative serves to excuse present dissatisfaction, while the inevitable narrative serves to block reform imagination, and I keep seeing this exact double deployment in cases where the speaker wants both to lament current arrangements and to deny that they were genuinely avoidable.

Does this pattern have an established name in the literature on historical lock-in, or is it merely an unexamined tension between contingency and determinism? I'm not asking for a bare label; I want the carving. What distinguishes this rhetorical move from ordinary debates about historical necessity—does it require the same agent to deploy both frames, or can different parties in a debate split the labor? Where are its edges: does it apply to biological evolution and legal precedent, or only to human-designed systems with explicit network effects? And if there is no recognized term, what conceptual work would be needed to define one that captures not just the coexistence of both explanations, but the selective invocation of each to serve opposing argumentative goals?
