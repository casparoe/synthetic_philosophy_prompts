I serve on the review board for long-term institutional designs—policies meant to persist for decades, like data architectures, governance protocols, or resource-lock mechanisms. I want a reusable audit procedure based on Nietzsche’s eternal recurrence, interpreted strictly as a normative filter rather than a cosmological claim: a structural feature is permissible only if it is one you could will to recur identically across every iteration of the institution’s lifespan, with no reliance on historical accident to mitigate harm.

Write the procedure as an ordered checklist for a board member with no philosophy training. It should include: (1) a pre-filter that separates structural invariants from contingent implementation details; (2) the core recurrence test, specifying what counts as “identical recurrence” (here distinguish token-recurrence—the very same decision-node—from type-recurrence—the same structural role filled by different tokens—state which the procedure adopts and why, and note the misfire if this distinction is ignored); (3) the “will” check that distinguishes active affirmation from passive resignation, with one concrete behavioral sign to look for in the proposal’s defenders; (4) explicit stop-points where the answer is “no”; (5) explicit hand-off points where the question is values-based rather than structural and must go to human deliberation rather than the checklist.

Then run it on two proposals: (a) an irreversible open-access genetic database with no sunset clause (this should trigger rejection, even though openness is usually valued); and (b) a permanently restricted resource pool with strict, transparent access rules that survive leadership turnover (this should pass, despite looking like hoarding). For each, show the step where judgment enters, flag the misfire risk—especially applying the procedure to one-off moral choices rather than institutional architectures—and state what the procedure genuinely cannot do. Keep every step runnable in under ten minutes in a meeting, specify the evidence needed at each gate, and be honest about where the procedure will yield answers the board will not like.
