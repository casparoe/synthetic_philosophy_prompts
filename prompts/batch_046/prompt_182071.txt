ok so here's roughly where I've landed on Stoicism and I want a consistency check before I present it to my discussion group. Might have a typo or two in my notes but you get the idea.

(1) I take the dichotomy of control literally: what's up to me is my assent to impresssions, my intentions, and my moral character; outcomes, other people's opinions, my bodily health, and whether Stoicism survives as a living school are all externals.

(2) I think the modern revival — Holiday, Pigliucci, that crowd — is mostly self-help fluff because they cut out the Stoic physics and the idea of living according to universal reason / logos. Without the cosmology the ethics just floats.

(3) I apply this in practice: I do local climate organizing and vote, but I deliberately don't check poll numbers or emission reports, because tying my self-worth to those results is exactly the mistake the dichotomy warns against.

(4) At the same time I think CBT is closer to ancient Stoicism than those pop books are, since CBT and Epictetus both locate emotional distress in our judgments about events, not in the events themselves.

(5) I hold that vice is the only true evil and that suffering caused by externals can't damage my eudaimonia; but I also think societies have a genuine duty to prevent needlessly harm, and that letting people suffer is a kind of injustice, not just a missed preference.

(6) Lastly, if I'm being consistent I shouldn't waste mental energy worrying that people misrepresent Stoicism or that it might die out — that's about other people's minds and the future, both clearly not up to me.

So: is this a coherent package? Specifically, is there a hidden contradiction between (2) and (4) since both drop the cosmology? Is (5) pulling against the strictness of (1) by giving suffering a moral weight it shouldn't have? And does (3) actually hang with (1), or am I secretly treating activism like it's up to me? Rank the real tensions by severity and tell me what's the least costly revision — I'd rather not just list everything as "problematic."
