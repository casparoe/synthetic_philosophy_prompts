Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism denies that fire, cotton, or any created substance possesses intrinsic causal efficacy, attributing each particular burning event directly to divine will with no mediating necessary connection. Contemporary interventionist theories of causation, by contrast, analyze causal relations in terms of invariant patterns that hold under idealized interventions on variables, without requiring metaphysical powers or essential natures. Given this, if we model an occasionalist universe as a causal graph in which God is the only exogenous variable and all created entities are purely endogenous nodes with no edges between them, haven't we shown that occasionalism does not eliminate causation but rather centralizes it? Specifically, wouldn't this framework treat Al-Ghazālī's position not as the denial of causal structure, but as a limiting case of interventionism where every intervention traces back to a single source? And if that reconstruction succeeds, why do philosophers of science continue to dismiss occasionalism as incompatible with empirical methodology rather than treating it as a degenerate but formally valid causal model?
