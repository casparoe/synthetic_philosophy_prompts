I sit on the allocation committee for our university's shared HPC cluster, and we've accidentally built a bidding system that works like the dollar auction but with GPU-hours and institutional credit. Every quarter departments submit sealed bids for cluster time; the top bidder gets the slots, but both the winner and the runner-up have to pay their full bid into a maintenence pool. There's also a rollover rule I think is key: if you lose, 40% of your burned credit carries over as a loyalty multiplier on your next bid, which means departments that keep losing actually gain bidding power for future rounds. Over the past year the bio and physics groups have sunk enormous staff hours into proposal writing that exceeds the value of the compute they win, and now half the committee wants to kill the all-pay structure entirely while the other half says the escalation proves real need.

I'm writing the memo for Thursday's meeting. Recommend a concrete course of action with a timeline: either reform the rollover rule by the end of this quarter, switch to a flat fee model next quarter, or keep the current mechanism with a hard cap on bids. You should take seriously the view that the sunk costs are rational signals of departmental priority rather than a trap.
