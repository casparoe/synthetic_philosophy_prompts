I've been refining my position on welfare design ahead of a policy discussion with some friends from the rationalist-adjacent crowd, and I want a consistency audit before I present. Here is the package I currently hold: (1) the category of the undeserving poor is conceptually incoherent—there is no administrable desert-base for poverty, and evolutionary debunking undermines our reciprocity-based judgments about who merits aid; (2) despite this, I defend strict workfare and conditionality, not on moral-desert grounds, but purely as an information-revealing mechanism—mandatory job-search creates a costly signal that separates claimants with hidden alternatives from those genuinely lacking them, and I accept the associated dignity cost for the screening gain; (3) I simultaneously believe universal basic income is the superior long-run architecture precisely because it eliminates the undeserving-poor framing and removes invasive screening altogether; (4) yet I also maintain that adopting unconditional UBI strengthens, rather than weakens, the justification for soft paternalism—restricted-use transfers, mandatory savings vehicles, or in-kind benefits—because unconditional cash creates moral hazard that threatens the reciprocity norms required to sustain political support for the program; (5) I take the welfare state's core purpose to be social insurance against arbitrary misfortune rather than poverty reduction per se, which implies benefits should be universal but modest; and (6) I hold that economic reasoning about information asymmetry and signaling is not subject to the same evolutionary debunking that applies to our moral intuitions about desert and reciprocity. The set feels like it pulls in opposite directions—workfare versus UBI, unconditional support versus stronger use-conditions, debunking reciprocity while relying on it politically. Please go through the pairs, rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, identify which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve on inspection, specify for each genuine tension what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs me in terms of the other commitments, and do not assume I already know which thesis I would drop—just trace the commitments.
