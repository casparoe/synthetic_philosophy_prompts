economists debating why some nations are rich keep reaching for the split-country analogy -- north vs south korea, haiti vs dominican republic, east vs west germany -- like its a twin study where geography and culture are the shared DNA and institutions are the different upbringing. they say this lets us read causality straight off the outcome gap. but im skeptical. can you map it out precisely: what corresponds to the twins, the shared genome, the separated environment, the measured phenotype? then tear it apart: the division wasnt random assignment, borders often trace pre-existing ethnic or economic fault lines, theres massive spillover and migration across the line, and institutions are a moving bundle not a single treatment. also, does framing this as a natural experiment smuggle in medical-trial assumptions about exogeneity and unit homogeneity that just dont apply to macro-history? and if the twin analogy falls apart, can the institutionalist argument still stand on its own, or was the analogy carrying the weight the whole time? answer in american english please.
