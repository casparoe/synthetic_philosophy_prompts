I've been working through a metaphysics of dependence for an advanced seminar on fundamentality, and I'd like a consistency audit before I commit to the package in writing. Here is where I've landed:

(1) Grounding is a single, unified relation of ontological priority—irreducible to causation, supervenience, or epistemic explanation—but I allow its formal behavior to be domain-sensitive: deterministic and law-like in physics, constitutive in normativity, and entailment-based in logic.

(2) The relation is well-founded and irreflexive for all contingent chains, terminating in exactly one fundamental stratum: the concrete physical world (fundamental physics or spacetime regions). Every contingent non-physical fact is ultimately grounded there.

(3) Mathematical and logical truths are necessary and do not depend on physical entities for their existence; they are either ungrounded or grounded in purely structural relations that are themselves necessary, though these relations do not belong to the contingent physical base.

(4) Normative ethical facts are grounded in complex configurations of natural, descriptive facts via a necessary, non-causal connection: precisely duplicate the natural base, and the normative properties are fixed.

(5) Institutional and social facts—property rights, currency, citizenship—are grounded in collective intentionality, which is grounded in individual mental states, which are grounded in brain states and hence in physics, with no circular dependence running back upward.

(6) Grounding is hyperintensional: necessarily co-extensive descriptions can differ in whether they serve as the ground for a derived fact.

(7) I also hold that necessary truths may enter benign, closed loops of mutual grounding, which I do not treat as violating well-foundedness because that principle governs only contingent descent.

I'm particularly worried about whether (2) can survive (3) without either reducing mathematics to physics or multiplying fundamental strata; about whether (4) and (6) can coexist if hyperintensionality allows a necessary-equivalent redescription of the natural base to break the normative link; and about whether (5) genuinely avoids circularity once the content of collective intentionality is recognized as referring to the very institutions it grounds. But I don't want to prejudge which of these is the serious fracture and which only looks like one. Could you audit the set? Rank the genuine tensions by severity, specify for each what must be revised and at what cost; identify the tension that dissolves on closer inspection and explain why; and say whether a single revision—say, weakening the uniqueness claim in (2), abandoning hyperintensionality, or restricting well-foundedness more explicitly—would resolve the largest cluster of problems together.
