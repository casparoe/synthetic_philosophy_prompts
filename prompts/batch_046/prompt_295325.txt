After assuming legal guardianship for my father, whose frontotemporal dementia has progressively dissolved his linguistic and practical patterns, I have observed those same patterns resurfacing—sometimes precisely, sometimes distorted—in my own behavior. This has made Douglas Hofstadter's suggestion that an I can be distributed across brains feel urgently concrete rather than merely theoretical. I am applying for a one-year philosophy-of-mind dissertation fellowship and need a formal two-page proposal. My half-formed thesis is that Hofstadter's account of self-persistence in others is incomplete because it relies on pattern similarity alone, neglecting what I will call loop-closure: the condition that the receiving brain must actively maintain the migrated patterns within the same artifactual and environmental feedback structures that originally sustained them. Without loop-closure, I suspect, what transfers is not the self but a degraded symbolic echo.

Please construct the proposal with the following elements, in a polished academic register: (1) a precise statement of the research question—specifically, whether loop-closure is a necessary condition for genuine distributed persistence of an I or merely an optional reinforcement; (2) an explanation of why Hofstadter's loop-based theory and standard psychological-continuity accounts both fail to settle this, identifying the gap each leaves open; (3) the proposed method—conceptual analysis of clinical and cross-generational pattern-migration cases, coupled with a philosophical model of feedback-loop dependency—together with a justification for choosing this conceptual-philosophical approach over empirical neuropsychology or purely textual interpretation; (4) a clear description of what a successful result would look like (for example, a criterion that distinguishes true inter-brain persistence from echo, with illustrative boundary cases) and what would count as project failure (for example, if loop-closure collapses into ordinary causal influence or proves indistinguishable from standard continuity conditions); (5) a realistic month-by-month timeline for the fellowship year, assuming no laboratory access and no need for primary empirical data collection. Do not include a bibliography, literature review by citation, or requests for references; I will add those myself. The proposal should remain answerable without online search and should avoid invoking highly specialized technical literature that a general philosophy-of-mind reader would not recognize.
