I'm drafting a novel about a surgeon who developed pain asymbolia after a thalamic lesion — she feels the cut, the fracture, the burn, but none of it comes with that usual "get away from this" pull. I keep writing her injury scenes wrong: either she comes off like a stoic superhero or like a broken robot, and neither feels true. I want a reusable procedre I can run in about five minutes before I draft any bodily-damage scene, grounded in whatever philosophy actually knows about whether pain is bad because of its sensory texture or because of its affective-motivational grip. Specifically, I need ordered questions that check whether the sensory signal is present, whether the affective tagging is coupled or decoupled, whether there's a cognitive overlay (she knows it's damage but doesn't feel the aversion), and whether secondary harm is building up despite the lack of withdrawal. Each step needs a clear yes/no/maybe with what counts as enough to call it, plus a hard stop where I declare "this is genuinely bad for her" and one where I say "this is neutral data." There has to be a gray zone where the procedre hands the decision back to me because it's a values call — like, do I want the reader to feel awe or pity? — and I need you to flag that explicitly.

Then run it on three scenes: one where she holds her palm flat on a hot stove and watches the skin blister without pulling away; one where she breaks two ribs falling off a ladder and keeps talking calmly about the surgery schedule; and one where the answer comes out cold and utilitarian in a way I probably won't like as a writer — maybe chronic joint erosion she ignores for months because there's no felt urgency. Tell me exactly where the procedre misfires or can't settle the case, especially with accumulated damage that lacks any single dramatic moment. And be honest about what this tool can't do: it can't tell me whether her lack of suffering makes her more free or more alienated, and it definitely can't write the sentence for me. Don't send me a bibliography or ask me to look up papers; I just need the checklist, the three worked examples, and the warning labels. My editor's waiting, so keep it practical.
