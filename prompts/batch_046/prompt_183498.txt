I'm preparing remarks for a graduate seminar on Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling, and I've been keeping a running record of my advisor's verdicts on which figures genuinely achieve the teleological suspension of the ethical. Her track record so far is consistent but not obviously reducible to one formula: Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac counts as a true suspension, since the particular stands in an absolute relation to the absolute and the ethical universal is genuinely suspended; Agamemnon's sacrifice of Iphigenia does not count, because he remains fully within the universal of state and family, suffers as the tragic hero, and is intelligible to the community; Jephthah's sacrifice of his daughter also fails, since the vow binds him within a religious-ethical universal that the people can understand and mourn together; a modern silent conscientious objector who refuses military conscription on the basis of an incommunicable private revelation, accepts imprisonment without protest or explanation to any court, and reports only profound inner anguish, is classified as a genuine knight of faith; but a political theologian who assassinates a tyrant claiming direct divine mandate and then publishes a manifesto universalizing the command for all citizens is not, because he mediates the particular into universal discourse.

The case I need her to adjudicate next is that of Dr. Elena Voss, a trauma surgeon who, on the eve of an emergency operation, becomes convinced she has received a direct, particular command not to operate on this specific patient. She cancels the procedure, the patient dies, and she calmly explains to the medical board and the family that God forbade her specifically, using only particularist language with no appeal to universal medical ethics or general moral principle. She accepts revocation of her license and criminal liability for negligence without resistance, reports no anguish but rather serene certainty, and never attempts to justify her action as a model for others. Predict her verdict, identify the closest analog among her earlier cases, and lay out the competing implicit rules—perhaps one centered on the impossibility of mediating the particular into universal ethical language, one centered on the affective structure of suffering isolation, and one centered on the direct override of the universal by a particular divine relation regardless of communicative posture—stating what each predicts about Voss. Give your best-guess verdict with reasoning, and then design one further hypothetical case that would discriminate between the two rules that happen to agree on Dr. Voss.
