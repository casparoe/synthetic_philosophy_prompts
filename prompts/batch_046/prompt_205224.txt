Write a joke that turns on the conceptual core of the Gould-Lewontin spandrels critique of adaptationism. Frame it as a deadpan fictional exchange—perhaps a Q&A after a seminar—between a committed Panglossian adaptationist and a spandrels advocate. The adaptationist should defend an obviously non-adaptive human trait (choose one: the philtrum, the chin, or the particular consistency of earwax) as a direct product of selection for its current function, constructing an elaborate just-so story about mate choice, acoustic resonance, or pathogen trapping. The spandrels advocate should rebut not by denying selection altogether, but by showing that the trait is a necessary architectural byproduct of another developmental feature—facial fusion, mandibular geometry, or glandular layout—and that any present utility is an exaptation rather than the original adaptive target. The punchline should depend on the adaptationist being forced, by the internal logic of the spandrels argument, to accept an implausibly specific selective pressure, while the advocate notes that the trait varies or disappears in precisely the patterns predicted by the underlying architecture rather than by functional optimization. The humor must come from the actual methodological dispute—whether every biological feature demands an adaptive explanation—not from generic biology jokes. Make it precise enough that someone familiar with evolutionary theory recognizes the real distinction between adaptations and spandrels, but do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific pages or publication years; the joke should stand on its own for a reader with no access to external sources.
