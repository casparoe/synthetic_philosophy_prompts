In a seminar on philosophy of cognitive science, we've been working through Dennett's taxonomy of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures—and I keep encountering an analogy that people use to translate it for audiences in artificial intelligence: the four levels are treated as successive generations of computational search architecture. A Darwinian creature is mapped onto a hard-coded lookup table with zero exploration; a Skinnerian creature onto a hill-climbing reinforcement learner that probes an environment and caches successful policies; a Popperian creature onto a model-based planner that runs internal simulations before committing to action; and a Gregorian creature onto an agent that queries an external cultural database—language, shared representations, and tools—thereby offloading computation it does not perform itself. The analogy is frequently deployed to argue that contemporary large language models are essentially Gregorian entities that have skipped the lower evolutionary layers, and that this absence of embedded Skinnerian trial-and-error or Popperian world-modeling explains why they lack genuine understanding or grounded intentionality.

I'd like you to audit this analogy systematically. First, lay out an explicit correspondence table: for each creature type, identify the analog in computational search—what plays the role of the fitness landscape, the exploration policy, the internal world model, and the external knowledge base—along with what corresponds to evolutionary time, energy cost, and representational format. Then show where the mapping fractures: does the software analogy misrepresent the biological hierarchy by treating the layers as strictly cumulative substrates rather than as independently available strategies? Does framing Gregorian cognition as an API call to culture obscure the way language is also an internalized, embodied capacity rather than purely external retrieval? Separate the disanalogies that genuinely undermine the normative conclusion—that missing lower layers makes an LLM an incomplete mind—from those that are merely decorative. Finally, assess whether the claim that LLMs lack understanding because they bypass Darwinian-Skinnerian-Popperian development survives once the analogy is dismantled, or whether the analogy itself was carrying the entire persuasive weight.
