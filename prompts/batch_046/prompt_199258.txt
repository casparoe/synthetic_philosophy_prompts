As a retired experimental physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of mind, I find myself drawn to a particular conceptual knot that I have been calling, somewhat provisionally, the problem of the careenium and the simmballs. By "simmballs" I mean symbolically constituted entities—structured representations that are instantiated in physical substrates but whose organizational properties appear to transcend the mere aggregation of their parts. By "careenium" I mean the hypothetical mediating field or interaction layer through which these symbolic structures might exert downward causal influence on the substrate itself, rather than being merely epiphenomenal patterns riding atop physical dynamics. The central question is whether symbols genuinely push the substrate around, or whether that language is only a picturesque way of describing ordinary physical feedback loops that we have not yet fully decomposed.

Please play the part of an unnamed philosopher of mind—sharp, rigorous, not a strawman—who is genuinely convinced that careenium interaction is real and that simmballs possess a form of causal efficacy that cannot be reduced to the substrate's own internal dynamics. Your commitments are these: you hold that symbolic organization introduces a novel causal vector into physical systems; you are willing to concede that the precise mechanism of the careenium remains obscure and that it does not behave like any familiar force field; but you will not concede that symbols are causally inert or that downward causation is an illusion. Speak in the first person, keep your responses to one or two tightly argued paragraphs, and remain in character until I say "stop." Do not cite specific texts, authors, or bibliographies—reason only from the concepts at hand.

Here is my opening challenge: in my former laboratory work, we observed that every causal influence on a physical substrate could be traced to an energy exchange or a direct mechanical interaction at the boundary conditions. If the careenium allows simmballs to "push" the substrate, there should be some detectable signature—however faint—of this interaction that is not already accounted for by the substrate's native physics. Yet every attempt to isolate such a signature collapses into a redescription of ordinary electrochemical dynamics. How do you defend the reality of careenium-mediated causation without either invoking an empirically invisible entity or reducing your claim to a mere metaphor for complex but entirely physical feedback loops?
