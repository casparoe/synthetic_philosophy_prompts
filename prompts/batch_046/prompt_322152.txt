Next month I present my dissertation chapter at the joint philosophy-of-mind and clinical neuropsychology colloquium. The thesis I defend is that pain asymbolia—reported cases in which subjects retain intact nociceptive discrimination and sensory intensity ratings but deny that the experience is unpleasant, aversive, or action-motivating—does not vindicate the separation thesis that affective badness is a contingent, separable layer added to an otherwise neutral sensory core. Rather, I argue that asymbolia reveals a failure of what I term evaluative binding: the structural integration by which a first-order nociceptive representation is incorporated into the subject's practical self-model with the weight and directional valence required to generate avoidance behavior. On this account, pain's badness is neither an intrinsic phenomenal quality (as the essentialist phenomenologist claims) nor a purely representational content about bodily damage (as the representationalist claims), but a mode of ownership in decision architecture. The audience includes a representationalist about pain affect who holds that badness just is the representation of disvalue; a phenomenologist committed to the view that aversiveness belongs to the essence of pain, so that asymbolia must involve misreporting, dissociation, or a change of subject; and a clinical neuropsychologist who interprets these cases as disconnection between anterior insular and prefrontal valuation circuits, and therefore regards my philosophical framing as an unnecessary redescription of a mechanistic disconnect. Give me the objections in roughly the order they will be raised, phrased as each interlocutor would phrase them, noting for each whether I should concede, hold firm, or qualify. Identify the weakest point in my own position that I should actively defend rather than hope goes unremarked. Finally, include the objection I am most hoping nobody asks—likely from the phenomenologist about whether evaluative binding is merely a functionalist redescription of the very aversive quality I claim is absent in asymbolia—and indicate how to answer it in the two minutes available.
