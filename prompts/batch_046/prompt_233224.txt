I take the view that Wigner's problem regarding the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics in natural science is dissolved rather than solved: physical theories are formulated in mathematical terms because only mathematically tractable regularities survive sustained predictive testing; mathematicians frequently develop tools in response to physical intuition rather than in isolation; and failures of mathematical modeling—particularly in high-complexity biological, ecological, or social domains—are systematically ignored, generating an illusion of universal applicability. I further hold that this dissolution requires a modal-structuralist ontology in which mathematical objects are not independent Platonic entities but possible forms of consistent description, so that applicability is not a mysterious bridge between realms but the selection of an appropriate descriptive frame within a single modal space. What would have to be true for this dissolution to fail? I want the evidence, arguments, and conceptual results that should move me, sorted by decisiveness: for example, genuinely predictive deployments of mathematics developed in complete isolation from physical motivation and only later found precise physical analogues; domains where mathematical tractability systematically misleads rather than illuminates; or philosophical arguments that modal structuralism collapses into a disguised Platonism. I also want an honest assessment of whether the position risks unfalsifiability—whether any apparent counterexample can be dismissed as falling outside the domain of physical law or as not being the right kind of mathematics—and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a claim of this kind. Finally, can the selection-bias account accommodate the specific effectiveness of continuous, linearizable structures in quantum field theory without reducing that effectiveness to a tautology, or does that case expose a genuine residue that the dissolution cannot absorb?
