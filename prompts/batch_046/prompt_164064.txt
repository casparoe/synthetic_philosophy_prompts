ok so my friend and i got into it about hegel's master-slave dialectic and recognition after a couple beers, and i can't shake this pattern i keep noticing. in the classic version the master demands recogntion from the slave but can't value it because the slave isn't free, right? but there's this other configuration where the master literally can't exist without the slave's labor — like those open-source maintainers who take every patch, rewrite it in their own idiom, and present the result as one pure vision, or like celebrity chefs who take line-cook recipes, rename them, and present the menu as pure auteur genius. the contributers or cooks are technically voluntary, but their work gets absorbed and anonymized. the "master" needs that flow or the project or restaurant dies, yet if they admit the others are co-creators the whole hierarchy of "visionary vs raw material" collapses. so they depend on the slave staying a slave, but also need them to keep producing. my friend said that's just exploitation with extra steps, but i think it's a specific failure mode of recognition — the master needs the slave's labor but has to deny them the status of a recognizer to keep being the master. is there a name for this? not just capitalism, but the exact point where dependency and denial of recogntion lock together? and what's the boundary — when does ordinary dependence become this parasitic thing where you need someone to stay invisible so you can stay visible?
