As a journalist who covers science and philosophy of mind, I have come to hold a specific view about explanation and ontology that I want to stress-test rather than defend. I take Daniel Dennett’s idea of real patterns not as stance-independent entities but as products of predictive compression, and I treat the physical, design, and intentional stances not as ascending levels of abstraction but as competing compression schemes with no neutral arbitration procedure between them. On this account, a pattern is real precisely when adopting it yields predictive gains that cannot be matched by any translation into another stance; the design stance is not a genuine intermediate layer but a degenerate case of the intentional stance applied to systems that lack recursive self-modeling. A tension has recently struck me: imagine a synthetic cognitive architecture—call it a reflexive predictor—that maintains a high-fidelity physical model of its own substrate only when operating below a certain computational threshold, but switches to an intentional self-model above that threshold. The same system then seems to instantiate real patterns in two stances simultaneously, with no principled reason to privilege either account. What would genuinely count against this overall position? I want decisive defeaters separated clearly from merely awkward ones: which empirical findings, formal results, or conceptual distinctions could undermine the claim that stance-choice is pragmatic rather than ontological? In particular, what would it take to show that the design stance is genuinely autonomous—neither reducible to physical dynamics nor fully absorbable into intentional explanation? And has my view been qualified into unfalsifiability by allowing any system to shift stances ad hoc depending on context, and if so, is that insulation a serious defect for a thesis of this kind? Please also indicate what a committed defender of this compression-based stance-relativism would themselves concede as counting against them, not only what outside critics allege.
