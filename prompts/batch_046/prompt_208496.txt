I've been defending a pretty specific reading of Neurath's ISOTYPE project in my thesis: that his picture-language system isn't just pedagogical but rests on a kind of visual logical atomism, where complex social statistics can be decomposed into discrete, recombinable pictoral units without losing relational meaning. So ISOTYPE becomes not just a teaching aid but a genuine rival to propositional logic for social-science representation. What would actually count against this? I don't want the standard 'pictures are ambigious' objection. I want defeaters aimed at the atomism claim itself: results in cognitive science or icon semiotics about how people parse visual relations, cases where decomposing data into discrete icons destroys the very statistical relationship being shown, and whether modern data-visualisation work undermines the idea of a universal visual grammar. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a committed Neurathian would concede versus dismiss, and flag whether the position risks being insulated into unfalsifiability.
