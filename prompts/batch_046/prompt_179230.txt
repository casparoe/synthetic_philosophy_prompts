Please explain the conceptual gap between diachronic coherence conditions—those that prevent an agent from being Dutch-Booked across sequential choices—and synchronic coherence conditions—those that enforce the independence axiom within a single decision frame—in arguments for expected utility maximization. In particular, address the following tension: an agent may be fully protected against intertemporal money pumps, yet systematically violate the Sure-Thing Principle in one-shot choices if her evaluation of outcomes depends on the "path" by which they are reached, provided that this path-dependence cancels out across separate betting markets. Clarify why such path-dependence need not generate a detectable Dutch Book, and whether it therefore undermines the coherence argument for expected utility maximization or merely reveals that coherence conditions must be applied jointly across decision frames rather than evaluated separately within them. Finally, assess whether this kind of framing-sensitive utility representation constitutes a normatively significant failure of rationality, or whether the coherence argument was never intended to rule out representations of this form. Ground your answer in the structure of preferences, belief updating, and betting behavior rather than in specific named theorems or bibliographic references.
