As a software engineer without formal philosophical training, I have noticed a recurring argumentative pattern in debates about algorithmic design and engineering ethics, and I am trying to determine whether it corresponds to an established concept or whether it needs a distinct name.

Consider two situations. In the first, an advocate for a content-ranking system observes that the current ordering "simply tracks observed user engagement" — a descriptive claim about mechanism and data. From this, they argue that replacing engagement with another criterion, such as source credibility, would "introduce an external value judgment" into an otherwise neutral pipeline. If pressed directly on whether user engagement implies that engagement ought to be maximized, they deny any normative inference and insist they are only describing the existing baseline. In the second situation, an auditor proposes an explicit normative framework for ranking, and critics respond by treating that proposal as subjective interference, while treating the engagement-based baseline as an objective, value-free default. When it is pointed out that maintaining the engagement baseline itself requires normative commitments — for instance, treating attention as a proxy for merit — the advocate retreats to the claim that they are merely describing system behavior, not endorsing it.

The pattern is not a straightforward derivation of "ought" from "is," because no explicit moral conclusion is drawn from a factual premise. Instead, the descriptive state of a deployed system is treated as a privileged, neutral reference point, and any departure from it is framed as a normative intrusion, despite the fact that selecting, maintaining, and optimizing that baseline involves normative choices. It also differs from ordinary psychological status-quo bias: the defense is not reluctance to change, but an epistemic framing in which the current implementation functions as a default against which alternatives are measured as "interventions."

Is there an established term for this specific configuration — one in which a system's descriptive state is used as an implicit normative anchor without an explicit normative premise? How does it relate to, and differ from, the naturalistic fallacy and the is-ought problem? And does it connect to any recognized discussion of implicit normativity or baseline neutrality in technology?

If no precise term exists, I would like a proposed name and definition, together with clear boundary conditions: specifically, what separates it from ordinary design conservatism, from appeals to nature or to the way things are, and from cases in which a system genuinely lacks normative commitments? I am not seeking a bare label; I want the carving — exactly what the phenomenon includes, what it excludes, and where its edges lie.
