I'm writing up my metaphyics of persistence for a discussion group and want a consistency check before I present. Here are my commitments:

(1) I'm an endurantist - ordinary objects exist fully at each moment they're present, with no temporal parts spread out like worms.
(2) I think change is real and unproblematic: the same thing can be bent at t1 and straight at t2, and we avoid contradiction by indexing properties to times.
(3) I think in a brain-fission scenario both successors are genuinly identical to me - psychological continuity doesn't have to be one-one, so identity can branch.
(4) I reject four-dimensionalism partly because instantaneous temporal parts can't stand in causal relations.
(5) I think artifacts like ships survive total part-replacement if functional continuity holds, which is just endurantism applied to designed things.

Do these hang together? Specifically, doesn't (3) clash with (1) since I'd be wholly present in two bodies at once? Is (4) actually a decent reason, or am I smuggling in an endurantist bias? Which tension is worst, which ones are only apparent, and what's the cheapest revision - weakening (3), giving up (1), or ditching (4)? Rank them by severity and say what each fix costs me.
