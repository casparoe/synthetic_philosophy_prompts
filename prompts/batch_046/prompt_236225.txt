I am preparing materials for an advanced philosophy-of-physics seminar, and two long-time interlocutors—one who defends structural substantivalism about spacetime points and one who defends full relationalism—have agreed to suspend their usual polemics and produce a joint adversarial-collaboration document on Einstein's hole argument. Rather than rehearse the textbook presentation, they want to examine a specific, somewhat under-discussed extension: a hole-region containing not only the gravitational metric but also a minimally coupled scalar field, with an active diffeomorphism that is the identity outside the region but rearranges both geometric and matter properties inside it. They need to determine whether this coupled case strengthens the relationalist conclusion, because both sectors are now redistributed, or whether it gives the structural substantivalist room to argue that points are still individuated by the combined qualitative relational structure of both fields, leaving only a harmless gauge redundancy rather than ontological indeterminacy.

Write the joint statement they should both be able to sign. Begin with a numbered section of agreed claims—covering the formal invariance of the coupled field equations, what the original argument demonstrates about representational redundancy, the precise setup of the extended hole region, and what each party takes to be genuinely at stake ontologically. Follow this with a disagreements table that lists the remaining disputes in parallel columns: state the structural substantivalist's position in wording she would accept, then the full relationalist's position in wording he would accept, with each formulation precise and non-caricatured. Next, identify the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, specify a concrete argument, formal result, or staged thought experiment that both parties agree bears on it (for example, whether curvature invariants combined with scalar-field values provide canonical point-labels inside the hole, or whether quantum reference-frame considerations dissolve the symmetry), and record in advance what each side predicts the outcome will show about their own view.

Conclude with a brief jointly authored paragraph assessing whether any portion of their disagreement is merely verbal—about the word point rather than about physics or mathematics—and, if so, identify exactly which portion and explain why. The entire document should be formal and polished; it must not request bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations, and any reference to historical reasoning should be paraphrased without quotation marks. Keep the tone constructive and exact, so that a reader unfamiliar with the debate could understand both the common ground and the precise points of residual divergence.
