Write the desiderata that an evolutionary debunking argument in metaethics must satisfy to count as genuinely undermining moral realism, rather than offering an evolutionary just-so story. I want the specification to cover: the explanatory targets it must actually hit, distinguishing between debunking specific first-order judgments and debunking the general reliability of the moral faculty; the distinctions it must not collapse, such as between the causal origins of a belief and its epistemic status, or between selection for cooperative behavior and selection for tracking moral truth; the consistency conditions it must meet with evolutionary biology and with our best epistemology of other domains; and the specific result it must deliver about normative authority if it succeeds. For each desideratum, give the case or conceptual tension that motivates it. Crucially, include and address one non-standard constraint: the argument must account for the possibility that evolutionary pressures selected for cognitive mechanisms that generate normative outputs structurally isomorphic to moral truths within particular ecological niches, without being causally sensitive to moral facts themselves — and flag whether accommodating this possibility renders the standard independence requirement jointly unsatisfiable with the explanatory-demand desideratum. Identify which single desideratum eliminates the largest number of popular formulations, and distinguish genuinely non-negotiable criteria from demands that merely reflect realist or anti-realist prejudices. Briefly indicate how the classic genealogical challenge, a counter-debunking defense that appeals to third-factor explanations, and a hybrid view conceding partial evolutionary tracking but denying normative force fare against the list — but keep the scoring brief, since the criteria themselves are the deliverable.
