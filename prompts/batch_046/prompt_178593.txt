I'm organizing a three-year conceptual research program for a small philosophy-and-computation group—about ten people, no large-scale ML lab—focused specifically on the problem of agents transcending their own formal architectures, what is often discussed in connection with Hofstadter's framing of formal self-reference and particularly the MU-puzzle as a case study in closure from within. Rather than a survey or reading list, I need a defended agenda for where limited effort should actually go, since not every deep-sounding problem in this space is upstream of practical agent design.

The conceptual space seems to divide into three clusters. First, whether a self-revising system can maintain diachronic coherence across successive framework updates without either freezing into dogmatism or collapsing into arbitrariness. Second, whether there is a principled distinction between genuinely jumping out of an initial system and merely expanding one's axiom set or rule-base from inside it. Third, whether closure results analogous to the MU-puzzle apply with equal force to probabilistic, distributed, or neural architectures, or whether the problem dissolves or transforms once we leave classical symbolic formalisms behind.

For each cluster, I want an ordering defended by explicit criteria: how upstream it is for the others, how tractable it is for philosophers using conceptual analysis, formal toy models, and thought experiments rather than empirical engineering, and what specific theoretical result, counterexample, or design failure over the next few years should cause a major revision in its priority. I'm particularly interested in which question, if settled well, does the most work for constraining artificial agents, which sounds profound but is largely a red herring that wouldn't change practice, and whether there is an overlooked dependency—say, that resolving the boundary problem between internal expansion and genuine transcendence is actually a precondition for even posing the architecture problem sensibly. No citations or researcher lists; just a ranked agenda with the reasoning that would justify it to a funder.
