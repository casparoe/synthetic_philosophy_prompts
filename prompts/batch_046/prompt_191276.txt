Construct a thought experiment about material constitution—the statue-and-clay puzzle—engineered to the following specifications. The scenario should be set in a realistic, non-scientific context (a museum restoration workshop or a living craft tradition, not a teleportation lab) and should be no longer than one paragraph. Invent, within the scenario, a culturally specific maintenance practice for an artifact—describe it concretely—so that the object’s original material is entirely replaced over time, yet an institutional or functional continuity is preserved. The case must be designed so that three theories deliver the following verdicts: (1) a strict material-identity view holds that the original artifact was destroyed and replaced; (2) a constitution-without-identity view holds that the original artifact survives, now constituted by new matter; (3) a four-dimensionalist or perdurance view denies that any single object endures through the change, instead treating the sequence as a spatiotemporally extended series of parts unified by form and function. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate the reasoning for each of the three theories, identify the precise structural feature that makes the constitution view diverge from the material-identity view, explain whether removing that feature collapses the case into a standard statue-and-clay scenario, and show how a defender of the material-identity view would attack the constitutionist reading. The scenario must not appeal to exotic physics, perfect predictors, or demons. Finally, say what consideration or argument would change your assessment of which feature is doing the work, and flag the point in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to failure.
