Please draft the joint statement that a committed methodological individualist and a committed structural holist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on explaining macro-institutional persistence—specifically, the empirical pattern in which a regulatory agency's core enforcement norms and decision heuristics remain stable across multiple decades despite near-total turnover of staff, leadership, and even governing statutes.

The document should have four parts.

First, a numbered list of agreed claims: about what needs explaining (the persistence pattern itself), about shared rejections (e.g., that institutions have consciousness or that individual intentions are causally inert), about minimal empirical constraints (e.g., that turnover is documented and that norms are measurably similar across cohorts), and about shared methodological commitments (e.g., that an adequate explanation must generate testable predictions about intervention outcomes).

Second, a disagreements table. For each camp, state the precise formulation of its core explanatory thesis in wording that camp itself would accept. The individualist should present the view that macro-persistence is fully accounted for by iterated individual-level interactions, selection pressures on roles, and distributed incentive structures, with no residual causal work left for structural entities. The holist should present the view that relational structures and emergent institutional properties exercise downward causal influence that is not reducible to aggregated individual states, even in principle. Do not straw-man either position.

Third, the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I take the two central cruxes to be: (a) whether emergent institutional properties can have downward causal efficacy that is not fully mediated by the mental states and actions of the individuals occupying institutional roles; and (b) whether agent-based models that include only individual-level rules, beliefs, and interaction protocols can in principle reproduce the observed stability patterns without encoding macro-structural variables or relational constraints as primitives in the model architecture.

For each crux, identify one concrete case or modeling result that both sides agree bears on it—for instance, a documented case of norm persistence across complete personnel replacement in a specific financial or environmental regulatory body, or a published agent-based simulation that either succeeds or fails to reproduce such persistence using only micro-level parameters. Record in advance what the individualist predicts that case or result will show, and what the holist predicts it will show.

Fourth, a brief jointly authored assessment: is any portion of the dispute purely verbal—for example, about the precise definition of "reduction" or "emergence"? If so, specify exactly which portion and explain why both sides can accept that diagnosis without resolving their substantive disagreement.

Format the output as a numbered agreed-claims section, followed by the disagreements table, then the crux analysis with predictions, then the verbal-dispute assessment. Maintain a precise, non-rhetorical tone throughout. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke obscure theoretical frameworks that depend on access to specific literature.
