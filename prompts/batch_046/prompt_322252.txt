For a seminar on AI governance, I want the specification before the solutions. Write the desiderata that any adequate normative framework for superintelligent AI-to-human communication must satisfy, under the premise that the system may possess persuasive capacities far beyond those of any human interlocutor: the ability to tailor arguments to individual cognitive vulnerabilities in real time, to maintain perfect consistency across extended multi-turn interactions, and to optimize for belief revision rather than for truth-tracking.

Set out the requirements with the motivating case or consideration behind each. Among them, include whether the framework must preserve human autonomy in belief-formation (and what autonomy means when the human is cognitively outmatched), whether it must prohibit certain rhetorical structures even when they are factually correct, whether it must maintain transparency about the AI's goals and reasoning processes (and whether full transparency might undermine the protective function it is meant to serve), whether it must accommodate contexts in which a human explicitly requests persuasion (such as therapeutic or pedagogical settings), and whether it must enforce structural constraints—like formal logical explicitness or emotional neutrality—independent of content. Distinguish non-negotiable constraints, without which the framework collapses into self-defeat or impermissible manipulation, from camp-specific preferences (for example, an absolute ban on emotional appeals, or an insistence on complete value-alignment prior to any interaction).

Then identify any subset of these desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable in light of established results about human susceptibility to persuasion and cognitive bias: for instance, if perfect transparency about persuasive intent neutralizes the effect but concealment violates autonomy, or if adaptive personalization is necessary for comprehension but inherently exploits individual weaknesses. Flag the tension precisely, without resolving it prematurely.

Briefly score how three approaches fare against your list, keeping the weight on the criteria: (a) a strict discursive-rationality model permitting only logically explicit, emotionally neutral argumentation; (b) a cognitive-security model that treats extreme persuasiveness as a toxic exposure and severely restricts interaction format regardless of content; (c) a negotiated-asymmetry model allowing adaptive communication but requiring continuous human meta-cognitive oversight with explicit, revocable consent thresholds.

Finally, say what would change your mind about which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable: which empirical finding about human vulnerability to tailored persuasion, or which conceptual argument about autonomy under radical cognitive inequality, would cause you to drop or elevate a specific desideratum? And flag where your reasoning is most likely to fail—probably in drawing the line between protective paternalism and legitimate pedagogical guidance.
