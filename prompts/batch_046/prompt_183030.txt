Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a constitutive-closure theorist and a causal-systemic-inclusion theorist on the democratic boundary problem—who belongs to the demos?

The constitutive-closure position holds that membership in the demos is constituted by a practice of mutual recognition among agents who stand in a relation of co-authorship of binding collective decisions; geographic presence and causal impact are neither necessary nor sufficient for this standing. The causal-systemic-inclusion position holds that membership should be determined by tracking all agents—individual, collective, or institutional—whose welfare or preference structures are systematically and irreversibly altered by a polity's decision architecture, with standing weighted by the magnitude and irreversibility of the causal link rather than by relations of mutual recognition.

Produce a single document that both sides could sign, structured as follows: a numbered section of agreed claims about the formal requirements of democratic legitimacy, the inadequacy of purely territorial or citizenship-based boundaries, and the empirical challenges of mapping causal influence across institutional networks; a disagreements table with two columns—the closure formulation worded so its proponents accept it, and the inclusion formulation worded so its proponents accept it—for each residual divergence; the cruxes, identifying at least two sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side, one concerning whether operationalizing mutual recognition inevitably smuggles in causal-impact criteria and another concerning whether causal-systemic tracking imposes an unmanageable epistemic burden on democratic institutions; for each crux, specify one concrete governance design—such as a transnational climate assembly that includes algorithmic advisory nodes, or a municipal charter extending deliberative standing to non-resident stakeholders affected by irreversible zoning decisions—on which both sides agree the crux bears, and record in advance what each camp predicts about how that case should be evaluated; and a brief identification of any portion of the dispute that is purely verbal, with an explanation of why.

Begin with the document itself, with no preface, commentary, or restatement of the prompt. State how confident you are in your claims and identify the specific point about which you are least certain.
