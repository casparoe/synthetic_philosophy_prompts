I am designing a decision-tree worksheet for an advanced value-theory seminar that asks whether tranquility—the complete absence of craving and suffering—is not merely the highest good but the only intrinsic good. The instrument should take a respondent from an initial claim about whether positive mental states (joy, knowledge, aesthetic experience) can be intrinsically valuable even in the absence of craving, and then sort them through three further binary distinctions: (i) whether craving and suffering are conceptually separable or two manifestations of a single aversive state; (ii) whether achieving both absences is sufficient for the best possible life, or only necessary; and (iii) whether the thesis applies only to beings with a first-person narrative or extends to any system capable of aversive experience, including artificial ones. Each leaf must carry either the name of an established position (strict tranquilism, modified Schopenhauerian negative axiology, the unnamed sufficient-but-not-necessary hybrid) or the label unnamed/unoccupied, together with a single sentence describing its most awkward commitment—specifically, what it must say about a hypothetical being that lacks craving and suffering entirely but also possesses no memory, no creative capacity, and no relational bonds. The tree should flag any pair of leaves that cannot be separated by the given branches, and it should explicitly diagnose any logical gap—especially the combination that treats craving and suffering as inseparable yet denies that their absence is sufficient—stating whether that space is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Every branch node must be phrased as a concrete claim that a student can affirm or deny without knowing the position labels in advance. The worksheet is for seminar discussion, not for citation; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or source references. Size: exactly six leaves, including at least two unnamed or unoccupied combinations.
