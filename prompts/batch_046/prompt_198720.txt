My friend insisted that if a model can preserve syntactic and semantic regularities across dozens of languages with near-human fluency, then dismissing the process as “mere statistics” misses something structurally important about representation. I remained skeptical, but the disagreement left me wondering whether the best way to expose the absurdity of conflating correlation with comprehension is to push the opposing claim to its deadpan extreme.

The abstract should exaggerate real features of Hofstadter’s actual concern (that translation engines lack contextual awareness, never understand what they render, and merely shuffle surface patterns) by reinterpreting each of those limitations as proof of profound cognitive achievement. At the end, include a brief mock reviewer comment, also deadpan, that cites the shallowness critique and asks whether “semantic curvature” is anything more than an elegant redescription of distributional correlation, without breaking the parody’s straight face.
