My dissertation advisor has rendered the following verdicts on personal identity in conceptual edge cases. She judges that a patient who suffers total retrograde amnesia but retains intact personality, values, and first-person narrative continuity remains the same self. She also judges that gradual replacement of every neuron with a functionally identical synthetic analogue over a decade preserves identity, as does destructive scanning and uploading into a single digital substrate. Yet when the same destructive scan produces two simultaneously running copies, she rules that the original self is destroyed and two new successors emerge, neither identical to the original person. She denies that a fully coherent artificial intelligence—one maintaining a consistent autobiography, self-model, and decision history—counts as a self at all, and she denies that a corporation with centuries of legal and strategic continuity is a self, despite its narrative coherence. Conversely, she accepts that a collective of one hundred humans linked by irreversible direct brain interfaces, permanently merged into one continuous experiential stream with no individually separable consciousness, constitutes a single self, though of an unprecedented kind.

The case for next week falls between these rulings: a patient undergoing gradual synthetic brain replacement enters a final seventy-two-hour transition in which her consciousness is continuously maintained on both the remaining biological tissue and an externally coupled server. The two streams are causally linked in real time, share identical content, and are experienced as one seamless awareness, yet they are physically distinct and independently sustained. At the end of the seventy-two hours the biological tissue is fully dissolved, leaving only the server-based stream, which reports perfect continuity and no sense of division. Predict her verdict—does the original self survive, was there a destructive branching during the overlap, and what is the metaphysical status of the person during those seventy-two hours?

In your answer, identify the competing rules that could generate her earlier pattern—such as a substrate-flexible narrative-center view that tolerates transformation but not duplication, a psychological-continuity view constrained by a strict anti-branching condition, and an embodied-singularity view requiring one spatiotemporally continuous causal stream—state what each rule predicts about this transitional case, and defend the interpretation of her track record you find best supported. Finally, specify one further hypothetical scenario that would most clearly distinguish the remaining competing rules from one another.
