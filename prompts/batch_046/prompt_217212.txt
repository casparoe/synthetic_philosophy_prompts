I'm in an undergraduate course on the philosophy of the state, and during our unit on Weber's monopoly on legitimate violence a classmate invoked "defund the police" to shut down a defense of incremental reform. The phrase seemed to be doing incompatible work: for some it meant fiscal reallocation within existing institutions; for others it meant stripping the state's domestic coercive arm entirely; for still others it seemed to deny that any centralized violence could be normatively legitimate. Can you separate the precise theses hiding in that slogan — claims about budgetary redistribution, about institutional replacement of policing functions, and about the normative status of state violence itself — and for each say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible position, or false? In particular, which reading actually supports the leap from Weber's descriptive claim about the state's monopoly to a normative conclusion about abolition rather than reform, and which ones smuggle that conclusion in without argument? I want to see the menu of meanings laid out with verdicts, especially the one that was being used to end the argument in class.
