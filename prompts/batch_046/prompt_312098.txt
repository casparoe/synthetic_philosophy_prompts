Write a proposal for an independent-study research project (approximately 3,000 words, one semester) addressing the following precise question: in Dennett's framework of real patterns and the three explanatory stances—physical, design, and intentional—does the design stance apply genuinely and non-reductively to artificial systems that display robust, projectible behavioral regularities but possess neither evolutionary selection histories nor mental states, or does such application necessarily collapse into the physical stance (mechanistic explanation) or the intentional stance (attribution of beliefs and goals)?

Frame the problem by specifying the conditions under which a pattern counts as real rather than merely useful: predictive leverage, compression of data, and counterfactual robustness. Then explain why existing discussions of the three stances leave the status of engineered, non-biological systems ambiguous—particularly whether the design stance requires that functional organization be the product of selection pressures, or whether mere functional decomposability is sufficient. Propose an approach: construct a conceptual test using adaptive algorithms or large-scale predictive models as case studies, asking whether their outputs can be predicted and explained at the design level (e.g., by reference to optimization objectives, architecture constraints, and information-flow patterns) without either reducing to hardware-level dynamics or importing intentional vocabulary. Contrast this approach with the obvious alternatives—purely philosophical analysis of Dennett's texts, or empirical study of AI behavior—explaining why a conceptual case-study method is more tractable and decisive for the specific question. State what a positive result would look like: demonstration that design-stance explanations are irreducible and predictive for these systems, with criteria for success clearly separated from criteria for failure (e.g., failure if every design-level claim can be translated without loss into physical-level mechanics or requires implicit intentional attribution). Conclude with an honest scope assessment: which boundary cases you will exclude (e.g., embodied robotics, evolutionary computation), how many case studies are feasible, and what conceptual risks—such as conflating predictive utility with ontological commitment—could derail the project within a single semester.
