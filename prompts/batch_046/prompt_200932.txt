I am writing as a student in an advanced philosophy of language seminar where we have been working through deflationary theories of truth—disquotationalism, minimalism, and prosentential accounts—alongside more substantive correspondence and coherence frameworks. Our readings have focused mainly on ordinary linguistic practice and logical form, but a recent conversation with a friend in atmospheric science made me think about a case that rarely appears in the philosophy literature: the phenomenon of robust agreement across structurally heterogeneous climate simulations. When models with different parameterizations, grid resolutions, and physical approximations all converge on the same qualitative trajectory—for instance, that a specific warming threshold will be crossed by a particular decade—the convergence seems stronger than accidental coincidence yet conceptually different from shared representation of a single underlying thermal dynamics.

Please write an essay that asks whether this kind of scientific robustness can be fully accommodated by a deflationary theory of truth, treating cross-model agreement as nothing more than shared acceptance of disquotational sentences within a scientific community, or whether it necessarily presupposes a substantive correspondence relation between the simulations and an independent climate system. Weigh the main arguments on both sides, including the deflationist appeal to ontological parsimony and the objection that robustness without reference amounts to empty coordination, but do not end on a non-committal note: reach and defend a specific conclusion about whether deflationism genuinely explains this phenomenon or whether the case exposes a limitation that pushes us toward a substantive account. In particular, say whether any conceptual residue—whatever cannot be captured by the deflationary framework—belongs to the philosophy of science rather than to the theory of truth itself. Present your response entirely in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if composing a formal letter.
