My design collective is commissioning a permanent public installation for a civic plaza: an algorithm will continuously reconfigure a modular, 3D-printed structure using real-time biometric inputs—pedestrians' heart-rate variability and gait velocity—translated into geometric parameters through rules designed by a single lead artist. No human approves or modifies any particular configuration as it emerges. The municipal arts board is split. One faction holds that because no individual exercises direct, moment-to-moment intentional control over the specific form, the work lacks an author and is merely automated engineering. The opposing faction holds that the artist's creative vision is fully embedded in the translation architecture itself, making every emergent shape an expression of her original design, analogous to a composer whose score is realized by an orchestra without her conducting each note. I sense that both positions rely on unstated assumptions about where artistic agency can reside—whether it requires proximal mental causation of a specific outcome, whether it can be genuinely distributed across an autonomous system, and whether an algorithmic mediator necessarily severs the link between mind and artifact—but I cannot isolate the error in either view. Can you map the precise philosophical structure of this dispute onto the relevant conceptual distinctions, and apply them to determine what is genuinely at stake in classifying the installation as art rather than process?
