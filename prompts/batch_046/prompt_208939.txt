For a graduate seminar on collective moral responsibility, I am constructing a taxonomy—structured as a decision tree—of normative positions on what I will call mnemonic accountability regimes: the obligations of a political community to maintain institutionally anchored, public acknowledgment of past collective wrongs. Rather than sorting by national case, I want the tree built from the logical space of answers to four diagnostic questions, with post-1945 Germany’s Vergangenheitsbewältigung, postwar Japan’s state and cultural remembrance patterns, the Republic of Turkey’s official posture toward the Armenian genocide, and the American South’s contested commemorative practices (Confederate monument removal, slavery-reparations discourse, and curricular mandates) serving only as illustrative leaves.

The four branching nodes should be phrased as concrete questions a respondent could answer without knowing which position they lead to:

1. Ontology of the collective subject: Does the present community count as the same diachronic moral agent that perpetrated the wrong (continuous-state thesis), as a successor state that inherits structural benefits without agential identity (structural-inheritance thesis), or as a culturally continuous but politically discontinuous community (cultural-continuity thesis)?
2. Modality of acknowledgment: Is formal, legally binding state acknowledgment—constitutional inscription, criminalization of denial, or official apology—necessary; is pedagogical and cultural memorialization sufficient without formal political admission; or is sovereign control over the national narrative, including suppression, morally permissible?
3. Recipient orientation: Is the primary duty owed to victims and descendants (rectificatory), to the moral integrity of the perpetrator community itself (expressive or purificatory), or to global audiences for preventive education (cosmopolitan-pedagogical)?
4. Temporal scope: Are obligations permanent and structurally unexpired, or do they diminish across generations, yielding something like a mnemonic statute of limitations?

Each leaf should carry either a named position or the label “unnamed,” together with one sentence on its defining commitment and one sentence on its most awkward consequence. The tree must locate at least: (a) the institutionalized-penitence model associated with Germany; (b) the ritualized-ambiguity pattern associated with Japan; (c) the denialist-sovereigntist position exemplified by official Turkish state narrative; (d) the American South’s bifurcation between localized reparative commemoration and national commemorative resistance; and (e) a cosmopolitan-pedagogical view that demands global curricular integration without requiring domestic state apology.

Please also map the logical space fully: identify combinations of answers that correspond to unoccupied nodes—for example, a view that affirms diachronic agent-continuity yet denies any need for formal acknowledgment, or one that accepts generational discontinuity but insists on permanent structural reparations—and for each gap diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. If any two leaves share identical answers on all four branches and differ only in rhetorical emphasis or national context, flag them explicitly as inseparable by this tree.

Finally, indicate how confident you are in the placement of each named position within its branch, and single out the specific node or leaf about which you are least sure—clarifying whether your uncertainty concerns the logical coherence of the position itself, or the empirical accuracy of attributing that combination of answers to one of the four national cases.
