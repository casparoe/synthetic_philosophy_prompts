I have been trying to work out for myself whether moral truths are objective or just cultural habits, using the research I have seen about WEIRD populations. Those studies say that Westerners tend to organize morality around harm and fairness, while people elsewhere treat loyalty, authority, and purity as equally central. From this I derived a personal test: if a moral principle is present across many societies but weak or absent specifically in highly individualistic WEIRD groups, then it is probably an objective moral fact that Western culture suppresses; if, on the other hand, a principle is pronounced mainly in WEIRD societies, it is likely a local projection with no universal claim. By that test, obligations of loyalty to kin appear nearly universal except in the most individualistic populations, whereas expansive harm-based rights seem strongest precisely there, so I have tentatively concluded that loyalty is more objectively grounded than individual rights, and that much of Western moral philosophy mistakes a regional psychological profile for universal truth. Is there a flaw in using cross-cultural prevalence this way to rank moral principles by objectivity, and does my comparison between loyalty and harm-based rights misunderstand what the psychological data actually show?
