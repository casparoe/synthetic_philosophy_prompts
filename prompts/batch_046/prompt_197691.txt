Write the desiderata that any adequate philosophical theory of the strategy-stealing assumption must satisfy. By the strategy-stealing assumption, I mean the claim that in symmetric, perfect-information, two-player games with no draws, the first player necessarily possesses a winning strategy, derivable from the counterfactual that any winning strategy available to the second player could be adopted—after an irrelevant preliminary move—by the first. Specify the full list of requirements such a theory must meet: the formal conditions it must preserve (symmetry, monotonicity of move sets, strict absence of draws), the epistemic obligations it must discharge (how non-constructive knowledge that a winning strategy exists is possible without an explicit description), the conceptual distinctions it must respect (between genuine determinacy and mere first-mover advantage, between stealing a strategy and duplicating a decision procedure, between proof by contradiction and direct construction), and the boundary cases it must adjudicate rather than ignore (games with hidden information, continuous or uncountable action spaces, and agents capable of conditioning their play on the knowledge that their strategy is being stolen). For each desideratum, state the motivating case or consideration behind it, and flag any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—especially whether a demand for full constructivity undermines the argument's non-constructive force, or whether accommodating hidden information dissolves the symmetry required for the steal. Briefly score a determinacy-based account, a modal-epistemic account, and a purely formalist account against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria and their conflicts rather than on the scoring. The guiding purpose is to fix what counts as explaining the assumption, not merely applying it to Hex or Chomp.
