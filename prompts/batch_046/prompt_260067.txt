For my study group I have to write down my commitments on reference and modality so people can poke at them. Here they are, I think they're mostly standard but I might be mixing things weirdly:

- Proper names are rigid designaters, full stop.
- Natural kind terms like "water" or "gold" don't just get baptized causally; our best scientific theory partly fixes what they refer to.
- So if we found XYZ on Twin Earth with the same macro-properties, whether we call it water depends on whether our chemistry still groups it with H2O, not just on causal contact.
- "Water is H2O" is metaphysically necessary but we only know it a posteriori.
- Identity between two rigid terms is necessary if it's true at all.
- "Cicero is Tully" is necessary, but "Cicero is the guy who denounced Catiline" is contingant because descriptions aren't rigid.
- Phenomenal concepts like "this exact headache quality" are rigid designaters for mental states.
- Still, if my brain were wired differently then "this headache" might pick out a different neural state, so phenomenal reference feels world-sensitive.
- Modal rationalism is false: necessary doesn't mean a priori knowable.

Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems. Also flag if any tension is just apparent and dissolves once you check rigidity. Don't need sources, just logic.
