i keep seeing people compare buddhist anatta to hume's bundle theory — like the self is just a bunch of skhandas with no string tying them, same as hume's perceptions with no owner behind them. people lean on it to say buddhism and hume are basically saying the same thing, that anatta is just a psychological bundle view. but i recently came across a framing where the aggregates aren't static bits but a kind of process-relational field where even 'bundle-ness' is conventional, not just the owner. how far does the hume-bundle comparison actually stretch? lay out the mapping — what's the grapes, the string, the hand holding them, the observer seeing the bunch — then where it breaks, especially around pratītyasamutpāda and the fact that in buddhism the aggregates themselves are dependantly arisen and empty. does the analogy quietly import an atomism that anatta rejects? and if it collapses, what's a better comparison — maybe something from process philosophy or distributed cognition — that keeps the relational bit without sneaking in parts and wholes?
