I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about dispostions and whether they survive finks and masks, going by gut on cases. A glass vase wrapped in foam is fragile but masked — the fragility's still there, just blocked. A sugar cube in a sealed jar is soluble but masked. The classic finkish wire: it's live, but touching it flips a breaker and kills the current. I want to say the wire isn't really conductive, or maybe it is but the disposition gets canceled? I'm not sure.

Then there's this thing I'm calling a reactive mask — the blocker doesn't exist untill the object starts to manifest. Like a lock that reconfigures its pins only after you insert the key partway, so the key was disposed to open it but now it's not. Or a poison that triggers an antidote only upon ingestion; was it disposed to kill? My verdict: the foam-wrapped vase is still fragile (mask doesn't destroy). The sugar cube is still soluble. The finkish wire — I think it's NOT conductive, because the disposition is tied to actual power flow, not just potential. But wait, the reactive-lock key: I feel like it WAS disposed to open the door, and the lock cheated by adapting. So the disposition existed and was then thwarted, not absent.

But then there's a group case: five people are collectively courageous, but if any one of them starts acting bravely, the other four panic and restrain them. Is the group courageous? I say yes, but it's weird because the blocker is internal and triggered by the manifestation attempt itself. That feels like a fink spread across people. There's also a "sleeping guard" drug: it's harmless unless you take it twice in one day, in which case the second dose activates a toxin. Is the first dose disposed to harm? I say no. But then why is the reactive-lock key disposed?

There's also a computer program with a bug that crashes it only when it tries to solve a specific problem, but the crash fixes the bug so it can solve it on reboot. Is it disposed to solve the problem? I keep flip-flopping — sometimes I think yes, because the crash is part of the mechanism, other times I think no, because at the moment of trying it fails.

Is there a coherent principle here? Maybe something about whether the blocker is external vs internal, or whether it's present before the attempt? But the group case blurs that. Or maybe it's about counterfactual stability: if the blocker were removed, would it manifest? I want a principle, or maybe two competing candidates, with the case that motivates each clause, what they say about the reactive mask and the sleeping-guard drug, and an honest statement of which of my verdicts they can't accommodate — especially whether the reactive-lock key should be treated like a masked vase or like a finkish wire. And what should I say about the program?
