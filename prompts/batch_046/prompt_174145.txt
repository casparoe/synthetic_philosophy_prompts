My partner and I have been trying to decide whether to have a child, and we've stumbled into a puzzle that feels logical rather than emotional. If we bring a child into existence and they live a genuinely good life, it seems like we've done something kind but not obligatory—there was no specific person waiting to exist who was wronged by our delay. But if we bring a child into existence and they suffer severely, it feels as though we've wronged an actual individual. That one-sidedness strikes me as strange: the harm seems to attach to a real victim, while the benefit seems to float free of any recipient.

We also talked about adopting a child who is already alive and in need. In that case, helping feels clearly required if we can do it without excessive cost, because there's an identifiable person on the other end. So now I'm wondering whether the moral difference between having a child, adopting, and remaining childless really tracks whether there's already someone who can be affected. If that's right, does it mean creating happiness is morally neutral, creating suffering is wrong, and relieving existing suffering is good? Or am I missing a hidden mistake—perhaps about identity, or about comparing possible lives to actual ones—that makes the three cases look more separate than they actually are? I want to know whether my intuition about this asymmetry is pointing at something real, or whether it collapses once you press on it.
