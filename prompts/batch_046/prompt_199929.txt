I run a book club and we're doing Charlotte's Web next month. Read it through animal ethics and speciesism, but skip the standard Singer vs Regan framing every model spits out. Instead, use the niche idea of proxy moral standing — where an animal gets ethical weight mainly through a human or human-like character vouching for it — and pair it with the replaceability argument, that saving one farmed animal just frees up space for another. Ask whether White actually thinks Wilbur has intrinsic value, or if he only matters because Fern and Charlotte have made him special. Then look at Charlotte's death: why doesnt anyone try to save her? Is that pure speciesism, or does the novel quietly treat her as replaceable labor whose job is finished once Wilbur is safe? Focus especially on the county fair scenes, the web messages, and Fern's withdrawal into adolescence. Start with your bottom line in the first sentence, then unpack the scenes.
