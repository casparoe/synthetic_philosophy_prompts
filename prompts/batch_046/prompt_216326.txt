Design a decision tree that sorts philosophical positions on the badness of pain, using pain asymbolia—cases in which nociceptive signals occur without negative affect or suffering—as the principal diagnostic branch point. The tree should begin with a concrete commitment: whether a subject with complete pain asymbolia has any reason, other than long-term tissue damage, to avoid the noxious stimulus. From there, branch on three further questions that genuinely separate the views: (i) whether the badness of ordinary pain resides primarily in its phenomenal affect, its representational content (e.g., signaling bodily damage), or its motivational force (the imperative to withdraw); (ii) whether asymbolia demonstrates that affect is separable from nociception and therefore not essential to pain's badness, or whether it reveals that true pain is missing in these cases; and (iii) whether a view must treat the absence of suffering in asymbolia as a normative improvement or merely a descriptive anomaly.

Each leaf should be occupied by a named position if one exists (e.g., pure representationalism, strong affectivism, hybrid evaluative views, or eliminativist accounts that deny pain intrinsic badness) or marked 'unnamed' if the combination is defensible but unoccupied. For each leaf, state the position in one sentence and identify its most awkward commitment—specifically, what it must say about whether a physician should prioritize treating affective distress over nociceptive signaling in clinical settings.

The tree should distinguish positions that standard encyclopedia entries often conflate, and you should flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot separate (for instance, if two views share every verdict on asymbolia but diverge only on metaphysical claims about mental representation that do not affect the clinical or normative conclusions). Do not include bibliographic references, citation requests, or appeals to specific authors unless indispensable to naming the leaf; phrase every branch node as a yes/no or either/or question that a student could answer without knowing which philosophical label it corresponds to. Keep the tree to six or eight leaves, and note any unoccupied combinations in the logical space together with a brief diagnosis of whether the gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect.
