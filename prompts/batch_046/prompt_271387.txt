As a novelist attempting to construct a narrative about the collapse and reconstruction of belief within a closed community—something like a monastery or a scientific enclave—I have found myself returning repeatedly to Wittgenstein's On Certainty and the literature it has spawned on hinge epistemology. I am not writing philosophy, but I want the conceptual architecture beneath the story to be coherent rather than decorative.

In thinking through how my characters might hold certain propositions immune from doubt—not because they are self-evident, but because doubting them would undermine the very framework within which doubt operates—I have begun to distinguish, for my own purposes, between what I might call load-bearing hinges and scenic hinges. A load-bearing hinge is a certainty whose removal destabilizes entire domains of judgment (the existence of the external world, the reliability of memory); a scenic hinge is a certainty that functions locally and can be suspended for particular narrative purposes without systemic collapse (the trustworthiness of a specific witness, the continuity of a particular institution). I do not know whether this distinction exists in the literature under other names, and I do not ask for a literature survey.

What I need is an agenda for the conceptual work that actually matters. If one had limited philosophical resources—say, the equivalent of one serious monograph's worth of attention—to devote to open questions in hinge epistemology, which should take priority? Specifically:

First, is the central unresolved problem the ontological status of hinges themselves—whether they are propositions, rules, or practical dispositions—or is it the dynamics of hinge revision, particularly how a community negotiates the transition when a hinge fails? Which of these is upstream?

Second, how should one weigh the importance of extending hinge analysis to social and testimonial belief—what I have been calling the interpersonal hinges—against the importance of clarifying the boundary between hinge certainty and ordinary empirical knowledge? If the goal is to understand how belief survives collective trauma, which line of inquiry yields more?

Third, there is the question of whether hinge epistemology offers any genuine resources against global skepticism, or whether it merely relocates the problem. Is this implication for skeptical argumentation a live and productive research front, or is it largely settled and therefore overrated?

Please defend your ordering. I am less interested in a survey of the field than in a reasoned allocation of intellectual effort: for each priority, why it governs other questions, how tractable it appears with current conceptual tools rather than empirical methods, and what kind of result—conceptual, argumentative, or illustrative—would cause you to reorder the list. I write fiction, but I want to know which philosophical uncertainties are genuinely load-bearing for anyone trying to understand belief.
