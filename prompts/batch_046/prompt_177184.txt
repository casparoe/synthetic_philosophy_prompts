Please write the opening remarks for the first session of an institutional review board retreat on research ethics involving adults with fluctuating decision-making capacity—specifically, patients in early-stage neurodegenerative conditions who can comprehend discrete facts but struggle to maintain a stable, integrated preference hierarchy over time. The audience is mixed: neurologists, clinical researchers, bioethicists, legal scholars, and patient advocates, not all of whom work with philosophical concepts on a daily basis. Your remarks must treat consent not as a procedural checkbox but as a normative structure shaped by three interacting conditions: deception (including benevolent framing that obscures the true shape of risk), coercion (structural rather than overt, such as enrollment being embedded in the only accessible care pathway), and competence (understood not as global rationality but as domain-specific integrative capacity that may be present for some decisions and absent for others). The philosophical framework should do real architectural work: determine what the board can legitimately demand of a protocol, what it must not claim to have secured with a signature, and why consent in this setting is better conceived as an ongoing, revocable authorization rather than a discrete event. Comfort the room without false reassurance—do not imply that refining the consent form resolves the underlying moral problem. Speak in a tone appropriate to the occasion, make the conceptual structure accessible to non-specialists without reducing it to platitude, and avoid citations, bibliographies, or references to specific scholars or texts.
