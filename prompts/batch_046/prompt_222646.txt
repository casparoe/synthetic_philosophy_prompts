I sit on the Governance Reform Committee of a large public university. By next Friday we must recommend to the Senate whether to adopt a proposed constitutional amendment that permits any coalition of 500 students or staff to trigger a binding referendum on tuition and fee structures, on condition that the proposal first completes a two-week structured deliberation phase moderated by randomly selected faculty-student panels. Proponents claim this remedies a democratic deficit in financial governance; opponents, led by the Vice-Chancellor, argue it exposes complex distributive decisions to transient majoritarian sentiment and undermines the principle that institutional authority should track relevant expertise. A distinctive clause states that if turnout falls below forty per cent, the result becomes advisory, though the administration must then publish a reasoned justification for any deviation within thirty days. Please draft the Committee's formal opinion memo addressed to the Senate. It should recommend approval, rejection, or approval with specific conditions; present a clear, actionable bottom line with concrete reasoning; and engage honestly with the strongest argument on the opposing side. Write in British English, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading.
