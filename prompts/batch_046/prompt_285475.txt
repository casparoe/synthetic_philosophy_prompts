I am preparing a ninety-minute seminar for first-year philosophy-of-mind graduate students, most of whom enter with a cognitive-science background and tend to treat the knowing-how versus knowing-that distinction as a settled taxonomy of explicit versus implicit propositional storage—essentially viewing Ryle's regress argument as an obsolete puzzle about working-memory encoding. Rather than rehearse the standard undergraduate survey version, I want to construct a session that makes the distinction visible through a single, carefully bounded carrying example: a concert violinist who continuously adjusts bow pressure and contact point in real-time response to irregular acoustic feedback in a non-standard hall geometry, achieving intonation corrections she cannot articulate as rules, set against an acoustics engineer who commands the complete formal description of wave interference but is physically unable to execute the adjustment. I want to weave in a somewhat niche conceptual framing—call it a practical mode of presence—in which knowing-how is understood not as hidden propositional content but as a direct, non-representational attunement to situational affordances, though I want to avoid importing heavy phenomenological terminology that would derail the discussion. Please plan the explanatory arc: specify the dependency order in which the components must be introduced so that the distinction emerges rather than being asserted, identify what conceptual groundwork must be in place before the example can do its work, describe the misconceptions students will likely generate in sequence—including the claim that the violinist merely possesses tacit knowledge-that, that the engineer's representational knowledge is more fundamental, and that the skill reduces to subpersonal motor programming—and indicate the precise point in the sequence where each should be deflected. Also specify what the carrying example must include and what it must deliberately exclude to prevent it from being read as either mystical intuition or mere reflex, outline a check for understanding that demonstrates they have grasped the structural point rather than memorized the labels, and state explicitly what should be left out at this level—such as detailed debates in the intellectualism literature or specific neuroimaging findings—while saying so openly.
