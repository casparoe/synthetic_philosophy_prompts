I serve on my high school’s Student Research Ethics Review Board. A junior researcher has proposed a perceptual study in which participants view rapid sequential color shifts arranged to induce a color-phi illusion, then immediately report the temporal order of motion and color change. She argues that if subjects consistently report the color change occurring before the motion that produces it, the data support an Orwellian retroactive revision of conscious experience rather than a Stalinesque post-hoc confabulation, and she wants to treat these reports as direct evidence about the architecture of subjective timing. Half the board holds that inducing such illusions without explicit warning fails informed consent because subjects may literally experience retroactively revised contents they could not have anticipated or authorized; the other half regards this as routine methodological deception correctable by debriefing. Please draft the formal opinion I will circulate before Tuesday’s meeting: recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, specify a concrete implementation or revision time frame, treat the retroactive-revision objection to consent seriously rather than collapsing it into ordinary deception, and explain whether the study’s Orwellian-versus-Stalinesque framing should influence our ethical judgment. After the memo, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with the fields: recommendation, time_frame, core_reasoning, strongest_losing_side_argument, and conditions_or_follow_up.
