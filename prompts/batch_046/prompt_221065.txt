I'm trying to pin down when psychiatric classifications genuinely loop back onto the people they classify, and when they merely reveal pre-existing patterns. Here are my gut verdicts.

When 19th-century French clinicians classified 'hysterical fugue,' patients began exhibiting precisely the fugue behaviors demonstrated in clinical lectures. That feels like deep looping: the category seemed to generate the performance.

When autism criteria were broadened to include Asperger's, adults began identifying with the label and reshaping autistic culture through the neurodiversity movement. Yet the underlying cognitive profile looks unchanged; the loop altered social meaning without inventing the condition. So looping is present but superficial.

When Japan recognized 'hikikomori' first as a social observation and then as a clinical category, families started treating withdrawn youth as patients, and some youth adopted the sick role. That feels like deep looping: the diagnosis reshaped the family dynamics sustaining the withdrawal.

But when bipolar disorder was split into type I and type II, I don't think patients started having milder hypomanic episodes to match the new boxes. The split just re-sorted existing presentations. So no loop.

The hard pair: reports of dissociative identity disorder spiked in North America in the 1980s and 1990s, with patients presenting far more alters than in other cultures or eras, often matching therapists' expectations. That looks like classic looping. Yet post-traumatic stress disorder was formalized around the same period, and its prevalence also rose—but I think PTSD reveals pre-existing trauma reactions rather than manufacturing them. So looping seems to happen for DID but not for PTSD, despite both being trauma-related and both seeing prevalence spikes after classification.

Is there one principle that captures this pattern? Perhaps whether the classification supplies a performative narrative script that patients can enact, versus whether it maps onto an observable dysfunction that exists independently of labeling? I want a principle—stated precisely—that explains why hysteria, hikikomori, and DID loop, why autism loops only socially, why bipolar subtyping doesn't loop, and why PTSD doesn't loop either. It should also tell me what to think about the recent proliferation of 'complex PTSD' diagnoses: are clinicians and patients co-constructing a new symptom cluster, or just recognizing an old one that standard PTSD missed? And please be explicit about which of my verdicts the principle cannot save without becoming ad hoc.
