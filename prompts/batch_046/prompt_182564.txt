I'm trying to sort out my views on valid consent for a seminar and want to check if they actualy hang together. I think consent is basically a speech act that needs both competence and absence of coercion, but I'm also drawn to this epistimic externalist idea: what matters is whether the consenter's belief about what they're agreeing to tracks reality in a reliable way, not that they have explicit full knowledge. So a patient who trusts their doctor and gets the gist is okay even without knowing the biochemsitry. At the same time I think deception breaks consent specifically because it breaks that reliability — if the doctor lies about success rates the tracking fails. I also hold that competence is graded and domain-specific: you're fully competent for a haircut but need higher standards for experimental surgery. But coercion only counts for me if it's physical threat or explicit blackmail; if your family guilt-trips you into being an organ donor thats still valid because you technically could have said no. Do these pull against each other? Like, does the graded competence view clash with the binary idea that consent is just valid or not? And in a case where a doctor is usually reliable but lies once about side effects — does the externalist view say consent is still okay because the process is generally good? Which tensions are real and which ones just look bad? Rank them by severity and say what'd have to give.
