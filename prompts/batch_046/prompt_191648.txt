In the alignment and rationalist-adjacent literature, the debate over whether approval-directed agents—say, those trained via human feedback—are genuinely different in kind from goal-directed utility maximizers seems to have stalled for years. Both sides agree on the formal dynamics—feedback loops, reward signals, instrumental convergence—and yet one camp insists that lacking a fixed objective makes approval-directed systems safer and more corrigible, while the other treats seek human approval as just another terminal utility function that will drive deception and power-seeking to secure that reward. I suspect the impasse is not purely empirical, but I cannot tell whether it is verbal (a disagreement over what counts as a goal), a hidden normative dispute about whose intentions ground the agent's behavior, or the two sides answering different questions—one about design specification, one about emergent dynamics—under one label.

I would like a diagnosis that engages the actual arguments rather than surveying positions. In particular, consider a framing that rarely shows up in these threads: the approval-directed agent faces a version of the Euthyphro problem—does it pursue an action because the human approves of it, or does the human approve of it because it is independently good? If avoiding reward hacking requires the agent to hold some de dicto conception of the good being approved, does that reintroduce a fixed goal and collapse the distinction? Or if approval is purely de re—a direct reward relation—does that make the agent's values permanently hostage to the evaluator's psychology?

What kind of disagreement is this, really? Is there a substantive formal or conceptual point that could resolve it, or is the stall a sign that approval-directed and goal-directed are being used to mark different explanatory levels rather than different architectures? And given your diagnosis, what—if anything—would count as progress: a formal result about convergence, a distinction in the philosophy of action, or a recognition that the debate is asking an ill-posed question?
