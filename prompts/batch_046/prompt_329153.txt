After a long, somewhat circular argument with a friend about whether a constitutional order can coherently contain a rule that governs its own alteration — especially when that rule might be used to lower the very threshold required to invoke it — I want to set aside particular answers and specify the full desiderata that any adequate theory of nomic self-reference and legal self-amendment must satisfy.

Give me the requirements list. For each, state the case or paradox it addresses, why it is a genuine constraint rather than a prejudice of one jurisprudential camp, and whether it is non-negotiable or negotiable. The list must cover: (1) the standard amendment-regress case, in which a provision authorizes its own modification but does not specify whether that authorization extends to the conditions of its own exercise; (2) the cascading-threshold scenario, where a clause requiring a two-thirds vote is amended — perhaps by a simple majority that first lowers the threshold — to change its own content, and the theory must say whether the second step is valid without begging the question; (3) the distinction between procedural self-reference (a rule changing how it is applied) and substantive self-reference (a rule altering the norms it expresses), and why collapsing this distinction produces either paradox or trivialization; (4) the reflexive-entrenchment case, in which a clause declares itself unamendable but does not specify whether the declaration of unamendability is itself within the scope of the clause; (5) consistency with the empirical fact that functioning legal systems do amend their basic structures over time, so the theory cannot treat all self-amendment as conceptually impossible; (6) preservation of a distinction between change within the system (amendment) and change of the system (replacement of the rule of recognition), with a clear criterion for which is which; and (7) the logical constraint that the theory must not generate an infinite hierarchy of meta-rules each governing the one below, unless it can show where and why the hierarchy terminates without arbitrariness.

Then tell me which single desideratum does the most damage to the most popular theories — whether hierarchical, conventionalist, or dynamic-rule accounts — and briefly score those three families against the full list, keeping the weight on the criteria rather than on the scorecard. I am not asking for bibliographies or citations; I want the specification itself, because that is where our disagreement actually lives.
