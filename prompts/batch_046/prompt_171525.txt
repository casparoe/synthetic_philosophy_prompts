As a novelist constructing a conceptual scaffold for a novel about a philosopher who loses faith in language, I have sketched an argument that seems to collapse every metaontological position into self-contradiction. I want to know precisely where the scaffolding breaks.

Here is the chain:

1. If a claim is true only within an optional conceptual framework, and that framework can be adopted or abandoned without rational error, then the claim expresses no substantive constraint on reality.

2. The metaontological deflationist holds that every first-order existence claim—e.g., that numbers exist—is true only within an optional framework.

3. The deflationist's own thesis—that existence claims are framework-relative—is itself a claim about what there is, namely that there are no framework-independent ontological facts.

4. By the deflationist's own standard in (2), that thesis is therefore framework-relative.

5. If it is framework-relative, then by (1) it expresses no substantive constraint; adopting or rejecting deflationism is then a pragmatic choice with no rational obligation attached.

6. But if there is no rational obligation to adopt deflationism, then its truth gives us no reason to prefer it to ontological realism.

7. The same structure applies in reverse: the realist asserts that there is a substantive fact about whether numbers exist, but that assertion is itself an existence claim about ontological facts, so by the realist's own lights it must be evaluated substantively—yet the realist offers no non-circular criterion for doing so.

8. Hence both positions, when applied consistently to themselves, dissolve into optional stances with no claim to correctness.

9. Therefore, no consistent metaontological view is possible: every attempt to classify a dispute as verbal or substantive undermines its own authority.

The conclusion strikes me as intolerable—it would render my character's entire intellectual crisis moot—yet each step feels like ordinary philosophical housekeeping. Which step should be abandoned, and what does abandoning it cost?
