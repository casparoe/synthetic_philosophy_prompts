I have been noticing a recurring pattern in debates about artistic value, particularly when people invoke Tolstoy's criterion that true art infects the audience with a feeling the artist has genuinely experienced. The pattern concerns works that seem to satisfy infection and sincerity yet are denied artistic status because of where the feeling originates or how far it travels.

Consider a public monument designed by someone who never lived through the tragedy it commemorates; it relies on conventional symbols of grief, and every visitor reports being genuinely moved, yet critics deny it is art, saying the emotion is rented from collective memory rather than transmitted from the artist's own experience. Contrast this with a private letter written by someone who actually suffered that grief, expressed in unskilled language, read only by close relatives who weep upon reading it; here infection is acknowledged, but the work is dismissed as mere document because its reach is too narrow.

In both cases, the dispute is not about technical skill, audience sincerity, or whether infection occurs. It is about whether the artist's feeling must be personally owned rather than culturally shared, and whether infection must be universal to count. Tolstoy's writings appear to require both personal sincerity and broad accessibility, but they do not clearly settle what happens when one is present and the other absent, or when the personal feeling is indistinguishable from a shared cultural script.

Is there an established term for this specific tension — the denial of artistic status to works that are genuinely infectious but whose infection operates through collective rather than individual feeling, or that are individually sincere but fail to reach beyond a private circle? How does it differ from Tolstoy's concept of counterfeit art, which involves insincerity or emotional deadness beneath technical perfection, and from the broader notion of kitsch? If no precise term exists, how should the boundary be drawn: is the decisive factor the origin of the feeling in the artist, the mechanism of transmission, or the scope of the audience?
