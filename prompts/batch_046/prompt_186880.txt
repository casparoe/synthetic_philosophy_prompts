I've come round to thinking the absent-minded driver problem isn't really about memory lapses at all — it's about whether rationality can involve binding your own future reasoning, like a Ulysses contract for belief. The driver who takes the exit without pausing is rational not because he forgot the map, but because he's executing a pre-commited policy that treats intermediate deliberation as a cost to be avoided. Tell me what would count against this view — not just the standard 'expected utility at the node' reply, which doesn't favour the binding idea, but which intuitions about agency, which results from intertemporal choice theory, and which formal results about computation costs in decision-making it stands or falls with. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Which ones would a defender of this Ulysses-style position actually conceed, and which would they dismiss as missing the point?
