please write the procedure as a sequence of ordered questions: what to ask first, what counts as a sufficient answer to each step, where the flow stops with a hard "no", where it hands the decision to a human because it's values not facts, and flag the spots where judgment is just dressed up as logic. then apply it to three cases:

1. a standard ivf mix-up: couple intended embryo X, but embryo Y was implanted and a healthy child was born. is this child "the intended child" for legal and medical-history purposes?
2. monozygotic twinning: one embryo splits in the dish, two children are born. does origin essentialism treat them as one origin with two bodies, or as separate origins?
3. chimeric fusion: two distinct embryos accidentally fuse in culture, and one child is born carrying cells from both original zygotes. what's the identity call here?

make sure you say exactly what the proceedure can't do, and include at least one case where it gives us an answer we'll probably hate and have to live with anyway. also inject the distinction between strict gamete-essentialism and zygotic-developmental essentialism somewhere in the framework, since that's the bit our lawyers keep arguing about.

write it in british english, keep the tone casual, and a typo or two is totally fine.
