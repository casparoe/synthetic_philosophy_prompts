I work as a backend software engineer with no formal training in philosophy or historiography, but I volunteer with an interdisciplinary digital archive that curates open-access primary-source datasets on twentieth-century conflicts. Our advisory board — composed of academic historians and quantitative data scientists — recently had to classify three submissions as either legitimate historical revision or denialism warranting exclusion. In the first case, they excluded a 2019 monograph that denied the Holocaust occurred altogether, dismissed all survivor testimony as fabricated, and offered no archival evidence; in the second, they included a 2022 peer-reviewed statistical study that used newly digitised Ottoman census fragments to revise Armenian genocide mortality downward from roughly 1.5 million to approximately 900,000, while maintaining that systematic deportation and state-ordered killing occurred and that the Young Turk government bears primary responsibility; and in the third, they excluded a 2021 government-funded multimedia exhibit on Nanjing that acknowledged only "isolated incidents of violence", estimated casualties at about 20,000, denied systematic sexual violence, attributed all deaths to accidental crossfire, and relied exclusively on Japanese military communiqués while dismissing Chinese survivor accounts and international tribunal records.

Now a fourth submission has arrived: an anonymous, open-source project that trained a computer-vision pipeline on thousands of previously unindexed Japanese military diaries and Chinese civilian records from 1937–38, then applied a probabilistic graphical model to estimate Nanjing casualties at approximately 110,000. The project confirms widespread sexual violence and identifies execution clusters that appear locally ordered rather than centrally directed. All data, code, and model weights are published openly; there is no named author, no institutional affiliation, and no traditional peer review beyond a preprint server. Quantitative historians have praised its transparency, yet denialist forums have widely circulated it as "independent proof that Chinese figures are inflated". Given only the board's three precedents, what do you think they will rule, and what implicit principle are they running? I am less interested in the verdict itself than in identifying the two or three most plausible rules that fit their track record, stating what each predicts for this fourth case, giving your best guess of the actual ruling with your reasoning, and naming one further hypothetical submission that would best tell those rules apart. In particular, please recommend the decision that best coheres with the board's own precedents, identifying the closest analogues and stating honestly what principle the board would be adopting either way. Please respond in British English.
