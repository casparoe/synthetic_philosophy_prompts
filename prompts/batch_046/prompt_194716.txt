After a long argument with a friend about whether knowing one is in a borderline perceptual state requires knowing that one knows it, I have sketched notes for an essay. The central idea is to distinguish indexical self-awareness, which I treat as luminous, from propositional knowledge-that, which I treat as non-luminous, and then to apply the KK principle asymmetrically across these two levels. On this view, the familiar anti-luminosity argument succeeds only if it assumes a single threshold governing both levels; once separated, the regress that threatens first-order knowledge dissolves into a layered structure in which second-order self-attribution can satisfy KK without forcing first-order borderline judgments into contradiction. I contend this preserves the intuitive force of luminosity for self-report without collapsing into the reliability-based objections that usually accompany it, though it may make the KK principle vacuous at the first-order level. I would welcome comments on three points: whether this distinction is genuinely stable or collapses upon analysis; whether it avoids the circularity of defining indexical awareness without implicit appeal to propositional knowledge; and whether the resulting layered account is interesting or merely relocates the problem.
