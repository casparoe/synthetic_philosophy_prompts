Reproductive clinics and adoption agencies screen hard for risks that a child will suffer, but there's zero institutional push to conceive or place kids who'd be exceptionally well-off — and deciding not to have kids is treated as morally neutral, not a missed opportunity for good. What theory of value or obligation does that institutional pattern presuppose? Does it commit us to the classic procreation assymetry — that causing harm is wrong but failing to cause benefit isn't — or can a symmetric welfare view explain the same rules through tractability and rights? Reconstruct the implicit view, say whether the practice strictly requires it or could sit with rival theories, and sketch what a symetric reproductive policy would have to change. Keep what's settled separate from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic, don't present one side as the consensus.
