Specify the full set of desiderata that an adequate theory of dream consciousness must meet if it is to adjudicate the Malcolm–Dennett dispute without begging the question. For each criterion—whether it concerns the authority of waking reports, the possibility of narrative confabulation upon arousal, the interpretation of lucid volitional reports during REM atonia, or the reconciliation of reported visual imagery with suppressed REM-related cortical signatures—state the conceptual or empirical consideration that makes it a requirement, and mark it as either non-negotiable or as the preference of a particular camp (for instance, real-time neural-correlationism versus retrospective-judgment theory). Identify any subset of these desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable, especially where phenomenal continuity during sleep conflicts with the absence of working-memory consolidation. Then briefly assess how Malcolm’s view that dreams are direct experiences and Dennett’s reconstruction model fare against the list, but keep the emphasis on the criteria and their internal tensions rather than on the scorecard.
