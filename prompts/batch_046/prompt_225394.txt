hey, me and a friend got into it on the forum about embedded agency and i still dont know if im being dense or shes being dense. wanted to get some outside eyes.

she started by saying embedded agents can never have real self-knowledge because any model of "me" inside the world-model is just a representation, not the actual mental state. she called it "the proxy gap" - since the agent is embedded in the very ontology it describes, it can only ever access representations of itself, never a first-person view. its like trying to lift yourself by your own bootstraps but worse, because the boots are made of the same stuff as the ground.

i said thats overblown. humans model themselves as objects in the world constantly. embeddedness just means the model is smaller and approximate, not that its fundamentally broken. the proxy gap sounds fancy but its just the normal representational distance you have with tables or other people too.

then she came back with: okay, but tables dont update their models based on observing themselves act. with an embedded agent, the decision procedure is inside the world being modeled, so every action feeds back into the observations that update the self-model. she claimed this creates "reflexive drift" - the agent models itself wanting X, acts on X, observes that, updates toward Y, and never converges on a stable self-image. so embeddedness isnt just an epistemic blindspot, it makes self-modeling actively unstable.

i replied that sounds like ordinary learning dynamics with a fancy label. if your update rule is well-behaved you can converge just fine. the real error is thinking embedded agents need some magical non-embedded viewpoint to "really" know themselves. reflexive drift might be an engineering headache but it doesnt prove theres a deep philosophical gap.

we went in circles. can someone adjudicate this? rate each of our four moves from clearly wrong to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. also tell me whos right about what, and what both of us are missing that we should have said. and be honest - is "reflexive drift" a real term people use or did she just coin it?
