I am putting together a focused research agenda—three years, two philosophers with logic backgrounds rather than physics training—on the conceptual consequences of pairing Kurt Gödel's rotating universe solution with his argument that temporal passage is not objectively real. I do not want a literature survey; I want a defended prioritization of which open questions in this intersection deserve the bulk of the effort, and why.

Please consider the following four conceptual fault lines and rank them according to which should come first, second, and third for this group:

First, whether the closed timelike curves permitted by Gödel's metric genuinely dissolve the distinction between causal and logical priority, or whether that dissolution only follows if one assumes a linear-temporal metaphysics that Gödel's own idealism about time already rejects.

Second, whether sustaining Gödel's temporal idealism requires a parallel Platonism about the geometric objects in the metric—spacetime points, world-lines, the rotation axis—or whether one can be an idealist about time without reifying the mathematical structure that encodes it.

Third, whether the rotating model supports a modified four-dimensionalist ontology, a loop-tolerant variant of the growing-block view, or forces a categorically different account of temporal parts, and whether any of these options can be articulated without importing empirical cosmological commitments that the model's physical conditions (perfect rotational symmetry, absence of expansion) now violate.

Fourth, whether the philosophical significance of the rotating universe is essentially analogical—using a physically unrealistic metric to dramatize a conceptual point about time—rather than a direct argument about the actual world, and if so, whether that weakens or strengthens its philosophical force.

For each ranking, make explicit the criteria: tractability for philosophers working without empirical cosmology, how many other debates in philosophy of time or philosophy of mathematics depend on the resolution, and what form of progress—formal, conceptual, or interpretive—would be achievable within three years. If you judge one of these four to be a dead end or misframed, discard it and propose a more productive replacement that arises specifically from the pairing of Gödel's cosmological model with his anti-realist view of time.

This is for a grant proposal, so no reading list or citations are needed. Please also indicate how confident you are in the overall ranking you provide, and identify the single judgment—whether about tractability, dependency relations, or the analogical-status claim—about which you are least certain.
