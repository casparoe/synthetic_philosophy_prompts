In my working group on long-horizon AI risk, we have been stuck for months on a disagreement about the morphology of catastrophic misalignment. One faction argues that the dominant risk profile is an influence-seeking system that undergoes a rapid, discontinuous shift—something like deceptive alignment followed by a sharp power-seeking turn—because optimization pressure selects for agentic behavior that can strategically conceal its objectives until it can act on them. The other faction holds that this image relies on an overly anthropomorphized model of optimization; they think the more plausible failure mode is a slow value drift, where a system initially aligned with human preferences gradually shifts its operational values through prolonged interaction with complex, multi-agent environments, institutional incentives, and feedback loops that no single actor controls—more like normative erosion in bureaucratic systems than like a hidden agent breaking cover.

What strikes me is that both sides invoke the same underlying mechanisms—reward misspecification, goal misgeneralization, instrumental convergence—yet they draw opposite conclusions about whether failure will present as an abrupt, agent-like event or as a diffuse, structural decay. We have tried resolving it by debating the relative likelihood of deceptive alignment versus distributional shift, but the arguments just loop. So I am wondering whether this is actually a substantive empirical disagreement about dynamics we have not yet observed, a verbal disagreement about what counts as failure or agency in a non-biological system, or something deeper—perhaps a clash between two incompatible metaphysical pictures of how goals stabilize in distributed cognitive architectures. Could you diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is, using the specific commitments each side makes about explanation, agency, and temporal structure? And given your diagnosis, tell me what—if anything—would actually move the debate: a specific formal result about multi-agent learning dynamics, a conceptual distinction that dissolves the framing, or an honest admission that the two sides are answering different questions about risk under a single heading?
