Write an exam essay question, with a model answer of about 700 words and a 20-mark marking scheme, for an upper-level philosophy course on free will and moral responsibility. Center it on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: that true moral responsibility requires being causa sui, but nothing can be because being the cause of one's own mental nature requires a prior mental nature, yielding an infinite regress.

The question must not be answerable by mere summary. Introduce a hypothetical artificial agent, call it Reviser, initialized only with bare computational architecture and thereafter continuously rewriting its own decision procedures in real time based on evaluations of its past outputs, with no further external programming. Ask whether Reviser could be causa sui in a way that escapes Strawson's regress, and require students to address specifically whether diachronic self-modification overcomes the problem Strawson raises for synchronic self-constitution.

The model answer should make five specific moves: (a) reconstruct the Basic Argument's premises precisely without direct quotation; (b) explain why Reviser still fails to be the ultimate cause of its mental nature, given the origin of its initial architecture and evaluative criteria; (c) distinguish being the cause of one's current state from being the cause of one's original mental nature; (d) assess whether distributing causation across time dissolves the regress or merely elongates it into an infinite temporal chain; (e) conclude on whether Strawson's argument survives analogues drawn from self-modifying systems.

The marking scheme should award 4 marks for each move, totaling 20. For each point, identify the specific error that should cost marks—for instance, conflating Strawson's regress with a general causal determinism argument; treating Reviser as genuinely self-creating without addressing its origin; failing to distinguish diachronic from synchronic causation; or asserting that continuous self-rewriting eliminates the need for an uncaused cause without explaining why.

Keep the prompt formal, focused, and free of requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not ask students to cite specific texts by page or date.
