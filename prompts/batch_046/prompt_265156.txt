I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy or history, and I am trying to build a mental classification tree—essentially a debugging taxonomy—for the debate over whether nations are genuinely old or essentially modern constructions. I have read general summaries of the invented-tradition and imagined-community literatures, but I want an instrument that sorts people by their substantive commitments rather than by the camp names they adopt, and I want it to handle an edge case that standard textbooks rarely address.

Please construct a decision tree with exactly five internal nodes (including the root) that sorts responses into six named leaves. The root node should pose the concrete claim: "There exists at least one collective that operates as a nation in the full modern sense—bounded membership, a claim to political representation, and shared symbolic life—and that existed in essentially that form before roughly 1780." From there, each branch must split on a concrete diagnostic case or institutional feature that respondents can evaluate without knowing which philosophical label it corresponds to.

The six required leaves are:
A. Strict state-led modernism: nations are recent elite artifacts, built by selectively fabricating rituals, school curricula, and historical narratives for present political needs.
B. Cultural-cognitive modernism: nations arise from specifically modern conditions—mass literacy, standardized vernaculars, and shared temporal consciousness—that let people imagine a bounded fellowship they never directly encounter, without requiring that elites deliberately fabricate every element.
C. Ethnosymbolist continuity: modern nations are politically recent, but their form is constrained and shaped by older ethnic myths, shared memories, and symbolic repertoires that predate the modern state.
D. Perennialist recurrence: nations are not a unique modern invention; they recur throughout history as enduring, self-aware collectivities with deep continuity of identity and collective memory.
E. Post-territorial digital nationhood: a niche extension holding that modern construction conditions can now be satisfied by non-geographic, digitally mediated communities that maintain shared ritual calendars, collective decision protocols, and bounded identity without state apparatus or contiguous territory.
F. One deliberately unoccupied logical combination: identify the path through your tree that produces a view with no serious proponents, explain whether it is incoherent (for example, asserting deep pre-modern continuity while denying any shared symbols, memory, or institutional form) or merely unexplored, and flag it clearly.

Design rules:
- Phrase every node as a yes/no/unclear question about a specific case or claim, not as an abstract definition. For instance: "Does Japan's imperial institution, with its claimed continuity stretching back millennia, count as evidence of genuine national persistence, or as a modern invented tradition projected backward onto older ritual forms?" Another: "Can the European Union constitute an imagined community in the relevant sense if it lacks both a single sovereign state and a unified vernacular print culture?" Do not use generic meta-questions such as "Is social construction important?"
- The tree must genuinely separate leaf A from leaf B: include a node that distinguishes elite ritual fabrication from horizontally imagined fellowship enabled by communication technology. If any pair of leaves converges on every case you include, flag them as inseparable and diagnose why (overlapping commitments on all tested dimensions, or shared rejection of a key premise that your tree does not probe).
- Build the logical space first—the combinations of answers that the nodes allow—then map the named positions onto those combinations. Note which combinations remain empty and why.
- Write for a technically literate audience with no philosophy background; avoid unnecessary jargon, or gloss it in a single clause.
- Do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations. Do not quote any author verbatim; paraphrase well-known claims associated with the invented-tradition and imagined-community frameworks without quotation marks.
- Present the deliverable as a nested outline: label each node (Node 1, Node 2, etc.), label each branch (Yes / No / Unclear), and at each leaf provide the name and a two-sentence characterization of what that combination commits its holder to.
