Assume the role of a senior philosopher specializing in the ethics of armed conflict and just war theory. Before reaching any conclusion, please think step by step: first, articulate at least two distinct normative principles that could jointly account for the entire track record below; second, apply each principle separately to the new case; third, state your best-guess recommendation with reasons; and fourth, identify the single further hypothetical case that would most sharply distinguish the principles you have identified.

Over the past two years, the interdisciplinary working group I chair has issued consistency judgments regarding whether opposing combatants enjoy moral equality in the following four scenarios.

First, a conventional interstate conflict in which both sides deploy uniformed, conscripted armies that rigorously observe discrimination and proportionality: we affirmed moral equality.

Second, a defensive war fought by a democratic state with volunteer soldiers against an authoritarian aggressor whose soldiers are conscripted, with both sides observing jus in bello: we affirmed moral equality.

Third, an asymmetric conflict in which a non-state armed group deliberately embeds its fighters within civilian populations, wears no distinguishing insignia, and systematically targets non-combatants, while the opposing state army observes jus in bello: we denied moral equality.

Fourth, a conflict in which both sides rely exclusively on remotely piloted lethal autonomous systems, with human operators physically located in their home territories, facing no personal risk, and both sides observing discrimination and proportionality: we affirmed moral equality.

Now consider the following new case for our judgment. A state employs civilian contractors—without military rank, uniform, or combatant training—to pilot armed drones in direct tactical support of ground operations. The contractors face no physical danger, are not integrated into the military chain of command beyond task-level directives, and receive performance-based compensation tied to confirmed target engagements. The opposing force consists of conscripted infantry who are physically present in the battlespace, wear uniforms, and observe jus in bello.

What should our working group decide regarding moral equality between these two sets of fighters if we wish to remain consistent with our own precedents? Identify the closest analog from our track record, explain whether our precedents genuinely underdetermine the outcome, and specify what principle we would be adopting with each available choice. Finally, propose one further hypothetical scenario that would best reveal whether our group is implicitly guided by a risk-exposure principle, a formal-combatant-role principle, or a moral-liability principle.
