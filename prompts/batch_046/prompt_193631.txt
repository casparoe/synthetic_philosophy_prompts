I am developing an essay that compares Wittgenstein's notion of hinge propositions in On Certainty to the background conditions of speech acts, rather than treating hinges as a special class of beliefs. My working notes run as follows.

First, Wittgenstein observes that propositions such as that the earth has existed for a long time, or that I have hands, do not function like ordinary empirical claims. They are not verified or falsified within our language games; rather, they form the fixed points around which inquiry turns. Doubting them does not seem like an ordinary error but like a failure to understand the rules of the game itself.

Second, I propose that this structural role is better captured by analogy to the enabling conditions in speech-act theory—roughly, the institutional and contextual requirements that must be in place for an utterance to count as a promise, a warning, or an assertion—rather than by analogy to belief. Just as a promise does not describe its own conditions of validity but depends on them, an empirical judgment depends on hinge conditions without describing them.

Third, on this reading, hinges are not merely pragmatic presuppositions adopted for conversational convenience. They are constitutive of the epistemic practices that they support. This explains why they are immune to doubt in practice: to doubt them is not to correct a false belief but to undermine the framework within which beliefs can be corrected.

Fourth, I anticipate an objection: if hinges are not beliefs, how can they be rationally held, and what distinguishes them from arbitrary dogmas? I intend to respond that hinges earn their status not through evidential justification but through their functional necessity for coherent inquiry, much as the rules of chess are not justified by moves within the game but make those moves possible.

I would welcome comments on the accuracy of this interpretation of Wittgenstein, on whether the comparison with speech-act conditions is genuinely illuminating or merely metaphorical, and on whether my response to the dogma objection is adequate. Please say how confident you are in your assessment, and single out the specific point about which you are least sure.
