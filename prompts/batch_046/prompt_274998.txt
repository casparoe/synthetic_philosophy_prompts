I'm working through objections to hedonist theories of well-being and keep running into a pattern that feels more specific than the usual appeals to "reality" or "authenticity," yet I can't place it in the literature. The familiar setup runs like this: someone cites Nozick's experience machine and argues that people reject plugging in because they want genuine achievement, real relationships, or direct contact with the world rather than simulated satisfaction. I then point out that many of those same people derive deep well-being from novels, immersive games, or virtual environments—experiences that are causally disconnected from real-world achievements—without feeling that anything vital is missing. The standard reply is that fiction and games preserve a kind of double consciousness: you are immersed, but you also maintain an awareness that the experience is constructed, so you never fully confuse it with reality. 

But this reply looks unstable once you compare cases more carefully. People often reject deeply immersive real-world states—such as method acting that erodes an actor's off-stage identity, or religious ecstasy that dissolves the boundary between self and the divine—precisely because that framing collapses, even though these states are causally "real." Conversely, people embrace flow states in athletics or music where immersion is total, yet the framing remains recoverable: you can step back, evaluate the performance, and own it as yours. So the boundary does not seem to track fiction versus reality, or mediation versus immediacy, but rather whether the subject retains a recoverable meta-position—a kind of exit frame—from which the experience can be assessed, interrupted, or claimed as one's own. In my notes I have been calling this the exit-frame condition.

Is there an established concept in philosophy of mind, aesthetics, or well-being theory that captures exactly this distinction? Or is it best treated as a refined variant of Nozick's contact-with-reality objection, or of demands for autonomy and authenticity? I want a committed answer rather than a menu: take a definite position on whether this is genuinely distinct, say precisely what it is not, and sketch the boundary cases—instances where the framing is present but well-being is still undermined, or where it is absent but well-being is preserved—so I can see where the edges of the phenomenon actually lie.
