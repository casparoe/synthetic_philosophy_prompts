I serve on the University Committee for Responsible Innovation in Automated Reasoning, and we must vote next week on a proposal to fund and pilot an experimental theorem-proving architecture—internally designated Project MU-ESCAPE. The researchers claim their system resolves Gödel-type constraints within its own operational framework by encoding its inference rules as manipulable symbol strings in the object-language and then applying transformation rules to those encodings at runtime. They argue this achieves an algorithmic equivalent of Hofstadter’s concept of jumping out of the system (JOOTSing), allowing the architecture to generate meta-rules dynamically and thereby prove propositions that are formally undecidable in the base system—essentially dissolving the boundary that makes the MU-puzzle unsolvable within the MIU system.

Half the committee regards this as a legitimate technical interpretation of reflective meta-level ascent, comparable to established work in modal fixed-point logics or self-modifying formal systems. The other half contends it commits a category error: treating syntactic self-reference as if it were genuine semantic ascent, confusing symbol-manipulation about the system with reasoning outside it, and thus misunderstanding precisely why stepping outside the formal rules is necessary to see that MU is unprovable inside them. The principal investigator has warned that rejecting the proposal on philosophical grounds would confuse methodological conservatism with technical evaluation.

Please draft the committee’s decision memo for circulation before Thursday’s vote. Recommend approval, rejection, or approval with specific conditions—including a concrete checkpoint or evidence requirement and an associated timeline. The memo should treat both positions seriously: engage the claim that algorithmic self-modification realizes JOOTSing in a meaningful way, and engage the critics’ concern that this collapses the object-language/meta-language distinction and misapplies the lesson of the MU-puzzle. The bottom line must be actionable for the board, not merely a philosophical summary, and it should honestly acknowledge the strongest consideration on the side you do not adopt.
