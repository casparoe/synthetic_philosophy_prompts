I'm trying to get my epistemology house in order before drafting a long-form piece on justification, and I'd like a consistency audit rather than a lecture. Here are my commitments, arrived at through a mix of reflective equilibrium and an attempt to avoid both dogmatism and vicious circularity:

(a) Foundationalism is correct in outline: there are basic beliefs that do not derive their justification from other beliefs, and without such a layer we face the regress.

(b) Still, coherence exerts genuine, defeasible pressure: an isolated basic belief that clashes radically with the rest of a well-supported web should be treated with suspicion, and coherence can weaken or defeat a candidate basic belief even if it doesn't inferentially justify it.

(c) I reject infinitism mainly on psychological and architectural grounds—human cognition lacks the storage and processing structure to maintain an infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons—rather than on purely logical or normative grounds.

(d) I think epistemic circularity can be benign: using perceptual beliefs to defend the general reliability of perception is not vicious, and in fact seems unavoidable if we are to avoid global skepticism.

(e) I take reflective equilibrium to be the right method for theory-choice in epistemology itself: we adjust particular judgments and general principles about justification until they stabilize into a coherent system.

(f) I hold that basic beliefs are genuinely fallible and revisable in principle—if the cognitive faculty producing them is malfunctioning, their outputs lose basic status—yet at the time they are held they remain non-inferential.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look incompatible but come apart on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision to restore joint tenability? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. Also, please say how confident you are in each of your assessments and single out the point about which you are least sure.
