I'm putting together my philosophical profile on Marx for a discussion group and want to recieve a consistency check before I present. Here are my commitments on exploitation and alienation:

(a) I think Marx's critique of exploitation is fundamentally distributive: capital appropriates surplus labor, and that's wrong regardless of how workers feel about it.

(b) At the same time, I think the real moral weight of Marx's view comes from alienation — especially species-being estrangement — and that a purely distributive wrong wouldn't be enough to justify revolutionary change.

(c) I reject the labor theory of value as an economic theory; prices aren't determined by socially neccessary labor time.

(d) Still, I think value-form analysis (the idea that capital imposes abstract labor as a social form) is correct and necessary to explain how exploitation works structurally.

(e) I hold that capitalism necessarily produces alienation in all four of Marx's dimensions, including alienation from thier fellow workers.

(f) But I also think that under "real subsumption" workers can internalize capital's logic so thoroughly that they no longer feel alienated, even though surplus extraction continues.

(g) And I think worker cooperatives inside a market economy could eliminate the structural dimensions of alienation without fully socializing ownership, which would count as a partial socialist victory even if some surplus extraction remains.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like it. Rank the tensions by severity — don't just list them all as equally bad. For each real tension, say what's the least costly revision (which commitment gives, and what that costs me). Also tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems at once. Don't lecture me on Marx 101; I want an audit of my own set.
