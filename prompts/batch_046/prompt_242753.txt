Read Nolans Memento through the Gödelian analogy in Hofstadter's GEB — the move from self-referential formulas (Gödel numbering a statement about its own unprovability) to the idea that minds become self-aware through strange loops or tangled hierarchies. Leonard Shelby's memory system — tattoos, polaroids, notes he writes to himself — looks like a physical attempt to build a self-referancing loop outside his own head. Does the film dramatize that kind of loop generating consciousness, or does it show that any externalized memory is always derived intentionality, never original?

I'm especially curious about the narrative structure itself: the black-and-white/color reversal means the audience experiences the plot in a loop that mirrors Leonard's broken chronology. Is that a formal analog to Gödel's formula encoding a statement about its own provability? Or is it just a clever editing trick?

Where the analogy seems strongest — maybe in how Leonard's notes point back to an author who is also their subject — and where it breaks down? For instance, Gödel's result requires a consistent formal system, but Leonard's system is constantly being rewritten by Teddy and Natalie. Does that suggest self-awareness needs a kind of system integrity that actual minds don't have? And since the film ends where it begins, are we looking at an undecidable loop, or just a story that refuses to close?

I'd like a reading that takes both the film's mechanics and Hofstadter's argument seriously, and says whether Memento ultimately supports the idea that consciousness is just a sufficiently tangled self-reference, or whether it shows that analogy misses something essential about embodiment and deception.
