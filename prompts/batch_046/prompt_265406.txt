I work in hierarchical reinforcement learning, and I think naturally in terms of policies, reward functions, environment distributions, meta-learning rates, state representations, exploration bonuses, value alignment, and the distinction between behavior cloning and adaptive generalization. Take the debate between virtue ethics—specifically the Aristotelian account of hexis as a stable disposition, phronesis as flexible practical judgment that integrates particular circumstances, and the unity of virtues as coherence across moral domains—and the situationist critique, which holds that observed moral behavior shows little cross-situational consistency and is driven more by situational features than by enduring character traits. Restate both positions entirely within my vocabulary.

For the virtue-ethical side: translate hexis into claims about agent architecture. Is it a slowly updated meta-policy parameter, a regularized backbone shared across contexts, or a latent character vector that conditions sub-policies? How should phronesis be rendered—as a high-level value function that reweights lower-level policies, as an adaptive exploration strategy, or as a form of hierarchical credit assignment? And how does the unity of virtues map—does it imply that all moral sub-policies share a single reward-function backbone, that generalization across domains should be perfect, or that there is a unified state representation of the good?

For the situationist side: translate the empirical pattern of context-dependent behavior into claims about overfitting, distribution shift, narrow training environments, or environment-specific heuristics. Does the absence of cross-situational consistency mean there is no stable character vector at all, or only that the observed distribution of environments was too sparse to reveal one?

Then report the residue systematically. Which normative claims survive—say, that moral development requires repeated practice, which might become a claim about curriculum design or reward shaping? Which claims distort: does the idea that virtue involves emotional attunement reduce awkwardly to reward-signal calibration, or does it vanish? Which claims have no expression whatsoever—perhaps that the good life is a teleologically unified whole, or that practical wisdom includes an irreducibly first-person perspective on flourishing? For each residue, say whether it points to a genuine limitation of reinforcement-learning vocabulary—something about normativity, human flourishing, or embodied emotional response that agent architectures genuinely omit—or whether it reveals that the original philosophical claim was less substantive than it appeared, functioning mainly as a black-box label for behavioral regularities that RL describes more precisely. Keep the translation honest: do not smuggle in RL assumptions, such as the idea that all behavior is reward maximization, and present them as faithful renderings of virtue ethics. Treat the failures of translation as the most interesting part of the exercise.
