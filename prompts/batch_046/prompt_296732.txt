Design a final-exam essay question (90 minutes, approximately 1000 words, 25 marks) for an upper-level seminar on strategic rationality and game theory, centered on the backward induction paradox in the centipede game. The question must introduce an original variant—the opaque-termination centipede—in which the game is guaranteed to terminate at some node between 4 and 10, but the exact terminal node is fixed by an external mechanism that is not observable to either player and does not change the visible payoff rules (continuation roughly doubles the shared pot; taking yields an immediate asymmetric split with a small risk of mutual loss). Ask students to evaluate the following thesis: backward induction cannot begin in this variant not because the agents are irrational or boundedly rational, but because the induction requires common knowledge of a precise base case that is mechanistically hidden; consequently, the classic paradox confuses a property of rational agents with a property of epistemically transparent game mechanisms.

Provide exactly three items:

1. The exam question itself, clearly stating the time limit, word target, and an instruction that answers should not cite named authors, texts, or bibliographies.

2. A model answer (around 600 words) that distinguishes conditions on rational agency from conditions on epistemic access to the mechanism defining the game tree, explains why hidden termination blocks the induction without rescuing cooperation, and addresses whether the paradox in the standard game is thereby dissolved or merely relocated.

3. A marking scheme totaling 25 marks, built around five specific conceptual moves a top answer must make. For each move, state the marks it carries and include a one-line note on the typical mistake that causes candidates to lose those marks. Do not ask for citations or reading lists anywhere in the deliverable.
