heres the answer:
Reductionists like Hume say testimony is never a basic source of knowledge but just derives from perception and inductive reasoning about speaker reliability. Anti-reductionists like Reid and Burge treat testimony as fundamental, comparable to perception. Reid's principle of credulity states that hearers naturally tend to believe what they are told and that speakers naturally tend to tell the truth, which removes the need for prior verification and answers Hume directly. Burge's acceptance principle says a listener is entitled to accept testimony unless they have specific defeaters, and this avoids circularity because you do not need independent proof first. But if listeners sometimes reject testimony that conflicts with background beliefs, then anti-reductionism collapses into reductionism, since individual reasoning is doing all the work. Therefore Reid and Burge mostly succeed, though not completely.
