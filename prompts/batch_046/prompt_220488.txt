People have been arguing about Williams's integrity objection and the one-thought-too-many problem since the 70s and honestly it feels like we're running in cricles. The utilitarian reply is usually that you can bake the calculation into your dispositions or social rules so the husband saves his wife out of love and only the system does the math, but Williams-style critics say that still leaves the agent with the wrong kind of reason. My hunch is that part of the stall is that one side is asking a normative question about what reasons are permissible and the other is asking a descriptive psycholoy question about how actual agents experience deliberation, and they share a vocabulary but not a target. But maybe I'm wrong and it's actually a verbal disagreement about what "integrity" requires, or maybe there's a formal point about agent-relative reasons that hasn't been resolved. Diagnose the stall for me: is it substantive, verbal, or two different projects under one heading? Use what each side actually says, not a strawman. And tell me what would count as real progress here — a distinction, an empirical study about deliberation, a formal result? I want to know what's settled enough that I shouldn't waste time on it versus what's genuinely contested. Don't present either camp's interpretation of Williams as the consensus view.
