I am preparing a graduate seminar on epistemic injustice and wish to evaluate whether several proposed extensions of Miranda Fricker's framework represent genuinely distinct forms of harm or merely derivative redescriptions of testimonial and hermeneutical injustice combined with broader social injustice. Please consider the following five candidate phenomena: (1) algorithmic testimonial deflation, in which automated credibility-scoring systems systematically downgrade the epistemic authority of speakers from particular linguistic communities without individual human prejudice being the proximate cause; (2) hermeneutical overcrowding, in which dominant interpretive resources are so plentiful that minority conceptual frameworks are not ignored but drowned out, rendering them practically inaccessible to shared inquiry; (3) institutional epistemic gaslighting, in which an organisation systematically induces members to doubt the reliability of their own perceptual or testimonial capacities; (4) credibility inflation asymmetry, in which members of a dominant group receive unwarranted epistemic surplus that cannot be explained by merit and that structurally prevents the correction of false beliefs within that group; and (5) silent expertise suppression, in which individuals possess specialised knowledge but are structurally positioned so that their knowledge is never solicited, not because they are deemed unreliable, but because the social architecture of inquiry never reaches them.

Rank these five candidates from 1 (the strongest case) to 5 (the weakest case) according to how well they satisfy three jointly necessary conditions for counting as a genuinely distinct form of epistemic injustice: (A) the harm must be specifically epistemic — it must damage someone's capacity to be recognised as a knower or to participate in shared meaning-making, not merely cause a social or material disadvantage; (B) there must be a structural asymmetry in power that is not reducible to individual prejudice; and (C) — the condition I wish to stress — the harm must be non-derivable: it must not be fully analysable as an instance of testimonial injustice, hermeneutical injustice, or a combination thereof together with an independent social injustice.

For each rank, provide a concise justification in British English, explaining how the candidate fares against conditions (A)–(C), with particular attention to (C). If you judge any candidates to be tied, assign them the same rank and explain why. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I require only conceptual analysis.
