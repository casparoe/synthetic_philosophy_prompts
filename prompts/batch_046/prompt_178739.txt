I'm drafting a taxonomy handout for a philosophy-of-mind reading group—mostly longtime rationalist-blog readers who are already fluent in the Chinese Room and the intentional stance, but who still conflate "derived" with "fake" and "original" with "magically intrinsic." I want a decision-tree instrument that sorts people by their actual commitments on original versus derived intentionality, using Dennett's wandering two-bitser and Searle's syntax-is-not-semantics argument as the central reference points, but without requiring anyone to name-drop philosophers.

Please construct a four-level taxonomy tree. At each internal node, pose a concrete case or claim that splits the logical space; phrase it so a respondent can answer without already knowing which philosopher the answer corresponds to.

Root node: Dennett's wandering two-bitser vending machine tracks quarters, updates an internal state-register, and behaves in ways that make "it wants more quarters" a useful prediction. Does this system possess intentionality? If yes, is it original, derived, or purely heuristic? (This yields three branches.)

Second-level split: Do biological brains possess original intentionality? Offer three branches: (i) yes, in a metaphysically irreducible, biologically intrinsic way (the Searlean option); (ii) yes, but only in a functional or evolutionary sense that is not metaphysically mysterious (the Dennett-style option); (iii) no—brains are just complex artifacts of natural selection, so even human intentionality is ultimately derived.

Third-level split: Introduce a hybrid case. A neural prosthesis has gradually replaced 40% of a person's dorsolateral prefrontal cortex with silicon that is functionally isomorphic but originally engineered with derived intentionality (like a scaled-up two-bitser module). The remaining tissue still produces beliefs. Are the hybrid system's planning states originally intentional, derivatively intentional, stacked or mixed, or is the distinction inapplicable? (Four branches.)

Fourth-level split: Is predictive accuracy—getting the right behavioral predictions—ever sufficient by itself to justify an intentional attribution, regardless of substrate, evolutionary history, or design origin? (Two branches: yes, sufficient on its own; no, never sufficient without some further condition.)

Every leaf should be labeled with a position name (for example, "Searlean intrinsicism," "Dennett-style original/derived dualism," "pure interpretivism," "evolutionary derivation view," "hybrid-stacking view") or marked "unnamed" if no named view occupies that combination. Each leaf needs one sentence describing its most awkward or counter-intuitive commitment. You must also explicitly flag any pairs of leaves that your tree cannot separate—i.e., combinations of answers that map onto the same named position despite different branch choices.

In addition, list the unoccupied combinations and briefly diagnose each gap: is it incoherent (for example, granting original intentionality to the vending machine while denying it in brains), unattractive (for example, requiring biological substrate but accepting pure predictive sufficiency), or merely unexplored?

Constraints: Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation requests. The instrument should be gradable by someone without web access. Do not rely on obscure theories that a well-read but non-specialist rationalist audience wouldn't recognize—stick to the conceptual machinery of original versus derived distinction, the intentional stance, and the Chinese Room, plus the hybrid prosthesis scenario described above. Keep the tone formal and polished.
