Construct a formal taxonomy—structured as a decision tree with binary or trinary branch points—of philosophical positions on Ulysses contracts and constitutional precommitment, intended for use in a graduate political-philosophy seminar. The root node should pose the question: is it rationally permissible for an agent, whether individual or collective, to restrict its own future option-set in order to prevent anticipated failures of judgment or will? From there, build branches that separate positions on: (i) whether legitimate precommitment requires that the future self's anticipated welfare be improved, or whether it may be justified even when the future self predictably dissents; (ii) whether the binding mechanism must be transparent to the bound party, as with an explicit constitutional clause, or may operate opaquely through institutional architecture that conceals the constraint; (iii) whether democratic majorities possess intergenerational authority to entrench substantive policies, or only procedural rules governing future revision; (iv) whether the risk of misidentifying one's future interests—due to preference change, adaptive preference formation, or epistemic humility—defeats the case for binding altogether; and (v) whether the scale or irreversibility of the committed course introduces a distinct deontic constraint against locking in. Each node must be expressed as a concrete scenario or normative claim, for example a constituent assembly that entrenches a climate-policy mandate which a later fully informed democratic majority wishes to repeal but cannot, so that a student could evaluate it without knowing which philosophical view it supports. The leaves should locate named positions where they exist—rational-precommitment accounts in the tradition of Elster, democratic anti-precommitment views associated with Waldron, generational-sovereignty presentism in the Jeffersonian line, and dual-track constitutionalism—while also marking any logically available but unoccupied combinations with a one-sentence diagnosis explaining whether the gap is due to incoherence, instability, or mere neglect. For every named pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate given these five branching questions, flag the ambiguity explicitly and identify the shared commitment that causes the overlap. Finally, for each leaf state in one sentence its most awkward normative commitment. The taxonomy should require no bibliographic lookup, cite no specific texts, and rely solely on conceptual distinctions available to a reader without web access.
