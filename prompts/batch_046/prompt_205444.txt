I'm not a philosopher, but I've been arguing with a friend about whether you need to actually be able to do otherwise in order to be morally responsible for what you do. Would you write a dialogue between two thoughtful non-specialists — Jordan and Casey — who disagree on exactly this?

Jordan holds that responsibility requires what Jordan calls a live mental fork: not merely that the world could have turned out differently, but that the agent's own mind genuinely holds open more than one path at the decisive moment. Casey thinks this is too demanding and describes a modified Frankfurt-style case: imagine a concert pianist, Elena, playing a difficult piece. Unknown to her, an automatic device is wired to her hands; if she were about to play a wrong note, it would instantly correct her fingers, though she herself intends to play correctly and does so smoothly, never triggering the device. Casey describes this dormant, never-activated mechanism as a kind of shadow will sitting behind Elena's own, and asks Jordan whether Elena still possesses the live mental fork Jordan demands, and whether Elena's complete ignorance of the device makes any difference to her authorship.

Jordan should defend the view that responsibility tracks the actual causal chain of the agent's own decision, not counterfactual shadows that never enter it, and should answer Casey's point about ignorance. Casey should press back by asking whether Jordan's live mental fork can survive cases of reflexive or habitual action where the agent never consciously represents an alternative, and whether knowing about a hidden constraint changes its moral significance even if it never activates. Have them respond directly to each other's arguments rather than talking past one another, with neither side reduced to a strawman. Let them conclude by pinpointing exactly where their disagreement lies — whether it is about the nature of possibility itself, about the relevance of knowledge of constraints, or about what properly counts as the agent's own contribution.
