That feels philosophicaly weird and inconsistaint — like gender is fluid for marketing but fixed for security. What theory of gender as a kind does this split presuppose? Does it assume gender is socially constructed in public life but biologically essential for legal identity? Or is it just about tractability — granular data for engagement, binary simplicity for fraud? Reconstruct the implicit theory that makes both sides hang together, say whether the practice actually needs it or is compatible with rival views (maybe cluster-style family resemblance, or pure administrative self-declaration), and tell me what an account-recovery system built on the idea that gender has no deep essence but is a practical cluster would have to change.
