I'm prepping an interveiw for my column on the weirder edges of legal philosphy — I cover how institutional rules bump up against logic without anyone noticing. Next guest is Dr. Lena Marchetti, a legal philosopher who defends a pretty specific line on constitutional self-amendment: she thinks Ross's old paradox about a rule that amends itself isn't really a contradiction, but a kind of nomic loop that gets dissolved once you split the constitutional text (the object-language) from the social practice that accepts amendments (the meta-level). Her key move is a "continuity convention" — not a legal rule itself, but a shared institutional habit that lets Article V-style clauses be replaced without circular authority. I want about eight interview questions that don't let her just restate that frame. I need one that presses the infinite-regress worry: if the continuity convention isn't in the constitution, what authorizes it, and doesn't that just push the problem up a level? For each, I want a follow-up keyed to her usual deflection that I'm "confusing object-language and meta-language levels." I also want one question on whether her view is descriptive of actual constitutional practice or normative, plus a closing one about what real-world institutional breakdown or court decision could actually change her mind. Can you draft these? Make them sound like I'm actually talking to her, not grading a paper.
