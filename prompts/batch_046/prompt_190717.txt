In discussions of global workspace theory and the access-phenomenal distinction, I have noticed a recurring configuration that does not map neatly onto either category alone. A subject can genuinely report experiencing a feature—say, the orientation of a briefly masked figure—while the global broadcast is active, yet all adaptive behavioral effects (priming, motor correction, learning updates) are mediated exclusively by the earlier local sensory representation, with no causal contribution from either the report or the workspace activation. This is not standard blindsight, since phenomenal awareness is present; it is not pure access-consciousness failure, since broadcasting succeeds; and it is not ordinary epiphenomenalism, since the decoupling is specific to the behavioral pathway rather than a global absence of causal efficacy. Is there an established name for this precise tri-partite separation—phenomenal presence, global access, and behavioral efficacy each intact but causally isolated from one another? If not, how should its boundaries be drawn against nearby cases such as cognitive penetration failures or dissociated control, and what further example would best distinguish it?
