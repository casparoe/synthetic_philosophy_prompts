I just finished a long, somewhat exhausting argument with a friend about whether knowing ordinary things requires tracking every far-fetched skeptical possibility. We kept circling Nozick’s tracking conditions and whether they genuinely break epistemic closure, but the confusion seemed to come from never laying out the conceptual dependencies in the right order—my friend kept treating tracking as a fancy name for reliability, and then assuming that if you fail to track one entailment you must fail to know the base claim. I want to teach this properly, not to my friend but to a specified audience: a fifty-minute session for advanced philosophy undergraduates who have worked through Gettier but have never encountered modal conditions on knowledge. I do not want the explanation dumped on me; I want a teaching plan that constructs the idea so it does not collapse into “knowledge is just well-supported belief.”

1. The dependency chain (about 350 words): The precise order in which the components must be introduced.
