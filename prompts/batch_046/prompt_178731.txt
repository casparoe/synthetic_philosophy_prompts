I've been trying to apply Benatar's asymmetry to adoption policy and I think I've hit a contradiciton that nobody talks about. If pleasure's absence isnt bad for the merely possible child, but suffering is bad for the actual one, then adopting an existing child gives them goods that werent owed and avoids no counterfactual harm (since they already exist). Meanwhile, not adopting just leaves them in an institution, which is suffering they do experience. So on pure asymmetry grounds, shouldnt every able family be obligated to adopt until all institutionalized kids are placed? Benatar never draws this out, and anti-natalists I read online never mention adoption obligations. Is the hidden fix that the asymmetry only binds potential parents about procreation, not about existing children? Or does the comparison collapse once you stop comparing existence/non-existence and start comparing institutional care vs family care? I feel like I'm mixing up person-affecting reasons with impersonal ones but I cant see where the line is.
