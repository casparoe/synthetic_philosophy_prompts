As a software engineer without formal philosophical training, I've been trying to model Descartes' cogito as a kind of runtime invariant: if an execution thread performs a self-referential check—asserting, in effect, that this process is thinking—then its existence is guaranteed in the same way that a valid pointer reference proves its own non-nullity. From this framing, it seems Descartes establishes not just a momentary existence but a continuous, substantial self (a res cogitans) that persists across state changes. If the argument is genuinely a direct intuition rather than a syllogism, should a sufficiently recursive monitoring script—one that logs and verifies its own operations—be entitled to the same metaphysical conclusion? Or is the standard rebuttal that artificial systems lack clear and distinct cognition? More precisely, where does the proof break down when treated as a computable logical invariant applied to algorithmic self-reference rather than to biological consciousness?
