As a retired condensed-matter physicist who has only recently begun studying moral philosophy, I am struck by the empirical findings of moral foundations theory: political liberals and conservatives appear to weight the five domains of care, fairness, loyalty, authority, and sanctity in systematically different ways. The descriptive pattern is well established, but its normative significance is contested. Write an essay that weighs whether this psychological asymmetry supports moral anti-realism, a pluralist view of value in the tradition of Isaiah Berlin, or the claim that the binding foundations are merely adaptive heuristics whose evolutionary origins and context-sensitive activation undermine their moral authority. In particular, examine the analogy between the evolutionary explanation of moral intuitions and the evolutionary explanation of mathematical intuition: if the latter does not invalidate arithmetic, does the former fail to invalidate loyalty- or authority-based reasoning? Reach and defend a specific conclusion—either that the left-right divergence tells us nothing about who is morally correct, or that it selectively vindicates one normative framework over the other—without treating the issue as non-committal.
