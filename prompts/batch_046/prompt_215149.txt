I've been hanging out in moral philosphy forums and keep seeing the same weird dance. Someone brings up a case — like the trolley problem but with a remote button, or a charity case where you can save one person you know versus ten strangers. People split on the right answer. Then instead of arguing about the principle, they start labeling each others intuitions: you're just biased toward physical proximity, or that's evolutionary disgust talking, not morality. Meanwhile they treat their own gut reaction as tracking the actual moral fact. What's strange is that everyone in the thread agrees that some moral feelings are biases — nobody thinks every instinct is sacred — but the line between bias and genuine moral judgment always lands exactly where each person's own intuitions sit. Is there an established name for this specific asymmetry, where people partition moral intuitions into real and fake using a boundry that just happens to protect their own view? I know about motivated reasoning and confirmation bias, but those feel too broad — I'm asking about the particular move of using bias as a selective debunker. If there's no precise term, how would you draw its edges: is it different from plain moral disagreement, and what would distinguish it from someone genuinely recognizing a bias in themselves?
