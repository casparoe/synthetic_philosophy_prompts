Since I recently began apprenticing in antique watch restoration, I have encountered a conceptual slippage I cannot precisely name. In our workshop, a replacement gear produced by an authorized supplier is treated as an authentic restoration, whereas an identically dimensioned gear fabricated independently—whether by hand or additive manufacturing—is dismissed as a copycat part, despite neither being original to the movement. The evaluation seems to turn on social authorization rather than on physical or functional properties, yet both judgments invoke the same vocabulary of integrity, originality, and fidelity. I observe an analogous drift in current disputes over generative music trained on a specific composer’s corpus: copycat slides between legal infringement, aesthetic betrayal, and technical reproduction without anyone marking the transition. Is there an established philosophical concept for this kind of microdomain fluidity, in which terms such as copycat and authentic drift across provenance, intent, and regulatory status within a tightly bounded practice? How should it be distinguished from ordinary ambiguity or from Wittgensteinian family resemblance? And if it lacks an established name, what defining boundaries would separate it from both?
