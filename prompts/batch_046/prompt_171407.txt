Given that Dewey treats democratic politics as a mode of social inquiry in which collective problems are addressed through the experimental testing of proposed solutions, and given that formal models in distributed epistemology now show that groups converge on more accurate beliefs when individual inputs are weighted by demonstrated track-record reliability, it seems Dewey should have endorsed an epistemically calibrated deliberative procedure rather than the equal-participation model he actually defended. Where exactly does the analogy between scientific experimentation and democratic deliberation break down so as to block this weighting scheme, and does Dewey’s commitment to the growth of shared, communicative experience prevent democratic inquiry from being reduced to any form of calibrated belief aggregation at all?
