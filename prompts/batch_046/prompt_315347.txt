I'm trying to work out what I actually think about tearing down statues, renaming streets, and all that commemoration ethics stuff, going by my gut on cases rather than some pre-baked take. There's this bronze of a Victorian shipping baron outside the old town hall in the centre -- he funded the local library but made his fortune in the slave trade, and the statue went up in 1901 with a plaque praising "civilising commerce". Verdict: take it off its plinth, but don't melt it down; stick it in the local museum with full context. That feels right.

Then there's the same baron's name on the local primary school. Verdict: rename it, no question. Naming a school after him feels honourific in a way that just putting up a statue doesn't... or maybe thats backwards?

There's a bridge named after an engineer who designed half the sewage system but was also a vocal eugenicist. Verdict: keep the name, maybe add an explanatory plaque, but don't rename. The bridge is named for the engineering achievement, not for moral endorsement. But I'm not sure thats not just a rationalisation.

There's a monument to conscript soldiers who died in a clearly unjust colonial war. Verdict: leave it standing, but recontextualise -- they were victims too, not heroes of empire.

Then there's the case I keep going back and forth on: a statue of a 17th-century merchant who funded the town's first hospital. His ships also carried enslaved people, though he wasn't primarily a trader in them -- more that he invested. The statue was put up in 1920 by a group that wanted to celebrate "pioneer spirit". Sometimes I think it should come down because the commemorative intent was racist and the money was tainted. Other times I think putting it in a museum hides the problem, and leaving it in the square with a big counter-monument or plaque is actually more honest. I genuinely can't settle it.

There's also a park named after a local activist who fought hard for workers' rights but was also involved in an attack that killed civilians. Verdict: I think the name should stay, because the good he did outweighs the violence? But then why am I so sure the school should be renamed after the baron, who also funded the library? That pair feels hard to square, and I can't seperate them cleanly.

And tearing down a statue of an actual fascist collaborator that was erected during the occupation: verdict is take it down and store it in a depot, don't display it prominently. But melting it still feels wrong -- like destroying evidence.

I've been reading a bit about counter-monument ideas, where altering the context is more powerful than removal. Maybe thats part of the principel I'm groping for.

So: is there a coherent principle behind all this, or do I have to drop one of my verdicts? I'd like it stated precisely, with what case motivates each clause, what it says about cases I haven't considered, and an honest admission of which of my gut feelings it can't save. Especially, what does it say about the merchant statue I keep oscillating on, and does it force me to either rename the bridge or keep the school?
