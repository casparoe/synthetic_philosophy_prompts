As a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun auditing philosophy seminars, I have been attempting to navigate the debate between mathematical platonism and nominalism without relying on specialist literature. In a recent discussion group, a fellow participant summarized an unnamed position she called "calibrated nominalism," apparently drawn from a working essay in philosophy of science. The summary was as follows. Calibrated nominalism denies that mathematical objects possess any mind-independent existence, yet it also rejects the idea that they are merely subjective mental constructions; instead, every mathematical claim that survives rigorous scientific scrutiny is said to be anchored to invariant relational structures extracted from calibrated physical measurement apparatuses. On this account, we do not causally interact with abstract entities, so Benacerraf’s well-known epistemological challenge to platonism is dissolved—not because we have discovered a hidden causal pathway to a non-physical realm, but because mathematical knowledge derives entirely from the reproducible behavior of instruments operating under standardized conditions. The summary further claimed that this view is essentially structural realism applied to mathematics, and that it therefore entails the rejection of pure mathematics lacking any conceivable instrumental interpretation, since such mathematics would be deprived of the physical anchoring necessary for truth.

I am inclined to think that this argument conflates epistemic access with ontological commitment, and that the identification with structural realism may be too quick, but I am not certain where the missteps lie. Could you audit this summary for me? Please separate what is accurate about standard nominalist or structuralist commitments, what counts as a defensible popular simplification, and what involves a subtle misrepresentation—particularly regarding whether instrument calibration can genuinely substitute for causal interaction in addressing Benacerraf’s problem, whether calibrated nominalism is genuinely a form of structural realism rather than a distinct hybrid, and whether the rejection of non-instrumental pure mathematics actually follows from the premises given. I am not seeking a bibliography or a list of further readings; I want to know whether the reasoning holds together once its parts are disentangled, and whether a defender of either orthodox nominalism or orthodox platonism would recognize this position as a coherent alternative or as a category mistake.
