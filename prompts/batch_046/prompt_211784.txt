I just finished a long, exhausting argument with a friend about whether large language models force us to expand Dennett's taxonomy of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—beyond its four categories, or whether they are ultimately just hyper-scaled Skinnerian systems with enough parameters to mimic Popperian error-correction. She proposed a fifth kind she called "Stochastic Synthesizers": architectures that learn not by generating falsifiable internal hypotheses, not by environmental reinforcement of successful outputs, but by interpolating across high-dimensional statistical manifolds in ways that produce behaviorally appropriate responses without explicit representational states or selective retention of rewarded trajectories. I argued that this is a redescription of associative learning with sufficient dimensionality, not a genuinely distinct cognitive architecture. I want to move past the benchmark-level debate and ask something more structural. What's your actual credence—expressed as a number—that Dennett's four-kind taxonomy is exhaustive for both biological and artificial cognition, such that no genuinely distinct fifth kind exists, neither in current LLMs nor in any plausible alternative architecture? Decompose it precisely: (1) the probability that the four kinds mark genuinely discontinuous architectural boundaries rather than arbitrary points on a smooth continuum of cognitive complexity; (2) the probability that present large language models instantiate anything deserving the name "Popperian"—internal generation and testing of hypotheses—rather than operating through purely statistical pattern-completion and reinforcement-shaped output selection; (3) the probability that my friend's "Stochastic Synthesizer" describes a coherent, distinct kind of mind that could learn effectively without either Popperian hypothesis-generation or Skinnerian environmental reinforcement, as opposed to being a mischaracterization of scaled associative processes; (4) how these terms combine into your overall credence, with explicit attention to whether they are independent or coupled; and (5) which of these sub-questions you are least confident in, and what specific evidence or conceptual argument over the next few years would move it most. I am not asking for a literature review or a taxonomy of Dennett interpreters; I want the defended number, the dependency structure among its parts, and the explicit weak point where your reasoning is most exposed.
