You are an expert in legal philosophy and formal normative systems, with particular attention to self-reference, hierarchical rules, and constitutional design. Before drafting anything, please take a moment to brainstorm the logical architecture of stratified rule systems, then think step by step and proceed with care.

As a novelist constructing the philosophical backbone for a work of speculative fiction, I need you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on what I will call the Problem of Reflexive Amendment. The first camp, the Formalists, holds that a legal order can coherently institute a meta-rule—call it a Rule of Recursive Authorization—that permits the system to alter its own amendment procedures, provided the hierarchy is strictly stratified: primary norms, secondary powers of amendment, and a single tertiary rule of recognition that identifies valid changes without itself being subject to amendment except by its own specified procedure. The second camp, the Reflexive Skeptics, contends that any rule purporting to govern its own revision generates a paradox of legal self-reference structurally analogous to logical antinomies, so that the system must either be incomplete or depend on an extra-legal, non-legal foundation for its ultimate authority.

Please produce a formal, polished document that both sides could sign. Structure it exactly as follows.

First, a numbered section of agreed-upon claims about the formal conditions for valid amendment, the distinction between altering a rule and violating it, and the necessity that any self-amending provision specify both its conditions of activation and its conditions of termination.

Second, a disagreements table setting out the precise formulations of their residual opposition. Each formulation should be worded so that the camp it describes would accept it as an accurate statement of its own position. Within this table, clearly separate disagreements that are genuinely substantive from those that dissolve once terminology is fixed.

Third, the cruxes. Specifically address: whether a hierarchical ordering of rules can terminate without an external anchor; whether a self-amending rule can consistently specify the conditions of its own invalidation; and whether the distinction between recursive authorization—the power to change the rules of change—and reflexive entrenchment—a provision that binds its own future modification by requiring a supermajority threshold that itself may be lowered—can be maintained without contradiction.

Fourth, for each crux, present a concrete hypothetical case or constitutional mechanism that both sides agree bears directly on it, together with each side's predicted resolution recorded in advance. For instance, consider a constitutional Clause Sigma stating that this clause may be repealed only by unanimous consent, and that this consent-requirement itself may be altered by simple majority. Or consider a tribunal empowered to review the constitutionality of the very amendment that established its jurisdiction.

Finally, conclude with an assessment of whether any portion of the dispute collapses once the distinction between recursive authorization and reflexive entrenchment is made precise, and indicate which single crux, if settled by argument or evidence, would most likely move one camp.

Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific works. You may invoke well-known conceptual distinctions—such as primary and secondary rules—but do not depend on obscure theories, named paradoxes, or scholars outside common philosophical knowledge. Keep the register rigorous and suitable for inclusion in philosophical research notes.
