everyone in my philophy of mind group keeps comparing split-brain patients to two roommates who share an apartment but had the intercom cut -- they say the left hand literally doesnt know what the right is planning, so we should treat them as two seperate conscious subjects sharing one lease. they lean on this to push back against anyone who insists there's still just one person in there. how far does the roommate analogy actually hold? i want the correspondence table -- apartment, lease, intercom, shared fridge, the fact that they still sleep in the same bed. then the breaks: does the body act like a landlord neither roommate chose? is there a shared bank account (hormones, sleep cycles) that real roommates dont have? and does their conclusion -- that we clearly have two subjects -- survive once you list what the source analogy smuggles in? specifically, roommates can eventually move out, but these hemispheres are stuck with the same heart and gut feelings. finally, is there a less loaded analogy that doesnt bake unity or disunity into the framing from the start?
