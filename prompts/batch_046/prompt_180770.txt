My roommates Maya and Jon got into it over dinner about the lottery paradox and the preface paradox. Maya said there literally the same bug: you believe each of 1000 lottery tickets will lose, but you also know one wins, so you've got 1000 beliefs that clash with a known fact. Same with an author who believes every claim in her 400-page book but admits in the preface that some are wrong. Jon said thats too quick -- in the lottery your tracking objective frequencies, but in the preface your tracking your own fallibility as a reasoner, so treating them identically is a category mistake.

Maya came back: if the mechanisms differ, why do both produce the exact same logical tension? She proposed this "action-block" fix -- we only need consistency within the beliefs we're actually using for the decision right now, not across our whole mental library. So you can hold all 1000 lottery beliefs because you never act on them all at once.

Jon said thats just gerrymandering. He asked why the block should be 5 beliefs or 500? Without a principled boundary, you're not solving the paradox, just hiding it in the partition. He also pointed out that on her view belief becomes weirdly context-dependent: if I consider ticket 1 and 2 together, do I suddenly have to drop one?

Maya replied that context-dependence isnt weird, its just practical reasoning. The boundary isn't arbitrary because its fixed by whatever problem is on the table -- working memory has limits, so closure should respect them.
