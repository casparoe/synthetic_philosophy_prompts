For my graduate seminar on comparative skepticism, I am testing an interpretation of the butterfly transformation passage in the Zhuangzi that moves beyond the familiar comparison with Cartesian dream skepticism. In a recent faculty colloquium, two interlocutors advanced competing readings, and I would like a rigorous adjudication of their exchange.

The first speaker argued that the passage is not skeptical in any recognizable epistemic sense. On her view, Zhuangzi's point concerns ontological transformation—hua—rather than knowledge. The boundary between Zhuang Zhou and the butterfly is not an epistemic veil to be penetrated but a relational boundary that dissolves under scrutiny. The remark about not knowing which form one occupies is therefore not an expression of Cartesian doubt but an acknowledgment that identity is processual and situational. She introduced the notion of indexical transparency: in the dream-state, the first-person perspective is fully coextensive with the form it takes, leaving no hidden true self behind the appearance. Consequently, asking whether one is really the butterfly or the human is, on this reading, metaphysically malformed—like asking which of two equivalent descriptions captures the real number.

The second speaker countered that this domesticates the text's genuinely disruptive force. He insisted that if transformation is universal and there is no stable criterion to privilege waking consciousness over dream consciousness, then any belief formed in either state is equally unsupported. The absence of a cogito-style escape route, he added, makes Zhuangzi's position more radical, not less: it is global skepticism without the Cartesian remedy. He granted that the affective tone differs—Zhuangzi reports a kind of joyful ease rather than anxious doubt—but maintained that the logical structure remains skeptical. He further argued that indexical transparency is not a metaphysical insight but an epistemic deficit relabeled: the lack of reflexive distance means the absence of a check, not the presence of a deeper truth. By abandoning substance ontology for a process ontology, the first speaker avoids skepticism only by changing the subject—eliminating the persistent subject about whom skeptical questions could be raised.

In reply, the first speaker conceded that her view requires rejecting a substance-based account of the self, but she denied that this is ad hoc. She claimed that Zhuangzi's ontology is event-based, so there is no single persisting I that could be deceived across states. The second speaker responded that this simply relocates the problem: if there is no persistent self, there is no subject of knowledge, which is a different philosophical position altogether, not a resolution of the skeptical challenge within a shared framework.

Please adjudicate this disagreement move by move. For each round, identify which speaker makes the stronger point, where both go wrong, and what each is missing. In particular, address whether indexical transparency successfully distinguishes Zhuangzian transformation from Cartesian skepticism, or whether it collapses into a form of anti-realism about the self. Then offer an overall verdict: is Zhuangzi best read as advancing a form of dream skepticism, an ontological theory of transformation, or a position that deliberately undermines the framework in which that very distinction makes sense? Be precise about what is at stake philosophically in each option, and note any tensions in your own adjudication.
