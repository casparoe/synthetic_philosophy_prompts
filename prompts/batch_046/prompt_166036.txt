I am preparing a feature on the philosophy of social science for an audience that follows conceptual debates but is tired of the same binary framing, and I need a pedagogical exercise that makes the tension between methodological individualism and holism felt in real time rather than summarized in prose. For a ninety-minute graduate workshop with sixteen participants—none required to have taken a prior stand—please design a structured simulation in which small groups must jointly explain the rapid emergence, collapse, and reconstitution of an artificial “institution” that exists only inside the game’s rules. The mechanism should include a hidden feedback loop: each participant’s payoff in round N is partly determined by the collective interpretation assigned to round N-1, yet every player sees only their own local action history and never the aggregate interpretation, so that identical individual choices produce opposite outcomes once an invisible threshold of collective assignment is crossed. Build in assigned roles—“micro-observers” who may cite only individual intentions and strategic reasoning, “macro-observers” who may invoke collective properties and institutional frames, and rotating “mediators” who must translate explanations across the boundary—along with precise decision points, physical or digital mechanics for delivering hidden feedback, and a timeline that forces the group to render an explanation before the loop is revealed.

The rules and incentives should be arranged so that participants first converge on individual-level accounts—defection, signaling, rational expectation—only to watch those accounts fail systematically once the institution acquires what feels like normative force from the outside, reshaping payoffs for everyone regardless of personal strategy. Include a debrief that maps specific moments in the simulation onto the explanatory gap between aggregation and emergence, predicts the two failures that will occur—participants claiming they “would have predicted it” with full information, and conflating observed correlation with autonomous collective causation—and tells me exactly how to manage each without resorting to a lecture. Be frank about which feature of a robust holist position—specifically, the claim that social structures possess irreducible causal powers that cannot be reconstructed from any compositional account of individuals—the simulation cannot reproduce, and specify how I, as the facilitator, must supply that missing element in the closing discussion. Keep the design runnable without digital tools if necessary, avoid any dependence on named theorists or canonical texts, and do not ask participants to produce bibliographies or cite literature; the point is to generate the conceptual friction from the mechanics of play itself.
