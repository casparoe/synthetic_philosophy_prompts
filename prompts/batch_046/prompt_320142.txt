everyone in philosophy of mind compares intuition pumps to optical illusions -- like the Muller-Lyer lines or that rotating snakes illusion -- the idea being that just as an illusion exposes a quirk in vision by breaking it cleanly, a Gettier case or the trolley problem exposes a quirk in our concept of knowledge or morality by breaking our usual judgments. people lean on this to argue that thought experiments are legit discovery tools, not just rhetorical tricks. but i dont think the mapping is obvious. can you lay out the correspondence table -- whats the retina, whats the illusionist, whats the "real world" being misrepresented, whats the error-correction mechanism -- then show where it breaks? do we have independant access to the "correct" concept like we do with physical line length? does the analogy smuggle in that philosophical intuitions are always mistakes rather than data? and tell me whether the conclusion -- that intuition pumps are basically scientific instruments -- survives once the disanologies are out, or if the analogy was carrying it the whole time.
