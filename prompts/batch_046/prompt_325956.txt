Write a dialogue between Mara and Jules, both sharp philosophers of ethics, sitting in a bar after a conference panel on how norms like slavery and dueling actually disappear. Mara takes the Williams-style line: she thinks moral arguments are mostly post-hoc rationalization, and that dueling collapsed because the state monopolized violence and slaveholding lost its economic base, not because anyone was genuinely persuaded out of it. Jules is closer to Appiah: he thinks honor codes contain internal resources for revision, and activists changed what "honor" meant so that killing over a slight started to look cowardly rather than brave, which is reasoning doing real work inside the code.

Have them actually respond to each other's points, not talk past one another. Mara should press Jules on cases where economic interest stayed constant but the norm still flipped, and ask how he distinguishes real conceptual change from people just finding new words for the same coercion. Jules should push back that Williams can't account for "normative mutation" - practices like dueling turning into regulated sport fencing, or chattel slavery turning into debt peonage - and ask whether Mara thinks that's the violence ending or just recieving a legal costume change. Have Jules ask Mara if she can point to even one case where reasoning changed a norm without riding on state power or economic shift. Make them equally competent; neither should be a strawman. Keep it casual, you can slip in a typo like "recieve" or miss a comma, and don't have them cite papers or ask for a reading list.
