I sit on a county zoning board and every week we get equity proposals -- developers asking for variences if they include affordable units, or rezoning a wealthy single-family district for dense public housing. I need a reusable decision procedue my staff can actually run in about 30 minutes with a spreadsheet before I sign off, not just philosophical vibes. Ground it in Rawls's veil of ignorance and the difference principle, but turn it into steps: the data to gather, the question at each stage, where the answer is automatically no, and where we stop because it's a values clash that needs a board vote, not a calculation. Handle the hard case where property rights or free-association claims conflict with redistribution -- specify exactly which consideration wins and when. Also deal with the indexing problem: since the least advantaged aren't uniform, say whether we rank them by income, primary goods, or capabilities, and flag where the procedue breaks if we switch metrics. Apply it to two proposals: (a) tax abatements for a market-rate tower with 15% affordable units, and (b) upzoning a wealthy neighborhood for high-density affordable housing. One result should go against what I personally want, but the procedure should still deliver it. Be honest about which steps are real checkboxes and which are judgment calls in a checklist costume. And say flat out what this can't do -- like when census data is too old or missing to rank the worst-off. No citations, no reading lists, just the tool.
