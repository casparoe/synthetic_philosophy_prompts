I serve on a county apprenticeship advisory board that must recommend whether to accept, reject, or restructure a private endowment proposal tied to a pending estate-tax measure. The proposal comes from a family whose wealth spans three generations of agricultural patents and land consolidation; they offer $12 million to create a technical-training fund for county youth, but only under a perpetual-trust agreement that reserves 35 percent of placements for direct descendants of the county's original settler families and allows the remaining 65 percent to be filled by open lottery. They claim that without this protected share, the proposed estate tax on holdings above $18 million would force asset liquidation that destroys the very capital generating the gift, and that a private trust delivers training faster than any public program the tax revenue could build in the first ten years. I see a real philosophical conflict here: the whole point of limiting dynastic wealth, as I understand it, is to keep birth from determining who gets ahead, yet rejecting this gift means relying on a tax that may never produce an equivalent opportunity pool, and the open 65 percent is genuinely larger than the projected public yield. I need a formal decision memo recommending one of the three paths—accept with the clause, reject and back the tax, or propose a modified trust structure—with clear reasons the family and the council could actually respond to. Please begin with your bottom-line recommendation in the very first sentence, then lay out the reasoning, including the strongest consideration in favor of the option you do not choose. No preamble, no restatement of this request, and no requests for citations or further reading; the argument should stand entirely on its own.
