Write a satirical job advertisement of approximately 450 to 500 words, composed in the deadpan bureaucratic register of a UK university humanities faculty hiring committee, announcing a permanent chair in “Anti-Event Structural History.” The advertisement should parody the extreme structuralist reading of Fernand Braudel’s longue durée—specifically the view that geographical, climatic, and economic structures operating across centuries or millennia render political events, individual biography, and archival testimony essentially epiphenomenal. Invent a fictional institutional home (for example, an interdisciplinary centre that rejects all research spanning fewer than three centuries) and include one invented methodological protocol—such as a “Centennial Significance Threshold” that disqualifies sources produced within a 150-year window—so that the satire depends on extending real historiographical logic rather than inventing an unrelated absurdity. The voice must remain entirely straight-faced: no winking, no explicit signalling that the post is ridiculous, and no named living individuals as targets. Instead, exaggerate recognisable features of the position: the dismissal of the French Revolution as “a brief atmospheric disturbance”; the insistence that climate-proxy data (tree rings, sediment cores) count as the only admissible primary sources; the requirement that successful candidates demonstrate fluency in at least two dead languages used exclusively for recording grain prices; and the framing of biography as “methodological contamination.” By the end, a reader familiar with Braudel should recognise that the advertisement is a reductio ad absurdum of structural determinism, while a general reader should understand precisely which features of historical practice are being mocked—particularly the tension between slow-moving structures and the lived experience of historical actors. Use British spelling and formal academic diction throughout.
