Write a joke that turns on an internal tension in cause-prioritization frameworks scoring interventions by importance, tractability, and neglectedness. Focus specifically on the saturation problem: high-importance, low-neglectedness causes can dominate the calculus even when tractability approaches saturation, while a hypothetical fourth criterion — call it epistemic fragility, meaning the risk that an impact estimate collapses under modest model uncertainty — usually remains implicit. The punchline should hinge on the recognition that formalizing this fourth factor does not resolve the trade-off but merely relocates the ambiguity: one cause may be robustly high-impact yet fragile in its reasoning, while another may be epistemically fragile yet so neglected that even its collapsed lower-bound exceeds the saturated alternative's expected value. The joke should be genuinely amusing to people who actually apply these frameworks, not a generic observation about charity being complicated.
