I just spent the better part of an evening arguing with a friend about Buridan’s ass—specifically, whether the scenario reveals a genuine paradox about rational agency or is merely a contrived logical toy. She takes the standard decision-theoretic line: if the two bales are genuinely identical in every relevant respect, then selecting one over the other requires either a hidden asymmetry or a randomizing mechanism, and calling that “choice without preference” confuses causal determination with rational justification. I kept pushing back with what felt like a phenomenological point—that in ordinary life people break ties arbitrarily without feeling irrational, and that treating every tie-breaker as a concealed preference seems like an ad hoc rescue of the principle that rational action must be preference-driven. By the end we were clearly talking past each other: she was treating the ass as a model for optimal choice under perfect information, while I was treating it as a limit case for the metaphysics of the will. The literature seems to have circled the same moves since the medieval commentators—some saying the will can suspend judgment, others saying nature abhors a vacuum and the ass dies, others reducing the problem to an epistemic failure about hidden differences—without anyone conceding ground. Is this impasse substantive, or are we just using “choice” for different phenomena—one for rational selection among ranked options, one for the bare exercise of agency? Or is there a hidden empirical claim about human decision-making buried beneath the logical examples? I want a diagnosis that engages these specific positions—preference-reductionism, spontaneous-voluntarism, and epistemic-fallibilism about equality—rather than a survey of interpretations. Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a conceptual distinction between selection and volition, a formal result showing tie-breaking is always reducible to prior weights, or an admission that the scenario is impossible and therefore philosophically empty?
