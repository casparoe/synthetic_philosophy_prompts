So I've been stuck on this question about Adam Smith's sympathy and the imparial spectator, and it feels like every paper I read just repeats the same moves: either the spectator is a real internalized other (like Mead but earlier) or it's just a metaphor for rational reflection. The debate hasn't moved in years, at least in the circles I'm in. But here's a weird angle I'm trying to work out: what if the stall is happening because people are treating sympathy and the spectator as answers to the same problem — like, sympathy gives us the data and the spectator processes it? I think Smith might actually be running two different projeccts that got collapsed into one: sympathy as a kind of affective callibration between bodies, and the spectator as a normative standard that doesn't depend on feeling anything at all. So is the disagreement substantive (they're describing different mechansims), verbal (we're using 'sympathy' for two things), or are they just asking different questions — one about moral psychology, one about moral justifcation — under the same heading? And if it's that kind of split, what would actually count as progress? A historical discovery about Smith's notes? A conceptual distinction we don't have yet? Or is the whole framing broken?
