Consider the Reverend Thomas Bayes, revived from 1761 into the present. He is presented with the Judy Benjamin problem: an agent receives successive messages about her location and must revise both her belief in a conditional extraction protocol and her belief about which antecedent holds. Bayes is then shown a contemporary military-robotics analogue—an autonomous drone behind contested lines receives a noisy first signal establishing that, if it is in Sector Red, aerial extraction will follow, and if in Sector Blue, ground extraction will follow; a second, partially corrupted signal updates its belief about its sector, but does not clearly resolve whether the extraction protocol itself remains valid. Bayes is asked to design the drone's prior and updating rule. Would he require strict conditionalization over a fixed space of worlds, allowing only the location-credence to shift? Or might he permit the conditional belief about extraction to be revised in tandem, and if so, how does that relate to his own inverse-probability framework? In particular, discuss whether Bayes's rule can accommodate the asymmetry—central to the Judy Benjamin case—that learning the antecedent can seem either to preserve or to destabilize the conditional, depending on how the prior partitions the space of protocols. Write as if Bayes himself were advising the engineering team, maintaining internal consistency with his known commitments on prior probabilities and conditional reasoning.
