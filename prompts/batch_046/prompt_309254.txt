I've been thinking about split-brain patients and whether they're one person or two, and my intuitions are all over the place. In the classic callosotomy setup where the left hand grabs a cup and the right grabs a pen and the patient can't explain why, I still feel like it's one guy with a communication breakdown, not two people. But if both hemispheres can type answers on seperate keyboards and they consistently disagree about whether a risky medical treatment is worth it, I suddenly feel like there are two subjects in there. Then there's the partial split where only the anterior part is cut, so emotional reactions cross over but object recognition doesn't - that feels like one consciousness with a weird blind spot, not a split mind. And in a gradual case where surgeons cut one fiber tract per month and the patient eventually says there's a stranger in my head making choices, I think by then it's genuinely two, but I can't tell you at which month it flipped. So is there a principle behind these - maybe about narrative integration or access consciousness - that saves all my calls? Or do I have to drop the idea that the typing-disagreement case is genuinely two? Also what should I say about a case where the left brain verbally reports happiness but the right brain's facial expression shows distress, with no cross-hemisphere correction?
