I'm trying to work out if there's actually a consistent rule behind when I think it's okay to care less about stuff just cos it's further away in time. Going by my gut: if I can have £40 now or £60 in six months I'd probably take the £40 now and not feel bad — but if it's £40 now versus £600 in six months then grabbing the £40 feels like I'm being stupid, not just impatient. Same with my own life: I'd rather blow some savings on a summer holiday now than lock it all away for uni in three years, but I wouldn't take a pill that makes me euphoric today if it means I'm miserable at forty. So far that sounds like normal hyperbolic discounting or whatever?

But then climate change messes it up. I think we should definately spend loads now to cut emissions even if the worst suffering is in like 80 years, because those people are still real. But if we're talking about protecting people from some disaster in 10,000 years, I don't feel the same urgency — in fact I think it's okay not to bankrupt ourselves for them. That feels like hypocrisy, but I can't shake it. Is there a principled line between "distant but within human civilisation" and "so far away it's basically another species"? Or am I just being inconsistent?

There's also this case I keep going back and forth on: imagine a medical treatment where you take a pill every day for ten years to stay healthy, versus one injection now that cures you instantly but slightly lowers your intelligence in thirty years. I'd pick the ten-year one, but I'm not sure if that's because I don't discount my own far-future self, or because I think "me in thirty years" is still me in a stronger way than strangers in eighty years are them. What principle — maybe pure time preference is irrational except when identity breaks down, or maybe there's a cutoff around generational continuity — gets all these verdicts? Or do I have to drop one?

Also, what would your principle say about a government building nuclear waste storage that costs billions now but only becomes dangerous in 500 years? Is that over-valuing the distant future, or just prudent?

Please tell me what principle (or small set of candidates) captures the pattern, state it precisely, say what it predicts for the nuclear waste case, and be honest about which of my gut feelings it can't save — especially the 80-years vs 10,000-years climate pair.
