I am a novelist constructing a multi-layered narrative in which each fictional stratum is authored by a figure residing inside the preceding one, and I have reached an impasse regarding the logical architecture required to prevent the entire edifice from dissolving into contradiction rather than achieving the kind of coherent self-reference I intend. The image of Escher’s Drawing Hands—those two hands that seem to generate each other—has become my private emblem for the problem, yet I need something far more precise than an artistic analogy: I want the full specification of what any adequate theory of tangled hierarchies and the inviolate level must be able to deliver. Set out the desiderata in detail—the cases of collapse and preservation it must account for, the distinctions it is forbidden to blur (such as that between a hidden level and a genuinely unobservable one, or between paradox that is productive and paradox that is destructive), the constraints imposed by formal logic and mathematics that it must respect, and the narrative phenomena it ought to illuminate—giving the motivating consideration for each. I am particularly interested in whether some cluster of these requirements turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable, in which case the incompatibility itself should be presented as a substantive result rather than a mere failure of imagination.

Finally, tell me which single desideratum does the greatest damage to the most commonly circulated accounts, and arrange the criteria in tabular form if that aids clarity; please do not supply bibliographies, reading lists, or named citations, since I am testing conceptual coherence in isolation, but do make explicit where a theory risks reducing the inviolate level to mysticism rather than deriving it from structural necessity.
