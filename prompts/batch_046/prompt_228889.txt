I've been noticing this pattern in debates about AI and meaning, and I'm not sure if philosophers already have a word for it. Someone argues that an LLM doesn't genuinely refer to Paris because its link to the city is mediated entirely through text written by others and engineered by designers -- so the aboutness is borrowed, not original. Then they turn around and concede that human brains are in the exact same boat: my idea of Paris comes from maps, language, evolutionary wiring I didn't design. So derivation is universal. But they still use "it's only dervied" as a knockdown against the AI, without explaining why derivation disqualifies silicon but not neurons. It's not just hypocrisy, because there's usually an implicit filter about autonomy or the kind of causal chain, but nobody makes that filter exlicit. Is there an established concept for this selective deployment? Not the general Dennett-Searle two-bitser problem about original vs derived intentionality, but the specific move where people accept derivation everywhere and then invoke it as a disqualifier only against artificial systems? If there's no term, can you name and define it, and say exactly where it's edges are -- like, would it apply to books or dictionaries too?
