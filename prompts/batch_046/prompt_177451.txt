As a novelist drafting a philosophical narrative about the architecture of subjective experience, I am helping to design the final examination for an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar. I need a single essay question that advanced students could address in ninety minutes, one that cannot be answered by reciting standard summaries of representationalism or illusionism. The question should center on consciousness understood as a user illusion—specifically, on whether treating phenomenal experience as an interface engineered for behavioral coordination rather than for metaphysical disclosure forces us to reconceive first-person authority. To prevent reliance on obscure bibliographic knowledge and to inject a precise conceptual framework, please build the question around a distinction I have introduced in the course: call properties belonging to the 'render layer' those qualitative features that appear in conscious experience, and properties belonging to the 'source layer' whatever physical or functional states generate them. Ask students to assess whether the intuition of transparency—that we seem to experience reality directly rather than through an intermediary—can be preserved if transparency itself is treated as an engineered feature of the render layer rather than evidence of unmediated access to the source layer. Then provide a model answer of roughly seven hundred words and a marking scheme totaling twenty-five marks divided across five specific evaluative criteria; for each criterion state its point value and a concise one-line note on how candidates typically fail it. The response must clearly distinguish settled matters—such as the vehicle-content distinction in theories of representation—from issues that remain genuinely contested among specialists—such as whether this framework entails a form of eliminativism about phenomenal properties or merely relocates them within a functional architecture. Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and avoid invoking highly niche theories or figures that might lie outside standard training; the prompt should be fully answerable without web access. Finally, separate in the answer what is established consensus from what is actively disputed, and do not present any contested interpretation as if it were the settled view.
