For my philosophy of language seminar, we have been working through the tripartite distinction drawn in Douglas Hofstadter’s Gödel, Escher, Bach between the interpretive frame that governs decoding, the outer message that a recipient actually derives, and the inner message carried by the raw symbol structure independent of that frame. Before I evaluate existing accounts, I want the full specification of what an adequate theory of the location of semantic content must deliver. Write the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain—including self-referential loops in which a frame contains instructions for its own dissolution, and cases where identical inner messages yield systematically divergent outer messages across frames without any ambiguity in the symbols themselves—the distinctions it must preserve (frame versus message, outer versus inner, and the boundary conditions where one collapses into another), and the consistency conditions with established results in formal semantics and cognitive science (for instance, that a theory cannot treat frames as fully transparent to inner messages if it also wishes to preserve the possibility of systematic misinterpretation or frame leakage, where properties of the framing system bleed into the interpreted content in ways that resist parsing as either pure frame or pure message). For each desideratum, state whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference (intrinsicist, contextualist, or hybrid), and indicate which pair or subset you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—especially whether requiring complete frame-independence for inner messages can be squared with the claim that frames are themselves interpreted as messages in recursive framing events. Then, briefly, score how pure intrinsicism, radical contextualism, and a two-level hybrid view fare against the list, identifying which single requirement eliminates the largest number of candidates. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring.
