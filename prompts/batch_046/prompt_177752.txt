In a podcast about prehistory, the host used this line to dismiss any suggestion that farming improved human life, leaving me unsure what precisely was being asserted. I want the different precise theses this slogan could express, arranged from the trivial to the radical, so I can see which ones actually support that dismissive move.

As I have turned the idea over on my own, several specific distinctions have come into focus, and I do not know which of them the slogan is meant to cover. So is the mistake the loss of leisure equality, or is it the irreversible invention of social rank once surplus exists?

Please lay out the readings as a menu — for instance, a purely nutritional thesis, a welfare-aggregation thesis, an equality thesis, and an irreversibility thesis — and for each say whether it is trivial, defensible, false, or genuinely radical. Then tell me which reading the podcast host was most likely relying on, and which ones, if true, would actually justify treating farming as a mistake serious enough to dismiss modern life over.
