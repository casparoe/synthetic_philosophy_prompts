Analyze the September 2008 decision to let Lehman Brothers fail, after the March 2008 rescue of Bear Stearns, through John Maynard Keynes's framework of risk, uncertainty, and the weight of argument. Treat the episode not as a miscalculation of probabilities but as a case of radical uncertainty: identify which elements of the decision—counterparty exposure, bankruptcy law, political backlash, systemic cascade—were quantifiable risks and which were true uncertainties in Keynes's sense, where probabilities are not merely unknown but unassignable. Then examine how the weight of evidence and conventional judgment shifted between the two episodes, and whether the reversal supports Keynes's claim that in deep uncertainty rational agents rely on convention, authority, and animal spirits rather than expected-value reasoning. Finally, ask whether the episode confirms or complicates Keynes's further claim that state intervention can stabilize private expectations when private calculation collapses, and say exactly where the theory is silent about the politics of choosing one convention over another.
