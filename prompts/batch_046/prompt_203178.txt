I am drafting a long-form feature on whether contemporary philosophy of meaning has genuinely moved beyond Camus, or whether absurdism and naturalistic accounts of value remain in an unresolved stalemate. Before I commit to the framework below, I want a consistency audit.

Here is where I have landed:

(1) I accept Camus’s framing of the absurd as the irreconcilable tension between human reason’s demand for rational unity and the world’s silent indifference, but I reject both his doctrine of revolt and any transcendence of the absurd through hope or faith; I treat it instead as a standing, non-negotiable constraint on human existence.

(2) I adopt a modified Wolf-style hybrid account: a life is meaningful when it combines subjective fulfilment with objective worth, where I understand objective worth in terms of intergenerational durability — a project’s capacity to maintain normative coherence across successive generations of practitioners without collapsing into mere custom.

(3) I hold that if objective worth is fully reducible to these naturalistic durability conditions, then Nagel’s external perspective should lose its metaphysical bite: the feeling of cosmic pointlessness becomes a category error, applying an inappropriate evaluative scale to locally durable practices.

(4) Nevertheless, I insist that the absurd operates independently as a modal fragility — it reveals that any durability condition is itself contingent and could collapse, and this contingency is not susceptible to further naturalistic grounding.

(5) I also endorse Nagel’s claim that the capacity for detached, external reflection is structurally ineliminable and essential to generating the absurd experience, but I deny that exercising this capacity undermines the first-person authority required for Wolf’s subjective-fulfilment condition.

(6) I maintain that the most profound instances of meaning often occur in precisely those private, solitary practices — unrecorded artistic labour, intimate care that leaves no institutional trace — that fail my intergenerational-durability criterion, and that these cases represent central rather than marginal examples of human flourishing.

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear so, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Rank the tensions by severity, specify the cheapest fix for each, and identify which single revision would resolve the most conflicts.
