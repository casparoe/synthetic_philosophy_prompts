I'm taking a course on the ethics of belief — James vs Clifford and all that — and I've noticed a move people make in arguement that I can't find a label for. It keeps showing up when someone defends holding a view despite weak evidence, but they don't quite defend it as a full-on belief. Like, my roommate says she "believes" that people are fundamentally decent, not because she has proof but because she needs to live that way. But when I ask if she'd bet on it, or if she thinks it's literally false that people are decent, she says "no, I'm not claiming it's factually true, it's more like a stance I take." Then, two minutes later, she's furious at someone who treats people as selfish, as if they've denied an obvious truth. So she gets the emotional and practical privileges of belief — it guides her life, she defends it in arguement — but dodges the epistemic obligations, like revising it if they recieve counterevidence or admitting it's false.

I see the same pattern with free will. A guy in my study group says he "chooses to believe" in free will for practical reasons, but when neuroscience comes up he retreats to "I don't literally believe it, I just operate within that framework." Yet he blames people for bad choices, which only makes sense if it's true. It's not quite James's will to believe, because James seemed to think you actually form the belief — you're genuinely convinced of the live option. And it's not Clifford's involuntary belief either, since they're clearly doing something active. Is there an established concept for this specific shuttle between treating something as belief for practical/emotional purposes and denying it's belief for epistemic accountability? If there is, how exactly does it differ from pragmatic encroachment or from what people call "faith"? If not, take a definite stand: either say it's just a misapplication of James, or coin a precise term with clear boundary conditions that separate it from self-deception, half-belief, and plain equivocation. Don't just lay out the menu — pick one and defend it.
