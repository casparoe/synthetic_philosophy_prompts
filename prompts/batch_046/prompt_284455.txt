I run a phil seminar for about 10 grad students, 75 minutes. I want them to actually feel the dogmatism paradox — you learn one belief in your set is false but not which, so keeping each individual belief seems rational, yet you still hold a set you know is contaminated. Design a structured game: they write five 'obvious' claims, then draw a card saying exactly one is false. They vote on which to drop, but the rules reward holding a belief unless there's specific defeat, so they keep all five. Then they must jointly endorse a statement summarizing the set, knowing it's false. Debrief should map each move onto the paradox, distinguish suspension from rejection, show where they could resist (local conservatism vs global consistency), and be frank about what the game can't reproduce — mainly teh normative dynamics of actual belief revision. No lecture up front, don't name it until the end, keep it runnable with paper only.
