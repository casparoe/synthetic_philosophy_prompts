I'm working through a conceptual problem in metaphysics and philosophy of science for a graduate seminar I'm auditing, and I'd like you to construct a sustained, first-principles argument rather than survey existing literature. Consider what I will call the Shielded-Base Case: two competing ontological frameworks, call them Tropic Hierarchy and Continuous Field Ontology, generate precisely identical predictions for every observable phenomenon within our causal past and future light cone. The sole divergence concerns entities residing inside a region that is permanently causally isolated from ours—say, beyond an event horizon with which our world-lines will never intersect. Tropic Hierarchy holds that these entities instantiate discrete, countable, part-structured tropes arranged in a branching tree; Continuous Field Ontology holds that they instantiate continuous, non-countable field-properties lacking any fundamental parts. By construction, no physical signal—gravitational, electromagnetic, or otherwise—can ever traverse the boundary, so there exists no actual or counterfactual experimental procedure that could adjudicate between them.

The standard philosophical response is to invoke some form of ontological deflationism or local structural realism: since the distinction makes no observable difference, it should be treated as a pseudo-question, and we ought to identify ontology only with what is causally accessible, selecting the remaining framework on pragmatic or explanatory grounds alone. I want you to argue against this deflationary move. Specifically, defend the claim that even in the Shielded-Base Case, there remains a genuine, non-epistemic ontological fact of the matter about whether the shielded entities have discrete or continuous intrinsic structure, and that this fact is not rendered meaningless, indeterminate, or merely conventional by our permanent epistemic isolation from it. You should address the objection that without causal access there can be no truth-maker for the distinction, and you should explain why treating the shielded divergence as pragmatically selectable undermines the normative structure of scientific ontology more broadly—particularly in local cases of underdetermination where we do have access but the evidential situation is structurally analogous. Do not offer bibliographies, reading lists, or attributions to specific texts; build the argument using only widely available conceptual resources, and treat the Shielded-Base Case itself as a genuine metaphysical scenario rather than a mere heuristic.
