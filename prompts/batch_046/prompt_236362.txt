I've been working through Fitch's paradox of knowability for a small reading group that likes to get into the weeds of modal epistemic logic without just recycling the standard lecture-notes version. The derivation itself is settled enough—if every truth is knowable, and knowing p lets you know you know p, then with closure over conjunction you quickly derive that every truth is actually known, which feels wrong—but I'm less satisfied with how resolutions are usually framed. Too often they either dismiss closure as an obvious casualty or retreat to an idealized, atemporal knower that has nothing to do with actual bounded cognition.

If we restrict the principle to truths that are not just logically accessible but computable within realistic resource bounds—say, within PSPACE for a finite-memory agent—then the collapse from possible knowledge to actual omniscience might be blocked without giving up the intuition that ignorance is never fundamental. I don't present this as standard; it's a deliberate injection to see whether resolutions survive contact with a resource-bounded epistemology.
