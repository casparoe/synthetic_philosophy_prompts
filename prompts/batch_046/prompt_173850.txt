On her account, religious belief is not a cluster of propositions about supernatural entities but a form of life structured by cyclical ritual repetition, and its internal coherence depends on the temporal architecture of practice rather than on evidential justification. She argues this avoids both fideism and naturalistic reductionism, but I want the conversation to stay in the weeds of the view itself rather than drifting into biography or survey-level exposition.

Please write a progressive sequence of interview questions that start from her interpretation of Wittgenstein and move to the pressure points where the literature genuinely pushes back. Next, ask about religious disagreement: if two communities share no ritual calendar and therefore no common temporal grammar, does her view render inter-religious criticism conceptually impossible, or merely practically difficult? I want the questions precise, content-driven, free of generic prompts like "what drew you to this topic," and with no request for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
