I've been trying to work through the Judy Benjamin problem—she's dropped in a region split Red/Blue and Second Company/HQ with priors 70/20/10, then gets a radio message saying basically "if you're Blue you're in HQ and if you're Red you're in Second Company." Most explanations say Bayes breaks here because her belief in being Red changes after she hears the message even though she didn't learn anything direct about Red territory. I think the real confusion is that people are treating the message like a logical constraint on her belief partion rather than as evidence, so they expect Stalnaker's thesis—that P(Red | Red→Second) should just equal P(Red)—to hold automatically. But if you instead treat it like a Jeffrey update that zeros out the off-diagonal quadrants, her Red credence stays put at 0.7 and Bayes is saved. So is the paradox just a mix-up between constraint-based revision and probabilistic conditioning, or does Jeffrey's rule still miss something important about how we actually process condtionals?
