I'm serving on the advisory board for the National Heritage Risk Fund, and we've got this pilot proposal on our desk from a macrohistory group. They want to apply Turchin-style secular-cyle forecasting—demographic-structral feedback loops, elite overproduction indices, climate-stress coefficients—to predict which regions are heading into destabilising phases over the next 20 years, so we can prioritise digitising archives before things get messy. Half the board think this is pseudohistory with fancy maths; the other half say ignoring quantifiable macro-patterns is negligent. I need a short written opinion circulated before Tuesday's vote: recommend approval, approval with conditions, or rejection, with a concrete time frame and an actionable bottom line. The memo should engage the strongest philosophical objection seriously—specifically that treating societies as cyclical systems collapses individual agency and smuggles in methodological holism that our charter doesn't permit—rather than just saying history isn't predictable. Keep it formal enough for the board but don't ask me for a bibliograhy; just reason it through. There's also this weird hybrid index they use that combines elite saturation with agricultural yield stress, which isn't standard Turchin but an extension—make sure the recommendation addresses whether that kind of model-specific innovation makes the pilot more or less trustworthy.
