For a workshop on social ontology I am drafting my philosophical profile on cohort kinds so that colleagues can stress-test it. I am a sociology PhD student working on demographic cohort analysis and the philosophy of social kinds. Here are my commitments: (1) Generational labels—Boomer, Millennial, Gen Z—are not natural kinds, but they are instrumentally projectible in limited explanatory domains, analogous to race or gender categories on a constructivist account. (2) The ontological ground of a genuine cohort is shared exposure to a macro-social shock during a specific developmental window (approximately ages 12–18), not mere chronological proximity. (3) The conventional boundary years (e.g., 1981, 1996, 2012) are administratively arbitrary, yet they are causally anchored in inflection points of technology-adoption diffusion curves—specifically, the threshold at which regular adolescent access to a transformative medium exceeds fifty percent in a given region. (4) Our intuitive belief in generations is partly debunkable: it is amplified by an availability heuristic driven by retrospective media framing and marketing narratives. (5) Despite (4), cohort effects are causally distinguishable from age and period effects through APC (Age-Period-Cohort) identification strategies, so there is a real statistical signal that survives the debunking. (6) I also hold a more specific, non-standard view: within Gen Z there is an ontologically significant fracture. Those who experienced the 2020 pandemic during secondary school form a distinct sub-cohort—different risk-preference and institutional-trust profiles—from those who experienced it during university, which means Gen Z is not a single kind but two dynamic clusters in a high-dimensional social space.
