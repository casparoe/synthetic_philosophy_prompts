Draft the joint statement that a procedural-minimalist camp and a pluralist-substantive camp could both sign after an adversarial collaboration on democratic backsliding in Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the US. Introduce within the collaboration the analytic pair of a reversibility threshold—the point at which institutional capture becomes structurally irreversible without constitutional rupture—and an institutional viscosity index—the resistance of captured agencies to correction through ordinary electoral turnover. Present the deliverable as exactly five numbered items, each one to two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. Item one should state the shared commitments both sides accept, including agreement that elections alone are insufficient indicators and that Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the US must be assessed with the same conceptual tools. Item three should identify the cruxes, specifically whether reversibility and viscosity are empirically measurable properties or normatively loaded judgments, and whether a high-viscosity but formally intact system should be classified differently across the four cases. Item four should specify the comparative case or institutional argument both sides agree would bear on those cruxes—such as post-electoral reform trajectories in Poland versus Turkey—with each camp’s prediction about viscosity scores and reversibility outcomes recorded in advance. Item five should distinguish which disagreements are genuinely substantive and which dissolve once the reversibility-threshold and viscosity-index framework is adopted, noting any residual verbal dispute about the term “democracy” itself.
