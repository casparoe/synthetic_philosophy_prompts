I sit on my uni's philosophy curriculum board and we're stuck on a proposal for a new module, 'Natural Mastery and Dependency: Aristotle to the Platform Economy'. The course takes Aristotle's Politics Book I argument about natural slavery — that some people lack the deliberative part of the soul and so benefit from being ruled — and applies it not to defend old chattel slavery, but to analyse modern algorithmic management and AI-assisted care work. The claim is that these relations might mirror the structure of natural mastery, where one side lacks full practical reasoning and the other directs for mutual benefit, without the historical violence of chattel systems. Half the board thinks this is a sharp, non-anachronistic philosophical tool; the other half says it's reckless to rehabilitate the concept even analytically, especially since students might conflate metaphysical capacity with social construction and recieve harmful messages about dependency. We need a memo for Friday's meeting recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with a concrete course of action and time frame we could actually enforce, not vague hand-waving. It should take the strongest worry from the losing side seriously rather than dismissing it, and include reasoning the course designers could actually respond to. Please draft it in British English.
