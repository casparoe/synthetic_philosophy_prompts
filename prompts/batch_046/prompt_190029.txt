Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises when Bradley's regress—the argument that if relations are genuinely real, then relating those relations to their relata requires still further relations, producing an infinite regress—is applied to the unity of propositional thought rather than to metaphysical relations. Specifically, construct a case involving mental representation in which treating the representational link between a subject-concept and a predicate-concept as an explicit, separately representable element either initiates an infinite hierarchy of linking representations or collapses the distinction between representing an external state of affairs and representing one's own representational apparatus. Your example should illustrate why neither decomposing the proposition into its constituent representations nor invoking an unanalyzable mental act of synthesis fully dissolves the tension.
