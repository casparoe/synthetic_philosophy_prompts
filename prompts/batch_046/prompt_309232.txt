You are an experienced philosophy instructor and workshop designer specializing in experiential metaphysics. Before proposing anything, take a moment to think through how hidden-state dynamics and forced narrative absorption can make a conceptual problem felt rather than explained.

Design a 75-minute in-class activity for 12 upper-level philosophy undergraduates that makes them experience the specific force of Zhuangzi's butterfly-dream skepticism — particularly the collapse of the boundary between dreamer and dreamed (the transformation or hua) where the very act of doubting one's waking state operates as a move within the dream-logic rather than an escape from it.

The setup: Use only paper, pens, and sealed envelopes. Divide the class into four triads. Each student receives a hidden role card — Waking Observer, Dream Figure, or Uncertain Transformer — and a brief private instruction defining how they must reason about identity continuity. The group task is to co-author a single first-person narrative that explains who they are and how they know they are not currently being dreamed. Midway through, at around minute 30, distribute contradictory memory logs that directly conflict with each member's private instruction. The rules then require the triad to absorb all contradictions into one continuous account; they may not discard any log, and they must assign each voice in the account to one of the three roles.

The payoff is not monetary: each triad receives a collective narrative-coherence score, judged first by themselves and then revealed against a hidden rubric that rewards the inclusion of contradiction rather than its resolution. The debrief should map the three phases — initial confident coordination, contradiction injection, and forced narrative absorption — onto the structure of the skepticism. Identify the precise moment in the activity that corresponds to the collapse of a privileged waking standpoint, and explain how the Transformer role embodies the hua. Be frank about which feature of Zhuangzi's idea — the genuine metaphysical possibility that there is no outside — the classroom simulation cannot reproduce, and state exactly what you as facilitator must supply verbally to bridge that gap.

Also specify one predictable failure mode (for example, students treating the contradiction as a logic puzzle to solve rather than an existential position to inhabit) and how you will redirect it without breaking immersion. State clearly what students should come away seeing about dream skepticism that they could not have learned from a standard lecture. Do not ask students to read passages, cite texts, or consult sources during the session. Write in American English.
