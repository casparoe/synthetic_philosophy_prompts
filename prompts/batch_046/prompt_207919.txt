I'm defending a thesis I'm calling asymmetrical relationalism at a philosophy of perception seminar next Tuesday. The claim is that disjunctivism survives the argument from illusion only if we reject not merely the common-factor view but also the content-parity assumption—the idea that illusory experiences carry the same kind of intentional content as veridical ones, just falsely. On my view, veridical perception is a direct acquaintance relation to environmental objects; illusion is not acquaintance with a non-existent object but a disrupted predictive process that lacks any intentional object altogether, so there is no shared mental state available to explain phenomenal sameness.
