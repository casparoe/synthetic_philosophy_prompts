I'm preparing a consistency audit for a philosophical seminar on sexual ethics, framed largely within a Russellian outlook but with some modifications I've adopted. Here are my commitments: (1) I take the moral evaluation of sexual and marital arrangements to depend primarily on their contribution to human flourishing and the reduction of unnecessary suffering, rather than on conformity to traditional religious or social norms; (2) I deny that monogamous pair-bonding carries any intrinsic moral privilege, holding instead that plural or temporary intimate arrangements are permissible—indeed, sometimes preferable—provided they are entered into with full transparency and mutual consent; (3) I also maintain that there is a distinct intrinsic good in long-term exclusive companionship, something like a shared narrative identity that unfolds over decades, which is not reducible to aggregate pleasure, to individual autonomy, or even to the welfare of any children involved, and which is partly constituted by the very exclusivity that (2) treats as non-privileged; (4) I endorse an affective contractarian model, according to which intimate relationships should be governed by explicit, renegotiable agreements about exclusivity, duration, and emotional disclosure, with the state's role restricted to enforcing these explicit terms rather than imposing default marital norms; (5) I believe that some minimal, non-vicious form of possessive concern—what we might call a residual attachment-guard—is psychologically necessary to secure the stable long-term investment that makes the intrinsic good in (3) achievable; and (6) I think the state should mandate comprehensive education that presents sexual jealousy as largely irrational and that treats plural arrangements as morally on a par with monogamy. Please audit this set: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, specify for each what must be revised and what the least costly fix would be, and then say which single revision would resolve the greatest number of inconsistencies. Be specific about whether the intrinsic exclusivity in (3) can survive within the contractarian framework of (4), whether (5)'s psychological claim undermines (6)'s educational program, and whether (1)'s flourishing-based framework can accommodate (3)'s non-reductive intrinsic value without collapsing into an ad hoc exception.
