Write a deadpan, in-universe "2024 Annual Impact Report and Stakeholder Charter" for a fictional international NGO called the Center for Asymmetrical Welfare (CAW), rendered in the polished, metric-obsessed bureaucratese of actual nonprofit annual reports—complete with an executive letter, "by the numbers" highlights, a brief case study, a stakeholder testimonial sidebar, and a "Looking Forward" strategic note. The satire should target the institutional logic of negative utilitarianism and suffering-focused ethics, not any real organization or person.

The piece must exaggerate real features of the view rather than inventing absurd ones: the procreation asymmetry (that there is a moral reason not to create suffering beings but no corresponding moral reason to create happy ones); the idea that preventing suffering carries vastly more weight than generating equivalent pleasure; the tendency of suffering-focused frameworks to treat potential people as statistical liabilities whose non-existence is a welfare gain; and the bureaucratic impulse to quantify moral outcomes in "suffering-adjusted" metrics.

To keep the prompt in the weeds without relying on obscure bibliographic knowledge, invent and embed a niche but plausible-sounding institutional vocabulary: CAW should report "Net Suffering Averted" (NSA) figures, celebrate "non-births facilitated" as a primary outcome metric, and reference an internal audit framework called the "Leiden Scale of Projected Harm" used in pre-existence counseling. Include a fictional metric—"Suffering-Adjusted Life-Years Prevented" (SALYP)—calculated against a baseline of "counterfactual lives brought into being." The executive letter should praise donors for funding "interventions that never happen," and the case study should profile a couple who, after completing CAW's "Asymmetry Counseling Protocol," decided not to reproduce, with the report framing this decision as a heroic altruistic contribution equivalent to saving thousands of lives.

Keep the irony sustained and deadpan: the author should never wink, and the reader should be able to tell that the report's cheerful celebration of non-creation and suffering-prevention is being ironized, precisely because the logic is followed with such bureaucratic consistency. The tone should remain that of a legitimate, well-funded NGO—professional, grateful, data-driven—so that the satire lands on the view's practical implications rather than on cartoonish villains. Aim for approximately 700 words. Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or references to specific texts; the prompt should be fully answerable from general knowledge of negative-utilitarian reasoning and standard nonprofit report conventions.
