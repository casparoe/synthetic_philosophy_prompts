Two people in my study group argued about split brain cases and consciousness unity. (1) Ana said Sperry-style experiments show two minds in one body: the right hemisphere picks out an object with the left hand that the verbal left hemisphere insists isnt there, so there must be a separate conscious subject experiencing it. (2) Ben said thats anthropomorphizing -- the right side is just running non-conscious recognition, like an automatic reflex, and since the patient reports feeling like one person thats the only real evidence we have. (3) Ana replied that feeling like one person is just the left hemisphere's narrative, because language is on that side; the right could have its own inner life we cant access, so absence of report isnt absence of experience. (4) Ben said then you'd have to grant consciousness to isolated brainstem nuclei too, since they process info without language, which makes the concept useless. (5) Ana said there's a principled line: the right hemisphere has full cortical architecture, just disconnected, so its representational complexity is on par with a normal conscious brain, unlike a nucleus. (6) Ben said complexity is a gradient and she hasnt said where on that gradient processing turns into experiencing, so her line is still arbitrary. Please go through the six moves one by one, rate each from clearly wrong to clearly right, briefly justify the rating, then say who is closer to right overall and what both are missing, especially about whether unity should be judged functionally or phenomenally.
