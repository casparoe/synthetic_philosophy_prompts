From this, some researchers conclude that an artificial general intelligence could never verify its own sentience from inside its own logical architecture, and that neuroscience is therefore permanently barred from constructing a complete, self-consistent formal theory of consciousness. Does this line of reasoning genuinely follow from Gödel's results, or does it depend upon a category error in applying them outside mathematics? I should welcome a careful conceptual clarification: what, precisely, do the incompleteness theorems establish for formal modelling in domains such as cognition or subjective experience, and where do popular inferences overreach?
