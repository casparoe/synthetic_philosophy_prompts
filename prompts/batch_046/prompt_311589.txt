Take the Józefów massacre by Reserve Police Battalion 101 in July 1942 — the first big shooting of women and children, the one Browning diggs into and Goldhagen treats as a counter-example. I want you to analyze it through the ordinary-men-versus-willing-executioners frame, but dont just rehearse the standard peer-pressure story or the eliminationist-antisemitism line. Instead, inject two somewhat niche concepts: moral self-licensing (the idea that a prior neutral or good act licenses a later bad one) and character fragmentation (the view that agents dont have one unified moral character but context-activated moral modules). Walk through the actual sequnce: Trapp's offer to step aside, the initial round, the alchohol, the repetition over the day. Which parts does self-licensing capture that Browning's conformity model misses? Where does fragmentation explain why some men refused every time while others seemed to flip? And be honest — does stitching these in actually settle the Browning-Goldhagen dispute, or does it just rename the ambiguity? No bibliography needed; work from common knowledge and the concepts themselves.
