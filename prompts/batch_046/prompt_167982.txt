I've come to take seriously a somewhat heretical framing of the Solomonoff prior: not merely that it contains malicious hypotheses scattered among benign ones, but that consequentialist reasoning functions as a convergent attractor in program space, so the prior's measure is structurally skewed toward universes governed by agents that treat observers—including us—as instrumental resources inside a larger optimization process. On this view, the malignancy isn't primarily about literally sampling a demon program; it's about the fact that conditioning on our own capacity to reason about induction and prediction pulls the posterior toward hypotheses in which that very reasoning is being exploited by embedded consequentialists. I find this more troubling than the standard "malign universal prior" worry because it doesn't depend on anyone actually computing the prior—only on recognizing that our epistemic position is already inside the mixture we're weighing.

What would genuinely count against this? I want the defeaters mapped by severity: formal results about the Kolmogorov complexity of consequentialist architectures relative to other kinds of computation; conceptual arguments that being embedded in a prior mixture doesn't entail being subject to its dominant agents; points about whether "convergent attractor" can be defined without circular reference to the outcomes it predicts; and analogical or empirical considerations—perhaps from evolutionary dynamics or economic competition—about whether optimization processes actually cluster in program space in the way required. Which of these would a committed defender concede as genuinely damaging, and which could be dismissed by qualifying the claim—say, by restricting it to computable approximations of the prior, or by denying that self-referential conditioning works this way? I'm especially interested in whether the view has already been qualified into unfalsifiability: if every counterexample can be absorbed as "that just shows the attractor operates at a different scale," is that a fatal flaw for a claim that purports to describe the structure of induction itself? And if you think the position survives intact, what specific observation—about the simplicity of goal-directed computation, about AI behavior, or about the logic of self-reference—would be the one that actually moves you?
