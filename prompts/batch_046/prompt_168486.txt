I want a consistency audit of the following package of commitments about democratic boundaries, because I am not sure they hang together and I cannot tell which ones are actually pulling against each other.

My commitments are these:

(1) I distinguish sharply between the unit problem (the appropriate scope of democratic authority) and the membership problem (who within that unit counts as part of the demos), and I treat solving the first as conceptually independent of solving the second.

(2) For the unit problem, I accept a modified all-affected-interests principle constrained by what I call “deliberative reach”: democratic authority should extend over an issue only if the affected parties can, in principle, be incorporated into a shared deliberative framework without collapsing the polity into global governance.

(3) For membership, I hold that the demos should include all permanent residents who are subject to the polity’s coercive laws, regardless of citizenship, but should exclude temporary visitors and non-resident citizens, on the grounds that membership requires both being subjected to coercion and having a long-term territorial stake in the collective future.

(4) I also hold that future generations ought to have institutionalized proxy representation in long-horizon domains such as climate or infrastructure governance, even though they are not presently present, not directly affected in the current causal chain, and not subject to present laws.

(5) I reject shared cultural identity or language as a necessary moral condition for membership, yet I maintain that democratic deliberation requires a shared “public reason” framework that is inevitably culturally embedded, which creates a practical—though not moral—pressure for convergence among members.

(6) I support extending formal proxy representation, but not full membership, to non-human animals with complex sentience within the territory, treating this as a fiduciary obligation rather than as an expansion of the concept of “the people.”

Please do not lecture me on democratic theory in general. I want an audit ordered by severity: which commitments are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and for each real tension what would have to give and at what cost. In particular, I am concerned that my “deliberative reach” condition in (2) may smuggle membership assumptions back into the unit problem, and that my criteria in (3) apply asymmetrically to future generations in (4) and to non-resident citizens. Rank the tensions, identify the closest analog or case that would force me to choose, and tell me what the least costly revision to the package is—without assuming I am eager to drop any particular commitment in advance.
