I've been working through the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — the challenge that pleasures seem phenomenologically diverse, whether the warmth of sunlight, the satisfaction of grasping an abstract argument, or the taste of something sweet, and that attempts to identify a uniform qualitative feature common to all of them either overgeneralize or collapse into circularity. Rather than retreating to a pure representational account, on which pleasure is just the representation of some state of affairs as good for the subject, or to a pure dispositional account, on which pleasure is whatever reinforces behavior regardless of conscious quality, I have been trying to defend a more specific hybrid position. On this view, pleasures are unified not by a shared phenomenal quality but by a shared functional-phenomenal architecture: they are states in which a positive affective evaluation is both phenomenologically salient and cognitively accessible for deliberative weighting, irrespective of sensory modality. What makes both the enjoyment of a melody and the relief of solving a puzzle count as pleasures, on this account, is not an identical inner feel but an identical structural role in practical reasoning — they present themselves as non-inferential reasons to continue or repeat the activity that produced them, and they do so with a kind of transparency that allows them to be weighed against competing considerations without further mediation. I think this avoids both the circularity of defining pleasure in terms of feeling good and the eliminativism of reducing it to reinforcement, but I want an honest assessment of how exposed the view really is.

What would count against it? I am looking for a map of its vulnerabilities, not just the standard objections found in introductory treatments. Which empirical or conceptual findings would be decisive — for instance, well-described cases of subliminal reward states that motivate behavior without being available to deliberation, or cases of aversive obsessive-compulsive urges that present themselves with exactly the same deliberative transparency? Which of these would a committed defender of my hybrid view concede as genuine damage, and which could be dismissed as describing peripheral or non-paradigmatic instances? I also want to know whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability: if any counterexample can be relegated to the status of a non-central pleasure, does that insulation protect the theory or merely drain it of explanatory content? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish predictions or commitments that my position makes which pure phenomenalist, pure representationalist, and pure dispositionalist rivals do not share, and say whether you think the insulation problem is a serious theoretical vice for a view whose purpose is precisely to explain the unity of pleasure across heterogeneous domains.
