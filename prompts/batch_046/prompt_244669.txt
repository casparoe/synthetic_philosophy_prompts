My friends got in a fight about whether a perfect forgery of a lost painting is worth anything. (1) She said its value comes from being the actual object the artist touched, so a copy is just a prop with zero authentic value. (2) He said thats mystical - if every brushstroke and pigment is identical, the experience is identical, and value lives in experience, not in some invisible causal chain. (3) She replied that hes conflating sensory pleasure with historical value; the original is a survivor with a biography, and copies are biographically empty. (4) He said biography is just framing - if we rescued the copy from a fire and told its story, we'd value it the same, so the object itself carries nothing special. (5) She said that just proves her point: the copy only gets value by parasitically borrowing the original's causal history, like counterfeit money that works only if mistaken for real. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the five moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Give your answer as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
