Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a discontinuity theorist and a continuity/transformationalist on whether societies “collapse” or merely change, using the Late Bronze Age Eastern Mediterranean, the Classic Maya lowlands, and Rapa Nui (Easter Island) as shared cases. First, list the claims both can sign: the archaeological record shows rapid settlement abandonment, demographic contraction, and the cessation of large-scale monumental construction in each region; palaeoclimatic data indicate severe drought or resource stress; and inferring collective intention from material remains is methodologically hazardous. Next, state the precise disagreement in wording each side accepts: the discontinuity side treats the simultaneous loss of state-level administration, writing, and long-distance trade as a systemic failure with irreversible damage; the continuity side treats the same patterns as adaptive decentralisation, ritual reorientation, and strategic contraction, insisting that “collapse” imposes a modern teleological narrative. Identify the cruxes—whether ecological stress is sufficient to cause political fragmentation without assuming failure; whether post-abandonment elite ceramic production in Maya peripheries and continued moai carving after Rapa Nui deforestation indicate persistent social capacity rather than breakdown; and whether Holling’s ecological resilience framework usefully maps onto these historical transitions or merely imports anachronistic biological metaphors. For each crux, specify the concrete archaeological or palaeobotanical evidence both agree is relevant, and record in advance what each side predicts it shows. Finally, distinguish any purely verbal disputes—particularly over whether “failure” carries normative weight that “transformation” avoids—from substantive empirical disagreements. Write the response in British English.
