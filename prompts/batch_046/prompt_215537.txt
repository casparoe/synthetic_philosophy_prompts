I'm a retired marine biologist who's been reading a lot of political philosophy lately, mostly about the welfare state and this idea of the 'undeserving poor' — though I think the phrase is a bit loaded. Anyway, I sit on a small municipal ethics panel that reviews pilot welfare programmes in our region, and we've built up a bit of a track record. I'm trying to figure out if we're actually running a consistent rule or just making it up as we go along.

Here are our past rulings:
1. We approved 'Job Search Plus': claimants had to log 15 suitable job applications per week, with a four-week benefit reduction for non-compliance, but with clear exemptions for documented illness or caring responsibilities.
2. We rejected the 'Universal Mandate' that demanded 20 applications regardless of suitability and offered no exemption pathway.
3. We approved 'Family Link', tying part of child benefit to school attendance above 85%, but only after a three-month support period and with the sanction being temporary.
4. We rejected a proposal for permanent disqualification from all benefits after any fraud conviction, even for a first-time overclaim of £40.
5. We approved 'Choice Path', where claimants could satisfy the condition by either job-search OR completing an accredited skills course, with seperate sanction structures for both tracks.

Now we've got a new proposal — the Green Reciprocity Pilot. It's a bit niche, I know. Claimants recieve a 30% top-up if they complete 10 hours a week of local ecological monitoring — specifically counting pollinator visits along standardised hedgerow transects, with data submitted to a university partner. They get a one-day training course. If they don't do it, they only lose the top-up, not base benefits. There's no job-search requirement if they take the eco-track, but they can switch back to job-search and still keep the top-up if they prefer.

The panel is split. I want to know: what should we decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Which of our earlier cases is the closest analog? Lay out the different rules that could fit our track record, say what each one predicts about this pilot, and give your best guess with reasons. If our precedents genuinely underdetermine the answer, say what principle we'd be adopting if we approve it, and what principle if we reject it. Also, name a further case that would actually tell those rules apart.
