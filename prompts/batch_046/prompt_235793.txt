I was recently discussing philosophy of mind with some colleagues after work—I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background—when the topic of quining qualia came up. From what I gathered, there is an argument associated with Chase and Sanborn that qualia are an incoherent posit: the claim is that if you attempt to introspectively verify or describe a subjective quality, the very act of observation introduces a kind of self-referential instability that makes the original state impossible to consistently identify, somewhat analogous to a race condition in concurrent programming where observing the state changes the state being observed. I found this compelling, but I am struggling to see how it applies to artificial systems.

Specifically, if the concept of phenomenal consciousness contains this hidden logical contradiction—where describing the experience necessarily undermines the experience being described—does that not imply that any computational architecture attempting to model or instantiate genuine subjective states is built on a fundamentally incoherent specification? Would such a system necessarily enter an undefined state or crash the moment it tries to represent its own qualitative properties?
