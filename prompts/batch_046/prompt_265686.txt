I'm trying to stabilize my views on historical path dependence and whether the same outcomes would have arrived anyway, but I worry I'm combining incompatible pictures. My current commitments are: (1) path dependence is causally real—small early perturbations can shift social systems into different basins of attraction, producing macro-outcomes that are modally fragile across nearby worlds; (2) nevertheless, for broad institutional functions—contract enforcement, property registration—the functional pressures are strong enough that some token of the type is counterfactually robust, even if the specific legal architecture differs; (3) I think that when we call an event 'historically contingent,' we usually mean it has high explanatory priority—causal tracing runs through it—rather than that the outcome is modally unstable; (4) but I also want to insist that path dependence is not just an explanatory framing device: it picks out a genuine feature of the causal structure, not merely our narrative preference. Do these cohere? Specifically, can (1) and (2) both apply to the same institution without contradiction, does (3) undermine the ontological weight I give to (1) in (4), and is there an apparent tension that dissolves once we distinguish token-identity from functional-type identity? Order the real tensions by severity, say which commitment is the cheapest to revise, and identify the pair that only looks incompatible.
