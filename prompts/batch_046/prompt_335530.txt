everyone in my study group keeps saying that performing a speech act is like pressing a button on a remote — you say "i do" and the marriage happens, just like you press power and the tv turns on. they use this to argue that perfomatives don't need inner mental states at all, just the right conventional wirering. but i'm not convinced. can you audit the anology?

give me the correspondence table — speaker, utterance, institution, remote circuitry — then the places it breaks, especially around Austin's misfire/abuse distinction. does the conclusion that mental states are irrelevant survive? and what about cases where the performative misfires but still leaves a trace in the institutional record, like an unauthorized officiant whose ceremony gets registered before anyone notices? that kind of infelicity seems to have no button equivalent.
