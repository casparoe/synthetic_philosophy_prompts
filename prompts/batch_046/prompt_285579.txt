I'm preparing a position paper for a workshop on collective epistemology and want a consistency check before I circulate it. Here is my current package: (1) I accept Aumann's agreement theorem as normatively binding for idealized rational agents—shared priors plus common knowledge of posteriors entail convergence. (2) I think the principle of indifference often determines a unique rational prior in policy-relevant domains, so persistent disagreement about risks or values frequently reflects a failure to apply that principle rather than legitimate divergence. (3) I am a strong anti-paternalist about belief formation: institutions should not design curricula or information environments to steer citizens toward particular priors, even for clear epistemic gains. (4) At the same time, I think democratic disagreement is usually rational—different life experiences generate different priors, and there is no single correct prior that all citizens ought to share. (5) I deny that common knowledge of posteriors is achievable in actual societies, not because of irrationality, but because interpretation is theory-laden in a way that prevents the "same evidence" condition from being satisfied. (6) Still, I hold that if a well-structured deliberative forum genuinely achieved common knowledge of posteriors, then failure to converge would be blameworthy. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear incompatible, and what is the least costly revision for each real conflict? Please rank the tensions by severity and specify what has to give.
