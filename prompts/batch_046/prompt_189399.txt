Consider the institutional treatment of labour in contemporary automation policy and welfare administration. Government reports on artificial intelligence and robotics routinely warn that mass displacement will erode not merely wages but human dignity, purpose, and social contribution—language that suggests certain forms of work possess non-instrumental value that income transfers cannot replicate. Yet the same administrative systems treat unemployment as a problem of income and job-search compliance: claimants must apply for a fixed quota of positions regardless of craft, skill, or vocation, and suitable employment is defined almost exclusively by pay and proximity. Meanwhile, corporate productivity infrastructures for remote knowledge work—algorithmic tracking of keystrokes, calendar density, and focus-time metrics—reduce labour to continuously measurable output, as if work were exhaustible by quantifiable performance indicators.

What philosophical commitments about the meaning and value of work does this triad of practices—automation rhetoric that treats work as a source of existential purpose, welfare rules that treat it as fully substitutable income, and surveillance metrics that treat it as pure output—collectively presuppose? Are the three consistent, or do they quietly rely on incompatible theories of value? Reconstruct the implicit account or accounts: does the welfare system presuppose a purely instrumental view, does the automation discourse presuppose something closer to a perfectionist or recognition-based theory, and does the productivity metric presuppose that all valuable labour is in principle quantifiable? Distinguish what each practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates, and say whether a rival view—say, that work matters primarily for the development of practical capacities, or purely for distributive justice—could license all three without contradiction. Finally, describe what an institution that genuinely embraced a non-instrumental conception of work would have to change in its unemployment rules, its response to technological displacement, and its internal measurement of employee contribution.
