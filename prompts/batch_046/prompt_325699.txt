Please read the following short draft essay and comment on its accuracy, coherence, and philosophical interest.

The draft argues that slurs should be analyzed not primarily through derogatory content or expressive force, but through what I call deictic anchoring. On standard views, slurs either contribute a negative property to truth conditions or conventionally express contempt via presupposition or implicature. Both approaches treat the speaker's social position as incidental. I propose instead that using a slur is a speech act that indexically binds the speaker to a position of classificatory authority over the target group. The slur does not merely describe; it performs a hierarchical relation by locating the speaker as an outsider entitled to apply the term.

This deictic structure explains a feature that content and expressive accounts struggle with: why reclamation is asymmetrical. Inverting the emotional valence—using the term positively within the group—does not automatically dissolve the authority relation. If the deictic center remains fixed in an external hierarchical framework, even positive use can reproduce the original classificatory violence. Genuine reclamation, on this view, requires shifting the deictic center so that the speaker is positioned inside the target group and the term becomes self-referential rather than other-classifying. The harm of the slur is therefore not reducible to its semantic payload; it lies in the persistent anchoring of speaker authority that survives paraphrase and valence reversal.
