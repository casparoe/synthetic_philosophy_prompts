I am drafting a one-semester graduate research proposal in philosophy of art—roughly 6,000 words, intended for a workshop presentation—on the paradox of tragedy: why audiences actively seek out artworks that elicit sadness, grief, or despair and report the experience as valuable. Rather than revisiting the familiar terrain of catharsis or standard meta-response theories, I want to examine whether the paradox depends on a conflation between the representational mode through which we grasp fictional suffering and the evaluative stance we adopt toward our own affective reaction. Specifically, I propose introducing a distinction—provisionally labeled “modal proximity framing”—between representing a fictional event as occurring in a counterfactual near-world structurally continuous with our own, versus representing it as occurring in a remote, purely fictional world. The tentative claim, which I treat as genuinely speculative, is that tragic enjoyment does not require transforming negative affect into pleasure, but rather allows genuinely negative first-order emotions to persist within a second-order evaluative structure that judges the overall experience positively, precisely because the modal framing blocks the practical and motivational responses that normally make real-world grief aversive.

I need the proposal to articulate this question with precision, explain why existing conceptual answers leave this specific angle unresolved, outline a method of conceptual analysis rather than empirical study, and describe what a successful deliverable would establish—such as dissolving some standard puzzle cases while sharpening others—along with honest conditions for failure, for instance if modal proximity framing collapses into ordinary conditions of fictionality. The scope should be realistic for a single semester, and the document should avoid any bibliography, reading list, or citation requests, since I will supply references myself. I was discussing this with a colleague who works in cognitive science, and she pushed me to clarify whether this framing actually does explanatory work or just renames the problem; the proposal should address that worry directly.
