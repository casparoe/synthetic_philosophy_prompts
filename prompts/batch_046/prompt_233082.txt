Consider this argument I sketched for a seminar on conceptual methodology, which now strikes me as suspicious because it threatens to dissolve every intuition pump while relying on one itself.

(1) A philosophical intuition pump works only if the audience can generate a single, stable mental model of the scenario described.
(2) Generating that model requires filling in unspecified parameters—social norms, physical laws, psychological dispositions—that are not part of the explicit premises.
(3) Because these parameters are not fixed by the text, different audiences construct different models; there is no one shared argument, only a family of structurally similar but logically distinct simulations.
(4) If there is no intersubjectively fixed argument, then an intuition pump cannot establish a universal conclusion; at best it maps individual cognitive biases.
(5) But the claim in (1)-(4) itself depends on asking you to represent "different audiences constructing different models"—which is itself an intuition pump about mental representation.
(6) So either (5) collapses the argument into self-refutation, or (1)-(4) are false and intuition pumps can indeed be intersubjectively fixed.
(7) Meanwhile, philosophers routinely treat cases like the violinist scenario or the experience-machine as delivering definite conclusions about real-world ethics.
(8) Therefore either philosophical reasoning is systematically deluded about its own method, or one of (1)-(4) must be rejected.

If I want to preserve the idea that intuition pumps are genuinely epistemic tools rather than mere rhetoric, which step should I give up? Explain why that step seemed plausible, and price the alternatives—especially whether rejecting it forces us to treat pumps as disguised persuasion, or whether there is a middle path that saves intersubjective validity without pretending mental simulation is logically transparent. Give a committed answer: name the specific step, defend the rejection with reasons, and say clearly what we are buying and what we are paying.
