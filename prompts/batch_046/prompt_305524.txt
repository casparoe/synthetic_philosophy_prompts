I've been arguing with my roommate about free will and mechanical behavour ever since we watched a nature doc that mentioned that wasp — Sphex, I think it was — that drags it's prey to a burrow, checks inside, then pulls it in, but if you move the prey while it's checking it just loops back and starts over. Anyway, I started thinking about what actually makes something sphexish versus just rigid in a normal way, and I have these gut reactions that don't line up neatly.

So here's my verdicts on cases. The wasp loop feels totally sphexish — it's locked in, no freedom. A thermostat flipping heat on and off feels mechanical but not sphexish, since it's continuously tracking the room. My aunt checks the stove four times every morning; she says she can stop if she really tries, but the pull is strong. I think she's acting sphexish in that moment, but I still think she has free will, which is weird because the wasp doesn't. Then there's a bird trained only on blue buttons that keeps pecking blue when food is behind red — that's sphexish, and I don't think free will is even on the table. But a soldier following old orders after the situation clearly changed — that's sphexish too, yet I feel like he's giving up his free will in that moment, not that he never had it. And there's this hypothetical AI I made up that always applies the same reasoning template no matter how weird the input is; it's rigid, but since there's no 'trying' going on I don't know whether to call it sphexish or just broken.

My half-baked idea is that sphexishness is rigidity plus the absence of what I'd call 'interruption reversibility' — the ability to break the loop from inside without an external shove. But that makes my aunt and the soldier both have it (they can break it) yet I still think only the soldier is acting unfreely. So my principle is already cracking. Is there a cleaner account that keeps my verdict that the wasp and the bird are sphexish/unfree, the thermostat isn't sphexish, my aunt is sphexish-but-free, and the soldier is sphexish-and-unfree? Or do I have to drop one of those? And what would your repaired principle say about the made-up AI — is it sphexish if there's no internal life to be interrupted?
