As a retired physicist recently turned to philosophy of science, I want to test whether scientific realism survives the pessimistic meta-induction by restricting it to quantifiable structural continuity rather than ontological commitment. Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between myself, holding that position, and an anti-realist who grants the historical record of radical theory replacement but denies that structural retention can be measured non-circularly. Present the result as exactly five numbered sentences, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: first, the shared claims about the historical record and about what realism must explain; second, my structural-continuity position phrased in terms I would accept; third, the anti-realist counter-position phrased in terms they would accept; fourth, the central crux—whether structural retention across theory change can be assessed without presupposing the theories under evaluation—together with a concrete historical case both sides agree is relevant and each side's predicted assessment of it; fifth, an honest judgment of whether any portion of the disagreement is merely verbal, with an explanation of which portion and why.
