I sit on the Institutional Repair Council of a large public university. After a years-long cover-up of research misconduct in our medical school, we are redesigning our accountability protocol. One faction proposes a "guilt architecture": private disciplinary hearings, anonymized findings, and reparative obligations focused on correcting specific wrongs. Another faction proposes a "shame architecture": public disclosure of responsible parties, ceremonial acknowledgment before affected communities, and reintegration contingent on visible status restoration rather than private remorse. The philosophical question embedded in our choice is whether guilt or shame is the more appropriate moral emotion for institutional betrayal, and whether an organization can legitimately engineer which emotion dominates its culture. Draft the Council's decision memo recommending one architecture (or a structured hybrid), specifying at least two concrete mechanisms for implementation and a timeline for rollout. The memo must fairly represent the strongest objection to your recommendation—particularly the concern that deliberately selecting a moral emotion violates either individual dignity or cultural autonomy—and should not invoke outside authorities but argue from the nature of institutional harm and repair.
