In alot of consciousness labs there's a methodoligical rule where you only call a percept 'consciously accessed' if the subject gives one unified report — button press or verbal answer, doesn't matter which modality, but they have to agree. If eye-tracking shows attention to X while the subject reports Y, the standard practise is to discard one stream as pre-conscious noise or measurement error. But a few groups now use something they call a multi-track reportability score, which lets conflicting reports from different modalities stand without forcing them into a single narrative. What view of the mind — maybe the Cartesian theater idea that there's one central screen where it all comes together, versus Dennett's multiple drafts picture where there's no single canonical version — does the old single-report rule presuppose? Does adopting the multi-track score actually commit you to multiple drafts, or can you keep a unified self-model and just say the reports access different layers? Reconstruct the implicit epistemology behind each method, say whether they're genuinely incompatable or just tracking seperate aspects of processing, and tell me what'd have to change in experimental design if we dropped the assumption that there's always one correct conscious report to extract.
