I'm working through Douglas Hofstadter's tripartite distinction—frame message, outer message, and inner message—as developed in Gödel, Escher, Bach, and I want to know what any serious theory of semantic location must actually be able to deliver before I defend a particular account. The schema seems adequate for ordinary face-to-face communication, but I'm troubled by cases that don't fit a single sender-receiver dyad: a Wikipedia article that evolves through hundreds of anonymous edits and eventually conveys a coherent thesis none of the individual contributors intended; an AI system that generates a sustained argument about consciousness that wasn't present in its training objectives; and automated legal mediators that extract obligations from contracts that the drafters never explicitly articulated. In each instance it's unclear whether the "outer message" is missing, distributed across a collective, or reconstructed by the receiver in a way that should count as part of the meaning rather than as misreading.

So I'd like the full desiderata: what must a theory of where meaning resides explain or preserve? List the requirements—handling recoverable authorial intent when it exists, accommodating robust unintended interpretations, accounting for messages with absent or distributed senders, distinguishing causal origin from semantic content, and whatever else genuinely belongs—along with the motivating case or puzzle behind each. Say which are non-negotiable (for instance, not collapsing the literal frame into the reconstructed inner message) and which are camp preferences (for instance, privileging the psychology of a single speaker). Crucially, tell me whether any cluster of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—say, if maintaining strict outer-message authority for all texts is incompatible with giving full weight to genuinely emergent interpretations in collective or algorithmic production. I want the criteria and their motivating puzzles first; a brief note on where intentionalist, descriptivist, and reception-oriented approaches tend to fall short against your list is welcome, but don't turn this into a survey.
