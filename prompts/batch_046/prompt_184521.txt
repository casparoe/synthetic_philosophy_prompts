I am a doctoral student in philosophy of language studying recursive structures in semiotic framing, and I am trying to work through a difficult passage in Charles Sanders Peirce's Silliman lectures on logic as semiotic. Peirce argues that a representamen determines its object only through the mediation of an interpretant, but he then specifies that this interpretant is not a mental image but another sign, which itself determines the object through a further interpretant, generating an infinite regress. The part I find obscure is his claim that the conditions governing how the first interpretant should be read—conditions that Douglas Hofstadter in Gödel, Escher, Bach distinguishes as frame messages—are not external to the sign-chain but are themselves constituted within it. This makes it seem as though the framing layer has been collapsed into the inner message, leaving no hierarchical separation between the rules for interpretation and the content being interpreted.

Can you help me understand whether Peirce is genuinely describing a self-framing sign-structure in which interpretive conditions are recursively embedded in the interpreted content, or whether I am misreading him by importing Hofstadter's linear nesting of frame, outer message, and inner message into a system that was never meant to support such layers? In particular, is there an established concept for this kind of internalized framing—what I have been tentatively calling frame-collapse, where framing conditions are absorbed into the message they govern rather than enclosing it—or does the description mark a structural error that should be corrected? I am not asking for references; I want to know whether the phenomenon I am describing is conceptually coherent and how its boundaries should be drawn against ordinary recursive framing.
