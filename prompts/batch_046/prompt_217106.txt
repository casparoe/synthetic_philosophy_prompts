For a philosophy of practical reason seminar I am auditing — after a recent experience with reproductive genetic counseling that made the abstract suddenly concrete — please specify the desiderata that any adequate theory resolving the procreation asymmetry must meet, with the case or consideration behind each, marking which are non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences.

The theory must explain why there is typically a weighty reason not to deliberately bring into existence a person who will suffer severely, yet no parallel reason to bring a happy person into existence, and it must do so without collapsing into a generic utilitarian aggregation. Specify the boundary phenomena it must cover: cases of mild or compensable suffering; identity-affecting reproductive choices (timing conception, embryo selection, or gamete choice) where the “victim” never exists to be made worse off; and the distinction between harming an existing child and creating a suffering one. It must also respect the judgment that we have no general obligation to maximize the number of happy lives, remain consistent with our ordinary obligations to existing people, and not depend on a metaphysically contested comparison class of “possible people” that may not exist.

For each requirement, state the motivating case and whether it is indispensable or a prejudice of a particular camp (person-affecting, impersonal, rights-based, virtue-ethical). Crucially, address whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable: in particular, can a theory both insist that harm is genuinely person-affecting — requiring an actual subject made worse off — and still condemn the deliberate creation of suffering lives in identity-affecting contexts? If that tension is unavoidable, present it as a structural result rather than a failure of one side. Additionally, inject the following niche constraint: the theory must account for selective-reproduction scenarios in which genetic screening prevents one potential child and creates a different one, clarifying whether any wrongness attaches to the agent’s disposition, to the act-type, or to the outcome-state, without treating the non-existent child as a bearer of counterfactual harm.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their logical relations — especially which requirement does the most work in excluding candidates — rather than on a full scorecard of named theories, though you may briefly indicate which major approaches can satisfy the non-negotiable conditions and which fail them. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the reasoning must stand without external lookup.
