Popper proposed falsificationism to solve the demarcation problem: a theory counts as scientific only if it risks empirical refutation. Lakatos then argued that scientists routinely protect core theoretical commitments through auxiliary hypotheses, replacing Popper's binary criterion with an evaluation of whether adjustments yield progressive or degenerating problemshifts. But if any belief system—including astrology—can construct such a protective belt and claim that its adjustments are progressive, hasn't Lakatos's framework collapsed the demarcation boundary entirely? In other words, doesn't the shift from direct falsifiability to research-programme evaluation demonstrate that there is no principled line between science and pseudoscience, and if so, why do philosophers of science still treat demarcation as a live problem rather than conceding that falsificationism offers only a historical description of scientific practice?
