I'm taking a seminar on theories of well-being and we've been examining tranquilism—the view that only the absence of craving and suffering matters for welfare, with no independent positive value in pleasure, achievement, or desire-satisfaction. For a creative assignment, I need a short story that engages this idea directly but avoids the standard ascetic-monk or hermit tropes. Please write a story set in a near-future clinical facility called the Stillpoint Ward, where volunteers undergo a non-pleasurable neural modulation that eliminates all craving and suffering, leaving them in sustained tranquil absence without joy or distress. The protagonist should be a care-worker who discovers that the patients, though free from suffering, have lost any basis for preferring continued existence over non-existence, and she must decide whether to preserve the ward's power supply when an outage would restore their cravings along with their capacity for attachment and happiness. Take a definite position in the narrative: either defend tranquilism by showing that this absence is genuinely good and should be protected, or reject it by portraying the state as a kind of living death that cannot sustain human dignity. Do not remain neutral; commit to one reading and let the plot and imagery carry your reasoning. After the story, include a brief author's note—no more than 150 words—indicating where the narrative takes liberties with actual tranquilist doctrine or relies on a contested interpretation of negative welfare theories.
