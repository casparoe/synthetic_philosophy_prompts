1. Agreed claims (numbered list, roughly 8–10 items): State neutral propositions both sides can endorse about current nosologies such as DSM-5 and ICD-11, about the observable clustering of symptoms, about the reality of patient suffering, and about documented cultural and historical variation in prevalence and presentation. Disagreement table (about 4 rows, with columns for Issue, Realist formulation, and Looping-effect formulation): For each divergence, present the dispute in language that each camp would accept for its own position. For the decoupling scenario, state what the realist expects regarding underlying biological unity across the two assessment methods, and what the looping-effect advocate expects regarding divergence driven by classification-dependent self-reporting rather than by biological heterogeneity.
