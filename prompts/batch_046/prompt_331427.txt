I just turned eighteen and registered to vote for the first time, and now I'm suddenly obsessed with whether I'm actually doing something wrong every time I tick a box. I was discussing it with my flatmates over dinner — one of them studies law, another does maths — and we ended up building this argument that feels suspicious because the conclusion is basically that democracy makes everyone a villain. I'm writing an essay for my philosophy moduel and I can't figure out where the sleight of hand is, so I need an exit.

(1) A vote is both an expressive act — it communicates who you want in power — and a causal act — it changes the probability of who actually wins.
(2) If it's purely expressive, then you're not responsible for outcomes; you're just saying something, like shouting in a crowd.
(3) But if it's causal, then you're partly responsible for whatever the winner does, in the same direct way you'd be responsible if you physically pushed someone onto a train track.
(4) In any real election the candidates are flawed, so you vote for the lesser evil; that means you causally contribute to policies you think are wrong, just less wrong than the alternative.
(5) Abstaining also changes the probabilities — it increases the worse candidate's chance — so abstaining is also a causal contribution to harm.
(6) So in a competitive election with flawed candidates, every option — vote A, vote B, or abstain — makes you causally responsible for some harm.
(7) But democracy requires mass participation, which means democracy necessarily requires universal moral complicity.

I feel like something slips between (3) and (4), or maybe between (5) and (6), but I can't name it. Is it that my individual vote almost never changes the result, so treating it like pushing someone is a category mistake? Or is the argument actually sound and the cost is just accepting that democratic citizenship is permanently tainted? I'd prefer to keep (1) and (4) if possible — they match how voting actually feels — but tell me what I'm paying. Please answer in British English, and don't just survey every voting theory ever; I want to know which step to reject and what rejecting it costs me.
