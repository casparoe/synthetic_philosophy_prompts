I am working on a dissertation chapter on the political dimensions of logical empiricism, and I want to test whether Carnap's largely overlooked planning framework can illuminate an actual institutional case without anachronistic distortion. Analyze the creation and first decade of the Tennessee Valley Authority (1933–1943) through Carnap's distinction—developed in his political writings—between the logical-instrumental structure of a plan, treated as empirically assessable and value-neutral, and the material value commitments that determine its goals, treated as non-cognitive preferences to be settled by democratic agreement rather than scientific expertise. First, map the major controversies surrounding the TVA—federal preemption of state authority, centralized technical management versus local democratic control, and the trade-offs between flood control, electrification, rural displacement, and downstream ecological costs—onto Carnap's distinction: identify which conflicts concern empirically resolvable means and which concern irreducible value choices. Second, evaluate whether Carnap's framework illuminates these conflicts or systematically mischaracterizes them; in particular, examine whether the TVA's hybrid governance, in which engineers designed river-basin infrastructure within congressionally mandated goals, actually realizes Carnap's ideal of separating scientific planning instruments from democratic value selection, or whether technical decisions—such as dam siting and electricity-rate structures—inevitably embedded normative judgments about risk and welfare distribution that Carnap's framework relegates to an external domain. Third, discuss what this case reveals about Carnap's broader claim that large-scale planning can be scientifically rational without being ethically authoritarian, particularly given that the plan's instrumental calculus treated community displacement and ecological externalities as outside its scope of evaluation. Conclude by assessing whether the TVA should be read as a confirmation, an anomaly, or a boundary case for Carnap's politics of planning.
