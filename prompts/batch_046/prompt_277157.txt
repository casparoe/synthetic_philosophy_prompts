hey - i'm trying to map out which open problems in the nationalism / invention-of-tradition literature are actually worth working on, as opposed to just recycling Hobsbawm and Anderson for the millionth time. we all know the basic story: nations aren't ancient, they're modern constructions held together by invented rituals and print-capitalist imagination. but where does that leave us?

specifcally, i'm interested in three clusters that seem to pull in different directions. first, there's the metaphysical cluster: if collective identity is constructed, does that undermine the normative authority of the nation-state, or is that a category error? second, there's the empirical / historical cluster: how do we distinguish genuinely invented traditions from ones that are just slowly evolved, and does the distinction even hold up under close archival work? third - and this is the weirder one - there's the emerging problem of algorithmically mediated cultural memory. reccomendation systems and generative models are now producing synthetic "traditions" that feel old and shared, but have no organic community behind them. is extending Anderson / Hobsbawm to cover this a productive research program, or is it stretching the framework past breaking?

i want an agenda, not a literature review. if you had three years and a small grant, where should the effort go? which of these clusters is upstream of the others - e.g., does solving the metaphysical question about legitimacy make the applied questions about synthetic culture easier, or are they independent? what's tractable with current methods vs. what's just speculative? and which questions, if answered, wouldn't actually change how anyone thinks about politics or cultural policy? rank them with criteria: importance for practice, tractability, neglectedness, and dependency on other answers. also be honest about what's overrated - maybe the metaphysics of collective identity is a dead end and we should just do the sociology of algorithmic tradition-building instead.

no need for citations or reading lists - just the reasoning.
