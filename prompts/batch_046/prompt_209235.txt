I am an undergraduate student in a philosophy course on ancient metaphysics and the philosophy of perception. For a creative assignment, I need a short story—roughly 600 to 900 words—that reimagines Plato's allegory of the cave through the lens of contemporary neural prosthetics. The protagonist should be a patient fitted with a cortical visual prosthesis: her visual field is reconstructed from sparse electrode data processed by an adaptive algorithm, producing images she initially accepts as direct perceptions of an external world. As the narrative progresses, she should begin to suspect that these reconstructed scenes are mediated shadows—shaped as much by the algorithm's priors and her own neural interpretation as by any external reality. Through this realization, the story should explore whether there might exist a form of sight or visual truth that transcends both the prosthetic apparatus and the brain's interpretive work. Please write the story in a literary but accessible style, avoiding explicit philosophical citations, named texts, or technical bibliographies, and focusing on the lived experience of discovering that one's perceived world may be a constructed image.
