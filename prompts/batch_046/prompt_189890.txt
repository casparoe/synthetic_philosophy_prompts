I serve as an ethics auditor for a regional hospital network, and every quarter I must sign off on whether our clinical quality-incentive programs—those tying bonuses and accreditation to readmission rates, infection scores, and patient-satisfaction ratings—have been captured by the metric rather than serving genuine patient welfare. I want you to convert the philosophical distinction between wireheading and reward tampering into a reusable audit procedure I can execute in about forty-five minutes per program using only in-house documentation and outcome data. The procedure should be an ordered sequence of tests or decision points, written in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and it must preserve a three-way distinction that I find constantly collapsed in practice: direct reward tampering, in which staff manipulate the measurement instrument itself, such as altering diagnostic codes; first-order proxy drift, in which they optimize the measured variable through actions that genuinely affect the target but displace unmeasured harms; and what I will call ecological wireheading, in which they restructure the clinical environment so that the metric improves not because the problem was solved but because it was displaced—say, by transferring high-risk patients to non-participating facilities or shifting complex procedures into unmonitored settings. You should specify which of these distinctions can survive operationalization with the limited data I have, mark every point where judgment cannot be eliminated and must be acknowledged as such, identify the hard cases where documentation is ambiguous or causation is unclear, and apply the procedure to three hypothetical incentive schemes: one that clearly fails, one that clearly passes, and one—perhaps a sepsis-reduction bonus that lowers reported infection rates mainly by encouraging clinicians to reclassify borderline cases as non-infectious—that produces a verdict I will not want to accept. Finally, you must state explicitly what this procedure cannot detect, particularly regarding slow institutional adaptation that unfolds across quarters rather than within my review window, and you should avoid any request for bibliographies, citations, or further reading lists, since the point is to make the conceptual machinery usable by someone without access to a library.
