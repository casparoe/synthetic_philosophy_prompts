I am a student in an upper-division philosophy of mind seminar, and this week we are discussing a hypothetical cognitive architecture called a careenium. In this framework, symbolic representations are physically realized as discrete dynamic patterns within a substrate composed of elementary carriers called simmballs—units that possess local physical properties such as position and interaction rules but no intrinsic semantic content. The guiding question is whether symbols in a careenium genuinely push the substrate around, meaning whether symbolic-level states exert downward causal influence on the arrangement and motion of simmballs, or whether they are merely descriptive abstractions over substrate-level dynamics.

During our study-group session, two classmates engaged in a sustained exchange about this. Below is their disagreement, reconstructed as faithfully as I can. I am not taking a side; I want an adjudication.

(1) Maya argued that symbols clearly do push simmballs. In a functioning careenium, the symbol corresponding to a conditional rule is implemented by a dedicated physical gate. When the antecedent symbol is activated, it triggers that gate, which physically directs simmball flow into the configuration representing the consequent. The symbolic state is therefore part of the causal chain that rearranges the substrate.

(2) Leo replied that Maya confuses implementation with causation. The gate is nothing but more simmballs arranged in a particular shape. All causal work is performed by local physical interactions—mechanical or field-based forces—between individual simmballs. The label "if P then Q" is an interpretation we impose; if we assigned the same gate a different symbolic reading, the physical pushing would remain unchanged. Hence the symbol does no independent work.

(3) Maya countered that Leo's point establishes only multiple realizability, not epiphenomenalism. In this specific careenium, the gate's functional role is fixed by the symbol it realizes. The reason those particular simmballs form that gate rather than being randomly distributed is that the system was structured—by design or selection—to instantiate that exact symbolic rule. Consequently, the symbol constrains and explains the substrate's organization.

(4) Leo said explanation is not causation. Even if the symbol explains the gate's presence, the actual pushing remains bottom-up. He offered an analogy: saying a symbol pushes simmballs is like saying the average temperature of a gas pushes the molecules. The average is a useful statistical summary, but every causal interaction occurs at the molecular level. The symbol is analogous—an abstract pattern with no separate physical force.

(5) Maya responded that the analogy fails because careenium symbols are not statistical averages but discrete, structurally specific tokens with dedicated carriers. A symbol for P is not a mean over simmballs; it is a particular configuration playing a distinct functional role. Because the careenium's dynamics are organized around these discrete roles, altering the symbolic interpretation without altering the substrate is practically impossible, even if conceivable in principle. Thus the substrate is literally organized by the symbols.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly correct, briefly justify each rating, and say who is right about which specific point. Identify the feature of the case that does the main work—likely the discrete, role-specific nature of the symbols versus continuous substrate dynamics—and explain whether removing that feature, for instance by treating symbols as continuous statistical distributions over simmballs rather than discrete tokens, collapses the disagreement back into Leo's analogy. Finally, note any point where both sides are partly correct but talking past each other, and answer in American English.
