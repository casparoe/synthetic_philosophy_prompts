I am a retired physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy, and I serve on the governance board of the Meridian Digital Heritage Foundation, which preserves artifacts from obsolete virtual worlds. Last month, an unauthorized intrusion destroyed the sole remaining instance of the Lattice Spire, a collaborative virtual sculpture built by over four hundred participants in a now-defunct environment; our backups retain only the geometric data, not the social record of its collective construction. The board must decide whether to classify this event as genuine vandalism of a cultural object—triggering our restoration protocol and a public statement of censure—or as mere data corruption lacking special moral weight beyond technical recovery. Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend one of these concrete courses of action with a thirty-day implementation timeline, treat seriously the strongest consideration that virtual harms are ontologically parasitic and therefore categorically different from physical damage, and examine whether an artifact's normative standing can depend on its social practice of creation rather than on substrate independence. The document should be composed in British English.
