Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on original versus derived intentionality: Camp One defends Dennett's view that biological minds possess original intentionality while artifacts—including Dennett's wandering two-bitser and, by extension, modern artificial systems—possess only derived intentionality that depends on evolutionary history or user interpretation; Camp Two defends Searle's position that all genuine intentionality is intrinsic to conscious biological brains and that the original/derived distinction either collapses or leaves artifacts with mere as-if intentionality that explains nothing.

The collaboration should produce a single constructive document—written in American English—with four parts. First, a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, covering shared commitments: that aboutness is a real feature of mental states, that evolutionary selection can ground functional norms like error and success, that artifacts can be interpreted as representing without themselves being conscious, and that current large language models generate syntactically structured outputs statistically correlated with worldly states but lack biological evolutionary history of the kind that shaped brains. Keep these neutral and avoid committing either side to whether such correlation constitutes representation.

Second, a disagreements table with each divergence stated in wording both camps accept as accurate to their own view. Include at least: (a) whether the two-bitser genuinely possesses a second, minimal kind of aboutness or only simulates it through design; (b) whether derived intentionality is an ontologically real category or merely a heuristic for describing how interpreters project meaning onto artifacts; (c) whether Searle's Chinese Room demonstrates that syntax is never sufficient for semantics, or only that one particular architecture lacks understanding; and (d) whether an autonomous system running without human interpretive oversight—such as a language model guiding robotic action in an isolated environment—retains any intentionality at all, and if so, of which kind.

Third, identify the cruxes: the sub-questions that, if settled, would move at least one camp. The central cruxes should be whether aboutness necessarily requires intrinsic normative conditions (the capacity for genuine error) and whether evolutionary history or current functional role is the relevant source of original intentionality.

Fourth, for each crux specify one concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree is relevant, recording each side's advance prediction. One agreed case should be the autonomous-system scenario described above: both camps should state in advance whether they predict it will exhibit correctible misrepresentation tied to its own functional goals or merely produce syntactically appropriate but semantically empty outputs. Another agreed case should concern whether removing all human designers and interpreters from the loop eliminates intentionality or reveals a pre-existing derived kind—again with predictions recorded.

Keep the wording precise and non-rhetorical. Do not request citations, reading lists, or bibliographies. Do not invoke obscure secondary literature; rely on the core positions as described. The deliverable should be a text both parties could endorse except for the precisely marked disagreements.
