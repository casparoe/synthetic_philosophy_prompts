I am writing to seek your counsel on a professional difficulty that has begun to feel philosophically intractable. I serve on the editorial board of a small interdisciplinary journal, and a colleague and I have been co-editing a forthcoming special issue on the symbolic function of constitutional monarchy in Scandinavian democracies. She has carried out extensive archival research in Oslo and Stockholm, examining unpublished correspondence between royal households and parliamentary leaders during the constitutional revisions of the late twentieth century. I have developed the conceptual framework that connects these institutional practices to broader questions about democratic legitimacy and symbolic authority.

The difficulty is this. My colleague insists that her contribution be acknowledged only as research assistance, on the grounds that the conceptual framework is entirely mine and that for her to claim co-authorship would be to misrepresent her role as merely auxiliary. I, however, feel that without her empirical discoveries—particularly the evidence she uncovered regarding informal negotiations over royal assent in Norway during those revisions—I would have no framework worth presenting. Yet when I proposed joint authorship, she responded by demanding that I sign a written statement affirming that the theoretical architecture belongs solely to me, and she has since refused to share her remaining notes until I do so. From my perspective, this feels like a demand that I disavow my own intellectual dependence upon her labour; from hers, it appears I am attempting to colonise her archival work by subsuming it within my theoretical authority.

I am unsure whether to sign the statement, withdraw from the issue, or propose an alternative arrangement. More fundamentally, I should like to understand what is genuinely at stake in this conflict—whether it concerns autonomy, recognition, or the conditions under which one person's work can be said to belong to another—and what course of action would best resolve it without merely repeating the patterns that have brought us to an impasse.
