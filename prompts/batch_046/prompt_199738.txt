Please compare Darwin's inversion of reasoning—where evolutionary processes generate complex adaptive competence without any accompanying comprehension or intentional design—with Turing's inversion—where a mechanized system can reproduce reasoning-like outputs without possessing the internal understanding traditionally taken to underwrite them. Focus specifically on how each model treats the relationship between error and convergence: in Darwin, maladaptive variants are eliminated through differential survival across generations, while in Turing's learning machinery, erroneous pathways are suppressed through feedback-driven reorganization of connections. Which of these two mechanisms presents the starker dissolution of the link between successful performance and genuine comprehension? And in what sense, if any, does the absence of a central representational corrector in both cases make their respective forms of "blind" competence structurally analogous despite operating at entirely different temporal and organizational scales?
