(2) Leo replied that this misses the lived experience of moral decision: people genuinely feel a kind of release or exemption when they walk past a risky rescue they could have attempted, which only makes sense if there's a recognized boundary they've stayed inside, not just a floor they haven't bothered to jump over. (4) Leo said that's actually a feature, not a bug, because moral status can shift with capacity and context, but he admitted that without a principled line between "above and beyond" and "barely adequate," Maya's floor-only model is at least cleaner, and he couldn't say exactly where the boundary sits without just pointing to intuition.
