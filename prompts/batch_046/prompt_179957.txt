I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, applying for a six-month writing fellowship to produce an 8,000-word philosophical essay on an unresolved tension in Kierkegaard's critique of the press and the public. The standard reading treats his concept of the public in his social writings as a uniform moral condemnation: mass anonymity dissolves individual responsibility and produces the leveling crowd. But Kierkegaard also assigns the press a specific epistemic role, generating a phantom audience that holds opinions without being accountable for them. The precise question is whether this phantom-audience mechanism depends essentially on the temporal structure of print—simultaneous dissemination to dispersed readers who never assemble—or whether it requires only the structural separation between those who produce opinions and those who consume them. Is he diagnosing a technological feature of asynchronous broadcast, or a moral-psychological failure reproducible even in a medium with direct, synchronous accountability?

Existing interpretations split between proto-media theorist and theological moralist; neither specifies the exact conditions for phantom opinion-formation, so they cannot distinguish these explanations. My method is conceptual reconstruction followed by counterfactual testing, not historical comparison with modern platforms. Over months one and two I will extract from the pseudonymous and signed writings the exact conditions Kierkegaard requires for genuine versus phantom opinion-holding—particularly whether presence, risk, or temporal simultaneity is decisive. In month three I will construct a minimal counterfactual: a forum with the same producer-consumer separation but mandatory synchronous defense of every view, then test whether his objections still apply.

If the phantom dissolves under synchronous accountability, the critique is technologically bounded; if it persists, the problem is a deeper structure of evaded commitment that survives any medium. A successful result delivers a clear decision procedure tied to these conditions. The project fails if the textual conditions prove too vague to support the counterfactual. The scope avoids archival work and citation-heavy reviews; I will supply references myself. Please begin with the proposal text itself, with no introductory preface or commentary.
