I am writing a novel about an intellectual salon where philosophical arguments become personal, and I want to construct a classroom scene that embodies a real conceptual tension without naming it prematurely. Design a 75-minute workshop activity for sixteen graduate students—intimate, with small tangible stakes—centered on intellectual property and the ownership of ideas, but pushed well past standard copyright debates into the specific problem of simultaneous mental invention and the non-rivalrous nature of concepts. Inject this niche tension: the Lockean labor-mixing justification for property assumes that labor transforms an unowned resource into something exclusively held, yet mental objects are not depleted by use, and identical labor can occur in isolated minds without either party having access to the other. The participants should feel the pull of ownership before they understand why it collapses.

The deliverable should include: a three-phase simulation—discovery, claim, arbitration—with physical materials such as concept cards and tokens; rules arranged so that participants develop ideas independently, then discover that independent pairs have produced identical cards, and derivative blends make every boundary ambiguous; a payoff scheme using points convertible to real but minor privileges, such as selecting the next seminar text or receiving a coffee voucher; specific roles that compel participants to defend exclusivity claims that the mechanics systematically undermine; and a debrief that maps each phase onto the philosophical structure—discovery onto labor-mixing intuition, claim onto the exclusivity condition of property, arbitration onto the failure of rivalrousness—while noting what will predictably fail when the activity is first run, particularly the tendency of participants to invoke temporal priority, and being explicit about which feature of the idea the activity cannot reproduce, namely the social-utility defense of intellectual property regimes that operates independently of conceptual coherence.

Please separate, in your design notes, what is settled—such as the non-rivalrous character of ideas and the physical-resource basis of classical Lockean mixing—from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, including whether mental labor can be analogized to physical labor, whether simultaneous invention defeats all ownership claims, and whether policy arguments can salvage intellectual property even if the conceptual foundation is unstable. Do not present any contested position as the scholarly consensus.
