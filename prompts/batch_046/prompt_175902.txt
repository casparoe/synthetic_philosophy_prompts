I sit on a municipal heritage board deciding whether to approve a plaque at City Hall declaring that our city bears continuing moral guilt for a 1903 relocation ordinance, despite complete turnover in government and population. The dispute turns on whether collective guilt can survive generational replacement: one faction holds that guilt requires individual intentionality and expires with the agents, while the other holds that an institution retains guilt so long as it maintains legal continuity and derives present benefits from the original wrong, even if no current member participated. The proposal invokes Karl Jaspers's separation of criminal from political guilt but applies it to a municipal corporation rather than a nation-state, leaving both sides without direct precedent. Write the board's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with an actionable bottom line, the reasoning behind it, and an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Deliver it as exactly five numbered items, each one to two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
