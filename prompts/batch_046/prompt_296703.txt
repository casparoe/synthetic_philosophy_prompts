You are a professional philosopher of science with deep familiarity with W.V.O. Quine’s naturalism, his criterion of ontological commitment, the rejection of the analytic-synthetic distinction, and his skeptical treatment of normative ethics as conceptually closer to social convention or evolutionary adaptation than to empirical science. I want you to write a sustained deadpan parody — about 600 words — in the form of an official “Frequently Asked Questions” document issued by a fictional regulatory body, the Federal Bureau of Ontological Hygiene, which applies strict Quinean criteria to all public moral discourse. Before drafting, briefly brainstorm the specific Quinean theses you will deploy: that to exist is to be the value of a bound variable in a regimented first-order theory; that moral predicates resist such regimentation; that appeals to moral intuition are unreconstructed phenomenology lacking empirical checkpoints; and that naturalized epistemology treats normative belief-formation as a descriptive cognitive science rather than an independent source of justification.

The satire must exaggerate real features of Quine’s view rather than inventing fake ones. Citizens writing to complain that cruelty is objectively wrong or that justice demands a particular policy should receive bureaucratic responses explaining that their utterances contain no quantifiable entities, that “ought” has failed translation into canonical notation, and that grievances must instead be filed as reports on observed behavior, neural activation patterns, or evolutionary conditioning. Include a sidebar titled “Regimenting Your Grievance: A Checklist,” listing the steps by which a moral claim might be reduced to descriptive science, with most steps ending in formal rejection. Address at least one common objection — for instance, that without moral values cruelty is no different from color blindness — and answer it by remaining rigorously inside physicalist, descriptive language without smuggling in hidden normativity or slipping into compatibilism. Keep the tone relentlessly literal and official: no winking, no direct admission that this is satire, and no invented philosophical positions that Quine did not hold. A specialist should recognize the exaggeration as fair; a general reader should still sense the absurdity of treating morality as an ontological hygiene violation. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or verbatim quotations from any text; paraphrase Quine’s positions in your own polished American English.
