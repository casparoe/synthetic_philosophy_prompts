student answer:
the symbol grounding problem is that symbols in a computer only have meaning if they are anchored to something outside the symbol system, like real world objects or sensory experience. Harnad said that to really ground symbols you need a robot that can interact with the world through sensors and motors, not just process text. he called this the total turing test. then there's Searles chinese room, where a person follows rules to answer chinese questions without understanding chinese. Searle uses this to show that syntax isnt semantics, and he also says computers only have derived intentionality, which means thier meanings come from programmers, not from themselves. Harnad agrees with Searle that derived intentionality cant exist without intrinsic intentionality in the system. so both think LLMs dont really understand anything because they lack sensorimotor grounding and intrinsic intentionality. but LLMs learn embeddings that map words to other words in huge datasets, so they kind of ground symbols in statistical patterns, which might be enough if you think derived intentionality is okay.
