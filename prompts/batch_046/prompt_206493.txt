I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background auditing a graduate seminar on normative ethics, and the instructor has asked me to help draft one final-exam essay question. The course covers moral particularism versus generalism, but I want the assessment to separate students who grasp the conceptual crux from those who can only recite summaries of standard debates. To that end, please design a single 90-minute essay question worth 30 marks that frames the dispute through a niche, concrete scenario: the ethics of "technical debt forgiveness" in open-source software maintenance, specifically whether a volunteer maintainer may defer patching a known security vulnerability when the affected user base is very small, the upstream dependency is deprecated, and the maintainer's labor is entirely unpaid. The question should require students to argue whether the moral assessment of such deferral is irreducibly context-sensitive in the particularist sense, or whether it can be captured by a universal principle such as a weighted harm-minimization rule, and then to defend their position against the strongest objection from the opposing camp. Crucially, the prompt must not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and it should not depend on knowledge of obscure texts; instead, it should inject the philosophical tension directly into this engineering situation so that understanding, not memorization, is tested.

Along with the exam question, provide a model answer and a marking scheme. The model answer should make five specific conceptual moves: first, a precise distinction between feature-based particularism and holistic particularism and why neither reduces to moral relativism; second, an explanation of why particularists deny that the weight or valence of a moral reason is constant across contexts, using the maintenance scenario to illustrate variable salience; third, a rigorous generalist reconstruction that formalizes the harm calculus without smuggling in hidden context-dependencies; fourth, the particularist's best reply—whether it genuinely avoids collapsing into arbitrary intuitionism or whether it can invoke holism about reasons to preserve determinacy; and fifth, a judgment about what this case reveals regarding whether particularism is a meta-ethical thesis about the structure of moral reasoning or merely a heuristic for complex applied judgments. Assign explicit marks to each move so the total is 30.

The marking scheme should specify, for each of the five moves, both what a top answer must include and the specific misconception or shallow response that should cost marks—for instance, conflating particularism with subjectivism, treating the harm principle as self-evidently context-independent, or failing to see that the particularist's appeal to context-sensitive salience is not automatically an appeal to personal preference. Keep the difficulty at the graduate-seminar level, avoid referencing specific page numbers or requiring familiarity with texts outside common philosophy curricula, and ensure the scenario is described clearly enough that a respondent needs no prior knowledge of software engineering beyond what is given in the prompt.
