my soc phil prof has made some rulings on whether generation labels are real kinds. boomers: no, just an arbitrary birth window plus traits that come from being old, not from cohort membership. millennials: tentatively yes, since they hit the 2008 crash right as they entered the job market and got smartphones during adolescence, though she admits the borders are arbitary. gen z: probably yes, because social-media saturation hit during their teen years for basically the whole group, but she says the datas still thin. generation alpha she dismissed as pure marketing. now a researcher proposes the bridge cohort—born about 1995-2001, dial-up childhood then smartphones in high school, old enough to remember 9/11 but not old enough for it to shape their politics, entered work post-covid but before the ai hiring freeze. do you think she'll call them a real kind? im less interested in yes/no than in the principle she's actually using, what other principles fit her earlier calls just as well, and what hypothetical cohort case would best tell those principles apart.
