I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I have noticed a recurring pattern in both data analysis and public discourse that I cannot name precisely, and I want to know whether philosophy or sociology already has a concept for it.

In engineering we frequently discretize continuous variables—age, income, years of experience—into bins for reporting and modeling. The boundaries are administratively chosen: if I shift an age cutoff by one year, the membership of the "Gen Z" or "Millennial" bucket changes, but the underlying behaviors of the people near the boundary do not suddenly shift. Despite this arbitrariness, these cohort labels are routinely treated in marketing reports, policy debates, and casual argument as if they denote real kinds with shared internal essences or causal powers. For example, analysts attribute a preference for short-form video to "Gen Z values" rather than to life stage or platform availability, and commentators claim that "Millennials killed the housing market" as if the birth-year interval itself explains economic outcomes, ignoring that the same people slide from one label to another as the calendar turns. The within-group variance is enormous, the edges are fuzzy, and the category is used as an independent explanatory variable.

This is not quite stereotyping, because the issue is not attributing a trait to every individual inside the bin; it is treating the bin boundary itself as ontologically meaningful. It also differs from the ecological fallacy in its usual statistical form, since the mistake is not inferring individual properties from aggregate data, but rather treating an administratively defined demographic interval as a natural kind—something closer, in my field, to treating a type-cast or discretization artifact as if it were a discovery about reality.

Is there an established concept that captures exactly this move: the reification of an arbitrarily bounded cohort interval into an explanatory kind? If so, how does it differ from plain reification, from essentialism about social groups, and from the ecological fallacy? If there is no precise term, please coin one, define its scope, specify its boundary conditions, and indicate what nearby phenomena fall just outside it. Please answer in American English.
