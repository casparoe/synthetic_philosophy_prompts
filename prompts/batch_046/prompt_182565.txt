I've been thinking about the idea that minds come in distinct kinds—Darwinian creatures that survive by evolutionary selection, Skinnerian ones that learn through reinforcement, Popperian ones that can imagine and test hypotheses internally, and Gregorian ones that use language and shared culture to build thinking tools. I don't have an academic background in philosophy or cognitive science, but I've been trying to apply this to artificial intelligence, and I keep running into confusion.

It seems like popular discussion treats AI progress as movement along a single intelligence scale. But this four-part framework suggests there might be real discontinuities—sharp jumps rather than smooth gradients. I'm especially stuck on two things. First, whether large language models are genuinely Popperian—capable of generating and discarding internal representations of possibilities—or whether they're essentially sophisticated Skinnerian systems that match patterns without internal hypothesis-testing. Second, whether achieving fluent language use automatically makes a system Gregorian in the meaningful sense, or whether Gregorian status requires something specific about cultural transmission, tool-building, and the modification of one's own cognitive architecture through external artifacts.

Given this, I want a prioritized agenda of the open conceptual questions in this area that actually deserve sustained attention over the next few years—not a survey, and not a bibliography. Please identify three or four specific questions, rank them, and defend the ordering using explicit criteria: which ones are upstream of the others (so that settling one changes how we approach the rest), which are tractable for conceptual work without requiring new laboratory experiments, and which ones, if resolved, would actually change how we design or evaluate intelligent systems. I also want you to be honest about which parts of this four-kind framework are doing real explanatory work and which parts are probably just metaphorical scaffolding. If the Popperian-versus-Skinnerian boundary for current AI turns out to be undecidable with present tools, say so, and explain whether that makes the Gregorian question moot or independent. I'm writing this for my own clarity, not for publication, so I care most about the reasoning behind the priorities rather than the list itself.
