Please construct an argument that the conventional defense of the 35-hour workweek as a universal leisure entitlement mischaracterizes the normative problem by treating labor hours as a homogeneous quantity and leisure as mere recuperative absence. Drawing on Weber's distinction between vocational calling and instrumental employment—but without reducing the Protestant ethic to a simple historical precursor of capitalism—show that Japanese karoshi is better understood not as excessive labor but as the collapse of organizational loyalty into a surrogate vocation, which reveals that the harm lies not in hours worked but in the absence of a coherent framework for refusing work without existential disintegration. From this, defend the claim that any coherent right to leisure must be reconceived not as a negative entitlement to rest but as a positive entitlement to the practice of capacities that wage employment structurally suppresses, and anticipate the objection that this framing collapses the distinction between voluntary calling and coerced productivity by romanticizing exploitation.
