Write an essay examining whether the phenomenon of multiple realisability genuinely undermines type-identity physicalism, or whether it can be accommodated within a suitably refined physicalist ontology. Your analysis should centre on a constructed case of "mosaic cognition": a unified cognitive system in which distinct functional modules—such as perceptual categorisation, mnemonic consolidation, and executive arbitration—are instantiated in physically heterogeneous substrates (for instance, silicon-based circuitry, biological neural tissue, and photonic processing arrays) that share no common lower-level physical properties. These modules are integrated not through direct causal interaction but via a high-level informational protocol that governs their joint operation. Weigh carefully the argument that this arrangement demonstrates the autonomy of mental kinds from any particular physical base against the reply that the system-level organisational pattern itself constitutes a single, albeit highly abstract, physical kind. You should reach and defend a definite verdict: does mosaic cognition strengthen the anti-physicalist force of multiple realisability, or does it indicate that physicalism requires revision rather than abandonment? Maintain an academic register and use British spelling throughout.
