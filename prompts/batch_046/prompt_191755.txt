Draft the joint statement that a reconstructionist historian—who holds that rigorous archival work can, in well-documented domains, converge on a uniquely correct narrative of events, in the spirit of Ranke's aspiration to represent the past as it actually occurred—and a structural narrativist—who holds that every historical account is irreducibly shaped by present concerns, narrative tropes, and rhetorical form, in the manner emphasized by E. H. Carr and Hayden White—could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

The statement should contain:

- A numbered section of agreed claims about the nature of primary sources, the inevitability of selection and arrangement, the existence of archival gaps, and the fact that social power systematically influences which events leave traces.
- A disagreements table stating each side's position in wording its own proponents would accept, addressing whether narrative underdetermination is local or global, whether cross-referencing can correct for missing records, and whether objectivity is an achievable endpoint or only a regulative ideal.
- At least three cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party—one of which must address what this prompt terms "the asymmetry of archival silence": the non-random correlation between missing records and the social power of the actors involved.
- For each crux, specify a concrete historical case or body of evidence both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance what each side predicts about it. For example, both should agree that the archives of the 1848 European revolutions—where state documentation survives extensively but insurgent correspondence was largely destroyed—bear directly on the crux; the reconstructionist should predict that cross-referencing state and foreign diplomatic sources can recover a stable core narrative of key events, while the narrativist should predict that the surviving gaps necessarily license multiple equally supported macro-narratives that differ in causal and moral framing.

Format the deliverable as a numbered agreed-claims section, followed by the disagreements table, followed by the cruxes with paired predictions. Write in American English. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or source verification.
