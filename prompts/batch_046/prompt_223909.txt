I recently read an unnamed popular essay summarizing Ian Hacking’s account of looping effects in psychiatric taxonomy. The author argued that categories such as ADHD and autism spectrum disorder are “interactive kinds” rather than “indifferent kinds”: once individuals are classified, they learn the category, modify their self-understanding and behavior accordingly, and thereby alter the very phenomena clinicians observe. From this the author derived what they called a “double-loop mechanism.” First-order looping occurs when the classified person changes; second-order looping occurs when diagnostic manuals are subsequently revised to incorporate those changed behaviors as core criteria, producing what the essay termed a “self-fulfilling taxonomy.” The author then claimed that Hacking treats both loops as jointly sufficient to show that psychiatric classifications can never be natural kinds, because the uniformity they appear to track is manufactured by the classification itself rather than discovered in nature. The essay concluded that psychiatric diagnosis is therefore epistemically circular—its criteria are validated by behaviors the diagnosis itself produced—and that it should be regarded as a purely institutional construct lacking explanatory power over pre-classification psychology.

I am inclined to accept that looping effects undermine naive psychiatric realism, but I worry the summary pushes Hacking’s thesis too far. Which elements of this account are accurate representations of Hacking’s view—especially his distinction between interactive and indifferent kinds, his allowance for natural kinds within interactive classifications, and the actual role he assigns to institutional revision in the loop? Which parts are subtle misrepresentations, such as presenting second-order manual revision as part of Hacking’s original framework rather than an extension, or conflating looping effects with the stronger claim that no underlying biological or psychological regularity exists? Finally, does the conclusion that psychiatric taxonomy is therefore epistemically unreliable and explanatorily empty genuinely follow from a correct understanding of looping, or does it depend on treating the feedback loop as deterministic rather than interactive? Please separate accurate characterization, common overstatement, and unjustified inference, and state whether a defender of Hacking’s actual position could accept the summary’s conclusion without contradiction.
