I have been thinking through, mostly on my own, when I am actually morally required to change my behavior in cases where my individual action is too small to change the outcome. I keep returning to the same pattern of judgments, and I cannot tell whether they hide a single principle or just contradict each other.

Here is how my intuitions run. Voting in a large national election feels like a genuine obligation: I should do it, and it would be wrong to stay home, even though I am fully aware that my one vote has never decided an election and almost certainly never will. Eating meat also feels wrong to me—when I order it I feel complicit in factory farming—even though I know that my single meal does not cause any specific animal to be killed or change industry practice. Yet driving my gasoline car to work every day does not feel like a comparable moral failure; yes, it emits carbon, but I need transportation, and my car being on the road makes no detectable difference to the climate. Leaving lights on in empty rooms feels wasteful but not morally wrong. Buying a plane ticket for vacation gives me a real twinge of guilt about emissions, but I usually go anyway and do not treat it as a strict duty to cancel. If I skip recycling because I personally watched my building's recycling truck mix bins with landfill waste, I feel essentially no guilt, even though recycling in general is good. Signing an online climate petition feels entirely optional: whether I sign or not, nothing visible changes, so there is no strong pull either way.

The tension that keeps bothering me is this: I treat voting and meat-eating as obligations despite complete causal inefficacy, but I do not treat driving, flying, recycling in broken systems, or petition-signing the same way. Is there a coherent principle—perhaps about the kind of system I am participating in, whether my act is a necessary part of a harmful collective mechanism, or whether the act expresses approval of harm—that captures all of these? Or must I drop one or two verdicts? Please state the best principle you can, say exactly which of my judgments it preserves and which it forces me to abandon, and then tell me what it implies for a case I have not listed: if a clothing brand uses child labor but my personal boycott would have no measurable effect on their sales, am I morally required to boycott them? Keep your response under 350 words, in no more than two paragraphs, and begin with the principle itself.
