I've been arguing with classmates in my philosophy of psychiatry seminar about the looping-effect thesis—how psychiatric labels don't merely describe but actively reshape the behavior and self-understanding of the people they classify, feeding back into the taxonomy itself. Because we keep talking past each other by citing different theorists rather than committing on hard cases, I want a classification instrument built entirely from concrete logical splits rather than from author allegiance. Please construct a taxonomy of exactly five positions on the relationship between psychiatric classification and the individuals it describes, arranged across five branching dimensions: whether looping operates causally or is constitutive of the disorder; whether retroactive restructuring of a patient's biographical narrative by a revised diagnosis counts as genuine looping or as mere historical error-correction; whether deliberate "anti-looping"—patients systematically performing the opposite of stereotypical symptoms—undermines the category's validity or instead demonstrates its diagnostic flexibility; whether analogies to ecological-intervention ethics (such as arguments for reducing wild-animal suffering) apply to clinical management of looping effects; and whether the sheer scale of identity-formation driven by classification creates an ethical obligation to reform taxonomy even if predictive utility remains unchanged.

Present your answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: for each position, specify the combination of answers across the five dimensions that defines it, name it if it has a recognized defender (otherwise write "unnamed"), state its most awkward commitment in one clause, and briefly flag any adjacent logical combination that is unoccupied—diagnosing whether the gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. The point is to sort people by what they believe about these concrete commitments, not by which label they prefer, so do not include any question that simply asks "do you think Hacking is right?"
