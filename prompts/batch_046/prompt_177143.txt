tbh i've been chewing on pain asymbolia lately and i think i've landed on a view that's probably wrong but i can't shake it: the badness of pain isn't in the sensory feel itself, but in whether an aversive binding layer gets activated. in asymbolia people report the hurt is there but don't withdraw, don't suffer, don't treat it as bad—so the nociceptive signal is intact but the suffering-module is offline. that makes me think a being with perfect pain-fidelity but zero affective tagging wouldn't actually be wronged by feeling it, which undermines the idea that pain is intrinsically awful.

what would actually move me off this? i want defeaters ranked from fatal to awkward, not just the usual "but they still call it pain" objection. specifically:
- what intuitons or constructed cases (chronic adaptation, frontotemporal laughter-at-injury scenarios, phantom limb without distress) stand or fall with this separation?
- do results about persistence conditions for suffering, or about whether valence can be reconstructed purely from behavioral dispositions, make the view collapse?
- what would a defender of the "pain-is-bad-just-in-virtue-of-qualia" camp concede counts against them?
- has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability? if every apparently-bad-without-affect case gets explained away as hidden tagging, is that a real problem for a conceptual claim about badness?

also be honest: is this position well-insulated, and is that okay for a claim of this kind? i don't need citations or reading lists, just straight reasoning. (sorry for any typos—wrote this on my phone)
