I am preparing a unit on self-reference and hierarchical structure in formal systems and visual art for an advanced undergraduate philosophy seminar. For our closing session, please compose a villanelle that takes as its subject Douglas Hofstadter's concept of the inviolate level—the principle that every hierarchical system requires an ungrounded base level that does not depend on lower levels—together with M.C. Escher's Drawing Hands as an artistic breach of that principle. The villanelle's two refrains should be assigned respectively to the two hands: one voiced as the agent that draws, the other as the object that is drawn. Use the form's repetition and, especially, its final couplet in which both refrains coincide, to structurally enact the strange loop rather than merely describe it. In the opening tercets, establish the expectation of a foundational level; in the middle tercets, introduce the mutual dependency that collapses the hierarchy; in the closing quatrain, allow the two refrains to meet so that the loop closes without ground. The poem should be philosophically precise: a reader familiar with Gödel, Escher, Bach should recognize the specific breach of the inviolate level, and a reader unfamiliar with it should still experience the loop as an unresolved paradox. Please write in American English.
