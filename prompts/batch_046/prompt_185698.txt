I'm working through a taxonomy of anti-Humean positions for a small reading group that takes the rationalist habit of decomposing philosophical arguments into their underlying gears very seriously. We've been asking whether the familiar dichotomy between Humean regularity theories and anti-Humean necessitarian accounts actually obscures finer distinctions in how each camp handles modal stability, explanatory asymmetry, and the metaphysical status of conservation laws. In particular, we want to evaluate positions not by their overall elegance but by their performance on a specific stress test.

Please rank the following five accounts in tier-list form—S, A, B, C, D—according to how well they resolve what I'm calling the interventionist fragility problem: the worry that if laws are metaphysically necessary or grounded in essential properties, then counterfactual scenarios that violate those laws become conceptually unstable rather than merely physically impossible, which undermines scientific modeling of broken symmetries; meanwhile, pure Humean accounts can treat violations as merely improbable regularities but then struggle to explain why scientists consistently treat law-violating counterfactuals as coherent thought experiments rather than category mistakes. The five positions are: (1) the naive best-system Humeanism weighted by simplicity and strength; (2) dispositional essentialism, which takes fundamental properties as essentially directed; (3) the governing conception of laws as relations of metaphysical necessitation holding between universals; (4) a hybrid modal-structuralist view I've sketched in our notes, on which laws are necessary relative to a contingent modal structure that can itself be locally suspended in counterfactual reasoning; and (5) the anti-Humean powers ontology, which treats laws as derivative from causal powers but denies that those powers entail universal regularities.

For each tier, provide a one- or two-sentence justification focused specifically on interventionist fragility—how the view handles the coherence of law-violating counterfactuals and the modeling of broken symmetries—rather than on general metaphysical virtues. Write in American English, maintain an analytical rather than polemical tone, and do not include bibliographic references, author citations, or suggested readings; I want this evaluation to remain fully self-contained for anyone in the group who cannot search external sources.
