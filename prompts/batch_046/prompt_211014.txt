Two people in my study group got stuck arguing about a guy who ditched his roommate's moving day to sit with another friend mid-panic-attack. One said its a classic particularist case: the reason "keep your promise" doesnt have a fixed weight here—given the irreversibility of the crisis and the dependency involved, that reason literally changes its moral shape or even drops out, not just gets outweighed. The other said thats wrong; particularism still needs the reason to exist so it can be defeated, and what actually happens is the crisis-reason outweighs it, leaving a moral remainder that requires repair—so its generalism with extra steps. The first fired back: if reasons can just "transform" based on relational context, youre not reasoning, youre just telling a story that fits your preference; the second admitted she cant give a decision procedure for spotting real context-shifts vs bias, but said generalism solves bias by flattening real differences—like bailing for convenience vs vulnerability—into one scale. The first said that admission kills particularism, since without a procedure its post-hoc rationalization. They want an outside call. Whos right about whether the promise-reason is outweighed or transformed, is moral remainder actually compatible with particularism, and is lacking a procedure fatal? And what are both missing about how context actually functions in cases like this?
