Write a short dialogue between two philosophers—call them the Archivist and the Steward—about whether rationales can float free of reasoners. The Archivist defends that reasons don't need minds; the Steward says every reason is anchored to some agent's standpoint. Have them work through this made-up case: a glacier holds a self-running algorithm left by an extint civilization that optimizes water distribution, with no users left, just code running on solar power. The Archivist claims its outputs are real reasons for any explorer to follow, not just tools they might borrow; the Steward says they're only descriptive patterns until an agent endorses them, and he should push her on whether she's smuggling in a hidden observer. Make them actually respond: if she says reasonhood is structural like math, he should ask why bare structure obligates anyone; if he insists adoption is necessary, she should ask whether adoption creates the reason or just recieves it. Keep it casual, drop a comma or two, no citations or reading lists.
