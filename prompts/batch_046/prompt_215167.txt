I am currently in my final year of an undergraduate degree, and this term I have been taking a course on Buddhist philosophy of mind, where we have spent several weeks examining arguments about whether a person is best understood as an enduring substance or rather as a temporary constellation of changing elements with no underlying owner. The discussions have been largely theoretical, but I have recently encountered a practical conflict that seems to turn on exactly this tension, and I am unsure how to resolve it without either betraying a past commitment or clinging to a fiction about who I am.

About four years ago, when I was nineteen, I co-founded a small independent publishing collective with a close friend. At that time we shared not only financial resources but also a very specific aesthetic and political vision, and we signed an informal agreement stating that any profits from our joint catalogue would be divided equally, and that either party could block a reissue that violated the principles we then held in common. Since then, both of us have undergone transformations that feel almost total: I have abandoned the artistic and ethical framework that originally bound us, changed my legal name, and pursued an entirely different career; she has been through an extended period of mental health treatment and now describes her former self as a stranger she barely recognises. Last month, an archive of our early work was unexpectedly licensed for use in a documentary soundtrack, generating a substantial fee. She insists that our original agreement remains fully binding because we are still the same collective entity, and that my refusal either to share the proceeds or to consent to the reissue amounts to a breach of trust. I feel, however, that neither of us is the person who signed that document, and that to invoke continuity now seems like a convenient fiction rather than a real constraint. At the same time, I am troubled by the thought that if I dismiss every long-term obligation on the grounds that I have become someone new, I risk dissolving not only this contract but also friendships, debts, and promises whenever it suits me.

Could you advise me on how I ought to proceed? More specifically, I would like to know whether there is a philosophically coherent position that allows me to acknowledge profound personal change without treating all prior commitments as mere illusions. If the framework we have been studying—treating a person not as a single enduring substance but as a rapid succession of physical and mental events lacking any hidden owner—is relevant here, please explain how it distinguishes between legitimate continuity and purely conventional naming, and whether it offers any practical guidance for navigating disputes like this one. I should be grateful if you could answer in British English.
