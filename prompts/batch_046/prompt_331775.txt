Draft the adversarial-collaboration document that a Nomic institutionalist and a Nomic externalist could jointly publish after resolving, as far as possible, their disagreement over recursive constitutional amendment. The institutionalist maintains that a legal system can contain a supreme self-amending rule without paradox, provided one rigorously distinguishes the rule's propositional content from the conditions that confer authority upon it. The externalist maintains that any such distinction eventually collapses, leaving the authority of the self-amending rule covertly dependent on an unarticulated social practice that lies outside the system of rules itself.

Center the collaboration on a specifically constructed hypothetical—the Mirror Amendment, or Article R—which possesses three jointly defining properties: (a) Article R governs the amendment procedure for Article R itself; (b) Article R permits amendment of itself only through unanimous consent of all persons affected by the amendment; and (c) Article R itself defines, within its own text, both the extension of affected persons and the criteria for unanimous consent.

The joint document must be divided into two parts. Part One: a numbered list of agreed claims that both parties can sign without reservation. These should address the formal logic of self-reference in normative systems; the exact scope and limitation of the chess-master analogy familiar from the literature on legal self-amendment; the structural properties of Article R; and the conditions that any successful resolution of the paradox must satisfy. Part Two: a disagreements table. Each row should present a precise formulation of divergence, with one column giving the institutionalist's statement of her position in wording she would accept, and the other column giving the externalist's statement of his position in wording he would accept.

Within Part Two, identify at least two cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would genuinely move the opposing party. For each crux, specify the argument or institutional scenario both sides agree is probative (the Mirror Amendment should serve as the central case), record each side's explicit prediction about what the case demonstrates, and indicate whether either party treats that result as decisive or merely as one weight among others. The cruxes should include whether legal authority can be fully internal to a rule-system, and whether Article R generates a variant of Ross-style paradox applied specifically to amendment conditions.

Conclude with a brief joint assessment: distinguish substantive disagreements from verbal disagreements about terms such as authority or validity; state whether there is any empirical development—such as the observed stability or instability of actual constitutional amendment procedures—that both parties agree should update their respective positions; and confirm that the document contains no bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests. Write in formal, polished prose appropriate for a philosophy of law venue.
