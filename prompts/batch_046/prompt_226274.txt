I'm a referee for these small credibility-staking groups we run -- people pledge future actions to solve coordination problems, and I approve or block them. The concept is Parfit's hitchhiker: sometimes it's rational to bind yourself to do something that'd be irrational later, otherwise nobody helps you now. I need a quick procedure, maybe fifteen minutes, to tell if a pledge is a real case of rational irrationality or just someone locking themselves into a bad deal. It needs to handle emotional payoffs, not just money, and cases where the group dosen't fully know the pledger's future incentives. Give me ordered steps, what counts as passing each, where I reject outright, and where I have to hand it to the group as a values call. Apply it to two cases: first, a member promising to reject a profitable but exploitative contract after the collective bails them out; second, a pledge that looks rational on paper but dumps hidden costs on outsiders and risks breaking long-term trust. Say what the procedure definitely can't catch, too.
