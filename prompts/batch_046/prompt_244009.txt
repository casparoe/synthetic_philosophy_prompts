Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for an advanced philosophy seminar on Davidsonian radical interpretation. The tree must classify positions on how an interpreter should respond when confronted with an agent whose behavior exhibits not occasional error but structural, sustained incoherence — for example, an agent embedded in a practice that systematically rewards avowing beliefs they consistently violate in action, with no hidden coherence detectable.

Design the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of author names. Each internal node should pose a concrete diagnostic question or case on which positions genuinely diverge, answerable without prior knowledge of philosophical labels. The branching criteria must include:

1. Whether the interpreter should first construct a "calibration model" of the agent's own meta-cognitive standards for error — treating this as either a precondition for radical interpretation, an internal revision to the principle of humanity, or an illegitimate psychologizing addition.
2. Whether structural incoherence in one domain warrants global suspension of the principle of charity, local suspension confined to that domain, or reinterpretation as hidden coherence at a deeper intentional level.
3. Whether an agent whose first-order attitudes are unrecoverable without such a calibration layer still qualifies as having propositional attitudes at all, or should be redescribed in purely behavioral or non-intentional terms.

Every leaf must state the position's label (use "unnamed" if no standard name exists) and one sentence identifying its most awkward commitment. Identify any unoccupied nodes in the logical space and briefly diagnose them as incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored. Flag positions that converge on identical paths through all branches rather than forcing false separation. The taxonomy should contain no more than seven leaves, should avoid bibliographic requests, and should be usable by someone familiar with Davidson's basic framework of charity and humanity but without access to external sources.
