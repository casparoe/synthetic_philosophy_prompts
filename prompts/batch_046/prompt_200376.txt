I am a high school senior who hosts an interview series for my school's philosophy club, and next week's guest is a philosopher known for advancing what she calls the "institutional implicature thesis." Rather than treating Gricean speaker meaning as a private psychological intention that the hearer must recover, she argues that implicatures are normative statuses assigned by the interpretive practices of particular institutions—courtrooms, clinical consultations, bureaucratic filings—so that what is implicated depends less on what the speaker privately meant and more on what the institutional framework licenses an audience to take her to have committed herself to. I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the Cooperative Principle or the four maxims. I need at least one question on the classic letter-of-reference scenario—where the writer praises only the candidate's neat handwriting and punctual attendance—to ask how her normative account handles that case without collapsing into either bare literalism or pure speaker psychology, and for each main question I would like the anticipated reply followed by a sharper follow-up keyed to it. I also want a question that presses whether her view can genuinely distinguish implicature from mere institutional convention, with a follow-up that asks what happens when the governing norms of two institutions conflict over the same utterance. Finally, I would like a question about artificial language generation: if speaker meaning is not anchored in a psychological state, does her framework allow an LLM to generate implicatures, and what institutional norms, if any, govern interpretation when there is no human speaker behind the text? The tone should be rigorous but accessible to an audience of advanced high school students, and I do not want any generic biographical questions.
