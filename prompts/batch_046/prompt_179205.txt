Write a short story set in a research institute situated outside conventional spacetime, where physicists study a multiverse in which string theory has never received direct empirical confirmation but is accepted solely through non-empirical arguments—mathematical uniqueness, explanatory coherence, and the absence of viable alternatives. The protagonist should be a philosopher of science working with the team, who begins to observe neighbouring branches of the theoretical landscape in which the very same non-empirical criteria vindicate mutually incompatible descriptions of physical law. Rather than using the multiverse merely as backdrop, let the narrative explore whether non-empirical confirmation can remain rational when it applies with equal force to contradictory realities. Through dialogue and a critical decision faced by the protagonist, show where the boundary lies between empirical inadequacy and philosophical overreach, and allow the characters to discover a subtle asymmetry—perhaps in the constraints on modal reasoning or in the structure of theoretical variation—that either rescues or fatally undermines their original confidence. The story should feel conceptually precise, avoid bibliographies or citations, and conclude with the protagonist confronting a choice that hinges on whether mathematical beauty can still serve as a guide to actual worlds.
