After a recent stretch of unusually vivid dreams that left me genuinely unsure of my state upon waking, I have been treating Zhuangzi's butterfly passage as a live skeptical challenge rather than a poetic image. In the discussions I have encountered, it is read as an Eastern counterpart to Cartesian dream skepticism: since I cannot now rule out being a butterfly that dreams it is human, I lack knowledge of my present condition, and Zhuangzi's admission that he did not know whether he was Zhuang Zhou or the butterfly is taken as a frank concession of global epistemic limitation. Yet I have come across a more specialized interpretive claim — that the passage is not doing epistemology at all, but illustrating wuhua, the transformation of things, in which the instability of identity across states undermines the very framework that makes knowledge-claims possible. My confusion is this: if the passage is genuinely about ontological transformation rather than epistemic ignorance, does that dissolve the skeptical problem, or does it merely relocate it into a question about whether there remains any stable subject to possess knowledge? And in treating the episode primarily as skepticism, am I imposing a Cartesian frame that misses the normative point about the fluidity of the self?
