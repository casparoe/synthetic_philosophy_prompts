I run a seminar on historiography with about 8 grad students, 90 minutes. I want an activity that makes them feel why "legend becomes history" isn't a line you cross but a ratchet that tightens behind you. Use three cases—Trojan War, King Arthur, and the Exodus—but don't let them just vote yes/no. Give each group evidence cards split into material, textual, and social-transmision layers. The hidden mechanic: there's a confidence token that starts low. Every time they promote one case from legend to history, the token jumps and the burdon of proof for the remaining cases gets heavier, but they don't see the scale until round two. Assign roles: one skeptic with a veto, one defender who gets reputation points for saving a case, one archivist who tracks contradictions. They should discover in round three that they're fighting over the standard itself, not the cards. Debrief should map the ratchet onto real debates—like how Schliemann's finds at Troy changed what we demand of Arthur—note what's predictable (they'll defend Arthur longest because the token is highest) and be honest: the game can't reproduce how social identity locks the standard in place, you have to supply that. No lecture up front, don't name the ratchet until after, and keep it runnable with only the cards I hand out—no one should need to look up dates.
