I've ended up convinced by a kind of hybrid prioritarian view: the moral weight of improving someone's life scales with how far they fall below a context-sensitive adequacy threshhold, but once you're above it the weights flatten out completely. So it's prioritarian below the line and roughly egalitarian above it. I think this gets round the leveling-down objection — there's no pressure to drag the well-off down because their weight is already zero — but I'm not sure if it just collapses into sufficientarianism with extra steps. What would actually count against this view? Not just the generic leveling-down-is-counterintuitive line, but which specific cases or formal results show the threshhold idea is unstable, and whether defenders of pure prioritarianism or pure sufficientarianism would see my position as a betrayal rather than a refinement. Also, does it risk some weird discontinuity right at the line? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to awkward.
