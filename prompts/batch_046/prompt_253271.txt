The historical Jesus crowd has been fighting over criterias of authenticity—multiple attestation, dissimilarity, embarrassment, coherence—for decades now, and it feels like nobody ever actually moves anyone. Crossan runs the toolkit and gets a Cynic sage; Meier runs basically the same set and gets an apocalpytic prophet; Wright says the whole framework is busted because it pretends Jesus and the church don't overlap. So here's my question: is this stall even about the texts? I'm wondering if it's actually a disagreement about what kind of reconstruction we're even attempting—are we writing biography, or doing something closer to tradition-history archaeology where mutation is the point? Or maybe the real split is hidden: do people disagree about what counts as a historical claim versus a theological one, and they're just using the same words? Give me a diagnosis, not a verdict. What kind of disagreement is this—verbal, methodological, or about bedrock intuitions? And if it's structural, is there anything that could break it—a formal model of oral transmision, a conceptual distinction between recovering a speech-act vs recovering a life—or should we just accept that the criterias can never settle who he was?
