My friend curates affective-art installations and has rendered these judgments: audiences genuinely enjoy the sorrow of a live staged tragedy; they do not "enjoy" an unedited documentary of real suffering presented purely for information; they enjoy the fear in a horror film because narrative resolution frames it; they do not enjoy a misery memoir read solely for schadenfreude about real people; and they reject as non-aesthetic an immersive VR experience in which one physically feels a character's pain. The proposed new work is "Synthetic Mourning": a non-representational, biometrically adaptive sound installation that induces profound grief calibrated to the listener's physiology, containing no plot, characters, or representational content, experienced in a gallery with full knowledge that it is algorithmic and with the freedom to leave at any moment. Predict whether she will classify this as a genuinely enjoyed tragic experience, identify the implicit rule she is running—psychological distance and control, narrative integration of negative affect, cathartic conversion, moral framing, or something else—state which alternative rules fit her earlier verdicts just as well, and specify the further case that would best distinguish among them.
