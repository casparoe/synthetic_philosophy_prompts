Write a blank-verse poem of about twenty to thirty lines that uses the temporal rhythms of Athenian democratic failure—Mytilene’s rapid reversal, the Sicilian Expedition’s prolonged yet reckless deliberation, and the trial of Socrates as the exclusion of a dissent that outlasted political patience—as its formal principle, not merely its theme. Structure the poem in three sections whose cadences enact these different speeds: clipped, urgent lines for Mytilene; expansive, accumulating syntax for Sicily; and near-static repetition for the trial. Do not resolve whether democracy was ruined by haste, by misapplied patience, or by an inability to hear what exceeds its own tempo; leave the tension between collective decision and individual conscience open. The form itself should make the reader feel the mismatch rather than argue it. Please answer in British English.
