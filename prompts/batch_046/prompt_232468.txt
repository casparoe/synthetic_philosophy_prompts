I'm a grad student in philosophy of biology and I've kept seeing this manouvre in debates about ring species and hybrid zones. Someone insists population A and B are different species because they don't interbreed in nature, but then you point out they actually do interbreed where ranges overlap, and suddenly the reply is that interbreeding was never the real criterion — it's ecological niche separation. Then if you show the niches overlap too, they pivot to phylogenetic clustering, and each individual move sounds defensible, but the overall effect is that the taxanomic judgement they started with never actually gets put at risk. It's not straightforward pluralism, since pluralists usually admit upfront that different contexts call for different tools; here it feels more like rotating between criterions to shield a prior classification. Is there an established term for this defensive rotation? If not, can you coin one and say exactly how it differs from honest pluralism and from ordinary ad hoc rescue?
