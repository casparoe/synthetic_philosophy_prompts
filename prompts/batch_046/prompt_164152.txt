I was arguing with my friend who works in logistics about Hannah Arendt's thing on Eichmann — we watched that old documentary and now we're both kind of obsessed — and I built this argument that feels airtight but lands in a place that can't be right. Here it is step by step: (1) Arendt said Eichmann wasn't some deep-down evil monster with hatred in his heart; he was just thoughtless, going through bureaucratic motions without ever really picturing the actual people he was sending away. (2) She called it the banality of evil, meaning huge harm can come not from wicked intent but from a kind of mental blankness, a failure to really think about what you're doing. (3) So if evil can be banal like that, then having malicious motives isn't actually necessary for serious moral guilt; you can be seriously guilty just for failing to think hard enough about the meaning of your actions. (4) But here's the thing — almost every job inside a big company or government office works exactly like that. People follow rules, hit internal metrics, process forms, and they don't have the time or structural reason to sit around reflecting on the full moral weight of every decision. (5) That means for pretty much anyone in an institution, the conditions Arendt spotted in Eichmann are present: they act without deep moral reflection, guided by procedural rationality instead. (6) So whenever one of these ordinary workers accidentally contributes to harm — like a logistics manager optimizing routes that displace a neighborhood, or a data clerk running a model that denies loans unfairly — they're guilty in the exact same banal way, because they too failed to think. (7) But that can't be right, because then every negligent middle-manager or overworked clerk is morally on par with a bureaucrat organizing mass deportations, and that wipes out the difference between catastrophic evil and an ordinary workplace screwup. I definatly want to keep (1) and (2) because they feel like what Arendt actually meant, but I'm willing to drop (3) if that's the weak link. Which step should I reject, and what's it going to cost me to reject it?
