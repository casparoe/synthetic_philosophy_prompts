Consider H.P. Grice, who died in 1988, suddenly confronted with contemporary large language models that produce fluent conversational outputs despite lacking beliefs, intentions, or any mental life whatsoever. I want you to reconstruct how he might have responded to a specific conceptual tension: these systems routinely generate utterances that carry standard conversational implicatures—for instance, responding to a prompt about a committee's evaluation with the sentence Several members found the proposal adequate, thereby generating a scalar implicature that not all members did so, even though the system has no communicative intention and no awareness of the contrast set. Grice's framework sharply distinguishes speaker meaning, or non-natural meaning, which depends on the recognition of an intention to produce a belief, from natural meaning and from what is said. Would he have maintained that such outputs are genuinely cases of implicature, or would he have classified them as purely natural signs that only acquire implicature-like effects through the interpretive practices of human users?

In particular, address whether his Cooperative Principle, which presupposes rational agents engaging in purposive exchange, could be analogically applied to a predictive model, or whether he would have insisted that without an agent whose intentions are meant to be recognized, there can be no speaker meaning at all—only simulated discourse that misleads interpreters into projecting intentional structure where none exists. Finally, consider whether he might have introduced a new category, perhaps derived implicature, to capture cases where human interlocutors legitimately recover unstated content from system outputs, and whether allowing such a category threatens to collapse the boundary between genuine communication and sophisticated pattern matching. I am especially interested in where he would locate the normative force of the maxims—whether they bind only intentional agents, or whether interpreters can be rationally required to apply cooperative reasoning to any structured output regardless of its origin.
