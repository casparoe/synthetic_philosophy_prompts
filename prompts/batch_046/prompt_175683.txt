I'm putting together a proposal for a small philosophy-of-mind workshop next fall and I keep circling this thing about consciousness being a user illusion—like the desktop on your computer doesn't actually live in the hardware. Most people treat it as one big yes/no question, does the illusion exist, but I'm wondering if we're missing the structure inside it. Specifically, what if the user isn't one centralized observer but a distributed interface that has to maintain coherrence across modular subsystems? I want to propose a six-month independent study asking whether a distributed user-illusion model actually predicts the graded discontinuities we see in severe dissociation and split-brain cases, or whether those cases just prove the illusion breaks entirely.

The precise question: if consciousness is an interface abstraction—similar to Dennett's idea but without assuming a single observer-point—does cognitive fragmentation show multiple incoherent interface instances running in parallel, or does it show a single interface failing? Existing positions don't settle this because the standard illusion-model either dismisses the phenomenology as illusory noise, or it treats modularity as an explanation without asking what modularity means for the interface layer itself. My method would be to develop a conceptual framework borrowing from software virtualization—where virtual machines share resources without a master process—and map it onto predictive proccessing accounts of self-modeling. I want to derive three predictions about graded rather than binary phenomenological discontinuity, then check whether those predictions conflict with or align with what's already documented about cognitive fragmentation.

The deliverable would be a 6000-word paper laying out the distributed-interface framework, the three predictions, and an honest breakdown of where the software analogy stops being useful. Failure means either I can't distinguish this view from standard predictive processing, or the virtualization metaphor collapses into pure analogy with no conceptual leverage. I think one semester is enough if I stay away from solving the hard problem and just reframe the interface architecture. I'll handle citations myself, I just need the framing sharp enough to convince the committee it's not just Dennett with extra steps.
