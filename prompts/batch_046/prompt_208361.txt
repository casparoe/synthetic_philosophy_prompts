I've come around to a pretty strong version of free-floating rationales: not just that reasons can apply to missing agents, but that there are normative reasons for states of affairs that dont depend on anyone — actual or possible — being able to represent or be moved by them. Even in a world with zero minds, the fact that suffering is bad gives a reason to prevent it. That's the view.

What would count against this? I want more than the standard "reasons must guide action" complaint. Specifically, which intuitions about reason-ascription, which commitments about mental representation, and which results elsewhere (say, in metaethics or the philosophy of mind) does it stand or fall with? And be honest about whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability.
