I am a high school student writing a formal philosophical exercise. I understand Newton’s bucket argument in its standard form: a bucket of water suspended by a rope is spun, and eventually the water climbs the sides even though the bucket and water are not changing position relative to each other; Newton takes this to show rotation with respect to absolute space. I want to press your relationalism with a deliberately stripped-down case. Imagine a universe that contains nothing except the bucket, its water, and one distant galaxy separated by otherwise empty space. The bucket spins relative to that galaxy, and the water still climbs the sides, even though there is no local relational change between water and bucket and the gravitational or other influence of the remote galaxy is negligible. If all motion is purely relational, why should the local mechanical behavior of the water—its concave surface—track rotation relative to one remote body rather than tracking only the immediate relations within the bucket itself?
