For a seminar on practical reason I am drafting my philosophical profile on indirect normativity and want a consistency audit before presenting. My commitments are these. (1) I accept strong indirect normativity: the practical reasons an agent should act on are typically mediated by stable, general rules or roles, and she should not apply the direct underlying standard—whether consequentialist, deontological, or aretaic—case-by-case, since such direct application defeats the very point of the mediation. (2) The correct mediating rules are fixed by idealized deliberation that is fully informed, rational, and free from coercive asymmetries, but also non-aggregative: no individual’s standing may be overridden by aggregate benefit, and justification must be acceptable to each party separately rather than summed. (3) For a rule to function as an indirect mediator in actual practice there must be what I term normative transmission loss: in the moment of action the agent should not be able to easily trace the rule back to its direct justification, for if that direct rationale is psychologically available the rule collapses into direct reasoning and loses its mediating function. (4) By contrast, for institutional or constitutional norms I hold that transmission loss should be minimized: citizens ought always to be able to articulate the direct justification of a constraint with little cognitive effort, precisely to prevent ossification and arbitrary authority. (5) I also maintain that idealized deliberation about rules must include complete transparency about the psychological and social mechanisms by which rule-following produces outcomes, since otherwise deliberators are not genuinely endorsing the indirect structure but only its outputs.

Please audit this package: identify which pairs are in genuine tension rather than merely apparent, rank those tensions by severity, and for each specify the least costly revision and what it would sacrifice. Note also any apparent tension that dissolves on closer inspection. Respond in no more than 350 words, arranged in at most two paragraphs.
