I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, but I've been reading in philosophy of physics and keep running into a specific argumentative loop that I can't find a label for. The classic case is Newton's bucket: water in a rotating bucket curves, and the question is whether that curvature is explained by rotation relative to absolute space or relative to distant matter. What interests me is not the general debate but a narrower pattern that keeps recurring whenever people try to resolve it. First, a local dynamical effect—like the water's surface shape or a local gyroscope's precession—is observed. Then one side proposes that the effect is mediated by relations to distant bodies (for the bucket, Mach's appeal to the fixed stars; for a modern analog, frame-dragging near a massive rotating body, where local inertial frames seem coupled to distant mass). The other side then objects that those distant bodies are too remote to exert direct local causal influence, so there must be some intermediate local structure—whether "absolute space" or a local metric field—carrying the effect. The first side replies that this intermediate structure is just a relational network described locally, and the loop repeats. I can see at least two concrete instances—Newton's bucket and gravitational frame-dragging—where the same standoff appears: local inertia versus distant relations versus a local mediator. Is there an established term for this specific loop, distinct from the broad substantivalism-versus-relationism dispute? How does it differ from an ordinary auxiliary-hypothesis problem, since each side's move seems individually defensible but together they make the debate immune to local empirical resolution? If there is no standard name, what would be the right boundaries for coining one—should it cover only rotational inertia, or any case where distant-relational explanations are blocked by locality concerns and replaced by local-field explanations? I am not looking for a reading list; I just want to know whether this carving already exists and where its edges are.
