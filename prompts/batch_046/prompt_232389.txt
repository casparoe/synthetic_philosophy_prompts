Consider the philosophical thought experiment involving Aunt Hillary and the ant colony known as the Ant Fugue, in which distributed, caste-based interactions among ants are proposed as a possible substrate for a single, unified mind rather than merely for collective intelligence. I want the full specification—before judging any particular account—of what a theory of genuine colony-level mindhood must deliver in order to count as attributing one integrated subject of experience and agency to the colony itself, not just functional organization spread across many bodies.

Write the desiderata: list the phenomena, boundary conditions, and conceptual distinctions any adequate theory must accommodate and preserve. These should include, but need not be limited to, the distinction between distributed cognition and integrated subjectivity; the problem of generational turnover versus diachronic personal identity; the absence of a central nervous system and the presence of caste-specific sensory modalities that may lack a common representational format; the conditions under which colony fission creates, destroys, or duplicates a mind; the difference between trophallactic information exchange and phenomenal binding; and the biological constraints from entomology that any attribution cannot violate. For each requirement, state the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps (for example, whether determinism of colony behavior or empirical equivalence with standard entomology is required).

Then identify the single desideratum that does the most work in ruling out popular reductionist and purely functionalist accounts, and flag any pair of requirements you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable given current understanding of ant neurobiology and theories of consciousness. Keep the weight on the criteria and their justification; you may briefly indicate how an informational-integration view and a biological-natural-kind view fare against the list, but do not turn this into a literature review.

Finally, say how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the specific point about which you are least sure.
