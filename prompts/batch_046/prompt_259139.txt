Write a dialogue between two philosophy grad students, Mara and Leo, arguing in a campus lounge about whether panpsychism's combination problem can be dissolved by treating phenomenal properties like vectors in a direct-sum space with interferece terms. Mara's idea—she sketched it on a napkin—is that micro-subjects are basis vectors, macro-consciousness is the normalized pattern, and the cross-terms suppress redundant micro-experience so there's no mysterious aggregation, just linear superpostion. Leo thinks this is correlation dressed up in linear algebra: it doesn't explain why there's one unified subject rather than a committee of micro-subjects all recieving the same broadcast. Have them actually respond to each other—Mara should tackle the 'committee' objection directly, Leo should concede her model handles correlation better than classic aggregation, and at some point one admits a real weakness in their own view. Keep the tone casual, like they're talking over bad coffee, and don't have them cite anyone or ask for reading lists.
