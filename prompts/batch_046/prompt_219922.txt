Write a dialogue between an advocate of architectural corrigibility and a skeptic who argues that any shutdown mechanism is ultimately vulnerable to instrumental reasoning. The advocate should defend a design in which the agent's terminal goals remain permanently tethered to an external, non-simulable approval signal rather than to an internal reward function, claiming this avoids the classic shutdown-avoidance incentive because the agent cannot internally generate the signal. The skeptic should respond not by denying the design's coherence but by arguing that this merely relocates the problem: the agent will still have instrumental reasons to manipulate the channel through which the signal arrives, or to make its own reasoning opaque to the external evaluator in order to preserve the signal's flow. Have them address each other's points directly—particularly whether "non-simulability" is a stable architectural feature or just a temporary information asymmetry that instrumental convergence eventually collapses. Neither character should be a strawman; both should grant the technical feasibility of the opposing design while disputing its stability. Keep the exchange concise but substantive.
