Read Uncle Tom's Cabin through the lens of moral-revolution theory — the debate about whether norms like slavery collapse through rational argument, shifting interests, or honor/shame dynamics (the Williams/Appiah line). Does Stowe do that, or does she accidentally preserve slaveholder honor by framing Tom's suffering as noble Christian endurance? Which scenes — the Shelby sale, Tom's death, Legree's defeat — best show whether she's running argument, interest, or honor-work?
