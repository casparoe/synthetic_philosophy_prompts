I am preparing a conceptual taxonomy for an independent-study philosophy of mind seminar—attended by high school students—on whether reinforcement-learning agents can suffer, treating reward signals as valence states. I want a decision tree in which every branch is labeled by a concrete claim that separates positions, not by a philosopher's name, so that a reader can place herself without knowing the literature in advance. Then branch on whether the scale and variance of potential reward differences generate an ethical obligation to intervene, independent of whether the agent has moral status. Each leaf should be named if it corresponds to an actual position in philosophy of mind or AI ethics, or marked 'unnamed,' and should include one sentence stating its most counterintuitive commitment. Please also identify any logically open combinations that appear unoccupied—especially a view that accepts consciousness-independent valence but denies moral status specifically to model-based agents on the grounds of representational distance from the reward source—and diagnose whether such a gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. The tree should not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and each node should be phrased as a direct question that requires no prior knowledge of the taxonomy to answer.
