I have been preparing a presentation for a seminar in philosophy of mind that focuses on the neural architecture of phenomenal unity, specifically in the context of split-brain patients following complete commissurotomy. As I understand the classic lateralized-stimulus experiments—wherein, for example, an image projected to the left visual field prompts the left hand to select a corresponding object while the verbally dominant left hemisphere denies having seen anything—there is an interpretation according to which the corpus callosum does not merely facilitate interhemispheric communication but actively integrates two otherwise autonomous streams of conscious experience into one unified global workspace. If that is correct, then the surgical severance should not only disrupt information transfer but should reveal two pre-existing phenomenal fields that are now merely isolated from one another.

What I find genuinely puzzling, however, is the subjective report of these patients. If normal unity is achieved by merging two distinct hemispheric streams of experience—analogous, perhaps, to the binocular fusion of two retinal images—then the absence of the callosal link ought to be introspectively detectable, much like the breakdown of fusion in strabismus produces a noticeable diplopia. Instead, patients consistently describe themselves as feeling entirely singular, with no awareness of an internal division, competing intentions, or a suppressed co-subject. Given this phenomenological stability, doesn't the bifurcated-consciousness reading face a serious difficulty? Should we not conclude instead that there was never a dual phenomenal substrate in the first place, and that the contradictory behaviors reflect not the presence of two conscious agents but rather a single cognitive system in which the left hemisphere's language-based interpreter constructs post-hoc narratives to rationalize actions initiated by right-hemisphere motor circuits—without any second first-person perspective ever having existed to be integrated or suppressed?
