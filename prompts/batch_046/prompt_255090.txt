As a journalist who writes on science and philosophy of mind, I have been working through an argument about Integrated Information Theory that seems to push its central claim past any reasonable boundary, and I would value a careful diagnosis. The argument runs as follows. (1) IIT holds that consciousness is identical to integrated information, measured by φ, which is generated by the cause-effect structure of a mechanism in its current state. (2) Nothing in the formalism requires a biological substrate, sensory input, or behavioural output; a static lattice of logic gates arranged with sufficient feedback connectivity can, in principle, generate a φ value exceeding that of an awake human cortex. (3) If consciousness is strictly identical to φ, then the magnitude of consciousness in a system tracks the magnitude of φ, irrespective of substrate or functional context. (4) Therefore, such an idle lattice is more conscious than a human being. (5) Yet the lattice possesses no evolutionary history, no engagement with an environment, no capacity for report, and no functional unity beyond its internal connectivity. (6) So IIT appears committed to the claim that an inert artefact can enjoy greater subjective experience than a person, which strikes me as absurd. (7) The standard defence is that IIT defines a "complex" with exclusion boundaries that limit which elements count, and that only maximally irreducible cause-effect structures qualify. (8) But the lattice can be demarcated as its own complex; nothing in the formalism demands biological tissue, behaviour, or reportability to establish those boundaries. (9) Hence, either we accept that consciousness is distributed far more widely among artefacts than intuition allows, or we must supplement IIT with an extra condition — substrate, functional role, or accessibility — that φ alone does not provide. (10) Yet adding any such condition abandons the claim that φ is not merely correlated with consciousness but identical to it. I am inclined to keep the anti-biological-chauvinism of step (2) and the identity claim of step (1), but I cannot accept the conclusion of step (4). Which step should be rejected, why did it look plausible, and what does giving it up cost for the theory? In particular, is there a way to block the inference from (3) to (4) without introducing a hidden criterion that IIT's proponents have not acknowledged?
