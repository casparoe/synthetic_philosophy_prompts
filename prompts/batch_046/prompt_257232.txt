I'm working with a philosophy of science reading group that has recently turned to anthropic reasoning, and in our discussions one participant repeatedly deploys the maxim that "you ought to reason as though you are randomly drawn from the reference class of all observers" to close debates—using it, for example, to dismiss the Doomsday Argument on the grounds that our early temporal position carries no predictive weight, and to defend the thirder position in the Sleeping Beauty problem. I can see that the maxim is functioning as a black box, but I don't know which precise thesis is being loaded into it. Could you disambiguate the slogan into its distinct possible readings: what kind of "ought" is involved (a normative constraint on rational belief, a descriptive claim about correct probabilistic updating, or a methodological rule for constructing models); what counts as the "reference class" (all actual humans who have lived or will live, all possible conscious beings across worlds, all observers with experiences indistinguishable from mine, or some other set); and what "randomly drawn" means formally (a frequentist sampling assumption, a Bayesian principle of indifference over indexical positions, or something closer to the self-indication principle)? For each reading, please say whether it is uncontroversial, genuinely contested among philosophers and statisticians working on anthropic reasoning, or clearly mistaken, and indicate which—if any—actually support the specific conclusions my interlocutor draws about Doomsday reasoning and Sleeping Beauty.

Please also address how these formulations relate to the well-known "presumptuous philosopher" objection—clarifying whether that objection undermines every version of the maxim or only particular choices of reference class and prior—and, more generally, separate what is relatively settled in the literature on self-sampling versus self-indication assumptions from what remains an open dispute, without presenting any single contested interpretation as the consensus view.
