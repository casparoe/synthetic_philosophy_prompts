One strategy, developed by Sidgwick, claims all pleasures contain an identical agreeable consciousness that varies only in intensity and duration, turning apparent qualitative variety into quantitative variation and preserving axiological uniformity. A second strategy, formulated by Bentham as a functionalist pro-attitude theory, denies any intrinsic feel and defines pleasure as being disposed to want an experience to continue for its own sake, so the common factor is attitudinal and supports uniform valuation regardless of content.
