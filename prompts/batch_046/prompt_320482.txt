I help run an allotment in Leeds and we've got this weird tension over harvest rules. We always said people can take 'a reasonable amount' of tomatoes, which everyone understood fine until one bloke started bringing a kitchen scale and weighing out exactly 400g every time, saying that removes all ambigousness. But then he started weighing different varieties seperately and arguing that 400g of cherry tomatoes is 'the same share' as 400g of beefsteaks because weight is objective, even though everyones else feels that's clearly unfair. The comittee is split: some say he's ruined the spirit of the rule by making it precise, others say precision is exactly what we needed. I can't work out whether the problem is that 'reasonable' was always vague, or whether we've accidentally created a new rule about weight that doesn't capture what we actually care about. What's going on philosophically, and is there a coherent view that lets us keep the flexibility without letting people game it?
