I'm running a philosophy of logic grad seminar, about 10 people, one 90-min session. I want them to actually feel the collapse of an inviolate level—not just nod at Escher's Drawing Hands, but run into the loop themselves.

Design a structured role-play with three roles: a 'constitutional convention' writing Article 1 (the inviolate ground rule), an 'appellate board' applying it, and a 'revision council' that can amend procedure. Give them a concrete dispute—say, whether the convention violated Article 1 by changing voting rules mid-session—to resolve in 30 min. The rules should tangle so that resolving it requires invoking Article 1, but Article 1 excludes itself from revision, while the council's authority comes from Article 2, which Article 1 authorizes. Small stakes: public commitment cards or reputation points, no cash. They should build the strange loop thier own actions before anyone names Hofstadter.

Need clear rules, timeline, debrief mapping each moment onto tangled hierarchy and the inviolate level, an honest note on what can't be reproduced (probably formal Gödel numbering or true infinite ascent), and predictions of failure modes—like one student breaking character or the group voting to paper over the contradiction. What should they come away seeing? That a hierarchy can't stabilize itself when its base is also inside the loop. Keep it casual; a typo or two is fine.
