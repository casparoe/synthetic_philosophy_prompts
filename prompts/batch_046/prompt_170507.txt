I've been attempting to formalize the hinge of history hypothesis — the claim that the present era represents an unusually consequential branching point for civilization's long-run trajectory — within a reference-class forecasting framework that uses path-dependency metrics. The framing I keep returning to treats "hinge-ness" as an observable statistical property: if we are genuinely living through a hinge, shouldn't this century register as an extreme outlier in some distribution of historical decision-density or counterfactual leverage, relative to a uniform sample of prior centuries? But I suspect I am committing a category error, conflating a structural claim about future-sensitive nodes with an empirical claim about retrospective variance in the historical record. Is that the right confusion to correct? And if the thesis is indeed about counterfactual branching rather than about being a statistical outlier in past data, what kind of evidence — short of actually observing which counterfactual futures fail to occur — could even in principle distinguish a true hinge from ordinary epochal noise that merely feels special from the inside?
