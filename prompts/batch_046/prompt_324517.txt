You are an expert in the history and philosophy of early modern science, with particular familiarity with the intersection of celestial mechanics and natural philosophy in the seventeenth century. I am preparing to record an episode of a philosophy podcast in which I will interview a scholar—let us call her Dr. Elena Voss—who defends the view that Johannes Kepler’s writings contain a coherent, mechanistically-informed but genuinely astrological cosmology, not merely a cynical concession to patronage. Before the interview, I need you to think carefully about the actual structure of Kepler’s arguments, especially as they appear in texts like De fundamentis astrologiae certioribus and Tertius Interveniens, and to draft for me a sequence of seven interview questions.

The questions should avoid biographical generalities and should turn on the content of Kepler’s position: his rejection of horoscopic determinism and zodiacal divisions alongside his acceptance of planetary aspects and terrestrial correlations; his claim that celestial influence operates through geometric harmony rather than through occult qualities; the role he assigns to the receptive capacities of the human soul in registering these harmonies; and the tension between this cosmology and his own mathematical laws of planetary motion. For each main question, please include a brief parenthetical note describing the answer Dr. Voss is most likely to give, followed immediately by a sharper follow-up designed to make that reply harder to maintain. At least two questions should directly address the consistency problem: how can someone who derives precise elliptical orbits also maintain that Saturn’s position modulates human temperament? Another should probe whether Kepler’s appeal to the soul’s receptive faculties renders his astrology empirically empty rather than empirically vulnerable.

Finally, conclude the sequence with a question asking what specific empirical result—concerning, for example, the absence of statistical correlation between planetary configurations and personality, or the purely gravitational and tidal mechanisms by which planets physically interact with Earth—would count as genuine counter-evidence to Kepler’s restricted, physical astrology, as opposed to merely refuting the popular astrology he already rejected. Write in a formal, polished tone appropriate for an academic interview, and present the questions in numbered order.
