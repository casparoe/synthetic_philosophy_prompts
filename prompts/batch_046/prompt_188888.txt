I am grading responses for an upper-level philosophy exam on formal systems and cognition. The exam question asked students to distinguish JOOTSing—stepping outside a formal system to alter its rules—from merely exploiting an internal loophole, to explain the role of the MU-puzzle in illustrating undecidability within a system, and to evaluate whether such undecidability supports the stronger claim that human mathematical reasoning involves strange loops that necessarily transcend any formalization.

Here is one student’s answer: 'JOOTSing means leaving a system and changing its rules from outside, like a programmer editing source code, whereas a loophole is just using an existing rule unexpectedly inside the system. Hofstadter introduces the MU-puzzle to show that the string MU cannot be derived within the MIU system’s four rules, and this proves that formal systems are incomplete. Because we can stand outside and see that MU is not derivable, human minds are clearly not formal systems—this is the same lesson as Gödel’s incompleteness theorem. So JOOTSing is exactly what minds do that computers cannot do, and any formal model of cognition fails.'

Please grade this answer. Give me exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion. Item one should state the letter grade and its core justification. The other items should separate the accurate parts—such as the contrast between meta-level exit and internal manipulation—from the subtle errors, particularly the conflation of in-system undecidability with a proof that cognition is non-formal, and the misattribution of Hofstadter’s purpose in introducing the puzzle. The final item should specify one precise conceptual clarification—perhaps regarding strange loops or self-reference—that would repair the argument without altering its basic structure.
