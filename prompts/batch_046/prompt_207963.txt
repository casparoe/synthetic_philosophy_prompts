Design a final-examination essay question, with a model answer of approximately 750 words and a detailed marking scheme, for an upper-level philosophy of social-science seminar. The topic is whether culture can genuinely function as a cause, using Weber’s Protestant Ethic thesis as the focal case, but the question must be framed so that students cannot earn high marks by summarizing the familiar claim that ascetic Protestantism fostered capitalist rationalization. Instead, require them to confront the philosophical problem of explaining with values: religious and ethical values are not spatiotemporally localized mechanisms like physical forces, yet Weber insists they have historical efficacy through elective affinity (Wahlverwandtschaft) rather than through deterministic causation or mere ideological reflection. To keep the task resistant to generic pretraining responses, build the exam around the tension between hermeneutic adequacy—grasping the subjective meaning that makes action intelligible—and causal adequacy—establishing that a value actually made a difference to an outcome. Ask students to articulate why treating elective affinity as either strict determinism or as pure post-hoc correlation misreads Weber, and to evaluate whether Weber’s distinction between adequate causation and accidental causation resolves the problem of value-based explanation or merely relocates it. Further require them to apply a precise conceptual distinction—between causal production (values operating as mechanisms) and causal selection (values structuring the field within which mechanisms operate)—to show whether values can be causally relevant without being causally productive in the mechanistic sense. The marking scheme should allocate 25 marks across five must-hit analytical moves: (1) correctly characterizing the logical form of elective affinity as non-deterministic and non-necessary; (2) explaining why standard counterfactual dependence struggles with explanations that cite meanings and values; (3) distinguishing hermeneutic adequacy from causal adequacy and showing why Weber needs both; (4) diagnosing the determinism-versus-correlation misreading and explaining why each fails; and (5) delivering a critical verdict on whether Weber succeeds, with attention to the cost of accepting causal selection without causal production. For each move, specify the marks available and the specific misconception or omission that typically costs them. Include a brief note separating an A answer—which recognizes that values can shape outcomes without functioning like physical mechanisms, and explicitly states the philosophical price of that view—from a B answer—which describes Weber’s thesis accurately but collapses elective affinity into hidden determinism or into epiphenomenal correlation. The question should be answerable in about 75 minutes. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, citations, page-specific references, or lists of secondary authors; the evaluation must depend entirely on conceptual analysis of the problem itself.
