As a novelist, I think about authenticity in terms of authorial voice, manuscript provenance, intertextuality, pastiche, and the reader's implicit contract with the text rather than in metaphysical terms. Restate the philosophical debate about whether an original artwork holds value that a perfect, undetectable forgery necessarily lacks—specifically the dispute between causal-historical theories of artistic value and those reducing value to perceptible form—entirely within that craft vocabulary. For each side, give the precise translation, then deliver the residue report: which philosophical claims map cleanly onto distinctions you actually draw when writing or editing, which require distortion to fit, and which have no counterpart at all in craft practice. In particular, examine whether the appeal to causal origin survives translation into anything beyond biographical knowledge of the author, and whether the claim that value is strictly intrinsic to the object can be expressed without smuggling in a metaphysics of texts foreign to editorial judgment. Conclude by saying whether the untranslatable residue reveals something the philosophical account neglects—perhaps the reader's constructed narrative of origin—or instead marks a hard limit of craft vocabulary itself.
