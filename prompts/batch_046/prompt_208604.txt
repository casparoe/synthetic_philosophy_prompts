2. The best reason we praise heroic acts is that they involve serious personal costs that morality never demanded—call this the cost-bearing view.
3. At the same time, praise should track motives: if someone saves a stranger mainly for fame, it's not genuinely supererogatory even if the cost is huge.

5. I also think a group can do something collectively supererogatory—say, neighbours pooling resources to rescue a stranger—when no individual member exceeds their personal duty, as long as the pooled burden is well beyond what any one person had to contribute.
