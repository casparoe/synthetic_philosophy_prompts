I'm writing a dissertation chapter on non-classical metaphysics in the Indian traditions. Please argue for the following interpretive claim: Jain anekāntavāda is not primarily a doctrine of epistemic humility or inter-religious tolerance, but a metaphysical thesis about the irreducible, multi-aspected complexity of every substance (dravya), according to which any single predication necessarily abstracts away genuinely instantiated properties. Defend the view that this metaphysical reconstruction allows anekāntavāda to avoid the standard logical objection—that simultaneously attributing contradictory properties violates the law of non-contradiction—by treating contradiction as an artifact of partial description rather than ontological conflict. At the same time, argue that this reading undermines the traditional deployment of syādvāda as a framework for inter-doctrinal negotiation, and assess whether that loss is a theoretical cost or a clarification of the doctrine's original scope. Please distinguish clearly between claims that are well-established in Jain philosophical scholarship and those that remain genuinely contested, and do not present any disputed interpretation as the consensus view.
