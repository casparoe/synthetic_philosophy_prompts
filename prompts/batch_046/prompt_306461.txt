In a debate about hedonism's heterogeneity problem — that the pleasures of eating a ripe peach, following a proof, and hearing a fugue seem to share no common phenomenal quality — my interlocutor kept repeating: "Pleasure is pleasure; it's the same coin in different pockets." He used it to dismiss the objection outright, as if asserting a shared currency proved there was a shared nature worth valuing. I need to take this apart before I reply. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses the slogan could be expressing — tautological, phenomenological, intentional, normative, and metaphysical — and for each say whether it is defensible, false, or question-begging in this context? In particular, distinguish the claim that all pleasures contain an identical qualitative ingredient from the claim that they are all valued under the same concept, and say whether either actually answers the heterogeneity worry or merely renames it. Also indicate which interpretations defenders of hedonism usually intend versus which ones actually do the argumentative work. Please separate what is broadly settled about the variability of pleasure from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers who work on value theory, and do not present any disputed reading as if it were the consensus.
