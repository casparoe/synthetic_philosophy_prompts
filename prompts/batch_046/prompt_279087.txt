I am currently drafting the conceptual framework for my dissertation on artificial cognition, and a disagreement in our interdisciplinary seminar has become personally urgent: I need to determine whether large language models are best understood as possessing a minimal form of instrumental agency, as mere simulators of agency, or as something that genuinely escapes both categories. On one side are colleagues who maintain that LLMs instantiate goal-stable representational structures that are updated through interaction and that explain multi-turn behavior more parsimoniously than pure pattern-matching; on the other side are colleagues who contend that every apparently rational feature is better explained as epistemic ventriloquism—the projection onto outputs of the rational architecture embedded in training texts, absent any internal commitment structure of the model's own. I want the joint statement these two camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration, structured precisely as follows: first, a numbered list of agreed claims about LLM architecture, observed behavioral regularities, and the current limits of mechanistic interpretability; second, a disagreements table stating each position in wording its own proponents would endorse, including whether a third category beyond agency and simulation remains live; third, the cruxes, which I take to be whether counterfactual robustness across extended reasoning distinguishes genuine agency from high-fidelity simulation, and whether indexical anchoring—the capacity to tie representations to one's own temporal and contextual position—is a necessary condition for agency that LLMs necessarily fail to meet; fourth, for each crux, one concrete empirical case or argument both sides agree is relevant, with each side's explicit prediction about what the case demonstrates; and fifth, an honest note on which agreed claims are most vulnerable to being overturned by near-term empirical work. Please also conclude by stating what specific finding or conceptual argument would cause you to revise your framing of one of these cruxes, and identify the precise point in your reconstruction where misrepresentation of either camp is most likely.
