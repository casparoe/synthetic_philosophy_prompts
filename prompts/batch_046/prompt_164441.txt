For my A-level philosophy coursework on reproductive ethics, please construct a thought experiment that meets the following precise specifications. The scenario should be realistic — situated in a standard clinical IVF context with no science fiction, no perfect predictors, and no exotic physics — and presented in a single coherent paragraph.

The case must involve a couple who, because of a documented uterine condition, can safely carry exactly one pregnancy to term and never more. Through routine preimplantation genetic screening, they have access to two embryos: Embryo S, predicted to develop into a life of chronic but not unbearable suffering with welfare just barely above the neutral point; and Embryo F, predicted to develop into a life of clear flourishing with welfare well above that point. They must also retain the genuine option of implanting neither embryo.

Engineer the case so that the standard person-affecting procreation asymmetry and an impersonal totalist account deliver opposite verdicts on the very same reproductive choice, and ensure that the divergence stems specifically from the asymmetry itself rather than from collateral maternal risk, rights claims, or resource allocation. Specifically, the asymmetry view should hold that replacing non-existence with Embryo F is morally neutral or not required, whilst replacing Embryo S with Embryo F is strongly required or clearly permissible; the totalist should hold that creating Embryo F is an unalloyed moral good regardless of whether Embryo S or mere non-existence is replaced.

After presenting the scenario, set out the reasoning for each theory and state its explicit verdict. Then identify the precise structural feature that forces the asymmetry view to treat the replacement of an existing suffering life differently from the replacement of absence, and demonstrate that removing this feature — for example, if only one embryo were available — collapses the disagreement into a familiar non-identity problem. Finally, explain how a defender of the asymmetry might attempt to undermine the case by challenging the welfare predictions for embryos, and clarify why that response fails to address the structural point.

Please write in formal, polished British English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for further sources.
