Write a final-exam essay prompt, together with a model answer and a detailed marking scheme, for an upper-level undergraduate epistemology seminar (90 minutes, 2,000 words, 30 marks). The prompt must address the Meno problem—why knowledge is more valuable than mere true belief—but must avoid being answerable by summarising Plato’s dialogue or reciting standard textbook distinctions. Instead, frame the problem through a niche hypothetical: imagine a cognitive system that produces true beliefs by tracking environmental regularities, yet does so via a mechanism that is modally fragile (it yields false beliefs in nearby possible worlds where background conditions differ slightly). Ask the examinee to determine whether beliefs formed by this mechanism constitute knowledge, and whether—if they do or do not—their value exceeds that of an equally true belief formed by a robust, counterfactual-sensitive process. The model answer should make the following specific moves and no others should be treated as sufficient: (a) precisely state the value gap as the problem of explaining why knowledge is not merely instrumentally but also non-instrumentally superior to true belief; (b) apply the modal-fragility scenario to show that reliability in actual conditions is insufficient for knowledge, distinguishing this from Gettier-style luck without merely naming Gettier cases; (c) evaluate whether the extra value, if present, derives from modal safety or from a virtue-theoretic credit for cognitive achievement; (d) consider and rebut the pragmatist suggestion that knowledge is valuable only because it is more stable or useful, arguing whether stability can be reduced to instrumental value; and (e) conclude with a defensible position on whether the value gap is real or collapses into a distinction between lucky and non-lucky true belief. The marking scheme should allocate marks as follows: 5 marks for (a), 7 marks for (b), 7 marks for (c), 6 marks for (d), and 5 marks for (e), with deductions of 2 marks each for: conflating the value problem with justification problems; failing to engage the hypothetical mechanism; offering bibliographic references, reading lists, or citation lists; or writing in American English. The entire response should be in British English and must contain no verbatim quotations from named authors.
