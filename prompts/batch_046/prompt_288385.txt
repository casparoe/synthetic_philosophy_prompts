As a novelist preparing a companion interview for a podcast about my forthcoming historical novel set in the twilight of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, I wish to speak with an unnamed historian who advocates applying the outside view—reference classes, base rates, and structural forecasting—to imperial dissolution rather than to narrative causation. Please draft eight to ten formal interview questions that press well beyond a simple exposition of the method. I require at least one question addressing how she constructs the reference class when distinguishing empires that collapse from internal fiscal strain from those destroyed by external conquest; at least one confronting the tension between her base-rate predictions and the kind of singular, contingent decision that drives novelistic plot (for instance, the choice of one archduke to visit a provincial capital); and at least one probing her concept of "narrative residue"—those qualitative, apparently non-quantifiable details she acknowledges but treats as epistemically disposable once the forecast is fixed. For each of these three, include a follow-up calibrated to her most probable reply: that reference classes must be defined by institutional structure rather than geography, that singular events are statistical noise around the base rate, and that narrative residue is a luxury historians can indulge only after the probabilistic judgment has been made. The remaining questions should explore how she responds to the objection that every empire is sui generis, whether base rates can be updated meaningfully without collapsing into the inside view, and what she considers the minimal evidentiary threshold for assigning an empire to a particular class. Maintain a polished, literary tone throughout; avoid generic biographical inquiries, and do not request bibliographies, citations, or lists of further reading. Do not invoke any specific statistical study or dataset whose details would require external verification.
