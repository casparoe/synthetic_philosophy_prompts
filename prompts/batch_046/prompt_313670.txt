I run a philosophy of politics workshop, about 9 people, 75 minutes. I want them to feel the dirty hands problem in a way that isn't just "bad means for good ends." I want them to run into the weirder version: where being honest about what you're doing actually destroys the good you're trying to save, so transparency itself becomes the enemy.

Design a role-play or structured vote. Set it up: they're an emergency council deciding on a water-rationing plan that only works if people think they chose it freely — it's built on behavioral nudges, and if citizens learn they're being nudged, compliance collapses and the city runs dry in 48 hours. They have to vote in two seperate rounds: first in secret, then sign a public statement explaining the decision. There's a built-in audit rule in the fictional charter: if their private vote and public justification match truely exactly, the plan is automatically suspended for review.

The rules should make consistency self-defeating — they either lie in public or kill the policy. Debrief should map that moment onto the idea that dirty hands leaves a moral remainder you can't wash away just by having good intentions. Also tell me what's predictably gonna go wrong (someone will try to invent a magical third option that doesn't exist) and be honest about what feature this misses — probably the long-term corrosion of democratic trust even when nobody finds out.

Casual is fine, don't need citations or reading lists, and please avoid asking for quotes.
