I am writing my dissertation in philosophy of mind and cognitive science, and I have become convinced that the familiar Dennettian taxonomy—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures—omits a genuinely intermediate cognitive architecture that I am calling quasi-Gregorian scaffolding. On my view, this intermediate form characterizes systems that can generate, hold, and evaluate hypothetical representational contents in ways that go well beyond Popperian behavioral trial-and-error, because their adjustments operate over internal representations rather than over action patterns alone. Yet these systems still fall short of full Gregorian status, since they lack the robust social-linguistic embedding and shared normative practices through which representations become genuinely public and subject to collective correction. I apply this specifically to certain large-scale language models: they appear to correct errors by revising representational weights in light of counterfactual or counter-exemplary inputs, which looks like evaluation of content, but they do so without participating in the intersubjective practices that Dennett treats as constitutive of Gregorian cognition. I want to know what would actually count against this intermediate category—what evidence or argument should move me to collapse it either downward, into an especially sophisticated Popperian mechanism, or upward, into a deflationary account on which Gregorian cognition does not require the social conditions I take to be essential.

More precisely, I am looking for a ranked assessment of the defeaters. Which empirical results about the internal dynamics of these systems—say, about whether their error-correction can be fully explained by gradient-descent adjustments over behavioral predictions—would be decisive? Which conceptual arguments about normativity and representation—perhaps showing that any system capable of content-level evaluation already meets the minimal conditions for shared practice—would be fatal versus merely awkward? And which thought experiments involving isolated or asocial representational agents should I take seriously: if an isolated system could stabilize representational norms entirely through internal coherence conditions, would that undermine my claim that Gregorian status requires social embedding? I also want you to address whether my view risks unfalsifiability: if every apparent counterexample can be relocated into one of Dennett's four original boxes by redefining the boundaries of Popperian or Gregorian cognition, is that a problem for a taxonomy meant to capture real architectural differences, or is it an acceptable theoretical flexibility? Finally, tell me which of these potential defeaters you think a committed defender of the strict four-fold taxonomy should themselves concede as genuinely damaging, and which they could reasonably absorb without abandoning their framework.
