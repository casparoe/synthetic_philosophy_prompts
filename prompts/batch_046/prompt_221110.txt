Design a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on Hofstadter's concept of jumping out of the system (JOOTSing), using the MU-puzzle in the formal system MIU as the central case. The tree must be built from logical combinations of answers rather than from a pre-existing list of named theories. Construct exactly four diagnostic nodes, each presenting a concrete scenario or claim that respondents can evaluate without knowing philosophical labels: (1) whether an exhaustive computer enumeration of all MIU derivations that reports the absence of MU performs the same recognition-act as a human observer who sees the absence; (2) whether adding a formal reflection rule to MIU that lets the system state its own non-derivability resolves the puzzle or merely relocates it to a meta-level; (3) whether the capacity to recognize truths lying outside a formal system applies specifically to arithmetic-level structures or extends generally to any sufficiently complex rule system; and (4) whether a human who grasps the MU result is best understood as applying an implicit meta-rule, as accessing an irreducible semantic perspective, or as running a larger but still fully formal neural computation. Wherever the answers of two leaves coincide, flag the overlap rather than forcing a false split. The audience should be readers familiar with basic logic and Gödel's incompleteness theorems but without specialist background in philosophy of mathematics. Keep the tree to exactly four branching nodes and six leaves, label each branch by its separating question, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. At the end, state how confident you are in the taxonomy's ability to sort these positions correctly, and identify the single branch or distinction about which you are least certain.
