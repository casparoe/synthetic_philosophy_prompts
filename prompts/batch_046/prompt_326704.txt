Write a decision tree for the debate over whether knowledge is closed under competent deduction given Nozick’s tracking conditions (sensitivity and adherence). Root node: “I accept that S knows p and competently deduces q from p, but I deny that S thereby knows q in at least some cases.” First branch: does tracking fail across deduction because the counterfactual worlds in which q is false are not among the closest worlds where S uses this method (tracking-failure view), or does closure hold because adherence alone is preserved and sensitivity is either relativized to the method or dropped (closure-preservation view)? Second branch, applied to both sides: is the failure or preservation localized to skeptical scenarios (e.g., knowing one has hands does not entail knowing one is not a handless brain in a vat) or does it extend to mundane inferences? Include a leaf for a hybrid I will call “modal-contextual tracking”: closure holds within a fixed modal context, but collapses when the deduction crosses a boundary where an error possibility becomes modally proximal, and specify what this commits its defender to about the metaphysics of modal distance. For every leaf, provide the position’s name (or a descriptive label if unnamed) and one sentence on its most awkward consequence for ordinary knowledge attributions. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate, and phrase every branch as a concrete question answerable without prior knowledge of the taxonomy.
