Write the desiderata that a realist theory of representations in neural networks must meet to be adequate, with the conceptual tension or motivating case behind each. The theory must account for distributed and polysemantic representations—including features compressed into overlapping directions—distinguish representational vehicles from mere statistical correlations, and explain how content is fixed in deterministic feedforward architectures without explicit error-correction mechanisms. For each requirement, indicate whether it is non-negotiable (for example, consistency with linear-probe recoverability and targeted-ablation effects) or camp-specific (for example, requiring localized single-unit encoding, geometric isomorphism with task structure, or a network-internal consumer for decoding). Explicitly state whether any subset—such as demanding both fully localized content and complete distributed superposition—is jointly unsatisfiable, and identify which desideratum does the most work in excluding candidates. Separate settled points (for instance, that useful features are distributed) from genuinely contested ones (for instance, whether interventionist counterfactual dependence alone fixes semantic content, or whether representation requires a functional role analogous to a biological consumer). Do not present a contested interpretability position as the consensus view, and keep the weight on the criteria rather than on grading particular frameworks.
