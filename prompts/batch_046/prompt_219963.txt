I'm preparing an argument for my philosophy independent study, and I want to test its exposure before I present it. The position I am defending is this: in Montaigne's essay comparing the Brazilian group he describes with European religious violence, he does not endorse cultural relativism. Instead, he appeals to a single universal standard of natural justice—one I reconstruct as a form of phenomenological neutrality. On this reading, judgment is suspended not because moral frameworks are incommensurable, but because any positive evaluation necessarily imports the observer's embodied habits of disgust and pride. Montaigne is therefore not a relativist but a skeptical universalist, committed to a negative standard (the absence of cruelty rooted in self-conceit) rather than to any positive cultural norm.

What would have to be true for this reading to be wrong? I want the defeaters sorted honestly: which interpretive moves in the text would need to be read differently, which assumptions about Montaigne's debt to Stoic cosmopolitanism would have to be correct instead, and what developments in comparative ethics or anthropology—perhaps about the incommensurability of moral frameworks, or about the cultural specificity of disgust—would undermine the universal standard I attribute to him? Which of these would be decisive, and which merely awkward?

I also want to know whether the view is genuinely at risk or has been qualified into unfalsifiability. If I treat every apparent counterexample as just another instance of embodied bias smuggled into judgment, have I insulated the position? If so, is that insulation a fatal problem for a skeptical universalist view, or only an awkward feature?

Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. For each, indicate whether a committed defender of this reading would concede damage or dismiss it, and what revision would be least costly to absorb. Finally, say what this view predicts that its rivals—whether a straightforward relativist reading or a Stoic universalist one—do not, and whether that predictive exposure is real or only apparent.
