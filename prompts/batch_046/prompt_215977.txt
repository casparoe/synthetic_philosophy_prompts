Explain whether honoring sunk costs is always irrational, but do so specifically through the lens of sequential self-interaction: treat the agent not as a single atemporal optimizer but as a chain of decision nodes that observe one another's behavior and update beliefs about the agent's commitment type. In particular, address whether continuing a failing project can serve as a credible signal to one's future selves (or to other agents) that strengthens resolve in structurally similar future dilemmas, and whether this rehabilitates sunk-cost reasoning or merely collapses it into standard strategic commitment. Take a committed position—do not just lay out both sides—and defend it against the objection that any such signaling value is already captured by ex ante contracting or reputation mechanisms, leaving no independent rational space for honoring sunk costs.
