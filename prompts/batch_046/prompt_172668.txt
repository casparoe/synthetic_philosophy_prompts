I keep noticing this loop in policing debates about reform vs defund vs abolish, and I dont know if its named. Someone argues the state's monopoly on violence is illegitimate because its enforced selectively along race and class lines. The defender replies that the monopoly is just a legal principle -- only the state can authorize force, regardless of enforcement patterns. Then the critic says fine, if its just a legal principle we can keep it and replace police with unarmed community mechanisms. At that point the defender jumps back and says those mechanisms cant maintain order without actual coercive capacity, so we're back to talking about who holds force in practice. Its like the idea of 'monopoly' slides between being a normative claim about legitimacy and a descriptive claim about practical coercive power, and arguers flip which face is active depending on which is easier to defend. I've seen this in discussions of Weber and in abolitionist writing but I cant tell if there's a specific term for the slide itself. Its not quite motte-and-bailey bc both versions are contested, and its not plain equivocation bc the two meanings are genuinely related. Is there an established concept for this oscillation, and if not can you coin one with clear boundary conditions -- like how to tell it apart from just changing the subject?
