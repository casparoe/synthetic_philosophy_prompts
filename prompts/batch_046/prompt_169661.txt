I'm serving on the board of a neighborhood cooperative that runs a shared garden. Last year we received a grant that paid for both a new composting hub and an upgraded irrigation network; since then, member retention has improved sharply. At our next meeting, one director proposes to establish whether the composting caused the improvement by comparing our retention rates with those of a demographically similar cooperative that received no grant. Another director proposes a three-month trial in which we lock the compost bins but leave everything else running, predicting that retention will fall if composting is the true cause. A third director objects that locking the bins would breach the grant conditions, automatically triggering the removal of the irrigation equipment, and that the comparison cooperative is not truly comparable because grant eligibility required our neighborhood to have previously failed a soil-quality audit, which itself affected retention. I feel the second director's trial is conceptually defective in a way the first director's comparison avoids, yet the third director's objections seem to undermine both approaches. How should I think about which method genuinely isolates whether the composting hub caused the improvement?

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then provide the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating the question.
