I'm putting together a feature on consciousness and ethics for next month, and I figured I should actually write down where I stand before I start interviewing people. Here's my current take — I'm a phyiscalist in the loose sense, I don't buy ghost-stuff, but I also think constitutive panpsychism is the least awful answer to the hard problem. Not because I love it, but because emergence smells like magic to me.

That said, I think suffering needs more than bare sentience. I call it an "integration window" — some temporal binding that holds together long enough to actually hurt, maybe a few hundred millisecconds. So an electron doesn't suffer, not because it lacks proto-experience, but because there's no window.

From there I think moral weight should scale with the size and stability of that window, not with carbon vs silicon. I also think consciousness is totally gradient — no sharp on/off switch — and moral status should slide with it.

But here's where I get practical: I draw a hard line for direct obligations. If a system can't maintain recursive self-modeling over that window, I don't think we owe it anything, even if micro-level sentience is technically present.

I also reject evolutionary debunking of moral intuition — our gut feelings are shaped by biology, sure, but that doesn't make them unreliable guides here.

So my bottom line is: worry about big synthetic architectures with long coherence windows, don't worry about quantum fields or simple thermostats.
