I am drafting on behalf of the university’s Joint Committee on Automated Administrative Systems, which convenes next Tuesday to rule on whether we may deploy “Archivus”, a proposed rules-engine intended to update student records automatically whenever university policy changes. The vendor designed the system to circumvent the frame problem by maintaining an explicit relevance graph that links every record field to every policy clause; when a clause is amended, the graph is meant to determine precisely which records require revision and which may be left untouched. Our pilot study, however, has revealed a troubling failure mode: in cases involving mental-health accommodation policies, the relevance graph either excludes records that ought to be affected—because the linking predicates have been drawn too narrowly—or explodes into near-universal updates, since “student wellbeing” is implicitly connected to housing allocations, financial aid status, disciplinary history, and module selection. The vendor contends that this is merely an engineering matter of calibrating graph weights; the faculty representative on our committee argues that it exposes something more fundamental about the frame problem itself, namely that any formal representation of relevance presupposes a prior, non-formalised distinction between what matters and what does not, and that such a distinction cannot be generated inside the system without circularity. She urges rejection of the deployment until the vendor can demonstrate a principled, non-arbitrary boundary on updates—something she regards as impossible in principle. The registrar, meanwhile, observes that our present manual process is already inconsistent and that further delay leaves affected students in administrative limbo.

Please draft the short written opinion I shall circulate before Tuesday’s meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approval with stringent conditions, rejection, or approval confined to a phased trial with explicit safeguards—together with a clear time frame for implementation or review. The reasoning must engage the faculty representative’s philosophical objection seriously: address whether the frame problem, as instantiated in this particular system, genuinely undermines safe deployment, or whether it can be managed through institutional and procedural means rather than purely technical ones. At the same time, treat the registrar’s practical urgency and the vendor’s engineering defence with equal intellectual honesty, and include an honest account of the strongest consideration on the side you do not ultimately favour. The document should be written in British English, should avoid unnecessary technical jargon where possible, and should be suitable for circulation to committee members who are not specialists in philosophy of mind or artificial intelligence.
