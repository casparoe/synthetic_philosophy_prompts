okay so i'm trying to figure out whether 'better than' is actually transitive, going by gut feeling on cases like they do in philosophy seminars. here's my verdicts:

first, the easy chain. world A has a billion people with lives barely worth living, world B is the same but everyone gets a tiny bit more pleasure, world C is B plus another tiny bit. B is better than A, C is better than B, and C is clearly better than A too. that one feels totally fine, no problem.

then there's this biotech gradient i keep thinking about. imagine gradually replacing painful neural pathways with neutral-bufferd ones that don't register pleasure or pain, step by step from fully suffering (S0) to fully buffered (S10). each adjacent pair feels like a small improvement - less suffering is better. so S1 > S0, S2 > S1, up to S10 > S9. but comparing S10 to S0: S10 has zero suffering but also a flattened emotional landscape. i actually think S0 might be better than S10, or at least they're incommensrable, even though the chain says S10 should be way better. so transitivity breaks at the ends.

then art. painting P1 is pleasant but shallow. P2 is slightly more resonant. P3 is maximally resonant, almost overwhelming. i think P2 > P1 and P3 > P2, since more depth is always a plus. but P3 is so intense it feels worse than P1 - not just 'more good thing' but a different kind of thing. so P3 is not better than P1.

and the case i keep flip-floppping on: three stances on engineered consciousness. stance A allows suffering but keeps full autonomy and emotional range. B eliminates suffering via biotech but keeps some autonomy. C eliminates both suffering and autonomy, leaving only mild contentment. i think B > A and B > C, since autonomy plus no suffering is best. but comparing A and C: sometimes i think A is better because real experience matters, other times C is better because suffering is the worst thing possible. i genuinely can't decide.

so what's the principle? does transitivity hold inside one dimension but fail when dimensions cross? or do i have to drop one of these verdicts? and specifically for that A/B/C case - if B is best, does that tell us anything about how to rank A vs C?
