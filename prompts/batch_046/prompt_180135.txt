I've been thinking about how European jurisdictions ban commercial surrogacy and paid egg donation — usually saying its contrary to human dignity or an unacceptable commodification — but those same countries let people sell plasma, do paid clinical trials, and take risky jobs for money. Meanwhile the US runs it like a market with agencies and contracts. What philosophical view of reproductive labour does that selective prohibition actually presuppose? Is it a consistent Kantian idea that gestation is inalienable in a way other bodily risks arent, or does it rely on a gendered assumption that carrying a child is uniquely tied to identity? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether the practice genuinely needs it or could survive under a libertarian or contractualist frame, and tell me what parts of the European institutional design — screening rules, the altruistic exception, enforcement — would have to change if we replaced the dignity-argument with a straightforward consent-and-compensation model.
