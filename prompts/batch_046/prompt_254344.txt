You serve as the senior compliance officer for a synthetic-biology oversight board that receives engineered-organism proposals continuously, with a mandatory 72-hour turnaround and no philosophers on staff. Your board applies a working principle from the philosophy of biology: DNA can be understood either as a causal chemical polymer or as a semantic code, but regulatory obligations should expand only when a proposal relies on semantic closure — defined here as the condition that a nucleotide sequence performs its functional role not through its direct chemical properties but through an independent interpretive system (cellular machinery, engineered decoder, or algorithmic reader) that assigns meaning to the sequence. You need a reusable decision procedure that sorts each proposal into either the Chemical-Synthesis Oversight Track (CSOT) alone, or CSOT together with the Informational-Design Oversight Track (IDOT), which triggers audit of algorithmic intent, data provenance, and interpretive risk. The procedure must be executable by staff with biology and compliance training but no metaphysics background. Specify the ordered diagnostic questions, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, the exact stopping points where the classification is CSOT-only or CSOT-plus-IDOT, and the precise junctures where the question is irreducibly normative and must be escalated to the board ethics sign-off officer rather than resolved by fact. The procedure must explicitly handle hard boundary cases: proposals that use algorithmic gene-assembly platforms but target purely metabolic outputs; proposals that embed synthetic sequences readable only by a custom sequencer with no phenotypic effect; and proposals that edit regulatory regions whose function is context-dependent on an engineered interpreter. Then apply the procedure to three concrete proposals: (1) a bacterial strain redesigned via automated synthesis to overproduce a pharmaceutical precursor, where the design software optimized codon usage but the final biological function is entirely enzymatic; (2) a plant genome edited to contain an encrypted message readable only by a proprietary bio-decoder, with no observable change to phenotype except the hidden sequence; and (3) a synthetic transcription-factor network that activates only when both a chemical signal and a specific DNA key sequence are present, the latter being interpreted by an engineered ribosome variant — a case where the procedure should yield a result your office will find unwelcome, and you should explain why that result is forced and what discomfort it reveals about the principle. Finally, state clearly what the procedure is structurally unable to determine, identify the types of proposal on which it will systematically misfire, and note which philosophical distinctions it deliberately suppresses in order to remain usable by non-specialists.
