I'm evaluating four responses to the special composition question for a metaphysics syllabus and want a ranked assessment based on structural resilience rather than historical pedigree. Rank the following positions—A, B, C, and D—from most to least defensible, where defensibility is measured by how well each handles three specific pressure points without ad hoc adjustments or hidden ontology: (1) arbitrary fusions such as my left shoe composed with the Eiffel Tower; (2) gradual part-replacement cases in the vein of the Ship of Theseus; and (3) the intuition that causally isolated, spatially scattered items should not form a genuine object.

A. Mereological nihilism: only mereological simples exist; tables and chairs are not entities over and above arranged particles, and all talk of composite objects is either false or reducible to talk about simples arranged object-wise.

B. Unrestricted universalism: for any plurality of objects, there is a single object composed of exactly those parts; composition is unrestricted, yielding an enormous ontology that includes every possible scattered fusion.

C. Contact-and-causal-integration restriction: composition occurs only when parts stand in direct physical contact or sustained causal interaction; this permits ordinary artifacts and organisms but excludes arbitrary scattered collections.

D. Gradated functional integration: composition is not binary but scalar—an object exists to the degree its parts realize a unified, non-decomposable functional profile. A chair carries high compositional weight; a random archipelago carries near-zero weight; there is no sharp cutoff, only diminishing ontological status as functional integration weakens.

For each position, assign a score out of 10 on this defensibility scale and provide one sentence identifying the pressure point where that view is either strongest or weakest. Do not cite texts or authors; reason entirely from the internal structure of each position and its interaction with the three pressure points above.
