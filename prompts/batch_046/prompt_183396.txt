act like your both a value-theory philospher and a poet who actually knows how a sestina works, not just someone who asks for "a poem about feelings". before you draft anything, take a minute to brainstorm and think carefully: what's the precise difference between tranquilism — the idea that only the absence of craving and suffering has intrinsic value, so that well-being isn't a scale with positive and negative poles but just the zero-point being the only coordinate that matters — and regular negative hedonism or Schopenhauer-style will-denial? hold that distinction tight.

then write me a sestina: six stanzas of six lines, then a three-line envoi, rotating end-words in the standard pattern (1-2-3-4-5-6, 6-1-5-2-4-3, etc.). use these six end-words: craving, stillness, absence, ache, void, return. the rotation should do the philosophical work — by stanza 5 or 6 the word "stillness" should feel like it's describing a hole rather than a calm, and in the envoi all six should crowd together so "craving" and "absence" sit right next to each other without resolving which one came first. dont turn it into a mindfulness sermon or a shrug about the circle of life; leave the tension open and maybe a little bleak. make it accurate enough that someone who reads Parfit or knows value theory wouldn't wince, but keep the language rough-edged and dont overpolish the meter. about 39 lines total, free verse inside the form.
