Design a 90-minute structured deliberation for an upper-level environmental-philosophy seminar of 12 students. The aim is to make participants feel, through rules and symbolic stakes rather than lecture, the conceptual tension in asking whether habitat destruction reduces net wild-animal suffering—specifically by forcing them to apply a formal “welfare ledger” that treats the non-existence of future suffering-capable organisms as a welfare gain of destruction, without naming the philosophical origin of this asymmetry.

Setting and roles: Divide students into three groups of four—Ecological Integrity Advocates, Welfare Aggregators, and Temporal Accountants. Give each group identical scenario cards describing a development project that eliminates 30 percent of a wetland’s breeding habitat over 50 years. Provide token budgets: 20 green tokens (current observable suffering/welfare states), 15 blue tokens (ecosystem functional value), and 10 black tokens (representing “prevented future suffering individuals”). The Temporal Accountants must apply a binding accounting rule: for every blue token lost, they may allocate black tokens equal to the habitat’s projected replacement rate, but only if they formally record these allocations as “net welfare improvements.”

Rules and stakes: Groups negotiate for 30 minutes. Consensus requires all three groups to sign a “net welfare statement.” If they reach consensus, every student receives 3 symbolic points; if not, Welfare Aggregators and Temporal Accountants receive 1 point each for documenting the ledger, while Ecological Integrity Advocates receive 0—creating an incentive to formalize the suffering calculus even when intuition resists it.

Debrief (20 minutes): First reveal that the black-token rule embodies an axiological asymmetry between existence and non-existence applied at ecosystem scale. Map specific moments: (a) deadlock over green versus blue tokens to the aggregation problem; (b) discomfort assigning positive value to black tokens to the replacement/asymmetry problem; (c) refusal to sign to the ecosystem-integrity objection. Be explicit about what the game cannot reproduce: the metaphysical status of merely potential beings and the long-run evolutionary dynamics of suffering, which the facilitator must supply conceptually. Predict the predictable first-run failure: students will conflate black tokens with “lives saved” rather than “suffering prevented,” requiring the facilitator to correct the framing without resolving the underlying debate.

Constraints: Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. In your response, clearly distinguish what is conceptually settled (for example, that wild animals experience suffering) from what remains genuinely contested among specialists (for example, whether preventing the existence of suffering-capable organisms can count as a welfare improvement, or whether ecosystem integrity carries non-instrumental value). Do not present any contested position as the consensus view. Limit your response to 400 words.
