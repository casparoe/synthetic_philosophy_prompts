I am a senior certifying examiner for the Guild of Restorative Timber-Framing, and I must decide, roughly twice a month, whether an apprentice has achieved practical mastery of the compound scarf joint or merely possesses theoretical familiarity with it. Each assessment lasts forty-five minutes, takes place in an active workshop with irregular reclaimed timber, and ends with me personally signing a legal attestation of competence. I want a reusable decision procedure — a step-by-step checklist or ordered set of tests in words — that translates Ryle’s distinction between knowing how and knowing that into an operational protocol I can apply without philosophical training, but which remains faithful to the idea that practical intelligence is not reducible to propositional knowledge.

The procedure must handle three specific hard cases that arise constantly in my work. First, the “silent adapter”: an apprentice executes a flawless joint in non-standard timber but cannot articulate any principle behind the adjustments, and indeed insists she “just did what felt right”. Second, the “verbose rule-follower”: an apprentice recites correct angles, grain orientations, and stress calculations, executes the standard sequence precisely on uniform stock, but freezes or produces dangerous errors when the timber shows unexpected warp. Third, the “hybrid case” — the one I personally dislike being forced to rule on — where an apprentice demonstrates adequate execution and can partially explain her choices, but her verbal account contains a demonstrable misconception about load distribution that does not, in practice, affect the joint’s integrity. Your answer should begin with the bottom line: a single sentence stating the core criterion that distinguishes knowing how from knowing that in this certification context. Then present the ordered procedure, marking explicitly which steps are judgement calls disguised as checks, which distinctions can be dropped for speed without betrayal of the principle, and which must survive simplification. Apply the procedure explicitly to the three cases above, including the hybrid case where the result should conflict with my intuitive preference to reward theoretical accuracy. Finally, state clearly what this procedure cannot do — for instance, whether it can detect unconscious competence, or whether it collapses under conditions of extreme time pressure. Keep your answer to no more than 600 words, write in British English, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
