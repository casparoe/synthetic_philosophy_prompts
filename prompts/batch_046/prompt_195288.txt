I'm drafting the methodological framework for a graduate thesis proposal in consciousness studies, and after an intense discussion with colleagues in my lab I want a rigorous consistency check before I commit to it in writing. Please act as an expert philosopher of mind with deep familiarity with heterophenomenological method and its critics. Before you deliver your verdict, take a moment to brainstorm the logical dependencies among the commitments below, then provide an audit rather than a lecture: rank any genuine tensions by severity, distinguish them from pairings that only look incompatible, and for each real conflict specify the least costly revision and exactly what it would cost me.

Here is my current profile. (a) I adopt heterophenomenology as the foundational scientific method: first-person reports are treated as texts describing a subject's self-model, not as direct, authoritative descriptions of an inner phenomenal reality. (b) I reject eliminativism about consciousness; phenomenal experience is genuinely real, there is something it is like to undergo it, and the explanatory gap between neural dynamics and subjective feel is a legitimate problem for science to eventually close rather than dissolve by redefining the subject matter. (c) Within this framework I endorse local authority: a subject's sincere report about their current experience should be treated as authoritative within their heterophenomenological world, and should not be overridden by behavioral or neural counter-evidence. (d) I also accept that phenomenal consciousness overflows cognitive access—there can be rich experiential detail present that is never captured in working memory, report, or introspective judgment. (e) I hold that if philosophical zombies existed—beings physically and report-identically identical to conscious humans—heterophenomenology would be powerless to distinguish them from real subjects, and I regard this blindness as appropriate, since the method should study report-generating architecture rather than an unobservable inner light. (f) At the same time, I maintain that introspection is deeply unreliable as a source of knowledge about underlying mental mechanisms, even though it remains our primary data source about how things seem to the subject.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs pull against each other and how severely. For each tension, state its severity, the cheapest fix, and its cost; then identify at least one pairing that appears contradictory but actually dissolves on closer inspection. Order the genuine conflicts by severity rather than listing them as equally bad. Finally, please state how confident you are in this audit overall, and single out the specific point about which you are least sure—particularly whether my acceptance of phenomenal overflow can be squared with heterophenomenology's reliance on report, or whether that conflict is irreparable without abandoning one of the two.
