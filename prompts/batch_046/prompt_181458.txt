I am preparing a graduate seminar on philosophy of mind and language, and my students have been going in circles over whether Twin Earth genuinely undermines internalism about mental content. Rather than stage another conventional debate, I want them to draft an adversarial-collaboration document between a committed semantic externalist—who holds that the causal-historical chain linking a speaker’s term to its referent partly determines the content of that speaker’s mental states—and a committed conceptual-role internalist, who holds that mental content is fixed entirely by the internal functional, inferential, and phenomenal profile of the thinker, regardless of external causal history.

To prevent the exercise from becoming a rehearsed exchange about the classic thought experiment, frame the collaboration around a modified scenario: Oscar and Twin-Oscar are not biological humans but synthetic cognitive architectures with identical internal processing structures, sensory modules, and qualitative states; neither has ever had direct perceptual contact with the local liquid, but both have received only mediated reports and instrumental measurements. Oscar’s linguistic community is causally anchored to H2O on Earth, while Twin-Oscar’s community is anchored to a chemically distinct but macroscopically indistinguishable substance—call it “aquium”—on Twin Earth. The document should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides can genuinely sign, including about the formal structure of the scenario, about what exactly needs explaining, and about which standard no-go results are irrelevant here; (2) a disagreements table in which each side’s position is phrased in wording that side would accept, specifying precisely where they part ways on whether indirect causal anchoring can penetrate mental content when internal architecture is held constant; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party—such as whether mediated reference-fixing suffices for wide content and whether identical narrow architectures can nevertheless host different mental contents; and (4) for each crux, a concrete test case or argumentative move both sides agree would bear on it, with each side’s predicted outcome recorded in advance. The collaboration should also state explicitly whether any portion of the dispute turns out to be verbal rather than substantive, identifying which portion and why.
