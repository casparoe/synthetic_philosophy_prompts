Here is the precise claim I want evaluated: in a patient with a complete corpus callosotomy performed for intractable epilepsy, there are two numerically distinct streams of phenomenal consciousness—one tied primarily to each cerebral hemisphere—rather than a single consciousness that has merely lost access to some of its own contents.

Please give me your actual credence in that claim, expressed as a number between 0 and 1, but do not stop there. Decompose the reasoning into at least four parts: (1) the probability that phenomenal unity requires direct interhemispheric integration and cannot be achieved through subcortical coordination alone (for instance, via the anterior commissure or superior colliculus pathways); (2) the probability that the left hemisphere's verbal reports about the right hemisphere's experiential absence are authoritative indicators rather than post-hoc confabulations; (3) the probability that cross-cueing behaviors—say, the left hand correcting an error made by the right hand without any verbal mediation—indicate a single underlying agent rather than inter-subject cooperation between two subjects; and (4) the probability that consciousness in these cases is graded rather than binary, so that framing the issue as "one or two streams" is itself a category mistake.

For each part, provide a rough number and one line of justification. Then combine them—explicitly or implicitly—to show how you reach your overall credence. Next, tell me which of those four terms you are least confident in, and specify what kind of empirical finding or conceptual argument would shift it the most. Finally, if I told you that my own credence sits at about 0.55, identify which sub-question is the most likely locus of our disagreement and why.
