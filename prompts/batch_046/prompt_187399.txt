Two people in my study group got stuck in an arguement over Carroll's tortise and the justification of deduction. Riley said the tortise accepts A and B imply C, plus A and B, but refuses C unless you add the rule itself as a premise, and then demands the next rule forever. So Riley thinks Carroll proves logical deduction needs an infinite chain of rules -- there's no bottom, just regress all the way down. Sam said thats misreading it: the tortise is mixing up using a rule with listing it as a premise. In a formal system you have derivation rules that arent premises, so theres no infinite chain, just a primitive rule thats part of the system. Riley came back: but even that primitive rule can be questioned, so youre just pushing the regress up a level. Sam replied that at some point you hit a constitutive norm -- like chess rules -- and if you reject those youre not playing the game, not reasoning badly inside it. Riley then said that makes logic just conventional, like a game, so Carroll actually undermines objective deduction. Sam said no, the rules can be constitutive and still objectively correct for reasoning; the tortise just opts out of reasoning altogether.
