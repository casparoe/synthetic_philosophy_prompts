I am preparing questions for a podcast interview with a scholar who defends Max Weber's Protestant ethic thesis as a live explanation of how cultural values can cause economic transformation, not merely reflect it. I do not have an academic background in sociology or philosophy of history; I have been thinking through this on my own, and I want to understand whether culture can be a cause in any robust sense. I need eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her to summarize Weber. Instead, please include: at least one question pressing on how the theological anxiety about salvation avoids being read as a post-hoc rationalization of prior economic anxiety; one on whether Weber's image of values as railway switchmen makes culture causally inert rather than genuinely directive; one on what kind of historical or counterfactual evidence could show that modern capitalism would have emerged even without the specific vocational ethic Weber describes; and for each, a follow-up keyed to the defense she is most likely to give. I also want one question built around an idea I have been calling reverse-engineered vocation: the suggestion that the concept of a calling gained its causal power not because theology shaped labor, but because emerging capitalist structures made religious language the most available vocabulary for justifying relentless work, so that the causal arrow runs backward through social practice rather than through belief. Ask how this differs from a standard materialist rebuttal and whether Weber's thesis can survive it. Please avoid generic biographical openers and keep every question tied to the actual mechanics of her position.
