For an upper-level philosophy of logic and epistemology seminar, draft a 90-minute final-exam essay question together with a model answer of about 700 words and a marking scheme totaling 20 points, on Fitch's paradox of knowability. The question must be unanswerable by summarizing the standard derivation; instead, inject the following constructed framing: suppose the knowability principle is restricted not by contingency but by an agent's computational budget, so that a truth p is "budget-knowable" only if an agent with resources R can come to know p, with cross-world accessibility relations indexed to R. Ask students to (i) show precisely where the original Fitch derivation breaks under this revision, (ii) assess whether the break genuinely saves knowability or merely relocates the problem into the modal definition of R-accessibility, and (iii) evaluate the objection that tying epistemic possibility to practical computational capacity collapses the distinction between epistemic and practical modality and thereby undermines normative epistemology. The marking scheme should name five must-hit analytical moves—e.g., correct identification of the derivation's use of an unrestricted knowability principle; diagnosis of the modal cost of indexing accessibility to R; evaluation of whether the restriction is ad hoc; engagement with the practical/epistemic collapse objection; and a clear, reasoned verdict—allocating marks to each and noting, in one line per move, the typical mistake that costs marks. Do not request citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or named texts. Say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point about which you are least sure. Begin immediately with the exam question, model answer, and marking scheme, with no preface, commentary, or restatement of the prompt.
