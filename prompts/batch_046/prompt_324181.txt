Write a deadpan satirical memo, about 650 words, in the bureaucratic voice of the fictional "Bureau of Value-Causal Review." The memo should lay out formal evaluation criteria for social-science grant proposals that claim to explain a specific modern institutional outcome—such as the widespread adoption of open-plan offices or the normalization of asynchronous work—by reference to a cultural value, in the manner of Weber's Protestant ethic thesis. Structure it with numbered procedural rules, a "common methodological errors" sidebar, and a mock evaluation rubric that includes one invented metric (for example, an "elective-affinity coefficient" or a "value-residual score"). The satire should expose, without ever stating them outright, the circularity problem in which values are inferred from the very outcomes they are meant to explain, the difficulty of isolating value-orientations from material conditions, and the unfalsifiability of value-based causal claims. Keep the tone consistently deadpan and readable to someone with basic familiarity with Weber; do not request citations, bibliographies, or references to specific scholars beyond Weber, and do not use verbatim quotations—paraphrase the underlying conceptual issues. The humor should arise from the internal logic of the criteria themselves rather than from absurdities unrelated to the actual debate about explaining social change with values.
