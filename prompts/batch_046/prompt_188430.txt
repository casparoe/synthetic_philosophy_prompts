I'm trying to identify a specific failure mode in philosophical argumentation involving extreme or physically impossible scenarios. A thinker constructs a case—such as a person gradually replaced by synthetic parts over centuries, or a society where one pill eliminates all suffering—where ordinary biological, psychological, and social constraints are suspended. They then elicit an intuitive judgment about the case and treat that judgment as authoritative for the corresponding concept in ordinary life. The distinctive feature is not just that the scenario is far-fetched, but that its far-fetchedness disables our usual experiential error-correction, and the thinker presents this disablement as a methodological virtue, claiming that stripping away contingent details reveals the concept's pure form. Is there an established name for this configuration? How does it differ from Dennett's intuition pumps, from imaginative resistance, and from standard analogical reasoning? If it lacks a standard label, propose one with boundary conditions that distinguish legitimate conceptual isolation from this particular misuse.
