For a philosophy of mind reading group, I am drafting a brief profile of my current views on consciousness through the user-illusion framework, and I want an internal consistency audit before I present it. Here is roughly where I have landed. First, I take consciousness to be a user illusion in the literal interface sense: the brain constructs a streamlined, first-person “desktop” that hides underlying neural computation, much as a graphical interface hides binary processes. Second, I am a physicalist about this process—there is no Cartesian theater, and the self-model is distributed, continuously reconstructed, and fully realized in predictive neural dynamics. Third, I hold that phenomenal consciousness—what it is like to have an experience—is real but entirely constituted by this recursive self-modeling; there is no extra “phenomenal glow” that outstrips the model’s representation of itself having experiences. Fourth, I think the zombie scenario remains genuinely conceivable: a being physically and functionally identical to me could lack this interface-level self-model and therefore lack phenomenal consciousness altogether. Fifth, I treat conceivability in this specific domain as a fallible but genuinely indicative guide to metaphysical possibility. Sixth, I deny that small language models are conscious, not because they lack biological tissue, but because they lack the requisite recursive depth and counterfactual richness in their generative models to construct a stable user interface for themselves. Seventh, I maintain that if we engineered an artificial system with the appropriate recursive predictive architecture—one that models itself as having experiences in a counterfactual, error-minimizing loop—that system would possess genuine phenomenal consciousness, not mere behavioral simulation.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and how severely each tension runs. In particular, examine whether my physicalist constitution claim in the third point can survive my acceptance of zombie conceivability in the fourth point together with my reliance on conceivability in the fifth, and whether my substrate-independence claims in the sixth and seventh points sit comfortably with the physicalist framing in the second. For each real tension, identify the least costly revision and what it costs me elsewhere in the profile. Then rank the tensions by severity and tell me which single revision—if I had to make only one—would clean up the most inconsistencies. Please write your response in American English.
