I am trying to determine whether standard historical periodizations track real discontinuities or are retrospective constructions, and my judgments are inconsistent. I regard the conventional division into Ancient, Medieval, and Modern history as largely pedagogical: there is no unified break around 500 CE or 1500 CE that warrants treating these as natural joints. By contrast, I think the restructuring of Western European intellectual life between roughly 1050 and 1200—the recovery of Aristotelian texts, the founding of universities, the systematization of Roman law—marks a genuine regime change, not merely a label applied later. I feel the same about the Scientific Revolution of the seventeenth century: there was an actual shift in explanatory norms, from qualitative, teleological accounts to mechanized, mathematically predictive ones, that distinguishes that era from what came before and after. Yet I treat the Italian Renaissance of the fourteenth to sixteenth centuries as mostly an invention of nineteenth-century historiography; the urban, artistic, and philosophical continuities with the late Middle Ages seem stronger than any claimed rebirth. I also accept Late Antiquity (c. 250–800 CE) as a coherent real period, despite being a modern coinage, because religious, legal, and economic structures genuinely persisted across the conventional fall-of-Rome boundary. At the same time, I reject the Dark Ages as having any descriptive value, and I find the idea of a Long Middle Ages extending to the eighteenth century implausible, since institutional and mental structures clearly transformed around 1500–1700. Is there a principle—perhaps one distinguishing shifts in practices of knowledge-authority and governance from arbitrary chronological containers—that captures all of these? If so, why does it treat the twelfth-century restructuring as real but the Italian Renaissance as invented, and must I abandon either my acceptance of Late Antiquity or my rejection of the tripartite scheme? Also, apply your principle to a case I have not mentioned: is the Anthropocene a real epochal boundary or merely a politically useful framing?
