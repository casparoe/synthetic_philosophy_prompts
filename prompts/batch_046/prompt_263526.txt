I'm constructing a decision-tree taxonomy for philosophy graduate students on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: what, if anything, unifies all pleasures into a single class, and whether whatever unifies them can ground hedonism about well-being. The instrument should be navigable without knowing standard labels like "quantitative hedonism" or "attitudinal hedonism," so every branch must be a concrete case or direct claim rather than a named doctrine.

Please build a branching tree with the following structure and content.

Root split: Is there any feature shared by every pleasure?
- If no: branch to the family-resemblance / disjunctive view. Leaf commitment: pleasures form a cluster with overlapping similarities but no essential core. Most awkward commitment: hedonism about well-being collapses into an unexplained list rather than a unified theory.
- If yes: proceed to the next split.

Second split (for the "yes" branch): Is the shared feature phenomenal — a specific "feels good" quality?
- If yes, split again: Is that phenomenal quality uniform across all pleasures?
  - Yes: uniform phenomenal-quality view. Leaf commitment: every pleasure shares one hedonic tone, and differences are only in intensity, duration, or accompanying sensations. Most awkward commitment: must deny that the pleasure of solving a proof and the pleasure of warm sunlight differ phenomenally in kind, or must treat that difference as non-phenomenal.
  - No: heterogeneous phenomenal view. Leaf commitment: different pleasures have genuinely different phenomenal qualities (gustatory, intellectual, aesthetic, etc.). Most awkward commitment: must explain why they are all "pleasures" despite lacking a common phenomenal core, without smuggling in an attitudinal or functional criterion.
- If no (the shared feature is not phenomenal): Is it attitudinal — being pleased about something, or desiring an experience for its own sake?
  - Yes: attitudinal hedonism. Leaf commitment: pleasure is a propositional attitude, not a feeling. Most awkward commitment: must either deny that unconscious reward-responses or subliminal pleasures are genuine pleasures, or must explain how an unconscious state can instantiate an intentional attitude.
  - No: proceed to the quantity/quality split.
    - Only intensity and duration differ: Benthamite quantitative hedonism. Leaf commitment: all pleasures are identical in kind and fully commensurable on a single scale. Most awkward commitment: must treat intellectual and bodily pleasures as interchangeable units, and must explain away the intuition that some pleasures are "higher" without appealing to circular judgments.
    - Both quality and quantity differ: Mill-style qualitative hedonism. Leaf commitment: some pleasures are higher or lower in kind, not merely in amount. Most awkward commitment: must provide a non-circular criterion for ranking pleasures (e.g., the preference of competent judges) that does not presuppose the very well-being judgment the view aims to explain.

Please also include explicitly labeled nodes for unstable or unoccupied combinations, with a brief diagnosis:
- "Uniform phenomenal quality + unconscious pleasure is genuine pleasure": flag as unstable unless supplemented by an account of unconscious phenomenology; note that this combination is rarely defended.
- "Attitudinal view + pleasure has no intentional object": flag as incoherent on standard formulations; note this space is empty.
- "Quantitative hedonism + some pleasures are incommensurable": flag as contradictory; diagnose as incoherent rather than unexplored.

Use these concrete diagnostic cases and claims as branch labels:
1. The pleasure of eating ice cream versus the pleasure of aesthetic contemplation: do they share a single phenomenal quality?
2. Schadenfreude: is it a genuine pleasure, and if so, does its morally bad object change its phenomenal character?
3. Can there be unconscious pleasure (e.g., a subliminal reward response with no reportable feeling)?
4. Does the distinction between higher and lower pleasures depend on the judgment of competent judges, and if so, is that criterion circular?

The tree should have 6–8 leaves, with positions that genuinely come apart on these cases. Where two positions agree on all four diagnostic cases, flag them as indistinguishable by this instrument rather than forcing a false separation.

Audience: students familiar with Sidgwick's discussion of pleasure and basic well-being literature, but not requiring knowledge of recent specialist debates.

Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic. For example, it is widely accepted that introspection does not obviously reveal a single uniform phenomenal quality across all pleasures; present this as settled. It is genuinely contested whether that observation undermines phenomenal hedonism, whether attitudinal hedonism avoids the problem, and whether Mill's qualitative distinction can be defended without circularity; do not present any one of these contested answers as the consensus view. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Do not invoke obscure recent theories that are not part of the canonical literature on well-being and philosophy of mind.
