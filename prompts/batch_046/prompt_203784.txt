A colleague in metaphysics summarized David Lewis's modal realism for me as follows. Lewis holds that all possible worlds are concrete universes, spatiotemporally isolated from ours and equally real, populated by genuine counterparts of familiar objects. Modal statements are existential quantifications over these worlds: saying "there could be a talking donkey" is true just if some world contains one. He also analyzes "actual" indexically—calling our world actual is like saying "here"—so that no world enjoys metaphysical privilege. From this indexical analysis together with the unrestricted plurality of worlds, he derives that the correct modal logic is S5; the main objection is ontological extravagance, but Lewis thinks the payoff—reductive accounts of properties, propositions, and counterfactual dependence—justifies it. Is this summary accurate? Go claim by claim and tell me what a Lewisian would accept outright, what needs correction or qualification, and whether the inference to S5 genuinely follows from the premises mentioned. I am especially unsure whether Lewis permits any overlap or shared parts between worlds, and whether his argument for S5 depends on something other than the indexical theory of actuality.
