I'm currently enrolled in an advanced undergraduate seminar on existentialism and the philosophy of value, and over the past three weeks we have been contrasting classical absurdist positions—especially the claim that human projects are rendered ultimately groundless by an indifferent universe—with contemporary analytic accounts of meaning in life, including subjective, objective, and hybrid frameworks. For my contribution to next week's discussion, my professor asked us to apply a conceptual framing she introduced called "the asymmetry of terminal commitment": the observation that any chain of justification for a meaningful project must eventually terminate in a commitment that is not itself justified by some further project, yet this termination point threatens to leave the entire chain either arbitrarily grounded or deficient in meaning.

With that framing in mind, I would like you to rank the following four positions into tiers—top tier (resolves the asymmetry without collapsing into arbitrariness or nihilism), middle tier (offers a partial resolution but at significant cost to some other intuitive commitment), and bottom tier (fails to address the asymmetry or resolves it only by abandoning a core tenet of the view): (1) a Camus-inspired absurdist stance that treats the absence of terminal justification not as a defect but as the very condition for authentic rebellion and continued engagement; (2) a purely subjective account according to which meaning is constituted entirely by an agent's pro-attitudes, so that the termination point is validated by the agent's own endorsement regardless of objective status; (3) a hybrid account that requires both subjective attraction and objective value, proposing that objective goods provide the terminating ground while subjective engagement sustains the agent's connection to them; and (4) an objective-list account that identifies meaning with the achievement of goods such as knowledge, moral virtue, or aesthetic value, treating the agent's psychological endorsement as inessential to whether the life is meaningful. For each tier, please explain in one or two sentences why the position lands there specifically with respect to the asymmetry of terminal commitment, rather than with respect to its general plausibility in other debates.
