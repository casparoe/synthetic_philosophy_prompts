I have been reading, in a layman's way, about Aristotle's view that money exists to facilitate exchange between households, and that using money purely to generate more money — chrematistike — runs against nature. Would Aristotle regard the negative-interest currency as closer to proper exchange, since it prevents money from becoming an object of hoarding? Or would he object that it corrupts money's role as a stable measure of value? And would he classify the algorithmic pricing of necessities as a particularly pernicious form of chrematistike — profiting from need itself — or merely as exchange made more efficient?
