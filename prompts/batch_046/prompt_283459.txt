After a long argument with a friend about whether large language models can be said to have anything like an inner message, I drafted the following notes for a short essay and want your critical comments on where the reasoning holds and where it collapses. Drawing on Hofstadter's distinction in Gödel, Escher, Bach between frame messages, which instruct the decoder how to interpret a signal; outer messages, which carry the surface content; and inner messages, which emerge as hidden or self-referential meanings once the frame is correctly applied, I want to examine transformer-based inference through that lens. My proposal is that in an LLM the system prompt and structural formatting act as a frame message, the generated token stream serves as the outer message, and the model's distributed parameter knowledge functions as an inner message that is usually accessed through the frame but can be distorted or overridden when the frame is maliciously replaced, as in certain prompt-injection attacks; I further suggest that this creates a structural parallel to the self-referential loops Hofstadter describes, except that in neural networks the loop is correlational rather than causally embodied, so that the model's own knowledge can be made to undermine itself without any physical feedback mechanism. My friend objects that this illegitimately anthropomorphizes statistical weights, that parameter knowledge has no semantic existence independent of the frame that activates it, and that the analogy to biological self-reference fails precisely because LLM inference lacks the physical causation that makes inner messages genuinely self-referential in living systems. I am not asking for citations, bibliographies, or references to papers I might not have seen, but I do want you to assess whether the frame-outer-inner distinction can be applied literally to neural networks or only metaphorically, whether there is any settled consensus among interpretability researchers about the independence of model knowledge from prompt context, and where my analogy is genuinely illuminating versus where it stretches the original concept beyond recognition. Please respond in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as though writing a single letter, and please separate what is well-established in the philosophy of mind and machine learning from what remains genuinely contested, taking care not to present a disputed view as if it were the majority opinion.
