Question: In the Pensées, Pascal addresses the relationship between justice and force in a well-known discussion. Clarify whether his observation that justice lacking power is ineffective, and that power lacking justice is tyrannical, operates mainly as a normative prescription for legitimate government, as a descriptive account of why people actually obey authority, or as a different kind of claim altogether. In your answer, address whether Pascal holds that true justice can be realized through human political institutions, and explain how his theological commitments bear on this view.

Student answer:
Pascal argues that legitimate government must actively unite justice and force: justice ought to be made forceful so that laws have real worldly effect, while force ought to be guided by justice to avoid becoming tyranny. This is a normative prescription for rulers, and it applies universally across societies. Pascal also maintains that true justice is ultimately divine and inaccessible to fallen human institutions; consequently, in practice people must rely on customary law that is backed by coercive power. From this he concludes that established customs should be respected and obeyed, because these customs already embody the necessary union of justice and force, and challenging them threatens the social order that keeps tyranny at bay.
