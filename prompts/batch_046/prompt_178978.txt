Please describe the most important implications of Leibniz's thesis that moral perfection consists in the rational ordering of appetitions toward the maximal harmony of created substances, particularly as this thesis interacts with his monadological claim that every substance expresses the universe from a unique perspective with varying degrees of perceptual distinctness, for the evaluation of moral agency in beings—natural or constructed—whose cognitive architecture is perspectivally fragmented yet whose volitional tendencies remain oriented toward the common good. I am especially interested in whether this conjunction yields a non-binary, gradated conception of moral responsibility that tracks the clarity of representational ordering rather than the success of outcomes, and in what institutional or normative practices would have to be revised if we adopted such a framework in place of the standard binary ascription of full agency or its absence.
