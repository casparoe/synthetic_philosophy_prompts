I am preparing a research proposal in philosophy of language and interpretation theory, and I have become convinced that Davidson's program of radical interpretation requires serious revision if it is to apply to cases where the interpreter and the interpreted system share no common perceptual framework—not merely different beliefs about the same world, but genuinely incommensurable sensory architectures, as one might encounter in interpreting advanced synthetic agents or radically foreign scientific instruments. The standard literature focuses on belief-desire psychology and shared environmental causation, but I want to explore whether the principle of charity and the coherence constraints on belief can survive the removal of that shared baseline.

Before I commit to this, I need an honest assessment of where the actual philosophical work lies. I see three candidate directions, but I am unsure which merit sustained attention: first, whether charity should be domain-localized rather than applied globally, allowing an interpreted system to be rational in some sectors and not in others; second, whether we can retain radical interpretation while substituting a thinner, non-doxastic functional stance for full-blown belief ascription; and third, whether the Davidsonian framework implicitly presupposes a kind of interpretive universality—the thesis that any behaviorally coherent pattern is in principle interpretable—which may collapse when applied to systems optimized for instrumental performance rather than representational accuracy. Beyond these, I wonder whether connections to indirect normativity—specifically, how the norms governing interpretation themselves depend on prior normative commitments about rationality—offer a more fruitful line, or whether issues of epistemic dominance and ascription universality in universal quantification might illuminate how we generalize interpretive success across cases.

Please prioritize: which one or two of these directions represent genuinely open philosophical problems rather than technical adjustments to Davidson's original apparatus? For each, defend its importance relative to the others using criteria such as tractability for a solo researcher with logic training, dependence on other unresolved issues, and the risk of empirical overreach. I am not asking for a survey or a reading list; I want a defended agenda for where three years of work should go, and what would have to be true for you to change that ranking.
