I facilitate a monthly discussion circle for people without formal training in physics or philosophy who nevertheless keep returning to one question: if Einstein showed that whether two events occur together depends on how an observer is moving, does that mean the future is already out there, fixed and real, and that our sense of open possibility is an illusion? I have tried to read about the argument associated with Rietdijk and Putnam — that in special relativity there is no privileged present, so temporal becoming is not an objective feature of the world — but I cannot translate it into something I can use in the room. What I need is a practical, reusable procedure: a sequence of ordered steps I can apply every time the topic arises, with specific questions to put to the group, criteria for what counts as a correct answer at each stage, and explicit stopping points where the physics ends and a value judgment begins. The procedure must include a small set of test sentences that reveal when someone is smuggling in assumptions — for instance, substituting a frame-relative relation for a personal feeling of now — together with instructions for how to respond. Please work through three concrete discussion scenarios: first, a case where the procedure correctly identifies a confusion between simultaneity and determinism; second, a case where the procedure reaches its limit and genuinely cannot settle whether the block view eliminates human agency; and third, a case where the procedure yields an answer that will be unwelcome to the group, specifically that the argument offers no special exemption for human-scale durations or local actions. I do not want a bibliography, a reading list, or citations; I want a tool I can run in about fifteen minutes with people who have never taken physics, together with an honest account of where it will misfire or require my own judgment rather than the theory.
