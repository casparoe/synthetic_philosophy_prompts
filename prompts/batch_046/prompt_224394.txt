Since my daughter was born last year climate stuff has stopped being abstract — I keep wondering what we actualy owe people who won't exist for centuries. I think the deepest wrong isn't just the damage, it's a kind of democratic presentism: our political instituions are built to ignore anyone without a vote, which makes future generations structurally invisible in a way that's closer to disrespect than to bad luck. Play it straight: what would count against that framing? I want the arguments or evidence that'd move me away from it, sorted by how decisive they are, plus an honest take on whether the view can just absorb every objection by redefining presentism. Is there a real risk of unfalsifiability here, and is that a problem for this kind of claim?
