I'm preparing material for an advanced discussion group on comparative ethics, and we've been circling the same stalemate for weeks: defenders of Confucian role ethics insist that morality is fundamentally constituted by relational roles—parent-child, ruler-subject, elder-younger—while universalist critics maintain that any adequate moral theory must ground obligations in features shared by all persons, not in contingent social positions. Rather than continue the back-and-forth, I want to see what an honest adversarial collaboration would actually yield.

Please draft the joint statement that a committed Confucian role ethicist and a committed liberal universalist critic could both sign after sustained good-faith engagement. Structure the document as follows: first, a numbered section of agreed claims about the phenomenology of role-based living, the distinction between ritual propriety (li) and mere social convention, the universalist's core commitment to equal moral standing, and the shared rejection of both unreflective traditionalism and atomistic egoism; second, a disagreements table in which each remaining divergence is formulated in wording that its own proponent would accept—especially regarding whether roles supply non-derivative moral reasons or only instrumental ones, and whether universal dignity can be expressed relationally without collapsing into hierarchy; third, the cruxes—here I think the decisive sub-questions are (a) whether fulfilling one's role can leave a legitimate moral remainder that the role structure itself cannot absorb, and (b) whether relational autonomy counts as genuine self-governance or as adaptive preference-formation shaped by power; and fourth, for each crux, one concrete scenario or conceptual test both parties agree is relevant, with each side's predicted assessment recorded in advance.

The aim is not a verdict but a constructive text both could publish jointly: separating verbal disputes from genuine ones, and making the residual disagreement exact enough that an observer could see what evidence or argument would actually move either party. Please avoid attributing caricatured positions—neither side should be made to defend unreflective obedience or atomistic egoism.
