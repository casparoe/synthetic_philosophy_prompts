I defend a reconstruction of Buddhist ethics as impersonal consequentialism: since anatta denies a persistent moral agent, karma ought to be interpreted as a causal pattern of mental conditioning rather than a personal ledger, leaving the ethical task as the minimisation of dukkha across all sentient continua without privileging one's own mental stream. What would genuinely count against this view? I want the objections, distinctions, and potential counterexamples — particularly whether the Abhidhamma analysis of momentary consciousness undermines the cross-lifetime aggregation required for consequentialist weighing, and whether upaya introduces deontic constraints that survive the denial of self — ordered by how decisive they would be. Please include an honest assessment of whether the position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability through flexible definitions of 'dukkha' or 'continuum', and whether that insulation is a defect for a view of this kind.
