I sit on my uni's research ethics panel and we've got this proposal that feels like it's trying to run Mary's room in actual meatspace. The PI wants to recruit subjects with congenital anosmia — complete absence of smell — train them for months on every physical fact about olfaction, then expose them once via targeted nasal stimulation and measure whether their reports contain 'novel phenomenal knowledge' they couldn't have had before. The PI frames it as testing Jackson's knowledge argument, but my worry is that the debrief protocol treats any subjective report of 'learning something new' as data confirming non-physicalism, which feels like it bakes the conclusion into the methodology. Half the board think it's routine psych with philosophical framing; half think it's essentially deceiving participants about the study's metaphysical aims. I need our written opinion for Thursday — approve with conditions, reject, or whatever — with concrete recommendations the researchers can actually respond to. It should take seriously the strongest arguement for letting it go ahead — probably that phenomenological reports are legitimate data and blocking this is just anti-realist prejudice — but land on a clear decision with a time frame. Keep it practical, not an essay.
