Explain the conceptual tension in the absent-minded driver problem, reframed for an autonomous agent with partial observability. The agent must reach a destination that requires passing through the second of two indistinguishable checkpoints, yet a transient sensor fault can cause it to forget whether it has already passed the first. The agent can adopt an ex ante plan, but its local decision module evaluates only immediate expected utility without access to that plan. Clarify why the planner, anticipating this amnesia, might rationally commit to passing the first checkpoint without stopping, even though the decision module—once at the checkpoint and lacking memory of the commitment—calculates that continuing is optimal. Then evaluate whether introducing an observable but unfalsifiable "commitment token" resolves the paradox or merely relocates the conflict between plan-optimality and choice-optimality to a different level of the architecture.
