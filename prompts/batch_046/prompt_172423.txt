At a philosophy-of-science reading group I attend, two regulars—call them Alex and Blake—got into a sustained disagreement over whether dialetheism can be made coherent in an applied setting, specifically for interpreting contradictory outputs from continuous measurement systems operating near a sharp threshold boundary. I stayed out of it, but I want an external adjudication of the arguments as they actually unfolded.

(1) Alex opened by arguing that dialetheism is practically self-defeating in any engineered context. If a sensor genuinely reports both P and not-P at a boundary, and we treat both reports as true, then the system’s own error-diagnostic rules—which depend on consistency to flag corruption—are immediately undermined. Without a global consistency constraint, there is no functional distinction between a meaningful contradiction and a broken signal.

(2) Blake replied that this misses the specific point of paraconsistent logic: explosion is blocked, so accepting one localized contradiction does not license arbitrary conclusions elsewhere. The system can isolate the contradiction to the boundary node, preserve all non-related inferences, and maintain its diagnostics everywhere except that quarantined site. Global consistency is therefore unnecessary if the contradiction is structurally contained.

(3) Alex countered that “structural containment” is only a design hope unless there is a meta-rule certifying that the isolation is stable under updates. But if the threshold is dynamic—if the model shifts or the boundary conditions drift—the contradiction can migrate into adjacent modules. Without a classical meta-theory guaranteeing that the quarantine rules themselves are not contradicted, you have no principled barrier against spread; you are substituting an engineering prayer for a logical guarantee.

(4) Blake responded that this demands an impossible transcendental certificate. Isolation can be achieved modularly—through relevance constraints, information-flow architecture, and update protocols—without invoking classical consistency at any level. The real issue is containment design, not metaphysical consistency. Alex is treating global consistency as a necessary condition for rational belief rather than as one heuristic among others, and thereby smuggling in the very classical framework that dialetheism is meant to replace.

Please adjudicate move by move: for each of (1) through (4), rate the move from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify the rating. Then take a definite, committed position: which party is right about the core issue, what specific point each actually wins or loses, and whether there is a coherent middle position that accepts localized contradictions without requiring either global consistency or pure engineering optimism. Give your verdict with confidence; do not merely lay out the options and leave the choice to me. If your assessment depends on a contested reading of paraconsistent logic or on the feasibility of modular containment, say so explicitly and note where you are leaning.
