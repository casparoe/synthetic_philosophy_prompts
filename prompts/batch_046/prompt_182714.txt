I was grading essays for my phil of mind course and one student wrote about Dennett's Orwellian vs Stalinesque revision, specifically with the color phi illusion and that stopped-clock thing (chronostasis?). Question: "Dennett distinguishes Orwellian and Stalinesque revisions of conscious experience. Explain both, then apply them to the color phi phenomenon (red dot then green dot with apparent motion, but subjects report green motion from the start). Does this case clearly favor one model? Also discuss chronostasis -- the illusion where a clock's second hand seems to hang -- and say whether it works as a parallel case of temporal revision, especially given that recent work suggests color and motion might bind in slightly different windows (around 40ms vs 80ms)."

Student answer: "Orwellian revision is when your brain actually has the wrong experience first and then retroactively overwrites it, like in 1984 where history is rewritten after the fact. Stalinesque is when there's never a wrong experience at all; the brain waits for all the data and then constructs the correct experience post-hoc, like Stalin editing a film before release.

In color phi, people see red at spot A then green at spot B, but report seeing green smoothly moving between them, as if they anticipated the color change. Dennett clearly favors Stalinesque here: the brain collects both color signals and then builds one unified green-motion experience, rather than first experiencing red motion and then Orwell-style correcting it. This shows consciousness is never live in a Cartesian theater.

Chronostasis is parallel. When you glance at a clock, the second hand seems stuck. Your brain stretches that first second of attention and fills in extra duration afterwards, constructing a long tick retroactively. So it's also Stalinesque, not Orwellian, because there was never a normal-length tick that got overwritten -- the long tick was built from the start. Both cases prove that temporal experience is always edited after the fact, and the different binding windows for color vs motion just show the editing happens feature-by-feature."
