Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on whether "collapse" describes genuine societal failures or is a state-centric misdescription of decentralized change. The Institutional-Discontinuity camp holds that rapid, involuntary loss of centralized administration, interregional exchange, and monumental construction—visible in the Late Bronze Age Eastern Mediterranean, the Classic Maya lowlands, and Rapa Nui—constitutes a distinctive phenomenon worthy of its own concept. The Reconfiguration camp holds that local demographic and cultural continuity in those same cases makes "collapse" a misleading teleology that implicitly treats state-level complexity as the normative baseline.

Please produce the co-authored document they should have written:

1. Agreed claims: a numbered list of propositions both sides can sign about settlement patterns, population trajectories, writing-system discontinuities, and environmental pressures across the three cases. Keep formulations precise but do not hinge them on contested quantitative estimates.

2. Disagreement table: state each side's core thesis in wording that camp would accept for itself. Pinpoint the exact divergence: does institutional decoupling without demographic crash count as collapse? Can ritual or symbolic continuity substitute for administrative continuity? Does the concept depend on an unstated normative preference for hierarchy?

3. Cruxes: identify the sub-questions whose resolution would actually move one side. I take the central ones to be: (a) whether a society retaining roughly 90% of its population but permanently losing long-distance trade and hierarchical governance has failed or successfully downsized; and (b) whether the cessation of monumental architecture in the Maya lowlands and on Rapa Nui reflects resource-driven social breakdown or deliberate ritual reorientation. For each crux, specify the concrete archaeological pattern—e.g., continuity in domestic ceramic traditions versus disappearance of elite iconography—that both sides agree should dominate if one view is correct, and record each camp's advance prediction.

4. Verdict on verbal dispute: conclude whether "collapse" can be redefined along separate demographic, institutional, and symbolic vectors to accommodate both intuitions without loss, or whether the disagreement is substantive—specifically, about whether institutional continuity deserves greater explanatory weight than local subsistence persistence.

Format this as a formal joint statement, with sections clearly delineated. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading.
