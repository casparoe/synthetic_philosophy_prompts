ok so here's roughly where i've landed on Jeffrey condtionalization and uncertain evidence — want a consistency check before i present it at the workshop. (1) i take Jeffrey's rule to be correct for all cases of genuinely uncertain evidence: you partition the space, keep your conditional probabilities rigid, and redistribute credence according to the posterior weights on the cells. (2) i think rigidity is best read structurally, not psychologically — P(H|E) stays constant because the evidential relation is stable, not because rational agents have some mental habit. (3) but i also want to draw a sharp line between learning that E holds to degree r and learning that an instrument with reliabilty r outputs E; Jeffrey applies only to the first, and applying it to instrument reports is a category mistake that confuses doxastic updating with calibration. (4) i think the old-evidence problem dissolves once you see that Jeffrey conditioning targets your experiential partition, not the proposition E itself — so if you already knew E but now grasp its relevance, you're updating on a new phenomenal cell, not on E. (5) i think rational priors should never assign zero to empirically live cells of a partition, because then Jeffrey updating is undefined, and that's a diagnostic feature: it shows your prior was already incoherent with experience. (6) finally, i think nested uncertainty — being unsure about the reliabilty of your own updating mechanism — should be handled by applying Jeffrey conditioning at the meta-level over a partition of possible reliabilty values, using the same structural rigidity conditions.

please go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. for each tension, say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. also tell me which single revision cleans up the most. and please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know this stuff — don't present a contested take as if it's the consensus view.
