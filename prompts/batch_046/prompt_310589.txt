I am not an academic philosopher, but I have been reading on my own about how we understand people whose worldviews seem alien to us, and I want to check whether two frameworks I have encountered are actually different or just two descriptions of the same thing. The first is Donald Davidson's idea of radical interpretation: to understand a speaker we must treat them as largely rational and largely correct about the world, working out what their words mean and what they believe simultaneously rather than separately, with the principle of charity forcing us to maximize agreement. The second is W.V.O. Quine's idea of radical translation, where translating an unknown language always leaves room for multiple incompatible manuals that all match the behavioral evidence, so that belief and meaning are underdetermined. I am trying to compare them on a precise point. Davidson's charity seems to block massive error and contradiction, whereas Quine's underdetermination seems to permit translation manuals that assign radically false beliefs, as long as the scheme preserves overall empirical fit. But I wonder whether Davidson's charity is just the internal result of picking the most coherent manual within Quine's space—whether it adds a real constraint or merely names the optimal point. To test this, consider a speaker who behaves rationally in every other respect but consistently asserts, of a single uniform patch, that it is both entirely red and entirely green all over. Would Davidson's framework compel us to reinterpret the color terms or the logical structure so that no contradiction remains, while Quine's framework allows us to preserve the contradiction in our translation manual provided the rest of the manual is empirically adequate? Or do both frameworks force the same reinterpretation? Please compare the two precisely: is the difference between charity and indeterminacy substantive, and if so, which framework better explains why we resist attributing flat contradictions even when the behavioral evidence could technically support them?
