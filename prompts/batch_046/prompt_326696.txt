Mira defends a view she calls retrocausal closure: she claims Death's appointment functions not as a prediction but as a boundary condition on the timeline, and that the merchant's flight to Samarra is not an escape but the necessary causal path that realizes the only consistent endpoint. Jonas argues for epistemic displacement: he holds that the merchant's belief in the report alters the causal structure of the world, so that Death's presence in Samarra is an artifact produced by the knowledge itself rather than a pre-existing fixed point.

The dialogue should unfold during a late-night graduate seminar on fatalism and modal logic, with both participants treating the reconstructed report-version as their shared premise. Each character must be equally competent: Mira should press Jonas on how knowledge can retroactively constitute a causal endpoint without collapsing into a self-fulfilling loop, and Jonas should challenge Mira on whether her closure view can genuinely distinguish between a fixed point and a post-hoc rationalization of coincidence. They must respond directly to each other's actual arguments rather than delivering parallel monologues, and the exchange should feel technically sharp yet spoken—around 800 to 1000 words. Do not attribute the parable to any specific author or text, do not include bibliographic references, and do not invoke named contemporary theories that depend on specialist literature; keep the disagreement grounded in the logic of the reconstructed scenario itself.
