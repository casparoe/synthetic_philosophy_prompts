Following a long and exhausting argument with a friend after our seminar on epistemic normativity, I want to understand why the debate over pragmatic encroachment keeps stalling at precisely the same juncture. We were focused on whether the influence of practical stakes on knowledge attributions is best modeled as a contextual shift in the threshold of justification—what contextualists treat as the movement of epistemic standards—or instead as a domain-restriction effect, where high-stakes environments do not globally raise the bar for belief but selectively disable knowledge of propositions that bear on action, leaving other beliefs epistemically intact. My friend insisted that if pragmatic encroachment is genuine, it undermines contextualism, since contextualism requires that the same subject, holding the same epistemic position, can be evaluated differently by different attributors; yet in the classic bank case, it is the subject’s own practical situation that seems to change whether she knows, not the speaker’s conversational context. I countered that this is exactly what a refined contextualism should predict: the subject’s practical environment is part of the context that fixes the standards governing her own knowledge attributions. By the end we had traced every variation—pilot cases, lottery-ticket skepticism, the hiker facing a life-threatening misstep—without either of us conceding ground.

So I am asking not for a verdict on who is right, but for a diagnosis of the disagreement’s structure. Is this a substantive dispute about the mechanics of knowledge attribution, a verbal disagreement about whether “context” properly includes the subject’s practical environment, or two separate inquiries—one semantic, one normative—occupying the same terminological space? Specifically, do the sides differ about the data (our shared intuitions about the bank and pilot cases), about what counts as an adequate explanation of those intuitions, or about whether the question is even properly about “knowledge” rather than about warranted assertion or responsible belief? Given your diagnosis, what conceptual distinction—perhaps between attributor-sensitive and subject-sensitive parameters, or between epistemic reasons and practical reasons—would need to be drawn sharply to make progress possible? Or should we conclude that the stalemate is permanent because both sides organize the same intuitions with incompatible explanatory priorities, and that no new example or formal result could break the symmetry?
