Write the full specification of desiderata that any adequate normative response to the separateness-of-persons objection against utilitarian aggregation must meet. For each requirement, state the motivating case or conceptual distinction, and mark it as non-negotiable or camp-specific. In particular, include: (1) whether the theory must preserve complete normative isolation between persons—the idea that benefits to one cannot mathematically cancel burdens to another, and what cases like life-saving organ redistribution or forced labor for collective gain show about this; (2) whether it must accommodate porous or overlapping personhood—such as conjoined twins, long-term merged identities, or collective agents—without collapsing back into aggregation, and whether accommodating such cases weakens the objection itself; (3) whether it must distinguish distributive unfairness (who bears costs) from interpersonal justification failure (whether a burden can be justified to the one who bears it), using a concrete scenario where both seem present but come apart; (4) whether it must rule out all forms of impersonal moral arithmetic, or only certain kinds, and what the boundary case of risk-pooling across lives (e.g., vaccination policy with unavoidable side-effects) reveals. Then identify any subset of these desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—especially whether full normative isolation can coexist with any theory that permits trade-offs for the greater good. Finally, briefly score utilitarianism, prioritarianism, and a Scanlon-style contractualist view against your list, noting which failures are fatal and which are repairable. Keep the weight on the criteria and their tensions; the scoring is secondary.
