Second, the Turing inversion: the claim that a machine can acquire intelligent behavioral competence—exemplified by Turing's proposal of an educable "child-machine" shaped through rewards and punishments—without being given an explicit symbolic encoding of the rules it comes to follow, and thus without anything we would call comprehension or understanding of those rules.
