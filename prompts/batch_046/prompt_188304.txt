(1) I accept that transformative experiences involve both an epistemic transformation—wherein one learns what an experience is like only by undergoing it—and a personal transformation, wherein one's core preferences and values shift in ways that are not merely incremental.

(2) I maintain that rational choice, properly construed, requires the ability to project one's future preferences with at least approximate accuracy, and therefore that genuinely transformative experiences lie outside the domain of standard expected-utility reasoning.

(3) Despite this, I hold that individuals should often choose to undergo transformative experiences—such as becoming a parent—precisely when they cannot rationally evaluate them in advance, and that this is not merely permissible but sometimes genuinely obligatory.

(4) I reject any essentialist 'true self' that survives personal transformation intact; instead, I endorse a narrative-self view according to which continuity is constructed through autobiographical coherence rather than underlying psychological sameness.

(5) I further claim that if transformative experiences are genuinely rationally opaque in the way (2) suggests, then the existentialist notion of 'authentic choice'—where one chooses with full awareness of what one is becoming—collapses into incoherence, because the chooser and the chosen self are necessarily discontinuous.

Rank the tensions by severity, specifying for each what must be given up.
