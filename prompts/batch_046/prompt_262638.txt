Write a decision tree that sorts philosophical positions on the conflict between luminosity and the KK principle, intended for a graduate seminar on epistemic architecture for bounded agents. The root question should be whether a subject who knows p is necessarily in a position to know that she knows p. From there, branch on (i) whether the KK principle is rejected entirely, modified, or retained; (ii) whether mental conditions can be luminous at all; and (iii) whether borderline or vague instances of knowing should be admitted in a self-monitoring model. Each leaf must be a named, coherent stance—such as strict anti-luminosity, graded-knowledge gradualism, or a modified transparency view—and each internal node must be phrased as a concrete yes/no or either/or question that a student could answer without already knowing which position it points to. The tree should contain exactly five branch nodes and should flag any pair of leaves that it cannot cleanly separate. Do not include bibliographies, citation requests, or lists of further reading.
