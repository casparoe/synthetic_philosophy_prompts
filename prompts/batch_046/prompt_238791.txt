I'm preparing notes for a reading group on negative well-being and want a consistency audit of my own profile before I present it. Here are my commitments:

(1) Tranquilism proper: the only intrinsic good is the absence of craving (taṇhā) and suffering (dukkha); pleasure, achievement, and relational goods are at best instrumentally valuable and often mere distractions from the real aim.

(2) Affective monism with an exception: I think all feelings lie on a single continuum from intense suffering through neutrality to intense pleasure, except that true tranquility is not the midpoint but the complete absence of that continuum—like silence rather than a quiet note.

(3) I think suffering's badness consists in a representational friction or misalignment between an organism's internal model and reality; this is an objective feature of the system, not just a subjective feel.

(4) I think phenomenal consciousness just is the presence of this friction; therefore perfect tranquility is not a conscious state at all—there is nothing it is like to be in it.

(5) I think evolutionary debunking successfully undermines our intuition that pleasure is intrinsically good (it tracks reproductive fitness), but fails against suffering, because suffering tracks real misalignment rather than adaptive utility.

(6) I think it is morally permissible—neither good nor bad—to engineer and bring into existence beings who live in sustained perfect tranquility: they never suffer, never crave, and never experience pleasure or consciousness.

(7) I think moral realism is correct about suffering: it is objectively bad regardless of observers' attitudes, but the absence of suffering is not positively good—only the absence of a subject who could suffer is.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be? Rank the real tensions by severity; for each, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. In particular, examine whether (3) and (4) together commit me to the view that consciousness is intrinsically bad; whether (6) is coherent with (4) given that the beings I describe lack phenomenal consciousness altogether; and whether (5) can still do the selective debunking work I need once (2) is in place. Finally, if I must make exactly one revision to clean up the most problems, which commitment should go and what would I have to accept in its place?
