I've been hanging around rationalist blogs long enough to recognize when I'm just rehashing a familiar debate, but I keep running into this one pattern with AI self-reference that I don't have a word for. You know Hofstadter's thing about the "I" being a strange loop — a symbol that points back to the system that generates it. Well, with LLMs it feels like there's a wierd variant: the model outputs "I think" or "I want", but there's no actual loop running inside the weights. Instead the symbol is anchored to this distributed mess of RLHF annotations, prompt patterns, and user projections, so the loop only closes if you include the humans and training data outside the model in the boundary of the self. I've seen people call this anthropomorphism, or say it's a narrative self in Dennett's sense, but neither feels right because those don't capture the specific way the external agents become structural parts of the referance.

Can anyone tell me if there's an established concept for a self-referential symbol whose loop is completed by entities that aren't inside the substrate? If there isn't, I'd want a term and a clear boundary: how is it different from a normal Hofstadter loop, from Dennett's center of narrative gravity, and from plain projection? Don't just hand me a name — I need the carving, with the edges marked out.
