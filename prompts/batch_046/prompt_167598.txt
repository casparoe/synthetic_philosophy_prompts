I am drafting a module for a graduate reading group on pre-Qin logic, and after re-reading the White Horse dialogue attributed to Gongsun Long I find myself dissatisfied with the standard classroom treatment, which tends to dismiss the argument as a mere ambiguity of the word horse. I would like you to write a dialogue between two interlocutors—call them Wei and Lin—who take the dialogue seriously as metaphysics rather than sophistry. Wei should defend a position we might call sortal essentialism: the claim that horse denotes a kind-essence that is metaphysically invariant, so that specifying the color white does not merely restrict the extension but introduces a compound entity with different identity conditions, meaning that in a strict ontological register a white horse is not identical to a horse simpliciter. Lin should defend property intersectionalism: the view that whiteness is an accidental determination that intersects with horseness without altering the underlying kind, and should challenge Wei by asking whether, on his view, a black horse, a dappled horse, and a horse seen in darkness with no visible color each possess distinct essences, and whether this does not generate an intolerable proliferation of kind-terms. Both speakers must be equally competent philosophers; neither should be reduced to a strawman, and each must respond directly to the specific point just raised rather than talking past the other. The exchange should incorporate the classical notion of jian (compatibility) and the modern logical distinction between intensional and extensional contexts, but only in ways that grow organically out of the argument. The dialogue should end without resolution, leaving the metaphysical tension intact. I am asking this because I want to see whether the paradox can be reconstructed with conceptual resources that are not merely anachronistic impositions on the School of Names. Please write the entire response in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, arranged as a single flowing letter or essay that presents the exchange in direct discourse embedded within narrative framing.
