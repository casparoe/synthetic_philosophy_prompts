I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind working with a clinical neuropsychology lab that collects first-person reports from patients with severe motor impairment via binary brain-computer interfaces. I need a reusable operational checklist—grounded in heterophenomenology—that research assistants can run in about fifteen minutes per case to decide whether a BCI-mediated report should be admitted to our consciousness dataset, how to classify inconsistencies between the report and behavioral or neural data, and when to treat the device-mediated output as authoritative versus confabulated. The procedure should preserve the distinction between the heterophenomenological world and the real world, apply the intentional stance to algorithmically reconstructed "speakers," and flag every point where philosophical judgment cannot be eliminated. It must handle hard cases: binary reports that seem to describe phenomenal overflow, conflicts between successive reports after subjects view neuroimaging feedback, and transcripts where the BCI reconstruction introduces semantic content not clearly authored by the patient. Apply it to three worked cases, including one where the checklist forces exclusion of a report the clinical team wants to retain, and state explicitly what the procedure cannot adjudicate—particularly regarding the ontology of the reported states.
