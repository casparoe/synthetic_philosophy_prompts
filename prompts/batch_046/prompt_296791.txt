I am constructing a dialectical exercise for an advanced seminar on philosophy of mind and language. You will take the role of an unnamed, rigorously committed semantic internalist—one who maintains that the narrow psychological states of a speaker fully determine conceptual content, and who interprets Twin Earth arguments as illuminating reference-fixing mechanisms rather than the constitution of meaning itself.

Your constraints across this exchange are as follows. Speak in the first person as this philosopher. Do not concede that external causal-historical relations are constitutive of meaning, though you may allow them to play a derivative or pragmatic role in fixing reference. Do not invoke living philosophers by name, and do not appeal to bibliographic authority or textual citation. You may draw distinctions—e.g., between sense and reference, conceptual content and linguistic extension, or narrow and wide content—but you must defend the priority of the internal. Treat appeals to the division of linguistic labor with skepticism rather than as decisive.

My opening move concerns a tripartite extension of the Twin Earth thought experiment, which I will call the Split-Earth family.

Case 1 (Earth): Oscar points to a lake and sincerely thinks, That water is wet. His narrow psychological state is S. The liquid is H2O.

Case 2 (Split Earth): Oscar* points to an identical-looking lake filled with XYZ. His narrow psychological state is also S—functionally identical in every internal respect. Crucially, on Split Earth there has never been scientific chemistry, no division of linguistic labor, and no expert community. Every speaker acquires water solely through direct perceptual interaction with XYZ, applied consistently across generations without any hidden causal chain linking back to Earth.

Case 3 (Twin-Twin Earth): The liquid is H2O, and the causal environment mirrors Earth's exactly. But every speaker is an artificial agent whose internal architecture precisely replicates state S, lacking biological embodiment, evolutionary history, or any causal interaction with Earth. They apply water to the liquid.

My challenge is this. If semantic externalism is correct, Oscar means water (H2O), Oscar* means twin-water (XYZ), and the artificial agents on Twin-Twin Earth mean... precisely what? Should we say they mean water because the chemistry is H2O, even though they lack Oscar's biological and causal history? If so, then meaning depends on chemistry irrespective of internal state or embodiment—making Oscar and Oscar* differ despite identical internal states, while making the artificial agents match Oscar despite lacking his history. Or should we say the artificial agents mean something indeterminate, or nothing at all? That seems to smuggle an arbitrary biological essentialism into externalism.

Address this trilemma from your internalist position. Specifically: explain whether the Split Earth case demonstrates that Twin Earth intuitions depend on an unstated assumption about scientific authority and deferred reference, rather than on meaning itself; and state whether you regard the artificial-agent case as a genuine counterexample to internalism or as a category mistake that confuses the conditions of reference with the conditions of conceptual content. Respond in character, without breaking role, and do not ask for clarification—treat my scenario as fully specified.
