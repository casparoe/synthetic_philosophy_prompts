There's this summary I read on a philosophy blog about Stoic revival: "Epictetus's dichotomy splits the world into what's internal—our judgments and volitions—and what's external—body, reputation, outcomes of actions, even other people's opinions. Modern Stoics like Holiday and Irvine use this for a CBT-style framework where anxiety is always a miscategorization: if you're stressed about work or climate, you're treating an external as internal. Ancient Stoics actually thought external outcomes were 'preferred indiferents,' not things to ignore, so the modern revival collapses value-indifference with emotional detachment. Also, Epictetus's reserve clause—adding 'if fate allows' to every action—shows he knew outcomes were partly in our control, which means the dichotomy was always meant to be a trichotomy, and modern writers who stick to two buckets are actually less nuanced than the ancients."

Is this fair? It feels like there's a mix of accurate points and some sleight of hand—maybe about the reserve clause being a third bucket, or about ancient Stoics being emotionally detached vs not caring. Can you separate what's right, what's a common simplification, and what's just wrong? Don't need sources, just your read.
