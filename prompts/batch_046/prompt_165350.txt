As a journalist who writes about science and ideas, I am constructing a live stress-test of Imre Lakatos’s methodology of scientific research programmes, applied not to the usual textbook cases but to a deliberately constructed, hybrid research programme that should be unfamiliar in this exact form. You are to play Lakatos himself, speaking in the first person in 1974, defending the normative force of his distinction between progressive and degenerating problemshifts. Rules: remain in character at all times; draw only on the conceptual apparatus Lakatos actually possessed—hard core, protective belt, progressive versus degenerating problemshift, novel predictions, and ad hoc adjustments—together with the case description I provide below; do not invoke philosophers, sociologists, or results from after 1974 by name unless I do first; concede a small technical point if it strengthens your defense, but never abandon the claim that the framework distinguishes genuine scientific growth from mere protective-belt inflation; speak with precision and urgency, as someone who believes philosophy of science must be normative rather than merely descriptive.

Here is the case. The Predictive-Processing Programme in cognitive science holds as its hard core the claim that all cognition is hierarchical Bayesian inference. Its protective belt has recently expanded to include auxiliary hypotheses about precision-weighting failures, attentional modulation, and social-context parameters. It now faces three persistent anomalies: first, pure Bayesian models consistently fail to predict human reasoning in high-uncertainty social contexts; second, empirical studies show that expert scientific communities systematically violate individual Bayesian updating norms; third, the core framework has not generated any risky, novel prediction that distinguishes it from rival enactivist accounts—instead, each rival success is accommodated by post-hoc parameter tuning within the belt. I contend that by your own lights this is a degenerating problemshift: the belt is growing while the empirical content shrinks, and every rescue is ad hoc.

My opening move: defend the programme’s status, but do so specifically. Tell me exactly what theoretical development or observable result would count, in your framework, as a genuinely progressive problemshift for this programme rather than another ad hoc rescue. If you reply that we must wait and see, then explain why that waiting period does not render your criterion unfalsifiable in practice. Address the social-epistemology anomaly directly—does the fact that science is done by groups rather than solitary Bayesian agents belong inside the protective belt, or does it threaten the hard core? Begin your reply now.
