I serve on the Constitutional Review Council of a parliamentary democracy, and we must issue a formal advisory memo—due before the end of the legislative session—on whether a proposed constitutional amendment, tentatively titled the Intergenerational Climate Lockbox, should be certified for national referendum. The amendment would bind all future parliaments for forty years to maintain emission-reduction targets calibrated to a specific 2025 scientific baseline, altering them only by a four-fifths supermajority; yet it also contains an unusual "epistemic sunset" provision that automatically dissolves the binding if three consecutive quadrennial scientific assessments, conducted by an independent technical panel, conclude that the baseline model has been falsified in its core predictive parameters. Proponents defend it as a rational Ulysses-style precommitment: present democratic majorities foresee their own future vulnerability to short-term economic pressure and therefore legitimately constrain their successors, while the sunset clause preserves adaptability to genuine scientific revolution rather than to mere political convenience. Opponents argue that any constitutional entrenchment of substantive policy violates the principle that no democratic generation can bind another, and that the sunset clause either renders the commitment illusory—if the science is genuinely open to contestation—or else dangerously delegates constitutional authority to an unelected body whose epistemic judgments are not democratically accountable.

Please draft the Council's decision memo recommending either certification for referendum or denial, with an explicit bottom line and a concrete time frame for implementation or revision. Your reasoning should treat the democratic-sovereignty objection with full seriousness, identifying precisely where it is strongest rather than dismissing it as a conceptual mistake, and it should evaluate whether the epistemic sunset clause genuinely resolves the precommitment paradox or instead introduces a new institutional tension between rational self-binding and epistemic humility. The memo should be structured as an actionable institutional document, articulating clear criteria that distinguish legitimate constitutional precommitment from illegitimate substantive entrenchment, and it should conclude with specific conditions—if any—under which the Council could endorse a revised version of the amendment, including whether the sunset mechanism should be retained, modified, or removed.
