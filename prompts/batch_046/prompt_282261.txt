One party reads Putnam's internal realism and his sustained critique of metaphysical realism as licensing a genuinely cognitive religious discourse: religious claims can be true within a conceptual scheme without requiring supernatural ontology, analogously to how scientific claims possess objectivity within scientific internal realism. The opposing party reads Putnam's later emphasis on forms of life and his rejection of the picture theory of language as showing that religious discourse is a practice of commitment rather than a scheme-dependent description of reality, so that mapping ethical objectivity onto religious contexts either collapses into scientism or retreats into an uncritical fideism. Then present the cruxes, including whether Putnam's distinction between objective and merely subjective judgments can survive translation into religious language without losing its normative force, and whether his anti-psychologism about meaning operates differently in religious language-games than in scientific ones—an asymmetry that, if genuine, would undermine the analogy between scientific internal realism and religious belief that the first party relies upon. For each crux, state what argument, case study, or conceptual clarification both agree would bear upon it, with each side's predicted outcome recorded in advance.

Please write in a formal, polished register appropriate to an academic joint statement, and inject the specific conceptual tension described above rather than rehearsing a generic debate about faith and reason. Separate what is settled in the relevant scholarship—Putnam's identification with Judaism, his rejection of verificationist dismissals of religion, his critique of metaphysical realism—from what remains genuinely contested about the coherence of his religious philosophy, and do not present either the internal-realist or the quietist interpretation as the consensus view among those who know the topic well.
