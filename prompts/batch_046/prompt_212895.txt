I'm a volunteer editor for a small open-access journal on early modern political thought, and I keep recieveing submissions where the author basically grades dead people on a curve they invented yesterday. I need a reusable proceedure I can run in about ten minutes per paper to flag presentism, without becoming an expert in every century. The core idea is separating "they failed by their own lights" (call it internal-standard failure) from "they failed because they didn't share our framework" (call it framework-transplant failure). I want a decision flow in words: the gates in order, the exact question I ask at each, what counts as passing, and where I have to make a judgment call because the philosophy runs out. It needs to catch both moral back-projection and causal framing leakage—using concepts that depend on outcomes the actors couldn't have foreseen. Flag the misfire cases, especially when careful contextual history still sounds judgmental.

Then run it on three hypothetical submissions:
1. A paper calling 1790s French republicans "morally deficient" for not extending citizenship to women, without showing they possessed the concept of gendered exclusion.
2. A paper saying the 1834 Poor Law failed because it ignored "systemic feedback loops" visible only after 20th-century cybernetics.
3. A paper criticizing an 1867 surgeon for refusing antiseptic methods—Lister published that same year, the surgeon was practicing in late 1868 in a region where the journal had arrived. I think this should clear, but I want to see if your proceedure gives me an answer I won't like, maybe by smuggling in modern medical ethics.

Also say what the proceedure can't do—like settling whether presentism is ever okay, or which internal standards are legitimate.

Finally, tell me what would change your mind about where the gate is drawn, and flag where your reasoning is shakiest—probably the boundary between internal failure and transplant failure when the historical actors themselves were deeply divided.
