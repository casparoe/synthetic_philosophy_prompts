ok so i've been thinking about incommensurable values and hard choices, specifically Ruth Chang's idea that some options are on a par rather than just tied or incommensurable. there's a bunch of open stuff here and i'm trying to figure out where to actually spend my time if i were writing a thesis on it.

so here's my rough list of open questions / lines of work:

1. is parity a real fourth relation (besides better, worse, equal) or just a failure of our comparison procedures? like, is it ontic or epistemic?
2. if parity is real, does that break expected utility theory in a fundamental way, or can we just patch it with some kind of permissive choice rule?
3. what's going on psychologicaly when people face hard choices? is the creative resolution Chang talks about actually happening, or are people just using hidden weights they won't admit to?
4. does the existence of hard choices undermine the idea that there's always a right answer in moral dilemmas? and if so, what does that do to normative ethics?

i want to know which of these is actually upstream of the others. like, if i settle (1) one way, does (2) collapse or does it stay open? and which ones are tractable with current philosophy/psych tools vs which ones are just going to stay muddy?

also, there's this weird implication people don't talk about enough: if parity is real and you pick one option arbitrarily, should you feel regret? not just sadness, but the specific kind of regret that comes from knowing you made a mistake? if parity means neither option is a mistake, does that dissolve regret in a way that's actually kind of disturbing?

anyway, which of these lines is most worth pursuing? i'm particularly interested in whether resolving the ontology of parity (1) is the crux that everything else depends on, or if that's overrated and the real work is in (3) or (4). defend an ordering: what's tractable, what's neglected, what's just going to spin wheels. don't just list them.
