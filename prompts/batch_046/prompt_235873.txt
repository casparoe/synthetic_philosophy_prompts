For my philosophy of science seminar on determinism and chaos, I want to construct a decision tree that sorts philosophical positions on whether deterministic chaos genuinely undermines determinism, going beyond the standard textbook taxonomy. The tree should begin with whether one holds that the fundamental laws are strictly deterministic at all scales. From there, branch through three concrete distinctions: whether sensitive dependence on initial conditions is compatible with deterministic laws; whether practical unpredictability arising from finite measurement precision is merely epistemic or reveals ontic openness; and whether the existence of nearby "shadow" trajectories—paths that remain arbitrarily close to an observed chaotic orbit despite divergence—restores predictive reliability, or whether such shadowing is physically irrelevant because real instruments never achieve the required precision. Each leaf should carry a named position (for example, hard determinist, epistemic chaos compatibilist, ontic openness advocate, or shadowing skeptic) plus one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Please flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, identify any logically possible but unoccupied combinations with a brief diagnosis of whether they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and phrase every node as a yes-or-no question that a student could answer without knowing in advance which interpretation it leads to. Limit the tree to about six to eight leaves and keep the framing suitable for an undergraduate handout.
