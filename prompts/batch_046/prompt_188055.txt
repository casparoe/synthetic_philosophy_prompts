I'm preparing a podcast panel interview with a political philosopher—left unnamed—who defends a position she calls threshold institutional conservatism. She fuses Burke's argument that durable institutions encode accumulated practical wisdom with Chesterton's fence principle: one ought not dismantle a social fence without knowing why it was erected. To this she adds a distinctive epistemic gate: an institution should be retained not merely because critics misunderstand its function, but specifically because they have failed to demonstrate both (a) that the harm they cite is causally attributable to the institution's design rather than to exogenous social conditions, and (b) that any proposed replacement can be enacted without generating irreversible structural vulnerabilities. She applies the framework mainly to democratic procedural rules and market regulations, explicitly distancing it from appeals to cultural tradition alone.

Draft eight to ten interview questions that engage the mechanics of this view rather than asking for a restatement. I want at least one question probing how she distinguishes genuine institutional wisdom from mere path dependency; at least one pressing whether her attribution condition in (a) places an unfair evidentiary burden on reformers seeking to dismantle structures that entrench historical injustice; at least one asking what qualifies as an irreversible structural vulnerability and whose judgment should settle that question; and at least one examining whether Chesterton's fence, interpreted through her threshold, permits any incremental reform or only preservation. For each of these, include a sharp follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to offer—for instance, if she appeals to causal complexity in social systems to defend (a), ask how her view avoids unfalsifiability; if she appeals to democratic deliberation to settle (b), ask why that deliberation ought to be constrained by her epistemic threshold rather than by straightforward majority preference. Do not include generic biographical or career questions. The audience is familiar with basic Burkean prescription and the fence metaphor, but not with her particular synthesis, so the questions should build that bridge in real time.
