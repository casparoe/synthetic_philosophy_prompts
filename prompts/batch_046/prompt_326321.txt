Write a dialogue between a Scanlon-style contractualist and a defender of "scale-sensitive" contractualism about aggregation in triage. The first should argue that you can never justify harming one person to save many from smaller harms, because contractualism requires pairwise justification—so even a hospital algorithm that ranks patients cant defend giving the worst score to one critically ill patient just to help five moderately sick ones. The second should push back that institutional rules are justified ex ante to everyone, so minimizing total serious harm is actually acceptable at the system level, and that treating every decision as pairwise makes large-scale coordination impossible. Have them actually respond to each other, not talk past each other, and maybe include a weird concrete case like an emergency drone that can either deliver one heavy life-saving payload to a remote clinic or split it into five lighter doses for different villages. Keep it casual and dont ask for references or reading lists.
