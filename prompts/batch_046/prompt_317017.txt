Based on the principle of indifference and with explicit safeguards against the ambiguities shown by Bertrand's paradox, please provide a reusable decision procedure I can run in under thirty minutes per case. Apply it to three worked cases: estimating the trajectory distance of a bullet over flat terrain; assigning a reaction-time distribution inside a sealed container with an unknown internal temperature gradient; and resolving a maritime boundary dispute framed in continuous two-dimensional geospatial coordinates, where rotational symmetry breaks uniformity and the procedure should yield the unwelcome conclusion that no single distribution is defensible. For each case, walk through the ordered steps, mark exactly where formal rules end and expert judgment takes over, note the limitations and misfire risks, and state clearly what the procedure cannot do. Please answer in American English.
