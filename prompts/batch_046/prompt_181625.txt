I have come to think that genuine moral dilemmas—situations in which you are genuinely required to do two incompatible things—are impossible. Whenever obligations appear to clash, I believe closer inspection always reveals that one was only an apparent duty, like a shadow cast by a stronger obligation; the lingering distress afterward is not proof of a real conflict but just the emotional echo of violating something that was never truly binding, perhaps a kind of failure in our emotional bookkeeping. What would count against this view? I want the arguments, intuitions, and possible cases that should move me, sorted by how decisive they would be—from fatal to merely awkward—including whether unavoidable moral wrongdoing is best explained as a genuine dilemma or as this kind of bookkeeping error. I also want to know whether defenders of my position have qualified it so that conflicts become impossible by definition; if so, has the view been rendered unfalsifiable, and is that a problem for a claim of this kind? Please distinguish what those who believe in real dilemmas themselves concede as counting against my view from what they merely assert against it, and give an honest assessment of how exposed the position actually is.
