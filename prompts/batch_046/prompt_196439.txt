I'm a philosophy prof who ended up on the IRB for our psych dept. There's a protocol up for review next tues that’s making everyone weirdly philosophical. The study uses VR to test inattentional blindness—subjects navigate a virtual room while a hidden object sits in their peripheral vision. Standard stuff, except the PI's theoretical framing leans hard on Berkeley's master argument. She writes that for any given subject, objects outside the current attentional window are literally unperceived, and since Berkeley showed it’s impossible to conceive of something existing unperceived, those objects dont exist for the subject during the trial. She uses this to argue that hiding the object isn’t deception—there is nothing actually present for them to be deceived about. She also wants to waive the usual post-experiment disclosure about the hidden stimulus, claiming disclosure would force subjects to conceive of something that, for them, never existed.

Half the board says this is just fancy language for they didnt notice, and the ethical rules dont change. The other half says she is using a metaphysical claim to dodge informed consent, and if we approve it we are endorsing a weird ontology just to avoid debriefing.

Can you draft our memo? Recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions—whatever you think fits. It needs to be something we can circulate thurs and that the PI can actually respond to. Don't just quote Berkeley at her; engage the argument seriously. And please address the strongest point for the side you are not taking—dont just strawman it.
