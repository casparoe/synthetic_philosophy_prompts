To me there's clearly two spheres, not one sphere existant in two places, so that feels like a knockdown counterexample to the idea that if everything is the same then it's the same object. But then I think about quantum mechanics, like two electrons in a singlet state where they share every property including a distributed position, and physics says they're genuinely indisernible—not just that we can't tell them apart, but that there's no fact of the matter about which is which. That feels totally different from the spheres, like in the quantum case they really don't have separate identities. Are they two things or one thing running twice? There's also the duplicate universe thought experiment—if there's a perfect copy of our world with every particle matching but zero causal connection, I think it's a different universe, but I can't name a single property that differs. The one I keep going back and forth on is server migration: I clone a running server, kill the original, and point all traffic to the clone with identical state and zero downtime. Did the same server survive, or is it just a perfect replacement? Sometimes I think continuity of the running process is what matters, other times it feels like I'm just fooling myself.

So is there a coherent principle about when objects stay distinct despite being indescernible that can handle the spheres, the electrons, the containers, and the clone?
