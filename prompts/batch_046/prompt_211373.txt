I work as a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, but a recent conversation with colleagues about whether artificial neural networks genuinely "think" led me back to the older question of whether the human brain itself is best modeled as a computer running software, rather than merely being a physical system that can be described computationally. I am not looking for a survey of the usual pro-and-con arguments; I want the specification first. Write the full list of desiderata that any adequate theory addressing "is the brain a computer?" must satisfy: the empirical phenomena it must explain, the conceptual distinctions it must preserve—such as that between literal algorithm execution and observer-relative as-if computation, or between digital symbolic processing and analog neuromorphic dynamics—the boundary cases it must classify correctly, and the results from neuroscience or computer science it cannot contradict, with the motivating consideration behind each requirement.

In particular, I want the list to include a criterion that addresses neuromodulatory stochasticity—specifically, whether phenomena like dopaminergic noise and stochastic resonance in cortical circuits should be treated as a form of intrinsic error-correction within a computational architecture, or as evidence that the brain operates through analog, non-algorithmic dynamics that resist the software metaphor altogether. I also want the theory to handle substitution cases, such as whether a brain-computer interface that replaces neural tissue can preserve the "same program" without changing the substrate, and what that implies about whether computation is intrinsic to the brain's physical organization. For each desideratum, flag whether it is genuinely non-negotiable or whether it reflects a prejudice of one particular camp. If any subset of these requirements turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable—for instance, if respecting the universal-computation thesis makes it impossible to claim the brain computes in any specially explanatory way—say so explicitly and identify which desiderata must be abandoned.

Finally, briefly score the three main contenders—classical symbolic computationalism, connectionist or neural-network functionalism, and the view that computation is not an intrinsic property of physical systems—against your list, but keep the weight on the criteria and their conflicts rather than on the scorecard. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I need something constructed from conceptual analysis alone, suitable for evaluation without web access.
