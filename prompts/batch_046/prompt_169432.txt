You are a professional philosopher of history with expertise in causal explanation, philosophy of science, and the ontology of social processes. Before composing the dialogue, briefly brainstorm the core commitments, strongest objections, and conceptual resources of each interlocutor. Think carefully about where their arguments genuinely intersect and where they risk talking past one another, then write with precision.

Write a formal dialogue between Dr. Elena Voss and Dr. Marcus Chen. Voss defends a position she calls symmetrical causation: pathogenic microbes, abrupt climate shifts, and volcanic winters should be treated as historical actors rather than mere background conditions, but only when they are captured by institutional feedback loops—what she terms translation mechanisms—that convert physical effects into durable social restructuring (for instance, a harvest collapse altering labor contracts, which then reshape property law). She insists this is not environmental determinism, because the physical event has no predetermined social outcome; its causal efficacy is mediated, contingent, and distributed across human and non-human nodes. Chen defends intentional mediation: he argues that attributing agency to non-human entities is a category error that risks precisely the determinism Voss wants to avoid. For Chen, translation mechanisms are not shared agency but purely human institutional responses; the explanatory work is done by intentional actors interpreting conditions and making contingent political choices. To call the volcano an actor, he claims, obscures the agency embedded in those mechanisms and slides toward treating societies as passively molded by nature.

The exchange must be real back-and-forth: each speaker must respond directly to the specific point just made, not deliver parallel monologues. Both should be equally competent—no strawman. They should address: whether mediated feedback loops justify distributing agency to nature or merely reveal human adaptability; whether denying agency to non-humans is an unjustified anthropocentrism or a necessary conceptual boundary; how to preserve the causal power of physical events without collapsing into environmental determinism; and what empirical or conceptual result would genuinely weaken Voss’s view—say, a case where an identical environmental shock produces radically different institutional outcomes with no mediating mechanism in between, or conversely a case where a mechanism operates with no environmental trigger at all. Keep the dialogue conceptually dense, formally polished, and entirely self-contained: do not cite named scholars, do not provide a bibliography or reading list, and do not ask for citations. The argument should stand on its own logic.
