I am a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun serious study in metaphysics and philosophy of science, and I have come to hold a position that modernizes Bradley’s regress argument about relations and applies it directly to scientific explanation. Specifically, I maintain that the unity of any complex proposition—including a theoretical law linking multiple measurable quantities—cannot be accounted for by external relations among its constituent terms, since every relation requires a further relation to connect it to its relata, generating an infinite regress. The only coherent resolution, on my view, is to deny that relations are ontologically fundamental and to accept that apparently distinct terms and relations are internal modifications of a single, non-relational ground, which I call the integrative field. This field is not composed of parts but manifests differentiated structures as its own self-determination. Consequently, explanatory unity in physics is not achieved through relational structures such as causation or logical entailment among discrete entities, but through the manifestation of one integrative field in apparently plural aspects.

What would genuinely count against this position? I am not asking for the standard dissolution of Bradley’s regress by treating relations as internal or by adopting pluralistic realism; I want to know which specific intuitions about scientific practice—say, regarding the reality of causal mechanisms, the independence of measurable variables, or the success of reductionist programs—would have to be abandoned if my view is correct, and which formal results in logic, physics, or the philosophy of mathematics either accommodate or undermine it. Which concrete cases—perhaps in quantum entanglement, in the individuation of fundamental particles, or in the explanatory failure of micro-reduction—would serve as decisive defeaters rather than merely awkward anomalies? Among these potential defeaters, which ones would a committed defender of this updated Bradleyan view be forced to concede as damaging, and which could be dismissed by redescribing the case as an appearance internal to the integrative field? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward.

I am especially interested in whether this view has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if every apparent counterexample can be reinterpreted as an internal modification of the field, does that insulation constitute a virtue of metaphysical coherence or a vice that renders the position empty? And if it is indeed empty in that sense, is that emptiness fatal, or can a metaphysical framework be permitted to be irrefutable without being philosophically worthless? I would also like an honest assessment of whether the position risks collapsing into a rival account—say, a form of structural realism or a Spinozistic monism—and if so, whether that collapse is a genuine defeat or merely a relabeling.
