Write a villanelle—nineteen lines in the traditional pattern of five tercets followed by a concluding quatrain, using only two rhymes and two alternating refrains—about Jorge Luis Borges’s metaphysical denial of successive, linear time, particularly as developed in his essayistic reflections on Schopenhauer, Hume, and Berkeley, where chronological flow is reduced to an illusion sustained by present memory and personal identity across moments is shown to lack any substantial ground. The poem should not merely invoke Borges’s familiar imagery of labyrinths, mirrors, or infinite libraries; instead, the villanelle’s formal machinery should perform the argument itself. Let the first refrain assert an apparent continuity of the self in time (for instance, a line such as I am still the one who waits), and let the second refrain assert its dissolution (for instance, the one who waits is never me). Through the five tercets, allow these refrains to undergo a minimal, systematic mutation—one word changed, inverted, or grammatically altered at each appearance—so that by the final quatrain they have either exchanged positions or collapsed into a single contradictory statement. Use the rhyme sounds consistently, and let the tercets trace three conceptual phases: first, the naive experience of narrative sequence and enduring selfhood; second, the reduction of past and future to present mental images and expectations, dissolving chronological flow; third, the consequence that if every moment is an isolated present, the self that claims to endure is only a grammatical fiction. The closing quatrain should present both mutated refrains together, leaving the paradox structurally unresolved rather than resolved by argument. Do not include citations, bibliographic references, or reading lists; do not quote verbatim from Borges’s texts. The result should be accurate enough that a reader versed in Borges’s philosophy of time recognizes the specific argument being enacted through form, and compelling enough as verse that the poem’s own structure is the demonstration.
