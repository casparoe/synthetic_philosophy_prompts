At a rationalist meetup last week two friends got stuck on whether self-deception is even coherant — specifically the kind where you build an automated filter (like a script that hides your own performance metrics until after you submit work) and then genuinely act like you dont know you'll do badly.

(1) Maya said thats not self-deception, its just distributed ignorance: the knowledge lives in the script, not in you, so theres no single mind holding both beliefs at once. The extended-mind framing is overblown here.

(2) Leo pushed back: if the filter is your own design, set up precisely because you know your weakness, then the belief is causally active in your system — youre just walling it off from conscious access. Thats functionally identical to self-deception, and calling it outsourced is just renaming the problem.

(3) Maya replied that functional identity isnt mental identity. A calculator holds answers but doesnt believe them; your script holds data but you dont believe it during the act. Self-deception needs simultaneous contradictory beliefs in one subject, and the script breaks the simultaneity.

(4) Leo said thats too quick — people already accept subconscious beliefs drive behavior without being conscious. If your subconscious knows and your conscious self denies, thats the classic structure. The script is just a prosthetic subconscious.
