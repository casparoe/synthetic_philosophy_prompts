hey, i'm putting together a taxonomy/decision tree for my philosophy of mind seminar — grad students they know the sphex wasp example but dont want them just naming philosophers. i want it to sort positions on sphexishness: is it a failure of freedom, intelligence, or neither once you take Darwin/Turing-style competence-without-comprehension seriously?

the tree should start with: do you think the wasp's problem is representational (it cant update its internal model) or volitional (it represents things fine but cant choose to act differently)? then split the representational branch by whether updating requires explicit reasoning or can be implicit/subpersonal. split the volitional branch by whether determinism makes all complex agents sphexish or whether evolutionary plasticity saves us. then add a third branch for people who reject the framing entirely — who think rigidity is just optimal competence and "free will" is expensive, wasteful flexibility.

the leaves should include: (1) a compatibilist flexibility view (Dennett-style, evolved plasticity, determinism irrelevant), (2) a libertarian break view (we can intervene in causal chains, the wasp cant), (3) hard determinism (were all loops, just nested ones), (4) unnamed "optimal rigidity" (freedom is the bug, not the feature), and (5) unnamed "recursive sphex" (Turing-style computation just pushes mechanical rigidity up to a higher-order loop). flag any pairs the tree cant separate — i think (1) and the implicit-updating view might collapse unless you ask about consciousness. also note any empty logical slots: is there a coherent view that demands explicit reasoning but still denies free will, or does that just reduce to (3)? phrase every node as a concrete claim a student can answer without knowing the philosopher-label.

please keep it to about 5-6 decision nodes, audience is grad students, and build it from the logical space not from a list of names. and if my framing confuses cognitive rigidity with determinism in a way that makes the tree garbage, say so plainly — dont politely build a broken instrument just to be nice.
