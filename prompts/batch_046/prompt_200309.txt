Rank these four interpretations of Al-Ghazali's occasionalism—from the strongest denial of creaturely efficacy to the weakest—by how defensible each is against the charge that occasionalism renders empirical prediction impossible: (1) pure occasionalism, in which fire has no connection to burning except divine custom; (2) occasion-regularity, where created things are stable occasions but God alone is the efficient cause; (3) a modified channel occasionalism proposed here, where secondary causes exist structurally but have zero independent efficacy, serving only as passive conduits for divine power; and (4) a derivative-power view, in which creatures possess real but entirely God-dependent causal capacities. For each rank, state in a sentence whether its compatibility with scientific practice is a settled point or genuinely contested among people who study kalam, and separate what Al-Ghazali actually held (to the extent that is settled) from what these interpretations add.
