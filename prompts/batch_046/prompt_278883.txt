A philosophy blog I read summarizes the particularist stance on conspiracy theories as follows: particularists claim that labeling something a conspiracy theory is purely descriptive and carries no default epistemic stigma; they argue that because documented historical conspiracies are common, each specific claim should be evaluated solely on its local evidence, exactly as we assess any other empirical hypothesis. The summary continues that particularists deny conspiracy theories possess any distinctive structural features—such as explaining events through the hidden actions of powerful agents—and that this refusal makes their view self-undermining, since without identifying such features they lack any principled basis for distinguishing warranted suspicion from unfounded paranoia. I am inclined to accept that particularists defend case-by-case evaluation, but I am unsure whether they really reject all structural characterizations or whether the self-undermining charge is an internal flaw rather than a restatement of the opposing generalist objection.
