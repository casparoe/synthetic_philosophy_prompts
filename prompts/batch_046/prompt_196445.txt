I'm on the ethics board for a hospital running clinical trials and we keep running into cases where a direct rule — like always getting explicit consent for every data reuse — clashes with the long-term institutional trust that makes the research possible. I need a reusable decision procedure grounded in indirect normativity (norms apply to our deliberative procedures and institutional dispositions, not each isolated act) but paired with a specific model of idealized deliberation: not an omniscient agent, but one with our actual cognitive limits minus self-deception and motivated reasoning. The procedure should run in about 15 minitues per case, tell me step-by-step whether a proposed deviation is permissible, include worked examples — especially one where the answer will feel wrong on the surface — and flag the spots where theory cant eliminate judgment. I have to defend the result to lawyers on the board who dont care about metaethics, so keep it practical and say what the procedure cant do.
