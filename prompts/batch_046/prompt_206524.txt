Design a thought experiment featuring an ordinary manufactured object—such as a coated metal component or a treated ceramic—that possesses a disposition D (for instance, to crack under rapid thermal stress), where D is simultaneously finked and masked, but the two interfering mechanisms are interdependent rather than independent. Specifically, the fink is activated only when the mask is present, and the mask is sustained only so long as the fink remains dormant. Present the scenario in one paragraph, using realistic properties and avoiding exotic physics or magical artifacts. Then show that the specification is met: describe the object, the disposition, the fink, the mask, and the precise interdependence linking them. Next, state the verdict that a defender of the simple conditional analysis of dispositions would reach about whether D genuinely belongs to the object, and identify the structural feature of the interdependence that drives this verdict. Finally, explain what happens—whether the case collapses into a standard fink example, a standard mask example, or something else—if the interdependence is broken so that the fink and mask can operate separately.
