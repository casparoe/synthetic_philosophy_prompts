I'm putting together a graduate seminar module for about fourteen people—one 75-minute session—on the architecture of consciousness, and I want an in-class experience that makes the access versus phenomenal distinction hit physically before anyone reaches for a textbook. You are an expert philosopher of mind with training in cognitive architecture and participatory pedagogy. Think step by step. Before you design anything, do a brief internal brainstorm: list three distinct ways a group could be made to act on information that is present in local experience but blocked from collective reasoning and report.

The participants have basic familiarity with global workspace theory, but I want them to feel the specific pressure point where local recurrent processing might sustain phenomenal character without achieving global broadcast. To keep this from being a standard blindsight rehearsal, I'm injecting a hypothetical mechanism we'll call a localized recurrence pocket (LRP): a temporary neural ensemble that sustains activation loops for roughly 200 ms, retains subjective character, yet fails competitive inhibition for workspace entry. The activity should make participants inhabit an analog of an LRP.

Design a fully runnable lesson plan with the following elements:

- Setting and materials: a philosophy-of-mind seminar with pen, paper, and only a deck of thirty modified index cards—no electronics, no hidden props.
- Rules and a non-monetary payoff or coordination scheme that forces tension between private phenomenal information and publicly accessible workspace information.
- A staged sequence: (a) participants receive card-based stimuli through different channels; (b) one channel reaches the group workspace (a public board everyone can see and cite); the others remain local (private cards that the holder may not share or articulate); (c) the group must make collective bets or coordinate choices using only workspace information, yet the private information subtly biases outcomes; (d) a forced-report round where participants justify choices without referencing the private channel; (e) a meta-access round where they rate whether they genuinely knew the private information, experienced it without knowing, or neither.
- A debrief that maps physical moments onto the formal distinction—local recurrence versus global broadcast, phenomenal versus access consciousness—with explicit reference to the LRP analog. - No bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for quotes from specific texts. Define the LRP fully within the prompt's rules so that no external lookup is required.

Write this as a polished, precise lesson plan.
