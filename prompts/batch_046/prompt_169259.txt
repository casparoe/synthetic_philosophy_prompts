Write an essay in American English weighing the self-sampling assumption (SSA) against the self-indication assumption (SIA) in anthropic reasoning, with focused attention on the presumptuous philosopher objection. To avoid a purely derivative treatment, work within a modified framework in which observer-moments are not uniform but are weighted by a "phenomenal resolution" coefficient that scales with the structural complexity of the conscious state—so that a universe containing fewer but cognitively richer observers can carry greater total observer-weight than one containing vastly more minimal minds. Within this graded-observer model, evaluate whether the presumptuous philosopher's reductio of SSA still succeeds, or whether the weighting scheme undermines the intuition that we should expect to find ourselves among the most numerous observers. Present the strongest considerations for both SSA and SIA under this framing, reach a definite conclusion about which assumption remains more defensible, and justify that verdict with explicit reasoning rather than bare intuition.
