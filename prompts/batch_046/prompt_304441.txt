Write a sonnet about higher-order theories of consciousness. Use the octave to lay out the basic claim: a mental state only becomes conscious when some higher-order representation takes it as its object, so raw first-order pain isn't experienced pain untill a second-order state is directed at it. The volta should land on the misrepresentation problem -- if being conscious just is being the target of that higher-order thought, then you could be conscious of pain with no actual first-order pain present at all. In the closing couplet, say exactly what this implies for whether the theory can keep the intuition that consciousness tracks something real rather than just constructing its own objects. Keep it technically right for someone who knows the view, but don't bother with citations or reading lists.
