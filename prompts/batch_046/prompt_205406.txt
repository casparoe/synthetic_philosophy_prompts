the nonidentity problem shows that if a 14-year-old has a child now, that child can't claim she was harmed by the decision, since if her mother had waited she wouldn't have existed at all -- a different child with different genes would be born instead. so the harm-based, person-affecting reason fails: there's no specific victim. this applies to climate policy because cutting emissions now changes migration, pairing, etc., so the people in 2100 depend on our choice. if we don't cut, they can't say they were harmed, since without our choice they wouldn't be here.

parfit solves this by moving to a threshold person-affecting view: we owe future people lives that clear some welfare floor, and climate mitigation is justified because it keeps them above that floor. the comparison that survives is intra-personal but applied modally -- we compare the welfare of representative individuals in world A vs world B, treating them as the same person across possibilities. since the representatives in the high-emission world are worse off, we have a reason to reduce emissions. this avoids the repugnant conclusion too, because we're not aggregating total welfare but comparing representative lives.
