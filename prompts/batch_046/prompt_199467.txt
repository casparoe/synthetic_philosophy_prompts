hey so at the rationalist meetup last thursday two regulars got into it over some workfare pilot they saw on a policy blog. the idea is you have to do community service to get benefits, but you can bank up to six months of unused eligibility and draw it later if you get fired without cause. so its conditional but with a strategic reserve.

anyway alex said this solves the undeserving poor problem without the usual cruelty. the condition filters out people who just want free money, but the bank means youre not punishing people for bad luck later. she called it bascially a deductible for welfare.

jordan pushed back hard. he said the bank is exactly where it collapses. if youre the kind of person who games welfare, youd just do the bare minimum service to keep banking months, then cash them in during a planned quit. meanwhile honest people burn through thier bank on real crises and end up with nothing. he said its adverse selection on steroids.

alex replied that jordan assumes people can perfectly time job loss, which is rare. she said the banking feature is mostly psychological — it makes people feel like theyre earning a right, which boosts compliance. she also said if adverse selection was that strong wed see it in health savings accounts, but we dont.

jordan said the HSA comparison is broken because health spending is mostly involuntary, while quitting is a choice you can plan around. he said the real problem isnt gaming but signal corruption: once you add the bank, the work condition stops being a credible signal of work ethic because everyone treats it as deferred compensation.

they went back and forth for like 20 minutes and neither budged. can you adjudicate this move by move? rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say what feature of the banking mechanism is actually doing the work, and tell me if the HSA comparison is a red herring or a real analogy. also dont just tell me whos nicer — tell me whose model of human behavior is closer to right given what we know about benefit fraud and conditionality. oh and if you think im missing a third option say so.
