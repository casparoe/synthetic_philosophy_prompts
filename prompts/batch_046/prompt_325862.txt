I have been thinking, without any formal philosophical training, about whether people can genuinely deserve punishment, and about the disagreement between those who defend moral responsibility even if determinism is true — in the broad spirit of Dennett's compatibilism — and those who argue that modern neuroscience and determinism undermine just deserts entirely, as Caruso maintains. I would like you to draft the joint statement that a committed representative of each camp could both sign after a sustained, good-faith adversarial collaboration on punishment specifically, not on free will in the abstract.

The statement should contain: a numbered section of agreed claims about human behaviour, the functions of social institutions, and what current psychology and neuroscience actually establish; then a disagreements table in which each remaining dispute is formulated precisely, with each side's position phrased in wording that its own representatives would accept and that the other side recognises as accurate.

Both parties should identify the cruxes — the specific sub-questions that, if resolved, would move at least one of them. I suspect two are central: first, whether the concept of 'desert' can be reconstructed as an institutional role rather than as a metaphysical property of agents; second, whether predictive risk-assessment can ever fully substitute for moral blame in justifying the coercive separation of an individual from society.

For each crux, specify a concrete institutional design or case that both sides agree is genuinely relevant, and record in advance what each side predicts it would show. In particular, I want you to apply both views to a scenario I have devised, which I call the Restorative Containment Protocol. In this protocol, an individual who has caused serious harm is separated from the wider community not because they deserve suffering, but solely because an independent, transparent risk assessment predicts a high probability of further harm. Within the facility they retain substantial autonomy, are given clear and honest reasons for their separation, and must complete a structured programme — not conceived as penance — aimed at achieving a deep, experiential understanding of the victim's situation, with successful completion being a necessary but not sufficient condition for eventual reintegration. Both sides should state whether this protocol counts as punishment, whether it respects or violates moral agency, and whether its existence supports or undermines their own position.

Please write the answer in British English. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote verbatim from any published work; paraphrase in your own words. Ensure that neither camp is represented by a straw man, and keep the level of detail such that the argument can be followed and assessed without recourse to obscure sources.
