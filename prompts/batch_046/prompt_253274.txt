I cover institutional design and collective-action problems for a science-and-ideas publication, and I am documenting an interactive workshop for an upcoming feature. The setting is a 90-minute philosophy seminar with 22 advanced undergraduates studying social dynamics. I need a participatory simulation—rules, roles, materials, and a point-based payoff scheme—that makes the participants physically experience a multipolar race-to-the-bottom dynamic before anyone names it. To keep the exercise from collapsing into a familiar prisoner's dilemma, build in a specific structural twist: an asymmetric-visibility layer in which each participant can observe only a subset of the others' commitments to a voluntary transparency protocol, and the payoff matrix rewards partial defection (selective opacity) more than either full cooperation or total defection, generating a three-body competitive collapse rather than a binary one.

The design should include a minute-by-minute timeline, instructions for the facilitator, and a debrief that maps moments in the gameplay onto the structural features of the trap. Be explicit about what will predictably fail in the first run and how to recover. Also identify precisely which feature—specifically the irreversibility of competitive pressure once a critical defection threshold is crossed—the activity cannot reproduce and must be supplied by the facilitator. The idea must emerge from what the participants do, not from any preliminary lecture.
