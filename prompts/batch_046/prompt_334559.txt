As a retired condensed-matter physicist now studying metaphysics, I am designing the final for an upper-level seminar on fundamentality. I want an essay question that cannot be answered by summarizing the standard grounding/causation distinction—e.g., Schaffer's contrastive analysis or Fine's logic of ground. Instead, present the BCS reduction of superconductivity: macroscopic zero resistance is derived from microscopic electron-phonon interactions producing Cooper pairs. Ask students whether this scientific reduction is also metaphysical grounding of the phenomenon in its parts, or whether explanatory derivation and ontological dependence come apart here. Require them to evaluate whether fundamentality is absolute (quantum fields are fundamental simpliciter) or relative (they are fundamental only with respect to superconductivity), and what each view implies about whether "levels of reality" are ontologically robust or merely heuristic. Provide: (1) the exam question, calibrated for a 90-minute essay; (2) a model answer of about 600 words that makes the precise philosophical moves; and (3) a marking scheme of 20 marks built around five must-hit points, with marks per point, a one-line note on the characteristic mistake for each, and explicit criteria distinguishing an A from a B answer. Do not ask for bibliographies or citations; the answer should stand on conceptual analysis alone.
