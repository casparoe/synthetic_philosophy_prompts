I'm preparing a handout for a graduate seminar on historical explanation that moves past the standard Hempel-versus-narrative framing. I want a decision tree that sorts respondents by their actual commitments about explaining institutional collapse—using, as the running example, the failure of a representative assembly under simultaneous economic and political stress.

The axes I want included are: (1) whether explanation requires subsumption under at least one general law; (2) whether it requires embedding events in a coherent narrative with a discernible arc; (3) whether it depends on counterfactual robustness—i.e., whether the same explanatory factors would apply if background conditions were slightly altered; and (4) whether the explanation must be portable across similar institutions or is bound to this particular sequence.

Each leaf should carry the name of the position (or "unnamed" if no prominent view occupies the combination) and, in one sentence, its most awkward commitment regarding the running example. Map any combinations the logical space allows but no named view occupies, with a brief diagnosis—incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate.

Keep the tree to six to eight leaves, suitable for a single session. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests. Finish with a final line beginning "Summary:" followed by one sentence that captures the tree's overall branching logic.
