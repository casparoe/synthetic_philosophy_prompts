ok so i was re-reading Dennett's brain-transplant puzzle (the one with Yorick in the vat and Hamlet running around remote-controlled) and my roommate cooked up a extension that i can't shake. here's the chain:

(1) In the original setup Dennett's brain Yorick is in the vat and Hamlet is the body being piloted.
(2) Wherever my first-person point of view is centered, that's literally where I am.
(3) Dennett reports feeling like he's out with Hamlet, not stuck in the vat, so in that case I am at Hamlet's location.
(4) Now imagine the radio link isn't one-to-one but multicast: Yorick's neural patterns are streamed to three identical bodies (Hamlet-A, B, and C) all at once, each waking up with the exact same stream of consciousness.
(5) So my POV is centered in all three bodies simultaniously.
(6) That means I am Hamlet-A, and I am Hamlet-B, and I am Hamlet-C.
(7) Identity is transitive: if I'm A and I'm B, then A is B.
(8) But A and B are clearly distinct — different GPS coords, can diverge once the feed splits, etc.
(9) So either we reject transitivity of identity (which feels like a logical law) or we accept that one person can be three people at once.
(10) But if we accept that, then personal identity collapses completely — there's no fact about whether this survivor is me.
(11) The only escape is to reject (2) and say POV-location doesn't determine where I am.
(12) But then in the original single-body case we have zero reason to say Dennett is with Hamlet rather than in the vat with Yorick. So the puzzle doesn't solve anything, it just destroys the question.

my roommate says this proves Dennett's thought experiment is self-undermining. i feel like there's a hidden slide between (5) and (6), maybe about what "centered in" actually means? which step should i actually give up, and what's the price? like, if i drop (2) do i become a brain-identity theorist? and if i keep it, do i have to bite the bullet on distributed selves?
