Write a deadpan satirical End User License Agreement of approximately 600 words for a fictional software suite named “Absolute Infinity v9.0,” published by an invented firm, “Cantor-Skolem Solutions.” The document should adopt the dense, capitalized legal-technical prose of actual software licenses and remain in character throughout—no winking, no breaking voice. Its clauses should apply the logic of Skolem’s paradox to consumer software: the product promises users access to genuinely uncountable sets (for instance, the full power set of the natural numbers rendered in a local environment), yet the fine print discloses that, because any first-order axiomatization admits a countable model, every “uncountable” object delivered to the user is in fact countable from an external meta-model perspective, even though the internal user interface insists on rendering it as uncountable. Invent conceptually coherent but absurd provisions—such as “Downward Löwenheim-Skolem compatibility disclaimers,” “countable-model warranty exclusions,” and “user-side cardinality-illusion maintenance obligations”—that exaggerate the real feature of the paradox: first-order set theory cannot uniquely determine the cardinality of its intended models, so “uncountability” functions as an internal, model-relative property rather than an absolute metaphysical fact. The satire should target the practice of treating formal axiomatic systems as if they transparently describe a single, mind-independent mathematical reality, especially when that formalism is packaged and marketed as consumer technology. The piece must stay internally consistent; a reader familiar with model theory should recognize that the corporate promises are direct analogues of the paradox’s structure rather than invented misconceptions. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific historical texts; the prompt should rely entirely on conceptual content. Write in polished American English.
