I've been thinking about Confucian role ethics and this idea that every role has a kind of shadow partner—like the father isn't just father but also implicitly the one who must be corrected by the child's remonstrance, and that this isn't an exception but built into the role itself. Can you read King Lear through that lense? Specificaly, is Cordelia's refusal to play the flattery game actually a correct performance of her daughter-role because she's trying to correct Lear's king-role, and does Lear fail because he treats role-obligations as one-way loyalty rather than this two-way check? Also, does the play end up vindicating her or punishing her for stepping outside? I'd like a reading that takes both the play and this shadow-role idea seriously, and says where Shakespeare dramatizes something the standard hierarchy model misses, and where the play just resists the framing.
