I am preparing a creative writing assignment for my high school philosophy elective, and I would like you to produce a satirical museum acquisition report of approximately four hundred to five hundred words, written in the deadpan voice of a curatorial registrar who must catalog a single object that is simultaneously Michelangelo's David and the marble block from which it was carved. The report should treat the problem of material constitution—specifically whether the statue and the marble are identical or merely coincident—as a practical bureaucratic crisis rather than an abstract metaphysical puzzle, so that the humor arises from taking the actual arguments seriously rather than mocking them from outside. Please address mereological essentialism, the puzzle of temporary parts, and the difficulty of assigning one accession number to an entity with conflicting persistence conditions; to keep the framing fresh, invent a specific institutional detail such as a new Ontological Clarity Policy that requires every acquired object to declare its own identity conditions upon entry. The target of the satire should be the tendency of metaphysicians to treat ordinary objects as having sharper boundaries and more definite existence conditions than any institutional practice could actually accommodate. Write entirely in continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if composing an internal memorandum or formal letter. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or references to specific scholars and texts by name, since I want the piece to work from the conceptual structure alone.
