Take the Caltech split-brain experiments from the 60s and 70s — Sperry and Gazzaniga's work with patients after corpus callosotomy — and analze them through the idea that consciousness has a unity threshold rather than being all-or-nothing. Don't just recount the left-hand/right-hand mismatches; treat each task setup as testing where that threshold sits. Which conditions force integration and which let hemispheres run parallel? Where does the two-minds reading overreach by assuming a binary split, and where does the unity view have to concede real fragmentation? And what does patients still feeling like one person do to the model — does it mean the threshold tracks reportability not processing, or that the model never applied to first-person feeling anyway?
