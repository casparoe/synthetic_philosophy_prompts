I am a high school senior in an interdisciplinary course that combines molecular biology with philosophy of science, and we have been examining why biologists still lack a universally accepted definition of life despite centuries of debate. Our instructor introduced the standard cluster-definition—cellular organization, metabolism, homeostasis, growth, reproduction, and evolutionary adaptation—but then presented two challenging borderline cases that seem to resist this framework: first, synthetic “xenobots” engineered from Xenopus laevis stem cells that exhibit kinematic self-replication and collective behavior without conventional genetic inheritance, and second, giant nucleocytoplasmic large DNA viruses (such as Mimivirus) that possess genes encoding translation-related proteins yet remain obligate intracellular parasites lacking independent metabolism. For the philosophy component of the course, I am required to write an argumentative essay addressing the following controversy: given these and similar synthetic or viral borderline entities, should we defend an essentialist definition of life anchored in autonomous metabolic homeostasis and nucleic-acid-based heredity, or should we instead adopt a gradualist, family-resemblance conception that places organisms, viruses, and engineered protocells on a continuous spectrum?

The essay must systematically weigh the principal arguments for each side—including worries about explanatory vagueness, anthropocentric bias in selecting criteria, and the practical implications for astrobiology and the ethics of synthetic biology—before reaching and justifying a clear conclusion. Possible verdicts include defending a modified essentialist criterion (for example, that open-ended evolutionary capacity is necessary and sufficient), arguing that “life” is a pragmatic category that should be replaced by functional descriptions in scientific contexts, or proposing a hybrid threshold model; whatever position you take, it must be defended rather than left as an open question. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests, as the assignment evaluates conceptual reasoning rather than source retrieval.
