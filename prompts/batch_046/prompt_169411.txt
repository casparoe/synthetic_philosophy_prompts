For my physics and philosophy independent study, I am trying to work out whether Newton’s bucket argument still poses a serious challenge to relationism about space. I understand Newton’s point: if a bucket of water hangs from a twisted rope and is released, the water eventually curves into a paraboloid even when the bucket and the water are not rotating relative to one another, which seems to show that rotation is defined with respect to something beyond the bodies themselves—namely, absolute space. I also understand the Machian relationist reply, which appeals to rotation relative to distant matter rather than to an empty spatial container. My confusion comes in with modern cosmology. We now have the cosmic microwave background, which picks out a precise universal rest frame relative to which we can measure any rotation. So it seems to me that Mach’s principle is essentially satisfied: there is always distant cosmic matter defining a frame, and therefore the bucket experiment can be explained relationistically without invoking absolute space. In other words, hasn’t the discovery of the CMB rest frame dissolved Newton’s objection by giving relationists the distant reference frame they needed all along? Or am I conflating the mere existence of a cosmic reference frame with an actual dynamical explanation of why the water curves?
