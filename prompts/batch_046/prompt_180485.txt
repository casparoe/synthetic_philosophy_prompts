There’s this thing in foreign-policy shops and strategic-studies programs where analysts are trained to treat historical analogies—Munich, Vietnam, the Thucydides trap—as live reasoning tools rather than just rhetoric. The practice has a weird implicit protocol: if you’re arguing for containment, invoke Munich and frame appeasement as a timeless moral failure; if you’re arguing against intervention, invoke Vietnam and warn about quagmire; and lately people cite the Thucydides trap when they want to say conflict between a rising and ruling power is structurally inevitable. What’s strange is that the same institution will reject an analogy for being superficial—say, comparing some modern rivalry to the Peloponnesian War because the economic interdependence is totally different—while still accepting Munich as relevant even when the power asymetry and ideological stakes are nothing like 1938. So there’s a filtering practice, but it applies different standards to moral cautionary tales versus structural predictions.

What philosophical commitments does that filtering practice presuppose? Does it assume history contains recurring causal mechanisms that can be recognized independant of context, or does it assume something about moral learning—that certain failures carry normative weight regardless of structural match? Is the practice even internally consistent, or does it quietly depend on two incompatible theories of past-relevance: mechanistic repetition on one side and exemplary moral narrative on the other? And if we actually enforced the idea that analogies only work when underlying causal mechanisms match, what would have to change about how Munich gets taught in these programs? There’s also a hidden commitment about agency—leaders can apparently choose not to appease like Chamberlain, but are simultaneously trapped in dynamics they can’t escape. Reconstruct the implicit theory of historical explanation and moral responsibility here, say whether the practice actually needs it or is compatible with several rival theories, and tell me what a curriculum built on only one of those theories would have to drop.
