ok so i recently inherited this weird patch of land upstate and i've been walking it every weekend trying to figure out wether i should let it rewild or just... not. and it's made me think about wild animal suffering in a way i can't shake. like, does tearing up habitat actually make things better for the animals?

here are my gut calls on specific situations, and i want to know if there's a principle hiding in them or if i'm just being inconsistent.

1. a logging company clear-cuts fifty acres of mature forest. tons of deer and small mammals lose shelter, but the tick load crashes and winter starvation events drop for the survivors. my call: long-run suffering probably goes down because r-selected rodents don't rebound as hard.

2. a wetland gets filled for a parking lot. frogs and water snakes are displaced; some die quick, others crowd into the remaining marsh and starve. my call: suffering clearly goes up, even though total biomass might drop.

3. climate change slowly melts a glacier, wiping out a cold-adapted beetle over fifty years with no acute trauma, just a gradual decline in carrying capacity. my call: this feels neutral or maybe slightly good? less suffering per unit area?

4. conservationists remove invasive rats from an island. native seabirds come back, overbreed, then crash from starvation and disease. my call: suffering went up, which makes me distrust 'restoration' as automatically good.

5. a highway cuts through a meadow. deer get hit by cars, populations fragment, but the ones left have way less competition for food. my call: suffering probably decreases, which feels wrong to say out loud.

6. a coral reef dies from warming. fish scatter and slowly starve. my call: bad, obviously.

so my tentative idea is that destruction reduces suffering only when it suppresses high-turnover populations without triggering rebound crashes or acute trauma. but case 5 makes that shaky, and case 3 doesn't fit well either. is there a cleaner principle that saves all six, or do i have to drop one? and what does it predict for the case i'm stuck on: if i let my land go to meadow and then mow it every year to keep trees out, am i reducing suffering or just creating a boom-bust occurance?
