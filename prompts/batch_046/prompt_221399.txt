The piece should exaggerate the real logic of multi-level governance, especially the principle of subsidiarity as it operates in Swiss direct democracy and in European Union competence debates, by treating sovereignty as a divisible administrative license that must be pushed downward until it reaches an atomic decision-maker incapable of acting, thereby generating a "veto cascade" rather than local empowerment. Include an invented quotation from a fictional Federal Councilor defending the reform in the flat, procedural language of bureaucratic rationality, and add a brief paragraph on American federalists attempting to import the model for healthcare administration, where state-level reflux disputes have already paralyzed three pilot programs.

The satire must remain legible to readers without expertise in Swiss constitutional law: explain the mechanics of downward delegation in plain terms, let the absurdity emerge strictly from extending familiar institutional features—cantonal autonomy, municipal direct democracy, EU-style allocation of powers—to their literal extreme, and avoid inventing false institutional names or obscure legal doctrines that depend on specialized knowledge. Maintain sustained deadpan irony throughout; do not wink, do not state the objection explicitly, and do not break character to signal that the proposal is ridiculous. A specialist in federalism should recognize the exaggeration as fair, while a general reader should grasp both the parody and the underlying conceptual problem—namely, whether there is always a clearly identifiable "lowest appropriate level" for every collective decision. Do not include bibliographies, citations, requests for further reading, or references to specific living figures; the article should stand as a fully self-contained parody of administrative reason pushed to its reductio.
