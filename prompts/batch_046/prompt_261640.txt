Given that the absent-minded driver problem is usually taken to show that backward induction collapses for any agent with imperfect recall, does it follow that chess played under conditions of "move amnesia"—where each player knows only the current board position, not the move sequence that produced it—must also reject backward induction? Or is the failure in the driver's case tied to a specific structural feature, namely that the information sets align with payoff-relevant distinctions in a way that creates a conflict between ex ante planning and ex post rationality, a conflict that need not arise when hidden histories are strategically transparent?
