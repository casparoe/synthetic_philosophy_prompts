Read Pixar's Inside Out through von Neuman's brain-computer comparison, especially his idea that the brain isn't purely digital but runs on statistical threshold mechanisms and analog redundancy. The film literalizes emotions as operators at a console and memories as glowing orbs—very computer-like. But von Neumann also argued neural 'memory' is reconstructed rather than retrieved like a file, and that affective processing might be the analog substrate making logical reasoning possible. Does the movie's visual metaphors accidentally commit to a storage-model of memory he rejected? When Joy and Sadness navigate reconstructed long-term memory landscapes, is that closer to his view? I'm writing for a philosophy of mind blog and want to avoid the usual brain-computer cliche—so I'm especially interested in whether the film distinguishes analog/emotional processing from digital/logical control, or collapses them. Where does the reading hold and where does it break?
