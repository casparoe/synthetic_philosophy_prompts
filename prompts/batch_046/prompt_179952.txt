Here is an exam question from a seminar on well-being and biotechnology, together with a student response. Please assess it with a specific grade and point-by-point comments that justify the mark, identifying both the visible strengths and any subtle errors.

Question: David Pearce's hedonistic imperative holds that biotechnology should be used to abolish suffering throughout the sentient world, replacing aversive states with graded well-being. Consider a proposed intervention—the Contrast Preservation Protocol—that genetically eliminates nociception and negative affect but deliberately retains a minimal contrastive awareness: organisms can discriminate better-from-worse phenomenal states without experiencing the worse ones as aversive. Defenders claim this preserves evolutionary-functional motivation; critics invoke experiential neutrality, the principle that engineering baseline phenomenal architecture is justified only to eliminate suffering, not to substitute one positive architecture for another. Does Pearce's framework commit him to endorsing the Protocol? Address specifically whether preserving contrastive awareness is compatible with the complete abolition of suffering, and whether the neutrality objection succeeds against the gradualist defense.

Student answer: Pearce's position is essentially negative utilitarianism applied to biotechnology, so he should endorse any intervention that reduces suffering, including the Protocol. By eliminating nociception and negative affect, the Protocol achieves Pearce's core goal. The retention of contrastive awareness does not violate experiential neutrality, because neutrality only constrains interventions that do not reduce suffering—since this protocol clearly reduces suffering, it falls within the permitted scope. Moreover, Pearce argues for replacing suffering with graded well-being, which implies that some differential phenomenal structure is necessary for motivation. Therefore, preserving contrastive awareness aligns with Pearce's view rather than undermining it. The critics are mistaken because they treat neutrality as prohibiting all positive engineering, but Pearce's framework permits engineering positive states as replacements for suffering. The only remaining question is whether uniform high-level well-being would be preferable to contrastive awareness, but that is practical, not principled. So Pearce should endorse the Protocol, though he might prefer a version that maximizes gradient intensity.
