I'm trying to stabilize my position on meaning, revision, and empirical constraint before drafting a longer essay, and I want an audit of whether the following commitments form a coherent package or collapse into contradictions I haven't seen.

(1) Quine's critique of the analytic-synthetic distinction in Two Dogmas of Empiricism succeeds: there is no principled boundary separating truths that hold solely by virtue of meaning from those that hold by virtue of fact, and in principle every statement in the web of belief is revisable.

(2) Mathematical truths are nevertheless epistemically distinctive: they are not confirmed or disconfirmed by direct observation, but are retained or abandoned solely on the basis of global pragmatic virtues—simplicity, conservatism, and explanatory integration—within the total network.

(3) Meaning holism is correct: the empirical content of any individual sentence is distributed across the entire web, and radical translation is necessarily underdetermined by all available behavioral and perceptual evidence.

(4) Despite this, there remain genuinely analytic statements—logical tautologies and explicit definitional equivalences—that are true purely in virtue of conventional meaning, and these function as fixed points that constrain translation manuals and prevent total indeterminacy.

(5) The observational core shared across competing theories is not a Carnapian theory-neutral given, but an artifact of shared perceptual physiology and linguistic training; it provides no extra-theoretical foundation for empiricism, yet it explains why translation is practically tractable despite theoretical underdetermination.

(6) I also hold that there is a substantive, scheme-transcendent fact about whether two conceptual frameworks are genuinely different ontologies or merely notational variants; this fact is not itself internal to either scheme and is not reducible to pragmatic choice.

Which pairs are in real tension, and which only appear to conflict? Specifically, does (2) genuinely conflict with the universal revisability asserted in (1), or is that tension resolvable within Quine's own pragmatism? How severe is the conflict between (3) and (4): can holism accommodate isolated analytic fixed points, or does accepting (4) require abandoning (3) altogether? And does (6) collapse under the pressure of (3) and (5), given that holism plus the denial of a neutral observational base seems to make scheme-comparison either impossible or purely pragmatic?

Please rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what commitment must be qualified or abandoned and what that costs, and identify any hidden dependency—for instance, whether accepting (4) commits me to a modified form of (3) rather than its rejection. Do not treat all conflicts as equally damaging; I want to know the least costly path back to consistency, and I am not signaling in advance which view I would surrender.
