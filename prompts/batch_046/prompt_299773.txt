I'm writing a position paper for my upper-level metaethics course, and I want to rank five prominent responses to Hume's is-ought gap and to Moore's naturalistic fallacy by how successfully they navigate one specific tension: avoiding both (a) the reduction of normative authority to pure description and (b) a mysterious, non-cognitive intuitionism that severs values from the natural world. The five positions are:

1. Hume's original logical-gap thesis (Treatise 3.1.1), interpreted as the claim that no purely descriptive premises can entail a normative conclusion without an implicit normative premise already smuggled in;
2. Moore's open-question argument, which treats "good" as a simple, non-natural property grasped only by intuition and declares any identification of it with a natural property fallacious;
3. Searle's institutional-facts derivation, which attempts to derive an "ought" from an "is" by embedding normative force in institutional structures such as promising;
4. Foot's "natural goodness" account, which defines human goods in terms of the flourishing proper to our biological kind, thereby rejecting Moore's fallacy by making evaluation internal to descriptive facts about living things;
5. Putnam's entanglement thesis, which argues that "thick" ethical concepts—such as cruelty or bravery—are simultaneously descriptive and evaluative, undermining the sharp fact-value dichotomy from the descriptive side rather than the normative side.

Please assign each position a tier—S, A, B, C, or D—according to how well it resolves that specific tension, and justify each placement in one or two sentences. Then, in a brief paragraph, state how committed you are to this ordering: flag any tier where your confidence is low, and indicate whether you regard the ranking as a robust philosophical judgment or as a tentative pedagogical framing. Finally, if you believe the task rests on a confused premise—for example, if these five positions address such different problems (logical entailment, semantic analysis, metaphysics of institutional reality, biological teleology, conceptual entanglement) that ranking them on a single axis is misleading—say so plainly, explain why the comparison fails, and do not force a tier list merely to comply.
