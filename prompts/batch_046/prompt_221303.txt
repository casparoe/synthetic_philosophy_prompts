i work for a regional ag co-op ethics board and every quarter we have to sign off on pest-management contracts that can affect literally hundreds of millions of insects. i need a reusable decision proceedure my team can run in about thirty minutes per proposal, grounded in that scale-arguement logic -- tiny probability of sentience multiplied by astronomical numbers -- but translated into actual ordered steps. make it a flowchart in words: what data to gather first, how to proceed when nobody can hand you a real number for sentience probability, where the math stops and a value judgment takes over, and exactly where the thing will misfire or spit out an answer we won't like. apply it to three proposals: aerial spraying over eight hundred hectares killing an estimated four hundred million insects; releasing a native predatory beetle that kills roughly the same number of pest larvae but over months; and a pheromone disruption system that kills very few but might cause unknown sublethall stress. be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as calculation. flag what the procedure flat-out can't settle -- for instance whether replacement populations cancel harm, or if sublethal stress counts the same as death in the scale calculus. no bibliographies or reading lists; just write the procedure so a non-philosopher can run it without guessing at probabilities they don't have, and include a stop-box that says "this needs a human sign-off, not a number." one of the three cases should come out in a way the board will hate.
