first, the classic snack loop: i prefer apple over banana, banana over cherry, cherry over apple, and there's a 50 cent fee to switch. if i keep trading around the loop i lose money every cycle. verdict: totally irrational, i'm being pumped.

same preferences but i sign a binding contract that locks me into apple forever. verdict: smart move, diachronic planning fixes it.

now hyperbolic discounting on saving: i take $100 now over $110 next month, but next month i'll take $110 in two months over $100 in one month. verdict: this feels irrational too, but different? it's not a loop with an outside exploiter, just me being inconsistent across time.

here's a weirder one i made up, call it the shadow pump. my generosity cycles with my mood: monday generous, tuesday stingy, wednesday generous, etc. my friend figures this out and only asks for loans on mondays, knowing i'll regret it tuesday but still pay back. verdict: i'm being exploited, but is it my fault? i feel like it's irrational that my preferences are so predictably cyclical, even though there's no explicit fee like in the snack loop.

so: is there one principle behind all of these, or do i need seperate ones for preference loops, hyperbolic discounting, predictable mood cycles, and belief cycles? what does the best principle say about the never-request-change case where nothing actually happens? and be honest about which of my verdicts it has to throw out.
