I'm giving the opening remarks at a workshop next week on recursive institutions—half the room are systems theorists who've lived inside strange loops since grad school, half are municipal planners who've never heard of an inviolate level. I want Escher's Drawing Hands to be the actual backbone of the talk, not just a cool slide: the claim should be that every functioning bureaucracy contains a hidden level that draws the rules for the levels above it, and that if you try to make that bottom level fully transperant you collapse the loop and the system stops working. Write about eight minutes, keep it conversational so neither crowd feels lectured, and build in one concrete, slightly weird example—maybe zoning boards that write the oversight rules they themselves are judged by. I need it to do real structural work, so swapping in another metaphor wouldn't work. A typo or two is fine; please don't make it sound like a journal article.
