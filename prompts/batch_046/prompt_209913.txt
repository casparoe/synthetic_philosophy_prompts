According to the reviewer, Christ's silent response and the kiss he bestows upon the Inquisitor at the end of the poem prove that Dostoevsky regards self-sacrificial love as institutionally powerless, with the kiss functioning not as redemption but as an acknowledgment of submission to necessary order. The reviewer further argues that the entire speech is essentially a welfare-optimization problem disguised in theological language—if the mass of humanity suffers under freedom, then removing freedom maximizes collective well-being, and Ivan's poem is meant to show that any moral framework preserving autonomy is therefore impractical.
