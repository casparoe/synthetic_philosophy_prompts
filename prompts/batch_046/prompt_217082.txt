I'm a program officer at a foundation distributing roughly fifty million dollars annually across interventions with radically different time horizons — some deliver measurable welfare gains within five years, others reshape institutional trajectories over decades, and a few are essentially irreversible technological or governance bets. I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded in the importance-tractability-neglectedness framework but modified for the constraints of a thirty-minute evaluation without access to full cost-effectiveness models or dedicated research staff. The procedure must be something I can run alone, sign off on personally, and defend to a board that includes both near-term global-health advocates and longtermist researchers.

Please construct this as an ordered checklist or flowchart in words. It should treat tractability not as a single scalar but as a function of marginal funding — flagging convex returns (where each additional dollar is more effective) and concave returns (where saturation hits quickly) — and it should include an explicit "irreversibility premium" for proposals whose outcomes cannot be undone if they go wrong. Add an "incommensurability flag" that activates when importance metrics don't share a common scale, such as comparing QALY-equivalent near-term health gains against existential-risk-reduction arguments. The procedure must handle four hard cases: (1) importance is extreme but tractability is undefined, as with foundational AI governance research; (2) neglectedness is negative, meaning the space is already crowded with well-funded actors; (3) the proposal involves outcomes that are irreversible and have unbounded downside risk; and (4) my personal prior about the intervention's value is strongly negative even though the framework scores it highly.

Include a small set of ordered test questions I can apply at each stage, clearly mark which steps are irreducibly judgmental and must carry my signature rather than being delegated, and apply the procedure to three hypothetical proposals: a near-term malaria bednet distribution program with well-estimated cost-effectiveness; a longtermist research program on international AI governance with high importance, unclear tractability, and negative neglectedness; and a geoengineering readiness proposal that scores well on importance and neglectedness but triggers the irreversibility premium and conflicts with my own risk assessment. For each application, show the step-by-step result, identify where the procedure gives an answer I will not like, and state explicitly what the procedure cannot do — including cases where it should force a "defer" or "reject" despite intuitive appeal, and cases where it will approve something I think is a mistake. Do not ask me to compute precise probabilities or utility weights; instead, specify the operational rules for proceeding when those numbers are missing. Keep the language precise but avoid relying on citations, named papers, or bibliographic references I could not verify without searching.
