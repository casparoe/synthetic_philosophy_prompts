A colleague summarized Wittgenstein’s later philosophy for me as follows: “Wittgenstein’s family resemblance is an anti-essentialist theory of categorization: a concept like ‘game’ applies not because all instances share a necessary and sufficient core, but because particular members overlap in crisscrossing features, like threads in a rope. This is structurally identical to Eleanor Rosch’s prototype theory in cognitive psychology, where membership is graded around a central exemplar, so Wittgenstein’s categories have radial structure with fuzzy boundaries. Consequently, language games are just the social practices that exhibit these family traits—there is no underlying logic of language, only a network of overlapping usages. The philosophical upshot is that traditional metaphysics asks for essential definitions of ‘knowledge,’ ‘justice,’ or ‘language,’ but these are family-resemblance concepts with no hidden essence; therefore Wittgenstein dissolves metaphysics into descriptive anthropology of how words are actually used.”

I am inclined to think the comparison to graded prototype membership is a subtle distortion, and that reducing language games to “descriptive anthropology” misses something about normativity and rule-following, but I am not sure where the line is between a tolerated simplification and an actual error.
