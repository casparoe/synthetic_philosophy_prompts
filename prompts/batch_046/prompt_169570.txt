Having recently attempted to apply Jain anekāntavāda to mediating disputes within my own family, I want a joke that works inside the actual logical machinery of syādvāda rather than treating it as generic relativism. Specifically, the punchline should turn on the tension between the first predication mode—syāt asti, “in some respect it is”—and the fourth mode—syāt avaktavyam, “in some respect it is inexpressible”—when both are applied to the same mundane domestic claim, such as whether my brother left the refrigerator door open. The joke should be intelligible to someone who understands that saptabhaṅgī is a systematic seven-fold schema for qualifying assertions, not a license for contradiction, and it should avoid reducing the doctrine to the cliché that everyone is partly right.
