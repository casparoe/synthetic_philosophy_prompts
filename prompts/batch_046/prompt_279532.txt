I'm currently enrolled in a graduate seminar on collective epistemology, and in our recent module on idealized architectures of deliberation we examined proposals for scaling human reasoning without introducing autonomous artificial cognition. During discussion, a colleague offered a concise synthesis of Paul Christiano's HCH framework and the accompanying notion of factored cognition, which I have reconstructed here as faithfully as I can: HCH is essentially a recursive peer-consultation tree in which each node is an identical copy of a single human agent who delegates sub-questions downward and then synthesizes the returned judgments into an answer for her parent; factored cognition, meanwhile, is the prior thesis that any complex reasoning task can be vertically decomposed into atomic, individually transparent steps that are then recombined through a mechanical aggregation procedure. My colleague insisted that the two proposals operate on entirely independent dimensions—HCH expanding judgment horizontally across parallel nodes, factored cognition reducing it vertically into simpler units—so that together they provide a complete, closed picture of how unaugmented human cognition could be amplified to arbitrary scale. He added that the central philosophical vulnerability of this pairing is an implicit assumption of logical omniscience at every node, since each copy must apparently grasp the full logical context of the root question in order to delegate meaningfully, which renders the model descriptively implausible for actual human deliberation.

The synthesis strikes me as elegant, yet I am uneasy about several points where precision may have been sacrificed for neatness. Would you audit this summary for me? Specifically: does HCH genuinely presuppose that the nodes are identical copies of one agent in the strong sense implied, or is the identity relation more structural or functional? Is factored cognition accurately characterized as a vertical decomposition into atomic steps that are mechanically recombined, or does it involve interdependent modules whose separation is conditional rather than absolute? Are the two ideas truly orthogonal in the way my colleague suggests—one horizontal, one vertical—or is factored cognition actually meant to operate within the nodes of an HCH tree, so that their relationship is nested rather than additive? And regarding the objection about logical omniscience: is this a recognized critique within the literature on scalable oversight, or has my colleague imported it from a different debate about idealized rational agents, perhaps misapplying it here? Please distinguish, point by point, what is accurate, what is a subtle overstatement or conflation, and whether the conclusion that together they form a complete amplification architecture survives once the misrepresentations are corrected.
