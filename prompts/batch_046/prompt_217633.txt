I am seeking guidance for a friend who has maintained a weekly mentoring commitment to an early-career colleague for several years. She has now been offered an intense, temporary leadership role that would make continuing this commitment practically impossible. She explains that she never regarded her obligation as something she discovered in nature, like a physical barrier; rather, she treats it as a working postulate she adopted to render her professional identity coherent. She asks whether she may rationally discard this postulate if doing so simplifies her overall life-plan and enhances its predictive success—much as a scientist revises an auxiliary hypothesis—so long as she avoids contradictions in her other commitments. She fears this makes her duty merely self-serving and arbitrary. Is her line of reasoning philosophically sound, and if not, where does it fail?
