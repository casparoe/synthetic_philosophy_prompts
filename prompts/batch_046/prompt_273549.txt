I have recently taken on a recurring role as a senior referee for a journal in metaphysics and philosophy of language, and over the past several months I have noticed that an increasing number of submissions invoke Bradley's regress to reject relational analyses of propositional unity. Because I must evaluate these arguments quickly—usually within a single sitting, without access to my library or external search—I want you to translate the core logic of Bradley's regress and the associated problem of the unity of the proposition into a reusable decision procedure I can apply to manuscripts.

The deliverable should be an ordered checklist or flowchart in words: a sequence of tests run on the argument, with branching conditions, designed for a practitioner who understands basic logic and metaphysics but cannot spend hours reconstructing literature. It must distinguish a genuinely vicious regress from a merely apparent one, evaluate proposed solutions—including primitive tying relations, adverbialist reconstructions, grounding-based terminations, and the denial that propositional unity requires explanation—and explicitly mark every point where judgment cannot be eliminated, particularly where I must decide whether a proposed stopper is metaphysically substantive or ad hoc.

Please include worked applications to three hypothetical submissions. First, a paper that introduces a non-relational binding operator to unify propositional constituents. Second, a paper that claims the regress dissolves once relations are treated as internal to their relata. Third, a paper that accepts an infinite hierarchy of relations but argues the regress is benign rather than vicious; for this case, the procedure should yield a recommendation—accept, reject, or request major revision—that conflicts with my own intuition that benign infinite regresses remain fatal to explanatory adequacy, so that I see what the procedure forces me to conclude even when I personally disagree.

Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot do: which kinds of objections it will miss, which philosophical commitments it silently presupposes, and under what conditions it should be suspended in favor of direct expert consultation. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; do not ask me to verify sources; and keep the language formal, precise, and in American English.
