Here's an argument I sketched about dispositional essentialism that now seems to prove far more than I meant.

(1) Anti-Humean dispositional essentialism holds that properties carry their causal and nomic roles essentially: mass essentially attracts with inverse-square force, negative charge essentially repels, and so on.
(2) If a property has a causal role essentially, then in any possible world where that property exists it plays exactly that role.
(3) Consider a hypothetical property—call it "schmass"—that in all actual conditions behaves exactly like mass, but in worlds with a gravitational constant below 10⁻³⁰ behaves repulsively.
(4) If schmass is metaphysically possible, then there exists a property qualitatively identical to mass in actual conditions yet bearing a different essential causal profile.
(5) But (2) implies that nothing can be qualitatively identical to mass yet have a different essential profile.
(6) So essentialism rules out schmass.
(7) Generalizing: for any actual property P, essentialism rules out every "schm-P" that duplicates P's actual behavior but diverges in other possible conditions.
(8) Therefore essentialism eliminates all modal variation in how properties behave across worlds.
(9) But then essentialism does not merely make actual laws necessary; it makes every counterfactual about properties false and collapses modal space into a single point.
(10) That proves too much: the view was meant to ground law-necessity, not to annihilate metaphysical possibility altogether.
(11) So we should reject dispositional essentialism in favor of a Humean regularity account, on which properties are categorical bases and laws are contingent patterns.

I'd prefer to keep (1) and (10) if I can—essentialism is attractive, and the conclusion really does feel excessive. Which step should I reject, why did it look fine, and what does each rejection cost? In particular, is the flaw in (4)'s assumption that qualitative identity can be fixed independently of causal role, or in (8)'s slide from "ruling out schm-properties" to "eliminating all modal variation"? And if I drop (2) instead, do I still have an anti-Humean view, or do I collapse back into Humeanism?
