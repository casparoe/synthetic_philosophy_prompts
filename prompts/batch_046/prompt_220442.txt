State your credence, on a scale from 0 to 1, that a cognitive system trained solely on static cross-modal pairings—e.g., text and images, with no temporal continuity, no proprioceptive feedback loop, and no capacity for intervention in the world—could ever achieve genuine semantic grounding for its symbols, where grounding means reference to real-world properties rather than mere functional coherence. Decompose your answer into four terms: (1) the prior probability that grounding requires direct causal-intervention capacity rather than predictive correlation alone; (2) the probability that internal model-building derived from static pairs can functionally substitute for that intervention capacity; (3) the probability that "genuine semantic grounding" is itself a well-defined, satisfiable condition; and (4) any interaction or normalization factor you think applies between them. For each term provide a rough number and a one-line justification, then combine them into your final credence. Finally, identify which term contributes the most to your overall uncertainty and is most susceptible to being shifted by a conceptual argument rather than by empirical results, and briefly describe what form that argument would take.
