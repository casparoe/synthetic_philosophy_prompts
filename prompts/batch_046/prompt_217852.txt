Consider a statue formed from a specific lump of clay in which, over centuries, every atom is gradually and imperceptibly replaced—atom by atom—by atoms drawn exclusively from a second, chemically identical lump that was never shaped. The substitution is guided continuously by an archived digital design registry that preserves the statue's exact form and structural relations, yet no physical substrate from the original clay remains. Give your credence that the object present at the end is numerically identical to the original statue rather than a new statue constituted by the second lump's matter, decomposed into: (a) the probability that material constitution permits identity despite total material substitution when formal and design-causal continuity is preserved; (b) the probability that the specific provenance of the replacement atoms introduces a historical discontinuity breaking identity; (c) the probability that identity over time requires minimal spatiotemporal overlap of original material; and (d) any other factor you judge essential, with a number and one line of justification for each term, then the combined result. State which term you are least confident in, and describe the argument or finding that would most shift your overall credence. Please answer in British English.
