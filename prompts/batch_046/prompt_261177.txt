I am drafting a six-month workshop proposal in philosophy of mind, and I need the conceptual framing sharpened into a precise, fundable question. The familiar paradox of phenomenal judgment for epiphenomenalism says that if phenomenal properties lack causal efficacy, then judgments about them—such as recognizing that one is now in pain—are either causally disconnected from their objects or systematically unreliable, which threatens the very arguments used to defend epiphenomenalism. Most treatments in the debate treat phenomenal access as a uniform capacity: a single mechanism by which mental states become objects of judgment. I want to inject a distinction that this literature largely ignores, dividing phenomenal judgments into (1) occurrent phenomenal-registration judgments—immediate, non-comparative reports of present qualitative character—and (2) diachronic phenomenal-comparison judgments—assessments that a current qualitative state differs in intensity, quality, or structure from a remembered prior one.

The proposal should state the core question precisely: does the causal inertness of phenomenal properties generate a genuinely distinct paradox for comparative phenomenal judgments that does not arise for occurrent registrations, and if so, can a non-causal structural-correlation account resolve the comparative case without abandoning epiphenomenalism? You must explain why existing positions—both epiphenomenalist and physicalist—leave this open, namely that they defend or attack phenomenal access as a monolithic capacity and therefore never test whether causal inertness selectively undermines comparison while leaving registration intact. Lay out the method in realistic monthly stages: conceptual analysis distinguishing the success conditions for each judgment type; formal modeling of what causal inertness permits for correlation-based comparison across time; and evaluation of whether the distinction collapses under scrutiny. Specify what a useful six-month deliverable looks like—likely a framework paper with explicit failure conditions—and define project failure: if both judgment types turn out equally vulnerable (or equally safe), then the paradox remains undifferentiated and the framing gains nothing. Keep the scope tight; do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, as I will supply those myself.
