Numerical-relativity simulations routinely discard metric configurations that differ only by a smooth coordinate transformation, retaining a single representative of each diffeomorphism equivalence class as the physical state. By contrast, certain quantum-gravity counting procedures treat every such gauge-related copy as a statistically distinct microstate that contributes separately to the path-integral measure or to entropy calculations. What philosophical commitments about spacetime ontology does each practice presuppose? Does the simulation rule implicitly commit one to a relationalist denial that spacetime points have independent identity, or can a substantivalist who accepts the hole argument justify the same computational convention on purely practical grounds? Conversely, does the counting practice tacitly reject the hole argument’s claim that these copies are physically identical, or can it be reconciled with a view that distinguishes physical equivalence from statistical distinguishability? Reconstruct the implicit theories behind each practice, indicate whether either strictly requires its apparent ontological stance or is compatible with rival views, and say what each practice would have to change if the other’s philosophical commitment turned out to be correct.
