Two friends of mine, neither with a philosophy background, got into a long argument after one of them read a summary of Schopenhauer’s idea that music is not like painting or poetry — those copy Platonic Ideas, but music copies the Will itself directly, without representation. Riley took this to mean that anything bypassing representational form is closer to Schopenhauer’s ideal, and argued that modern generative audio built from seismic data — pure low-frequency drones with no melody, harmony, or human compositional intent — is actually more Schopenhauerian than a Mozart symphony, because it strips away the “grades” analogy and expresses raw Will through vibration alone. Sam pushed back that Schopenhauer specifically maps bass, harmony, melody, and soprano onto the four grades of the Will’s objectification — mineral, plant, animal, and human — so without that hierarchical structure you do not have music expressing the Will, you just have disorder; requiring form is not smuggling representation back in, it is what makes music a direct image rather than random noise.

Riley replied that the voice-to-grade correspondence is only an analogy for how humans grasp music, not a definitional requirement, and that Schopenhauer’s essential point is direct emotional expression of universal striving, which seismic drones achieve even more purely by abandoning tonal structure altogether. Sam answered that if you drop the formal correspondence, Schopenhauer cannot distinguish music from a thunderclap or a scream, and his claim that music ranks above all other arts collapses because any direct emotional trigger would qualify. Riley then said Sam was confusing form with representation: painting needs form to depict an Idea, but music never depicts Ideas in the first place, so demanding tonal architecture is contradictory — the drone expresses Will through unmediated bass-level vibration, which is precisely the mineral-grade expression Schopenhauer describes. Sam finished by insisting that Schopenhauer’s hierarchy depends on music having a specific internal order that mirrors the world’s inner nature, and that without melody and harmony there is no mirror, only formless sound.
