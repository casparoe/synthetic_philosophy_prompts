I've been trying to do background for this novel I'm writing about a Neolithic settlement in the Orkneys, and I keep getting stuck on how archaeologists actually claim to know anything about minds that left no writing. I was talking with my friend Mara who did a course on this, and she walked me into this argument I can't exit.

(1) Any reading of an artifact—say, a polished axe burried with a body—has to use conceptual categories we already possess: 'gift,' 'status,' 'ritual,' even the idea of an 'object biography.' (2) If we try to purge our own categories and just describe the raw physical properties, we end up with geology, not archaeology; interpretation dies. (3) So archaeology is either pure anachronistic projection, or it's impossible. (4) But clearly it's not impossible—archaeologists distinguish a ceremonial pit from a rubbish pit, they talk about social memory embedded in monuments—so interpretation works. (5) That must mean our categories aren't projections but accurate mappings onto past mental structures. (6) Therefore when we find grave goods arranged with a skeleton, we can legitimately read that as evidence those people held a belief in material continuation after death structurally identical to ours. (7) Extend that consistently, and every burnt layer, every posthole pattern, every imported obsidian flake proves its makers shared our modern institutional logic—markets, religions, bureaucracies. (8) But that's absurd; surely Orkney farmers in 3000 BCE weren't running mental spreadsheets. (9) So we're stuck: either archaeology says nothing, or it proves ancient people were essentially modern rational actors.

I want to reject (8) without falling into (3). Where's the slip—maybe between using a category and attributing its full modern content? I also want to keep the intuition that a burried axe can genuinely tell us something about belief, without committing me to the idea that they had our exact concept of property rights in the afterlife. What's the least costly premise to drop, and what does giving it up cost me if I'm trying to write characters who feel real but not anachronistic?
