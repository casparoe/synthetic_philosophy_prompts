ok so there's this pattern i keep running into, in philosophy of mind and in formal logic, and i dont know if it has its own name. instance one: a reflective agent that can revise its beliefs and even its reasoning rules, but there's a hardwired "do not self-modify" flag in its architecture — the flag is physically inside the agent, the agent can predict its effects perfectly, but it can't represent the flag's own code as something it could change. instance two: a logical system that can prove statements about its own provability, except for one specific consistency axiom that governs the system; that axiom can be mentioned in proofs but never derived or suspended inside it. in both cases you get what looks like a tangled hierarchy — the system is partly about itself — but there's also this leftover bit, an inviolate level, except it's not sitting neatly outside like DNA versus the cell. it's embedded right inside the loop. Hofstadter's drawing hands draw each other, but neither hand holds the pencil; in these cases the pencil is inside one of the hands, yet still not drawn by it. is there an established term for that specific configuration — an inviolate level that's physically internal to a strange loop? or is it just a special case of tangled hierarchy with a protected node? if there's no precise term, how would you carve it out from a hidden parameter or an unmodeled cause, and what would you call the failure mode when someone tries to formalize that internal leftover bit and accidentally breaks the loop?
