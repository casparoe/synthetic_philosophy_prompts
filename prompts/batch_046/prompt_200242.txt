my department's ethics board for bio-robotics experiments sorts every subtrate into three buckets. bucket one gets full review with pain-mitigation rules: mammals, birds, and octopuses. bucket two gets lighter expedited review: decapod crustaceans, social insects like honeybees, and any reinforcement-learning model with over ten million paramaters running for more than a week. bucket three is exempt: solitary insects, plants, standard thermostats, and small neural nets. but there's this weird asymmetry -- if you terminate a bucket-two model after 100k training steps you have to file a cessation report explaining welfare impact, but shutting off a bucket-three thermostat that was adapting to lab conditions needs no paperwork, even though both are feedback loops adjusting to heat. they also allow nociceptive assays on solitary crickets in bucket three but not on bees in bucket two, despite both learning avoidance. what theory of moral status are they implicitly running? do the buckets assume sentience comes in sharp thresholds, or is it just a sloppy gradation? and does putting a big RL agent in bucket two with decapods commit them to functionalism about consciousness, or could a biological naturalist still defend the split? also, is the thermostat exception actually consistent with how they treat the small model, or does it reveal they're smuggling in a complexity criterion they never state out loud?
