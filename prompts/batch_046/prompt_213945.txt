I want a rigorous specification of what a theory of population axiology must actually deliver if it is to be adequate, not merely to avoid the repugnant conclusion. Please give me the full desiderata list, with particular weight on constraints that are often treated as peripheral but are conceptually central.

The requirements should include the following, each with its motivating case or conceptual tension and whether it is negotiable or non-negotiable:

1. The theory must adjudicate distributions where the same aggregate welfare is spread across lives of radically different qualitative richness—e.g., lives with high welfare but minimal autonomy versus lives with moderate welfare but extensive self-authorship—without collapsing the distinction between welfare-level and the structural quality of a life.

2. It must handle "neutral-range" additions: populations in which every added life is neither good nor bad for the one who lives it, yet the addition seems to alter the axiological status of the world in a way that pure welfarism cannot register.

3. It must be consistent with a plausible account of intergenerational obligation that does not treat potential people as merely instrumental to present welfare, but also does not grant them full present-moral-status.

4. It must deliver a determinate verdict on the mere-addition case—adding a very large number of barely-good lives to a smaller population of excellent lives—without appealing to an ad hoc discontinuity in the value function.

5. It must explain why the repugnant conclusion is genuinely repugnant without assuming that average welfare is intrinsically valuable, since that assumption is precisely what generates the paradox in the first place.

For each desideratum, please indicate which standard competing approaches—totalism, averageism, critical-level theories, person-affecting views—are eliminated or severely weakened by it. Then identify which single requirement does the most damage to the field of candidates, and assess whether the remaining desiderata are jointly satisfiable or whether the list itself reveals an inherent tension in the project of population ethics. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want the conceptual architecture evaluated on its own terms.
