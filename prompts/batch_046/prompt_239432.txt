Since my mother was diagnosed with Alzheimer's and I have taken on the coordination of her daily care, I have become preoccupied with a particular failure mode of her cognition: she does not lose isolated facts, but rather the implicit framing that tells her which properties of a situation are stable and which require updating. Watching her place a teacup on a chair she has just moved, as though the chair's new location were not part of the scene that needed revision, has made the philosophical frame problem suddenly intimate. In artificial intelligence, the problem concerns how a system can represent the consequences of an action without being forced to enumerate all the properties that remain unchanged; for decades, philosophers and researchers have debated whether it reveals a deep limitation of symbolic architectures, whether it is dissolved by embodied or statistical approaches, or whether it was never well-formed to begin with. Lately, I have found myself drawn to a less discussed possibility: that contemporary large language models do not solve the frame problem through any internal reasoning about persistence, but instead practice what might be termed salience borrowing—reproducing the statistical distribution of human descriptions in which contextual stability is already taken for granted, without ever constructing an internal model of what does not change. If this is right, then the longstanding disagreement between representationalists and their critics may be mislocated, because the core issue is not how a system tracks non-effects, but whether any internal mechanism for framing is necessary at all, or whether correlation with human narratives of stability is sufficient. Given this background, I am asking for a diagnosis of why the frame-problem debate has stalled: is the impasse substantive, verbal, or a case of different camps answering distinct questions—about symbol manipulation, about statistical learning, about embodied skill—under one shared label? Please specify the hidden commitments that prevent each side from conceding ground, identify whether an empirical finding, a formal result, or a conceptual distinction could break the stalemate, and say what progress would actually look like rather than mere restatement of familiar positions.
