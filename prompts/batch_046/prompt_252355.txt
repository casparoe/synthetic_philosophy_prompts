Please explain the conceptual tension between Lewisian counterpart theory and the thesis of transworld identity, with particular attention to a scenario that standard treatments rarely examine: gradual modal drift. Consider a continuum of possible worlds arranged so that, in each successive world, the qualitative profile of an individual is altered only infinitesimally relative to the previous one, producing a chain of counterparts that extends from the actual world to a distant world in which the final counterpart shares almost none of the original's salient properties. Analyse why this drift threatens to collapse the distinction that counterpart theory requires—between being identical across worlds and being a mere counterpart—without permitting the theorist to fall back on transworld identity. In particular, evaluate whether revising the similarity metric or introducing a non-arbitrary threshold can preserve a determinate boundary, or whether the scenario ultimately forces the counterpart theorist to concede that there is no objective fact of the matter as to whether the distant individual is still 'you'.
