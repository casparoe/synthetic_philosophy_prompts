I've been buying Dennett's multiple drafts thing pretty hard lately, especially after a weird dissociative episode last month where I literally couldn't tell if a memory was "mine" or just reconstructed on the spot. But I've added this twist: the Cartesian theater isn't just wrong about location, it's wrong about the conditions that force coherence. I think there's a kind of social stitching in the prefrontal cortex that only binds drafts into a pseudo-first-person narrative when you're answerable to an interlocuter — like in conversation or moral accounting. Without that pressure, you just have loose drafts with no owner. So consciousness isn't an internal movie; it's a socially-triggered binding error, basically.

What would actually count against this? I don't want the usual "but what about qualia" handwave. I want the real defeaters: which neuro results (say, about pre-frontal binding in isolation) or which phenominological cases (solitary confabulation? dreams?) would break it. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward. Would a committed version of this view have to concede that solitary introspection produces no binding? And is that a problem or a feature? Give me a committed take — don't just list options, tell me if this view is actually exposed or if it's been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that insulation is a vice for a view of this kind.
