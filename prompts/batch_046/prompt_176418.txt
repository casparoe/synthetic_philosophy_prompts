Rather than treating entrenchment as a purely linguistic or historical property of predicates, Voss argues that a predicate is projectible only if it corresponds to a causally homogeneous kind-structure that permits continuous modal interpolation: in other words, grue fails not merely because it is unentrenched, but because its satisfaction conditions require an unexplained discontinuous jump in the underlying causal architecture of emeralds at time t. She further claims that this explanatory continuity criterion solves a lingering tension in scientific taxonomy—specifically, it distinguishes genuinely lawlike biological classifications, such as the sharp but causally anchored boundary between monotremes and therian mammals, from ad hoc grue-style partitions, without collapsing into a conventionalist appeal to linguistic habit.
