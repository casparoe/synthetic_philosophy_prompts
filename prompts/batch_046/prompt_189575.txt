Design a 120-minute structured simulation for ten graduate students in the history of philosophy that makes them experience why Hobbes insisted geometry and civil philosophy must be synthetic — built by constructing figures through motion rather than by symbolic analysis — and why he therefore rejected Wallis’s algebraic methods as producing only defective knowledge. Divide the group into two factions of five: the Constructors, who may only generate a shared political agreement by physically moving tokens across a table according to rules drawn from Hobbes’s actual restrictions in De Corpore (no division of a continuous line into discrete points, no use of negative magnitudes, every figure must be traceable to the motion of a point that the students themselves enact), and the Analysts, who may use any symbolic notation, equations, or logical deduction to reach the same agreement. Give each faction an identical brief: to establish the terms of a commonwealth that resolves three staged conflicts — property, sovereignty, and succession — within 60 minutes, with the payoff that the group reaching a stable, unanimously endorsed contract first chooses the seminar’s next reading, but with the hidden constraint that any agreement containing an undefined term or an intermediate step that has not been physically constructed is declared void by the facilitator acting as geometric censor. Provide a precise timeline: 10 minutes for rules, 20 minutes for the first conflict, 20 minutes for the second, 20 minutes for the third, 20 minutes for direct comparison of the two contracts, and 30 minutes for debrief. Arrange the rules so that the Constructors initially move slowly and seem handicapped, yet discover that their enforced clarity about first principles prevents hidden contradictions that eventually undermine the Analysts’ faster but symbolically opaque agreement. In the debrief, map each stage onto Hobbes’s dispute with Wallis and onto the method of Leviathan: show where synthetic construction forced explicit agreement on definitions, where the Analysts’ shortcuts introduced ambiguity analogous to the analytic treatment of magnitudes as independently existing symbols, and where both groups faced the same problem Hobbes never solved — the transition from geometric bodies to the artificial body of the state. Be explicit about what the simulation cannot reproduce: Hobbes’s metaphysical commitment that motion is the sole cause of all phenomena, which cannot be staged without anachronism or physical absurdity, and which the facilitator must supply by direct explanation after the exercise; also note that the small material payoff cannot replicate the existential stakes of leaving the state of nature, so the emotional force of Hobbes’s argument must be supplied through guided reflection rather than through the game mechanics alone.
