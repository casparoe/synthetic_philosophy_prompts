I've been reading a lot of recent work on germs and climate as historical actors, and there's a specific move I keep seeing that I don't have a word for. An author will treat something like the 1815 Tambora eruption or the Black Death pathogen as genuinely directing events -- not just background noise but an actor that reshapes politics -- and then, just when the determinism gets too thick, they'll drop in a quick nod to human 'adaption' or 'cultural response' that doesn't actually change the causal story. The non-human thing still carries all the explanatory weight; the human part is like a disclaimer sticker. Is there an established concept for this? I don't mean plain old enviromental determinism, since that's at least consistent. I mean this split-level thing where agency is nominally shared but functionally one-sided. If it doesn't have a name, can you coin one with clear edges -- what counts, what doesn't, and how it differs from a genuinely multi-causal account where humans and microbes or volcanoes actually interact?
