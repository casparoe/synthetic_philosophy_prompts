I serve on my university's Committee on Interdisciplinary Program Accreditation, and at next month's session we must vote on a proposal to establish a joint Ph.D. track—provisionally named Embodied Mathematical Epistemology—administered jointly by the Department of Mathematics and the Institute for Cognitive Neuroscience. The proposal's core philosophical thesis is that Benacerraf's access problem can be resolved without abandoning mathematical realism if we reconceive mathematical objects not as causally isolated Platonic entities, but as invariant structural patterns that are physically instantiated wherever their realizers occur; on this view, the brain's predictive-processing hierarchies do not merely represent mathematics but enter into genuine causal-informational relations with these structures, thereby supplying the epistemic link that standard Platonism lacks. The mathematics faculty on the committee argue that this collapses ontology into psychology—tracking a neural encoding of a structure is not knowledge of the structure itself, and the proposal mistakes a theory of mathematical cognition for an account of mathematical truth. The neuroscience and philosophy-of-science members reply that insisting on a non-causal access relation begs the question in favor of an outdated dualism, and that the committee has no mandate to enforce Platonist metaphysics as a condition of institutional approval.

Please draft the committee opinion I will circulate before the vote: it should recommend approval, conditional approval, or rejection of the program, with a concrete timeline for implementation or for submitting revised accreditation criteria; it must treat the mathematics faculty's objection as genuinely serious rather than as mere disciplinary turf protection; and it should address specifically whether the predictive-processing framework actually closes Benacerraf's epistemic gap or simply displaces it into the ontology of structural instantiation.
