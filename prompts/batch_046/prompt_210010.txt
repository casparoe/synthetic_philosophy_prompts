Write an essay examining whether Gould and Lewontin’s spandrels critique of adaptationism retains its force once we recognize a particular class of evolutionary byproducts—call them dependency spandrels. These are structures that originate as non-adaptive architectural byproducts, but subsequently become so deeply embedded in an organism’s developmental and functional architecture that later adaptive modifications build directly upon them. Because excising the original spandrel would now collapse the derived adaptations, retrospective evolutionary analysis tends to treat the spandrel itself as if it had been selected for its present supporting role. Weigh the argument that this phenomenon does not rescue adaptationism, since the spandrel’s current indispensability remains a historical contingency rather than an original design feature, against the counter-argument that evolutionary explanation ought to be evaluated by present functional integration rather than by historical origin, so that dependency spandrels effectively become adaptations in all but name. Reach and defend a specific conclusion: does the existence of dependency spandrels undermine the spandrels critique, transform it into a more nuanced version, or leave it essentially intact?
