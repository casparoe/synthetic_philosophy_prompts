I serve as the senior communications officer for a regional water authority. Earlier this month, our laboratory detected trace concentrations of a recently synthesised fluorinated ether—present at levels well below existing statutory limits, yet for which no long-term epidemiological study exists. Our contracted toxicologist has produced an internal risk assessment concluding that consumption poses no measurable acute hazard, whilst explicitly recording substantial uncertainty regarding chronic, low-dose exposure. Yesterday, a journalist asked me, on the record, whether residents can be assured that the tap water is safe. I feel that an unqualified "yes" overstates what our evidence actually establishes; that "no" or "we do not know" would provoke disproportionate alarm; and that a carefully qualified reply will probably be truncated into a headline implying official admission of danger. I sense there is a deeper principle governing what I am permitted, or perhaps obliged, to claim when speaking in an institutional capacity, but I cannot name it precisely. Could you advise me on how I ought to respond? In particular, does my discomfort indicate a genuine boundary on proper institutional reassurance—something about the conditions that must be met before a public body may offer such assurance—rather than mere anxiety about media interpretation?
