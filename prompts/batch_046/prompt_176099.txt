I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about personal continuity if there's no self (anatta), going by gut reactions on cases. When a child in Sri Lanka claims past-life memories of being a dead villager and also shares his phobias and mannerisms, I feel like it's genuinely that villager continuing - not a soul, but teh same mental stream. But if the child only has the memories and is otherwise totally different in character, I feel it's just information carried over, not the person. With dementia: if my mum forgets who I am but still gets angry in exactly her old behaviour, I think she's still in there somewhere. But if brain damage wipes every emotional pattern and only reflexes remain, I think the person has ended, even though the heart still beats. And when I sit and notice thoughts arising with no owner present, I dont think I've vanished - I think I've seen through a useful fiction. The case I keep going back and forth on is this: suppose we map every neural and psychological pattern of a dying meditator, run them on a synthetic substrate that behaves identically, and the biological body stops. Is that the same stream, or just a perfect forgery? I feel pulled both ways. Is there a coherent principle - maybe something about causal dependence on living processes versus functional replication - that captures my other verdicts, and which ones does it force me to drop? Also, what does it predict for a case I haven't listed: if two people suffer identical injuries and both recover with swapped personalities?
