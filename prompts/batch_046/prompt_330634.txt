I'm currently taking a graduate seminar on democratic theory and collective decision-making, and for a course project I'm trying to operationalize the Condorcet jury theorem in a context far removed from its textbook binary-vote framing. Specifically, I want a reusable decision procedure that a municipal participatory-budgeting coordinator could apply—without statistical software and in roughly thirty minutes—to proposals that mix technical feasibility with severe value-laden trade-offs, such as a flood-barrier design that protects one neighborhood while displacing residents in another.

The procedure should preserve the theorem's core logic—independent judgments with competence above chance tending toward correct collective outcomes—while explicitly accommodating three complications that standard presentations bracket: heterogeneous competence across dimensions (citizens may understand local values well but not hydraulic engineering), asymmetric error costs (approving an unworkable design is far more damaging than rejecting a viable one), and correlation clusters within demographic or geographic groups that violate independence.

Please construct the deliverable as follows:

1. A step-by-step protocol ordered as a checklist, specifying the question to ask at each stage, what counts as a sufficient answer, and the exact termination point—whether "convene a citizens' jury," "defer to technical experts," "reject the proposal," or "escalate to an explicitly political value decision." Flag every step where judgment cannot be eliminated, particularly in weighing asymmetric costs or setting correlation thresholds, and do not disguise those judgment calls as mechanical rules.

2. A minimal set of practical tests the coordinator can run to detect correlation clusters and to estimate competence separately for the technical and value dimensions of a given proposal, using only information typically available in a municipal filing.

3. Three worked applications: (a) a rainwater-capture proposal where technical competence among residents is high but value disagreement is intense, with the procedure yielding an answer the coordinator is likely to resist; (b) a seawall proposal where affected residents form a tight correlation cluster and competence is uneven, causing the protocol to stop with expert deferral; and (c) a borderline case where independence and competence conditions barely hold, so the procedure returns an explicit uncertainty statement and hands the final call back to the coordinator.

Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; the aim is conceptual translation into practice, not literature review. Keep the framing general enough that it does not depend on verified titles or dates of specific historical works.
