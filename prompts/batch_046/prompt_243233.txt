I've been rewatching Arrival — the Denis Villeneuve one with Amy Adams — and I'm stuck on whether it's doing serious philosophy or just dressing up sci-fi tropes in Sapir-Whorf clothing. The heptapods write in those circular, simultaneous symbols, and Louise learns Heptapod B and starts perceiving time as a block where past and future are already laid out. But is the movie actualy claiming that language restructures cognition in the strong Whorfian sense, or is it smuggling in a metaphysical claim that she was always living in a four-dimensional universe and the language just gives her the conceptual tools to notice it? There's also the child she decides to have even though she knows she'll die young — does that dramatize a kind of compatibilist acceptance that only makes sense if you buy her new temporal ontology, or is it just emotional melodrama that breaks the philosophical logic?
