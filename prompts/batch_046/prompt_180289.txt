Write an essay on whether intellectual property can really be justified, but dont just rehearse the Lockean labor-mixing story against the utilitarian incentive argument. I want you to take seriously this specific twist: strong IP doesnt just fence off an idea-commons like the classic enclosure critique says—it actively degrades the shared conceptual vocabulary people need to even articulate resistance, a kind of "cognitive enclosure." Weigh that against the standard defenses, then reach a conclusion: is owning an idea more like owning land, or more like claiming private ownership over a dialect? Justify the comparison you pick. Also say exactly what'd change your mind—maybe if idea-scarcity mirrored physical scarcity, or if the degradation claim turned out to depend on a false theory of concept-learning—and flag where your reasoning feels shakiest.
