Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains (A1 and A2) that must recur exactly—on Robert Nozick’s tracking theory of knowledge and its refusal of epistemic closure. Let the first refrain state the counterfactual sensitivity condition (that if the belief were false, the subject would not hold it), and let the second state the entailment that fails to transmit knowledge (the sceptical hypothesis that an ordinary belief entails). The poem’s structural work should come from how these lines repeat and finally pair in the quatrain: their exact recurrence should enact the rigidity of the tracking conditions, while their shifting context across stanzas should show why closure still breaks.

Order the tercets as follows. The first establishes an ordinary tracking success—for instance, knowing that one has hands—where belief genuinely tracks truth across nearby worlds. The second introduces the counterfactual machinery that defines tracking, sketching the worlds in which the belief is false and the belief is absent. The third introduces the entailed sceptical scenario (for example, being a brain in a vat) and observes that belief in this entailment persists even in worlds where it is true, because those worlds are not the ones that falsify the original belief. The fourth isolates the precise failure of closure: tracking succeeds for the base claim but fails for its entailment, since the nearby worlds that make the entailment false are not nearby worlds that make the base belief false. The fifth returns to the opening situation—hands, perception, certainty—but now heard through the unclosed gap, with the refrains carrying the accumulated failure.

The refrain lines must keep their exact wording throughout, yet change in weight and implication so that by the quatrain they sound less like secured premises and more like an unresolved loop. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher familiar with Nozick’s counterfactual conditions and his specific argument that knowledge does not close under known entailment recognises the reasoning being staged, rather than finding a mere name-drop. Do not resolve the tension into sceptical despair or realist triumph; instead, let the villanelle’s circular form suggest that the distance between what tracks and what closes remains structurally open. Write in precise but accessible language, suitable to be read aloud at the close of a graduate seminar on epistemology. Use British spelling throughout, and include no footnotes, citations, references to specific editions, or bibliographic apparatus.
