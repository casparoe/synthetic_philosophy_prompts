I'm taking a graduate seminar on modal metaphysics, and we've been working through Lewis's counterpart theory and the Kripkean view that particular objects are transworld identicals with essential properties. The standard classroom examples—whether Nixon might have lost the election, or whether the Moon could have been made of cheese—feel too coarse-grained to me, because they treat modal persistence as an all-or-nothing relation applied to simple concrete particulars, and they don't expose the machinery that handles entities with distributed membership and self-modifying rules.

I'm trying to understand how the two frameworks actually diverge when applied to something that sits between a simple object and a pure abstraction: a functioning institutional protocol with distributed agency. Specifically, imagine a decision-making body—call it an epistemic council—defined not by a fixed roster of individuals but by a recursive charter that specifies (i) how members are selected through a weighted lottery tied to prior contributions, (ii) a deliberative rule requiring unanimous consent on procedural amendments but majority rule on substantive claims, and (iii) a self-modification clause allowing the charter to be revised only by the council itself acting under its current rules. Now consider a counterfactual history in which every original human member never existed, but a structurally isomorphic set of replacements—same cognitive profiles, same relational positions—emerged through an independent social trajectory, and the charter they adopted differs only in the weighting coefficients of the lottery and in one procedural safeguard that is redundant given the other rules. Under counterpart theory we might say that in that world there is a counterpart council but not the council itself; under transworld identity we might ask whether the council, conceived as a particular abstract-concrete hybrid, could have existed with those different origins and that modified charter. Could you explain the conceptual issue here—how counterpart theory and transworld identity come apart in analyzing such an entity, what each framework is forced to say about the modal persistence conditions of its charter and its membership, and whether the disagreement is ultimately about essential origins, essential structure, or about whether 'the council' denotes a kind that permits multiple instantiation? I want to avoid bibliography requests or citation lists; I'm just trying to get the metaphysical machinery clear without relying on standard textbook illustrations.
