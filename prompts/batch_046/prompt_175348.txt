I have no formal background in philosophy, but I have been working through a problem on my own about whether the patterns we use to explain things are genuinely in the world or only in our heads. I understand that one prominent framework divides our explanatory strategies into three stances: one that looks at physical structure and law, one that looks at designed function and purpose, and one that looks at intentional states such as belief and desire. The idea seems to be that a pattern is real if it allows reliable predictions from at least one of these perspectives, even when the others fail. I want to write a short essay arguing that this framework encounters a hidden tension when applied to systems that can alter their own operating rules—such as adaptive algorithms, learning organisms, or evolutionary processes—because these systems appear to sit simultaneously in the design stance and the intentional stance.

Here is the draft of my argument. First, the design stance treats a system's purpose as fixed by its origin or architecture, allowing us to predict behavior without knowing internal representations. Second, the intentional stance treats the system's own representations—its beliefs, goals, or models—as the drivers of behavior, which works best when those representations are somewhat stable but not mechanically determined. Third, a self-modifying system seems to occupy both categories at once: its current behavior is partly explained by its original design (which made self-modification possible) and partly by representations or rules that it has generated and adopted for itself. This overlap suggests to me that either one of the stances is redundant for such systems, or that we need an intermediate stance—call it the adaptive stance—that treats self-generated rules as real patterns in their own right, neither fully designed nor fully intentional.

I am not asking for a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations, since I want to see whether the idea can stand on its own logic without relying on references I could not verify. Could you comment specifically on three points? One, whether my characterization of the design and intentional stances misrepresents their actual relationship—particularly whether the intentional stance already accommodates self-modification in a way that makes my tension disappear. Two, whether there is a coherent distinction between a system that merely responds to feedback (which still seems to belong to the design stance) and one that genuinely updates the representations guiding its behavior (which might require the intentional or adaptive stance), since this boundary seems central but I have not defined it precisely. Three, whether my proposed adaptive stance is genuinely distinct, or whether it collapses back into either the design stance (because self-modification was itself designed) or the intentional stance (because self-generated rules are just another form of belief or goal). Any suggestions for sharpening the argument, or for identifying where it might depend on an unstated assumption about what counts as a representation, would also be valuable.
