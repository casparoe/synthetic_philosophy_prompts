Given that Hofstadter's various English renderings of Marot's A une Damoyselle malade in Le Ton beau de Marot demonstrate that preserving the original rhyme royal and decasyllabic meter invariably requires replacing clinical references to illness with domesticated endearments, should we conclude that formal-constraint translation generates not a translated text but a parallel sister-poem with an inevitably domesticated emotional register? And if this semantic displacement is structurally unavoidable, does Hofstadter's argument that translation can serve as a tombeau honoring the deceased not collapse into the paradox that we memorialize a form emptied of its original referential grief?
