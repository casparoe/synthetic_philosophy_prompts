Student answer:
Lakatos argued that science proceeds through research programmes built around a hard core of basic theoretical commitments that scientists decide not to abandon. Around this sits a protective belt of auxillary hypotheses that absorb anomalies and experimental failures. There is also a positive heuristic that guides future development, and a negative heuristic that directs criticism away from the hard core.

In superstring theory the hard core is the claim that fundamental entities are one-dimensional strings vibrating in ten dimensions of spacetime. The protective belt includes auxiliary assumptions about supersymmetry breaking scales and the precise value of the cosmological constant. Between 1984 and 1995 the programme showed progressive problemshift. Then in 1995 Witten showed that the five separate string theories were limits of a single M-theory in eleven dimensions. This unification was a novel prediction arising from the positive heuristic, and it received empirical corroboration through agreements with black-hole thermodynamic calculations. Because both adjustments came from the positive heuristic rather than being ad hoc, they avoid degenerating problemshift. The only weakness was that the hard core itself remained unchanged, but Lakatos's negative heuristic explicitly permits this, so it is not actually a flaw.
