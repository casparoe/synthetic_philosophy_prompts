Write a short research proposal—say for a one-semester philosophy workshop or a small grad grant—about the Pasadena paradox and what happens when expected value is literally undefined rather than just weird. The usual takes are either "two-box because causation" or "one-box because correlation," but I think both skip over the real problem: the predictor's accuracy is conditional on your choice in a loop that makes the utility calculation fail to converge. So frame a precise question, like whether decision theory should treat an undefined expectation as a failure of rationality (you can't decide) or whether there's an implicit consistency constraint—maybe on the dependancy graph—that lets you derive a determinate reccomendation without fixing arbitrary epsilon-close probabilities. Say why the obvious alternatives miss the point, outline an approach that looks at the structural conditions for well-defined expectation rather than just running the numbers, and describe what a result would look like versus what counts as failure (e.g., ending up with "it depends on interpretation"). Be realistic about scope—this shouldn't balloon into a full dissertation on non-standard analysis. Write in American English, casual is fine, and don't include a bibliography since you'll fill that in yourself. (Also it's ok if there's a typo or two—just write naturally.)
