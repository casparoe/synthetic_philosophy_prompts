I am composing a multi-generational novel in which a family ritual—locking oneself in a room and counting floorboards under stress—repeats across five generations, and I need a reusable decision procedure, derived from the philosophical distinction between sphexish mechanical rigidity and flexible agency, that I can run on any given character without consulting a philosopher. The procedure should function as an ordered flowchart of narrative tests applied to a scene: first, whether the behavior activates in the absence of its original trigger; second, whether the character can articulate a coherent counterfactual in which she acts otherwise; third, whether interruption produces relief or escalated distress; fourth, whether the repetition serves an end she could explicitly repudiate without psychological collapse; and fifth, whether she can modify the ritual’s form while preserving its compulsory structure. At each stage, specify what result pushes the rendering toward determinism, what pushes it toward genuine freedom, and—crucially—where the result is ambiguous and must be handed back to me as an authorial judgment call rather than resolved by the procedure. The stakes are practical: I face this decision repeatedly under deadline, the rendering must survive a reader familiar with the literature on sphexishness and free will, and I need to know in advance which cases will misfire. Apply the procedure to three generations: the founding ancestor, who invents the ritual during an actual siege; a middle descendant, who performs it in a safe modern apartment, feels profound shame, yet accelerates the counting when interrupted; and a late descendant, who transforms the ritual into a taught meditation practice but explodes with rage when a student alters the sequence. Include the specific case in which the procedure yields an answer I will resist—classifying one of these figures as freely choosing despite appearing to the reader as mechanically trapped—and state exactly why. Finally, delineate what the procedure cannot determine, particularly whether recognizing one’s own rigidity alters its moral or narrative status, and flag any point where values rather than facts must carry the decision.
