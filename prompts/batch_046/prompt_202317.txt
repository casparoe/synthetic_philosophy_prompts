I'm preparing a discussion for a graduate metaphysics seminar on modal ontology, and I've grown dissatisfied with the usual framing in which counterpart theory is praised for ontological thrift and criticized for violating intuition, while transworld-identity accounts are praised for preserving intuition and criticized for essentialism. What I actually need is the full specification—the desiderata that any theory of cross-world presence or modal representation must satisfy to count as adequate—rather than another round of impressionistic trading of examples. Give me the requirements list: the phenomena it must explain (de re modal claims about persons, artifacts, and natural kinds), the distinctions it must not collapse (between essential and accidental properties, between qualitative similarity and genuine presence, between having modal status and bearing a particular modal weight), the formal constraints it must respect (consistency with the semantics of counterfactual conditionals, compatibility with standard modal logic, non-violation of parsimony principles accepted in the metaphysics of science), and the bodies of results it must accommodate without contradiction (for instance, the actual practice of scientific modeling in which researchers speak of "the same coupled model" being instantiated across different parameter worlds). For each item, provide the motivating case or theoretical consideration, and mark whether it is a non-negotiable constraint or merely the preference of a particular camp.

I also want you to inject a less conventional but conceptually serious requirement: the theory must be able to account for what we might call "process-persistence" in distributed or institutional entities—multi-generational research programs, decentralized protocols, or organizational structures in which component members and parts are replaced over time—where we still speak of the entity as being present across counterfactual histories, without reducing that presence either to universal instantiation or to a family of qualitative counterparts. Then tell me whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given standard results in modal logic or philosophy of science; if so, that impossibility result is the part I most want to see. Finally, identify which single requirement does the most damage to the dominant contender theories, and keep the weight of your answer on articulating and justifying the criteria rather than on scoring every existing view.
