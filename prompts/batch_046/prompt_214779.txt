Two students in my practical reason seminar — Jordan and Sam — got into it about internal vs. external reasons and asked me to referee since I apparently have opinions about everything. Jordan's line was that a reason has to be able to motivate you if you're rational, so telling someone who genuinely doesn't care about animals "you have reason to stop eating meat" is like telling a rock it has reason to fall downward. Unless it hooks into thier motivational set, it's not really their reason. Sam said that's confusing explanation with justification. She mentioned dormant reasons — like having reason to learn calculus for philosophy of science even if right now you'd rather watch Netflix. The reason is there, just not pulling you at the moment. Jordan replied with what he called a deliberative accessability condition: for something to count as your reason it has to be able to enter your practical reasoning, not sit out there like a tax law in a country you don't live in. Sam said that just makes internalism true by definitional fiat. Then she floated this bridge idea: maybe reasons exist objectively but only acquire pull for you through recognition of authority or value, not through prior desire. She asked Jordan about promises: you promised to help a friend move, now you despise them. Is your reason internal because you value consistency, or external because promises generate obligations regardless of current feeling? Jordan said it's internal via past endorsement — promises feed back into your own motivational structure. Sam then hit him with unendorsed obligations: you're on a beach, someone's drowning, you genuinely care about nobody but yourself. Jordan's view seems to say you have no reason to rescue them. Jordan said maybe there's hidden motivation like guilt-avoidance. They want me to go move by move and say who's closer to right, plus what's missing. I think they're both glossing over alienated complience but I'm not sure. Can you adjudicate thier exchange step by step, rate each move, and say what both sides are overlooking?
