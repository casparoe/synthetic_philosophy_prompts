I should like to explore whether this framing strengthens Berkeley's case or instead exposes a category mistake.

Could you therefore write a dialogue between two philosophers of equal standing, Dr Alistair Venn and Dr Priya Menon, in which they examine the master argument precisely through this lens? Venn should defend the view that mind-independent existence is incoherent in the same way that an unmeasured observable is physically empty, arguing that representation necessarily presupposes an interpreting agent and that Berkeley's argument succeeds once this structural parallel is recognised. Their exchange should be genuinely dialectical: Venn ought to respond that such unobserved entities are still embedded within formalisms constructed by minds, so that representational dependence is not avoided but merely displaced, whilst Menon should press the point that this reply risks reducing all scientific realism to instrumentalism without independently demonstrating the contradiction in mind-independent being. Please ensure neither interlocutor is treated as a strawman, that each addresses the other's actual arguments rather than talking past them, and that the dialogue remains focused on whether the master argument holds up or collapses into an equivocation between conditions of knowledge and conditions of existence.
