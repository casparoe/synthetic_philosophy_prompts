I'm designing a single ninety-minute seminar for philosophy-of-mind graduate students—familiar with Davidson and with recent debates about phenomenal concepts, but unacquainted with Wittgenstein's later writings—on the private-language argument. Rather than opening with the conventional beetle-in-the-box scenario, I want to frame the session around the idea that private ostensive definition might be understood as an attempt at de re mental-file creation: anchoring a linguistic term to a phenomenal type in the manner that a name is anchored to an object across contexts. Plan the explanatory arc, not the explanation itself. First, specify the dependency order: what conditions for successful mental-file reference and for public criteria must be in place before the argument's grammatical point can land, and where in that sequence Wittgenstein's distinction between epistemic privacy and logical impossibility should be introduced so that students do not treat the claim as an empirical thesis about mental-state inaccessibility. Second, identify the single carrying example or modified thought experiment—explicitly stating why the standard beetle-in-the-box illustration is inadequate for this framing, or precisely how it must be altered—and describe the properties it must possess and the properties it must lack in order to isolate grammatical privacy from epistemic privacy. Third, map the predictable misconceptions in the order they will arise: that the argument entails behaviorism; that it eliminates private experience altogether; that it confuses an observer's ignorance with a speaker's logical incapacity. For each, indicate the exact point in the arc where it should be preempted. Fourth, provide one end-of-session diagnostic question that distinguishes a student who grasps the grammatical distinction between private reference and public criteria from one who has merely learned to parrot that private language is impossible. Finally, state clearly which standard interpretive layer—such as the rule-following considerations or the community-view exegesis—you are omitting at this level and why including it would derail the arc for this audience.
