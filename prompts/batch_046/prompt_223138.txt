Consider a political philosopher applying Simone Weil's framework from her essay against political parties, interpreted not as a single definition but as a cluster of criteria. For this reader, a collective body is a "party" in Weil's pejorative sense only if it both (i) demands that individuals subordinate independent judgment to collective solidarity, and (ii) treats the collective's own survival or victory as an absolute value capable of overriding particular truths or justice. The reader's prior verdicts are these: the Communist Party of France in the 1930s is clearly a party; a university faculty senate divided into partisan blocs is not, because its factions are functional and members retain structural independence; the institutional Catholic Church is a party, because it requires doctrinal loyalty that can suppress individual conscience in the name of collective authority; the French Resistance networks of 1943–1944 are not, because they lacked organizational continuity, possessed no loyalty mechanism beyond the immediate task, and dissolved once liberation was achieved; and a decentralized online activist collective with no formal leaders or membership rolls, coordinated through viral consensus, is also not a party, because without organizational persistence there is no sustained collective self to which individuals must submit.

Now consider a new case: a decentralized autonomous organization (DAO) governing a blockchain protocol by token-weighted vote. There is no formal membership—any token-holder participates—but community norms strongly penalize public dissent from majority proposals, and the collective's coherence depends on maintaining token value, which makes the protocol's survival a non-negotiable priority. Predict the philosopher's verdict. Identify at least two competing interpretations of the reader's criteria that both accommodate the five prior verdicts, state what each predicts for the DAO, give your best-guess judgment with reasons, and propose a further hypothetical case—one with formal hierarchy and explicit loyalty demands but no collective emotional investment in self-perpetuation—that would distinguish the two interpretations.
