I was talking with my freinds about democratic reform the other day and we got stuck on this specific design problem. You know how people propose using sortition — random selection — to fill part of a legislature, usually paired with hard term limits so nobody becomes a career politician? The idea is that you serve one term, maybe three years, then you're out for good. But I keep noticing this pattern in the arguments: the citizens who actually end up in the selection pool are almost never truly random. They opt in, which means you get a lot of retired professionals, activists, or people with enough free time and education to feel comfortable saying yes. So the "random" sample is already skewed toward a particular kind of civic volunteer.

Then there's the term limit part. Because these people serve only once and for a short time, they never really master the institutional machinery. They depend on permanent staff, legal advisors, and former members who stay on in consulting roles. Meanwhile, the people who do learn the ropes are forced out, and some of them just cycle into those advisory jobs. Over a few decades, you don't have career politicians in the traditional sense — no one gets reelected — but you do get this hidden pipeline where the same social circle keeps rotating between being a "random" legislator and being a paid advisor to the next batch. It's like careerism got pushed underground into a shadow track.

Is there an established name for this exact failure mode? I don't mean the general critique that sortition pools aren't representative, and I don't mean standard bureaucratic capture where civil servants overpower elected officials. I'm asking about the specific combo where term limits plus opt-in selection accidentally build a shadow career track through advisory and staff roles, so that the anti-careerist design produces a kind of accidental aristocracy of the willing. If there's no term for it, could you coin one and draw the boundary lines — like, how is it different from just having a professional civil service, and what conditions have to be present for it to happen? Please answer in American English.
