For a third-year philosophy of mind final exam (90 minutes, one essay, 30 marks): draft a question that asks students to evaluate whether treating conscious experience as a "user illusion"—where phenomenal qualities function as evolutionary interface icons rather than representations of objective reality—can be reconciled with the denial of a substantial self without collapsing into either eliminativism or a hidden Cartesian ego. The crux should force candidates to identify exactly which structural feature of the interface analogy (iconicity, evolutionary cost, or functional transparency) does the work of dissolving the self, and then to test whether that same feature survives when applied to the claim that there is not even an enduring "user" behind the interface. A top answer must do more than summarize standard expositions of the view; it should separate students who grasp the structural tension from those who have only memorized the labels. Give a model answer of roughly 800 words and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points, stating the marks for each point (totaling 30) and a one-line note on the typical failure mode for each. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Structure your response in three named sections in this order: (1) Exam Prompt (approx. 200 words), presenting the exact text students would see; (2) Model Answer (approx. 800 words); (3) Marking Scheme (approx. 400 words), listing each point with its weight, the precise analytical move required for full marks, and the common mistake that costs points.
