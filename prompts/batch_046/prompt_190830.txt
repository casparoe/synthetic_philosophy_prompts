After a minor infection, she requires a temporary feeding tube, and her physician asks the family whether to honor the directive.

The family is divided. One side argues that the earlier competent self retains full authority—this was her autonomous choice, and overriding it violates her dignity and precedent autonomy. The other side contends that the current person, despite severe cognitive decline, possesses present interests in comfort and continued existence, and that the directive should be set aside because the earlier self no longer exists in any meaningful psychological sense.

She feared being kept alive in a state that would render her life story radically discontinuous, not primarily because she anticipated suffering, but because she believed such an ending would make the life in question not truly hers.

Given this specific framing—precedent autonomy versus present interests, mediated by her own rejection of psychological continuity in favor of narrative coherence—what is the philosophically sound course?
