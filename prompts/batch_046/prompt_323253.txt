I've been turning over in my mind whether lying and being misleading are really different in a moral sense, and I put together a line of reasoning that seems airtight but lands me in a place I don't want to be. The steps go like this:

(1) Lying is wrong mainly because the speaker abuses the listener's trust to plant a false belief directly.
(2) Deliberate misleading—saying only true things arranged so the listener will almost certainly draw a false conclusion—uses the same trust to plant the same false belief, just through the listener's own inference.
(3) So if the core wrong is the intentional exploitation of trust to cause a false belief, then lying and misleading share that core wrong exactly.
(4) Any moral difference would have to rest on something outside that core—perhaps the literal falsity of the words, or the ease with which the listener can later correct the belief.
(5) But literal falsity doesn't change the listener's belief-state or the speaker's intent; and as for correction, a lie can be undone by a simple negation, whereas a misleading arrangement often leaves hidden premises that are harder to root out, so if anything misleading is worse.
(6) Therefore lying and misleading are morally on a par; if one is a serious wrong, so is the other, to the same degree.
(7) That means when I tell my partner "I'm fine" while I'm hurt, knowing she'll take it at face value and stop asking, I'm committing the same wrong as flatly saying "I'm not hurt"—both are designed to produce a false belief by exploiting her trust.
(8) Yet that feels wrong. There does seem to be a real difference in the "I'm fine" case, perhaps because I'm leaving her an opening she could take, or because I'm not actively constructing a false statement.
(9) So (6) must fail. But I want to hold onto (1) and (3), and I don't want to say that literal falsehood suddenly matters for its own sake in (4).

Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost me? If the problem is in (3), does that mean exploitation of trust isn't the heart of the wrong? If it's in (5), am I just special-pleading for lying? I'd rather keep (1) and (3) than (6), but I need to know what I'm paying if I do.
