I am taking a course on philosophy of mind and find myself stuck on a particular knot involving Douglas Hofstadter’s view of the self. He treats the “I” not as a soul or substance but as a strange loop — a self-referential symbolic pattern that can, in principle, be partly instantiated in other brains, much as he describes feeling traces of a lost loved one’s personality persisting within his own mental patterns. At the same time, philosophers of self-locating belief insist that an “I” is essentially indexical: it must be anchored to a specific spatiotemporal location and cannot be distributed across bodies without losing its first-person character.

Please explain the conceptual issue that arises when these two frameworks collide in the following scenario. Imagine two brains linked by a hypothetical high-bandwidth cortical bridge that allows direct sharing of working-memory states. Over time, a single self-referential loop emerges that runs across both: brain A contains symbols that refer to brain B’s states, and vice versa, so that together they form one continuous strange loop, though either brain can survive separation from the bridge. If the self is merely a functional pattern, it seems there ought to be one distributed “I” spanning both bodies; yet if first-person belief requires a single centred subject, there appear to be two “I”s, each partially hosting the loop.

Go further: suppose the bridge is degraded asymmetrically, leaving brain A with full loop-integrity and brain B with only fragmented, non-self-referential traces. Is there a determinate fact about whether the original self has migrated entirely into A, split into a primary and a shadow, or dissolved altogether? Your answer should clarify whether the tension is merely verbal or reflects a deep incompatibility between pattern-based and indexical accounts of personal identity, and it should do so without invoking any highly specialised technical apparatus that a well-read but non-specialist model could not follow. Please write in British English throughout, and finish with a one-sentence summary of the core tension on its own line beginning with “Summary:”.
