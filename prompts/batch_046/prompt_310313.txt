Is there a name for this? I keep seeing long-run predicitions fail and then get rescued in a specific way—think Malthus, but also some climate-econ models. Theory T predicts crisis by D1. D1 passes, nothing happens. Instead of dropping T, people say there's a hidden brake B that pushed things to D2. Then D2 passes too, and they claim B was stronger than expected, but also that B is part of T's dynamics, so T implicitly predicted B all along. Each rescue makes T harder to falsify, but every brake is real (tech, policy), so it doesn't feel ad hoc. It's not quite Lakatos's protective belt—the belt isn't shielding a hard core, it's stretching the timeline while insisting the stretch was inside the theory from the start. And it's not plain Popper immunization, since the auxilary conditions aren't invented; they're observed delays. Does this exact move—rescuing a forecast by recursive delay-attribution—have a name?
