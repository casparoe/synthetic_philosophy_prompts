I have been trying to understand, on my own, an idea I picked up from some popular science writing — that a person's sense of being themselves might not be anchored to any particular cell or memory, but might instead be a kind of strange loop, a pattern or symbol that keeps referring back to itself. I was talking about this with a couple of friends over coffee, and we started testing it against different situations to see where it holds and where it collapses, but I keep running into the same difficulty: I can tell which cases feel like real selves and which feel like imitations, yet I cannot state the exact rule that separates them.

Here are the verdicts I find myself giving, and I want to know whether one principle can cover them all without contradiction. In my own case, when I notice myself noticing my own thoughts — that circular awareness — it feels like the genuine article, and I am convinced there is a real self there even though the physical matter of my brain is replaced over time. By contrast, a computer program that prints its own source code achieves perfect self-reference, yet to me it is clearly just a mechanical trick with no inner presence whatsoever. Then consider someone who has lost every original memory and whose only remaining mental activity is the hollow repetition of internal phrases like I remember having remembered; the loop is technically still running, but it feels emptied out, and I am inclined to say that whatever self existed has collapsed, even though the formal structure of self-reference remains intact. Next, imagine two people who have been isolated together for years, where each one's entire mental life consists solely of modeling how the other models them — no references to the outside world, just a closed circle of mutual representation spread across two bodies. Individually neither contains the loop, but together they form one; my intuition pulls me toward saying they share a single distributed self, though I recognize how strange that sounds. Finally, a dictionary entry for the word dictionary that defines itself in circular terms strikes me as reference without a loop — there is no presence, only a logical knot.

These judgments leave me with tensions I cannot resolve on my own. The computer program and the isolated pair both seem to be pure formal loops without a single biological center, yet I reject the first and tentatively accept the second. Meanwhile, the amnesiac and the dictionary both involve circular reference that lacks original content, yet I treat them differently. Is there a precise principle — perhaps involving causal integration within one system, or the loop being used to guide action, or some threshold of representational richness — that delivers all five verdicts? Or must I abandon one of my intuitions, and if so, which one costs the least? Please also tell me what your principle predicts for a case I have not listed: an artificial system trained only to predict its own future internal states, with no sensory connection to the outside world, whose representations form a closed loop that updates itself continuously.
