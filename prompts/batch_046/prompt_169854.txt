Please describe the most important implications of Newcomb's problem for an agent who treats her own future reasoning stages as epistemic peers, each with access to the same predictive model of her present decision. Specifically, if she considers adopting a binding pre-commitment to one-box, knowing that her future self can forecast her present reasoning, that she can in turn forecast that forecast, and so on—generating an internal loop of mutual prediction—how does this second-order structure alter the standard causal-decision-theoretic and evidential-decision-theoretic analyses? Does it introduce a distinctive instability, such as oscillating commitment or a self-undermining prediction, that is absent when the predictor is an external party? And what, if anything, does this imply about modeling rationality over time when the forecasting mechanism is internal rather than exogenous?
