I am preparing remarks for a workshop on distributive justice and intergenerational fairness, and I want a consistency audit of my position before I present. Here are my current commitments: (1) I adopt a relational-egalitarian diagnosis of dynastic wealth—the central harm is not merely unequal life chances but the creation of enduring relations of social domination and status hierarchy between families. (2) I hold that fair equality of opportunity requires children's life prospects to be largely independent of parental economic status, which supports substantial inheritance taxation on large transfers. (3) At the same time, I think parents possess a weighty, non-derivative moral claim to provide for their children's welfare, generating a presumptive right to bequeath modest assets that cannot be reduced to efficiency or aggregate welfare considerations. (4) I reject both the decedent-property-rights objection and the double-taxation objection to inheritance taxes, yet I maintain that testamentary freedom for small estates should be protected because the expressive and relational value of bequest carries independent weight. (5) I believe family-owned productive assets—such as small farms or closely held businesses—should receive preferential treatment in inheritance-tax regimes, both because they sustain local community stability and because they embody labor-embedded rather than purely financial wealth. (6) I take the appropriate metric for evaluating inheritance policy to be the distribution of opportunities at the point of entry into adulthood, rather than the continuous equalization of outcomes over the life course.

Please examine these commitments for joint tenability. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are merely apparent, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each real tension, specify exactly what pulls against what, how severe the conflict is, and what the least costly revision would be—whether that means adding a quantitative threshold, narrowing the scope of a principle, or abandoning one claim entirely. Then tell me which single revision would resolve the most tension across the board, and whether my overall framework remains salvageable without giving up either the relational-egalitarian framing or the equality-of-opportunity core.
