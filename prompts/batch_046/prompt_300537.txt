Hey — I'm putting together a philosophical profile for a rationalist forum thread on governance and want an epistemic-hygiene check before I post. Here are my commitments:

(a) Performance legitimacy is real: citizens in high-growth authoritarian regimes (Singapore, post-reform China) can genuinely regard their rulers as legitimate because of delivered prosperity and order, not just propaganda.

(b) But normative legitimacy is ultimately procedural. Without democratic input, good outcomes can at best supply a temporary, crisis-only excuse for rule, never a moral right to govern.

(c) I split this further: 'prosperity-legitimacy' (wealth/welfare generating a claim) is mostly a myth, but 'order-legitimacy' (preventing collapse) carries limited, transitional weight.

(d) Given (a), I think it's rational for citizens in those regimes to feel patriotic loyalty — they've recieved real benefits.

(e) Still, I think patriotism is a vice unless it's anchored in liberal-democratic instutitions.

(f) From an outside evaluator's standpoint, though, I think we should weight outcomes over procedures, since outsiders don't bear the procedural costs.

Go through these and tell me which pairs actually clash, which only look like they do, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each tension, what's the least costly revision?
