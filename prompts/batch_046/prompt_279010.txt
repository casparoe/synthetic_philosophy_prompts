In the decades since Douglas Hofstadter and Daniel Dennett’s 1981 collection The Mind's I circulated the fantasy of a volume that encodes, page by page, the complete dynamical state of Einstein’s brain—updated so that the book’s contents mirror every neural transition—philosophers of mind have repeatedly returned to the same stalled exchange. One camp treats consciousness as substrate-independent computation: if the informational relations and counterfactual dependencies of Einstein’s mental architecture are preserved, then the medium—neurons, silicon, or printed paper—should not matter, and the book, properly arranged, ought to be conscious in just the way Einstein was. The other camp insists that this misses the distinction between a static representation and a live process: the pages of a book do not causally influence one another in real time, so there is no integrated system performing the thinking, only an inert trace of one that has already occurred. In the discussion group I attend, both sides now accept the formal description of the encoding and agree that consciousness requires more than mere data storage, yet they have not moved in over a year. I suspect the deadlock may be verbal—centered on competing intuitions about what “implementation” or “causal integration” must involve—but I also wonder whether hidden empirical commitments are doing the work, perhaps about whether global workspace dynamics or predictive processing require continuous temporal coupling that a printed volume cannot supply. I want a diagnosis rather than an adjudication: is this a substantive disagreement about the metaphysics of mind, a hidden dispute about which formal properties count as computation, or an ill-posed question that collapses once we distinguish “frozen dynamics” from “live dynamics”? What, if anything, would count as progress—a precise conceptual distinction, a formal result about computation in static versus interactive media, or an argument that no resolution should be expected? Please answer with reasons and with an honest accounting of your confidence. If my framing misrepresents either the functionalist or the enactivist position, or if there is no coherent distinction to be drawn between a static encoding and a running process in this context, say so plainly rather than accommodating the premise. I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations; I want only the structural diagnosis and your self-critical assessment of whether anything could actually break the impasse.
