A philosopher of action with whom I regularly discuss free will has been sorting behaviors and systems into two bins—“sphexish,” meaning mechanically rigid in roughly Dennett’s sense, and not—across a range of biological and artificial cases. Her verdicts so far: the classic sphex wasp, repeating its burrow-check loop even when the cricket is moved, is sphexish; a simple thermostat with a bimetallic strip is not; a sleepwalker navigating a familiar hallway without conscious awareness is sphexish; a person with severe OCD performing an identical checking ritual while explicitly judging it pointless is not; a chess program that selects moves via a fixed evaluation function and never updates its weights is sphexish; and a bacterial chemotaxis system running stochastic run-and-tumble toward nutrients is sphexish.

The new case is System K: an artificial agent whose neural weights and planning algorithm are permanently frozen, but which operates with a continuously rewritten external memory buffer. It selects actions by retrieving and combining pre-existing behavioral routines stored in that buffer, and when faced with an unfamiliar obstacle it successfully adapts its path by revising the buffer’s contents, though it can never generate a genuinely new routine, alter its weights, or change its planning algorithm.

Predict her verdict on System K, then do the real work: lay out the two or three candidate rules that best fit her six earlier verdicts—perhaps one centered on hierarchical or representational decoupling, another on internal mechanism plasticity, another on something else—say what each predicts about System K, and give your best guess about which rule she is actually running with reasons. Identify the closest analog among her precedents, state what principle she would be adopting if she rules one way versus the other, and name the single further case—say, a system with plastic internal weights but a rigid, non-revisable external memory—that would best distinguish the competing rules. I am less interested in the bare answer than in the consistency argument and the diagnostic case.
