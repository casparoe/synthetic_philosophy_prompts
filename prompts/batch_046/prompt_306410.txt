I'm mapping out my commitments on satisficing and bounded rationality for a workshop paper, and I want an audit of whether the package actually hangs together before I defend it. Here is where I stand:

(1) I take Simon's satisficing model seriously as a descriptive account: people search through options sequentially and stop at the first one that clears an aspiration level, not out of laziness but because global comparison is cognitively infeasible.

(2) I think bounded rationality is normatively binding, not merely descriptive: given our actual cognitive architecture, attempting full-blown optimization is rationally defective in something like the way running incompatible software is defective, not just difficult.

(3) I hold that aspiration levels are adaptively calibrated: they ratchet upward after success and downward after failure, so "good enough" is path-dependent and lacks any fixed cross-contextual standard.

(4) I think that in high-stakes, irreversible, time-sensitive domains—emergency triage, aircraft failure protocols, certain surgical decisions—we should override satisficing and mandate exhaustive, algorithmically aided optimization, because the stakes justify bearing the cognitive cost.

(5) I believe rational decision procedures should be framing-invariant: if two presentations contain exactly the same options with the same probabilities and outcomes, rationality should not depend on which option is listed or considered first.

(6) At the normative level, I take satisficing to collapse into constrained expected-utility maximization once information costs, memory limits, and computation time are properly internalized into the objective function; there is no irreducible procedural difference, only a budget constraint.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which ones only look tense but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real conflicts by severity. For each genuine tension, specify what has to give and what the cheapest revision would be. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most overall tension, and whether making that revision forces me to abandon Simon's core insight or just to reframe it.
