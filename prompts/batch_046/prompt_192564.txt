To do so, invent and apply the concept of "second-order nomic closure" — a structure in which the rule granting amendment-power is itself amendable only through its own procedure, with no separate meta-level sitting above it.

Design a realistic institutional scenario, in one paragraph and without science-fictional devices (no omniscient courts, no logical computers, no perfect predictors), in which a legislature amends the very constitutional provision that authorises amendments, using only the procedure specified in that provision. Engineer the case so that: (i) an account treating legal validity as fixed by an ultimate rule of recognition (in the manner of Hart) must declare the amendment invalid; (ii) an account treating validity as an uninterrupted chain anchored in a basic norm (in the manner of Kelsen) must declare it valid.
