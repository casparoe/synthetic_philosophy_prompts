Exam question:
Consider the following scenario. A marine biologist from a Pacific island nation documents multi-decadal coral-bleaching cycles through an oral tradition that encodes chronological sequence via kinship metaphors rather than Gregorian calendar years. When she submits these findings to an international conservation board, reviewers reject the submission as "anecdotal narrative" and demand satellite-derived quantitative datasets. The board notes that her community's knowledge system lacks "the standardized metrics required for peer-reviewed assessment."

Using Miranda Fricker's distinction between testimonial and hermeneutical injustice, analyze the case. Identify which form or forms are present, specify the mechanism of credibility or comprehension failure, explain the nature of the harm inflicted, and address whether the wrong is exclusively epistemic (for example, a loss or misdistribution of knowledge) or also involves a distinct moral injury.

Student answer:
This case clearly exhibits testimonial injustice. The board assigns the biologist a credibility deficit rooted in prejudice against indigenous knowledge practices, treating her testimony as unreliable because of her cultural origin rather than its actual content. This is a straightforward instance of identity-based credibility deflation.

The case also involves hermeneutical injustice, though in a less familiar direction. The board members themselves suffer from a hermeneutical gap: they lack the conceptual apparatus to decode kinship-based temporal mapping, which prevents them from interpreting her findings. Because the dominant scientific community has not developed shared interpretive resources for this form of ecological narration, a collective hermeneutical failure blocks understanding on the listeners' side.

Overall, the harm is primarily epistemic and distributive. The conservation project loses valuable longitudinal data, and knowledge fails to circulate across cultural boundaries efficiently. While the dismissal is certainly unfair, the core wrong is cognitive and organizational rather than a direct moral violation of the biologist's dignity or standing. On Fricker's view, the deepest damage is the deprivation of knowledge to the global community, not a personal affront to the speaker's capacity as a knower.
