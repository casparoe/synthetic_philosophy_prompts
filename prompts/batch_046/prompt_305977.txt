I defend a particular variant of illusionism about phenomenal consciousness that goes beyond the standard claim that phenomenal properties are misrepresented. On my view, the cognitive architecture underlying introspection possesses no representational target corresponding to subjective experience at all; instead, it operates as an uncalibrated confabulation mechanism that produces reports of consciousness in the absence of any internal feature capable of serving as the referent. The apparent immediacy of acquaintance with one's own mental life is structurally analogous to phantom-limb sensation, except that there is neither a missing limb nor any neural substrate that could function as the absent target—only a predictive model that receives no error-correction because there is literally nothing against which to correct it. Given this position, I want a map of its genuine exposure: what observations, arguments, formal results, or conceptual distinctions would count against it, and which of these should be treated as decisive versus merely awkward? In particular, which findings in predictive-processing neuroscience or interoceptive inference would undermine the claim that introspection lacks a representational target? Would evidence that introspective reports reliably track specific neural invariants defeat the view, or only force a revision of how those invariants are characterized? How should a defender respond to arguments that misrepresentation requires at least a candidate target, or that a system cannot confabulate an entire property-type it has never instantiated? Furthermore, has this variant insulated itself into unfalsifiability by defining phenomenal consciousness in such a way that no empirical result could challenge it—and if so, is that insulation a theoretical virtue or a damaging vice for a position of this kind? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely inconvenient, distinguishing what committed illusionists would themselves concede as damaging from what they would dismiss, and note any predictions this specific formulation makes that rival accounts—such as weak illusionism, representationalism, or direct-realist phenomenalism—do not share.
