For my graduate seminar on philosophy of mind and personal identity, please compare Daniel Dennett's conception of the self as a center of narrative gravity with the competing framework—advanced in recent philosophy of psychiatry and cognitive science—that treats the self as a predictive processing hierarchy whose coherence derives not from autobiographical storytelling but from the continuous minimization of prediction error across interoceptive and exteroceptive channels. Specifically, evaluate which account better accommodates severe dissociative conditions in which narrative continuity collapses yet some minimal form of self-reference persists. Your comparison should avoid bibliographic appeals and should treat the strongest objection to your preferred view with genuine weight, showing whether that objection forces us toward a sub-personal architecture or can be answered within a narrative model.
