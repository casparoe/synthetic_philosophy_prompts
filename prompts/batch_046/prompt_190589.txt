Consider the following examination question and a student’s response.

Question: In Wittgenstein’s On Certainty, certain propositions are described as “hinges” around which empirical inquiry turns. Can a hinge proposition ever be meaningfully doubted within the language-game it makes possible? In answering, distinguish hinges from ordinary empirical claims and make use of the riverbed metaphor.

Student answer: Wittgenstein holds that hinges — such as “the Earth exists” or “I have hands” — are propositions we treat as absolutely certain because they are backed by overwhelming empirical evidence. Unlike ordinary beliefs, they serve as foundations for knowledge, but they are still empirical in character, merely more secure. The riverbed image illustrates this: hinges form the riverbed, while ordinary beliefs flow like water. Nevertheless, the riverbed can gradually shift if enough water erodes it, which means hinges are in principle revisable should sufficient counter-evidence accumulate. Furthermore, Wittgenstein regards hinges as a form of synthetic a priori knowledge in the Kantian sense, since they are not derived from experience yet render experience intelligible.

Please assign a letter grade (A–F) and provide point-by-point comments that justify it. Assess philosophical accuracy, the use of the riverbed distinction, and the treatment of doubt, without stating your own agreement or disagreement with the position.
