i’m drafting a scene for my second novel where a human-rights lawyer reads a seestina aloud to a survivor who’s tired of the word genocide being stretched thin. she wrote the poem herself because she thinks the form’s repetition mirrors the law repeating the same exclusions. the six end-words have to be culture, politics, intent, group, destruction, and protection — in that order for stanza one — and the poem should turn on the fact that Lemkin’s draft included culture and politics but the 1948 Convention left them out and added the intent clause that makes prosecution almost impossible. i want the envoy to keep only the words that survived negotiation, so the form literally does the politics. don’t turn it into a sermon or a brief; make it something she’d actually read in a room where people are crying. no footnotes, no bibliography, just the poem.
