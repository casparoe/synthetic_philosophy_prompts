You serve as a senior protocol reviewer for the Tranquil Care Accreditation Board, a regulatory body that approves welfare interventions in long-term residential hospice care according to an explicit tranquilist charter: the only intrinsic welfare goods are the absence of suffering (phenomenal distress) and the absence of craving (motivational tension toward an object or state); pleasure, social engagement, cognitive stimulation, and achievement carry no intrinsic welfare weight, and count only instrumentally when they demonstrably reduce suffering or craving. Your reviewers are clinical staff with no philosophical training; each proposal receives thirty minutes of evaluation, must be resolved on a standardized sign-off form, and must not require literature consultation or external citation. Design a reusable decision procedure—ordered questions, conditions for a sufficient answer, explicit stop-rules, and clear escalation points to human judgment—that translates the charter into steps a reviewer can apply repeatedly. The procedure must operate on two separate registers (suffering-cessation and craving-cessation), must distinguish structural absence (the capacity or conditions for craving or suffering are removed) from occasional absence (the state is temporarily absent but its conditions remain intact), must treat temporary suffering or temporary craving as costs that can be offset only by permanent absence in the corresponding register, and must flag hidden trade-offs where an intervention produces pleasure or engagement that generates secondary craving or suffering. Then apply the procedure step-by-step to three proposals: (1) a pharmacological protocol that suppresses anticipatory craving in patients with advanced dementia, producing sustained emotional flatness without altering the underlying needs that generate the craving; (2) a group music-therapy program that produces reported moments of joy during sessions but also generates frustration and comparative craving when the sessions end and residents return to isolation; (3) a neurosurgical intervention that permanently eliminates chronic neuropathic suffering but requires a six-week recovery period with intense temporary suffering, and that removes the neural substrate for future craving altogether rather than merely satisfying it. For each case, record the register-by-register evaluation, the final form outcome (approve, reject, or escalate), and any point where factual determination ends and value judgment begins. Include at least one case in which the procedure yields a recommendation that clinical staff are likely to resist on intuitive grounds. Conclude by stating precisely what comparative judgments the procedure is not equipped to make—such as cross-personal trade-offs between suffering-reduction and craving-reduction, or the aggregation of welfare changes across a population—and explain why these limitations are built into the reviewer’s authority rather than being remediable by further procedural refinement.
