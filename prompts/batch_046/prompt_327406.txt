I'm working on a specification for what any adequate account of scientific verisimilitude—the idea that false theories can still stand closer to the truth than other false ones—actually has to deliver, especially once we leave behind toy cases where successive theories share a common ontology and vocabulary. Before comparing named proposals, I want the criteria explicit and separated from camp loyalty. Write the full desiderata list for a theory of verisimilitude, giving the motivating case or formal argument behind each requirement. The list must cover: (1) comparison across theories with genuinely disjoint ontologies, where one denies entities the other posits, without letting predictive accuracy alone do all the work; (2) the distinction between being closer to the truth about underlying mechanisms or causal structures versus being closer about observable regularities, and whether one metric can honor both without collapse; (3) formal constraints that block pathological rankings—specifically, that a tautology or contradiction should not come out nearer to the truth than a substantive false theory with real explanatory power; (4) navigation of well-known formal objections to quantitative content-distance measures, without requiring bibliographic citations or named papers; and (5) what the account implies for partial truth in conceptual-revision cases, where improvement comes from changing categories rather than incremental correction. For each item, mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular realist camp. Then identify any pair of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable, with the reasoning. Briefly score Popper's original program, a structural-realist implicit account, and a predictive-accuracy-only view against your list—but keep the weight on the criteria, not the scorecard. Finally, state how confident you are in the framework as a whole, and single out the specific point—likely either the mechanism-versus-regularity distinction or your joint-unsatisfiability claim—about which you are least sure.
