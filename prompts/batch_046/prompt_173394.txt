Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on the authority of advance directives during severe cognitive decline.

Camp One holds that an advance directive retains binding authority over the later patient because the earlier self’s values constitute a diachronic identity that the severely impaired or demented later self cannot legitimately repudiate; they treat the directive as expressing persistent narrative authority rather than a revocable preference.

Camp Two holds that the later, experiencing self possesses exclusive present-tense moral standing, and that the directive is best understood as a defeasible instrument of past preference—more like a delegated instruction that lapses when the delegator’s current interests, especially experiential welfare, diverge radically from the anticipated scenario.

Produce the document both sides could sign, structured as follows.

1. Agreed claims: List at least four specific propositions both camps accept—about the nature of cognitive transformation, the purpose of advance directives, the limits of informed consent under psychological change, and what empirical observation of patient experience in severe dementia establishes—without citing studies by author or date, but framing the shared factual baseline with precision.

2. Precise disagreement: State exactly where they part ways, using wording each camp would accept for its own position. Do not reduce the dispute to autonomy versus welfare; instead, isolate whether the disagreement concerns the metaphysics of personal identity over time, the normative structure of authority (testamentary versus contractual analogies), or the weight of counterfactual preferences. Give the formulation each side endorses.

3. Cruxes: Identify two or three sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. For each crux, specify what kind of argument or evidence—conceptual, phenomenological, or institutional—would bear on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts that evidence would show.

4. Verbal versus genuine disagreement: Assess whether part of the dispute is about words—for example, whether autonomy is being used to mean narrative continuity or present capacity for choice—and if so, say which part and propose a disambiguated vocabulary both could adopt.

5. Residual test case: Present a single, finely specified hypothetical case involving a patient who drafted a refusal of artificial nutrition, then developed a condition causing cyclical lucidity in which she affirms her earlier refusal during lucid intervals and expresses serene contentment with feeding during impaired intervals, with family members split between honoring the lucid statements and protecting the serene state. State what each camp predicts should be done, and on what grounds.

Write in formal, polished prose. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote any real text verbatim; paraphrase without quotation marks if referring to philosophical positions. Ensure the prompt does not depend on knowledge of obscure named theories or scholars beyond standard philosophical vocabulary, and inject the novel cyclical-lucidity element precisely so the reasoning cannot be regurgitated from standard textbook examples.
