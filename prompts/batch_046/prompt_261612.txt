Write a dialogue between someone defending legislative sortition and someone defending strict term limits. They should argue about whether either mechanism actually stops careerism or just reshuffles the same profesional class faster. The sortition advocate needs to claim that random selection breaks the party and media selection filter entirely, so you don't get careerists at all, though they should concede this introduces structural amateurism. The term-limits advocate should reply that the filter stays intact since parties still recruit and money still picks winners, rotation just accelerates turnover and deepens dependancy on permanent staff and lobbyists. Have them actually respond point by point: does breaking re-election incentives kill the pipeline, or does it just push ambition into staff and consulting roles? Is institutional memory a public good or a private capture mechanism? Make both sides sharp and equally competent, no strawmen, no slogans. This should feel like a real disagreement, not a debate-club script. Also feel free to be casual; loose grammar is fine, maybe even a typo or two.
