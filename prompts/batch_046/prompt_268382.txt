1. Carroll's point: if you need A and B→Z as premises, you also need (A and B→Z) → Z, then another rule for that, forever.
2. The standard escape is that inference rules arent premises - theyre not beliefs you hold, theyre norms that govern how beliefs update.
3. But if MP isnt a belief, then using it is an act or mental procedure. So believing Z isnt just having premises A and B→Z; its performing the procedure of applying MP to them.
4. Now if someone asks why my belief in Z is justified, i have to justify the premises and also justify that i applied MP correctly.
5. To justify applying MP, i need a higher-order norm: "when premises of form A and B→Z are present, apply MP."
6. But applying that higher-order norm is itself an act, so i need a norm for applying that norm, and were back in an infinite regress, just one level up.
7. So either the chain of justification never ends (which finite minds cant do) or at some point i apply a norm with no further justification - a bare leap.
8. But if bare leaps are rationally okay, then i could leap straight to Z with no premises at all and call it deduction.
9. So either deduction is never fully justified, or its not distinguishable from arbitrary guessing.
10. Yet we clearly do rationally derive things like "all men are mortal, socrates is one, therefore he dies."
