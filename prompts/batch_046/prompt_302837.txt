As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun formal study in philosophy of mathematics, I find myself stuck on a tension that never arose in the laboratory: whether actual infinities can be more than heuristic fictions. In particular, I am trying to understand whether Hilbert’s hotel demonstrates the conceptual coherence of an actually infinite collection of physical occupants, or whether finitist objections become decisive once we move from pure set theory to physical instantiation. Consider a variant that troubles me. Standard presentations treat the shifting of guests from room n to room n+1 as an instantaneous, costless reassignment. But suppose we require that each reassignment be physically registered—say, by updating a state record subject to a finite energy budget and an information-density bound analogous to Bekenstein’s limit on the entropy of a finite region. If the hotel is actually infinite and fully occupied, accommodating a new infinite bus of guests seems to require either an infinite sequence of physical updates with no temporal endpoint, or an instantaneous global reconfiguration that violates local causality and finite information density. The finitist might argue that this is not merely an engineering limitation but reveals that an actually infinite occupancy state is physically unstable: any perturbation attempting to implement Cantor’s pairing function collapses because the indexing infrastructure itself demands resources that cannot be localized. On the other side, one might reply that Hilbert’s hotel is a logical model, not an engineering proposal; physical constraints are external to the mathematical structure, and Cantor’s demonstration that ℵ₀ can be placed in bijection with a proper subset remains intact regardless of thermodynamic cost. Yet I wonder whether this response concedes too much—if actual infinities are claimed to describe genuinely possible states of affairs, they ought not to be shielded from every physical constraint by definitional fiat.

Please write an essay that weighs these considerations and reaches a defended conclusion: does the physical indexing problem genuinely undermine the coherence of actual infinities in any domain where they might be instantiated, or does it merely confuse mathematical possibility with physical feasibility? In your analysis, address precisely what Cantor’s theory of transfinite cardinals establishes about the structure of infinite sets, and what Hilbert’s hotel genuinely illustrates versus what it silently presupposes about occupancy and identity over reassignment. Be explicit about whether you think finitism is vindicated, qualified, or rejected in light of this argument, and do not end on a non-committal note. Finally, please indicate what specific argument or empirical finding—perhaps concerning information theory in quantum gravity, or concerning the logical distinction between potential and actual infinity—would lead you to change your verdict, and identify clearly where your reasoning is most vulnerable to failure.
