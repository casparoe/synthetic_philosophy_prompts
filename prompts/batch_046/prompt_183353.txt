I have been developing a position in philosophy of action that I have not encountered articulated in precisely this form, and I want to understand how vulnerable it is to refutation. The view holds that free will should be analyzed not as causal indeterminacy or as a first-order capacity to choose among alternatives, but specifically as anti-sphexishness: the architectural ability to suspend, revise, or abort an ongoing behavioral loop when environmental feedback reveals that the loop is maladaptive, even though each internal step of the loop remains coherent on its own terms. I treat the classic digger-wasp example not as a simple proof that insects lack freedom, but as a diagnostic model of what freedom is not — not the absence of instinct, but the presence of a mechanism that can discard the governing script of an instinct rather than merely iterating it. In a recent conversation with friends, one suggested this is just compatibilism about rational revision with biological window dressing, but I think it differs in targeting meta-level loop-interruption rather than ordinary deliberation among options.

What would genuinely count against this view? I am not looking for the standard objections — for instance, that it collapses into compatibilism or that it neglects moral responsibility — but for the specific defeaters that a defender should take seriously. Which empirical findings, say from comparative cognition or the neuroscience of habit interruption, would undermine the claim that loop-abortion is the correct criterion for freedom? Is there a philosophical objection — perhaps that any loop-interrupting mechanism must itself be governed by a higher-order fixed routine, producing an infinite regress or reducing the view to nested sphexishness — that is fatal rather than merely awkward? Would it be decisive if humans turned out to be far more sphexish than we assume, with our apparent flexibility arising only from very deep but still rigid hierarchical scripts? Or could a committed defender absorb that result by locating freedom exclusively at the highest reachable level of the hierarchy? Please rank these potential defeaters from fatal to merely uncomfortable, distinguishing what a serious proponent of anti-sphexishness would concede as damaging from what they would dismiss as missing the point. I also want an honest assessment of whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability — for example, by defining loop-interruption so abstractly that any flexible behavior counts — and if so, whether that insulation is a theoretical virtue or a vice for an account of free will. Please provide your answer in American English.
