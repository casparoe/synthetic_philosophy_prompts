I am drafting questions for a podcast interview with a philosopher who defends a hybrid position she calls stakes-sensitive contextualism with a stability clause. According to her view, pragmatic encroachment governs whether "knows" applies only in contexts where the operative epistemic standards are unstable across nearby worlds; where standards are institutionally or conceptually stable—such as in medical or legal certification—the practical stakes do not shift the knowledge threshold, so a surgeon's casual study never counts as knowledge regardless of urgency. She uses this to resist both invariantism, which ignores stakes entirely, and pure contextualism, which allows standards to shift even within stable practices.

Write eight focused interview questions that press the actual mechanics of this view. Include at least two that ask how she distinguishes unstable from stable standards without circular reliance on prior knowledge attributions; one that probes whether the stability clause makes her view collapse into invariantism for institutional domains; and one follow-up anticipating her reply that stability is fixed by institutional certification rather than subjective confidence, asking whether that renders knowledge overly sociologically contingent. Avoid generic biographical questions; keep each question tied to the content of her position.
