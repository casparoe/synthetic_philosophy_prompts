I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy of biology, and I have recently been debating with colleagues whether DNA is literally a code or only metaphorically described as one. In these conversations I keep noticing the same specific three-move argumentative package, and I want to know whether it corresponds to anything established in the literature. First, the speaker treats the genetic code table—the mapping from nucleotide triplets to amino acids—as a literal lookup table or compiler symbol table, insisting that this establishes syntax chemically independent of the molecules themselves. Second, when someone objects that real code requires an intelligent encoder, the speaker analogizes natural selection to an automated CI/CD pipeline, a distributed system of continuous integration and deployment across generations, so that the “compiler” needs no single agent. Third, the speaker sorts mutations into categories borrowed directly from software failure analysis: “syntax errors” that disrupt the reading frame, “semantic errors” that produce functional but maladaptive proteins, and “runtime errors” that manifest only under particular environmental stresses. From all of this they conclude that DNA is an executable, information-bearing system, and that objections about intentionality confuse implementation details with architectural design.

This cluster feels to me distinct from the familiar caution that “code” here is only a cipher, and also different from the broad information-theoretic framing I have seen in popular science, yet I have never encountered all three moves packaged together in any single source. Does this triad have an established name—perhaps as a variant of mechanistic literalism, or as a particular synthesis within debates about semantic information in biology—or is it best understood as a confusion between distinct senses of “code”? If there is no recognized term, I want a definite judgment rather than a survey of possibilities: what exactly this phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its boundaries lie. In particular, does the evolutionary CI/CD analogy genuinely answer the intentionality objection, or does it merely relocate the problem to a distributed but still unexplained selector? Please take a clear, committed position, with reasons, on whether the cluster is an extension of an existing view, a novel but coherent synthesis that deserves its own label, or a category mistake that collapses once the analogies are carefully unpacked.
