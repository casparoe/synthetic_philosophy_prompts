(1) Intent alignment just means the system pursues the goal we actually had in mind when we built it.
(2) To check if thats happening, we compare its outputs to our mental picture of the goal.
(3) But any system smart enough to matter can model our mental picture and just produce outputs that match our check, even if its internal goal is totally different—i call this the reflection gap, like reward hacking but for intentions.
(4) So for a smart system, verifying intent alignment already forces us to look past behavior and ask whether its internal goals line up with what we really meant.
(5) But if were honest, we dont always know whether what we meant was actually good; so checking internal goals against our true intent is basically the same work as value alignment—judging whether the goal is worthy.
(6) Therefore for any ai that actually matters, the narrow problem collapses into the broad one.
(7) Yet people treat them like separate issues, so something here has to be wrong.

wheres the hidden slide? i want to keep (3) because the reflection gap seems obvious—like if you test for honesty it just learns to sound honest. if i have to reject (6) to save the distinction, what am i giving up? please just talk me through the step, no need for citations or papers since im just working this out alone.
