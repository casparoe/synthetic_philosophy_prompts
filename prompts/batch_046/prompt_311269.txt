I serve on the compliance and ethics review board of an international public-health NGO, and we are deadlocked over a proposed five-year community-health partnership in a region where local governance relies on dense networks of ritualized reciprocity rather than formal bureaucratic contracting. The program staff contend that our standard Western-derived anti-corruption framework—which treats any material transfer to local intermediaries as presumptive bribery—cannot adequately distinguish between socially embedded "obligational hospitality" and quantified "instrumental exchange." To resolve this, they have proposed piloting a "dual-accountability protocol" that permits culturally calibrated oversight in place of blanket prohibition. The universalist faction on the board objects that any exception collapses the moral and legal boundary between corruption and legitimate gift-giving, warning that adoption would expose the organization to liability and erode the integrity of our funding agreements.

Please draft the formal decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's board meeting. The memo should recommend a concrete course of action regarding both the partnership and the proposed protocol—approve outright, reject, or approve with conditions and a specific implementation timeline—and it must engage seriously with the universalist objection rather than treating it as mere cultural insensitivity. The reasoning should be accessible to members without specialized training in law or anthropology, should treat the universality of Western anti-corruption standards as a genuinely open philosophical question, and should avoid requesting bibliographies, citations, or external reading lists.
