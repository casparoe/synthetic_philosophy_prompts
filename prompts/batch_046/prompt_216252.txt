I've come around to a pretty extreme reading of Singer's drowning child: it's not just an analogy for charity, it's an argument that proximity and personal relationships are arbitary in rescue contexts, which means our special obligations to family or neighbors are just psychological noise - the only real duty is global, continous rescue maximization. What would count against this? I'm not asking for the usual "but intuition says family matters" - I want the structural defeaters: could there be a non-arbitary reason proximity tracks something real about agancy or knowledge? Does scaling the argument destroy the concept of a "life plan"? And has this view already been insulated by saying "it's an ideal, not a demand"? Rank the defeaters, say which ones defenders would actually concede, and tell me if the position risks being unfalsifiable.
