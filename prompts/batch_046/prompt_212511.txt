In my recent novel I have been constructing a mental architecture I call the careenium: a dense, gelatinous substrate in which small symbolic objects—simmballs—are suspended. The simmballs carry internal relational properties, yet they do not merely rest within the careenium; their symbolic interactions appear to induce direct physical displacements in the substrate itself, as though the logic of the symbols pushes the matter around rather than being passively instantiated by it. I have noticed an analogous pattern in philosophical treatments of mind and computation, but the familiar discussions either treat the substrate as inert hardware running symbolic software, or invoke downward causation in a broad metaphysical register. The configuration I wish to isolate is more specific: a loop in which the substrate is responsive precisely to the internal relational structure of the symbols it hosts, so that altering a symbolic relation—without any change in external input—still produces a measurable rearrangement of the careenium. Does this particular causal loop, or the theoretical failure mode that arises when one assumes it operates automatically, have an established name? If there is none, I would like a precise carving: how it differs from standard symbol-grounding puzzles, from supervenience-based arguments, and from ordinary feedback loops in dynamical systems, and whether its absence from the literature signals a genuine conceptual gap or merely a conflation with concepts that already cover it.
