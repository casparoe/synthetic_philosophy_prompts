My religion prof has been mapping Maimonides' take on sacrifice and she's come up with these calls. First, she says the Torah's animal offerings are a concession — not ideal, just accommodating an old human need for tangible ritual killing. Second, prayer after the Temple fell is closer to the real point, directing the heart without the blood. Third, she ok'd a modern synagogue burning herbs instead of incense, since the sensory part is kept and nothing dies. Fourth, she nixed a vegan group's idea to replace offerings with tree-planting, saying the concession only works if there's a real loss or viseral weight, not just charity. Fifth, she said killing an animal painlessly by machine still counts, because what's being offered is still genuinely valuable. Sixth, she rejected using lab-grown synthetic meat for the ritual, arguing that if the practitioner knows there's no real cost, the psychologial mechanisim collapses. Now there's a new case: a VR temple where people go through the full sacrificial liturgy with haptick feedback and synthetic blood, they feel the emotional weight, and they pay real money that funds animal welfare — but no creature is harmed. Predict her verdict, tell me if she's running a functional-form rule, a real-cost rule, or a historical-material rule, and name the one further case that'd split them.
