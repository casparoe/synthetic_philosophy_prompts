I was chatting with my flatmate about dreams last night and we got onto this weird pattern I've noticed in how people talk about their own minds. You know when you're in a noisy pub and suddenly you can hear your friend clearly, but if someone asks what you heard a second before, you sort of invent a continuous version rather than admitting there was just a blur? People treat it like there was always one correct 'inner recording' playing for a central audience, and the blur was just interference that got filtered out before reaching the real you sitting inside.

I've seen the same structure come up in philosophy seminars when people defend first-person authority: they assume there's a definative timeline of experience — first this happened, then I noticed it — with one authoratative observer watching from a single seat. But if mental processing is actually lots of competing drafts being edited in parallel, then that central-screen picture isn't just a simplification; it actively misleads us about how mistakes and revisions happen. What's strange is that people will defend the reliability of their report by pointing to this imagined inner theatre, even when the neuroscience suggests distributed, provisional versions.

So I'm wondering whether there's a precise term for this specific folk-psychological mistake — the combination of assuming a unified inner audience plus believing there's one canonical, edited record of mental events? I know Dennett argues consciousness is more like multiple drafts being rewritten, but I'm asking specifically about the intuitive 'theatre' model that makes people think there must be a final cut projected somewhere central. Does that exact configuration have its own established name, and how exactly does it clash with the idea that there is no single authoritative version of any experience? I don't just want the broad claim that the self is an illusion — I'm after the precise carving of the central-observer assumption and why it fails when you take the distributed-draft view seriously. Also, is there a specific term for the retrospective smoothing I mentioned with the pub conversation, or is that just part of the same package?
