I'm preparing notes for a philosophy of cognitive science reading group and want to check whether my position on the architecture of minds is internally coherent before I present it. I've been working with Dennett's four-level taxonomy—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures—but I've added some commitments of my own that may not sit well together. Here is my current view:

(1) I treat the four levels as marking genuinely distinct cognitive architectures, not just convenient points on a single continuum of complexity.
(2) I think the transition from Skinnerian to Popperian cognition is best understood as quantitative: it involves greater working-memory capacity and more efficient internal simulation, but no new kind of representational mechanism.
(3) I take Gregorian cognition to be categorically different from Popperian cognition, because language and cultural transmission enable explicit, communicable second-order representations that restructure the architecture itself.
(4) I believe phenomenal consciousness is present in Popperian and Gregorian minds but absent in Skinnerian and Darwinian ones, since consciousness requires the capacity to model one's own mental states.
(5) I accept evolutionary continuity arguments: if consciousness emerges gradually from Skinnerian cognition, there is no principled, non-arbitrary boundary separating conscious from non-conscious minds.
(6) I hold that the hard problem—the explanatory gap between neural processes and subjective experience—is only genuinely intelligible for Gregorian creatures, because articulating the gap requires the linguistic-conceptual framework that language provides.

Do these hang together? I can see possible friction between (1) and (2), between (4) and (5), and perhaps a hidden conflict involving (6). But I also suspect that (3) and (4) might only appear to clash, depending on whether second-order representation needs to be explicit or can remain implicit. Please audit the set: rank the tensions by severity, distinguish real contradictions from ones that dissolve on closer inspection, and for each serious tension identify the least costly revision and what I would have to give up elsewhere in my framework. Be specific about which commitments commit me to which, and do not treat every pairing as equally problematic.
