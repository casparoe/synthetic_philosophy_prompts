hey i sit on my university's Historical Names and Spatial Justice Committee and we're deadlocked over the Marston Corridor. it's the covered pedestrian walkway connecting the School of Public Health directly to the Philosophy and Ethics building, named after Dr. Elias Marston, a 1950s epidemiologist who led the regional smallpox eradication campaign but also directed psychiatric ward experiments on patients without capacity to consent. along the corridor are four bronze relief panels: two showing the vaccination campaigns, two showing the ward work. some members want full renaming and removal of the panels; others want to keep the name but replace the panels with critical-context plaques; my co-chair argues that because the corridor physically links the building that teaches Marston's public-health legacy to the building that teaches research ethics, it functions as what she's calling 'indexical commemoration' — it forces students to literally walk the contradiction between his good and his harm every day, and she thinks that embodied dissonance is pedagogically valuable rather than purely harmful. opponents say that's just forcing survivors and students to traverse harm daily, and that spatial connection doesn't excuse honoring a name. we need a memo for thursday's vote reccomending one concrete course — rename, keep with contextualization, a hybrid timeline, whatever — with specific actions and deadlines (e.g., by fall semester), and it needs to honestly deal with the strongest argument on the losing side. please take the indexical-commemoration idea seriously, not just generic statue-ethics talking points, and don't just restate university policy.
