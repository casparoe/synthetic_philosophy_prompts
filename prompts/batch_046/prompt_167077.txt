Construct a decision tree for positions on the underdetermination of scientific theory by evidence. The root commitment is: there exist at least two incompatible theories that are empirically equivalent with respect to all currently available data. From there, branch by concrete diagnostic questions a respondent could answer without knowing the philosophical labels—whether theoretical virtues such as simplicity or unification can legitimately break the tie; whether the equivalence is permanent in principle or only temporary given our instruments; whether the competing theories differ in ontology or only in computational architecture; and whether pragmatic or conventional factors may legitimately determine choice. Each leaf should name an actual position (e.g., Quinean global underdetermination, local structural realism, scientific instrumentalism, conventionalism about theory choice) and state in one sentence its most awkward commitment. Build the tree from the logical space first: list the branch questions and the combinations they allow, then locate the named positions within that space, noting any unoccupied combination and briefly diagnosing whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored—particularly the combination that accepts computational but rejects ontological underdetermination while also denying that theoretical virtues have epistemic force. Finally, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, especially instrumentalism and conventionalism.
