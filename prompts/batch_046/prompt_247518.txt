i'm drafting a novel about a town that tries to switch from shame-culture to guilt-culture, and one character is a philosophy teacher who walks into this argument. (1) if moral emotions are culturally malleable, then a society can deliberately choose guilt over shame. (2) guilt targets specific acts and keeps the self intact, shame targets the global self, so guilt is clearly the better emotion for autonomy. (3) but if a culture can choose its dominant emotion, then that choice is itself an act of self-reflection—the exact kind that guilt-cultures encourage and shame-cultures suppress. (4) therefore only guilt-cultures already have the capacity to make the switch, which means shame-cultures are permanently stuck. (5) that smells like determinism and also kind of insulting. where's the slip—probably between (3) and (4)—and what does fixing it cost? i'd really like to keep (2) if i can.
