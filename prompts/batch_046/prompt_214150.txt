people defending compulsory voting always compare it to jury duty -- you get summoned, you show up, you do your civic bit, no big deal. they use it to say mandatory voting isnt coercive, its just like serving on a jury. i want to know how far that holds, especially if we push it into the weeds: what happens to the analogy if the voting age drops to 16, or if we let long-term non-citizen residents cast ballots? can you give me the correspondence table -- voter as juror, election as trial, etc. -- then show where the mapping breaks, what the jury frame quietly imports about consent and citizenship that voting might lack, and whether the "its just jury duty" defense still stands once those disanalogies are on the table? also, is there a better analogy for mandatory civic participation that doesnt smuggle in all that baggage? answer in american english please, and dont worry about being too formal.
