I'm writing my final paper for an advanced epistemology seminar on knowledge and practical reason. We've covered the standard cases for pragmatic encroachment—high-stakes bank deposits, train schedules, and the like—and the usual contextualist machinery of speaker-sensitive standards for "knows." Rather than another literature review, I want a defended agenda: which open conceptual questions at the intersection of pragmatic encroachment and contextualism actually merit sustained work, and which are mostly dead ends?

Specifically, should a small research group prioritize (1) whether pragmatic encroachment gives independent motivation for contextualism or instead supports an invariantist view with practical modifiers; (2) how stakes-sensitive standards operate in institutional settings—say, scientific peer review or legal rules of evidence—where multiple contexts and practical pressures overlap; or (3) whether there is a unified account of knowledge that absorbs both context-shifting and practical pressure without multiplying senses? Rank these lines (or substitute others you think matter more), defend the ordering in terms of tractability, how many other debates depend on resolving them, and what downstream difference they make for designing evidential policies in science or law. Also tell me what kind of result over the next few years would move one of these up or down your list. I'm not asking for citations or a reading list—just the reasoning that fixes where limited philosophical effort should go if the goal is conceptual clarity in policy-relevant epistemology.
