I have been considering the arguments for restituting the Benin Bronzes, and I keep returning to a tension in the provenance of the materials themselves. The brass used in the plaques and figures was not locally mined; it arrived through European trade networks, often in the form of manillas that were melted down in the Oba's foundries. This seems to imply that the artifacts are materially hybrid products of both the Benin court and the colonial economies that supplied the metal. Given that, does the standard restitution claim — that these objects belong to a discrete indigenous culture from which they were violently separated — not rest on a false presupposition of material and cultural autochthony? If the physical substance of the bronzes is already entangled with the very exchange systems that facilitated the 1897 expedition, should we not treat restitution less as returning stolen property to its singular origin, and more as adjudicating competing claims over an artifact that was never fully 'outside' the colonial economy to begin with?
