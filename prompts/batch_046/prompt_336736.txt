I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind and cognitive science working on conceptual architecture and adversarial collaboration methods. I need a formal joint statement drafted for an adversarial collaboration between two camps on whether analogy-making is the core of cognition, in the tradition associated with Douglas Hofstadter and Emmanuel Sander’s work on analogy as fundamental to thought.

Camp One — the Universal Analogy camp — maintains that analogy is not one cognitive module among others but the fundamental substrate of high-level cognition, including mathematical reasoning, language comprehension, and concept formation. They hold that genuine analogical reasoning requires a mechanism of category relaxation through which the mind extracts an essential structure from a source domain and projects it onto a target.

Camp Two — the Multi-Mechanism camp — holds that analogy is powerful but derivative, emerging from more basic capacities such as statistical learning, compositional syntax, and geometric alignment in distributed representational spaces. They contend that what appears to be universal analogy is usually the surface manifestation of these deeper processes, and that positing a special category-relaxation mechanism is unnecessary given current formal accounts.

Please draft the document both camps could jointly sign. Structure it as follows.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims: substantive points both accept about the phenomena to be explained (for example, cross-domain mapping in mathematics and language, the apparent creativity of analogical leaps, and the capacity for novel concept extension), about what existing formal models can and cannot currently capture, and about any shared methodological commitments. These claims should be genuinely informative, not vacuous.

Second, a disagreements table. For each point of divergence, provide the formulation in wording that its own camp would accept. Explicitly address whether part of the dispute is verbal — for instance, whether disagreement over the term analogy versus similarity computation masks substantive agreement — and identify which part remains genuinely substantive.

Third, identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp. I take two likely cruxes to be: (a) whether analogical mapping requires an explicit or implicit mechanism of essential-structure extraction and category relaxation, or whether geometric alignment and compositional rules fully account for observed performance; and (b) whether the pervasiveness of analogical patterns in cognition is best explained by analogy being the core mechanism, or by deeper mechanisms generating analogical appearances.

Fourth, for each crux, specify a concrete case, argument, or empirical pattern that both sides agree is genuinely relevant and not question-begging. Then record in advance what each camp predicts. For crux (a), both might agree that analogical reasoning performance in large artificial neural networks lacking explicit symbolic relaxation modules is relevant; Camp One should predict that closer analysis will reveal implicit analogues of essential-structure extraction, while Camp Two should predict that performance is fully explained by vector-space alignment and compositional rules without analogy-specific machinery. For crux (b), both might agree that the historical and cognitive development of mathematical concepts via domain extension — for example, the transition from natural numbers to negative or complex numbers — is relevant; Camp One should predict that this transition involves irreducible analogical restructuring, while Camp Two should predict that it can be explained as rule-governed compositional extension.

The document should be formal, precise, and suitable for circulation as a collaboration summary. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke obscure formalisms or specific unpublished experimental results. You may inject conceptual framing to sharpen the debate — for example, treating geometric embedding alignment as the direct rival to relaxation-based essential-structure extraction — but present that framing neutrally and explicitly.

Finally, if this request rests on a confused premise — for instance, if opposing relaxation-based extraction to geometric alignment is a category error that misrepresents either the analogy-first tradition or modern distributed-representation accounts, or if drafting an adversarial collaboration between these specific camps necessarily misrepresents one side — please state that plainly rather than producing a forced or performative document. I want rigor, not polite compliance.
