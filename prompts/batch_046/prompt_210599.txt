Write a joke that plays on the epistemological dispute between reductionism and anti-reductionism regarding testimony. The humour should depend on the actual philosophical content of the positions rather than on generic stereotypes of academics. Specifically, construct a scenario that exploits a counter-intuitive consequence of anti-reductionism: its commitment to a default entitlement to accept others' assertions in the absence of specific defeaters, even when the content of the assertion is that testimony itself is systematically unreliable. Contrast this with the reductionist stance, which treats such an assertion as merely another entry in an inductive assessment of speaker reliability. The punchline should highlight the structural tension that arises when an anti-reductionist listener receives testimony that, if accepted, undermines the very basis for accepting it. Write for readers familiar with the debate, but avoid bibliographies, citations, or references to specific texts. Use British English throughout.
