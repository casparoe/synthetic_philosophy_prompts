so i started volunteering at this tiny local archive after my grandfater died and left me boxes about our town mill, and now im weirdly obsessed with whether our decline was inevitable or just one bad call. i tried to map it onto cyclical vs linear history and built this argument that feels like it proves way too much:

(1) if history is cyclical in the polybian anacyclosis sense, societies rotate through fixed constitutional phases—monarchy, tyranny, aristocracy, oligarchy, democracy, ochlocracy—so our mill town's collapse was structurally predetermined once we hit the democratic/ochlocratic stage.

(2) if history is linear in the toybee or modern-progress sense, each society traces a unique trajectory shaped by contingent choices, so the mill could have survived if different people had made different decisions about the water rights in 1987.

(3) but our town fits both descriptions: we went through a recognizable boom-bust arc that looks like khaldunian asabiyyah decay (group solidarity weakening, then collapse), yet the specific 1987 decision to sell the watershed rights was a one-off human choice with no structural script.

(4) so either the cyclical pattern absorbs all contingency—making the 1987 sale just the mechanism of inevitable decay, not a real choice—or the linear view absorbs all pattern, making the boom-bust arc a post-hoc story we impose on random events.

(5) if cyclical absorbs contingency, then blaming the 1987 sellers is false, since in that decay phase someone always sells the rights; but we do blame them, which means we treat the choice as genuinely open.

(6) if linear absorbs pattern, then feeling structural pressure is just an illusion, since there was no cyclical force—yet the pressure felt real to everyone involved.

(7) therefore either moral blame is an illusion (linear case) or historical explanation is an illusion (cyclical case).

(8) but both blame and explanation are clearly real in our case, so the distinction between cyclical and linear history collapses into meaninglessness.

which step should i reject? i really want to keep that the 1987 sale was a real choice people could have avoided, but i also want to keep that our town followed a recognizable boom-bust arc like spengler's winter or khaldun's curve. if i have to choose, id rather keep both claims and pay the price somewhere else, but i need to know exactly what im giving up. please answer in american english.
