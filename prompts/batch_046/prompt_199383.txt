I am preparing a single session for a reading group of graduate students in literature and science studies—readers who know Russell only as the logician and have not encountered his social philosophy. I want to teach them the conceptual architecture behind his critique of conventional sexual morality, specifically the separation he drew between the institutional-contractual functions of marriage (property transmission, inheritance legitimacy, social status) and the ethical-affective evaluation of sexual relationships themselves. The session should not become biography or advocacy; the goal is to make the conceptual separation intelligible and to show why he thought conflating the two produces a particular kind of moral hypocrisy.

Plan the explanatory arc: which idea must be in place before the separation can even be posed as a question rather than a provocation? Choose one carrying example—perhaps a comparison between the marriage contract as it functioned in Russell’s era and a modern cohabitation or partnership agreement—and specify what that example must include (e.g., the presence of economic and legitimacy clauses, the absence of state-enforced fidelity provisions) and what it must deliberately omit so it does not collapse into a legal history lesson. Then map the misconceptions this audience is likely to bring in sequence: that Russell was merely advocating promiscuity, that he ignored the welfare of children, that he treated all social institutions as disposable, and perhaps that logical analysis has no bearing on intimate life. For each, indicate at which point in the arc it should be headed off and how. Finally, propose a check for understanding that demonstrates they have grasped the structural separation—the ability to distinguish an institutional failure from a moral failure in a hypothetical case—rather than simply adopting a progressive stance. And state explicitly what should be left out at this level, such as the details of Russell’s personal marital history or the specific statutes he cited, so that the conceptual point is not buried in anecdote.
