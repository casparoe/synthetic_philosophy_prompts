I am a doctoral student in political theory working on early modern conceptions of contingency and agency. For a 90-minute graduate seminar with ten students, I need a structured classroom simulation—complete with assigned roles, explicit rules, and small but real stakes—that makes participants feel, before anyone names the theoretical framework, how Machiavelli’s concept of virtù can generate the very instability it claims to master.

The conceptual target is not the familiar “fortune as river” image but a more structurally unsettling claim: that the prince’s strategic opacity and preemptive restructuring of the political field do not merely respond to fortuna but actively co-constitute the contingency they appear to overcome. In other words, the more aggressively virtù operates to secure mastery, the more unpredictable the environment becomes, and the prince must then present that self-generated instability as an external enemy requiring further virtù.

Please design a runnable game or role-play with the following elements:

- A division of roles (for example, a “prince” with hidden resource allocations, “courtiers” operating under partial information, and contingency introduced through rule-based mechanisms rather than by a human player) and a payoff structure with tangible consequences—points that affect a shared outcome, restricted speaking rights, or a collective resource pool.
- A precise timeline across the 90 minutes, identifying the specific moment—before the facilitator introduces Machiavelli—when participants should experience the paradox that their strategic moves to control chance have measurably amplified it.
- Rules arranged so that the group must repeatedly choose between transparency and strategic opacity, with opacity producing quantifiable instability that then justifies further preemptive action by the prince.
- A debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto Machiavelli’s argument in The Prince, explains what will predictably go wrong when the activity is run (for instance, participants attributing all instability to the rule-set rather than to the prince’s own moves), and is frank about which feature of this interpretation—perhaps the reflexive, self-undermining dimension of sovereign mastery—the simulation can reproduce and which feature the facilitator must supply directly.
- Take a committed position: does this framing reveal a circularity that Machiavelli suppresses about princely power, or does it distort his intent by treating virtù as self-defeating rather than adaptively responsive? Defend your choice and show how it shaped the rules and payoffs.

The group should come away seeing that fortune and virtù are not simply opposed forces but structurally entangled in a way that makes mastery itself a source of contingency.
