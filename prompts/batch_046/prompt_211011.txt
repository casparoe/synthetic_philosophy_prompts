Rather than rehearsing the familiar claim that minds are strange loops, I wish to examine a more specific and, I think, under-explored variant: the reverse Gödelian hypothesis that phenomenal self-awareness is necessarily incomplete in its self-representation — that a mind, like a sufficiently expressive formal system, cannot contain a full and consistent model of its own representational state.

Please state your credence, between 0 and 1, that this reverse analogy identifies a genuine structural constraint on consciousness rather than serving merely as metaphor. Decompose your reasoning into four parts, each with a rough probability and a brief justification:

(a) That Gödel-style self-reference has a non-trivial structural analogue in neural or functional architectures of self-modelling.
(b) That phenomenal self-awareness actually involves the kind of representational loop susceptible to an incompleteness result.
(c) That biological minds display the predicted representational gap, rather than circumventing it through distributed, non-formal processes.
(d) That the strange-loop concept, if valid, denotes a necessary condition for consciousness rather than a contingent evolutionary feature.

Then combine these terms into your overall figure, indicate which component carries the greatest uncertainty, and describe the empirical or theoretical result that would most alter your number. I do not require references or a bibliography; I am interested only in the decomposition itself.
