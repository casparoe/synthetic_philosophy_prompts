Question: Explain Molinist middle knowledge and how it differs from natural and free knowledge. Then apply it to this twist: suppose physical laws are deterministic but mental causation is genuinely libertarian—does this make the grounding objection stronger, weaker, or irrelevant? And briefly discuss the freedom objection (not the grounding one).

Student answer:
Middle knowledge is the knowledge God has of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom—what any free creature would do in any situation. It's in the middle between natural knowledge (necessary truths and possibles) and free knowledge (what actually happens). By knowing these counterfactuals, God can weakly actualize worlds where people freely choose good without coercing them, since he just picks the right circumstances. The grounding objection asks what makes these counterfactuals true if the creatures don't exist in those other worlds. Molinists usually answer that they're grounded in the creature's individual essence, or sometimes in God's decree to create that essence. Actually, this connects to teh modal collapse problem—if God necessarily actualizes teh best world, then teh counterfactuals collapse into necessities, which makes grounding easier but freedom harder. The freedom objection says if God knows I'll do X in situation S, then I can't do otherwise, but that's wrong because knowledge isn't causal. Flint raised this objection using transfer principles to show that middle knowledge plus God's necessary knowledge creates a modal chain that eliminates libertarian freedom. So teh deterministic physics don't matter, because mental causation is already constrained by God's prior knowledge.
