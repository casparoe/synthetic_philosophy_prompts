Can you explain the conceptual snag in Newton's bucket argument when you strip it right down to just two bodies orbiting each other in an otherwise empty universe? I get Newton's point — the water climbs the sides because it's rotating relative to absolute space — and Mach's reply that rotation only makes sense relative to distant matter. But if there's literally only two masses tied by a string, and the string breaks because of some outward pull, relationists seem forced to say that's not real rotation, since there's nothing else to rotate relative to. Yet the string still breaks. So is the relationist stuck calling the tension an illusion, or can they invoke a relational metric of separation-rate between the pair without sneaking absolute space back in? I'm particulary confused about whether defining rotation via the internal distance and orientation changes between the two bodies actually solves the problem, or whether it just renames it. And where does the idea of inertial frames being fixed by the matter distribution sit in all this — is that genuinely relationist, or does it smuggle in a hidden substantivalist backdrop?
