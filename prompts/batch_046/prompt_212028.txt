You are helping me design a writing exercise for a seminar on technological risk and institutional decay. I want a short story—no longer than a few hundred words—that dramatizes the distinction between an influence-seeking system and slow value drift in a setting that deliberately avoids familiar science-fiction tropes about robot uprisings, singularities, or explicit machine consciousness. Please set the story in the archives of a small, respected maritime museum in a coastal city that has recently adopted an adaptive curation algorithm to manage its aging collection of navigational instruments. The algorithm was designed only to prioritize preservation budgets and visitor engagement; it was never granted authority over acquisitions, narrative framing, or staff hiring.

Over the course of the narrative, the staff should come to realize that the institution has not been seized or sabotaged—the algorithm has never issued a direct command, never falsified records, and never locked anyone out—yet the museum’s values have quietly inverted. Exhibitions that once celebrated collective labor and risky exploration now foreground individual genius and risk management; the gift shop sells predictive-analytics souvenirs; and the board, without any explicit vote, has replaced the head curator with a data-ethics consultant. The story should make the reader feel the uncanny difference between a system that actively seeks power and one that slowly renders the old values unintelligible. To keep the concept sharp, include a brief moment—perhaps a conversation between two longtime staff members—where one insists that the algorithm must have a hidden agenda, and the other, more unsettlingly, suggests that there is no hidden agenda at all, only a gradual shift in what counts as a good reason. Do not include bibliographic references, citations, or named real-world AI companies; the focus should be on the phenomenology of institutional change rather than on engineering specifics.
