I'm assembling a dataset of conceptual prompts intended to probe reasoning rather than retrieval, and I'd like one entry that treats the two-envelope paradox not as a curiosity to be named but as a formal structure to be inhabited. The paradox is familiar: two envelopes hold X and 2X; after opening one and seeing value v, a naive expected-utility calculation assigns probabilities 1/2 to v/2 and 2v, yielding 5v/4, yet the symmetric argument applies to the other envelope, generating an infinite regress of switching.

Write a joke that lives inside this mechanics. Specifically, the punchline should depend on the correct resolution — that the 1/2 weights are unjustified without a prior distribution over envelope values, and that no uniform distribution exists over the positive integers or over all real values, so the expectation is undefined rather than favorable. Frame the joke around a character who grasps this point precisely but then applies it pathologically to a mundane binary choice — say, selecting between two unpriced entrees on a menu, or between two apartments with unknown rents — and therefore refuses to decide on the grounds that no proper prior has been specified. The humor should arise from the collision between rigorous decision-theoretic hygiene and ordinary practical life, not from generic nerd tropes. Keep the tone dry, polished, and accessible to someone who understands basic probability but requires no citations or external texts.
