I'm trying to stabilize my position on whether culture can be a cause, taking Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis and the broader problem of explaining with values as my focal case. Before I present this in a philosophy of social science seminar, I want a consistency audit of my own commitments rather than a lecture on Weber. Here is where I stand:

(1) I read Weber's thesis as an argument about elective affinity rather than linear causation: Calvinist inner-worldly asceticism and the rational capitalist ethos shared a structural resonance that made their convergence likely without being mechanically determined.
(2) I think cultural values are genuine explanatory causes in social science, but only in a non-mechanistic, meaning-adequate register: they operate by framing the subjective meanings through which actors interpret and respond to material constraints.
(3) I reject methodological individualism: macro-level cultural patterns and institutional configurations can be explanatory in their own right and do not need reduction to aggregated individual beliefs.
(4) I judge Weber's specific empirical-historical claim to be largely disconfirmed—capitalist development was not uniquely or primarily confined to Protestant regions—but I defend the conceptual framework as still illuminating.
(5) I accept that explaining social action requires grasping its subjective meaning, and that this grasp necessarily involves the researcher's own value-laden interpretive standpoint.
(6) At the same time, I think this value-ladenness introduces a vicious circularity into cultural explanation: we invoke values (such as ascetic discipline) to explain institutional transformations, yet those values are themselves constituted and reproduced by the very institutions we are trying to explain.

My request is an audit, not a survey of options. Which commitments are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision to restore consistency? I have a specific suspicion that there is a hidden incompatibility between treating values as independent causes in (2) and accepting the circularity of value-interpretation in (6), and I wonder whether (3) actually conflicts with (5) because subjective meaning sounds individualistic. But I do not know which of these is the real fracture and which dissolves on inspection. Rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what exactly has to be given up and at what cost, identify any single revision that would resolve multiple tensions at once, and say clearly which commitment you think I should abandon if I want to keep the rest intact. Take a definite position, defend your ranking with reasons, and do not present the conflicts as equally weighted possibilities for me to choose among.
