As a doctoral researcher in political philosophy examining the conceptual architecture of ideological space, I am constructing an adversarial-collaboration exercise. Please draft the joint statement that a committed structural-cleavage theorist and a committed coalitional-artifact theorist could both sign, addressing whether the left-right distinction is real and what holds each side together.

The structural-cleavage camp holds that the left-right axis reflects a durable, cross-domain moral-psychological cleavage—broadly between hierarchical-traditional and egalitarian-universalist orientations—that organizes issue preferences and group identity independently of party machinery. The coalitional-artifact camp holds that left and right are locally contingent political technologies: family-resemblance categories whose coherence derives primarily from negative partisanship, institutional path dependence, and strategic resource competition rather than from any shared normative core.

Your deliverable should be formatted as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims—statements both camps can endorse without reservation, including about the historical variability of left-right coalitions, the empirical correlation of economic and social attitudes in specific national contexts, and the conceptual distinction between an axis and a cluster. Second, a disagreements table with four rows: (a) the ontological status of the axis; (b) the primary mechanism of intra-side cohesion; (c) whether the distinction survives the decoupling of economic and cultural issues in post-industrial democracies; and (d) the predictive weight of cross-national attitudinal surveys versus parliamentary voting records and coalition behavior. For each row, present the structural-cleavage formulation phrased so its proponents would accept the wording, and the coalitional-artifact formulation phrased so its proponents would accept the wording. Third, a cruxes subsection listing the sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would cause at least one camp to revise its core commitment. For each crux, specify the empirical case, comparative-historical pattern, or conceptual argument both sides agree is relevant, and record each side's explicit prediction about what that evidence would show.

Please inject one specific framing device: treat negative integration—the tendency for each side to be defined more by opposition to the other than by internal agreement—as either a surface epiphenomenon (for the structural camp) or as the fundamental generative mechanism (for the coalitional camp). Do not cite specific authors, texts, or datasets by name; do not request bibliographies or reading lists; and avoid invoking highly specialized formal models that a generalist model could not be expected to reproduce with confidence.
