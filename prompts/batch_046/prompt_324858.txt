Consider my verdicts on whether the following configurations generate a single, unified conscious experience, and identify the principle—if any—that explains the pattern.

1. A healthy, awake human brain with intact recurrent connectivity: clearly yes.
2. A purely feedforward artificial network that reproduces every behavioural output of a human visual cortex but lacks recurrent loops: clearly no, despite perfect functional equivalence.
3. Two awake human brains linked by a direct, high-bandwidth neural bridge allowing continuous bidirectional information flow: I still feel there are two separate conscious subjects, not one merged consciousness.
4. A patient after complete corpus callosotomy, with both hemispheres alert and responsive: I feel there are now two distinct conscious entities.
5. A digital computer running a perfect causal replica of a human brain's neuronal dynamics, implemented on discrete silicon chips distributed across a server room: I am inclined to say yes—there is one conscious subject—because the internal causal powers and integration seem preserved, regardless of substrate or spatial distribution.

The tension lies between case 3 and case 5. In case 3, intense causal coupling across brains does not yield unity; in case 5, physical distribution and a non-biological substrate do not prevent it. Is there a coherent principle—perhaps involving maximally irreducible cause-effect structures and an exclusion boundary—that saves all five verdicts? Or must one be abandoned?

Please also apply your principle to:
(a) a brain whose inter-hemispheric connections are temporarily and reversibly suppressed by a targeted pharmacological agent, then fully restored;
(b) a spatially distributed swarm of small robots arranged so that their collective causal topology exactly mirrors that of a human brain, with no central processor.

Present your answer as a JSON object with these fields:
- "principle_statement": a precise statement of the principle, or "none is coherent"
- "case_motivations": an array of objects, each with "case_number", "clause_justified", and "brief_explanation"
- "new_case_verdicts": an object with keys "a_reversible_suppression" and "b_robot_swarm", stating the principle's verdict and reasoning
- "tension_analysis": an explanation of how the principle handles cases 3 and 4 together, or which of the original verdicts it forces me to drop
- "abandoned_verdicts": a list of case numbers whose original verdict must be revised

Write in British English.
