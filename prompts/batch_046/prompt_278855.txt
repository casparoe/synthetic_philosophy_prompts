I'm a retired physicist who recnetly got into philophy of science, and I'm trying to sort out my gut feelings about whether history is locked in — like, if you changed one tiny thing early on, would the big outcomes still arrive? Here are my verdicts on some cases, and they mostly hang together but there's one pair that really bugs me.

Case 1: Two weather simulations with the same equations but initial temperatures rounded differently by 0.001 degrees. After a month they predict totally different storms. Verdict: the specific trajectory is not locked in at all.

Case 2: Earth's long-term climate stats — mean temperature, storm frequency — stay inside the same range for millenia despite those tiny perturbations. Verdict: the macro-regime is locked in, even if the daily path isn't.

Case 3: Replay evolution from the Cambrian with one mutation changed. I think you'd still eventually get flight and vision (convergence on function), but not humans, and maybe not even vertebrates. Verdict: functional outcomes are somewhat locked, specific lineages are not.

Case 4: A deterministic economic model with different random seeds gives different company names and stock prices, but the same boom-bust shape and inequality curve. Verdict: statistical structure is path-independent, the entities are path-dependent.

Case 5 (the tension): Chaotic systems have strange attractors whose topology is structurally stable — small parameter tweaks don't change the attractor's shape. So if long-run stats are fixed by that attractor, isn't the macro-outcome locked in after all? But I also want to say evolutionary history isn't locked in, even though ecosystems are deterministic dynamical systems with niches acting like attractors. Is the difference that biological parameters keep coevolving, breaking structural stability?

Can you state one principle (or a small family) that captures cases 1 through 4, tell me what it predicts for evolutionary convergence versus climate, and be honest about which of my verdicts I'd have to drop to keep things simple? Also please predict what it says for a case I haven't listed — like replaying technological history, would computers still emerge? — and flag where your reasoning is shakiest and what kind of empirical finding about real biological or physical systems would actually change your mind.
