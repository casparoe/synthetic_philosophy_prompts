I'm trying to work out whether rural and working-class values are genuinely more defensible than urban professional ones, or if I'm just being sentimental. Here are my gut verdicts. A farmer who spends decades learning every quirk of his land and refuses to sell to developers: admirable. A consultant who spends decades optimising her career trajectory with the same intensity: less admirable, even though both are "mastering" something. A village that keeps a failing pub open through collective effort: good. A professional network that keeps weak ties warm purely for career leverage: somehow less good, though the social structure is almost identical. Teaching a child "don't get above your station": questionable. Teaching them "you can be anything": questionable too, but in a different direction. The case that breaks me is a rural town rejecting a factory that would bring jobs because it would change who lives there — I want to call that defensible, yet when an urban neighbourhood blocks affordable housing for "character" I think it's selfish. Is there a coherent principel — maybe about treating places and people as ends rather than instruments, or about maintenance versus optimisation — that saves the good verdicts without excusing the bad ones? If not, which of my calls should I drop? Please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who study this, and don't present a contested moral outlook as if it's the consensus.
