Imagine that G.E.M. Anscombe lived to see lethal autonomous weapons governed by predictive-targeting algorithms, where a commander sets statistical rules and the system selects individual victims without further approval, and that she had just read an unpublished essay arguing that this creates distributed moral agency—so that no single person directly intends any particular death, rendering the direct/indirect killing distinction she used in her 1956 protest against Truman's honorary degree structurally inapplicable. She is asked specifically whether this architecture dissolves the moral parallel she drew between ordering atomic bombings and direct murder.

Please respond as exactly five numbered points, each one to two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) whether she would accept that distributing causal roles across operators, algorithms, and manufacturers eliminates the direct intention she attributed to Truman; (2) how she would compare the commander's statistical foreknowledge of civilian casualties to Truman's foreknowledge of the Hiroshima and Nagasaki bombings; (3) whether she would regard the algorithm's specific targeting decision as carrying the directed will essential for murder, or as a mechanized indirect effect; (4) her response to the claim that lacking individual target choice, the commander cannot direct harm at innocents in the morally relevant sense; and (5) whether she would judge these weapons morally equivalent to, worse than, or categorically different from the atomic bombings she condemned, with reasoning anchored in her actual distinction between directing harm at a person and allowing harm as a side-effect.
