In the literary salon I attend, debates about whether a protagonist can exercise genuine moral agency are routinely ended by someone invoking the old line that being determines consciousness, or that the economic base determines the superstructure, as though that alone proves every choice is merely epiphenomenal to material conditions. Before I build my next novel on such an assumption, I want to understand exactly what precise claims are compressed inside that one-liner. Could you lay out the distinct readings as a menu: a weak statistical correlation between class position and belief; a strong causal determinism; a functionalist thesis that ideology stabilizes production relations; a hermeneutic claim about the horizon of intelligibility within a given mode of life; and perhaps less obvious intermediate claims—say, about temporal lag between economic change and cultural form, or about domain-specific asymmetry in how material constraints filter into narrative structure? For each, indicate whether it is a platitude, a defensible but substantive thesis, or actually false, and tell me which versions would genuinely deny autonomy to a fictional character and which merely set plausibility conditions on certain choices. I need this disambiguation so I can bring it back to the workshop and ask which reading, if any, actually supports shutting down the argument.
