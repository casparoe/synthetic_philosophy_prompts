They care about conceptual coherence rather than code efficiency, and while they are familiar with standard debates in machine ethics, they have not encountered my specific framework.

My thesis is that production-grade AI decision systems should not be built around either pure utilitarian aggregation or pure deontological rule-following. Instead, I propose a layered architecture: a primary deontic veto layer filters actions by testing them in a counterfactual stakeholder-contract simulation—representing affected parties as virtual agents in a stable non-coercion procedure and computing whether they could rationally reject the action—before a bounded consequentialist optimizer evaluates welfare only within the surviving action space. I will present the technical logic briefly: the veto layer checks for rights-violations in counterfactual agreement rather than polling preferences, and the optimizer then maximizes expected welfare subject to that fixed constraint set.

Please prepare me for the question period. I need a prep sheet, not a polished defense speech. Specifically:
