I've been trying to formalize Kant's universalizability tests as variants of a single modal constraint: whether a maxim can be coherently willed as governing all rational agents without generating a structural instability at universal scale. On this reading, contradiction in conception occurs when universal adoption undermines the very practice the maxim exploits (as with false promising), while contradiction in will occurs when universal adoption conflicts with some necessary end of rational agency (as with neglecting one's talents). Both, then, seem to be stress-tests for whether the maxim is scalable without self-defeat. Given that framing, I'm struggling to see whether the distinction between the two contradictions is substantive or mainly expository. Specifically: is there a maxim that produces a contradiction in will but not in conception—where the instability is not that the practice collapses, but that I cannot rationally endorse the resulting world—without that second failure being reducible to a hidden contradiction in conception? And if the two tests always coincide or reduce to one another, does that undermine Kant's claim that they capture genuinely different failures of universalizability?
