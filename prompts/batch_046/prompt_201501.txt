Write a dialogue between two equally sharp people — call them Alex and Jordan — arguing over whether money is socially constructed all the way down or whether hard funtional constraints (scarcity, divisibility, portability) limit what can count as currency regardless of social agreement. Don't make either one a strawman; they should actually answer each other's moves, not just restate positions.

Here's the niche twist: have them debate whether predicting that money will retain value tomorrow is vulnerable to something like Goodman's grue problem. Alex should argue that "value-retention" is grue-like — it looks inductivley stable only because we haven't hit the hidden institutional cutoff where the convention collapses, so trusting fiat money is structurally shaky in the same way emeralds might turn out grue. Jordan should push back that functional constraints aren't predicate-shifting like grue; scarcity and portability answer to physical and coordination needs that stay constant across institutional frames, so the induction is anchored in reality, not just convention.

Keep the tone casual, like they're talking over drinks. Include a typo or two. No citations, no "what do philosophers say" framing — just make them work through the argument themselves. Don't resolve it cleanly; let the tension stay live.
