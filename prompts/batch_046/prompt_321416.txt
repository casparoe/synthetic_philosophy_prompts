write me a deadpan internal memo, around 600 words, from a made-up agency called the Bureau of Historical Vitality. they just rolled out a mandatory "Tri-Modal Vitality License" where every citizen has to register as either monumental, antiquarian, or critical — straight out of Nietzsche's second Untimely Meditation. keep the voice pure bureaucratic irony, like an HR email that accidentally reveals horror.

the rules for each track should exaggerate the real disadvantages Nietzsche actually warns about: monumental license-holders have to reenact grand heroic gestures they never performed and are banned from ordinary daily action because it "lacks world-historical scale"; antiquarian holders live in climate-controlled heritage zones where change is fined and they're required to preserve broken traditions they personally hate; critical holders must submit weekly Form 1099-H reports documenting traditions they've destroyed, even the ones they still need to stand on. include a sidebar titled "Workplace Safety / Life-Health Risks" that frames Nietzsche's warnings — rootlessness, mummification, delusion of grandeur — as OSHA-style hazards.

the satire should land the actual philosophical point: that each mode is necessary for life but becomes poisonous in excess, and that the bureau's attempt to administratively separate them just reproduces the damage in triplicate. don't name Nietzsche in the text unless it feels natural, and don't ask for citations or a reading list. inject one niche invented detail, like a "Critical Track Refund Policy" for citizens who destroy their own past and then request an antiquarian appeal. write it casual, maybe misspell "monumental" as "monumentel" somewhere, and make sure it's readable to someone who hasn't read the essay but sharp enough that someone who has recognizes every distortion is fair.
