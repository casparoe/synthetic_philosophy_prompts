I serve on my university's Committee on Distributive Equity in Student Services, and after three months of hearings I need to circulate a formal decision memo before Thursday's vote. The committee oversees the allocation of mental-health, housing-stabilization, and tutoring resources across the student body. Our current model is sharply tiered: students assessed as being in acute crisis receive intensive, multi-year stabilization packages, while others receive lighter, modular support. A student coalition has now proposed "Plan Uniform," which would eliminate the intensive tier entirely, convert all resources into a single standardized moderate package available to every student regardless of assessed need, and defend this on explicitly prioritarian grounds—arguing that prioritarian weight should be read non-comparatively, so that the worst off are still prioritized in absolute budgetary terms even though no one receives differentiated care, and that the comparative stigma produced by visible tiering itself harms the worst off by marking them as failures. Two faculty members on the committee counter that this is not prioritarianism but its opposite: a classic leveling-down maneuver that withdraws the greatest benefits from those in the worst position solely to erase a status disparity, leaving the worst off objectively worse without proportionally improving anyone else, and that any adequate prioritarian framework must preserve intensity of support for the worst off even at the cost of equality of visible status. I am genuinely uncertain whether the leveling-down objection applies with the same force when the "better-off" group whose benefits are withdrawn is not a distinct social class but rather the same student population at a different life-stage or across a different dimension of well-being, and whether non-comparative prioritarian weight can genuinely justify the elimination of specialized care rather than merely its redistribution.

Please draft the memo I will circulate: it should be formal, addressed to the full committee, and recommend one concrete course of action—approve Plan Uniform with a binding implementation timeline, reject it with an alternative proposal, or approve it only under specific conditions—within a defined time frame. The reasoning must engage the strongest version of the opposing view honestly: if you recommend approval, you must show why the leveling-down worry is either misapplied in this institutional context or outweighed by the stigma-reduction benefit; if you recommend rejection, you must explain why non-comparative prioritarianism fails to justify the withdrawal of differentiated support rather than merely its expansion. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading; the memo should stand on its own reasoning and be actionable by administrators who will not have access to philosophical literature during the vote.
