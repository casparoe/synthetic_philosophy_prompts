ok so I'm writing up my commitments on population ethics for a semenar and I'm genuinely not sure if they cohere. I'm in this course on future generations and I keep going back and forth. Here's roughly where I stand:

(1) I buy Parfit's nonidentity problem pretty strongly — if a kid's life is worth living, you haven't harmed them by conceiving them now rather than later, even if the circumstances are rough.

(2) At the same time, I think climate change obligations are real and direct, not just indirect via present people, which seems to push me toward some kind of non-person-affecting principle.

(3) I reject the total view and the repugnant conclusion feels wrong, but I also don't like average utilitarianism because it lets us prefer extinction to a huge population of barely-happy people. So I'm kind of a critical-level utilitarian, I guess.

(4) I also have this half-baked idea I call "counterfactual standing" — future people don't exist now, but they have a kind of moral claim if there's a close possible world where they do exist with decent welfare, and our actions close off that world. So I want to use that to bridge (1) and (2).

(5) And I think evolutionary debunking undermines our intuitions in the 14-year-old case but not in climate cases, because climate reasoning is instutional and abstract.

Do these hang together? Specifically, is "counterfactual standing" just smuggling in person-affecting harm through the back door, which would conflict with (1)? Does (3) plus (2) actually commit me to the total view after all? And is (5) just special pleading? Rank the tensions by severity and tell me what the cheapest revision is — I'd rather not drop (2) if I can help it, but I'm open to tweaking (4) or (5).
