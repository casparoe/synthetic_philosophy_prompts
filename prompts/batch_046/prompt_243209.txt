I've started thinking transformative-experience arguments are basically a red herring for rational choice. My position: if an experience genuinely transforms your values in ways you cant pre-experience, then pre-transformation rational consent is structurally blocked — not just hard, but conceptually impossible. So any model that builds 'idealized preferences' by asking what you'd want after full reflection is either conservative to the point of paralysis or secretly authoritarian, smuggling post-change values in under coherence. What'd actually count against this? Skip the standard parenthood/vampire rebuttals. I want defeaters hitting the structural claim: formal decision-theory results that allow rational preference revision across epistemic barriers, cases where agents authorize shifts they cant understand, or arguments that the 'impossibility' just confuses not-knowing with being-wrong. Rank them fatal to awkward. Which ones would defenders of this skeptical stance concede as real damage, vs dismiss as missing the point? And honestly, has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if so, is that a problem for a position of this kind?
