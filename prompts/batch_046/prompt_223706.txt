I am planning a seventy-five-minute graduate seminar on supertasks and Thomson's lamp for philosophy students who have encountered the paradox but lack formal training in analysis or set theory. I want a teaching plan, not an exposition. First, lay out the dependency order: which distinction—between determinacy at each finite stage and the absence of a terminal stage—must be established before the paradox is introduced, and why inverting that sequence undermines the point. Second, specify the single carrying example (the lamp itself or a structurally modified variant) and state precisely what logical features it must retain and what physical, modal, or causal distractions it must exclude to isolate the conceptual problem. Third, map the predictable misconceptions—especially the conflation of 'no last toggle' with 'the state is undefined' rather than 'the state is not determined by the toggle rule'—and indicate exactly where in the sequence each should be intercepted. Fourth, propose one diagnostic question that distinguishes understanding the gap between task-completion and state-at-limit from mere familiarity with the paradox. Fifth, declare openly what should be omitted here—continuity mechanics, modal realist apparatus, or ordinal arithmetic beyond ω—and justify the omission pedagogically. Keep the plan rigorous and concise; do not provide a bibliography or reading list.
