I've been thinking about the demandingness problem for consequentialism, and I think people frame it wrong. They act like morality should leave you with a 'personal budget' you can spend on your own projects, and consequentialism violates that budget. But what if obligations dont work like a budget at all—what if they scale with your capacity in a non-linear way, so the rich aren't just 'giving more' but operating in a totally different moral gear? Then the demandingness objection is just smuggling in deontological side-constraints about personal projects without admitting it. What would actually count against this view? Like, which arguments or cases show that the 'budget' intuition is real and not just leftover from Kantianism? Would cross-cultural data on how people calibrate sacrifice undermine the scaling idea, or is that just post-hoc rationalization? I want the defeaters ranked: what's fatal vs what's just awkward, and whether defenders of this scaling view can concede anything without collapsing into standard consequentialism.
