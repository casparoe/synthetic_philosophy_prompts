Your a philosopher with a fiction habit — think step by step and do a quick brainstorm of the scene beats before drafting anything. I want a short story, maybe 350-500 words, that dramatizes Bernard Williams's integrity objection and the idea he calls one thought too many, but set somewhere weird and specific so it doesn't feel like a textbook summary with plot attached. Let's use a deep-sea Europa repair crew: the protagonist, an engineer named Ryn, wears a decision-support rig that constantly computes best outcomes. When a pressure breach traps Ryn's long-time crewmate in one compartment and two other divers in another, the rig advises sealing the compartment to save the two strangers. The story should show Ryn disabling the rig and saving her crewmate, not because she found a hidden utility loophole, but because that commitment is part of her life-project. Capture the one-thought-too-many moment: if she saved her partner only after checking that the rig's weighting for emotional bonds made it permissible, that'd be the wrong reason; she does it because they're hers. Make it visceral — maybe the rig's calm voice vs her panic — and don't cite anyone or ask for readings, just tell the story. Start with the brainstorm, then write.
