I chair our university’s Joint Committee on Human Subjects Research and Interdisciplinary Science Funding. Before us is a proposal titled “Calibrated Epistemic Challenge: Tailored Evolutionary Narratives and Moral Allocation.” The investigators, working across moral psychology and philosophy, intend to recruit adults with stable, articulated convictions about distributive justice. Each experimental participant will first rate confidence in specific moral judgments; they will then receive a personalized evolutionary account explaining the likely adaptive origins of exactly those judgments—framed not as false but as tracking reproductive fitness rather than mind-independent moral truth. A control group receives structurally parallel evolutionary narratives about non-moral domains (aesthetic preference, mathematical intuition). All participants then complete a real-stakes charitable allocation task, and the study tracks both belief revision and behavioral change. The researchers argue this is necessary to test whether evolutionary debunking arguments carry practical force; critics on the committee contend that selectively undermining moral confidence—while leaving other normative domains intact—manufactures epistemic harm and violates our duty of non-maleficence, particularly since debriefing may not restore prior confidence. Half the board views this as standard cognitive-science deception with adequate safeguards; half sees it as crossing into the engineered production of moral skepticism. Please draft the committee’s decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—recommending a concrete course of action with a clear timeline for implementation or investigator response. The memo should treat the strongest version of the “manufactured skepticism” objection seriously rather than dismissing it, and it should explicitly address whether the asymmetry of targeting morality alone (while sparing other domains) is methodologically defensible or ethically suspicious. The recommendation must be actionable: specify any conditions that must be met, and state by what date the investigators must reply.
