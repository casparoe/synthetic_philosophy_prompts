After a career in materials physics I have begun reading philosophy of science, and my intuitions about dispositional properties feel sharper than my vocabulary for them. Here are five judgments. A ceramic vase permanently sealed inside a shock-absorbing polymer gel is still brittle; the gel masks manifestation but does not alter the vase. A copper wire with an internal micro-breaker that severs any current upon contact is not conductive; the breaker is a fink that removes the disposition. A gene carrying an irreversible synthetic methylation lock on its promoter is not disposed to express its protein; the lock is now part of its functional architecture, unlike the external gel. A photonic crystal that emits blue light, placed inside a filter that absorbs all blue wavelengths, retains its emission disposition; the filter is extraneous interference. A logic gate structurally capable of switching at 5V, but permanently wired to a 3V supply in its installed role, lacks the switching disposition; the supply constraint is constitutive of its context. Is there a unified principle—perhaps about whether the interfering condition belongs to the categorical base, to the manifestation conditions, or to the object’s design context—that captures all five, or must one be abandoned? Please respond as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: state the principle; identify which of my five verdicts it cannot save and why; predict whether a synthetic enzyme chemically intact but permanently bound in an inactive conformation by a synthetic chaperone retains its catalytic disposition; name the single distinction that does the heaviest lifting in your account; and say whether the fink-versus-mask distinction survives your analysis or collapses.
