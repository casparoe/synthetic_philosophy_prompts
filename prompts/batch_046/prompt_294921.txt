I am constructing a taxonomy for a philosophy seminar on anthropic reasoning. The topic is the 'presumptuous philosopher' scenario, in which a reasoner considers cosmological theories predicting different total numbers of observers and asks whether she should favour those predicting more. Rather than listing named positions, I want a decision tree built from three concrete diagnostic questions that sort people by what they actually believe.

First, the root question: is self-locating evidence ever legitimate input when choosing between cosmological theories? Second, for those who answer yes: does a theory's prediction of a larger total observer population increase your credence in that theory? Third, applied regardless of the second answer: suppose you discover you are one of 10^30 identically reasoning simulated minds running your argument in a computation cluster, with only ten biological originals; should this change your belief about whether you are biological?

Present the taxonomy as a numbered list of exactly five items, with no introduction or conclusion. Each item should be one or two sentences: the first stating the combination of answers that selects it, the second summarising its stance on the original presumptuous philosopher case and flagging its most awkward commitment. Within the item that covers the root-rejected path, note that the four combinations in which self-locating reasoning is denied but subsidiary answers are assigned are structurally collapsed rather than incoherent. Separate what is settled about the formal distinction between self-sampling and self-indication from what specialists genuinely dispute, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus. Write in British English, avoid bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and keep every claim answerable from reasoning alone.
