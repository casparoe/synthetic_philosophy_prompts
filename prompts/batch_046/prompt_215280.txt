Two friends of mine got stuck in an argument last night about free-floating rationales — can there be reasons for something to happen when there's literally no reasoner, no mind, no agent that holds them? Friend A said reasons are inherently relational: there's no reason for the tree to grow toward light unless there's a subject for whom that's a reason. Without a reasoner, its just causation with fancy language. Friend B said thats mentalism by fiat and pointed to evolutionary biology: we say "the reason the beak is long is nectar access" without anyone actually thinking it, and that explanation genuinely does normative work, not just causal work. Friend A came back that this is just teleonomy — we're borrowing mentalistic vocabulary for causal patterns, and the "reason" lives in our explanation, not in nature. Friend B then brought up institutional reasons: a corporation has reasons to merge even if no single employee holds that belief as a mental state; the rationale exists at the system level. Friend A said thats cheating — corporations are made of reasoners, so the reason still traces back to shareholders and boards, you're just hiding the agents. Friend B countered with distributed cognition and artificial systems: a neural network adjusts weights to minimize loss; there's a structural rationale for the adjustment, but no single node "believes" it. So if we take non-human systems seriously, rationales can genuinely float free of any individual reasoner. Friend A said thats just functional explanation, not justification; justification requires the capacity to recognize and endorse the reason, and without that you're describing mechanism, not normativity.

They wouldnt budge. Whos right about which specific point, and what are both missing? Please say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point your least sure about.
