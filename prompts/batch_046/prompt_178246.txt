I have come to regard the so-called alignment problem not as a single problem but as a triad of conceptually distinct failures—specification failure (the gap between an intended objective and its formalisation), distributional failure (the divergence between aggregate outcomes and the particular burdens imposed on affected groups), and temporal failure (the drift between the values embedded in present design and those relevant to future iterations or successor societies). I take it that treating these as manifestations of one underlying misalignment mechanism is a category mistake that obscures where different kinds of intervention are actually required. What would genuinely count against this disaggregation? I want an honest map of the defeaters: which conceptual links, empirical observations from deployed systems, or formal results in multi-objective optimisation would show that the three failures share a root cause demanding a unified response? Please rank them from decisive—where a unified account clearly outperforms the triad—to merely awkward complications that a disaggregationist could absorb with auxiliary adjustments. Finally, assess whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability: if every apparent connection between the failures can be redescribed as contingent correlation rather than structural unity, is that insulation a theoretical virtue or a damaging vice for a framework of this kind?
