Her verdicts so far: it's rational to keep a 60-year ecological monitoring station running even though satellites now cover the same ground, because the continuous record is irreplaceable. It's irrational to finish rewriting a textbook chapter you've mostly scrapped, just because you already spent months on it. It's rational to maintain an obsolete telescope that now serves mainly as a teaching museum, since it embodies the department's history and was donated specifically for preservation. But it's irrational for a surgeon to keep using a technique she spent 15 years mastering, now that evidence shows a newer method is better — the years of practice don't justify it.

The new case: my old university digitised the complete handwritten notebooks of a defunct research institute and stored them safely. There's a proposal to keep funding staff to maintain the physical paper and run guided tours, costing about £100k a year. The digital copies contain every bit of data. But the original funding agreement promised alumni that the archive would be permanently preserved and accessible. Predict Dr. Patel's verdict, say whether she's running a heritage-continuity rule, a promissory-residue rule, or something else, and tell me which further case would tell them apart. Please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and don't present a contested view as the consensus. Answer in British English, please.
