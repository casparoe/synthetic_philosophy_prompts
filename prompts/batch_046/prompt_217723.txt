I'm writing a science-and-ideas column about hyperlocal tool co-ops, and theres one near me falling apart in a way that feels like a hidden logic puzzle. Two neighbors—Mike and Dave—set up a shared workshop in Mike's garage: Mike bought the table saw and dust collector, Dave handles repairs and runs Saturday open hours. They both showed up religiously for a year, but now Dave's missing shifts while still borrowing the lathe on weeknights. Mike wants to change the lock code, but he told me he's torn between cutting Dave off now versus letting him slide and maybe encouraging more of it. Heres the wrinkle: Dave's mom got sick, so Mike might be seeing free-riding where theres just exhaustion—noise in the signal, basically. They live on the same block too, so they cant just avoid each other. What's the rational cooperative move here? Is locking him out actually smart, or is there a long-game response that doesn't collapse into back-and-forth punishment when you cant tell bad intent from bad luck?
