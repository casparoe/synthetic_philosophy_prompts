I'm the senior curriculum editor for a multinational history-textbook consortium, and our chapter authors—working historians without philosophical training—need a reusable editorial procedure they can run in about fifteen minutes to decide how to phrase attributions of responsibility for the outbreak of the First World War. Because our books circulate in jurisdictions with sharply different public narratives and legal frameworks around historical blame, we cannot default to any single national framing; instead, we need a step-by-step checklist grounded in both the Fischer controversy—whether Imperial Germany deliberately pursued expansionist war aims versus interpretations that distribute causation across alliance structures and Austro-Hungarian choices—and in the philosophical distinction between holding a collective agent responsible, holding distributed individual actors causally responsible, and holding institutional structures responsible. The deliverable should be a flowchart-in-words: ordered questions, what answer permits a specific attribution, where the procedure must halt with a neutral causal description and avoid collective moral blame, and where it hands the decision to a human editor because the issue is one of values rather than facts; it should also flag explicitly which steps are judgment calls disguised as rules. Then apply it to three hypothetical passages: one analyzing the Schlieffen Plan's offensive strategic logic; one describing the cascade of alliance obligations during the July Crisis; and one concluding paragraph that assigns primary moral blame to the German nation as a collective agent for seeking continental hegemony—a result I expect the procedure should reject, with a precise explanation of where and why it stops. Keep the entire response under 400 words; lead with the numbered procedure, follow with the three annotated worked cases, and be honest about where the checklist misfires or requires editorial discretion.
