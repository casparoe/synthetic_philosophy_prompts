Write a short story about a sound engineer in a dystopian archive where every piece of music is legally required to be a variation on one ancestral melody. She buys into Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model — the idea that all creativity is just tweaking paramters like tempo, timbre, harmonic density on a fixed theme. One day she finds a hidden dial she can't name properly; maybe it's "causal direction" or "moral weight." When she twists it, the melody starts anticipating the listener's choices, not just evoking feelings but almost arguing with them. Keep the story in her hands and her head — the physical feel of the console, the way she tests the new paramter by playing it back, her growing suspicion that she hasn't discovered a new kind of art but just broken the machine. Don't turn this into an AI story; the point is whether enough knob-twiddling on one theme can produce something that isn't just a variation but a genuinely different kind of thing, and whether she can tell the difference.
