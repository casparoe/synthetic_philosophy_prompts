okay so me and my friend were arguing last week about whether people can actually be "in denial" in a real literal way, not just saying it as a figure of speech. i came up with this chain of reasoning and now it feels like it destroys the whole idea, but the conclusion is too strong so i know i messed up somewhere.

1. if you're decieving yourself about X, then you have to know X is true but somehow make yourself not know it.
2. but knowing something and then "not knowing" it at the same time sounds like a straight contradiction - it's not like forgetting, because forgetting takes time and this is happening right now in the same head.
3. so the only way out is that one part of your mind knows X and another part doesn't, like two people living in one skull.
4. but if that's the case, then it's not really "you" decieving "yourself" - it's more like subsystem A fooling subsystem B, which is weird because then which one is the real you?
5. apply this to anything: my dad refuses to check his bank account even though he knows he overspent. either he genuinely doesn't know he's broke (just ignorance), or there's a "bank account part" of him that knows and a "spending part" that doesn't.
6. and if every case of denial is just ignorance, then all those self-help books about "facing your denial" are nonsense, since you can't face information you never had.
7. but if denial is real, then we have to accept that one mind can hold a contradiction, which seems like it should break logic for practical reasoning too.
8. so either self-deception is impossible and denial doesn't exist, or minds are split in a way that makes "I" not a single thing. both feel wrong.
9. where does this slip? i want to keep that knowing and not-knowing at the same time is weird, but i also want denial to be a real thing people do.
