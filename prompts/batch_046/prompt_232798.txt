The summary seemed to blend Dennett's classic wasp example with ideas from predictive processing in a way that made me uneasy, but I want to check my intuitions before I rely on it. The handout claimed that Dennett uses the sphex wasp—whose burrow-inspection routine is so rigid that moving the prey during the check causes it to restart the entire sequence—to show that free will requires consciousness because the wasp lacks any representational updating and is locked in deterministic loops. It then asserted that Dennett extends this to humans, arguing that most human habits are similarly deterministic "strong-prior" loops that differ only in complexity, and that genuine freedom requires neural events that break these priors through non-deterministic error-correction. From this, the handout concluded that neuroscience finds no such breaks, so Dennett's view actually undermines compatibilist free will rather than supporting it, and that sphexishness is essentially identical to what predictive-processing theorists call "locked prediction-error suppression." Before I cite this framing in my chapter, I need an accuracy audit: which parts are faithful to Dennett's actual compatibilism and his distinction between rigidity and responsiveness, what subtly misrepresents his claims about consciousness, determinism, and human flexibility, and does the predictive-processing identification follow from anything Dennett actually argued?
