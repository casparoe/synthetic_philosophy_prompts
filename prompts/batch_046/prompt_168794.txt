I'm trying to work out what I actually think about institutional quotas and preferential treatment, going by my gut on concrete policies rather than on abstract slogans. Here are my verdicts.

Norway's mandatory 40 percent gender quota for corporate boards: justified. Board seats are positional goods with strong network externalities, and voluntary measures had stagnated for decades without closing the gap.

India's reservation system for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes in public universities and civil-service posts: justified, understood as remedial compensation for historically entrenched caste-based exclusion. But the "creamy layer" income exclusion applied to Other Backward Classes strikes me as arbitrary: if the moral basis is group-based historical wrong, drawing a line at a specific family income undermines the logic without replacing it with an individual one.

US race-conscious holistic admissions at selective universities, as practiced before recent court rulings: not justified. I feel race functions here as a proxy for disadvantage that is both over-inclusive and under-inclusive, and I would prefer targeted class-based or geographic criteria that address the structural barrier directly.

A proposed municipal policy requiring that 20 percent of police hires come from census tracts with the highest rates of violent crime, with no reference to race or gender: justified. It creates direct representational accountability to the communities bearing the costs of policing, and it targets a geographically defined harm rather than a demographic category.

A hypothetical university preference for first-generation college students that excludes anyone whose parents immigrated after age 12: not justified. The immigration-age cutoff feels like a disguised ethnic filter with no independent moral weight; it neither tracks disadvantage consistently nor remedies a specific institutional wrong.

The hard pair I keep circling: I endorse group-based quotas in Norway and for SC/ST reservations, yet reject them in US university admissions and reject the immigration-cutoff preference. Is there a principle that saves all four? One candidate is that quotas are justified only when they remedy a specific, documented structural exclusion tied to the institution in question, not when they pursue demographic parity or use a proxy for it. But then I have to ask whether Norway's board quota survives: gender disparity in corporate leadership partly reflects different career preferences and life-course choices, not purely exclusionary barriers, so does the principle actually support the Norway case or force me to drop it? And if I save Norway by appealing to network effects and positional goods, does that open the door to justifying the US admissions case after all?
