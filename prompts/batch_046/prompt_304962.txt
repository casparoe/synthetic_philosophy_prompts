I have no formal philosophical education, yet I have spent considerable time thinking about Jean-Paul Sartre's claim that we possess a radical freedom which we habitually conceal from ourselves through bad faith. I should like you to write a short story that dramatises this idea, not through a familiar Parisian café scene, but via a concept I have been turning over: an "ontological overdraft", by which I mean the accumulated burden of choices one has refused to own, now mistaken for an unavoidable fate. Present the narrative in exactly five numbered scenes, each no longer than two sentences, and include neither an introduction nor a conclusion. Choose an ordinary, quietly oppressive setting, and show the protagonist gradually realising that their feeling of entrapment is itself the product of self-deception. Please write in British English.
