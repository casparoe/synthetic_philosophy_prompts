I'm putting together a story on Dennett's old "Quining Qualia" essay and a weird extension I heard at a seminar last week. The speaker said if you take the Chase-Sanborn gap seriously—where scientists study functional correlates but miss the "real" qualia—then you can build an argument that doesn't just kill qualia but kills intrinsic properties everywhere. Here it is, step by step.

(1) Qualia are defined as intrinsic, non-relational properties of experience—what it's like, not what it does.

(2) By the Chase-Sanborn point, any scientific or third-person description only captures functional correlates, never these intrinsic features.

(3) So qualia are in principle uncapturable by any relational or public description.

(4) If something is uncapturable in principle, then no intersubjective check on reports about it is possible.

(5) But we do correct people about mental states—"you're not in pain, you're just anxious"—so there must be intersubjective checks.

(6) Any intersubjective check must be relational or behavioral.

(7) Therefore qualia must be capturable relationally.

(8) That contradicts (2) and (3), so the concept of qualia is incoherent.

(9) Now run the same logic on physical properties: mass, charge, spin, etc., all have intrinsic aspects—intrinsic mass, bare numerical identity.

(10) Apply (3)-(7) to intrinsic mass: if it's uncapturable relationally, then reports about it are uncheckable, but physics corrects measurements constantly, so intrinsic mass must be relationally capturable.

(11) So the argument eliminates not just qualia but every intrinsic physical property, leaving only pure structural relations—radical ontic structural realism with no objects left.

The speaker said this shows Dennett's move proves too much: it doesn't just quine qualia, it quines the physical world. I think their's a slip somewhere between (4) and (6), maybe an equivocation between "uncapturable for science" and "uncapturable for correction," but I can't pin it. Which step do you reject, and what does giving it up cost? Does the standard Dennettian response—just deny there's anything there to miss—block the slide to physics, or does it need an extra move like phenomenal bookkeeping, a kind of private Sanborn ledger that allows public auditing without revealing the entry? And if you save physics by rejecting (9), do you accidentally save qualia too, or can you keep them dead while letting mass stay alive?
