I'm a university ombudsperson who advises on faculty-student disputes involving academic-integrity accusations. I need a reusable decision procedure—grounded in the philosophy of standing to blame and the hypocrisy condition—that I can apply in about fifteen minutes per case. It should have ordered tests to determine: (1) whether the accuser has standing to issue this specific blame; (2) whether hypocrisy is present, and whether it is consistency-hypocrisy (the same vice, regardless of scale) or proportionality-hypocrisy (the same vice but with a severity mismatch that distorts the blame); (3) whether the hypocrisy nullifies the blame, merely weakens it, or is irrelevant to its force; and (4) whether there is a separate epistemic-standing failure even when hypocrisy is absent. Fix the role's constraints: I have no punitive authority, I must explain reasoning to non-philosophers, and I cannot investigate full biographies. Flag every point where judgment is unavoidable and cannot be dressed up as mechanical. Apply the procedure to three worked cases: (a) a professor who plagiarized in graduate school now blaming a student for minor citation errors; (b) a professor who occasionally omits citations blaming a student for deliberate data fabrication; and (c) a professor whose conduct is identical in kind to the student's but occurred under severe external coercion the student does not face—this is the case where the procedure should yield an answer the asker will dislike. State explicitly what the procedure cannot settle, such as whether standing can be regained or whether proportionality failures ever fully excuse the accused. Keep the vocabulary accessible but preserve the distinction between standing being undermined by hypocrisy and the underlying wrongness of the accused's act remaining intact.
