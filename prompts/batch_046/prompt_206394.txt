As a retired biologist who has recently begun reading philosophy of action, I have formed the following views on mechanical rigidity and free will, but I am unsure whether they cohere. First, I take sphexishness—the wasp's repeated burrow-checking loop—to show that apparently intelligent behavior can be driven entirely by hard-wired release mechanisms without any representational content about the goal; calling it "rigid" already risks anthropomorphizing a system that never possessed flexibility to lose. Second, I think human bureaucratic or habitual action can become genuinely sphexish in the same structural sense: when procedural habits suppress error-correction, the agent's behavior is mechanistically coupled to environmental triggers in a way that bypasses second-order evaluation. Third, I hold that free will requires not mere behavioral flexibility but specifically the capacity for representational revision guided by reasons, which is an emergent property of individual cognition rather than a socially distributed feature. Fourth, I believe that if a person is locked in sphexish patterns—say, compulsive protocol-following that resists counterevidence—then pharmacologically or cognitively enhancing their representational flexibility should count as therapy, not enhancement, because it restores a baseline capacity for agency. Fifth, however, I also maintain that the therapy/enhancement distinction is normatively empty and should be abandoned in favor of a purely ecological assessment: we should evaluate interventions by whether they reduce sphexish rigidity across the agent-environment system, not by whether they restore an individual's "normal" functioning.

Please audit these commitments for tension. Which pairs genuinely conflict, which only appear to? Does my individualist account of free will sit well with my ecological evaluation criterion, or does it commit me to treating social rigidity as irrelevant to therapy? And does my classification of flexibility-enhancement as therapy depend on a normative baseline that I reject in my fifth commitment? Rank the tensions by severity, indicate the cheapest revision for each, and tell me which single change would clean up the most inconsistencies without abandoning my naturalist outlook.
