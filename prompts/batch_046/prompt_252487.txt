As a PhD student in philosophy of mind, I am drafting interview questions for a podcast colloquium with an unnamed philosopher who defends a specific variant of the gradual neuronal-replacement argument for mind uploading. Her view is that replacing biological neurons one at a time with functionally equivalent silicon analogues preserves both phenomenal consciousness and diachronic personal identity, but only if the replacement sustains what she terms cross-temporal binding coherence: a global, non-decomposable pattern of integrated information that links successive temporal stages of the system, rather than reducing continuity to pairwise causal links between adjacent slices. She routinely deflects worries about fading qualia or subjective discontinuity by insisting these are empirical implementation issues, not conceptual objections to identity or consciousness preservation.

Write a focused sequence of six to eight questions that make this deflection progressively harder. I want them to cover: (1) the conceptual distinction between local functional equivalence and global binding coherence and why the latter is necessary for identity; (2) the epistemic problem of how an external observer or the subject herself could verify that coherence is preserved without circularly assuming the very consciousness or identity in question; (3) the challenge that if coherence is genuinely non-decomposable then its partial disruption should yield an abrupt, all-or-nothing collapse of consciousness rather than a gradual fade, and whether she accepts that prediction; and (4) whether any first-person report of discontinuity or any third-person behavioral anomaly could ever count as evidence against her view, or whether all such data can always be dismissed as local implementation failure. For each line, include a follow-up keyed to her most probable answer — for instance, that integrated-information metrics can track coherence, or that an abrupt threshold is precisely what her model predicts. Conclude with a single question asking what specific observation, if it occurred, she would genuinely concede undermines the view rather than merely indicating a glitch.
