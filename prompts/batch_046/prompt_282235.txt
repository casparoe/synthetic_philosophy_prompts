"The Platonia dilemma sets up identical rational agents who can choose whether to enter a shared situation. Because they are perfect reasoners with no private information, they should realize that whatever one concludes, all conclude. So if entering is best, they all enter together, which is good. The Luring Lottery tests this by offering a huge reward for picking a specific number if youre the only one who picks it. Rational agents should randomize evenly, but superrational ones think everyone else will avoid the lure, so they should take it - but then everyone takes it and nobody gets the big prize. This shows superrationality collapses in practice. Compared to standard rational choice, which just maximizes expected utility, superrationality adds this symmetry step. For part B with asymetric payoffs, the same logic holds: both agents recognize they share reasoning, so they should both pick cooperate. Even though A gets more, the symmetry of reasoning is stronger than the payoff gap, so they coordinate on cooperation anyway."
