I'm developing a position that I think defuses the classic experience-machine objection to hedonism, but I want an honest map of its vulnerabilities rather than a defense. The view holds that our resistance to entering the machine stems not from an intrinsic value of reality-contact, but from a psychological dependency: phenomenal well-being in actual human agents requires a background representation of causal efficacy, and if the machine implanted and sustained that representation with perfect stability—call this the "embedded authenticity condition"—the intuitive refusal should collapse. On this account, hedonism survives if it is supplemented by the claim that only phenomenal quality is fundamentally valuable, while causal connection is instrumentally valuable solely as the usual mechanism for sustaining the relevant meta-cognitive assurance. I am particularly worried that this collapses into a disguised preference-satisfaction theory, or that it becomes unfalsifiable by absorbing every counterexample through a further tweak to the embedded conditions.

What would genuinely count against this position? I want more than a restatement of the gut feeling that "we want to actually do things." Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal—say, formal results showing that representational stability is itself a form of achievement-value, or empirical findings that self-deception about efficacy is psychologically unstable in ways that undermine well-being—to merely awkward, such as having to concede that the view applies only to agents with a specific cognitive architecture. Which of these would a committed defender of the position concede as genuinely damaging, and which could they dismiss as misapplying a rival framework? Also assess whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if every imagined variant of the machine can be neutralized by adjusting the embedded authenticity conditions, is that a theoretical virtue or a sign that the view has lost its explanatory bite? Please answer in American English.
