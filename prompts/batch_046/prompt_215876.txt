Does Avicenna's claim that the suspended subject still knows he exists hold up under this stricter condition, or does it covertly depend on a minimal form of embodied self-awareness that contemporary enactivist accounts describe?

I want an explanation that gets into the conceptual weeds: whether the argument genuinely establishes a non-embodied mode of self-knowledge, or whether it implicitly presupposes the very bodily minimal selfhood it claims to have bracketed.
