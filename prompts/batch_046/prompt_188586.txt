As a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of mind, I have drafted the following notes for a short essay on original versus derived intentionality, specifically engaging Dennett's idea of a minimal two-bitser possessing only derived intentionality against Searle's claim that original intentionality is biologically primitive and irreducible.

The central analogy I wish to develop draws from instrument calibration. A particle detector registers voltage spikes that, through protocols established by scientists, come to represent momentum or energy. The detector's internal transitions are entirely causal; its representational content is borrowed, or derived, from the intentional framework of those who designed and interpreted it. In this sense, the detector resembles Dennett's two-bitser: a system that behaves as if it is about something without possessing any original aboutness of its own.

My tentative argument is that Searle's insistence on original intentionality as a feature of biological brains may depend on a distinction that collapses under closer physical analysis. If every intentional agent is embedded in a causal-physical system—neurons, chemical gradients, evolutionary history—then the boundary between calibrator and calibrated instrument becomes one of degree rather than kind. The brain, on this view, is not a unique source of original intentionality but rather an exceptionally complex, self-calibrating instrument whose representational states are derived from evolutionary and environmental feedback loops rather than from any intrinsic mental substance.

I am particularly interested in whether this analogy reverses the usual dependency: Dennett treats derived intentionality as parasitic on original intentionality, whereas my calibration model suggests that original intentionality might itself be parasitic on prior derived structures—language, culture, evolutionary design. If that reversal holds, then Searle's objection that syntax is never sufficient for semantics might apply not only to computers but to brains considered as physical information processors.

I would welcome comments on the conceptual coherence of this framing, especially on whether I have distorted Dennett's notion of the two-bitser or Searle's argument about biological originality. I am also unsure whether the calibration analogy genuinely illuminates the debate or merely imports physicalist assumptions that beg the question against Searle. Please focus on accuracy and on whether anything essential to either position fails to survive translation into this instrumentation vocabulary.
