I have just come out of a long, circular argument with a close friend about whether Dennett's distinction between cranes and skyhooks actually resolves anything about reductionism, or whether it merely restates the dispute in evolutionary costume. Rather than continuing to talk past each other, we want to formalize our disagreement as an adversarial collaboration. Please draft the joint statement that a committed 'crane-only' philosopher—who holds that every apparently irreducible phenomenon, including normative force, intentional content, and mathematical truth, can in principle be decomposed into stacked causal or algorithmic processes without remainder—could co-sign with a 'stopping-point' opponent who maintains that greedy reduction eliminates distinctions necessary for scientific explanation and that some levels of organization carry autonomous explanatory weight that cranes cannot reach.

The document should contain: first, a numbered list of claims both parties can genuinely accept, covering the formal logic of reduction, the actual structure of evolutionary explanation, and the specific phenomena we agree are genuinely contested rather than verbally ambiguous; second, a disagreements table in which each camp's position is stated in wording its own advocates would endorse—particularly on whether biological 'function' is exhaustively causal-historical or essentially normative, whether cognitive misrepresentation requires an irreducible teleological layer, and whether normative commitment can be fully accounted for by evolutionary dynamics without circularity; third, the cruxes, identified as the sub-questions whose resolution would actually shift at least one party's view; and fourth, for each crux, a concrete case, argument, or evidential scenario that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's explicit prediction recorded in advance.

We want the formulation precise enough to reveal whether part of our dispute is merely verbal—say, over the word 'explanation'—and we want no bibliographies, no requests for further reading, and no appeals to highly obscure technical literature that a well-informed but non-specialist interlocutor could not evaluate from general knowledge alone.
