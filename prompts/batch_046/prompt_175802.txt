I am writing my dissertation on the epistemic regress and need to anticipate my advisor’s reaction to a constructed case. In our seminar she has consistently held: (1) that a belief grounded in direct perceptual experience is justified even when the believer cannot articulate further reasons for it; (2) that a belief locked in a tight circular loop with two other beliefs, each supported only by the others, is not justified; (3) that a belief supported by an available, non-repeating, infinitely extending chain of reasons is justified; and (4) that a belief formed by a generally reliable cognitive process is not justified when nearby possible worlds would have yielded false beliefs through the same process.

Consider now case E: a subject S holds belief B, which is true and was formed by a reliable method. B is supported by reason R1, which is true and based on direct experience; R1 is supported by R2, which is false though S is unaware of this; R2 is supported by R3, which is true; and so on, with every even-numbered reason false and every odd-numbered reason true, extending infinitely without repetition or circularity. The false reasons do not undermine the reliability of the original method.

Predict whether my advisor will judge B justified. Identify at least two of the three standard positions—foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism—that are equally compatible with her four prior verdicts, explain what each predicts about B, state which prediction you think she will actually endorse and why, and specify the single further case that would best distinguish the two theories you have identified.
