okay so i've been thinking about this argument my friend threw at me about iterated amplification and distillation, specifically whether breaking a task down actually keeps both alignment and competence intact when you stitch it back together. here's the chain:

(1) if every subtask in a decomposition is solved by a model that's both aligned and competent, then the aggregate solution is also aligned and competent, since alignment and competence are both preserved under functional composition.
(2) iterated amplification with distillation works exactly like this: you split a hard problem into parts a small model can do, supervise them, then distill the results back into a bigger model.
(3) so if we start with an aligned and competent seed model, each round of amplification/distillation should produce another aligned and competent model.
(4) by induction, after n rounds we still have alignment and competence intact, no matter how big or capable the model gets.
(5) but we also know that in practice small misalignments and capability gaps compound with scale -- call it drift -- and eventually the system behaves in ways the seed never would have endorsed.
(6) so either drift doesn't happen (which is false) or step (1) is wrong.
(7) but if (1) is wrong then decomposition gives us no reason to trust the composed system, which undermines the whole justification for IAD.
(8) therefore we have to reject either the induction or accept that IAD eventually fails, and neither option feels right.

where's the slip? i feel like there's a hidden equivocation between "the subtask solver is aligned" and "the composed behavior is aligned" but i cant pin it down. i want to keep (2) and (5) if i can. which step should i actually reject, and what's the cost?
