I'm drafting remarks for a graduate seminar in epistemic modal logic and want a consistency audit of my position before I present. I hold the following commitments:

(a) Fitch's derivation is valid and demonstrates that the unrestricted knowability principle—every true proposition is in principle knowable—entails that every true proposition is actually known, which is false; so the unrestricted principle must be abandoned.

(b) I defend a restricted replacement: for every proposition that contains no nested epistemic operators and no self-referential knowledge attribution, if it is true then it is knowable in principle.

(c) There are genuinely unknown contingent historical facts—consider a particular physical event in a causally isolated region for which all traces have been permanently erased—such that no recovery of evidence is physically possible even for an idealized rational agent.

(d) I analyze "knowable in principle" not as bare metaphysical possibility augmented by an observer, but as compatibility with the actual causal-evidential structure of the world: p is knowable just in case there exists a physically possible observational pathway, consistent with actual laws, through which an agent could come to know p.

(e) The logic of knowledge includes both factivity (Kp → p) and unrestricted positive introspection (Kp → KKp), applied uniformly across empirical and epistemic domains alike.

(f) I reject verificationism and maintain that mind-independent truths may lie permanently outside the scope of any possible knowledge without thereby being rendered unreal or indeterminate.

Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity, indicating for each what would have to be surrendered and what that costs the broader view—particularly whether giving up (b), (d), or (c) does the least damage to the motivation behind the others. I am not sure whether the restriction in (b) actually saves me from the problem raised by (c) given how I have defined possibility in (d), or whether (e) quietly reintroduces the paradox within the restricted domain despite (b).
