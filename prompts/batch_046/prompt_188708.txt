I'm a retired physicist who's only recently started digging into philosophy and archaeology — proper digging, not the fieldwork kind, though I do like the pun. I've noticed a pattern in how people reconstruct beliefs and institutions from objects, and I'm not sure if there's an established name for it.

Here's what I mean. You excavate a settlement and find pottery decorated with symbols that seem to belong to one religious tradition, but the burial layout and the tools scatered about match a completely different social structure. Rather than concluding that our categories for 'religion' and 'social organisation' might be misapplied here, the usual move seems to be: 'ah, this must be a syncretic cult, but only in this valley, and only for people of a certain status, and the symbols probably mean something slightly different in this particular context.' Each little exception is individually reasonable, but together they build up a kind of protective layer around the original classification system. The framework never really gets tested, because every contradictory artefact gets its own bespoke sub-category.

In philosophy of science I read about auxiliary hypotheses, but that feels too broad — this is specifically about interpreting material culture when the objects don't line up with the institutions we're trying to reconstruct. Is there a more precise term for this slow, case-by-case patching of an interprative framework in archaeology or anthropology? And if there isn't one, how should I think about where legitimate refinement ends and just saving the theory begins?
