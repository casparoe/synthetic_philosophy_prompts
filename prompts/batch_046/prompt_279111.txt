Before responding, briefly outline to yourself the difference between (a) a metaphysical claim that consciousness is numerically one, (b) the quantum-mechanical claim that the state vector evolves unitarily for an isolated system, and (c) an ethical claim derived from non-duality—so that you do not conflate them in what follows.

A friend who recently read a popular essay on Schrödinger's later philosophy offers this summary: "Schrödinger held that the wave function's unitary evolution demonstrates that there is only one consciousness in the universe. Individual minds are not separate entities but localized disturbances—like ripples—within a single cosmic awareness that he explicitly equated with Vedantic Brahman. From this he concluded that materialism is false and pluralistic idealism is incoherent, and he further argued that ethics must be reconstructed on the recognition that injury to another person is strictly self-injury, since no real boundary separates one subject from another. For Schrödinger this was not an independent mystical belief but the unavoidable consequence of interpreting quantum mechanics without hidden variables."

Please audit this characterization claim by claim. For each sentence, indicate whether it is essentially accurate, subtly distorted, or plainly wrong, and explain what Schrödinger himself would likely correct or qualify. In particular, address whether he treated the unity of consciousness as a logical consequence of quantum formalism or as a metaphysical framework that quantum analogies merely illustrated; whether his ethical remarks genuinely depend on the identity doctrine in the strong form presented; and whether "localized disturbances within a single cosmic awareness" fairly captures his view of individual minds or instead smuggles in a panpsychist reading he would reject. Write your answer for an intelligent non-specialist who needs to know which parts of the summary are trustworthy.
