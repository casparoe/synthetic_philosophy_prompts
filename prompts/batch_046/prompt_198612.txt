my aunt just told me the painting hanging in our hallway is probably a forgery — it was supposed to be by some semi-known painter, but the gallery she bought it from went bust and now she’s freaking out. that got me thinking about why originals matter at all, and i’m trying to write a short essay about it, but i’m stuck.

here are my rough notes. the usual story is the causal-historical one: an original is valuable because you can trace the paint or stone back to the artist’s actual hand, so there’s a direct physical link. then there’s the study-idea — with an original you can see the real decision-tree of the artist, the false starts and corrections, not just the final image. i want to push this further and say the real value is counterfactual decision-tree access: an original lets you recover not just what the artist did, but which branching choices they actually faced and rejected, and a perfect copy only preserves the terminal branch, not the tree. that sounds fancy but i’m not sure if it’s just the causal view in fancier words.

then there’s this cross-cultural thing i read about — in some indigenous australian and japanese woodblock contexts, authenticty isn’t tied to physical uniqueness at all, but to whether the object was produced through an authorized ritual or workshop process. so a perfect physical replica made outside that process is definately a fake, but a brand-new physical object made inside the correct process can count as original even if it looks identical to an older one. if that’s right, the western obsession with the singular object starts to look like a local norm, not a universal fact. but i’m worried i’m misrepresenting those traditions.

i also want to apply this to digital art, where there is no unique physical object — just files. the causal-historical view seems to collapse unless we say the “original” is the file on the artist’s hard drive, which sounds silly. so maybe decision-tree access is the better framework, because you can still ask whether the file you have is the one that actually went through the artist’s editing process.
