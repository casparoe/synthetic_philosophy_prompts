Write an essay on whether present citizens of a nation can bear moral guilt for historical injustices committed by prior generations. Reconstruct the standard taxonomy—criminal guilt for individual acts, political guilt for state actions, moral guilt for individual conscience, and metaphysical guilt for universal human solidarity—but identify a gap: none of these clearly licenses collective moral guilt in a non-derivative sense. Then propose and defend your own framework, distinguishing diachronic institutional culpability (a property persisting through unbroken institutional identity across generations) from synchronic structural complicity (present-day benefit from and maintenance of unjust structures). Use this distinction to argue either that present citizens can bear a distinct form of collective moral guilt if institutional continuity is coupled with active preservation of the legacy, or that such guilt is always reducible to individual moral failure or political responsibility, rendering collective moral guilt a category mistake. Reach a definite conclusion and justify it by making explicit which commitments about personal identity, collective agency, and moral inheritance your verdict requires.
