I serve on the investment and ethics committee of the Meridian Civic Foundation. A donor proposes that we commit $200,000 to a new “Cascading Discovery Endowment” for climate-adaptation research, structured like a modified St. Petersburg payout: each year the grant either distributes its current balance—beginning at $25,000 and doubling—with 50% probability, or rolls over and doubles again, producing an unbounded expected return. The donor argues that this infinite expected social value directly fulfills our charter’s mandate to maximize long-term public benefit. Our finance officer counters that institutional fiduciary duty requires avoiding commitments with unbounded liability exposure regardless of probability-weighted return, and notes that in practice the payout will almost certainly be a small fraction of our initial commitment. The board must vote by Thursday. Please draft the committee’s formal recommendation memo to the full board: state a clear recommendation—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—together with a concrete implementation timeline; explain whether and how expected-value reasoning should govern philanthropic decision-making in this specific structural case; and provide an honest, substantive account of the strongest consideration on the opposing side, whether it concerns the rational-choice paradox, donor intent, or fiduciary limits.
