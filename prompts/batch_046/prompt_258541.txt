I'm a phd student in phil of mind and I'm writing my diss on something like a 'user illusion' model of consciousness, but I'm trying to push it past the usual interface metaphors. The specific version I'm flirting with is that consciousness is literally a control-display misattribution: the brain generates an error-correction dashboard for tracking internal states, and because this display gets projected onto working memory without any flag marking it as representational, the system takes itself to be 'present' in the data. So qualia aren't just simplified representations of the world; they're the system's own debugging symbols being misread as properties of experience. I want to defend this as a real metaphysical claim, not just a heuristic.

But I need to know what would actually count against it, not just the usual 'you're denying the obvious' objection. I'm looking for the decisive defeaters versus the merely awkward ones. For instance, would split-brain cases show that the illusion can be divided in ways the view can't accommodate? Would blindsight patients lack the illusion entirely, and if so does that mean consciousness is just one module among others? Are there formal results in cognitive architechture - maybe about self-modeling or predictive processing - that imply any system with this kind of error-monitoring necessarily produces phenominal binding, which would undermine my claim that it's just a misattributed display? I also wonder whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability: if I say the illusion is unavoidable for any system with this architechture, then no observation could ever disprove it. Is that a real problem for this kind of position, or is it like saying gravity is unavoidable for massive bodies?

I'd like the answer to rank these by how fatal they'd be, say which ones defenders of this kind of view would concede as counting against them, and be honest about whether the position is well-insulated. Also please don't just list standard objections I've already read; I'm especially interested in whether there's a consequence the view is committed to that turns out unacceptable, or a specific case it simply can't describe.
