Construct a thought experiment, confined to one realistic paragraph, set in a 1924 arbitration over customary irrigation rights between two alpine villages. The arbitrator is also the historian, with complete access to municipal ledgers, private correspondence, weather logs, and the physical remains of a contested sluice gate. Engineer the case so that strict adherence to Ranke's method—reconstructing every contradictory source-detail without selective framing in order to show the past as it actually was—produces an account that is technically exhaustive but decision-paralyzing and materially misleading about which village held customary precedence. At the same time, engineer the case so that Carr's scepticism—insisting that bare facts never speak for themselves and that the historian must select, weigh, and interpret—yields the only accurate determination of precedence, specifically because it treats the sluice gate's wear patterns as interpretively loaded rather than self-interpreting. Further, design the case so that Hayden White's analysis of narrative emplotment applies to both accounts: they share an unacknowledged genre (satire, not romance or tragedy) that determines which details are treated as significant, and this hidden genre is doing essential work in Carr's apparently rational synthesis. After presenting the scenario, provide the following: a step-by-step demonstration of what each of the three positions—Ranke's archival objectivism, Carr's interpretive scepticism, and White's narrative constructivism—says about the case and why; an explicit identification of which feature of the scenario is doing the theoretical work; an explanation of how the verdicts shift if that feature is removed; and an account of how a defender of Ranke's position would argue that the scenario cheats by conflating practical arbitration with historical explanation. Finally, state clearly that the purpose of the case is to test whether practical reasoning about customary rights can be epistemically prior to narrative coherence, rather than merely instrumentally aided by it, and that the scenario must generate these verdicts from the theories rather than stipulating them.
