People in bioethics keep comparing an advance directive to a contract your past self signs to bind your future self — like your'e a landlord locking in a lease for a tenant who hasn't moved in yet. They use it to argue the earlier version should always win. I want an audit: what's the correspondence table (offer, acceptance, consideration, breach?), where it's fall apart — especially since the "tenant" might not even be the same person, just someone with the same body? And here's the part that bugs me: the analogy quietly assumes personal identity is a straight line, but what if it's more like a tangled hierarchy where the later self is both inside and outside the authority of the earlier one? Does the "contract" framing still justify letting the past self overrule, or is it just smuggling that conclusion in? Keep it practical — I don't need names of papers, just the logic.
