I've been working through the interaction between Nozick's tracking conditions and epistemic closure principles with a study group, and after considerable back-and-forth I've settled on a qualified hybrid view that I think escapes the standard closure-failure objections — but I want an honest assessment of how exposed it actually is. The position is this: knowledge closes under competent deduction, but only within what I call a modal stability band. Specifically, if S knows p and competently deduces q from p, then S knows q just in case the closest worlds in which p is true but q is false are not closer to the actual world than the closest worlds in which p is false — in other words, the tracking condition for q must not be more demanding, relative to actuality, than the tracking condition for p. This allows closure for mundane inferences — knowing I have hands and therefore knowing I am not a handless brain-in-a-vat — but blocks it for high-stakes deductions, such as inferring from knowledge of astronomical lottery odds that I will not win, where the failure-world for the conclusion is modally closer than the failure-world for the premise.

What would count against this view? I am not looking for a mere restatement of the standard anti-closure examples or the familiar Nozickian reply; I want the defeaters that actually bite. Which specific cases or intuitions about knowledge attribution — for instance, nested deductions where stability fails at an intermediate step, or the inference from knowing this is a zebra to knowing it is not a cleverly disguised mule — genuinely threaten the boundary I have drawn? Are there formal properties of tracking relations, such as failures of transitivity across successive deductions, that show the modal-stability criterion collapses into either full closure or full rejection of closure? Is there a principled, non-ad-hoc way to fix the boundary, or does the view risk insulating itself by re-describing any counterexample as lying outside the band? Please distinguish the decisive objections from the merely awkward ones, indicate which a committed defender of this hybrid should concede as genuinely counting against the position, and say whether being forced to deny closure for apparently valid inferences like the zebra case is fatal or only uncomfortable. I also want to know whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, whether that is a problem for a theory of knowledge of this kind.
