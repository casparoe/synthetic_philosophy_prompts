Write the desiderata that an adequate explanatory theory of cross-cultural variation in philosophical intuitions must satisfy if it is to count as genuinely adequate rather than merely taxonomic. For each requirement, include the motivating case or conceptual consideration that makes it a genuine desideratum rather than a camp-specific prejudice; then flag which pairs you take to be in genuine tension—possibly jointly unsatisfiable—and defend your judgment about which are non-negotiable.

Specifically, address: (1) the range of documented asymmetries the theory must cover—knowledge-attribution judgments in Gettier-style scenarios, moral-luck assessments, and indirect-harm dilemmas—without dismissing either response pattern as noise; (2) a principled distinction between variation reflecting deep conceptual architecture, such as an epistemic verb encoding different success conditions, and variation reflecting performance conditions like framing, translation artifacts, or social-desirability bias, with a concrete hypothetical illustrating each; (3) the expertise-correction problem, requiring the theory to accommodate reverse expertise effects—cases where formal philosophical training amplifies rather than reduces cross-cultural divergence in a domain—and to state whether this undermines the claim that expertise reliably calibrates intuition; (4) treatment of intra-cultural heterogeneity of a magnitude that sometimes exceeds inter-cultural mean differences, so culture cannot be a monolithic causal determinant; (5) handling of linguistic clustering, where judgment differences map more precisely onto grammatical features such as aspectual marking of agency than onto broad civilizational categories; (6) a normative constraint that avoids chauvinism smuggling one culture’s intuitions in as default, yet preserves enough universal normative leverage to keep philosophical criticism from becoming entirely parochial; and (7) consistency conditions with adjacent empirical literatures—cognitive psychology of reasoning, anthropological folk-taxonomy studies—without allowing reduction to them.
