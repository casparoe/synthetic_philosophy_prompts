I just spent the better part of an evening arguing with a close friend about whether the classic distinction between finkish dispositions and masked dispositions really holds up once you move past the textbook examples. She kept insisting that an antidote is merely a slower-acting fink with a different temporal profile, and that if we take the structural conditions of manifestation seriously, the modal difference between losing a disposition and having it blocked collapses into a single causal story. I found myself unable to articulate exactly why the ontology should care about the difference in mechanism if the observable result is the same, and the argument ended with both of us conceding that we were talking past each other about whether manifestation conditions are best read as causal triggers or as modal constraints on the disposition's very identity.

I'm now preparing to moderate a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher—she works in metaphysics of science and has been developing an unnamed but specific position in recent papers—who defends what she calls an "essential-conditionality" view of masks. On her account, antidotes do not suppress or interfere with a pre-existing disposition; rather, they reveal that the disposition was always essentially conditional on the absence of some state, so that what looks like masking is actually the disposition operating exactly as its modal structure demands. I need eight to ten interview questions for this session that go well beyond having her restate the view.
