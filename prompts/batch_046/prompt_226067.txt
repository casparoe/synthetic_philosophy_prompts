Their central argument is that predictive algorithms are routinely misunderstood as possessing genuine causal powers—weights, architectures, and training regimes "producing" outputs—when, on an occasionalist reading, these elements are merely customary sequences (al-ʿāda) sustained by an underlying occasioning reality that is not intrinsic to the model itself. One faction regards the proposal as a necessary philosophical intervention against technological determinism and the reification of statistical correlation; another regards it as a form of mystification that could undermine public trust in predictive tools used for medical triage, climate modeling, and resource allocation.
