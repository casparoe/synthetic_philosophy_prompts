I serve as the student representative on my high school's Independent Research Ethics and Curriculum Review Panel. A proposal is before us for an extracurricular initiative—tentatively titled the Sentient Welfare Engineering Circle—in which students would collaborate with a faculty biologist to study genetic and neuropharmacological interventions aimed at reducing nociceptive suffering in non-human model organisms. The proposal explicitly frames this work within the philosophical position known as the hedonistic imperative: the view that biotechnology ought to pursue the complete abolition of suffering across all sentient beings, treating the elimination of negative affect as a moral priority comparable to curing disease. The faculty sponsor contends that this is an extension of standard veterinary analgesia and welfare science; however, two panel members—representing the parent constituency—object that the project smuggles an unverified metaphysical thesis into empirical research, namely that suffering is the sole intrinsic evil and that enhancement-level genetic modifications are morally obligatory rather than merely permissible. They further argue that our school's research policy contains no framework for evaluating projects that conflate therapeutic goals with what they call transhumanist welfare engineering, and that approval would implicitly commit the school to endorsing a controversial ethical doctrine. Please draft the formal decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approval, rejection, or approval with conditions—accompanied by an explicit time frame and operational criteria. The reasoning should take the faculty sponsor's scientific framing seriously, engage the parents' concern about metaphysical smuggling and the therapy-enhancement boundary without dismissing it, and propose safeguards that allow the panel to monitor whether the project remains within appropriate ethical and methodological bounds regardless of which philosophical interpretation one accepts.
