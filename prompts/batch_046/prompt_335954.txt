I'm helping to organize a small interdisciplinary workshop—philosophers of science and a few cognitive scientists—focused not on historical reconstruction but on live conceptual problems arising from Otto Neurath's picture-language and ISOTYPE program. We are working with tight constraints: two days, eight invited speakers, and a mandate to produce something useful for current practice rather than archival scholarship.

Among the issues I'm weighing are three that seem genuinely open and somewhat underexplored. First, whether Neurath's rejection of private mental imagery in favor of strictly intersubjective visual protocols offers any real resistance to contemporary representationalist and predictive-processing accounts of cognition. Second, whether the tension he acknowledged between the 'unity of science' ideal and the irreducible plurality of visual grammars across disciplines is a methodological dead end or a productive framework for modern scientific visualization. Third, whether his prohibition on metaphor and emotional suggestion in picture languages remains defensible given that contemporary data visualization and scientific illustration routinely deploy both for explanatory effect.

Given these options—and any others you think I am neglecting—which should we prioritize? I want a defended ordering rather than a survey: for each line you include, explain why it is tractable for philosophers and cognitive scientists rather than historians, what kind of argument or empirical result over the next few years would shift its priority, and how the resolution of one might depend on resolving another.
