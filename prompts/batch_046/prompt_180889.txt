I'm taking a course on metaphysics and the philosophy of logic, and I've been working through Bradley's argument concerning relations and the unity of judgement. A fellow student offered me the following summary, which she presented as a fair gloss of Bradley's position in his metaphysical treatise: Bradley argues that if relations are genuinely real, then linking a relation R to its terms a and b requires a further relation R', which in turn requires R'', producing an infinite regress; consequently, relations are not ultimately real, and reality is an undifferentiated Absolute in which every distinction collapses. To preserve judgement without this regress, Bradley holds that the subject and predicate are not separate entities connected by an external link, but are immediately fused into one whole that has no internal parts; therefore, a proposition lacks any analysable structure, and modern interpreters sometimes read this fusion as anticipating a non-relational bundle theory of properties. I suspect this account conflates Bradley's metaphysical claim about the Absolute with his logical analysis of judgement, and that it mischaracterises his view of internal relations and of the content of judgement. Could you assess the fairness of this summary? Please present your evaluation as exactly five numbered points, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introductory or concluding paragraph.
