After a long and somewhat circular argument with a friend about whether pain asymbolia actually undermines the claim that pain is intrinsically bad, I want a classification instrument for our philosophy-of-mind discussion group. Construct a branching taxonomy—structured as a decision tree with exactly four diagnostic nodes—that sorts positions on the ontology and axiology of pain using three concrete dissociation cases rather than pre-labeled philosophical names.

The three cases that must serve as the diagnostic material are: (A) a patient with classic pain asymbolia who reports a burning sensation, shows no withdrawal or emotional distress, and actively seeks repetition of the stimulus; (B) a masochist who reports an identical qualitative sensation but integrates it into a desired experience; and (C) a subject with painless tissue damage who avoids the injury site without any conscious hurt.

The four branch nodes must be concrete questions answerable without knowing which view they lead to. First: in case A, is the negative affective charge a constitutive part of the pain state, or can the sensory quality exist fully without it? Second: in case B, does the subject's endorsement cancel the badness of the sensation, or does a prudential harm remain independent of evaluation? Third: in case C, does the absence of conscious hurt combined with avoidance behavior show that the motivational function of pain can operate without sensation, or does it indicate a non-conscious pain state? Fourth: is the badness of pain identical to its qualitative feel, identical to its representational content (representing bodily damage), or identical to its motivational role?

The tree should yield named leaves for the following six positions: (1) strong qualitative essentialism; (2) affective-constitution essentialism; (3) pure representationalism; (4) pure motivational functionalism; (5) the family-resemblance anti-essentialist view that asymbolia reveals no unitary kind but a cluster of dissociable dimensions; and (6) the hybrid rational-evaluative view that pain is necessarily bad only for agents who lack overriding reasons to endorse it.

For every combination of answers not occupied by one of these six, provide a brief diagnosis—note whether the cell is likely incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Avoid obscure clinical taxonomies, neuroanatomical pathways, or DSM codes that depend on specialized memorization. Keep the deliverable between about 600 and 800 words, present the questions first, then map the positions onto the logical space, then present the empty-cell diagnoses and flagged overlaps.
