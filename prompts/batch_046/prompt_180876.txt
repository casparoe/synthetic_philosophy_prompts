Take the debate over whether sustained national prosperity is driven chiefly by institutions, geographic endowments, or cultural norms, together with the methodological objection that we observe only one realized history, that institutions and geography co-evolve over centuries, and that no parallel counterfactual worlds exist. Restate the core arguments of all three sides and this identification problem entirely in my epidemiological vocabulary: assign roles of treatment, outcome, confounder, mediator, and collider to the relevant historical factors; specify where exchangeability or consistency fail; and flag any positivity violations. Conclude by judging whether the untranslatable residue shows that historians are asking questions this formalism cannot capture, or whether it reveals that the historical debate is less rigorously causal than it presents itself as being. End with a single line: Verdict: [one sentence summarizing whether the translation succeeds or fails and why].
