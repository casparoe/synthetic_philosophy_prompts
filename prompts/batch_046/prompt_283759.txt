I'm a retired ecologist — thirty years tracking bird migration — and lately I've been poking around philosophy for fun. Anyway, I'm helping my neice organize a neighborhood repair cafe (toasters, bikes, that sort of thing). She wants to split volunteer shifts into three groups: Boomers, Millennials, and Gen Z, because she claims each "generation" has totally different problem-solving instincts. I told her my own "generation" includes people who learned on slide rules and people who learned on the first Macs, and my neighbor born in 1963 acts more like her "Gen Z" friends than like me (born 1958). She said I'm being a reductionist scientist and missing the "social reality" of generations.

So my question: is there a philosophically defensible reason to treat "Boomer" or "Gen Z" as a real kind of person — like treating "mammal" as a real kind in biology — or is it more like labeling an arbitrary slice of a continuous bird population a "subspecies" just because it's handy? I don't want a reading list; I just want to know if her split makes philosophical sense, or if I'm right that it's a made-up box we force people into. She threw out some name like Mannheim, but I care less about who said what and more about whether these boxes correspond to something genuinely in the world, or whether they're statistical blurs we pretend are sharp boundaries.
