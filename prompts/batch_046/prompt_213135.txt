I am a doctoral student working at the intersection of philosophical logic and theories of rational agency, and I am trying to specify the conditions that any adequate account of self-trusting reasoning must satisfy if it is to avoid the Löbian obstacle without retreating into either full logical omniscience or global skepticism about one's own cognitive processes. Rather than asking for a survey of existing proposals, I want the full specification of desiderata: the phenomena such a theory must accommodate—such as an agent's ability to maintain calibrated confidence in its future derivations, to update that confidence non-monotonically upon recognizing local errors, and to distinguish between trusting a particular inference rule and endorsing the global consistency of one's belief set—the distinctions it must preserve—particularly between fallible self-trust and unconditional self-endorsement, and between first-order reasoning failures and second-order misrepresentations of those failures—the formal constraints it must respect, including consistency with the fixed-point behavior described by Löb's theorem for modal arithmetic but without requiring that the agent actually instantiate the logical closure conditions that make the theorem apply, and the psychological or normative results it must not contradict, such as the empirical reality of bounded rationality and the normative demand that an agent not dismiss its own reasoning wholesale merely because it can conceive of error. For each desideratum, I want the motivating case or tension behind it, and I want you to flag which ones are genuinely non-negotiable and which are artifacts of particular formal traditions—say, the preference for monotonic belief revision or for representing belief as a classical modal operator. I also want you to identify whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable given standard fixed-point results, and if so, which single desideratum does the most damage to popular approaches in the literature—whether that be the demand for full introspective access, the demand for complete logical closure, or the demand that self-trust be unconditional rather than domain-restricted. Please deliver this entirely in continuous prose, as in a formal letter: no headings, no bullet points, no boldface, no tables, and no enumerated lists, so that the argument flows without structural scaffolding. I am not asking for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, and I want the analysis to stand on its own conceptual ground rather than on attributions to particular authors.
