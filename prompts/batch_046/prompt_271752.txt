Please argue that Hume's defense of suicide's permissability actually collapses if you apply his own empiricist framework consistently. In his essay he dismisses the idea that suicide harms society by treating people like interchangable parts in a big pool—remove one, the pool barely changes. But if social attachments work more like a tightly knit network with high clustering coefficients, where your friends are mostly friends with each other, then pulling one node out doesn't just reduce the count; it fractures local triangles and creates a kind of structural grief that can't be fixed by replacing you with a stranger. So on Hume's own terms, the social-obligation objection might actually bite harder than he admits. Work through the logic without just summarizing the essay.
