Analyze the 2003 Iraq invasion decision as a forcasting failure seen through reference classes and the outside view of empires. I know the usual story about Munich analouges and Vietnam quagmire fears; what I want is a disciplined mapping of who was forecasting what, which reference class they implicitly used (regime-change wars? democratic interventions? imperial policing actions?), and what the actual base rates for those classes looked like around 2003. Then bring in the outside view: if we treat the US not as an exceptional rising power but as an empire in late-stage overreach—comparing patterns from British India, Roman frontier wars, or Soviet Afghanistan—does that reference class predict failure better? Be honest about where the theory is silent: does the outside view tell us anything about the internal politics of the Baath regime, or about WMD intelligence errors, or is it just a macro frame? Also say whether this episode is a good argument that analogies like Munich are inevitable in democratic decision-making, or whether it's mainly a warning about class instability—where the same war can be sorted into "liberation" or "overreach" depending on framing. Don't force the theory to speak where it has nothing to say.
