Write a Petrarchan sonnet (rhyme scheme ABBAABBA CDECDE or CDCDCD) in iambic pentameter with occasional metrical freedom, treating the methodological reversal known as the F-Twist: the argument, set out in Milton Friedman’s essay within his 1953 book Essays in Positive Economics, that a theory’s predictive power can increase as its core assumptions become more wildly inaccurate descriptions of reality, because strategic unreality strips away predictively irrelevant detail. Do not name Friedman, and do not title the essay; honor Samuelson’s indirect attribution—he called it the F-Twist rather than naming it after Friedman out of courtesy—by letting the thinker appear only through pronouns, images of abstraction, and the logic of omission. In the octave, build the intuitive demand for fidelity: premises should mirror the world if conclusions are to be trusted, using images of scales, mirrors, and ground-level observation. At the volta in line 9, execute the precise logical inversion: the wider the gap between assumption and observed fact, the sharper the predictive instrument, since the false premise functions as a filter rather than a flaw. In the sestet, pursue the tension between productive fiction and mere falsehood—can any wildly unrealistic assumption be redeemed by correct forecasts?—and close with a couplet stating exactly what is surrendered (descriptive realism) and what is retained (fruitful prediction). Weave into the poem, without quotation marks, the substance of Friedman’s formulation: that truly important and significant hypotheses have assumptions that are wildly inaccurate descriptive representations of reality, and that in general the more significant the theory, the more unrealistic the assumptions. Let the rhyme scheme reinforce the reversal: pair an octave rhyme word (for instance, true or view) with its transformed counterpart in the sestet (for instance, untrue or review in the sense of predictive reassessment). The result should be recognizable to a philosopher of economics or science as the specific F-Twist rather than a generic defense of modeling, yet fully legible as poetry to a reader unfamiliar with the debate.
