Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a concluding quatrain, with two refrains in an ABA rhyme scheme—on akrasia understood specifically through hyperbolic discounting and the intertemporal-bargaining model of willpower. Do not treat weakness of will as simple moral failure; treat it as a dynamic-inconsistency problem in which a near-future self and a distant-future self hold competing preferences over the same choice pair, with preference reversing as the smaller-sooner reward draws nearer—the common-difference effect. Assign one refrain to the near-future self (the immediate, smaller reward) and one to the distant-future self (the delayed, larger reward). Let the cyclical repetition of these lines across the tercets enact the cyclical return of temptation, and let the rhyme scheme bind the two voices together even as they argue. In the final quatrain, bring both refrains together in the last two lines, but do not resolve the tension: the form should end on an unreconciled juxtaposition that mirrors the breakdown of the private contract across temporal selves. The poem should be serious and contemplative, without sermonizing, and accurate enough that a reader familiar with behavioral economics recognizes the hyperbolic mechanism—specifically preference reversal driven by a declining discount rate—rather than generic procrastination.
