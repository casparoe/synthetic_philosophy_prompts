I'm trying to work out my actual view on fatelism using these death-in-damascus-style cases I sketched after arguing with my roommate for like three hours. There's the classic parable where a guy learns Death will meet him in Samarkand tomorrow, so he rides hard to Damascus, but Death is waiting there and explains he was surprised to see him in Damascus because their appointment was always in Samarkand — meaning the flight was part of the causal chain that delivered him. I think this is genuinely unavoidable, not just self-fulfilling. Then there's the mirror image: he hears the same prophecy and decides "well, I might as well go to Samarkand and face it," rides there deliberately, and dies. To me this feels manufactered — he created the meeting, so calling it "fate" seems wrong. Third case: imagine a fully deterministic block universe with no prophecy or Death figure at all, just physics fixing that your heart stops at 3pm Tuesday; you take medicine, it doesn't work. This also feels unavoidable, but it's not about knowledge or prophecy changing anything.

Then there's case four, which is weirder: an AI predicts with 99% accuracy that anyone who learns about prediction X will do action Y, which is fatal, but if you avoid Y you'll do Z instead and still die. It tells you about X, you avoid Y, do Z, die anyway. I feel like the endpoint was fixed but the mechanism changed — maybe call it mechanism-sensitive inevitability? But then case five: the AI predicts you'll die in Damascus, so you stay in London your whole life and nothing bad happens. Here the prophecy was just wrong, or self-defeating, and it breaks the pattern. So my verdicts are that case one and three are real inevitability, case two is fake, case four is maybe real but in a strange way, and case five is an outlier. Is there one principle that captures all of these, or am I forced to drop one? And what would that principle say about the case I keep going back and forth on — where you hear the prophecy, stay exactly where you are in Damascus, and then die of an unrelated heart attack the same day?
