Consider five proposed resolutions to the paradox of legal self-reference — that a constitutional rule authorizing its own amendment must either rest on an unamendable meta-rule or invite infinite regress. The proposals are: (A) Hart's rule of recognition, treated as an ultimate social fact that terminates the chain; (B) Kelsen's Grundnorm, understood as a transcendental-logical presupposition rather than a positive norm; (C) Ross's empirical model of constitutional self-amendment bounded by a fixed procedural threshold; (D) a synthetic stability-indexed meta-rule (introduced here) that calibrates amendment difficulty to quantitative measures of institutional continuity, attempting to ground self-reference in a non-legal baseline; and (E) the skeptical position that self-amending rules are hollow and all constitutional change is ultimately extra-legal political fact.

Rate each proposal on internal logical coherence (scale 1–10) and on practical viability for a modern constitutional democracy (scale 1–10), then rank them 1 through 5 overall. In your reasoning, specifically evaluate whether proposal D genuinely escapes the regress or merely relocates it into the measurement protocol for institutional continuity, and address whether any proposal can survive the scenario in which the amendment mechanism is used to abolish the very institution that applies it.
