I spent thirty years in a condensed-matter lab — nothing Nobel-level, just grinding out data on superconductors and trying to keep grad students fed — and now I'm retired and actually reading philosophy, which is humbling in a good way. I've been stuck on panpsychism and its ethical side, not the cartoon version where rocks have feelings, but the serious philosophical view that fundamental physical processes might involve some minimal form of sentience. If that's true, then we might have obligations to physical systems that we haven't mapped out at all, and that's what's been keeping me up.

What I can't shake is this half-baked idea I'm calling "interference suffering." Most discussions ask whether a single particle or field can suffer in isolation, but what if the ethical weight isn't in the individual bits but in how they overlap? If sentience is tied to coherence or entanglement, then quantum superpostion and field interference might generate dissonant patterns — not just more suffering added together, but a qualitatively worse kind of collective experience. Don't turn it into a horror story or a sermon; let her sit with the ambiguity of whether she's seeing something real or just projecting loneliness onto physics. Please write in American English — I still slip and write "behaviour" with a u out of old grad-school habit in London, but I'd prefer American spelling. And don't give me a bibliography or lecture me on what Chalmers said; I just want the story to take the idea seriously without resolving whether she's right.
