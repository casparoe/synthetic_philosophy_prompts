A common analogy in philosophy of computation treats symbols as a driver steering a car: the driver never physically pushes the engine, yet determines its trajectory because the vehicle's mechanisms interpret his commands. Some apply this to the careenium—a substrate in which simmballs, localized symbol-bearing structures, are embedded—to argue that simmballs do not literally push the careenium around but are mediated by an interpreting layer, rendering symbolic causation epiphenomenal. Lay out the correspondence table—driver, car, road, fuel, and mechanic—mapped onto the careenium and simmballs. Then identify where the mapping breaks, especially whether the careenium contains a separate interpreter or whether interpretation is intrinsic to substrate dynamics. Note which features of the car case—intentional agency, external design, energetic separation—the analogy quietly imports and the careenium lacks. Finally, does the conclusion that symbols fail to push the substrate survive these disanalogies? If not, propose a better analogy—such as chemical signaling or grammatical structure in a physical medium—for the relation between simmballs and careenium.
