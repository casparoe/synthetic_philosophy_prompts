I have been thinking on my own, with no academic background, about whether climate change gives people like me a reason not to have children. In these conversations a single sentence keeps being used to shut the concern down: "You can't harm someone by bringing them into existence." The speaker always means that if I have a child now, that exact child would never have existed had I made a different choice, so I have not made anyone worse off. I feel sure that sentence hides several different claims, but I cannot tell which ones are tautologies, which are serious philosophical premises, and which are simply false. Could you lay out the precise theses that slogan might stand for — metaphysical claims about personal identity across possible choices, logical or conceptual claims about what "harming" requires, normative claims about duties to people who might never exist, and any other distinct interpretations you see — and for each say whether it is obvious, defensible but controversial, or mistaken? Please present this as a menu of readings I can take back to the discussion, not as one correct analysis. In particular, I want to know which of these readings actually supports using the slogan to dismiss climate-related worries about procreation, and which readings leave the argument open to other objections — for example, objections about the overall quality of lives that will be lived, or about wronging actual future people regardless of which specific individuals they turn out to be.
