I've been reading a bit about illusionism on phenomenal consciousness and I think I'm mostly on board with the idea that there's no inner movie, just cognitive representations that misrepresent themselves as having qualitative feel. But I want to push on a specific asymmetry that I haven't seen discussed much. In ordinary perceptual illusions—say the Müller-Lyer lines—there's always a correction path where you measure and compare, and the illusion is revealed as a discrepancy between appearance and reality. With consciousness, if it's an illusion, the 'correction' can only ever be another conceptual belief, never a direct perceptual revision, because there's nothing outside the system to check against. So my version of illusionism holds that the error isn't in experience itself but in the theoretical self-model that interprets access-related processes as phenomenal.

Specifcally, I want to know what would actually count against this view. Not just the standard objections about self-defeat or the hard problem, but concrete defeaters: for instance, if there turned out to be a neural marker that tracks qualitative character precisely and can't be reduced to access-related dynamics; if the hypothetical case of a behaviourally identical being that completely lacks introspective access turned out to be conceptually incoherent rather than merely empirically absent; or if the theory-error versus experience-error distinction collapsed in other domains of self-knowledge. I'd also like you to rank these from fatal to merely awkward, and to say honestly whether illusionism has been qualified into unfalsifiablity—if every finding gets interpreted as just more misrepresenation, is that a genuine problem for a view of this kind?
