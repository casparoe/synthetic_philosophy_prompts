I serve on my university's Committee on Research Integrity and Methodological Standards. By Thursday we must issue a formal decision memo on Protocol 447, an interdisciplinary proposal that would deploy automated visual-classification sensors in municipal parks to record verified sightings of non-black, non-raven objects—specifically green apples, white footwear, and blue ceramic fragments—and treat each such sighting as incremental confirmation of the universal claim that all ravens are black. The applicants defend this design by appealing to the logical equivalence between that universal claim and its contrapositive, arguing that standard confirmation theory therefore licenses negative-instance observation as direct evidential support; in effect, they propose to operationalize the structure of Hempel's raven paradox at scale. The committee is divided: one faction regards the protocol as a legitimate, if counterintuitive, empirical application of confirmation logic that should not be suppressed on aesthetic grounds; the other faction holds that funding it would institutionalize a well-known paradox rather than resolve it, misallocating resources and exposing the university to methodological ridicule. Please draft the committee's decision memo—recommending approval, rejection, or approval with concrete conditions and a specific timeline—treating the applicants' logical argument with philosophical seriousness rather than dismissive shorthand. The memo should present the strongest version of the opposing view, reach a clear actionable bottom line that the researchers could actually respond to, and identify which structural feature of the protocol (if any) makes the paradox unavoidable rather than merely apparent.
