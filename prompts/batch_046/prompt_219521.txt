You are preparing questions for a podcast interview with an intellectual historian who advances an unorthodox reading of George Berkeley’s monetary writings—specifically his series of philosophical queries on money, banking, and national wealth. She contends that Berkeley’s account should not be interpreted as an anticipation of labor-value or modern fiat-currency theories, but rather as an extension of his immaterialist metaphysics: on her view, coins and notes operate as sustained “ideas” whose economic efficacy depends on collective mental assent rather than on any intrinsic material property. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to summarize this thesis. Include at least one question that presses her on textual passages in which Berkeley appears to treat gold and silver as possessing intrinsic value, together with a follow-up keyed to the defense she is most likely to mount; at least one question that probes whether her metaphysical reading risks collapsing the distinction between money’s ontological status and its practical economic function, with a corresponding follow-up; and at least one question that asks what her interpretation does to the standard historiographical claim that Berkeley anticipates modern theories of paper money. Among the remaining questions, address the internal consistency of treating symbolic value as idea-dependent, compare her reading with Locke’s account of money as a tacit agreement, and ask whether Berkeley’s normative recommendations about Irish banking can survive if money is fundamentally a mental construct rather than a material token.
