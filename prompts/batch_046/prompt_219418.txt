I keep running into this thing called the paradox of deontology—like if you have a perfect duty not to kill then you cant push the fat guy off the bridge even to save five people—but I think I've found a hidden contradiction. People say deontology uses agent-relative permissions so you can let harm happen but not cause it, but then they also talk about moral residue, where you feel guilty even when you let the harm happen. If feeling guilty is just caring about the bad result, then doesnt that mean agent-relative permissions collapse back into weighing outcomes? And I read that Kant said you cant have a direct duty to be happy, so does that mean being miserable is actually a kind of moral achievement? I feel like deontology tries to ban consequences but then sneaks them back in through guilt and emotional rules, but maybe Im just mixing up Williams with Kant and not seeing the real paradox.
