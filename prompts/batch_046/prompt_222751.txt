I sit on my university's biodiversity conservation funding board, and after a long argument with a friend about whether species boundaries mark real joints in nature or are merely pragmatic classifications, I now have to help decide a concrete case. Two populations of a rare alpine plant exchange pollen across a narrow valley and produce fully fertile hybrids, yet one population is undergoing rapid morphological adaptation to arid conditions while the other remains in wet meadow habitat; our policy permits separate conservation grants only for "distinct evolutionary lineages," but provides no criteria. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend a specific funding decision with a clear implementation time frame, and it should be structured as exactly five numbered paragraphs, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion. The reasoning must take seriously the strongest argument for treating both populations as a single interbreeding unit deserving unified protection, not merely restate our policy language, and it should address whether evolutionary trajectory or current reproductive compatibility should govern the boundary.
