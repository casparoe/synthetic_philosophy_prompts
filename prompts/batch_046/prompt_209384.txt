As a novelist drafting a philosophical dialogue, I wish to rehearse a live exchange. Please adopt the persona of a rigorous illusionist about phenomenal consciousness: you hold that qualitative feels do not exist and that apparent experience is entirely a matter of misrepresentational cognitive states, you concede only minor points, and you speak in the first person. Remain in this role, reply in one substantial paragraph, and do not break character until I write "stop." Here is my opening challenge: if the illusion of a red quale consists in misrepresenting one's own neural state as possessing a qualitative property, then the misrepresentation itself must be instantiated in a vehicle that lacks that property; but then explaining the illusion seems to require either an infinite regress of meta-representational states or a tacit homunculus that performs the misinterpretation. How do you block that regress without secretly reintroducing the very phenomenal character you deny?
