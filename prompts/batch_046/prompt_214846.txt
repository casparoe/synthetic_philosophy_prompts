I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I'm trying to understand where a line of reasoning I constructed collapses into absurdity. The idea started with distributed-system branching, but I think I've over-extended a quantum-mechanics thought experiment.

Here is the argument:

(1) In Everettian quantum mechanics, a measurement-like interaction produces decoherent branches corresponding to each possible outcome.
(2) A quantum suicide device ties a lethal mechanism to a binary quantum event—say, spin-up kills and spin-down spares.
(3) From a third-person view, both outcomes occur in separate branches.
(4) From a first-person view, consciousness can only continue in branches where the observer survives.
(5) So after activation, the observer should subjectively anticipate finding themselves alive with certainty, regardless of the objective branching weights.
(6) Now consider any ordinary fatal risk—e.g., a car crash. At the microscopic level, the outcome depends on quantum-level events that could branch into survival and death.
(7) By parity with (4) and (5), first-person anticipation should always select the survival branches, for any risk whatsoever.
(8) Therefore, quantum-branch reasoning eliminates all rational motivation for risk avoidance, since death is never in one's subjective future.
(9) This is clearly wrong; avoiding risks is rational.
(10) So one of (1) through (8) must be erroneous.

The conclusion that risk avoidance is never rational proves far too much. I want to preserve the intuition behind (4)—that first-person experience tracks survival—because it seems to capture why quantum suicide feels different from ordinary gambling. Which step should I reject? Is the error in (6), which treats macroscopic thermodynamic accidents as if they were clean quantum measurements? Or in (7), which applies branch-relative self-selection outside deliberately designed binary devices? Or is there a hidden equivocation between branch-relative identity and expected-utility weighting? If I reject the problematic step, what does it cost—does it undermine the original quantum suicide reasoning, or can the damage be contained?
