For a workshop on rational agency I am drafting my view on epistemic self-trust and need a consistency check before presenting. I hold: (a) the Löbian obstacle is genuine—an agent with sufficient logical strength cannot consistently maintain universal self-trust of the form "if I derive X, then X is true" without thereby being able to derive arbitrary X; (b) this obstacle applies to artificial reasoning systems as much as to human mathematicians, since it depends on logical strength rather than consciousness; (c) reflective equilibrium is the correct method for resolving tensions among our epistemic commitments, including commitments about our own reliability; (d) the obstacle undermines only global self-trust, not domain-restricted trust, because Löb's schema applies to the unrestricted case and a locally confined trust-schema does not generate the same derivability; (e) nevertheless, selecting which domains are safe requires global meta-reasoning about one's own reliability, which is precisely the kind of reasoning the obstacle blocks, so domain-restriction is unstable. Are these jointly tenable? In particular, does (d) survive the pressure from (e), and does adopting (c) commit me to revise (a) or (b) rather than (d)? Rank the tensions by severity, distinguish pairs that only appear to conflict, and identify the least costly single revision that restores coherence.
