I'm drafting a one-semester independent-study proposal for my philosophy-of-mind committee, and I need to turn a vague intuition into a tractable project. Everyone knows the standard framing of Libet's readiness-potential studies: neural activity precedes conscious awareness of the decision by a few hundred milliseconds, which seems to threaten conscious free will, and the debate usually splits between those who salvage a "veto" or "free won't" and those who reduce that veto to another unconscious mechanism. I think both sides miss a deeper conceptual problem. They both assume what I will call a "punctate" model of agency, in which conscious control is a discrete, point-like event—either an initiation or a block—located at a specific moment in the causal chain. But if we take seriously an "extended" or "distributed" model, where conscious control is a temporally stretched process of modulation rather than a single mental act, then the empirical finding may not undermine agency at all; instead, it may reveal that the experimental paradigm itself presupposes the very punctate picture it claims to test. Write a proposal of about two pages that states the precise question: does the neuroscience of voluntary action force us to reject the punctate model, and if we replace it with a distributed one, do we dissolve the free-will problem or merely relocate it? Explain why the veto-versus-mechanism literature leaves this question untouched. Describe the method: a conceptual analysis of "control" in action theory combined with a critical examination of the task structure in Libet-type experiments, asking which model of agency the design implicitly encodes. Specify what success looks like—perhaps demonstrating that the standard metaphysical conclusions are circular relative to the experimental framing—and what failure looks like, such as discovering that both models face an identical neural challenge. Finally, assess scope honestly: can this be completed in one semester without sliding into empirical neuroscience, and what limits should I set to keep it philosophical?
