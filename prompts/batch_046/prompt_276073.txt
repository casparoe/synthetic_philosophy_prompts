In seminars on the socialist calculation debate, the market price system is often likened to a distributed telecommunications network: individual producers and consumers function as nodes, prices serve as compressed signals conveying scarcity and preference, and information travels laterally without any central authority having to gather the full underlying data. Defenders of Hayek and Mises deploy this to suggest that central planning is analogous to replacing that network with a single switchboard compelled to read the full content of every packet, whereas without genuine market exchange there are no authentic signals—only administrative noise. Proponents of Lange's iterative model counter that algorithmic adjustment can replicate the signalling function, essentially treating the socialist planner as a peer-to-peer protocol that updates prices round by round without requiring private ownership of the means of production.

I want you to audit how far this telecommunications analogy actually holds. Provide an explicit correspondence table—mapping nodes, signals, bandwidth constraints, latency, and the central switchboard onto economic agents, price mechanisms, and planning institutions—then separate the disanalogies that undermine the anti-planning conclusion from those that do not. In particular, address where the model smuggles in the assumption that economic information is non-rival and instantly transmissible like data packets, when much of the knowledge Hayek emphasises is tacit, embodied, and context-dependent. Does the peer-to-peer counter-analogy survive once we observe that Lange's iterations still depend on a central authority to set the prices for each successive round, and that the 'bandwidth' of price signals may be too narrow to coordinate all the dispersed knowledge that markets allegedly manage? Finally, say whether the relaxed conclusion—that markets are irreplaceable information processors—was genuinely supported by the analogy, or whether the breakdowns point toward a different framing altogether, perhaps one drawn from evolutionary biology rather than information theory.
