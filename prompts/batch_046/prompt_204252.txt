hey so I'm putting together materials for a workshop on corruption and gift-giving across cultures — we're looking at whether Western transparency norms are just one local moral economy among others. I want a decision tree / taxonomy that actually sorts people by what they believe about specific cases, not by which label they pick. The audience is grad students in pol sci and philosophy who don't need a bibliography, just the conceptual map.

Start with three concrete split-cases: (1) a local water official takes yams and a chicken from villagers right after fixing their pipe, in a region with basically no welfare state; (2) a pharma rep pays for a doctor's conference trip right after that doctor approves the rep's drug for the national formulary; (3) a party patron hires his niece's campaign worker for a civil service job, no explicit contract, but everyone knows the deal. The tree should branch on concrete claims: does corruption require breaking a written rule, or just violating the implicit obligations of a role? Does a gift become corrupt only if there's an explicit quid pro quo, or if it creates an unacknowledged dependency? Does the absence of alternative institutions (no welfare, no merit exams) change whether the exchange counts as corruption or as social insurance? And does judging it corrupt already assume an individualist, contract-based moral psychology?

The leaves should include: strict universalist (Western impartiality applies everywhere), institutional functionalist (corrupt only if it breaks the actual function of the office), relational moral economy view (gift and obligation are inseperable from politics, calling them corruption is a category error), and maybe one unnamed combo that nobody actually holds. Build the tree from the logical combinations first — like if you say yes to rule-breaking but no to dependency, where does that land? — then map the named positions onto them. Note any branch pairs your tree can't really seperate, and flag any empty branch: is it incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored? Keep it to maybe 6-8 decision nodes max, casual tone is fine, I probably misspelled something in my own draft.
