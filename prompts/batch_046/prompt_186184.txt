I am developing a research framework around what I call the Darwin–Turing inversion: the structural parallel by which adaptive design emerges through selection without a designer's comprehension, and intelligent behaviour emerges through formal computation without semantic understanding. Rather than treating these as separate analogies, I want to examine whether they share a deeper logical form in which competence is not merely unexplained but positively requires the absence of corresponding comprehension at the operating level.

Given that framing, which open questions should take priority? I am particularly weighing three directions: whether large language models constitute a genuine third instantiation of this inversion or only its surface simulation; whether evolutionary epistemology can survive if knowledge evolves without any knowing subject; and whether the inversion erodes the traditional epistemic privilege granted to first-person understanding in philosophy of mind. If you were directing a small group with three years and no experimental lab, how would you rank these for effort, and what criteria — tractability, dependency relations among the questions, or practical stakes — justify that ordering? I want a defended agenda rather than a survey.
