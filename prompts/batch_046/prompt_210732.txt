hey so i've been chewing on dennett's quining qualia argument—specifically that bit where chase and sanborn complain neurophysiologists are using "qualia" for properties that aren't the ones philosophers mean. i tried to formalize the anti-qualia move and now it proves way too much, but i keep losing the thread.

(1) call a property genuinely real only if its instantiation makes some difference that is detectible in principle, even if only indirectly.

(2) qualia are defined as intrinsically private and ineffable: there's no possible third-party check on whether your red-quale matches mine, or even whether you have one.

(3) so by (1) qualia fail the reality test. quine them.

(4) but apply the same standard to the token belief that it's raining, right now, in me. the relation me-believing-raining-at-t is also accessable only to me in the first person; no one else can literally stand in exactly that indexical relation to this mental token.

(5) therefore beliefs also fail (1).

(6) so the quining strategy doesn't just kill raw feels; it kills every mental state with first-person ownership—basically all of mind.

(7) full-blown eliminativism about mind is self-refuting, and nobody in this debate actually signs up for it.

(8) so something in (1)-(3) is broken, but i'm not sure which part.

i think the hidden slip might be between "undetectable by others because it's an intrinsic quality" and "undetectable by others because it's indexically owned." but maybe the argument is actually valid and we just have to bite a bullet? can you tell me which step is the weak link, and what giving it up costs? please also separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who actually work on this—don't present one side as the consensus, since i want to see where the live disagreement is, not just get a victory lap.
