I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of mind working on first-person authority, and I've constructed the following argument for a workshop on the cogito that now strikes me as suspicious: it seems either to dissolve Descartes' result into empirical neuroscience or to inflate it into a proof available to any self-modeling system. I want to know where the error lies, if any, and which premise is least costly to abandon.

(1) Descartes establishes in the Second Meditation that whenever he thinks, he exists, and that this is known by immediate intuition rather than inference or observation.

(2) If the justification for I exist depends only on the occurrence of thinking, and not on any proprietary mode of first-person access, then the conditions for knowing this fact are purely objective: they consist in the presence of a mental event.

(3) But mental events have neural correlates that are, in principle, detectable by an external observer with sufficient technology.

(4) Therefore, an observer who detects those correlates has access to the same occurrence that justifies the subject's existence-claim, and hence can justify that subject exists with the same immediacy—mutatis mutandis—that the subject justifies I exist.

(5) To block this, one might insist that the cogito requires an irreducibly indexical component: only the occupant of the first-person standpoint can apply I to herself.

(6) If so, the cogito proves only that a thinker exists corresponding to this particular indexical use, not that she is a substantial res cogitans with an essential nature or persistent identity.

(7) Yet Descartes treats the cogito as establishing precisely that he is a thinking substance—something whose essence is thought and which can survive the cessation of particular mental acts.

(8) So we face a trilemma: either (a) occurrence is not externally detectable in a justification-transferring way, (b) there is no special indexical component, or (c) the cogito proves far less than Descartes needs—only an indexed occurrence, not a substantial self.

(9) I am inclined to keep (1) and (5), which forces me toward (c). But I worry I have slipped between epistemic access and metaphysical substance somewhere between (6) and (7)—perhaps a scope shift on exists or a modal slide from exists now to is a substance. Which step should I reject, what does rejecting it cost, and is there a hidden equivocation that makes the argument valid but unsound?
