I've been trying to draft a proposal for this three-month summer grant in phil of language and I'm stuck on framing it. I took a seminar on two-dimensional semantics last semester and got really into how Chalmers and Jackson split intensions into primary and secondary ones, especially for natural kind terms. But I keep wondering if the whole apparatus falls apart when you apply it to thick ethical concepts—like 'cruel' or 'courageous'—where the descriptive and normative bits are supposed to be inseperable.

So here's my half-baked idea: instead of asking whether 2D semantics can accommodate thick concepts (which people have kinda looked at), I want to ask whether treating them in the 2D framework forces us to split the primary intension itself into a 'descriptive core' and an 'evaluative frame,' and whether that split is stable across epistemic worlds. If it's not stable, then maybe the necessary a posteriori story for thick concepts collapses, because the primary intension is already doing normative work that shifts with context. The proposal should lay out this question precisely, say why the standard debates about direct reference and 2D don't settle it, and propose an approach—probably formalizing primary intensions for thick concepts in a modified 2D model and testing for instability. I need it to say what a positive result vs. failure looks like, and be realistic about what can actually be done in three months part-time. Oh, and it should be pitched to a small philosophy committee that knows Kaplan's framework but hasn't seen thick concepts pushed through it this way.
