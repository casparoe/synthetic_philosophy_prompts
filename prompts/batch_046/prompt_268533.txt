Consider four national myths organized around military defeat or collective withdrawal: the American Confederate Lost Cause, the Masada narrative as integrated into modern Israeli state identity, the Serbian Kosovo Field tradition centered on the 1389 battle, and the British Dunkirk spirit surrounding the 1940 evacuation. For each, determine whether its redemptive structure operates primarily through deferred eschatology—treating the defeat as an unresolved debt that demands a future political or spiritual settlement—through synchronous transvaluation—redefining victory in moral terms at the very moment of military failure so that no temporal gap remains—or through cyclical repetition—freezing the event as an unfinished obligation that binds later generations to reenact rather than resolve the loss. Then rank the four from the one most dependent on deferred eschatology to the one most dependent on synchronous transvaluation, placing cyclical cases in between; for each rank, provide one sentence identifying the dominant mechanism and explaining why the other two structures fit that case less well.
