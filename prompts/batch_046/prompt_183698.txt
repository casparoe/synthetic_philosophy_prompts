A student in my graduate seminar on Indian philosophy offered the following summary of Nāgārjuna’s catuskoti, drawn from a recent popular essay: Nāgārjuna treats every metaphysical claim as falling into one of four exhaustive logical positions—being, non-being, both, or neither—and demonstrates that each collapses into contradiction, thereby rejecting all four wholesale. From this he derives that reality lacks any characteristic whatsoever (niḥsvabhāva), and that dependent origination is therefore only a pragmatic fiction with no conventional ontological weight. The student concludes that Madhyamaka is essentially a form of global semantic nihilism, closer in spirit to Pyrrhonian epoché than to any constructive metaphysics, and that the doctrine of the emptiness of emptiness is merely a self-canceling gesture that silences all discourse rather than grounding it.

Please evaluate whether this is a fair summary. I want you to separate, precisely, what accurately captures Nāgārjuna’s dialectical method—especially the targeted negation of svabhāva rather than of all conventional designation—from the points that are either common simplifications or subtle misrepresentations. I am particularly interested in whether treating the fourth lemma (neither being nor non-being) as a substantive metaphysical thesis that Nāgārjuna endorses, rather than as a further object of deconstruction, distorts his argument; and whether the inference that Madhyamaka entails a total suspension of conventional truth (samvṛtisatya) actually follows from the accurate parts. Do not provide bibliographic recommendations; I need an internal coherence audit that assumes only familiarity with the core texts and their standard interpretations.
