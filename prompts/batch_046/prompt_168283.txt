Write a short story exploring whether prosperity and public order can justify authoritarian governance, set in an imagined polity called the Meridian Consensus where a centralized authority has eliminated visible crime and delivered steady economic growth by systematically suppressing not only political opposition but also the private recording of collective grief. Your protagonist should be an internal auditor who uncovers that the regime's celebrated stability index is calculated by treating the emotional labor of enforced silence as a negative cost rather than an unpriced externality, and that the prosperity figures depend on this hidden subtraction. Through the auditor's discovery and a single conversation with a citizen who genuinely prefers the safety to the burden of remembrance, examine whether authentic popular satisfaction with order can ever legitimate the rule that manufactures it, particularly when the suffering that sustains the satisfaction is deliberately made invisible. Present your response as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a letter or email.
