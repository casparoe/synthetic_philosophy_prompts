Construct a thought experiment addressing which computations matter morally and where the boundaries of sentience lie. Design a realistic, near-technology scenario—no exotic physics, no demons, no perfect predictors, no substrates violating known engineering constraints—in which a single algorithm runs in two conditions that differ only in the presence or absence of what I will call a unitary causal envelope: a single continuous physical boundary within which every intermediate computational state is directly causally available to subsequent states without representational mediation or synchronization delay, versus an algorithmically identical implementation distributed across physically separated nodes that exchange representations with non-negligible latency and share no physical boundary. Specify the algorithm concretely: a closed-loop predictive-coding process that maps nociceptive signals onto affective-state representations, running for a fixed duration. Describe each condition in no more than one paragraph, holding substrate constant (synthetic and non-biological in both) and holding functional outputs, information content, and behavioral results identical.

The scenario must generate the following pattern of verdicts from three precisely characterized accounts of moral status:

- Functional-causal continuity (F): grants moral consideration when the unitary envelope is present, because the computation realizes one spatiotemporally continuous causal chain; denies it when the envelope is absent, because representational mediation between nodes breaks the required continuity, regardless of algorithmic equivalence.
- Biological-organismic chauvinism (B): denies moral consideration in both conditions, since neither involves a living, homeostatic biological organism; but in the envelope-absent case it further denies on grounds of distributed non-organismic structure, so that in that case B agrees with F in denial, while in the envelope-present case B alone denies while F grants.
- Integrated-information maximization (I): grants moral consideration in both conditions, on the grounds that the system maximizes integrated information (phi) within its causal boundary—within the single boundary in the envelope case, and across the synchronized network in the distributed case—and that this irreducible causal integration is preserved or even enhanced by the distributed links, which F’s local continuity constraint erroneously fragments.

After presenting the scenario, provide: (1) the reasoning each account gives for its verdict in each condition; (2) identification of the unitary causal envelope as the structural feature that makes I peel away from F and B in the distributed case; (3) an analysis of whether removing that feature—collapsing the distributed implementation back into a single envelope—collapses the disagreement back into a standard substrate-independent functionalism versus biological-chauvinism dispute; and (4) an honest statement of how a defender of the account that comes out worst would attack the case, specifically whether they would reject the claim that phi is preserved across distributed synchronization links, reject the relevance of causal continuity to moral status, or challenge the equivalence of the two implementations. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the argument should stand on its own.
