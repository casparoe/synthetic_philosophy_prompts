Design a decision-tree taxonomy for a seminar handout that sorts philosophical positions on vagueness by how they answer concrete cases, not by the labels people adopt. Start with a root split on whether borderline cases involve a hidden sharp boundary unknown to us, or genuinely lack one. From there, branch by commitments about practical reasoning in the borderline zone—specifically whether one should treat the case as falling under the predicate, suspend judgment, or apply a precautionary rule—using a running example of bureaucratic residency revocation in which permits are cancelled one by one with no identifiable transition point to non-citizenship. Add a further split on whether higher-order vagueness is benign or generates a vicious regress. Each leaf should carry the name of the view (epistemicism, standard supervaluationism, degrees-of-truth theory, ontic vagueness, or unnamed if unoccupied) together with one sentence identifying its most awkward commitment regarding the residency scenario. Note any logically possible combinations that have no defenders and briefly diagnose whether the gap stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or neglect. The instrument should let a reader locate their own stance by answering the case-based questions without prior knowledge of the technical vocabulary.
