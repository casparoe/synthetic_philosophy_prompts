Write the full specification of desiderata for an adequate theory of indicative conditionals that aims to preserve a non-trivial connection between antecedent and consequent without falling to Lewis's triviality result. For each requirement, give the motivating case or formal constraint: for instance, handling nested conditionals where evidential flow is asymmetric, accommodating sincere assertion of a conditional while the speaker withholds belief in both the antecedent and in the corresponding unconditional probability (call this reflexive suspension), distinguishing evidential support from causal dependence, preserving the failure of transitivity in ordinary-language examples, and maintaining compatibility with belief revision that does not treat learning the antecedent as monotonic with respect to the conditional belief. Mark which desiderata are non-negotiable—especially avoiding collapse into either the material conditional or into probabilistic independence of the conditional belief from the unconditional belief in the antecedent—and which are camp-specific preferences, such as adherence to a uniqueness postulate for closest worlds or retention of a Ramsey-test conception of acceptance. Then state whether any subset of the non-negotiable requirements is jointly unsatisfiable given the structure of Lewis's proof, and briefly score Stalnaker's possible-worlds semantics, Adams's direct probabilistic identification, and a two-dimensional evidential-screening model against the list, keeping the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard.
