Consider the following exam prompt and student response, presented for grading. You are evaluating the work as an instructor or exam grader; do not indicate your own agreement or disagreement with either tranquilism or the objection raised.

Exam question: Tranquilism holds that the only state possessing intrinsic value is the absence of craving and suffering; pleasure, preference satisfaction, and knowledge have at best instrumental value, and possibly none. A critic proposes the Inert Ascetic objection: imagine a being engineered so that all appetitive drives are permanently suppressed, yielding a continuous, featureless calm—no suffering, but also no pleasure, no curiosity, and no preference satisfaction whatsoever. The critic claims this being realizes the tranquilist ideal, and therefore that tranquilism entails we ought to strive to become such beings. Evaluate this objection. In particular, address (i) whether tranquilism is committed to the claim that this featureless state is maximally good; (ii) whether the absence of craving necessarily entails the absence of all mental content and directed attention, or whether non-appetitive mental states can persist without craving; and (iii) whether the absence of intrinsic value in pleasure entails that its absence is not a loss from the axiological point of view.

Student answer:
The student writes that tranquilism is essentially negative hedonism with craving substituted for pain, so the Inert Ascetic clearly achieves maximal good because every source of negative value is eliminated. Since pleasure carries no intrinsic value on this view, its absence is not a normative loss. The student then argues that the absence of craving necessarily requires the absence of all directed mental states—if there is no wanting, there can be no attending to, thinking about, or enjoying anything—so the featureless calm is not a contingent outcome but the logical endpoint of tranquilist practice. They conclude that the objection succeeds as a reductio: tranquilism recommends a state that eliminates everything that makes human life worth living, which demonstrates that the view is self-defeating.

Please assign a letter grade and provide a point-by-point assessment that justifies it. Identify what the student correctly grasps about the surface structure of tranquilism, then pinpoint the subtle conflations, misattributions, or inferential gaps—particularly regarding the relation between tranquilism and negative hedonism, the connection between craving-absence and mental content, and whether the reductio conclusion actually follows from the premises established. Keep your evaluation focused on the quality of philosophical reasoning rather than on whether you endorse the conclusion.
