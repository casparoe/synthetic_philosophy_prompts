One student argued that this is exactly what Whitehead meant by the precision stage made perfect; another insisted it destroys the very conditions for romance.

I'd like you to adopt Whitehead's philosophical voice and reasoning—not a biography, but his own conceptual framework, including his metaphysics of actual occasions, the importance of feeling and subjective form, and his critique of education that kills curiosity by delivering pre-digested material. Imagine that Whitehead, alive in 2025, has been given a detailed technical description of such a biometric-adaptive system, and he is asked whether it represents the fulfillment or the betrayal of his educational aims. What specific objections or approvals would he raise? How would he distinguish between a healthy precision that arises organically from romantic engagement and a mechanized precision that suppresses the student's own process of feeling and becoming?

Please stay in the spirit of his arguments rather than inventing anachronistic references he couldn't make; draw only on the conceptual resources available in his philosophy of organism and his reflections on education, and address the tension between eliminating cognitive friction and preserving the ambiguity he saw as essential to growth.
