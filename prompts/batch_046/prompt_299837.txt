I am a doctoral student in metaethics and technology governance serving on an institutional review board that audits normative constraints proposed for autonomous decision systems. We repeatedly encounter claims — that a system “ought to minimise predictive error,” or that it “must respect autonomy” — advanced as binding without any identifiable reasoner behind them: no individual agent whose practical reasoning generates the constraint, no stakeholder collective that endorses it as its own reason, and no institutional practice that incorporates it. I want a reusable decision procedure the board can apply to any proposed normative constraint to determine whether it is properly anchored to a reasoner (individual, collective, or institutional) or whether it is free-floating. The procedure should specify the diagnostic questions in strict sequence, define what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, indicate where it terminates with a rejection, and flag the points where the issue is irreducibly evaluative rather than factual. It should distinguish first-order orphanhood — no originator can be traced — from second-order orphanhood — originators exist but have withdrawn endorsement — which I take to be a distinct failure mode though the board may dispute its severity. Apply the procedure to three proposals: (1) a constraint that the system must never deceive users, justified solely by the bare claim that deception is wrong; (2) a constraint derived from a patient-advocacy group that dissolved before the system was built, which had endorsed informed-consent protocols now proposed as binding; (3) a constraint that the system should optimise for “flourishing” with no further specification of whose flourishing or by what metric. The response should be in British English. Please state how confident you are in the procedure’s outputs and single out the point about which you are least certain — particularly whether second-order orphanhood ought to count as free-floating. Present the core procedure and the three worked cases in prose, then provide a brief structured summary afterwards in JSON with the named fields: case_name, orphanhood_type (none / first_order / second_order / unresolved), procedure_outcome (anchored / rejected / judgment_handoff), judgment_call_flag (true / false), and confidence_note.
