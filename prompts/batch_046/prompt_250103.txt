Here's an argument my study group cooked up about Nāgārjuna's tetralemma and Madhyamaka emptiness and it feels like it should collapse the whole view but I can't find the exit. (1) The catuṣkoṭi rejects all four horns — exists, doesn't exist, both, neither — for anything with svabhāva. (2) So Nāgārjuna says all phenomena are empty (śūnya), including emptiness itself. (3) He also claims dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda) is identical to emptiness, not a seperate thing. (4) If they're identical, then applying the tetralemma to one applies to both. (5) So causation itself lacks svabhāva — there's no inherent nature to cause-and-effect. (6) But if causation has no inherent nature, then there's no real basis to seperate conventional appearance from ultimate reality; they collapse into one undifferentiated emptiness. (7) That means Madhyamaka can't explain why things appear to exist at all without smuggling in real causation it just emptied. (8) So either it's nihilism or it's incoherent. Where does this go wrong? I'd rather keep (3) and (5) if I have to choose. Please answer in British English — and no need for citations, just work with the concepts.
