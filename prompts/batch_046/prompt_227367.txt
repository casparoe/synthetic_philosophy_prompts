You are serving as a teaching assistant grading responses for an upper-division exam in the sociology of culture. The exam asks students to apply Max Weber’s framework for cultural causation, particularly his analysis of the relationship between ascetic Protestant ethics and the emergence of modern capitalism, to a constructed hypothetical case.

Exam question:
Consider a hypothetical society in which an ascetic religious community adopts rigorous double-entry bookkeeping not out of salvation anxiety, but purely to ensure communal transparency and prevent corruption. Over generations, this practice evolves into a fully rationalized system of capital accounting that drives regional economic growth. Would Weber regard the religious ethos and the accounting system as standing in an elective affinity (Wahlverwandtschaft) in this case? In your answer, distinguish between value-rational (wertrational) and instrumentally rational (zweckrational) action, and explain how Weber’s concept of adequacy of meaning (Sinnadäquanz) relates to causal explanation in the cultural sciences. Be specific about why the absence of salvation anxiety matters for the causal claim.

Student answer:
The hypothetical case differs from Weber’s actual argument because in The Protestant Ethic, bookkeeping was adopted not for transparency but as a methodical proof of grace. In Weber’s typology, action is value-rational when pursued for its own sake according to an ethical value, and instrumentally rational when it calculates means to an end. Here, the community initially adopts bookkeeping for a communal value—transparency—which is value-rational. Over time, however, the practice detaches from that value and is pursued purely for economic efficiency, becoming instrumentally rational.

Weber would deny that there is a true elective affinity here, because elective affinity requires that the economic practice and the religious value share an internal logical consistency—what Weber calls adequacy of meaning. Since the accounting system eventually serves capitalist growth, which bears no intrinsic connection to communal transparency, the two spheres lack the necessary congruence. The religious ethos is therefore only a background condition, not a cause of the specific capitalist form.

That said, Weber’s methodology allows us to treat the initial adoption as causally relevant. By comparing this society with one lacking the transparency ethic—using an ideal type of rational capitalism—we can see that the religious value shaped the particular path of rationalization, even if it did not determine the outcome. Thus, culture operates here as an indirect cause: it sets the initial orientation, but material conditions take over. This avoids both materialist determinism and the error of treating culture as a physical force.

Please assign a score out of 10 or a letter grade, and provide specific, point-by-point comments that justify it. Assess whether the student correctly applies the distinction between value-rational and instrumental action, whether they understand elective affinity, and whether their use of Sinnadäquanz and ideal-type methodology is accurate. Do not summarize your own general view of Weber; keep the assessment focused on the answer’s specific claims and reasoning steps. Identify any conflations, misattributions, or steps that do not follow, even if they are subtle.
