I'm presenting to a joint philosophy-of-logic and AI-safety seminar my claim that Hofstadter's MU-puzzle and the jump-out-of-system move (JOOTS) expose a structural limit in fully formalized value-learning: an alignment procedure that tries to internalize its own meta-level revision rules cannot, operating solely within those rules, justify the leap to a revised framework, which implies that open-ended moral learning in artificial agents requires extra-formal institutional scaffolding that the system itself can never fully validate. The audience is mostly formal epistemologists, decision theorists, and researchers in formal alignment who take reflective oracles and logical induction as serious responses to self-reference, who treat Hofstadter as popular science rather than rigorous argument, and who are deeply skeptical of substituting "social process" for formal guarantees. Prepare my Q&A defense sheet: the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased as a formalist or decision theorist would phrase them, ranked by damage, with what I should concede up front, the single reply that must land for the thesis to survive, a brief reply and weakness-note for each objection, and—explicitly—the question I am hoping nobody asks, together with my best two-minute answer and the weakest point in my own position that I should defend rather than hope they miss. Do not ask for readings or citations; frame every objection in terms of formalizability, reflective closure, and whether the analogy to the MU-puzzle actually carries any technical weight.
