In my democratic-theory reading group we've been stuck for months on whether populist appeals to 'the will of the people' are claims about a pre-existing collective intention, or just rhetorical moves that constuct the people retroactively. The constuctivists say there's no 'people' prior to the claim itself; the realists say deny that and you deny democracy any ground. Everyone repeats the same moves — Brexit slogans, Latin American examples, maybe a nod to Schmitt — and nobody moves an inch. My hunch is the stalemate isn't about politics at all, but about whether collective intentionality requires shared mental states, and both sides are smuggling in metaphysics of group agency without admitting it. Is that right? Or is it verbal — some treating 'will' as empirical aggregtion of preferences, others as a normative ideal? Or are they answering different questions, one about ontology of groups and one about legitimacy of representation? Diagnose it using what each side actually says, and tell me what would count as progress: a conceptual distinction, an empirical study of movement identity-formation, or admitting the question is ill-posed? Please separate what's fairly settled — like that populism involves an anti-elite framing — from what's genuinely contested, and don't present one camp's metaphysics as the consensus.
