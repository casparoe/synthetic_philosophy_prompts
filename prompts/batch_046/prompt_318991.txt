I'm currently taking a course on governance and law, and a discussion with friends last week turned into a genuine disagreement I haven't been able to resolve. The situation involves a small residential co-op I belong to, governed by a charter adopted in the 1970s. For decades, residents have informally used common spaces for tutoring collectives and shared meal programs—activities never formally approved but widely understood to be part of the community's purpose. Recently, a board member with a personal conflict against a long-time resident discovered an obscure charter clause permitting eviction for "activities incompatible with residential purpose." She is now demanding that the board invoke this clause to remove the resident for running a free tutoring program in the community room. The board president insists that the clause was lawfully adopted and remains in force, so refusing to apply it would mean substituting the board's private moral preferences for the written rules. A different board member countered that applying the clause in this specific context—where it functions as a vehicle for a private grievance and contradicts the actual practices that have sustained the community—reveals something defective about treating the charter as a genuine system of rules at all.

I'm trying to figure out how to advise the board at tomorrow's meeting. Is the president right that legal validity is one thing and moral wisdom another, and that we risk arbitrariness if we ignore the clause? Or is there a stronger claim that a rule's authority depends on conditions beyond its formal enactment—conditions that might already be violated here? I want to understand whether enforcing the clause preserves the rule of law in our small community, or whether doing so would actually undermine the very idea that we are governed by rules rather than by personal power disguised in legal form. What should I say to the board, and what deeper principle about the relationship between what is enacted and what is genuinely binding is really at stake in this case?
