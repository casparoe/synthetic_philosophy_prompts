Draft the adversarial-collaboration document that a defender of Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility and a defender of hierarchical self-constitution could jointly sign. The first camp maintains that true moral responsibility requires being causa sui in a sense that no finite agent can satisfy: any psychological feature that explains an agent's conduct must itself be explained by prior conditions—genetic, environmental, or psychological—outside the agent's control, generating a regress that only an impossible uncaused cause could terminate. The second camp accepts that bare causal determinism is not enough for responsibility, but holds that an agent can achieve a form of sourcehood through recursive reflective endorsement: a first-order desire becomes genuinely the agent's own when she identifies with it from a higher-order standpoint, and this identification can be stabilized by further reflection, without requiring an origin ex nihilo.

Provide: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about the formal structure of the sourcehood regress, about what hierarchical identification involves, and about which empirical or conceptual findings both sides take as common ground; (2) the precise remaining disagreements, each formulated in wording that the respective camp would accept, arranged in a clear table or structured list—pay particular attention to whether the regress applies with equal force to every level of the hierarchy or only to the base-level psychological traits; (3) at least two cruxes, including whether recursive self-endorsement can genuinely terminate in a self-constituting decision or must always bottom out in arbitrarily given material, and whether determinism undermines responsibility symmetrically across deterministic and indeterministic worlds; (4) for each crux, a concrete case or thought experiment that both sides agree bears on it, together with the explicit prediction each side makes about how the case should be evaluated; and (5) a brief assessment of whether any portion of their disagreement is merely verbal—specifically, whether disputes about "true self" or "real source" mask agreement on the underlying conditions—together with reasoning. Write the document in a formal, collaborative register, not as a dramatized dialogue, and avoid appealing to specific textual passages or bibliographic references.
