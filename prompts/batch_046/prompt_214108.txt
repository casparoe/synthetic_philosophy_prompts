I am preparing the opening material for a graduate seminar on African philosophy, and I would like a poem that can hold the room’s attention before we turn to technical readings on relational personhood. Please write a strict villanelle—exactly nineteen lines, with the ABA rhyme scheme and two refrains that alternate and finally converge in the closing quatrain.

The poem should take seriously the Ubuntu view that personhood is not an intrinsic possession but is constituted through communal relationship: one refrain should articulate the mutual constitution (for instance, that the self is woven into the we), while the second should voice the ontological fragility that follows—namely, that to be severed from the community is not merely to suffer harm but to have one’s very status as a person unravel. Let the form do the philosophical work: the circular returns of the refrains should feel like a tightening loop rather than a comforting repetition, so that the reader experiences relational identity as dependency rather than ornament. In the final quatrain, stage the figure of the expelled or outcast, not as a moral parable but as an ontological rupture, and let the two refrains meet side by side without resolving their tension.

The result should be accurate enough that someone familiar with Ubuntu philosophy recognizes the specific problem of exclusion within a relational ontology, rather than a generic hymn to togetherness. Keep the poem to nineteen lines; do not include commentary, notes, or citations.
