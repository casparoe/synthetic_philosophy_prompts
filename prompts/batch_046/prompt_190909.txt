My claim is that recognition isn't just mutual confirmation but creates what I'm calling a "recognition debt" — an asymmetrical obligation the recognizer owes the recognized, and in the Phenomenology the master doesn't just depend on the slave practicaly but is ontologicaly indebted in a way that makes his self-certainty structurally unstable. I apply this briefly to modern care asymmetries but the core is textual.

They care about textual fidelity and hate when people use Hegel for hot takes.

Prepare me for the objections in roughly the order they'll come.
