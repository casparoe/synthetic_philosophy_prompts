I'm currently enrolled in an advanced seminar on the philosophy of pain and consciousness, and I want to test a line of reasoning I've developed about pain asymbolia. Our discussions have centered on how the condition dissociates the sensory-discriminative profile—localized quality, intensity, bodily mapping—from the affective-motivational profile of unpleasantness and escape motivation. To push the point further, I want to introduce a hypothetical mirror-case that I am calling "inverse asymbolia": a state in which a subject reports overwhelming suffering, dread, and an urgent motivational drive to flee, yet insists that there is absolutely no localized sensory quality—no heat, pressure, sharpness, or bodily locus—corresponding to the distress. Given that standard asymbolia demonstrates sensory pain without badness, and inverse asymbolia demonstrates badness without any sensory substrate, does this not show that "pain" is not a unified natural kind but merely a conventional umbrella for two functionally independent processes? And if that is correct, is the long-standing philosophical dispute over whether pain is essentially bad not actually a category mistake—analogous to asking whether bachelors are necessarily lonely—because there is no single phenomenon about which the predicate "bad" could be essentially true?
