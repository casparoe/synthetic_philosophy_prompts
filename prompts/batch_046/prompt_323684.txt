Please grade the following philosophy exam answer. The exam question was:

"Distinguish between 'local' and 'environmental' Gettier structures. A local structure involves a justified false belief about a specific state of affairs from which a true belief is inferred (e.g., Smith's belief about Jones and the ten coins). An environmental structure involves a subject whose broader epistemic environment is systematically deceptive in some global respect—widespread false testimony, manipulated perceptual conditions, or hidden institutional misinformation—yet the subject forms a particular belief that happens to be true for reasons entirely independent of that deception. First, explain why the JTB analysis fails in both structures despite their different mechanisms. Second, evaluate whether Michael Clark's 'no false lemmas' condition successfully repairs JTB for both types, and identify one specific limitation of applying that condition to environmental structures in particular."

The student's answer was:

"In local Gettier cases like Smith and the job, the subject justifiably believes a false proposition—say, that Jones will get the job—and derives a true belief from it, like that the person with ten coins will get the job. Because the belief is justified and true, JTB seems satisfied, yet it isn't knowledge because the truth is accidental relative to the justification. Environmental Gettier structures are essentially the same mechanism applied globally: the subject lives in a deceptive environment (like fake-barn country) and justifiably believes something false about that environment, then derives a particular true belief that happens to avoid the deception. In both cases, JTB fails because falsehood is essential to the belief's formation.

The best fix is the 'no false lemmas' condition that Gettier proposed in 1963: knowledge is justified true belief that doesn't depend on any false premises. This handles local cases well, since Smith's belief depends on the false premise about Jones. For environmental cases, it also works if we treat the global deception as a false premise in the background. However, one limitation is that in environmental structures, the falsehood isn't actually a premise in the subject's reasoning—it's just background context—so Clark's condition might be too narrow to capture what's going wrong."
