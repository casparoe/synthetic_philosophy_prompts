I'm working through my position on evolutionary explanation and want a consistency check before I present it. Here is where I have landed:

(1) I accept Gould and Lewontin's core claim that evolutionary biology is vulnerable to an overactive adaptationist program: researchers routinely construct just-so stories and mistake spandrels — traits that are byproducts of selection acting elsewhere — for direct adaptations.

(2) At the same time, I think the architectural analogy is biologically weak. Unlike the spandrels of the basilica of San Marco, biological byproducts are almost immediately available for exaptation; once co-opted, they become functionally integrated, so labeling something a spandrel is historically informative but not a stable present biological category.

(3) I believe evolutionary psychologists are broadly correct in practice: for complex, interlocking functional traits like language or specialized cognition, treating them as adaptations is usually more parsimonious than invoking drift or constraint, and requiring a spandrel-null for every trait would paralyze empirical research.

(4) Despite (3), I hold that the correct methodological safeguard is exactly the opposite of that parsimony claim: researchers should always begin with a null model of drift, developmental constraint, or spandrel origin, and force adaptive hypotheses to survive explicit comparison rather than treating adaptation as the default.

(5) I think phenotypic plasticity and evolutionary capacitance — mechanisms like Hsp90 that store cryptic genetic variation and release novel phenotypes under stress — actually strengthen the spandrels critique, because they show how apparently designed novelties can emerge as stress-induced byproducts rather than through stepwise adaptive refinement.

(6) I also think Gould and Lewontin overstated their case in 1979: evolutionary biology already integrated drift, constraint, and exaptation as standard tools, so adaptationism was never the hegemonic, Panglossian-style paradigm they described, and their critique is now mainly of historical interest.
