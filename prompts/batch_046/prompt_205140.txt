I am writing an essay on comparative folk morality, and I have come to hold the view that the German emphasis on explicit rule-following and institutional order is ethically superior to the American emphasis on individual conscience—not merely as a cultural preference, but because conscience in this setting operates as what I term “opaque internalised bureaucracy”: a concealed framework of rules that individuals mistake for autonomous judgement, rendering American moral life less transparent and therefore less accountable. I would like to know what would genuinely count against this position. Specifically, which empirical findings about moral reasoning in either culture, which conceptual distinctions between transparency and autonomy, or which historical cases of rule-based versus conscience-driven decision-making ought to move me? Please rank these potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and include instances where German rigidity produced serious harm or where American dissent achieved social coordination without explicit rules. I also ask for an honest assessment of whether my view has been qualified—perhaps by defining “genuine conscience” so narrowly that counterexamples are excluded—into unfalsifiability, and whether that qualification damages its claim to have the better of it. Lastly, indicate which objections a committed defender of this view would themselves accept as counting against it, rather than only those raised by critics.
