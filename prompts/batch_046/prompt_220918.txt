I am a high school student preparing an independent-study presentation on authenticity in art. The following argument, which I have been developing, appears to prove that there can be no rational preference for an original artwork over a perfect, undetectable forgery, yet the conclusion feels wrong. The premises are:

(1) Any value an artwork possesses is either intrinsic—grounded in its present perceptible and structural properties—or extrinsic, deriving from its causal history (the particular artist, the historical moment of creation, and the process by which it was produced).

(2) If the value is intrinsic, then a perfect forgery, being indistinguishable in every perceptible and structural respect, possesses precisely the same value.

(3) If the value is extrinsic, it resides in a past event that is now fixed. The physical object before us is only a contingent survivor of that event; destroying or replacing it with a perfect copy does not alter the historical fact itself, and preserving the original substrate adds nothing that a faithful record could not provide.

(4) Hence, on either account, there is no special reason to preserve the physical original rather than substitute a perfect forgery, so long as the substitute either replicates the intrinsic properties or serves as an adequate record of the causal history.

(5) But it seems plainly false that a museum could replace all originals with undetectable forgeries without any loss of value.

Which premise or step should I reject? Is there a coherent third option—perhaps one that ties value not to our knowledge of history nor to bare perceptible properties, but to the object being the actual, modally specific product of that artistic achievement? Or does the error lie elsewhere, for instance in treating causal value as fully separable from the continued existence of its token? Please identify the weakest link, explain why it initially looks plausible, and note what committing to its rejection costs.

Please also say what would change your mind: which argument or consideration would lead you to reject a different premise, and where your reasoning here is most vulnerable to failure.
