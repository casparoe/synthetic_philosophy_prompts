Write opening remarks for an interdisciplinary workshop attended by cultural psychologists who study WEIRD and non-WEIRD patterns in folk morality and by philosophers who debate whether that variation undermines moral objectivity. The audience is split between those who see cross-cultural divergence in judgments about harm, loyalty, or purity as evidence against realism and those who defend objectivity by distinguishing thin moral concepts, which show broad stability, from thick ones, which vary. You must welcome both camps without declaring a winner and without citing specific studies, naming authors, or offering a reading list. Shape your remarks around one structural tension: if the very recognition of a dispute as moral rather than conventional or aesthetic is itself culturally patterned, then both realists and anti-realists share a hidden problem about which level of convergence objectivity requires. Let that tension constrain what you can honestly promise about the workshop's likely resolution. End with a single precise question that places the same conceptual burden on both sides. Present everything in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
