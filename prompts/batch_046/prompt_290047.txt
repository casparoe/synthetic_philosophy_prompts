Please construct an argument defending the claim that germs, climatic shifts, and volcanic eruptions can be treated as genuine historical actors—entities that help constitute the causal architecture of human events—without committing the errors of environmental determinism or anthropomorphizing nature. Your argument should propose a specific criterion for non-human agency, one that turns not on intentionality but on the capacity to generate irreversible socio-ecological branching points that restructure institutional and political possibilities. Distinguish this criterion carefully from both strong environmental determinism, which reduces human choice to biological or geological variables, and from purely instrumental accounts that treat nature as passive background. Then address the objection that, once intentionality is removed, this "branching-point" agency collapses into ordinary causal contribution and therefore adds nothing conceptually useful to historical explanation.
