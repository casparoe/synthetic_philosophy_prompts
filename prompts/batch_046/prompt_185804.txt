Two members of my philosophy of science reading group got stuck in an argument about ontology identification when information is permanently epistemically inaccessible. They had invented a hypothetical cosmological model—call it Model Φ—that is observationally identical to standard ΛCDM within our horizon, but postulates an additional class of “shadow fermions” existing only in regions causally disconnected from ours. Because those regions lie permanently beyond any future light cone, no signal can ever reach us; the shadow fermions differ from standard fermions solely in a global quantum number that is decoherence-shielded even in principle.

Maya argued that if two ontologies are structurally isomorphic across every accessible domain and differ only in entities locked behind an absolute epistemic wall, then they are not genuinely distinct ontologies but notational variants. She proposed an “identification constraint”: ontology should be individuated only by differences that can in principle enter our best explanatory framework, not by differences that are structurally isolated from all evidence. Leo countered that this collapses ontology into epistemology: the shadow fermions either exist or they do not, regardless of our cognitive access, and treating them as identical to standard fermions mistakes our limitations for metaphysical structure. He added that if Maya’s constraint were correct, we should also deny the existence of early-universe quarks whenever direct observation is impossible, which she presumably accepts.

Maya replied that quarks in the early universe differ precisely because they are linked to our observable domain through a continuous causal chain—the inaccessibility is contingent and temporal, not structural and permanent. She said Leo’s position forces commitment to entities about which we can never have even indirect structural evidence, violating a minimal scientific realism that requires some explanatory or structural hook. Leo then introduced a twist: suppose we later discover an unexplained asymmetry in cosmic microwave background polarization that Model Φ explains elegantly via interaction with the shadow sector, while standard ΛCDM can accommodate it only through ad hoc parameter adjustments. Even though the shadow region remains causally isolated, the local asymmetry provides indirect structural evidence. Maya admitted this would change her assessment, but said it proves her point—the ontology becomes distinct only when there is some accessible structural difference, however mediated. Leo charged that this makes her view circular: she defines ontological distinction in terms of accessible difference, then claims accessible difference is necessary for ontological distinction.

Please adjudicate move by move. First, is Maya right that permanent epistemic inaccessibility collapses ontological distinction? Second, does Leo’s quark analogy fail because of the causal-chain distinction Maya draws? Third, is Maya’s response to the CMB-polarization twist circular, or does it preserve a non-circular constraint? Fourth, what are both parties missing about how ontology identification works when information is structurally rather than merely contingently inaccessible? Give an overall verdict on who is more right, and identify the specific structural feature of the case—permanent causal isolation combined with potential indirect mediation—that does the philosophical work.
