I've come too think that the leveling-down objection only bites if you assume welfare is both cardinal and interpersonally comparable across every possible world, which I'm not convinced we should grant. My view is closer to a kind of "context-bound prioritarianism": within any fixed choice-set, benefits to the worse-off get weighted more heavily, but there's no moral requirement to destroy resources that benefit the well-off unless those resources can actually be redirected. So if you blind a rich person just to make everyone equally sighted, that's not an injustice to them—it's just a waste of sight—unless there's some redistribution mechanism I'm missing. I realize this might just be insulating prioritarianism by redefining harm, so I want an honest map of where it's exposed.

What would count against this? I'm not looking for the standard "but equality matters intrinsically" response—I'm asking which formal results, thought experiments, or consistency conditions would actually break the view. For instance, does it collapse into plain utilitarianism once you allow that destroying unredistributable goods is just inefficient? How does it handle cases where the "choice-set" is defined by policy boundaries that are themselves morally loaded—like if a state defines the choice-set to exclude taxation, does my view accidentally legitimize that framing? And if I extend this to future generations or non-human animals, where "redirection" is conceptually murkier, does the distinction between waste and injustice hold up at all? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a committed defender of this framing would actually concede versus dismiss out of hand.

One more thing: if the premise that we can cleanly separate "destroying value" from "harming the well-off" is just confused, or if this task can't be done well without relying on specific social-choice formalisms I haven't named correctly, push back plainly—I'd rather hear that than get a polite answer that pretends the framing makes sense.
