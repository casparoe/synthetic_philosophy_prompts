I'm prepping a seminar on ontology of patterns and got into a weird debate with a colleague who does philosophy of science. She thinks Dennett's real-patterns view — the one with the physical, design, and intentional stances — is basically just a taxonomy of predictive shortcuts, not a genuine metaphysical division. I tried pushing back by saying that if you take the design stance seriously, you're not just compressing intentional-level predictions; you're tracking causal structures that wouldn't show up in either pure physics or folk psychology. But then she asked me about language models: if we apply the intentional stance to an LLM purely for predictive convenience, do we get a *new* kind of real pattern that sits between design and intentional? Or is it just design-level computation wearing a mask? So here's what I want: compare Dennett's original three-stance framework with the idea that all stances are just compression levels in a predictive hierarchy. Which one actually explains why we treat some patterns as discovered rather than imposed? And if there is an intermediate pattern-type for artificial systems — not quite design, not fully intentional — does that break Dennett's taxonomy or just extend it? Don't worry about citations, I just want the conceptual comparison, especially whether the predictive-compresssion view can account for the feeling that design-stance explanations have thier own independant explanatory power.
