Goodman's grue paradox is usually read as showing that inductive logic needs a criterion of projectibility that cannot itself be justified inductively, since the hypothesis that all emeralds are green and the competing hypothesis that all emeralds are grue receive identical observational support. But this framing seems to conflate a logical point about predicate syntax with an epistemic point about justification: if the only way to break the symmetry is to appeal to the past entrenchment of green, then haven't we shown that induction requires a prior metaphysical inventory of natural properties rather than merely a conventional rule of language? And if that is correct, doesn't the paradox actually undermine Bayesian approaches that try to solve Hume's problem of induction by distributing priors over hypotheses—because any prior that assigns equal weight to green and grue is not epistemically neutral but self-defeating, while any prior that favors green already assumes the very inductive habit it was meant to justify? I want to understand whether Goodman's challenge is fundamentally about ontology, about the formal syntax of inductive rules, or about the circularity of epistemic justification, and whether there is a coherent way to separate these threads without presupposing what we set out to explain.
