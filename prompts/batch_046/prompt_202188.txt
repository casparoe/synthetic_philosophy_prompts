For my philosophy course, please analyze Albert Einstein's co-authorship of the August 1939 letter to President Roosevelt — urging American atomic research in response to potential German development — through the lens of his own views on religion, determinism, and free will. Einstein rejected a personal God and libertarian free will in favor of Spinozist causal determinism, yet he also described a cosmic religious feeling that grounds ethical commitment without expectation of divine reward. Given these commitments, what kind of agency was he exercising when he deliberately sought to alter the course of nuclear history? Does the episode reveal a performative contradiction between deterministic metaphysics and moral intervention, or can it be reconciled through a compatibilist account of ethical motivation? To sharpen the analysis, distinguish causal determinism about physical events from what might be called normative determinism about the origin of ethical motivation, and assess whether Einstein's action supports the claim that his denial of free will was substantive rather than verbal. Focus on how the philosophical framework illuminates the historical decision and where the decision exposes tensions or gaps in that framework; I do not need bibliographies or external citations.
