I am a software engineer without formal philosophy training, but my work on pattern-classification and font-rendering pipelines has led me to a specific view about letterform identity—roughly the problem that projects in computational letterform research address. I hold that there is no geometric or perceptual essence that makes every instance an 'a'. Instead, membership in the category is constituted entirely by a functional-relational position inside a given writing system: a glyph counts as an 'a' precisely when it occupies the differential role that distinguishes it from 'b', 'd', 'o', and the other alphabet members in contexts where that distinction carries semantic load. The category is therefore anchored not by a shared shape but by counterfactual robustness—if you systematically replaced the glyph with its nearest geometric neighbors, the writing system's information-carrying capacity would collapse in specific, predictable ways. Style, on this account, is not noise layered over a deep form; it is the primary phenomenon, and the letter is just the invariant node in the network of differences.

What would count against this? I want an honest map of its exposure, not a list of standard objections that can be dismissed as category mistakes. Which empirical findings—say, in human letter recognition, prototype effects, or historical paleography—would be decisive rather than merely awkward? Would the existence of writing systems in which the same functional role is filled by shapes that violate our intuitive geometric boundaries force a revision, or could the relational network be redrawn to absorb them? How should the view treat genuinely ambiguous glyphs that sit on the boundary between 'a' and another letter without contextual resolution—does it predict that all ambiguity is ultimately system-resolvable, and what if it is not? I am especially interested in whether defenders of this relational-functional account would themselves concede certain results as damaging—for instance, if feature-detection studies in visual cognition showed that humans identify letters via invariant geometric templates rather than via differential contrast with alphabet neighbors. Please distinguish the defeaters that a committed proponent should accept from those they could legitimately reject.
