I write about science and philosophy of mind for a general audience, and I have been tracking a recurring argumentative stalemate that I cannot find a precise name for. The pattern arises whenever empirical work identifies a psychological or neural mediator of a moral judgment—say, a particular emotional response, a cognitive framing effect, or a developmental trajectory. Critics treat the mediator as a bias vector: because it operates below the level of reflective endorsement and correlates with non-moral variables, it distorts judgment. Defenders treat the identical mediator as a calibration mechanism: it activates selectively in response to features that turn out, on examination, to be genuinely morally relevant, so its presence indicates a well-tuned preference rather than contamination. What makes the stalemate distinctive is not merely disagreement about a particular case, but the structural symmetry of the interpretations: any mediator that can be cited as debiasing evidence can be redescribed, with equal empirical footing, as the substrate of a legitimate moral taste. I have observed this with debates over disgust in purity judgments, over status-quo bias in distributive intuitions, and—more recently—over the weighting patterns in synthetic decision systems trained on human choice data, where engineers debate whether a given feature weight represents an imported human prejudice or the system's own stable preference architecture. This is not the general problem of distinguishing fact from value, nor is it identical to adaptive preference critiques or to standard moral debunking arguments, which typically target specific judgments rather than the meta-level ambiguity about the mediator itself. Is there an established philosophical concept that captures exactly this symmetry—where a single causal pathway can be read with equal plausibility as either bias-source or preference-forming mechanism—and that provides criteria for breaking the symmetry? If a term exists, how does it differ from nearby ideas in moral epistemology and in the literature on moral expertise? If there is no established term, I would like a coinage together with explicit boundary conditions: what distinguishes this configuration from ordinary cases of hidden bias, and from cases where the mediator is uncontroversially part of a rational preference structure?
