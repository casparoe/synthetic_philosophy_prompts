I am constructing a conceptual framework for a philosophical novel in which a neuroscientist and a phenomenologist debate whether consciousness survives the gradual synthetic replacement of neural tissue, and I find that the standard taxonomies of functionalism fail to capture the precise fractures that emerge once one takes both causal-role preservation and phenomenal binding seriously. Rather than asking for a list of authors or schools, I need a decision tree—suitable for a handout in an advanced philosophy of mind seminar—that classifies positions according to the answers respondents actually give to concrete diagnostic questions, not according to the labels they prefer.

The tree should begin with the commitment that mental states are individuated by causal-functional role rather than by intrinsic physical constitution, then branch through four successive questions: first, whether multiple realizability applies uniformly across all mental kinds or is restricted to cognitive and intentional states while phenomenal states remain substrate-bound; second, whether synthetic realization requires preservation not only of causal topology but also of a specific integrative dynamics—such as a rhythmic synchronization analog—to count as the same mental state rather than a mere simulation; third, whether simultaneous biological and synthetic instantiation of an identical causal profile entails branching personal identity, parallel instantiation without identity, or a contradiction that forces rejection of one pathway; and fourth, whether the absence of qualitative feel in a functionally isomorphic artificial system demonstrates that phenomenal properties lie outside the functional description, or instead reveals that realization has failed at an undetected physical level. Each leaf must identify the position—using standard names such as liberal functionalism, restricted phenomenal functionalism, biological naturalism, substrate-dependent emergentism, or the unnamed hybrid I am tentatively calling topological dualism—and state in a single sentence its most awkward commitment. Please flag any pair of leaves that the logical structure cannot cleanly separate, and diagnose at least one unoccupied branch—specifying whether its emptiness stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or simple neglect—without citing external texts or recommending further reading.
