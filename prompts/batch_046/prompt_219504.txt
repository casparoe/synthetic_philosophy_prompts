i run intake at a workers-comp pain clinic and we're piloting this new framework from the pain-asymbolia literature — basically pain splits into a raw sensory track and an aversive-binding track, and in asymbolia the first fires but the second doesn't. my director wants a reusable procedure (like a checklist or flowchart in words) that our nurses can run in ~10 mins per patient to decide whether a reported pain is "bound" (counts for disability and aggressive tx) or "unbound registered" (tx yes, disability no). the nurses don't know philosophy, so translate the two-track idea into concrete tests they can apply — behavior, language, context — and flag exactly where judgment can't be eliminated and the nurse has to sign off. include the hard cases: people who hide pain for cultural reasons, people who've dissociated from years of chronic pain, people gaming the system. work through three examples: one clear bound, one clear unbound, and one where the procedure gives the patient an answer they're gonna hate. say what the procedure flat-out can't do — like tell binding failure from malingering without imaging. and be committed about it: actually pick cutoff points and defend them, don't just lay out "on the one hand, on the other hand". keep it practical, no citations or reading lists needed.
