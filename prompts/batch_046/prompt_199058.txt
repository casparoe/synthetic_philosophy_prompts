I've been chewing on the competitiveness constraint and the allignment tax, and I'm kinda convinced the whole framing is a catagory error. The position I hold is that the "tax" isn't a persistent drag on capabilities but a front-loaded capital expense with a delayed return — once misalignment risk becomes legible to insurers and regulators, the market rewards safety as a premium feature rather than penalizing it. So the constraint only binds in a pre-legibility window, which makes it structurally temporary, not an enduring law of AI development. What would count against this? I want the arguments and evidence that should actually move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be — things like documented cases where safety investments were competitively punished even after risks were widely acknowledged, or formal models showing race dynamics have a discontinuity at some capability threshold C* that breaks standard equilibrium reasoning. I also want defenders' own concessions: what do they say would falsify or weaken the view, not just what critics claim? And I need an honest check on insulation: has the "legibility" condition been qualified into unfalsifiability, with defenders just shifting the goalpost whenever a counterexample appears? If the position turns out to be well-protected, say whether that's a virtue for a market-dynamics claim or actually a weakness. Be straight about how exposed the standard narrative really is.
