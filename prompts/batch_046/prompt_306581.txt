I've been looking at how some corporate wellness programs score employee wellbeing. They have people fill out surveys about job satisfaction, social connection at work, sense of purpose, and even "momentary joy" during the day, then they feed all that into one algorithm that spits out a single "flourishing score" for each team. The weird part is that the survey keeps the categories seperate, but management only ever looks at the collapsed number for bonuses and office layout decisions. What philosophical view of well-being does that presuppose? Like, do they have to believe there's one thing — call it happiness or pleasure — that all these different goods are just flavors of? Or is this just bureaucratic laziness that a pluralist could do too? And if hedonism's heterogenity problem is real and pleasures don't actually share a common nature, does that undermine the practice, or can they just say "we're measuring correlation, not ontology"?
