Maya started by saying it's obvious dreams are experiences: people report them, they have emotional valence, they feel like something from the inside, and lucid dreamers can even signal with eye movements that they're aware inside the dream state. So there's clearly a subject present undergoing something.

Jonah said that's exactly the mistake Malcom warned about -- we're importing waking-state criteria where they don't apply. He argued that Dennett's cassette theory still holds up: the "experience" is constructed retrospecitve upon waking, not lived through in real time. The brain during REM is running predictive-error correction and synaptic maintenance, not generating sensory content for a witness. He called it database maintenance without a user interface, which I thought was a neat line but maybe too neat.

Maya pushed back that REM activation maps onto waking perception networks, and that lucid dreamers can misreport what's happening inside the dream -- they think they're flying over the ocean but the narrative was actually about a boat -- so there's a real-time subject who can be wrong, which satisfies even Malcom's witness criterion. She said if dreams aren't experiences, we're left calling them just brain noise that we accidentally narrativize, which seems like a weird ontological move just to avoid admitting consciousness happens during sleep.

Jonah replied that maybe it IS just brain noise with post-hoc storytelling, and that calling it an "experience" is like calling a computer's defragmentation an experience just because the log file describes it coherently afterwards. He also said that if phenomenal externalism is right -- that experiences depend on actual environmental relations -- then dreams are automatically disqualified since there's no actual environment being engaged with.
