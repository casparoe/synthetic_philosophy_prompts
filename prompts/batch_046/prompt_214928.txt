Specifically, discuss how the gridfont model encodes stylistic coherence across an alphabet by distributing continuous parameters over these categorical roles, and why this generates a philosophical problem about whether letter identity is anchored in structural invariants (so that being an 'a' requires possessing a specific relational configuration regardless of stylistic variation) or in a family-resemblance cluster of perceptual features that tolerate graded deviation and context-dependent distortion. Clarify whether the gridfont representation genuinely resolves this tension by formalizing style as parametric noise around an invariant core, or whether it merely relocates the problem into the geometry of the parameter space, leaving the boundary between letter categories and stylistic variants underdetermined.
