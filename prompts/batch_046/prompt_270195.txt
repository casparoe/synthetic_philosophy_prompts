Write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—call them A and B—debating whether democratic authorization can cleanse the moral stain of politically necessary but gravely wrong acts. A defends the “residual taint” position: that an officeholder who authorizes, for instance, a constitutionally prohibited deception that saves lives acquires not guilt but a permanent diminution in normative standing to invoke the very values violated, and that no electoral mandate can restore this standing. B defends the “institutional translation” position: that within a legitimate democratic architecture the officeholder functions as a procedural node, so the wrong is translated into a collective burden distributed across the institutional structure and is cleansable through transparent accountability, leaving no irreducible personal stain. Require that each speaker respond directly to the other’s actual argument rather than talking past them. Present the result as exactly five numbered exchanges, each exchange being one to three sentences, with no introductory or concluding remarks. Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or references to specific texts; keep all references to positions and arguments at a conceptual level that requires no external lookup.
