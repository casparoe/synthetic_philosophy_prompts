I was rewatching Minority Report last night and got into this long arguement with a friend about whether the precrime unit is secretly retributivist. On the surface it's pure consequentialism — stop murder before it happens, maximize social utility, whatever — but then there's Anderton's whole arc where he hunts down the man who'd kill his son, and that feels way more like desert-based revenge dressed up as prevention. Can you read the movie through the lens of retributivism vs consequentialism, but skip the boring "utilitarian cops vs Kantian rebels" framing? I want to know if the determinism of the precogs actually breaks the retributivist framework: if the future is fixed and you can see it, does "punishing" someone for a crime they haven't committed yet still count as giving them what they deserve, or does it collapse into pure social engineering?

Also, the way the precogs are kept in that milky pool, basically used as human sensors — does that dramatize something both theories miss about agency? Like, if you treat people as nodes in a prediction system, you've already given up on them being moral agents who can be held responsible. I'm writing this up for a blog post, so I need the reading to actually engage with the film's visuls and plot mechanics and not just use it as an illustration. Where does the movie resist being read as either pure retributivism or pure consequentialism? And is there a moment — maybe when Anderton chooses not to kill the guy, or when he lets himself be arrested — that adds something to the punishment debate that philosophers writing abstract papers don't usually capture?
