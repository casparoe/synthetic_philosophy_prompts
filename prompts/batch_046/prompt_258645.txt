Write a deadpan parody of a university "Belief Ethics and Cognitive Conduct Policy", approximately 600 words, formatted as numbered regulatory sections with sub-clauses. The document should present itself as a binding framework that attempts to reconcile two competing codes: the Cliffordian Evidential Compliance Code, which prohibits assent without proportionate evidence and invokes the classic shipowner case, and the Jamesian Living Option Exemption, which permits belief in living, forced, and momentous options where intellect alone is insufficient. The satire should derive from applying these philosophical distinctions literally to everyday academic life—selecting a dissertation topic, joining a student society, expressing a seminar opinion—so that a specialist recognises the exaggeration is fair to both positions. Include one invented disciplinary case (for example, a student sanctioned for believing in the existence of a cancelled lecture) that demonstrates the absurdity of enforcing universal evidentialism, and one exemption clause so broadly interpreted that it effectively nullifies the Cliffordian rule. The voice must be that of an over-zealous administrator treating both philosophers as statutory authorities, with sustained deadpan irony and no winking. Do not quote any real texts verbatim; paraphrase the core claims without quotation marks. Refer to the thinkers only by surname. Keep the piece readable without specialist knowledge but accurate enough that someone familiar with the James–Clifford debate sees the logic being followed to its conclusion.
