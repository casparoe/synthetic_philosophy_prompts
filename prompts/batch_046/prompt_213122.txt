I sit in on this local prediction-market meetup that tries to be rigourous about epistemics. We've got this layered protocol: you write your private credence before seeing the crowd, then update publicaly, and we run pre-mortems where you have to list reasons you might be totally wrong before placing a bet. The whole point is to guard against self-deception. But lately I've been wondering what that institutional setup actually presupposses about whether self-deception is even possible. If it's conceptually incoherent—like, you can't genuinely hold a belief and also know its false—then aren't all these guardrails just theater? Or does the practice only make sense if you accept some partitioned-mind model where one part of you knows the other part is motivated? I want to figure out what theory of self-deception our little institution is smuggling in. Does it presupposse that outcomes depend on access between mental partitions? And is the setup consistent with the same group treating motivated reasoning as just a correctable bias rather than a structural feature? Can someone map out the philosophical commitments here?
