This asymmetry should shape what I can and cannot say. I must be able to acknowledge that archives are genuinely incomplete and that historians legitimately dispute figures, motives, and bureaucratic pathways, without that acknowledgment being weaponized by denialist logic. The remarks should navigate this tension for a mixed audience—accessible and respectful to the non-specialists, precise enough that the historians do not feel patronized.

Write in a formal, measured tone appropriate to an institutional opening. Where you rely on contested historiographical framing—for example, presenting the asymmetrical evidential burden as the defining feature of denialism rather than one symptom among several—include a brief closing note identifying exactly where the reasoning is most likely to fail and what kind of counter-example or conceptual clarification would change your mind about whether this asymmetry genuinely distinguishes denialism from merely inadequate scholarship.
