I am a retired research physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, has attended a philosophy of science seminar at my university. The convenor, a philosopher with a background in the physical sciences, has offered firm judgements on several episodes in which philosophical work intersected with scientific development. She has held that philosophy was genuinely useful to Einstein in formulating general relativity, given the conceptual crisis over gravitation and Mach's influence; that it was not useful in the interpretation of quantum mechanics, where she regards philosophical debate as post-hoc decoration upon an already successful formalism; that the logical and conceptual work of Church and Turing, though now classed as mathematics, was philosophically motivated and genuinely useful in founding computer science; and that the philosophical debates about behaviourism and mental representation in the 1950s contributed nothing useful to the emerging cognitive sciences, being either too abstract or actively misleading.

Next week we shall discuss a more recent episode: philosophers of neuroscience have developed detailed accounts of mechanistic explanation — specifying what it takes for a model to genuinely explain a neural phenomenon rather than merely predict it — and these accounts have recently been adopted by researchers in computational psychiatry to design interpretable machine-learning architectures intended to identify causal pathways in psychiatric disorders. The convenor has not yet given her view.

Please predict her verdict: will she judge this philosophical contribution genuinely useful to the science? Do so by identifying at least two plausible implicit rules that fit all four of her earlier judgements, showing that they diverge on this new case, and giving your best guess at her actual view with reasons. One candidate rule might concern whether philosophy aids science only during foundational crises; another might concern whether it provides conceptual frameworks that enable new empirical testing. Finally, tell me what further case — perhaps involving philosophical ethics in climate modelling, or metaphysics in synthetic biology — would best distinguish between these two rules. Please provide your analysis in British English.
