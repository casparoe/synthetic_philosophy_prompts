As a novelist constructing a philosophical dialogue between an astrophysicist and a metaphysician, I require a rigorous conceptual specification before I can render their debate with intellectual coherence. I am not soliciting a bibliography, a reading list, or verifiable citations; I want the desiderata derived from first principles, intelligible without external lookup.

Write the full list of requirements that an adequate cosmological argument—whether in its kalam form, its contingency form, or a synthetic hybrid—must meet in order to function as a genuine explanation rather than as rhetorical relocation of mystery. For each desideratum, identify the case or phenomenon it must accommodate, the conceptual distinction it must not collapse (particularly the distinction between explanatory termination in a being with modal priority and mere causal termination at a physically prior boundary condition that lacks such priority), and whether it is non-negotiable or a negotiable preference of a particular camp (for example, determinism, locality, or temporal presentism).

The specification must address at least the following domains: consistency with modern cosmology without requiring a literal interpretation of classical singularities; resolution or principled dissolution of both temporal and modal infinite regress; preservation of the difference between being caused and being contingent; compatibility with the rejection of global metaphysical presentism without covert adoption of a B-theory of time that undermines the argument's own temporal premises; and neutrality with respect to cosmological models that deny an absolute temporal origin, such that the argument does not fail automatically if eternal inflation or cyclic scenarios are correct.

In addition, introduce one precise, somewhat niche constraint that popular formulations often neglect: the argument must explicitly determine whether a quantum vacuum state governed by timeless physical laws can serve as an ultimate explanatory terminus, or whether such a state merely relocates the contingency problem onto the laws, constants, and structural conditions that govern it. This should be treated conceptually rather than technically—no dependence on memorized formalisms of specific quantum-gravity programs is required.

Next, examine whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable given known constraints—such as whether demanding both a temporal first cause and full compatibility with beginningless cosmological models produces an unavoidable tension, or whether an unrestricted Principle of Sufficient Reason applied to every contingent fact necessarily excludes an infinite hierarchy of contingent explanations. Clearly flag any such incompatible pair.

Then state which single requirement eliminates the greatest number of popular formulations of the argument, and provide a brief comparative assessment of how the classical kalam version and the Leibnizian contingency version fare against the complete list—keeping the analytical weight on the criteria themselves, since they constitute the substantive result, rather than on the scoring.

Present the result as a structured specification; a table is acceptable.
