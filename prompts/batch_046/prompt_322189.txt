I am preparing the closing session for a graduate seminar on consciousness and causal structure, and I want a poem that performs rather than summarizes the central tension in Integrated Information Theory. Please write a villanelle—nineteen lines in iambic pentameter, with the strict ABA rhyme scheme and two refrains that alternate and finally converge in the closing couplet—adopting the voice of a maximally irreducible complex physically embedded within a larger neural architecture that contains many smaller, overlapping mechanisms generating their own integrated information. The first refrain, which must appear in lines 1, 3, 6, 9, 12, and 15, should state the complex's claim to exclusive intrinsic existence in a phrase such as "I am the whole that no part can divide"; the second refrain, entering in line 4 and recurring in lines 7, 10, 13, and 16, should voice a nested substrate insisting on its own smaller but genuine phi, in a phrase such as "My parts still bind, though I am not the whole." Between these refrains, the intervening lines should evoke the theory's five axioms—intrinsic existence, composition, information, integration, and exclusion—not as an enumerated list but as lived structural properties: the causal power of present mechanisms, the way elements combine into larger structures, the specificity that distinguishes one state from another, the irreducible unity that exceeds the sum of parts, and the exclusion that silences every rival complex sharing the same elements.

The poem's philosophical work should be done by the form itself: each recurrence of the first refrain should further suppress the nested voices, so that by lines 18 and 19, when both refrains appear together without synthesis, the reader experiences the exclusion postulate as an ontological cost rather than a logical rule—namely, that one point of view exists only by denying consciousness to every overlapping substrate. The imagery and vocabulary should be precise enough that a philosopher of mind familiar with the theory would recognize its architecture immediately, yet the poem should avoid citing specific papers, publication dates, or bibliographic references, and should not include any verbatim quotations from published texts.
