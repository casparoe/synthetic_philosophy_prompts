me and two study buddies got into it after class about anatta and whether it actually destroys personal identity or just redescribes it. here's the back and forth:

(1) Sam said if there's literally no self, then saying 'i'm the same person who ate breakfast' is definatly false — there's just mental states with no owner. so personal identity is a flat illusion and we should stop treating first-person talk as real.

(2) Maya replied that buddhism still uses conventional self-talk for karma and rebirth, so anatta can't mean identity is totally fake. it just denies a permanent, unchanging soul behind the stream. conventionally i'm still me.

(3) Sam pushed back: if there's no substrate at all, then what's stopping me from being identical to a clone with my memories? continuity of mental processes is all that's left, so caring more about 'this' stream is totally arbitrary. anatta actually collapses personal identity into an open-ended thing where copies should matter equally.

(4) Maya said he's missing the practical point. anatta isn't a metaphysical license for copies; it's saying clinging to the me/not-me boundary is the root of suffering. the conventional self is a useful fiction for ethics, but his panic about clones is exactly the attachment buddhism targets. the boundary wasn't dissolved into copies — it was never a real boundary to begin with.
