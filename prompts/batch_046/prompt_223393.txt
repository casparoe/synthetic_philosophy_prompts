I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of physics, and I have been developing an essay for my graduate seminar on the metaphysics of special relativity. The piece centres on the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for eternalism: that because simultaneity is relative to inertial frame, there is no uniquely privileged 'now', and therefore all events—past, present, and future—must exist tenselessly within a four-dimensional block. Rather than simply rehearsing the standard reconstruction, I want to test a more speculative comparison. Specifically, I propose that the argument's elimination of a global hyperplane of simultaneity bears a structural resemblance to certain interpretations of quantum correlations across spacelike separation, in which the temporal ordering of measurements can be frame-dependent or even undefined prior to decoherence. My tentative claim is that if quantum mechanics permits genuinely indefinite temporal relations between events, then the block universe might not be the uniformly flat manifold that Rietdijk and Putnam envisage; instead, the ontology might allow for local 'fuzziness' in temporal structure without reverting to presentism.

Could you comment on whether my reconstruction of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument is accurate—particularly my claim that they treat the absence of a unique simultaneity hyperplane as sufficient for the ontological parity of all spatiotemporal points—on whether the analogy to quantum temporal indeterminacy is conceptually coherent or merely imports assumptions from quantum foundations that are foreign to classical spacetime ontology, and on whether my proposed 'fuzzy block' critique actually undermines eternalism or simply relocates the problem? I am not asking for references or further reading; rather, I would value your assessment of whether the essay's central move survives philosophical scrutiny, and whether there are disanalogies between the Lorentz transformation case and the quantum case that I have overlooked. If the comparison fails, I would also welcome suggestions for a more apt analogy that captures the idea that temporal structure might be locally indeterminate without licensing a global present.
