I'm a grad student in history and lately I've been noticing this weird pattern in revisionist literature about mass violence - specifically I've been comparing some Armenian genocide texts and Nanjing revisionist essays for a side project. The authors don't deny the deaths happened. They cite the same archives, same casualty figures, even the same eyewitness accounts. But then they introduce this framing where the killings get redescribed as "population security management" or "unintended demograpic collapse during wartime logistics." The factual record stays intact, but the category of "intent to destroy" just... dissolves into bureacratic process. It's not flat denialism - nobody is saying the bodies aren't there. But the moral/historical conclusion shifts completely. I've heard people call this "denial by other means" but that feels too broad. Is there a sharper concept for this specific maneuver - accepting every datum but restructuring the causal/normative frame so the genocide classification no longer applies? And how do historians distinguish this from legitimate revisionism that just corrects old numbers?
