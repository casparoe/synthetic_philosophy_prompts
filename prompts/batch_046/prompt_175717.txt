Write a short dialogue — maybe 600 words — between two philosophers, Mara and Leo, arguing in a campus cafe about why human extinction is bad beyond the deaths it involves. Mara should defend a tweaked version of Schefflers afterlife idea: she thinks present generations build up "project-completion debts" to the future — unfinished scientific, artistic, and moral work that only gets its full value when carried forward. Leo should push back that this smuggles in a weird metaphysics where value "waits" for a bearer, and that extinction harm is purely agregative: just all the good lives that wont happen. Have them actually fight over whether unfinished work can be wronged if theres no future worker, and whether Schefflers intuition holds if we reject the idea that culture acts like a collective person. Mara should bring up small-scale cases — like an isolated research colony dying out — where the specific trajectory of inquiry dies, not just a quantity of welfare. Leo should say thats just aesthetic grief in ethical clothing. By the end neither should win, but they should have sharpened the line between "value that needs a bearer" and "value that needs completion." Keep it casual, dont let either side turn into a strawman, and include a typo or two.
