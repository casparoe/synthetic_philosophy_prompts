Design a decision-tree taxonomy of philosophical positions on rational irrationality using Parfit's hitchhiker scenario—where a driver offers a ride conditional on a promise of payment upon arrival, yet once arrived the rider lacks any instrumental reason to pay—as the diagnostic core. The tree should branch through three levels of concrete commitments answerable without knowing the taxonomy's labels. First, determine whether rationality is evaluated ex ante (at the point of adopting the disposition or making the promise) or ex post (at the point of execution after arrival), allowing an intermediate branch for diachronic-binding or precommitment accounts that treat the two points as rationally linked. Second, determine whether such binding can be sustained without external enforcement, distinguishing positions that rely on internal psychological restructuring, on social-reputational mechanisms, or on contractarian mutual-advantage structures. Third, inject the collective-agent generalization: apply the same structure to a deliberative body (for instance, a climate-policy committee) that secures a beneficial agreement only if it can bind itself to an execution-stage decision that is instrumentally irrational for its members at that later point; ask whether the rational-irrationality verdict transfers unchanged, reverses, or dissolves into indeterminacy at the collective level. Each leaf should be labeled with the name of an established position that occupies that space—such as a causal-decision-theoretic resolution, a contractarian commitment strategy, a present-aim revision that denies ex-post authority, or a deflationary reconception of instrumental reasons—or marked 'unnamed' if the combination is unoccupied, followed in one sentence by its most awkward commitment. The tree should explicitly flag logical spaces that remain empty, particularly combinations that endorse rational irrationality for individual agents but reject it for collective agents, diagnosing whether each gap looks incoherent, merely unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Ensure that every node is phrased as a direct question about a case or claim, so the instrument sorts respondents by what they actually believe rather than by which philosopher they claim to follow.
