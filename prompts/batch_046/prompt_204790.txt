I'm trying to map my gut feelings on dirty hands in politics, but not the usual textbook ones — more like weird edge cases.

City councilor secretly bribes a zoning board to block a toxic plant near a poor neighborhood: dirty hands, but justified.

President lies about a minor security breach to avoid panic, knowing it'll come out in six months: dirty, not justified — feels like vanity, not sacrifice.

Senator votes for a war bill she hates because the alternative kills all refugee aid: dirty but okay.

Same senator leaks classified docs showing the war is built on false intel, breaking law but maybe stopping escalation: I keep flip-flipping. Sometimes it feels like the purest dirty hands; sometimes like breaking the system is its own stain that never washes off.

Mayor orders cops to break up a peaceful sit-in blocking hospital access, using force she knows will hurt people: dirty and not okay, even if patients die without it.

Then there's this pair that messes with me. Diplomat forges passports to smuggle activists out of a dictatorship — feels clean, not dirty at all. But she forges identicle papers to get a corrupt ally out before trial — feels filthy, even though the mechanics are the same.

So what's the principle, or maybe two competing ones, that captures these?
