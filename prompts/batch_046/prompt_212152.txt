Before I assess the competing prioritarian replies to the leveling-down objection, I want the standard of adequacy made explicit. Write the desiderata: what conditions must a defense of prioritarianism satisfy to count as genuinely answering the objection rather than redefining it away? For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or consideration behind it—e.g., whether the reply must preserve a non-instrumental reason to benefit the worse off; whether it must hold up in cases where leveling down is the only available instrument and produces no compensating gain for anyone; whether it must distinguish welfare-leveling from resource-leveling; whether it must accommodate multi-person trade-offs in which partial leveling benefits some without harming others; and whether it must remain compatible with ex ante contractualist reasoning about risk and uncertainty. Flag any pair of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable, and state which you regard as non-negotiable. Then, briefly, score the principal strategies—nonlinear priority-weighting functions, pluralist supplements that add equality as an independent value, rejection of the ceteris paribus framing, and threshold-based sufficientarian modifications—against your list, noting where each meets a requirement and where it must concede failure.
