You are a philosopher of social choice with particular expertise in collective rationality and institutional decision-making. I have recently been appointed to a university research ethics committee, and we have just faced a stark live case of the discursive dilemma: a majority of members agreed that a proposed study posed minimal risk, and a separate majority agreed that informed consent was properly obtained, yet a majority nevertheless rejected the conclusion that the study was ethically permissible. This has made an otherwise abstract logical puzzle urgently personal. I should like you to evaluate five proposed resolutions to such dilemmas and place each into one of the following tiers—S, A, B, C, or D—according to their combined philosophical defensibility and practical viability in real committee settings. Please provide a single sentence of justification for each tier, and do not cite literature; reason from first principles.

The five approaches are: (1) premise-based aggregation, in which the collective conclusion is derived deductively from the majority-supported premises regardless of whether that conclusion itself commands a majority; (2) conclusion-based aggregation, which treats the majority judgement on the final verdict as authoritative and regards the premises as merely advisory, accepting the risk of logical inconsistency; (3) distance-based minimisation, which selects the consistent set of premises and conclusion that minimises the total number of individual disagreements with that set; (4) reflective meta-deliberation, which requires the committee to reconvene and vote explicitly on which premises are load-bearing for the particular conclusion before any aggregation is performed; and (5) equilibrium-weighted threshold aggregation, a hybrid I have been considering, in which each premise is assigned a weight proportional to the committee’s expressed confidence in its relevance to the specific conclusion at hand, and deduction is permitted only when the weighted premises exceed a calibrated institutional threshold. Present your tier list clearly, and in particular explain where the equilibrium-weighted approach lands relative to the standard premise- and conclusion-based methods, noting whether its added complexity buys sufficient resistance to strategic voting.
