I'm moderating a podcast interview with a philosopher who defends an updated version of Simone Weil's claim that political parties are not merely corrupt but structurally hostile to justice, because they replace patient attention to reality with collective emotional loyalty. She applies this beyond formal parties to advocacy coalitions, academic "schools," and large-scale peer-review networks, arguing that any group demanding internal solidarity over solitary judgment reproduces the same pathology. Write me eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her to summarize Weil. Focus instead on: (i) the precise boundary between a temporary alliance for a specific end and a Weilian party—anticipate her likely appeal to affective glue, and follow up by asking whether algorithmic recommendation systems could manufacture that glue without formal membership; (ii) whether Weil's own involvement with the Free French and with labor movements undermines her critique or reveals an unstated criterion; (iii) how her argument avoids collapsing into generic skepticism about climate-science consortia or other epistemic institutions; (iv) what form of collective decision-making, if any, could avoid party-spirit; and (v) whether her standard of absolute intellectual honesty is compatible with democratic deliberation at scale. For each area, build in a follow-up keyed to her most probable reply. End with a question about what historical or institutional evidence could genuinely weaken her position.
