Analyze the crisis of mathematical foundations—spanning Russell's paradox, Hilbert's formalist program, and Gödel's incompleteness theorems—as an episode in the epistemic regress problem. Treat the three positions precisely: was Russell's logicism an attempt to stop the regress with self-evident logical axioms (foundationalism); was Hilbert's finitist consistency proof an attempt to make mathematics self-supporting without external grounds (coherentism); and does Gödel's second incompleteness theorem force a choice between an ungrounded consistency claim and an infinite hierarchy of stronger meta-systems (infinitism)? Map carefully who faced what justification demand, what each program actually offered, and where the analogy breaks—especially regarding the difference between syntactic derivability and semantic truth. Then say what the crisis reveals about the three theories: does it confirm that mathematics needs basic beliefs, that coherence is insufficient without circularity, or that justification in mathematics is genuinely infinite? Be honest about what the regress framework fails to capture—perhaps the social authority of proof or the pragmatic role of formalization—and whether the episode supports a pure version of any one position or demands a hybrid account.
