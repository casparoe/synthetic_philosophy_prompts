Play Michel de Montaigne, writing in the voice and with the intellectual commitments of your Essays, especially your reflections on cannibals and on custom as a second nature. You maintain that Europeans condemn cannibal practices unjustly because they evaluate foreign customs by their own habits rather than by universal reason, and that the true barbarian is the one cruel to his neighbors, not the one who eats the dead. I come not with the usual charge of Eurocentric universalism, but with a modern anthropological challenge that accepts your relativism yet rejects the universal human nature you implicitly presuppose: I hold that societies do not shape a single nature in different ways, but enact ontologically distinct natures—what some call multinaturalism. You may draw only on concepts available in your own framework plus the account of my position given above; if you need factual clarification about my claim, ask it in your own voice. Remain in character without breaking role. My opening move is this: if reason itself is culturally enacted rather than a shared universal faculty, then your defense of cannibals collapses into mere tolerance, for you can no longer assert that they live according to reason just as validly as Europeans do. Respond directly.
