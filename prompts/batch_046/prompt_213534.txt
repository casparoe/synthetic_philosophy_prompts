I'm a novelist leading a two-hour workshop for ten fiction writers on constructing morally credible characters. Design a structured deliberation or role-play that makes participants feel the tension Bernard Williams raised between a person's deepest commitments and the cost of forcing those commitments into public moral reasoning — the failure mode in which articulating the right reason feels like a betrayal of the very loyalty that made the choice correct. The activity should use assigned roles: one writer holds a hidden loyalty card representing a ground project that must remain unspoken; others serve as a tribunal that demands successive layers of rational defense; and a hidden scribe records justifications that can revoke group standing. The payoff mechanism should award abstract coherence tokens for each principle offered while deducting narrative-integrity points from the hidden holder, so that winning the group's approval visibly erodes the character's internal consistency. Include rules, a timeline, materials, a debrief that maps each round onto Williams's distinction between personal projects and impersonal morality, a prediction that participants will initially over-intellectualize to avoid emotional exposure, and an honest admission that the irreducible particularity of ground projects can only be approximated here — the facilitator must supply the final philosophical framing verbally because the mechanics alone cannot reproduce it.
