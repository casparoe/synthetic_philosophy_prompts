I've come to think Jain anekāntavāda—the many-sidedness doctrine—is best read not as metaphysical pluralism but as a normative protocol for scientific modeling. Specifically, I think every empirical model should be tagged with a syād qualifier: it's perhaps valid from this paramter range, perhaps not outside it, and never the complete story. So scientific progress isn't convergence on one true theory, but accumulating a lattice of qualifed, partial models. What would count against this? I want real defeaters sorted: whether Jain texts actually support applying syādvāda to modern science, whether the view collapses into trivial 'everything is partial' relativism, and whether it fails to distinguish good partial models from bad ones. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a defender of this reading should honestly concede, and tell me if insulating the view by saying well that's just another naya is a problem or the whole point. Separate what's settled in Jain scholarship from what's genuinely contested—don't present my science-modeling twist as standard doctrine, and don't pretend there's a consensus where there isn't one.
