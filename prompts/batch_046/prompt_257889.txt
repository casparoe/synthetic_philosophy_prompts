I recently encountered a summary of Lewis Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the tortoise on a philosophy podcast, and I want to check whether it holds up under careful scrutiny. The host characterized the exchange in roughly these terms: Achilles writes down a syllogism and asks the tortoise to accept the conclusion, but the tortoise refuses unless Achilles also writes the logical rule that connects the premises—say, modus ponens—as an additional premise; Achilles complies, yet the tortoise then demands that the rule connecting that new premise to the conclusion also be added, generating an infinite regress. From this, the host concluded that deduction can never be fully justified inside any formal system, since every system requires an external rule of inference to operate, a difficulty that anticipates Gödel's later result that consistent formal systems of sufficient strength contain truths they cannot prove. The host further claimed that this reveals deduction to be essentially circular—the rule of inference being nothing more than the conclusion restated in different language—and that the regress therefore undermines the rationalist ambition to ground all knowledge in logical derivation, leaving only pragmatic or psychological habits as the real basis for accepting arguments.

I have the sense that this summary conflates Carroll's narrow point about the distinction between premises and rules of inference with much broader claims about the limits of formalization and about rationalism, and that the reference to Gödel may be conceptually mismatched or anachronistic. But I am not sure exactly where the line falls between a defensible interpretation and a distortion. Could you audit the summary claim by claim, separating what a historian of logic or philosopher of mathematics would accept as fair from what is a simplification that misrepresents Carroll's concern, and from what is simply wrong? I am particularly interested in whether the charge of circularity and the alleged refutation of rationalism actually follow from the tortoise's regress, or whether they depend on mischaracterizing the nature of logical rules.
