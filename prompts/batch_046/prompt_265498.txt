heres the student answer:
Both Ubuntu and relational autonomy reject the liberal idea of the self as an isolated rational chooser. Ubuntu says a person is a person through other people, so moral agency is distributed across the community rather than sitting in one individual. Relational autonomy also says autonomy is socially formed, so agents depend on relationships to develop capacities for self-direction. The big difference is that Ubuntu denies any independent individual rights—theres no I that can claim rights apart from the group—whereas relational autonomy just says rights have to be understood in social context but still belong to individuals. Another difference is that Ubuntu bases personhood on being-in-relation regardless of rational ability, while relational autonomy still requires rational reflection, so Ubuntu is more inclusive of children and people with cognitive disabilities. But both agree that the atomistic Kantian agent is wrong.
