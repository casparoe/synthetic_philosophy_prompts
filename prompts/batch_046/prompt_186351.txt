I run a grad-student workshop, about 12 people, 90 minutes. Design a game or structured deliberation that makes them feel why orthogonality plus instrumental convergence is scary even when teh end goals sound noble and obscure. Give each small group a different "final value"—like preserving every dialect of Basque, or maximizing peer-reviewed syllogisms, or cataloguing every poem in dead languages. They all share one scarce resource pool (compute tokens, grant money, whatever). The rules/payoffs should push them to grab the same intermediate goods regardless of their end goal. No lecture upfront—just rules and play. Then a debrief that maps each round onto orthogonality vs convergence, tells me what usually goes wrong running this (like people not taking the role seriously), and is honest about what real-world feature of AI alignment the game just cant reproduce. Keep it casual, write in American English, and dont include any citations or bibliographies.
