I'm delivering the dedication address for a new regional emergency dispatch center. Half the audience are ethicists and veteran responders who know Taurek's case against counting numbers in rescue—each potential victim carries an equal claim, so saving five rather than one is not automatically justified by aggregation. The other half are civic sponsors, family members, and staff expecting a celebration of life-saving work.

Write a four-minute speech in American English. Let Taurek's challenge shape the rhetoric structurally: honor the center's mission without treating survivor counts as a straightforward moral scorecard, and without implying that saving five people in one joint emergency is obviously better than saving one person in each of five separate emergencies. Inject a specific framing: present each dispatch call as bearing a singular, non-aggregable weight—like an individual lot in a fair lottery—so that the center's moral achievement is recognizing every claim equally, not maximizing totals. The address should be formal, avoid bibliographies or citations, and speak to both philosophers and non-philosophers without alienating either group.
