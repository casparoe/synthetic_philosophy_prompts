I work as a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, though I have recently been discussing with colleagues how we describe complex distributed systems. We routinely speak as if a load-balancer intends to distribute requests, or a caching layer believes data is fresh, even though we could in principle describe the same behaviour purely in terms of physical electronics or designed code architecture. This reminded me of Daniel Dennett’s distinction between the physical stance, the design stance, and the intentional stance — the idea that we can understand a system at different explanatory levels. I have come to hold a stronger version of this view specifically for engineered software: I maintain that in sufficiently complex distributed architectures, the intentional stance does not merely provide a convenient predictive shortcut, but actually tracks real patterns of goal-directed behaviour that are genuinely irreducible to either the design stance (the documented modules and APIs) or the physical stance (the underlying hardware). For instance, emergent retry loops or self-healing behaviours that were never explicitly coded but arise from interaction dynamics seem to me to be properly described only by attributing intentions or beliefs to the system as a whole.

Given this position, I would like to know what would genuinely count against it — not just the commonplace objection that such talk is only metaphorical, but the specific observations, formal results, or conceptual arguments that should move a defender. Which cases would show that apparently intentional behaviour in software is always fully recoverable from the design specification plus physical implementation? Are there results in computer science or philosophy of mind about the reducibility of emergent behaviour that bear on this? I want the potential defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, including which ones a committed proponent of this view would themselves concede as damaging, and which they would dismiss as missing the point.

I am also curious whether this position risks being insulated into unfalsifiability — for example, by defining any emergent behaviour as intentional by fiat — and whether that insulation is itself a philosophical weakness for a claim of this kind. Please do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I am interested in the conceptual map of the view’s exposure rather than in references.
