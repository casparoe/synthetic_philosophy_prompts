ok so i've been going back and forth on wether there are actually genuine moral dilemmas — not just hard choices but situations where you're literally required to do A and literally required to do B and they can't both happen. here's roughly where i've landed and i want to know if it hangs together or if i'm smuggling in something that conflicts:

(1) i buy the standard "ought implies can" idea, so if A and B are genuinely incompatible then you can't have all-things-considered obligations to do both. that rules out true dilemmas.

(2) but i also think tragic cases leave a kind of moral remainder — not just emotional residue, but an actual unsatisfied pro tanto obligation to the party you don't help. like, the unchosen patient still has a standing claim that wasn't met, even though you picked the best available option.

(3) i think a moral theory should never output "you're going to violate a duty no matter what." if unavoidable wrongdoing is built into your framework, that's a theoretical failure, not a deep moral fact.

(4) i also think blame requires an all-things-considered wrong, so the agent in these cases is totally blameless — they did the best thing — even though there's still that leftover claim sitting there.

(5) and when reasons are perfectly symmetric, i think there's just no fact of the matter about which option is best; either is permissible. but i don't think that creates a dilemma, just underdetermination.

so does this package hang together? it feels like (2) is treating the leftover claim as a real violated duty, which sounds like a dilemma that (1) said can't exist, and (3) seems to want theories to erase all leftover obligations but (2) says they persist. meanwhile (5) might look like it conflicts with (1) since if there's no right answer maybe both are required? but i think that's just an illusion.

can you audit the tensions for me — which ones are real, which ones dissolve, and what the cheapest fix is? and please write your answer in American English, since i'm pulling this together for a course discussion.
