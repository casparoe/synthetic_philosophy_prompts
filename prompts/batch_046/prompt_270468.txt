I'm a novelist drafting a scene where my protagonist sits on the Meridian Foundation's Scientific Model Review Board. Thursday they vote on whether to fund Dr. K. Lin's integrated climate-economy model, which assumes every economic agent has perfect foresight and zero transaction costs. Half the board calls that classic Friedman — the F-twist, unrealistic assumptions are okay if predictions hold. The other half says its different because this model recommends carbon-tax levels, not just forecasts, so the idealization smuggles in a hidden normative claim about how markets ought to correct. They need an internal memo with an actual bottom line: approve, reject, or approve with conditions.

Please write the memo. I want it somewhat in the weeds — include the concept I'm calling "reflexive distortion," where an idealization doesn't just simplify reality but actually reshapes the institutional behavior the model is meant to guide. Treat the strongest opposing view seriously, probably that predictive accuracy should still be the only criterion even for policy models. No bibliography, no citations, no reading list — this is an internal document, not a seminar paper.

Organize it like this:
- Bottom line and recommendation (around 150 words)
- The case for approval despite the idealizations (around 200 words)
- The reflexive-distortion objection (around 200 words)
- The best rebuttal and why it still falls short (around 150 words)
- Concrete conditions if we go with approval (around 100 words)

Keep the tone formal enough for a board memo but readable for scientists who don't do philosophy. American English, please, and don't worry about perfect grammar — my character writes in a hurry.
