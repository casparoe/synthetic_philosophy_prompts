I'm trying to put a finger on a move I keep seeing in discussions about intuitionistic logic and constructivism, and I don't know if it has a name.

Here's the pattern. Someone is working inside a constructive framework where they reject the law of excluded middle globally. Then they hit a specific statement -- say, whether a particular Diophantine equation has a solution, or whether a given polynomial has a root in some interval -- and they argue that for this statement, bivalence is okay because "in principle we could just check all cases" or "a computer could eventually decide it." They don't actually run the check or provide an algorithm; they just invoke the idea that it's decidable in principle. Then they use that local bivalence to license a proof by contradiction, or to treat the statement as having a definite truth value, all while still claiming they're working constructively.

I've seen this happen with Goldbach-type conjectures, with specific root-existence claims, and even in back-and-forth about whether a weak counterexample applies. The move isn't full-blown classical logic, since they still reject LEM for general claims like "every continuous function is uniformly continuous." And it isn't exactly Markov's principle, as far as I understand it, because Markov's principle is an object-language rule about double-negation elimination for decidable properties, whereas this feels more like a meta-level appeal to eventual decidability to smuggle bivalence into one case.

So does this configuration have an established name? Is it just an informal local version of accepting Markov's principle, or is it closer to how Brouwer uses weak counterexamples, or is it a seperate thing entirely? If there's no precise term, I'd want to distinguish it clearly from both Markov's principle and from plain equivocation between "constructive" and "classical." What exactly are its edges -- for instance, does it apply only when there's an explicit decision procedure in principle, or also when people just feel confident one exists? And if it needs a name, what should we call it?
