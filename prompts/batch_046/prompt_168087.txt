I am currently enrolled in an upper-division course on the historiography of Nazi Germany, and I have encountered a characterization of the intentionalist-functionalist debate that I want to check for fairness. A study guide distributed in my seminar summarizes the disagreement as follows: intentionalists maintain that Hitler operated according to a rigid ideological blueprint for genocide developed by the mid-1920s, and that without his particular brand of biological antisemitism and personal authority the Holocaust would have been impossible. Functionalists, in response, argue that the Nazi state was a polycratic chaos in which radicalization emerged from bureaucratic rivalry and situational pressures rather than from a central design, which implies that any sufficiently authoritarian nationalist leader could have produced functionally equivalent outcomes, rendering Hitler's individual ideology causally redundant. The guide then concludes that, because functionalist analysis demonstrates structural overdetermination, counterfactual questions about a Hitler-less Germany are conceptually confused—structural forces made some form of authoritarian genocide inevitable, so individual counterfactuals are meaningless. I am inclined to think the functionalists are being overstated, but I am not sure where the summary crosses from simplification into misrepresentation. Could you audit this characterization? Specifically, please distinguish: (1) which claims are accurate representations of mainstream intentionalist and functionalist positions; (2) which parts are common pedagogical simplifications that defenders would accept with qualifications; (3) which parts are substantive misrepresentations—particularly regarding whether functionalists deny all causal significance to Hitler or claim inevitability rather than contingency; and (4) whether the guide's conclusion—that structural explanation dissolves the counterfactual question—actually follows from the functionalist premises it describes, or whether it commits an additional logical error of its own.
