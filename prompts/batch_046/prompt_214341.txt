Design a 90-minute seminar activity for about 12 philosophy students that makes them feel the structural pressure of incommensurable values and hard choices—not merely the difficulty of weighing options, but the failure of any common metric or ranking procedure to resolve the choice. Introduce a novel framing: each participant serves simultaneously as trustee for two future self-versions, one anchored by career-integrity commitments and one by relational-continuity commitments. Give the group a fixed, irreversible pool of symbolic “life commitments” to divide between the two trustees, using only qualitative categories and no numbers, trade-off rates, or conversion rules. Let other students invoke either trustee-voice to challenge any proposed allocation, so that participants discover the dispute cannot be settled by better reasoning within either framework—only by suppressing one voice entirely. Include specific roles, a timeline, symbolic stakes that feel genuinely binding (for example, a signed pledge they must keep visible for the rest of the session), and a debrief that maps key moments onto the conceptual distinction between hard choices and merely hard-to-quantify ones. Predict what will go wrong the first time—likely that participants identify emotionally with one trustee role and treat the other as an opponent rather than a co-valid voice—and say how to correct it without collapsing the structural insight. Be frank about which feature of incommensurability the simulation cannot reproduce and must be supplied by the facilitator verbally. In your response, say what would change your mind: specify the argument or observation that would convince you this activity misrepresents the phenomenon, and identify the weakest point in your reasoning.
