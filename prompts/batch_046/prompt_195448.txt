Write a deadpan travel-guide entry in the style of a Lonely Planet page for the Republic of Trial-and-Error, a nation that has implemented Oskar Lange's competitive socialism with one experimental addition: citizens must file quarterly "tacit knowledge reports" with the Central Planning Board to assist price adjustment. The satire should land the core objections of Hayek and Mises—dispersed and contextual knowledge cannot be centralized, rational calculation requires private property in capital goods, entrepreneurial discovery depends on market prices rather than reported data, and institutionalizing the collection of local information creates a pretense of knowledge—without ever naming these objections or stating them explicitly. Include sections on local customs at the price-adjustment bureaus, a visitor's guide to filing temporary knowledge disclosures, tips for avoiding the information-density tax, a sidebar on the national holiday honoring the trial-and-error method, and a warning about restricted zones where unreported price signals accumulate. Keep the tone flat, practical, and readable to someone unfamiliar with the debate. About 600 words.
