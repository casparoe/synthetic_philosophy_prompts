At the same time, the amendment clause itself is usually silent on whether it can be used to abolish the amendment process altogether, and legal scholars often speak as if certain norms are implicitly unamendable even though the text grants unlimited formal power. Meanwhile, in newer institutional designs—such as on-chain governance protocols—smart contracts are written so that token holders can vote to rewrite the contract's own rules, including the voting threshold itself, with no external meta-rule preventing the mechanism from being dissolved in a single vote.

What philosophical commitments about legal authority, sovereignty, and the nature of rules does this combined pattern presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly presupposes from what it merely fits with, and say whether the digital self-amending case actually exposes a contradiction that constitutional lawyers are papering over. If the presupposed view were replaced by a flat proceduralism—where any rule change that follows the current rules is valid—what would have to change in constitutional practice?
