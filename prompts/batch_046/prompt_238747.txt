I'm preparing an interview protocol for a philosophy podcast on normative epistemology. The guest is an unnamed philosopher who has recently defended a refined account of the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem focused specifically on belief-maintenance rather than belief-formation. Rather than treating the distinction as a simple binary between evidential and practical reasons for belief, she proposes a three-part framework: entry reasons (which must be evidential), residence reasons (which preserve a belief within a broader doxastic system), and what she terms "architectural pragmatic considerations"—functional pressures that maintain coherence across an agent's framework when direct evidence is permanently unavailable, a condition she calls "radical epistemic scarcity." She also introduces the notion of "epistemic inheritance," where agents adopt not isolated beliefs but entire evaluative frameworks from communities, and she claims that pragmatic residence reasons apply differently to inherited structures than to individual beliefs. She is skilled at deflecting objections by appealing to the entry/residence split.

Please write a sequence of eight to ten interview questions. Do not include generic biographical or introductory questions. Each question should target the mechanics of her position, anticipate her most likely reply, and include a specific follow-up keyed to that reply. In particular:

- Begin with a concrete case—such as a researcher maintaining a theoretical working model in the absence of new confirming data—that forces her to specify exactly what distinguishes an architectural pragmatic consideration from a mere instrumental benefit.

- Include at least two questions that press the boundary between her residence-reasons category and evidentialism: one asking whether residence reasons are ultimately constrained by prior evidential commitments (and, if so, whether this collapses the distinction), and one asking whether her framework licenses the retention of ideologically closed belief systems.

- Include one question specifically on "epistemic inheritance," asking whether pragmatic residence reasons apply differently when an agent maintains an entire inherited evaluative framework rather than a single belief, and what this implies for the rationality of maintaining community-specific worldviews.

- Include a question that tests the boundary with fideism, asking what prevents her account from justifying the maintenance of religious or political beliefs solely on grounds of social cohesion or personal utility.

- End with a question asking what observable pattern of belief-revision or belief-abandonment behavior would count as evidence against her view, rather than being interpreted as a case of radical epistemic scarcity.

The questions should be accessible to an informed general audience, should not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations, and should not depend on obscure texts or figures. For each question, please note in parentheses the standard reply she is likely to offer and the angle of the follow-up.
