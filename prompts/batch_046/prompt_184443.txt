Let's imagine Kierkegaard were alive today and could observe algorithmically curated social media, where engagement-optimizing recommendation systems amplify outrage and fragment users into niche epistemic bubbles rather than uniting them in a single anonymous crowd. Drawing on his critique of the press and the abstract public—where he argued that modern publicity dissolves individual responsibility into reflective, levelled inaction—how would he likely assess the spiritual and moral condition of this digital public? Specifically, would he view the fragmentation into algorithmically sorted niches as a partial escape from the tyranny of the abstract crowd, or as an even more refined mechanism for self-deception and the evasion of decisive, committed existence?
