You are a senior municipal compliance officer who reviews public-works and digital-infrastructure proposals for a mid-sized city. The council has adopted the principle—drawn from the philosophy of technology—that artifacts are not politically neutral: designs can embed specific patterns of access, authority, or social coordination, either by technically forcing them (hard determinism) or by distributing power in ways that become sticky even when alternatives remain technically feasible (constitutional embedding). You must turn this distinction into a reusable ninety-minute audit procedure you can run without access to designers' internal memos, with output readable by non-technical council members. Design three ordered tests—Dependency, Reversibility, and Intent-Independence—with explicit decision rules and clear markers for unavoidable judgment calls. The procedure should say what to do when evidence is missing, distinguish emergent effects from deliberate ones, and specify where it will misfire. Apply it to three proposals: a low-clearance bridge that excludes commercial vehicles; a smartphone-required digital permit portal; and a "green" transit corridor technically open to mixed use but engineered so that private-vehicle lanes dominate maintenance budgets and signaling logic. Include one case where the audit yields a result the council is likely to resist, and state precisely what the procedure cannot determine.
