Here is the position I am considering. Einstein appears to have held a distinctive synthesis: on one side, a Spinozan, deterministic cosmology in which the universe unfolds according to an impersonal, rational order—what he sometimes called the “Old One”—with no personal God who intervenes in human affairs; on the other side, a denial of metaphysical free will, since he compared human decision-making to the necessary flow of a river. Yet he insisted that moral responsibility, ethical striving, and even a kind of religious awe remain fully intact. His reasoning, as reconstructed by some interpreters, is that responsibility does not require that an agent cause its actions ex nihilo, but rather tracks how well a rational being integrates its behavior into the broader social and cosmic order—something like “compatibilist determinism with a cosmological aesthetic,” a framing rarely discussed outside specialist literature.

What would actually count against this particular synthesis? I am not asking for the standard objections to determinism in general or to Spinoza’s ethics in isolation. Instead, rank the defeaters specifically targeted at Einstein’s combination: for example, whether macro-level quantum indeterminacy undermines his determinism (or whether it is irrelevant because he already granted physical randomness); whether a complete neuroscience of voluntary action that eliminates any meaningful “agent integration” would be fatal; whether his reliance on an impersonal cosmic order collapses into a view he himself rejected; and whether his preservation of moral responsibility is just an ad hoc qualification that insulates the view from falsification. For each potential defeater, say whether a committed defender of this Einsteinian view would concede it as damaging or dismiss it, and explain why. Distinguish the fatal objections from the merely awkward ones. Finally, assess honestly whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, whether that is a genuine problem for a metaphysical view of this kind.
