Since encountering the Everett interpretation in a recent lecture, I have found it impossible to shake off the conceptual gap between quantum suicide and quantum immortality. I would like you to compare these two positions not as rival empirical predictions, but as competing theories of personal persistence across decoherent branches. Specifically, please examine whether quantum immortality presupposes a continuity of consciousness that quantum suicide explicitly severs, and whether either account can coherently reject the notion of a single privileged thread of selfhood in favour of a branching tree with no canonical trunk. I am less interested in standard physical objections than in the philosophical divergence: what does each view commit us to about the nature of first-person indexical reference when the world splits? Please adjudicate the conceptual tension carefully, and indicate where both positions might be missing a more radical option.
