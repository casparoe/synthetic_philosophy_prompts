I'm a compliance auditor for clinical decision committees that incorporate an ML recommendation module. Following any adverse patient outcome, I must file a failure-mode report that classifies the module's contribution using one of three explanatory frames drawn from philosophy of mind: the physical stance (hardware errors, sensor noise, bit-level corruption), the design stance (the behavior was predictably produced by the architecture, objective function, and training distribution without requiring mental-state attribution), or the intentional stance (the module represented a state of affairs and selected an action on the basis of reasons that warrant treating it as an agent with beliefs and goals). I need a reusable operational procedure—executable within thirty minutes using only system logs and committee transcripts, without requiring me to consult engineers—that applies Dennett's real-patterns criterion (that a pattern is real when it supports stable, projectible predictions across contexts) to determine which stance is appropriate. The deliverable should include: (a) an ordered checklist of diagnostic tests, with specific log signatures or behavioral markers that count for or against each stance; (b) three worked hypothetical cases—one clearly physical, one clearly design-level, and one where the procedure compels an intentional-stance classification that I, as the auditor, will find counterintuitive and resist; (c) explicit failure flags indicating when the evidence supports two stances equally, when predictive stability is present but context-dependent, and when the procedure should be suspended pending human judgment; and (d) a brief statement of what the procedure is not designed to do, particularly regarding moral responsibility attribution or legal liability. I have no formal training in philosophy; translate the conceptual distinctions into practitioner language. I will supply my own citations; please do not include a bibliography or reference list.
