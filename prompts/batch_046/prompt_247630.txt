For a seminar on nationalism and historical method I have been asked to write down my own commitments on how old nations are and whether they are invented or discovered, so the group can audit them for consistency. Here is my profile: I accept Benedict Anderson's thesis that every nation is an imagined political community sustained by print-capitalism and shared vernacular culture, and I apply this without exception even to states with centuries of dynastic continuity; I think Michael Billig's banal nationalism is the primary mechanism of reproduction, meaning nationhood is sustained by daily unnoticed habits—flags on packaging, nationally framed weather reports—rather than by explicit elite campaigns; I agree with Anthony Smith that modern nations need a pre-modern ethnie supplying shared myths and symbols to make imagination emotionally sticky, so pure constructivism is inadequate; I hold that a tradition's authenticity depends solely on its present social embedding, not on its chronological age, so a state ceremony designed in 1955 can be as genuinely traditional as a court ritual from 1055; and I routinely defend specific founding dates—say 1868 or 1945—when asked how old a nation is, even though I also think that question is conceptually malformed because it collapses state formation, national consciousness, and ethnic continuity into one timeline. Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs pull against each other and how severely, giving for each tension the cheapest revision that restores consistency; identify one pair whose tension dissolves on inspection and say why; and then say which single change to my profile would clean up the most conflicts. Please respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: item 1 should cover the most severe tension with its pair, severity, and cheapest fix; item 2 the second-most-severe tension in the same format; item 3 the apparent tension that dissolves and the reason; item 4 the remaining genuine tension with severity and fix; and item 5 should state the best single revision and what it costs.
