I'm currently enrolled in an advanced philosophy of science seminar that has spent several weeks on Daniel Dennett's distinction between cranes and skyhooks, together with his accusation that certain reductionist programs are "greedy" in the specific sense of explaining away rather than explaining the phenomena they target. During our discussion of evolutionary explanations of moral judgment—specifically, whether natural-selection accounts of normative cognition must either invoke irreducible, non-natural moral properties (a kind of moral skyhook) or collapse into greedy reductionism that eliminates the authority of moral claims altogether—a fellow student ended a long exchange with the line: "There are no skyhooks—only cranes, and cranes don't greedily reduce, they build." She used this to suggest that evolutionary normativity is unproblematic: if morality is built by cranes, it retains its force without needing mysterious foundations.

I am skeptical that this slogan actually resolves the tension, but I also realize I may be treating it as more precise than it is. Could you lay out, as a disambiguated menu, the distinct precise theses that this combined claim—"no skyhooks, only cranes; cranes build rather than greedily reduce"—might actually express? For each reading, please indicate whether it amounts to a near-tautology about explanatory continuity, a substantive but defensible claim about emergence and autonomy, a false metaphysical denial that any reduction can be greedy, or a normative prescription about theory construction. Crucially, for each reading say whether it genuinely supports the conclusion that evolutionary accounts of moral authority avoid both mysterious foundations and explanatory elimination, or whether it merely changes the subject. Finally, please separate what is broadly settled in contemporary philosophy of biology and philosophy of mind from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any single contested interpretation—particularly regarding whether Dennett's cranes genuinely preserve the normative phenomena they explain—as though it were the consensus view.
