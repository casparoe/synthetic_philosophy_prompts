Exam question:
Endurantists claim that persisting objects are wholly present at each time they exist, whereas perdurantists claim that objects persist by having distinct temporal parts at different times. A recently proposed view—call it processualism—denies that entities such as a 45-minute symphony performance are substances that either endure or perdure; instead, it treats them as processes that unfold over time without requiring any single bearer to be present across moments.

(a) Can an endurantist consistently maintain that the symphony is wholly present at both minute 5 and minute 40, despite having incompatible acoustic properties (for example, loud versus soft) at those times? Address the temporary intrinsics problem and the standard endurantist response.

(b) Does the perdurantist appeal to temporal parts genuinely solve this problem, or does it merely relocate the difficulty?

(c) Is processualism a genuinely distinct third option, or can it be reduced to one of the other two positions? Defend your answer with specific reasoning.

Student answer:
Endurantism holds that an object exists in its entirety at every moment of its lifespan, while perdurantism holds that an object is a four-dimensional entity composed of instantaneous or short-lived temporal parts, each existing only at one time. The temporary intrinsics problem arises because if the symphony is loud at t1 and soft at t2, and loudness is an intrinsic property, then an enduring symphony possesses contradictory properties. The standard endurantist reply is relational: the symphony is loud-at-t1 and soft-at-t2, so there is no contradiction. David Lewis demonstrated that this relational strategy applies to all apparently intrinsic properties, which shows that endurantism is fully consistent and that perdurantism is unnecessary.

Perdurantists avoid contradiction by assigning loudness to the part at t1 and softness to the part at t2. But this only relocates the difficulty: we still need an account of what unifies these parts into one symphony, and the continuity relation they invoke is a primitive that explains nothing. Hence perdurantism fails to solve the problem at its root.

As for processualism: it claims the symphony is not an object but a process. Yet this is really just endurantism applied to processes. The process is wholly present at each stage, but since its stages have different properties, the temporary intrinsics problem returns unchanged. Because processualism offers no new machinery for identity across time, it collapses into endurantism. Therefore the best view is perdurantism, since spatiotemporal continuity of parts at least gives determinate identity conditions.
