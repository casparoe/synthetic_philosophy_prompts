From this, he concludes that requiring AI firms to license their training data is as conceptually confused as charging that student royalties for every book she read. The analogy feels intuitively powerful to me, but I suspect it quietly imports features of human learning that do not carry over to statistical optimization. Can you audit it with precision? Second, identify exactly where the mapping fractures, especially with regard to temporary copies held in GPU memory, the encoding of parameter weights rather than propositional beliefs, the absence of subjective comprehension, and the industrial scale and commercial purpose of deployment. Third, separate the disanalogies that genuinely matter for the ownership question from those that are merely technical curiosities. Finally, please begin with your bottom line in the first sentence—does the conclusion that no licensing obligation exists survive once these breaks are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the entire argument? Then develop the reasoning with no preamble and without restating the question.
