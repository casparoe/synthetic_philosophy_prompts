I hold that Avicenna's flying-man scenario does not establish Cartesian substance dualism but rather reveals a pre-reflective, non-sensory indexical anchor—a bare first-person pointer that functions like a logical constant rather than disclosing an ontologically separable soul. On this reading, the suspended man's awareness of his own existence shows only that self-reference can occur without sensory mediation, supporting an epistemic priority of the first person that remains compatible with embodied cognition, provided we distinguish full bodily awareness from this minimal self-ascription.

To defend this interpretation against the standard reply that the flying man must retain some proprioceptive residue, I have introduced a further distinction: between spatial orientation (a sense of position, extension, and bodily boundaries, which he clearly lacks) and indexical anchoring (the bare structural capacity for self-reference, which he retains). Critics, I suggest, confuse the two when they insist that any intelligible first-person awareness requires a minimal embodied schema.

What would count against this refined position? I want more than the familiar objections that the scenario is incoherent or that he must feel his body. Specifically: what arguments or conceptual results—perhaps from philosophy of language on demonstrative reference, from phenomenology on the body schema, or from philosophy of mind on self-consciousness—would show that even this bare indexical anchoring requires some form of spatial or proprioceptive framing? Could the view collapse into either full dualism (if the anchor turns out to be a substance) or full physicalism (if the anchor turns out to be reducible to neural self-modeling)? Has the position been insulated by allowing any counter-case to be dismissed as merely spatial orientation rather than genuine indexical failure, and if so, is that insulation a virtue or a vice?

Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. For each, indicate whether a committed defender of the refined flying-man interpretation would concede its force or dismiss it, and say whether any of these objections have arguably already been made—without asking me for citations or reading lists. Conclude with an honest assessment of whether the position risks unfalsifiability, and if it does, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind.
