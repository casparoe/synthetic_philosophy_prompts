hey, i'm in a philosphy course on free will this semester and we just covered Galen Strawson's Basic Argument. i tried to extend it and now it feels like it's chewing its own tail. here's the chain i wrote out:

(1) To be truly morally responsible for doing X, you have to be responsible for the mental states that caused X.
(2) That means you have to be responsible for the mental states that caused those ones, and so on back.
(3) The only way that regress stops is if you're causa sui for some mental state — literally the ultimate source of yourself.
(4) Nobody can be causa sui.
(5) So true moral responsability, the kind that involves basic desert, is impossible for anyone.

then i pushed it further and now i'm stuck:

(6) If nobody is morally responsible, then nobody is responsible for what they believe either, including beliefs about free will.
(7) So if someone believes in moral responsibility, they can't be blamed for holding a false belief.
(8) But Strawson presents the Basic Argument as a reason we should drop our belief in moral responsibility.
(9) Giving reasons presupposes that people can recieve them and revise their beliefs.
(10) That kind of reason-responsiveness seems like a form of responsibility — at least epistemic responsibility.
(11) So if (5) is right, there's no point in presenting arguments about it; nobody can be on the hook for accepting or rejecting them.
(12) Therefore either (5) is false, or philosophical argument about free will is basically pointless.

it feels like there's a hidden slide between moral responsibility and epistemic responsibility, or maybe between being blameworthy and being able to respond to reasons? but i'm not sure which step is the actual exit. if i have to keep (1)-(5) intact, what's the cost of rejecting whatever comes after? do i have to deny that reasoning with people requires them to be responsible in any sense? or is there a different move?
