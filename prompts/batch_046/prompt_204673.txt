Give me your actual credence—expressed as a precise number between 0 and 1—that self-deception is genuinely possible in a cognitively normal adult, specifically under the mechanism I will call epistemic escrow: the agent intentionally places a belief that F into a compartment that remains available for instrumental and practical reasoning but is insulated from global belief revision, affective integration, and sincere verbal avowal, so that the agent behaves as if F in action-guiding contexts while sincerely denying F in reflective, self-reporting contexts, without experiencing the two as contradictory.

Decompose your reasoning into at least four sub-questions, assigning each a rough numerical probability and one line of justification:

(a) Conceptual coherence: what is the probability that belief storage is not globally unitary, but can be genuinely partitioned in the way epistemic escrow requires?

(b) Intentional structure: can an agent intentionally initiate and maintain such an escrow without the knowledge required to maintain it undermining the insulation?

(c) Empirical plausibility: does the best psychological evidence for motivated reasoning actually support doxastic partitioning of this kind, rather than only non-doxastic mechanisms such as attentional bias or affective suppression?

(d) Alternative explanations: how likely is it that apparent cases are better explained by partial belief, self-lying, weakness of will, or strategic ignorance rather than by true self-deception?

Show how these terms combine—whether multiplicatively, conditionally, or otherwise—to yield your overall credence. Then state which single sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and specify what kind of conceptual argument or empirical finding would move that term, and therefore your overall credence, the most.
