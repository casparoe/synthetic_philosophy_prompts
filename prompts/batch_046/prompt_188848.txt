I'm an independent writer with no formal philosophy training who has been working through Burke's reflections on prescription and Chesterton's fence analogy on my own for about a year, and I've been invited to present a short paper at a university seminar in political theory and intellectual history. The audience will be mostly graduate students and faculty in conservative political thought, plus one senior scholar of Burke who is known for being sharp on anachronism; they care deeply about historical continuity, they take Chesterton's Catholic social vision seriously rather than treating it as a folksy maxim, and they are instinctively suspicious of anyone who imports eighteenth-century frameworks into modern administrative debates. My thesis is that Chesterton's fence is not a general argument for institutional stasis but an epistemic burden-shifting rule that applies only to structures that have survived at least two generations of explicit, good-faith attempts at reform or removal; when read alongside Burke's distinction between prescriptive rights and merely contractual arrangements, the principle actually licenses rapid dismantling of modern bureaucratic bodies that lack that survival history, because their builders never faced the contestation that would have revealed the hidden failure mode the fence is meant to guard against. I need a prep sheet for the Q&A: about eight objections ordered by how likely and damaging they are in this specific room, each phrased exactly as a Burke scholar or conservative theorist would put it, with a two-minute reply and a frank note on where that reply is weakest. I especially want to be ready for the objection I am hoping nobody asks—whether my two-generation filter covertly smuggles in a progressive criterion of democratic legitimation that Burke himself would reject, and whether I have simply renamed "things I dislike" as "things lacking prescriptive status." Please target objections at the claim that the fence demands active demolition rather than cautious preservation, and avoid giving me generic conservative talking points I could have read in any introductory text.
