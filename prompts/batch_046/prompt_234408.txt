I have been studying Bradley's regress and its relationship to modern debates about ontological grounding and the unity of the proposition. As I understand the argument, if a relation R connects a and b, then R itself must be connected to a and to b by further relations, generating an infinite regress. It seems to me that this problem only gets off the ground if we treat relations as independent entities that stand in need of connection. If we instead adopt an adverbial conception—treating relational structure as a modification of a single substance rather than as an entity linking distinct relata—then there is nothing separate that requires relating, and the regress collapses immediately. Given that this adverbial strategy preserves relational discourse without multiplying entities, why do contemporary metaphysicians still cite Bradley's regress as an independent obstacle to relational ontologies? Does the regress survive in some form that the adverbial approach fails to address, or is its continued prominence mainly a historical artifact rather than a live philosophical problem?
