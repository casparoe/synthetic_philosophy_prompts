Okay so I've been reading this metaontology stuff since I retired from the lab, and I built this argument that feels like it collapses everything into word games. Here it is—tell me where I'm being sloppy, maybe there's an equivocation I can't see.

(1) If two people agree on every conrete fact and prediction, and only disagree on which abstract entities they call "real," then their dispute is just verbal.

(2) Take gauge symmetry in QED. One camp says it's a real physical symmetry, the other says it's pure reduncancy in our math. They both compute the same Lamb shift, same scattering amplitudes, everything.

(3) So by (1) that debate is verbal.

(4) Now apply (1) across physics: spacetime points vs relations, particles vs field excitations, wavefunction realism vs epistemicism. All agree on data, differ only on ontology.

(5) So all those debates are verbal too.

(6) But then physics has no ontological content at all—it's just vocabulary management. That can't be right; we discovered quarks, not just renamed them.

(7) So (1) has to be false.

But I actually like (1)—it feels right for tables and chairs. If I have to choose, I'd rather keep (1) than (4), but I want to know what I'm paying. Is (4) sneaking in an overgeneralization? Or is there a scope shift between (1) and (4) I'm missing? Help me find the exit.
