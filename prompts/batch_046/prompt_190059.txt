I'm an acquisitions editor at a science press, and every month I receive roughly ten to fifteen book proposals and manuscript outlines that claim some trait—behavioral, cognitive, or morphological—is an evolutionary adaptation "designed for" a particular function, often submitted by authors outside evolutionary biology who treat natural selection as the default explanation for any observed feature. I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded specifically in Gould and Lewontin's critique of the adaptationist program and in the conceptual distinctions that critique relies on—spandrels, exaptation, phylogenetic inertia, and developmental constraint—that my editorial team (none of us are evolutionary specialists) can run in about twenty minutes per proposal before deciding whether to advance it, return it for revision, or reject it for uncritical adaptationism. The procedure should take the form of an ordered checklist: the questions to ask in sequence, what kind of evidence counts as a satisfactory answer at each stage, the precise point at which the claim fails and must be stopped, the point at which it is conditionally acceptable but must carry a warning flag, and the explicit junctures where the issue is one of values, framing, or interpretive choice rather than empirical fact and must therefore be handed to an external peer reviewer. Include a small set of concrete diagnostic claim-types or test sentences that an editor can match directly against the manuscript text. Then apply the procedure to three cases: first, a proposal arguing that human aesthetic preference for open savanna landscapes is an adaptation for ancestral habitat selection; second, a proposal arguing that the human chin is a spandrel—an architectural byproduct of jaw reduction and dental restructuring with no independent selective history of its own; and third, a proposal arguing that human language capacity is an exaptation built on pre-existing motor-control circuits, where the procedure should yield an answer the author will likely find unwelcome—for instance, that the claim is structurally coherent but is misclassified as an adaptation rather than an exaptation, and that the manuscript therefore requires fundamental reframing rather than minor correction. Finally, state clearly what this procedure cannot do: which kinds of evolutionary questions fall outside its scope, where a checklist answer will still misfire without specialist biological knowledge, and what remains genuinely undecidable by an editor regardless of how rigorously the steps are followed.
