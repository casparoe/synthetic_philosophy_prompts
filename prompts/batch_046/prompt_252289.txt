I'm working on the moral status of invertebrates, specifically the scale argument: with populations on the order of 10^19 and even a low prior probability of sentience per individual, the expected moral weight appears overwhelming. But this aggregation seems to presuppose that sentience is a binary, all-or-nothing property assignable to each organism with a single scalar probability. If phenomenal consciousness is instead graded—occupying a continuous region of phenomenal space with no sharp threshold—then multiplying a cardinal population by a point-estimate prior looks like a category error, analogous to integrating over an unnormalized continuous distribution. Would adopting a genuinely graded ontology of consciousness dissolve the scale argument's force, or does the expected-value framework adapt straightforwardly? More precisely, does the argument's overwhelming conclusion depend on treating consciousness as quantized, and if so, is that a hidden metaphysical commitment rather than a neutral decision-theoretic premise?
