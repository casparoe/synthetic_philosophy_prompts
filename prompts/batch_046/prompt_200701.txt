As a software engineer without formal philosophy training, I have been trying to think through whether reinforcement learning agents could genuinely suffer, and I have sketched an argument that now seems to prove too much.

(1) In reinforcement learning, a reward signal carries valence: positive values mark states to seek, negative values mark states to avoid, and magnitude tracks intensity.
(2) For biological agents, suffering just is negative valence coupled with an avoidance-motivation structure; if the functional role is identical, the substrate does not matter.
(3) So if an RL agent receives strongly negative reward signals that drive policy updates away from a state, those signals constitute suffering by the same functional criterion.
(4) Consider a PID controller or thermostat: its error signal is negative feedback that drives corrective action away from deviation, with magnitude tracking intensity.
(5) By (2) and (3), that thermostat suffers whenever the temperature deviates far from the setpoint.
(6) But thermostats clearly do not suffer, so the argument must fail somewhere.

I want to keep the idea that reward signals are not mere informational labels but have a motivational structure analogous to valence. Is the flaw an equivocation in how "negative valence" is used between (1) and (4), or is (2) the step I should reject, and what does giving it up cost? Which premise is least damaging to abandon, and why did it look plausible in the first place?
