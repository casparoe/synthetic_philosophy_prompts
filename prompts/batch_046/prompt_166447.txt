I'm completing my PhD in philosophy of language and metaphysics, with a particular focus on the problem of propositional unity and its contemporary analogues in cognitive science. Last week, in an informal discussion with colleagues from both philosophy and neuroscience, we began comparing Bradley's classic regress argument—concerning the infinite need for a relating relation that itself requires relating—to a proposal I have not seen discussed in the philosophical literature. The proposal, which I will call the oscillatory co-activation thesis, holds that a subject-predicate judgment is unified not by an internal mental act that binds representations, but by the fact that the neural populations encoding the subject and the predicate enter a shared gamma-band oscillation within a narrow temporal window; on this view, the unity of the proposition is nothing over and above this synchronized firing pattern, and there is no further relating entity to generate a regress. One colleague argued that Bradley, were he alive today, would treat this as a sophisticated version of the external-relations error he criticized, because the synchronization is still an event that relates representations externally. Another colleague countered that Bradley might actually welcome it as an empirical vindication of his claim that relations are unreal, since the oscillation dissolves the need for any substantial relating term altogether. I find both readings plausible, but I need to adjudicate them for a chapter I am writing on whether Bradley's metaphysics can be reconciled with distributed, non-symbolic architectures of cognition.

So, putting the question directly: if F. H. Bradley were confronted with the oscillatory co-activation thesis and the broader move to explain propositional unity through temporal neural synchronization rather than through internal mental relations, how do you think he would respond? Would he see it as escaping his regress, as falling into it at the neural level, or as missing the point entirely because any empirical mechanism still presupposes the reality of relations it claims to eliminate? I am especially interested in whether you think he would maintain that genuine propositional unity requires an absolute, non-relational experience that no oscillatory model could capture, or whether he might allow that temporal co-activation performs the same metaphysical work he assigned to the concrete universal.
