1. i think advance directives are authoritative mainly because they protect narrative autonomy — your right to shape the arc of your own life, not just avoid pain.
2. i think personal identity is mostly psycholgical continuity plus some social stitching; bare biological persistence alone doesn't keep you the same person.
3. i think if someone with severe dementia is genuinely content and has zero access to their prior values, we should sometimes ignore the directive and prioritize present well-being.
4. i also tend to think of the directive as a kind of moral escrow: the earlier self deposits values, but the care system acts as trustee and can release the obligation if the present patient has become a genuinely different moral subject.
5. i also think promises stay binding even when people change radically, because the obligation came from an agent's act, not from who they are now.
6. and i feel like treating a directive like an absolute sovereign's command over a stranger's body is creepy; it's better to see it as a strong but defeasable preference from someone who used to be intimately tied to this patient.
