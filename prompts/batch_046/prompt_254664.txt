hey, need a quick grading check on a short-answer exam about the socialist calculation debate. the question was: compare Mises's calculation argument with Hayek's knowledge objection, then explain Lange's trial-and-error reply and say whether it actually answers both.

student answer (copy-pasted):

Mises said socialism can't work because without private property there's no prices, so planners can't do rational economic calculation. Hayek added that even with prices, planners can't gather all the dispersed local knowledge that markets use. Lange answered this with his trial-and-error model where a Central Planning Board sets prices, firms follow rules like marginal cost pricing, and prices are adjusted up or down based on shortages and surpluses, kind of like a Walrasian auctioneer but run by the state.

Lange basically solved Mises's problem because now there are prices that reflect scarcity through the adjustment process. For Hayek, Lange said local managers still have the information about production conditions, so the knowledge stays dispersed at the factory level while only prices go to the center. So Hayek's objection is answered too, since the board doesn't need to know everything, just the price signals.

One issue is that Mises eventually conceded in his later writings that algorithmic price calculation could work in principle, which is exactly why he rejected Lange's computer-aided approach — he thought it was technically feasible but missed the entrepreneurial discovery part. Also, the incentive problem for managers is solved in Lange's system through the shadow of the future in repeated interactions between firms and the board, analogous to tit-for-tat cooperation in iterated prisoner's dilemmas, so long-term relationships replace profit motives.
