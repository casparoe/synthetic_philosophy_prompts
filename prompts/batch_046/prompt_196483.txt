For a philosophy-of-mind thread I have been drafting my profile on Dennett's taxonomy—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures—and I want an audit before I post it. Here are my commitments:

(1) I take the four-level taxonomy to be exhaustive for naturally evolved cognition, though I allow artificial minds might realize hybrid configurations with no biological analog.

(2) I hold that Gregorian cognition is not a separate evolutionary grade with its own architecture; it is Popperian cognition that has externalized its hypothesis-generation and error-correction into linguistic and cultural scaffolds.

(3) I believe genuine Popperian status requires the capacity to represent and evaluate counterfactual states that lie outside one's actual learning history—for an artificial system, reasoning beyond the convex hull of its training distribution—and I take current transformer-based models to fail this, functioning as sophisticated Skinnerian interpolators rather than true Popperian reasoners.

(4) I take linguistic self-reference, the ability to model one's own mental states in language, to be sufficient for Gregorian status regardless of substrate or embodiment.

(5) I think phenomenal consciousness is fundamentally a Skinnerian-level phenomenon: an associative integration of sensory and affective states. The apparent explanatory gap is an artifact of Gregorian linguistic scaffolding attempting to report on these Skinnerian processes, so the gap is epistemic rather than ontological.

(6) Therefore I think an artificial system could be Gregorian in form—possessing linguistic self-reference—while remaining Skinnerian in its underlying reasoning architecture, and that such a system might possess consciousness (by 5) without possessing the autonomous counterfactual reasoning I take to ground full moral agency.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, ordered by severity. For each tension, specify exactly what must give—whether a revision to the taxonomy, to the criteria for Popperian status, to the sufficiency conditions for Gregorian classification, or to the account of consciousness—and state what the cheapest fix costs in terms of the remaining commitments. Flag any pair that looks tense but dissolves on inspection; in particular, I am unsure whether allowing artificial hybrids in (1) saves (6) or makes it worse. Do not deliver a general lecture on Dennett or consciousness; trace the conflicts precisely to which commitments pull against which others. End with the single revision that would clean up the most tension at once.
