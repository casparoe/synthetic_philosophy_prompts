So I've been reading about Nyāya and the four pramāṇas, and everyone explains them like tools in a toolkit — perception is the hammer, inference the screwdriver, upamāna the level, śabda the manual. The point is usually that they're irreducible: you can't build a table with just a screwdriver. But I'm stuck on whether this anology actually holds up inside the system. In Nyāya, even direct perception (pratyakṣa) is supposed to have both a nirvikalpaka and savikalpaka stage, which sounds more like one sensor that outputs raw data and then a processor that formats it — not like seperate tools. And inference (anumāna) needs a previously perceived middle term, so it's not independant like a screwdriver is from a hammer. Can you lay out the mapping precisely — what feature of the toolbox corresponds to irreducibility, to domain-specificity, to the idea that a pramāṇa produces its own kind of cognition? Then tell me where the analogy actually breaks, especially around design vs. natural function, and whether the pluralist conclusion — that you need all four and can't reduce testimony to inference, etc. — survives once the interdependence is on the table. Is there a better analogy, maybe sensory modalities or programming interfaces, that keeps the pluralism without the false independence?
