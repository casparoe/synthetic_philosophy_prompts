Human free will, on this view, isn't some magical break in causality; it's just being far enough toward the flexible end that we can revise our own revision-rules. I'm wondering what would actually count against this, not just the standard 'free will is an illusion' line, but the specific intuitions, possible cases, or formal results that would make the gradient collapse. Say, if severe OCD or AI meta-learning loops sit in the middle in a way that scrambles the axis — or if there's a result showing rigidity and flexibility aren't on a single spectrum at all — is that fatal, or can defenders just accomodate it by redrawing the line? Rank the defeaters from fatal (the gradient becomes useless) to merely awkward (like conceding wasps are more adaptable than Hofstadter said). I want to know which ones defenders of the view would themselves admit are damaging, versus which they'd dismiss as framing errors. And please answer in American English.
