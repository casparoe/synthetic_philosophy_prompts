I am preparing a three-month workshop fellowship proposal and need to turn a half-formed idea into a tractable research plan. The topic is Frege's posthumously published political diary, which records strongly nationalist and anti-Semitic sentiments, and whether it bears on his philosophy at all. The standard view treats the diary as biographically embarrassing but philosophically inert, on the ground that formal logic aims at universal validity and is therefore insulated from any speaker's particular commitments. I want to probe that separation more precisely, without resorting to psycho-biography or claiming that prejudice corrupted his mathematics. My specific angle is this: Frege's logical writings suppress indexical and context-sensitive variability—treating expressions such as "I," "today," and demonstratives as incomplete symbols that must be supplemented by an objective context—while his political writings suppress plural particularity by treating the German people as bearing a unique, non-interchangeable historical destiny. The project asks whether these two suppressions share a formal structure: does his philosophical mechanism for absorbing speaker-relative meaning into a universal logical framework conceptually mirror his political mechanism for absorbing group difference into a single national narrative?
