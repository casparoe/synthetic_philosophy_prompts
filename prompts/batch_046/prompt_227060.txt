I am a retired microbiologist who has recently taken up philosophy of history, and I serve on the exhibition review board of the Linden Museum of Natural History. We must render a decision on the proposed permanent gallery "Co-Authors of History: Microbial, Climatic, and Volcanic Agency." The curators advance a framework they call distributive historical agency, according to which non-human forces—pathogens, climate regimes, volcanic events—can function as genuine historical actors when they exhibit directional persistence, selective feedback, and irreversible branching, even in the absence of intentionality; they claim this avoids both anthropocentrism and crude determinism by mapping agency onto functional structure rather than onto minds. A minority of the board contends that extending "agency" in this way collapses the distinction between causal force and actor, invites an environmental determinism that erodes human moral accountability, and risks misleading the public into attributing responsibility to phenomena that cannot bear it. Please draft the board's decision memo—approve, approve with conditions, or reject—recommending a concrete course of action and providing reasoning that the curators could actually respond to. The memo should treat the distributive-agency framework seriously, address the determinism objection directly, and acknowledge the strongest argument on the side you reject.
