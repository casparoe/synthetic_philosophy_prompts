I am writing from the position of a student in an upper-level seminar that moves between philosophy of artificial intelligence and interpretive sociology, and a terminological collision has left me genuinely uncertain about the conceptual geography. In our module on classical AI, we treated the frame problem as McCarthy and Hayes formulated it: an agent that performs an action must update its representation of the world to include the direct effects, yet it appears compelled to represent every property that does not change—an apparently intractable representational burden. In the sociology unit, we read Goffman’s Frame Analysis, where a "frame" is an organizing schema that allows an individual to locate, perceive, and identify occurrences, effectively selecting what is relevant in a social situation without explicit deliberation. Because both literatures speak of "framing" and of the danger of being overwhelmed by irrelevant information, my peers and I have begun using the phrases interchangeably, but I want to know whether that interchangeability survives scrutiny.

Could you compare the AI frame problem and Goffman’s frame analysis with explicit attention to what plays the analogous role in each—representation versus interpretation, state-space versus social situation, automated inference versus tacit cultural competence? I am particularly interested in where the mapping breaks: whether Goffman’s frames are subject to anything like the monotonic or non-monotonic revision required in a planning system, whether the "irrelevant" properties excluded by an AI planner are conceptually parallel to the events a social actor ignores, and whether solving the technical problem of efficient state-update in artificial agents illuminates anything about how human beings stabilize meaning in interaction. Please do not treat the shared word as a mere pun; I want an honest audit of whether there is structural correspondence here, and if the answer is largely negative, what that tells us about why the same vocabulary migrated across disciplines.
