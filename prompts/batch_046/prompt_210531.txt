Consider an argument linking sphexishness—the mechanical rigidity of the digger wasp Sphex, which repeats an invariant burrow-inspection sequence even when the conditions that originally rationalized it have been removed—to the situationist critique and to free will. (1) Define sphexishness precisely as counterfactual invariance: an agent executes a fixed behavioral sub-routine regardless of whether the context that originally rationalized it still genuinely obtains, not merely as repetition. (2) Free will requires non-sphexish flexibility: the capacity to suspend or modify a routine when its rationalizing conditions are absent or only accidentally present. (3) Situationist findings show that human moral conduct is governed more by transient situational cues—framing, affect, social presence—than by stable virtue-traits. (4) The critical move: subjects typically do not alter their behavior in response to reflective recognition that the cue is misleading or that the warrant is missing; they run the "help," "obey," or "harm" script whenever the trigger fires. (5) Hence moral behavior exhibits counterfactual invariance and is genuinely sphexish. (6) So humans lack free will in moral domains. (7) But every practical decision can be decomposed into situation-triggered sub-routines; therefore the argument extends globally and yields free-will eliminativism. (8) Global eliminativism is self-undermining, since the reasoning that produced it presupposes the agent's capacity to evaluate and suspend sub-routines in light of reasons. (9) So the conclusion must be rejected. Which premise or step fails? I want to keep (2) and (8) if possible, but I need to know whether the error lies in (4), which equates situational sensitivity with counterfactual invariance, or in (7), which illegitimately generalizes from moral scripts to all reasoning, and what abandoning either costs for reconciling virtue ethics with a non-illusory conception of agency.
