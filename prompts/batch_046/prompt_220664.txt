You serve as senior philosophical advisor to the National Heritage Registration Appeals Board. The Board must vote by Thursday on Petition 2024-CR-11. The case concerns a large unfired clay sculpture registered in 2018 under the original artist’s design; in 2021 a studio fire destroyed the work, though roughly sixty percent of the original clay was salvaged, now thermally altered and chemically changed. The estate combined this salvaged material with new clay, re-fired the composite, and produced an object that differs in dimensions, surface texture, and structural integrity from the registered piece. The estate petitions to have heritage status transferred to the reconstructed object, arguing that the salvaged clay constitutes the “dominant material trace” and that continuity of matter should prevail over change in form. The holding museum counters that heritage registration requires sortal continuity—the same artistic object with the same essential properties—and that thermal damage, reshaping, and new material break identity, making this at best a derivative work requiring separate evaluation.

Draft the formal written opinion—approximately 900 words—that you will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. Your opinion should:

1. Recommend a concrete course of action (grant, deny, or grant conditionally with explicit conditions and an implementation timeline);
2. Present reasoning in precise but accessible terms, engaging the classic material-constitution problem—the tension between mereological continuity (the clay survives) and sortal-identity conditions (the statue does not)—without assuming specialist knowledge among board members;
3. Include a brief preliminary brainstorming section that maps the relevant distinctions (identity over time, essential versus accidental properties, dominant sortals, and the role of artistic intention in fixing kind membership) before applying them to the case;
4. Treat the estate’s strongest argument with full intellectual charity—explain exactly why material-trace continuity carries genuine metaphysical and emotional weight—before demonstrating why it nonetheless fails to satisfy the Board’s statutory criteria for heritage identity;
5. Conclude with an actionable bottom line: the specific motion the Board should adopt, any deadlines or documentation required (for example, a materials-analysis report or an artist-statement review), and the reasoning summarized in one paragraph suitable for the meeting record.

Write in a formal institutional voice. Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations. Think carefully and work through the philosophical mapping step by step before arriving at your recommendation.
