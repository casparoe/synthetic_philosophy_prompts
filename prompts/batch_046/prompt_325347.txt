I've noticed that in rationalist-adjacent discussions of origin essentialism — especially the "could I have come from a different sperm and egg?" question — people often lean on a computation analogy: you are like a specific binary executable compiled from a particular source file (the genome) at a specific build event (conception). The idea is that if the source file or the build parameters had been different, you'd have a different hash and therefore not "this program," which licenses the essentialist claim that origin is necessary for identity. But this analogy is usually deployed as if development were deterministic compilation, and I think that's where it hides its work.

Can you audit it properly? Give me the explicit correspondence table — genome to source code, zygotic machinery and environment to compiler, phenotype to executable, personal identity to build hash or binary fingerprint — then identify the disanalogies that actually matter for the argument: biological development involves stochastic gene expression, environmental interaction, and epigenetic noise, so the same "source" can yield phenotypically different "binaries"; genomes evolved for robustness and variability, not for reproducible builds; and personal identity, if it maps onto anything computational, is closer to a family of runs with shared inputs than to a single deterministic output. Separate the breaks that undermine the essentialist conclusion from the ones that are merely decorative. Then take a definite, committed position: does origin essentialism about sperm and egg survive once we replace deterministic compilation with a noisy, stochastic development model? Or does the corrected analogy point toward a weaker condition — perhaps that origin fixes a cluster of possible persons rather than a single necessary one? Don't just survey options; argue for the view the corrected analogy actually supports, and say what a better analogy (if any) would replace the compilation picture without smuggling determinism back in. Be explicit about which features of the source domain the analogy was quietly importing and whether those imports are biologically real.
