The maintenance manual for the National Hybrid Array Facility distinguishes two kinds of intervention on its careenium-simmball units. A careenium is the physical lattice of state-transition nodes; a simmball is the symbolic token that tracks a node's configuration in the registry. When a simmball registers an anomaly, the protocol requires a physical inspection of the underlying careenium node only if the symbol has been designated substrate-engaged; otherwise, technicians correct the registry entry without inspecting the hardware. This classification is institutional rather than empirical — it applies uniformly across all units regardless of observed correlation between symbol errors and physical faults. What theory of the symbol-substrate relation does this bifurcated protocol presuppose? Must it assume that symbols can literally push the substrate around, or is it compatible with a view on which symbols are purely descriptive and the distinction is only a pragmatic risk-allocation rule? Reconstruct the implicit ontology, say whether the practice strictly requires it or is philosophically underdetermined between several rival views, identify any internal inconsistencies — for instance, whether the manual treats all symbolic errors consistently or quietly imports different commitments for different symbol types — and describe what the maintenance protocol would have to become if one adopted the view that symbols never causally influence the physical substrate they represent.
