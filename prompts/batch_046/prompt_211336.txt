Edmund Gettier's 1963 counterexamples show that justified true belief is not sufficient for knowledge because a belief can be justified and true only by accident. In the classic formulations, a false lemma enters the reasoning, the conclusion happens to be true for unrelated reasons, and the believer lacks knowledge despite meeting all three conditions. I want to propose a variant that moves the structure from one individual to a research team, to see whether distributing belief across people changes anything essential.

Consider Team Alpha, which studies Compound C using Instrument I. Unbeknownst to them, Instrument I carries an undetected calibration error that makes every reading ten percent too high. Based on these flawed measurements, Team Alpha justifiably concludes that Hypothesis H is false—specifically, that C is toxic at dose D. Meanwhile, Team Beta, working in complete independence, uses Instrument J, which carries the opposite error: every reading is ten percent too low. Team Beta also applies an incorrect statistical correction that, by pure coincidence, cancels out Instrument J's downward bias. Their final dataset therefore matches the true values, and they rigorously justify the opposite conclusion: H is true, so C is safe at D.

Team Alpha reads Team Beta's published replication, examines its documentation, and updates its collective position. The team now holds the belief that C is safe at D, supported by extensive cross-lab justification. In reality, both instruments are broken, both statistical procedures are wrong, and the errors have canceled across the collaboration rather than within any single mind. The team's belief is true, and they possess what looks like excellent justification. Yet the truth of the belief seems accidental relative to the reasoning that produced it, because if either error had been slightly different, the conclusion would have been false.

My central question is whether this "distributed Gettier case" damages the JTB analysis in a way that individual cases do not. Another possibility is that collective belief is just an aggregation of individual beliefs, in which case my scenario collapses into two separate Gettier cases—one for Team Alpha and one for Team Beta—rather than posing a genuinely new problem.
