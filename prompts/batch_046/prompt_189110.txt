I am serving as a peer grader for our high school philosophy elective’s forecasting module, and I need to calibrate my assessment of a classmate’s exam response. Please assign a specific letter grade and provide point-by-point comments that justify it; I want to catch any subtle conceptual errors or misattributions without inventing flaws.

Exam question: Distinguish the “inside view” from the “outside view” in forecasting practice, and explain how the virtue of epistemic modesty relates to each. Then evaluate the following synthesis proposed by a forecasting club that is trying to predict whether the metaethical thesis that moral properties are irreducibly normative will achieve majority acceptance among surveyed professional ethicists within ten years. One member offers an inside-view confidence of 55%, citing the thesis’s recent rigorous defenses and the weaknesses of reductionist alternatives. Another offers an outside-view reference-class rate of 8%, drawn from the historical record of heterodox metaethical positions gaining majority acceptance within a decade. A third member proposes averaging these two figures to obtain roughly 32%, and then applying a uniform 25% downward “modesty adjustment” to yield about 24%, on the grounds that epistemic modesty requires mechanically deflating all subjective probability estimates regardless of their source. Is this synthesis sound?

Student answer (J. M.): The inside view analyzes the particular causal and evidential structure of the case at hand—here, the quality of recent arguments for irreducible normativity, the responses they have received, and the current disciplinary dynamics—while the outside view treats the case as one draw from a reference class and applies statistical base rates, such as the 8% figure. Epistemic modesty is the recognition that one’s own reasoning is fallible, that narratives can be seductive, and that reference classes may be misapplied; it therefore complements both views by urging caution.

J. M. then assesses the synthesis. Averaging the 55% inside-view figure with the 8% outside-view rate is appropriate because the two perspectives provide independent, non-redundant information, and simple arithmetic averaging is the standard integration method recommended by forecasting researchers such as Tetlock for combining qualitative judgments with quantitative base rates. Applying the uniform 25% downward modesty adjustment is also correct: since overconfidence is the dominant bias in human judgment, epistemic modesty demands a mechanical deflation of all probability estimates, irrespective of origin. The resulting 24% is therefore a properly humble forecast that respects both the particular merits of the thesis and the historical difficulty of overturning philosophical consensus. The only minor weakness is that the reference class might need further refinement, but this does not affect the core logic of the method.
