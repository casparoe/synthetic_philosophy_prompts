I've been trying to pin down my view on universals and want to check whether the package actually holds together. Here are my commitments: (1) I'm a sparse realist—only properties that figure in fundamental physics are genuinely universal, and everything else is nominalistic construction. (2) I think the One Over Many argument is sound, but only for these sparse properties; for colours, shapes, and social categories I think resemblance nominalism does all the work. (3) I deny that universals are "wholly present" in each instance in any substantial sense; instead I treat them as abstract structural nodes fixed by their nomic relations, closer to structural realism than to Aristotelian immanence. (4) I accept that qualitative tropes exist for derived properties, but they don't instantiate universals—they cluster causally, and that clustering explains predication without shared entities. (5) I hold that there are genuinely uninstantiated universals (e.g., being a tachyon if tachyons are impossible), and that their possibility is grounded in the law-structure rather than in actual instances.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. For each tension, rate its severity, say what the cheapest revision would be, and then identify which single change would clean up the most problems. I want the answer in British English, and I'm not after a bibliography or reading list—just an audit of the commitments themselves. One thing I'm unsure about: (3) and (4) look like competing theories of properties, but do they actually conflict once you notice they're meant to govern different domains? And is there a hidden tension between accepting One Over Many in (2) while rejecting "wholly present" in (3)?
