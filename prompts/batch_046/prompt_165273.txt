I serve on my university's Student Welfare Resource Board, and we must vote next week on a proposal to restructure our mental-health and disability-access budget according to a severity-weighted, threshold-sensitive prioritarian formula: funding is distributed so that the expected welfare gain per dollar is greatest for students in the worst conditions, with exponential weighting applied below a defined crisis threshold and linear weighting applied above it. Proponents defend this as direct institutional prioritarianism, while opponents—including student-government representatives and two clinical staff members—argue that because our budget is fixed, the formula will necessarily withdraw preventive and maintenance support from students in moderate but stable distress, leaving them measurably worse off without producing any extra benefit for the worst-off that a non-leveling alternative could not achieve; they contend this is not a legitimate trade-off but a practical leveling-down. Draft the board's formal decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—stating a clear recommendation with reasoning that both sides could actually respond to, and including an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Write in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as one would write a formal letter or email.
