If Aristotle were alive today and had to apply his framework of natural slavery and the hierarchy of capacities to three modern phenomena—advanced AI systems that reason without consciousness or biological need, universal basic income that decouples subsistence from labor regardless of cognitive ability, and institutional quotas reserving governance roles for neurodivergent individuals—how would he classify each? Would he treat capable AI as a new kind of natural servant, as a mere instrument, or as something outside his taxonomy entirely? Would he reject unconditional basic income as corrupting the teleological order in which rule and labor are assigned according to practical wisdom? And would he view cognitive-diversity quotas as violating the principle that authority belongs to those with the fullest deliberative capacity? For each case, specify whether he would need to revise his biological determinism, his teleology of human community, or his distinction between servants and tools. Then say explicitly what argument or modern finding—perhaps that his biological determinism was less rigid than the standard reading suggests—would change your reconstruction, and identify where your reasoning is most vulnerable.
