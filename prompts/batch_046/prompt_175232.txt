I have been reflecting lately on Bernard Williams's integrity objection to utilitarianism, particularly after a conversation with my partner about whether loyalty can ever be justified on impartial grounds. The scenario in which a husband saves his drowning wife but pauses to check whether the utility of her survival outweighs that of two strangers has stayed with me; the problem, as I understand it, is not that he takes too long, but that his commitment to her is mediated by an impartial calculus rather than functioning as a direct ground project. I am not looking for a generic joke about philosophers being indecisive; I want a joke that turns specifically on the structure of Williams's critique—the idea that for certain agents, having the utilitarian reason available as a justification for acting on one's own projects is already a corruption of those projects, and that the correct number of thoughts is zero, not one.

Could you write a joke in that vein? The punchline should depend on someone actually understanding that the failure is not mere delay or inefficiency, but that the very presence of the moral calculation undermines the integrity of the attachment. Feel free to set it in an ordinary situation—perhaps a dinner reservation, a misplaced key, or a minor household emergency—where a character's attempt to justify a preference for a loved one via impartial reasoning produces the exact failure Williams describes. The joke should make sense to someone familiar with the integrity objection and the drowning-spouse case, but it should not require knowledge of obscure secondary literature, specific page numbers, or any external source material.
