I'm preparing a twenty-minute job-market talk for an interdisciplinary seminar in philosophy of mind and cognitive ethology. My thesis is that sphexishness—the mechanically rigid, context-insensitive behavioral looping classically illustrated by the sphex wasp—should be reconceived not as the antithesis of free will but as a form of domain-locked rigidity that coexists with, and structurally enables, the domain-general flexibility that compatibilist accounts of moral agency actually require. I argue that human ritual, habit, and algorithmic cognition exhibit precisely this sphexish patterning, and that the normative work lies in distinguishing domain-general reasons-responsiveness from domain-specific mechanical locking, rather than in purging rigidity altogether. The audience is split: compatibilist philosophers of action who defend reasons-responsiveness across contexts and worry about diluting agency, and cognitive ethologists who study behavioral rigidity in invertebrates and are skeptical of armchair analogies to human cognition. They value empirical fidelity to insect behavior, they care about neural and functional distinctions rather than metaphor, and they will press me on whether this reconceptualization changes anything concrete for moral responsibility.

Prepare me for the question period: rank the objections by how likely they are to arise and how damaging each would be, phrase each objection in the vocabulary its source group would actually use (philosophers versus ethologists), indicate what I should concede up front to maintain credibility, provide the reply that must land within roughly ninety seconds, and flag the weakest point in that reply. Make sure to cover the tension between mechanical rigidity and flexibility explicitly. Include the objection I most hope nobody asks—perhaps that my framework collapses into the trivial claim that all behavior contains some rigidity, or that I am ignoring the specific ganglionic and prefrontal architectures that distinguish wasp reflex arcs from human executive control. State clearly what I should not concede even if pressed hard, and note where my argument is genuinely vulnerable rather than merely unpopular.
