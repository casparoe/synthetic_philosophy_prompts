I sit on our town's heritage board and we have to reccomend something concrete by next month about the bronze of Marcus Whitmore in the old square. He funded the free hospital in 1880, but he also bankrolled that eugenics journal and there's new letters where he pushes forced sterilization policies. The statue went up in 1923, paid for by his factory heirs, not the city. Our options are: keep it with a bigger plaque, move it to the museum with full context, or do the "defacement without destruction" thing where we paint the names of people affected by those policies right onto the pedestal but leave the bronze itself untouched. One board member argues that painting the pedestal just turns Whitmore into a monument to protest, so he actually stays centered in the public narrative rather than being decentered. Another says leaving the bronze untouched with only a plaque is worse because it signals the town still values him. There's also the claim that since the heirs paid for it in 1923 it's never been a real public endorsement, just private vanity, but others think that's naive after a century in public space. I need a memo with a clear recommendation and a concrete timeline, and it should take the "defacement keeps him centered" argument seriously instead of brushing it off. Don't just cite philosophers or list theories—give reasoning the board can actually vote on.
