Write a villanelle that takes up Zhuangzi’s butterfly dream not as the familiar skeptical puzzle about distinguishing waking from dreaming, but as an argument that indexical self-reference fails to stabilize across the two states: the “I” that says “I am Zhuangzi dreaming” and the “I” that says “I am the butterfly awake” do not share a single referent, yet neither is deficient or illusory. Use those two claims as your refrains, letting them turn through the poem so that they keep passing each other without convergence, and bring them together in the final couplet in a single line or parallel construction that refuses to rank one as primary. The villanelle’s cyclical returns should enact the continuous transformation (hua) that dissolves the boundary between states not through doubt but through delight—make the turn in the poem move from epistemic anxiety to something closer to joy in the dissolution of the self-boundary. Keep the philosophical rendering accurate enough that someone who knows the Zhuangzi recognizes the conceptual move, but do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or references to specific scholarship.
