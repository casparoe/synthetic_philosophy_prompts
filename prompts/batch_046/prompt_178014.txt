Next month I present at a philosophy workshop on collective action. I'm a retired ecologist turned amateur philosopher, and I'm arguing that the old inefficacy objection to skipping meat or voting gets it wrong if you think about stocastic resonance: individual actions don't scale linearly but can amplify near a social tipping point, like a weak signal riding on noise. So moral responsibility doesn't need universalization or threshold counting; it needs the system to be near criticality. The room will have three effective-altruists who know Parfit and Singer inside out, two economists who want game-theoretic payoffs, and one critical-theory person who'll say I'm blaming individuals for corporate structures. Give me the objections in rough order of how damaging they'll be, phrased like each group would actually say them, with what I should concede, what I should hold, and the weakest empirical spot in my own position that I should defend rather than hope they miss. Especially don't let me forget the objection I'm secretly praying nobody asks.
