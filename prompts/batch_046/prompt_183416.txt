I'm chairing a colloquium Q&A with the author of a recent monograph that applies Bradley's regress to the unity of propositions within modular semantic theories. She maintains that when a proposition is decomposed into discrete semantic modules, each carrying partial content, the relation that binds them into a unified judgment cannot be one of those modules; yet treating it as an external syntactic rule merely relocates the regress into the meta-language. To block this, she introduces an asymmetric dependency loop in which the binding relation is constituted by the very modules it binds, but only under a non-reflexive ordering condition she terms directed unification. She is apt to deflect objections by saying that directed unification is structural rather than predicative. Write me a sequence of interview questions that makes this deflection progressively harder to maintain: begin by pressing whether the ordering condition itself demands a further binding relation; proceed to ask how her account distinguishes benign functional interdependence from vicious circularity in other philosophical domains; and end with a question asking what specific breakdown in propositional comprehension—perhaps in ambiguous anaphora or split-brain report—would count as evidence that directed unification has collapsed rather than been misapplied. Please provide the questions in British English, and keep them tightly focused on the philosophical mechanics of her position, avoiding biographical or generic introductory material.
