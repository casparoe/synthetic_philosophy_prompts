The exam question read as follows. Consider the claim that grounding is irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive, and that well-foundedness requires every chain of ground to terminate in entities that are ungrounded. Now adopt the framework of distributed fundamentality, on which fundamentality is gradational and attached to nodes within a grounding web according to how few external grounds they require, rather than being a binary property of discrete entities. Apply this framework to the case of a fiat currency—call it the drachma—whose purchasing power is jointly grounded in (i) widespread collective intentional states and (ii) the physical properties of printed tokens, with neither factor alone sufficient to ground the monetary fact. First, does this joint-grounding structure threaten irreflexivity or asymmetry? Second, can the monetary fact itself serve as a ground for further social facts—say, that a specific debt is denominated in drachmas—without violating well-foundedness, given that its own grounds include non-fundamental intentions and artifacts? Third, does accommodating this case require priority pluralism, or can a monist treat the intentions-plus-artifacts complex as a single, internally structured fundamental base?

The student answered: Joint grounding poses no threat to irreflexivity, since the currency fact is numerically distinct from both the collective intentions and the printed artifacts; none is identical to another, so no fact grounds itself. Asymmetry is preserved because the monetary fact can ground derivative obligations—e.g., that a debt is denominated in drachmas—while those obligations do not in turn ground the currency itself, maintaining directional order. Regarding well-foundedness, intentions are grounded in neurobiological states and artifacts in physical structures, and these chains eventually terminate in fundamental physical entities, presumably elementary particles or fields, so the intermediate jointly grounded node introduces no infinite regress. The case is therefore compatible with both pluralism and monism: a pluralist can treat intentions and artifacts as separate fundamental bases that jointly yield the currency fact, while a monist can treat the entire complex as one internally structured fundamental whole. Nevertheless, distributed fundamentality reveals that fundamentality is gradational: intentions and artifacts are partly fundamental because they sit near the bottom of the web with relatively few external grounds. This undermines traditional monism with its single root entity. Indeed, Schaffer himself rejects linear grounding chains in favor of such distributed webs, which means well-foundedness should be reconceived as a holistic property of the entire web rather than as termination in discrete entities. Hence the monetary example supports distributed fundamentality and shows that joint grounding is best accommodated within a pluralist, web-based ontology.
