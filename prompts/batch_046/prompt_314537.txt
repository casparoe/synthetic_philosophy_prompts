lately i've been really convinced by Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model — the idea that creativity is basically sliding paramaters around in some high-dimensional concept space, tweaking dials like metaphor density or emotional valence until the mix sounds new. since i started writing songs i've seen it everywhere: even a 'breakthrough' feels like just turning a bunch of dails at once rather than jumping to a whole new board. i've started extending it to moral reasoning too — ethical innovation is just adjusting the weights on care, fairness, loyalty, etc. — and now i'm worried i've talked myself into something unfalsifiable. so what would actually count against it? i don't want the standard 'but mozart invented opera' line; i want the deeper defeaters. are there cases where genuinely novel concepts or emotions appear that can't be reconstructed as interpolation in the space? what do results in cognitive science or formal concept-space models say about real discontinuities? rank the defeaters for me: fatal, awkward, or ones a die-hard knob-twiddler would just absorb by saying 'that was a hidden dial all along'. also be honest — has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability, and is that actually a problem for a theory of this kind?
