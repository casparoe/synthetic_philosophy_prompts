I've been trying to sort out my actual view on well-being for a seminar paper and I want to check if my commitments hang together before I embarass myself in front of people who actually read the litterature. Here is what I actually think, not the polished version: (1) well-being is fundamentally phenomenal—two lives with identical experiential profiles have identical welfare, full stop. (2) The experience machine objection misses because Nozick treats being in touch with reality as an intrinsic good without arguing for it; it's at best an instumental preference that people happen to have. (3) Still, I think narrative coherence and autobiograpical ownership are necessary for human well-being, not just instrumental—without a unified story your pleasures don't add up to a good life. (4) But I also think that if you spend your whole life in a well-designed machine, never knowing, your well-being is only marginally lower—maybe ninety-five percent—because the experiential profile is preserved and the narrative you construct is internally coherent. (5) I reject desire-satisfaction theories because adaptive preferences show wanting isn't enough, yet I also hold that a stable, reflective desire to stay in the machine is authoritative about one's welfare. (6) I think infants and animals have well-being without narrative capacities, but that's just sentience-well-being, a different category that doesn't apply to adult humans. (7) Finally, I hold that virtual achievements contribute to well-being if they feel real from the inside, but I deny that authenticity requires metaphysical freedom—just that you are the causal source of your actions, which the machine actually preserves since it's responding to your choices. I also have this odd side-idea that well-being involves a diachronic transparency condition, meaning a good life has to be retrospectively evaluable without cognitive dissonance, which I think is satisfied in the machine but often broken in real life by hidden failures.

Do these hang together? I feel like there's a tension between my phenomenalism and my narrative-condition, and another between saying virtual achievements count and saying reality-contact isn't intrinsically valuable. But maybe some of these only look bad. Can you audit the pairs, rank the tensions by severity, say for each what has to give and what's the cheapest revision? Don't lecture me on hedonism versus objective list—just trace what my own commitments commit me to. And tell me whether that diachronic transparency idea fits or breaks things further.
