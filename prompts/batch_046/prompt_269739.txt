I was talking with my officemate and this other grad student in teh phil of mind seminar about Dennett's two-bitser example and whether it actually lands a punch against Searle's Chinese Room. We got into a kind of circular arguement and I want an outside take.

My officemate said that if you scale up the two-bitser architecture—just two state-flipping nodes coupled to sensors—into a self-modifying population embedded in an unsupervised environment, the bit-transitions stop being mere syntax interpreted by us and become causally anchored to survival-relevant conditions. She thinks this gives the system original intentionality, not just derived, because the "belief" that resource-R-is-near is now selected for by ecological feedback rather than assigned by a designer. So Searle's syntax-is-not-semantics objection misses the point: the semantics comes from functional-ecological embedding, not from an interpreter's projection.

The other student pushed back hard. He said Dennett never intended the two-bitser to have original intentionality—it's always an intentional stance adoption, derived from our predictive needs. Even if you put a million of them in an evolutionary loop, you're just distributing the derivation across history and population; the individual unit still lacks understanding, and the teleology itself is either observer-relative or requires controversial evolutionary intentionality. He thinks my officemate is smuggling in original intentionality through teh back door by calling ecological selection "semantic anchoring." Neither of them would budge on whether the scaled-up bitser counts as a genuine counterexample to Searle or just a more complex case of derived intentionality.

Who's actually right about which specific claim? Is there a missing distinction—maybe between distributed and individual intentionality, or between evolutionary selection and genuine aboutness—that both sides are glossing over?
