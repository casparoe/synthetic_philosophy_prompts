I want you to act as an advisor to a philosophy-of-science research group that has three years and two postdoctoral positions to devote to conceptual—not empirical—work on cognitive penetration of perception, especially as it bears on the epistemology of scientific observation. Rather than a comprehensive survey, deliver a defended agenda: identify the three or four open questions, conceptual distinctions, or lines of work that should receive the bulk of this limited effort, rank them, and justify the ordering.

Your defense should make explicit the criteria you are using—such as tractability for philosophers rather than neuroscientists, how many other debates depend on resolving this question, and what kind of theoretical or empirical development over the next few years would cause you to reorder the list. Among the candidates, please address whether the long-running distinction between architectural cognitive penetration (a breach of Fodorian modularity) and functional penetration (informational top-down influence without architectural breach) remains load-bearing in light of predictive-processing models of perception, or whether it has collapsed into a terminological dispute that now distracts from more productive questions—such as how to normatively evaluate the reliability of penetrated perceptual judgments in experimental settings. Treat that framing issue as one line of work competing with others for priority.

Please clearly separate what is settled in this domain—e.g., that some form of top-down influence on perceptual processing is well documented—from what is genuinely contested among specialists in philosophy of perception and cognitive science. Do not present any contested interpretation of predictive processing, of modularity, or of the normative implications for science as though it were the consensus view.
