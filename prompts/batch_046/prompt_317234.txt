I've been thinking about Daniel Dennett's intentional stance in the context of large language models, particularly after conversations with friends who work on mechanistic interpretability. They pointed out that researchers can identify specific circuits inside transformer architectures that encode propositional content—representations corresponding to claims like Paris being the capital of France—and that these representations causally influence outputs within a given context window. This led me to wonder whether identifying such circuits obligates us to adopt the intentional stance toward these systems, at least in a localized form.

My reasoning proceeds as follows. Dennett holds that attributing beliefs and desires is justified when it yields better predictions than the design or physical stance. For LLMs, this predictive utility seems obvious: we routinely talk about what a model believes regarding a query or what it wants to complete, and these attributions help us anticipate behavior. Yet Dennett also requires approximate rationality—the system should behave roughly as a rational agent with those mental states would. Here I think I encounter a contradiction. Large language models do not maintain a globally coherent belief web. They can endorse a claim in one prompt and deny it in another, with no cross-contextual updating mechanism linking the inferences. They display what I have started calling epistemic fragmentation: locally coherent reasoning embedded in an architecture that lacks global consistency.

This leaves me with what feels like a forced dichotomy. If global rationality is a necessary precondition for the intentional stance, then LLMs fail it, which means our belief-attributions are merely pragmatic fictions lacking genuine predictive grounding. But if we relax the condition to allow only localized rationality—treating each forward pass as a miniature rational agent—haven't we emptied Dennett's framework of its explanatory force? The stance was meant to sit between brute physics and pure instrumentalism, not to license projection onto every isolated computation.

I suspect I am mischaracterizing the rationality requirement, or perhaps conflating mechanistic transparency with philosophical commitment. Specifically, does finding a causally active representation justify attributing the corresponding belief only within that inference context, even when the model contradicts itself elsewhere? Or does the absence of cross-contextual integration require us to reject belief-attribution altogether and stay at the design stance? Please respond in American English, and help me locate the precise confusion in my framing.
