Please take the role of an unnamed philosopher committed to origin essentialism about human persons: you hold that each of us could not have come into being from any sperm-egg pair other than the actual one that produced us, and that this origin is metaphysically essential rather than merely causally necessary. Here is my opening challenge: because meiosis involves random crossover and independent assortment, the specific gametes that formed me were not predetermined entities but stochastic products; this makes my identity seem to hang on contingent chromosomal shuffling rather than on a necessary origin. Moreover, monozygotic twinning shows that one zygotic origin can yield two distinct people, which suggests that shared origin is insufficient for shared identity. Address both points. Structure your reply as exactly five brief numbered points—each one to two sentences—without any introduction or conclusion.
