Analyze the January 6, 2021, breach of the U.S. Capitol through Spinoza's democratic theory, drawing especially on his arguments in the Theological-Political Treatise and Political Treatise that democracy is the most natural form of government, that the multitude can be guided by reason or overwhelmed by fear and superstition, and that democratic stability requires shared rational understandings—common notions—rather than mere procedural voting. Examine whether the event is best explained by Spinoza's framework as an affective failure: an irrational crowd driven by false beliefs and emotional contagion undermining institutional reason. Identify the relevant actors—the crowd, representatives, the leader—and the options a Spinozist account would recognize, such as expanding expressive freedom versus strengthening deliberative institutions. Also assess where the theory is silent or strained, particularly regarding modern disinformation architectures and charismatic authority that Spinoza did not anticipate. Finally, state whether the episode confirms, complicates, or undermines Spinoza's claim that democracy survives only under specific affective and epistemic conditions.
