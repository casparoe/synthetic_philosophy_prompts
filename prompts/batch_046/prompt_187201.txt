Consider a statutory heritage-protection scheme that evaluates candidate landscapes against two independent criteria—ecological integrity and historical continuity—each governed by its own non-negotiable threshold. A landscape receives protected status only if it exceeds the threshold on at least one criterion; there is no composite index, no permitted exchange rate between the two values, and no mechanism for weighing moderate achievement on both axes against exceptional achievement on one. Consequently, a site that scores moderately on both ecological and historical measures, and might plausibly be said to possess substantial overall worth, is denied protection altogether.

What philosophical view of value and practical reasoning does this institutional design presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory: does it commit the scheme to the claim that ecological and historical goods are genuinely incommensurable, or could a value-pluralist who accepts in-principle commensurability defend the same architecture on grounds of tractability, democratic legitimacy, or the avoidance of moral corruption? Is the “threshold-only, no-trade-off” rule best read as an admission that hard choices between values are unavoidable and must be compartmentalised, or as an attempt to eliminate hard choices altogether? Carefully distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates, and identify any apparent tension between the scheme’s refusal to aggregate values and its implicit claim to be distributing protection justly.

Then, suppose the underlying philosophical framework were replaced by one that treats all values as ultimately comparable on a single metric of human flourishing or well-being. Which specific institutional features—the dual thresholds, the prohibition on aggregation, the all-or-nothing protection status—would necessarily have to change, and which might survive for independent administrative reasons? Finally, assess whether the scheme is philosophically underdetermined: could it be licensed equally well by a non-commensurable pluralist view, by a pragmatic anti-theory stance, or by a hidden monist view that uses the two criteria as imperfect proxies for a unitary good?
