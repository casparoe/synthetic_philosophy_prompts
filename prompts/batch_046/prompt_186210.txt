I've been thinking about this thing I call "value leakage" -- when you're comparing two genuinely different goods (like epistemic rigor vs researcher autonomy) and people keep smuggling in a hidden metric like total papers published to make the comparison work, even though that metric doesn't capture either good properly. I'm on the review board for an open-science co-op (very rationalist-flavored, we do our own peer review) and we get proposals that pit incommensurable values against each other every month. I want a reusable decision proceedure -- like a checklist or flowchart in words -- that my board can actually run in ten minutes before a vote. It needs to distinguish real incommensurability from just "hard to measure," flag when someone's using a leaked metric, say what to do when there's genuinely no common scale (vote? defer? split the difference?), and mark exactly where the proceedure has to hand off to human judgment because it's values, not facts. Ground it in actual philosophy of value pluralism and hard choices -- Ruth Chang's point that hard choices aren't just missing information, Berlin's pluralism, whatever actually applies -- but make it operational, not theoretical. Then run it on three proposals: requiring pre-registration for all studies vs letting researchers explore freely; anonymized peer review vs open signed review for accountability; and one where the proceedure outputs "these values can't be traded off" but the board still has to decide anyway because of catastrophic risk (like capping safety funding to preserve equity across domains even when the risk looks existential). Include the misfire cases and be honest about where it breaks.
