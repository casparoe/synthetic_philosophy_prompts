By profession I am a software engineer, and my native conceptual vocabulary is that of iterative system design: A/B testing, canary deployments, feedback loops, rollback protocols, accumulated technical debt, and distributed consensus mechanisms. Please restate Dewey's position—specifically his claim that democracy is fundamentally a mode of cooperative social intelligence rather than a mechanism for aggregating pre-formed individual preferences; that a public is constituted by indirect consequences requiring collective institutional control; and that sound policy should be treated as a revisable hypothesis enjoying only provisional justification rather than final certainty—using only the vocabulary and conceptual structures of agile development and distributed system engineering. For each element—the formation of the problem-public, the epistemic status of policy outcomes, the function of disagreement within the process, and the institutional mechanisms for revision—indicate what translates cleanly, what must be distorted to fit (for example, whether democratic conflict is reconceived as a system failure to be patched), and what has no counterpart whatsoever in engineering practice. Then provide a residue report: does the untranslatable remainder indicate that engineering frameworks genuinely lack resources for conceptualizing democratic legitimacy, or does it reveal that Dewey's normative commitments are carrying weight that procedural vocabulary cannot bear? I want to know precisely where the translation breaks down and whether the break is instructive.
