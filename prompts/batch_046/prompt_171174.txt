Next thursday I'm presenting a draft at our decision-theory reading group about Hofstadter's Luring Lottery - the setup where a bunch of people each decide whether to enter, and if exactly one enters they get a billion but if more then one enters nobody gets anything. My view is that the standard superrational answer - just randomize your abstention with flat probability - misses the point because it treats every agent like an isolated reasoning node with no structure. Instead I want to defend a clustering view: you should condition your choice on your epistemic position within the group, using a shared prior over how similar thier reasoning processes are. Basically, if you're near the center of the cluster and your thinking looks like everyone else's, you stay out; if you're the outlier with the weirdest reasoning path, you're the one who should enter. This handles noisy environments where pure correlation breaks down, and it doesn't need telepathy or direct causal links.
