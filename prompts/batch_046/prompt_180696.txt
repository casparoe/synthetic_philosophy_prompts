I have come to accept the standard reading of Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the principle of alternative possibilities: if an agent acts for reasons of his own while an external controller stands ready to intervene should he hesitate—yet never actually does—then the agent remains morally responsible despite lacking any genuinely open alternative. From this I am inclined to draw a stronger conclusion, namely that causal determinism poses no special threat to responsibility either, since the only obstacle Frankfurt cases needed to remove was the presence of blocked alternatives. But I want to test whether that stronger inference is actually licensed. To do so, I propose distinguishing structural blockage, in which an alternative is made impossible by an external mechanism that remains causally inert in the actual sequence, from causal closure, in which the agent's mental states deterministically entail the action with no branching possibilities anywhere in the chain. The Frankfurt scenario clearly involves the former, yet it seems silent on the latter. What would have to be true for the anti-PAP argument to fail to bridge that gap? Specifically, which features of the original setup—Black's dormancy, the counterfactual dependence of intervention on Jones's prior mental state, the fact that Jones's will is not itself produced by the controller—are carrying the moral weight, and which of these could be removed without collapsing the intuition of responsibility?

If we imagine a variant case—call it pure causal closure—in which there is no external intervener at all, but the agent's decision process is deterministically fixed by prior states, does the Frankfurt result still apply, or does responsibility now depend on something other than modal availability, such as the source or origin of the action? I want an honest assessment of how exposed the standard conclusion is: which assumptions a committed Frankfurt defender must concede are live rather than insulated, whether they would accept that pure causal closure presents a distinct problem that their argument does not solve, and what kind of finding—perhaps about agent-causation, sourcehood, or the metaphysics of mental causation—would actually count as a decisive defeater rather than merely an awkward complication. Rank the vulnerabilities from fatal to manageable, and indicate where the defender would dismiss the objection as changing the subject.
