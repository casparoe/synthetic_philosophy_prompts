Compare Hegel's notion of the cunning of reason in world history with the concept of normative path dependence—the idea that the moral trajectory of institutions depends not on an overarching purpose but on the sequential locking-in of earlier normative commitments, such that later outcomes are constrained by prior frameworks regardless of their current moral merit. In particular, assess whether normative path dependence can be reconciled with a weak, non-providential teleology (history tends toward justice not by design but by the cumulative pressure of internal contradictions) or whether it undermines any claim that history has a moral direction at all. To ground the comparison, construct a hypothetical case: imagine a legal practice initially justified by a theological doctrine that gradually loses its religious support but persists for centuries through bureaucratic inertia and incremental reform, eventually being abolished not by a single revolutionary insight but by the slow exhaustion of its supporting institutions. Analyze how each framework explains the timing and moral significance of the abolition, and identify which features of the case each framework must treat as accidental rather than essential.
