Write a joke that plays on a specific tension in Confucian role ethics. The joke should hinge on the doctrine that moral obligations are generated by one's position within the five cardinal relationships (wulun)—father and son, ruler and subject, husband and wife, elder and younger sibling, and friend and friend—rather than by universal moral rules. Introduce a modern scenario in which a person occupies a role that has no direct classical analogue—say, a cross-functional product manager who is simultaneously a "subject" to an executive, an "elder sibling" to junior staff, and a "friend" to a peer contractor—so that rectification of names (zhengming) cannot fix the obligations. The punchline should turn on the actual logic of role ethics: that failing to perform the specific reciprocal duty tied to one's named role (for instance, showing filial deference or benevolent authority) is the core moral failure, not breaking an abstract principle. Make the joke polished, conceptually precise, and funny mainly to someone who already grasps that in this framework morality is relational and asymmetrical rather than rule-based.
