Please explain the conceptual tension between Schrödinger's Vedantic monism—according to which individual consciousnesses are illusory pluralizations of one universal awareness—and the observer-dependence he emphasized in quantum mechanics. If all observers are expressions of a single mind, why does measurement appear to produce definite, mutually incompatible outcomes for different observers rather than one perspectiveless state? Address whether Vedanta resolves the measurement problem by dissolving it into illusion, or whether it leaves unexplained the structural appearance of branching reports, and identify the weakest premise in that dissolution.
