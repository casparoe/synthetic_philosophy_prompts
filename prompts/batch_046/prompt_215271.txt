Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in the archaeology and anthropology of societal change: the Discontinuity Camp, which holds that societies can undergo genuine collapse—a rapid, systemic loss of sociopolitical complexity, integrative institutions, and often welfare, with the Late Bronze Age Eastern Mediterranean, the Terminal Classic Maya Lowlands, and Rapa Nui (Easter Island) serving as central examples; and the Continuity Camp, which holds that "collapse" is an etic, observer-imposed reification, that what actually occurs is adaptive reorganization, political fragmentation, or decentralization with substantial cultural and demographic continuity, and that labeling these transitions "failure" imposes modern state-centric norms on past societies.

Structure the response in four named sections, in this exact order:

Treat these four cruxes:

(iv) The Bronze Age Synchronization Crux: whether the Late Bronze Age disruptions represent an interconnected system collapse (interdependent palace economies falling together through network failure) or a cluster of local adjustments to broader Mediterranean trade and climate shifts.
