Consider the epistemic bootstrapping problem: using outputs from a cognitive faculty to justify that faculty’s own reliability appears viciously circular. One proposed resolution distinguishes source-circularity (employing the faculty itself to validate its outputs) from target-circularity (using one specific output to validate another specific output). A further refinement introduces environmental calibration entitlement: the thesis that sustained successful operation within a particular environment generates a non-evidential entitlement to treat that environment as falling within the faculty’s proper domain, prior to justifying any particular belief formed there. Once a particular belief is formed inside that calibrated domain, it may be used—without circularity—to confirm that the environment indeed belongs to the domain, thereby indirectly supporting the faculty’s reliability in that context, but not serving as independent evidence for the faculty in general.

Question: Does this calibration-based strategy genuinely dissolve the bootstrapping problem, or does it merely relocate the circularity from the belief-level to the domain-level? Student answer: The calibration strategy succeeds because it cleanly separates domain-validation from belief-validation. Source-circularity occurs when a faculty is used to justify itself directly, and target-circularity occurs when one belief validates another; calibration avoids both because the entitlement derives from sustained operational success across the environment, not from any specific belief-state, so it is structurally non-evidential. This mirrors the claim by prominent entitlement theorists that basic rational entitlements are features of agency rather than pieces of evidence. Once the domain is calibrated, particular beliefs formed within it acquire justification through the entitlement framework, and those beliefs can then refine the calibration parameters. The direction of support runs from the calibrated environment back to the faculty’s domain-boundary, not from an individual perceptual belief back to itself, so there is no vicious loop—only a benign iterative refinement. The argument does depend on assuming environmental stability, but that is a practical boundary condition rather than an epistemic circularity. Consequently, reliability should be understood as domain-relative, and the bootstrapping problem is resolved by placing entitlement prior to, and independent of, evidential justification.
