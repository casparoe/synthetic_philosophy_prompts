I've been reading philosophy of economics since I retired from the physics lab, and I've tied myself in knots over pure time preference. Here's the argument I built — it feels like it proves we should neglect everyone alive today, which can't be right, but I can't spot the leak.

(1) A rational preference structure ought to be invariant under purely indexical shifts, just as physical laws don't depend on you're reference frame.

(2) "Now" is purely indexical — it's my temporal origin, no different in kind from "here" being my spatial origin.

(3) So if I prefer £100 now over £110 in a year solely because of that origin, I'm making my rationality depend on frame, which is structurally irrational — like claiming momentum changes because you moved your ruler.

(4) Therefore pure time preference is out; the only legitimate discounts are risk, growth, and perhaps breakdown in personal identity.

(5) But on reductionist views, my link to me-in-30-years is just psychological continuity, which fades gradually; there's no sharp boundary.

(6) So I should actually discount my own far-future self more heavily then I discount strangers who are psychologically continous with me, not less.

(7) Meanwhile, if we apply impartiality across all people, future generations deserve equal moral weight.

(8) Given standard population projections, there could be 10^40 or more sentient beings in the far future.

(9) With zero pure time preference, saving one life in 1000 years is therefore as important as saving one now, and there are astronomically more of them.

(10) So rationality demands I neglect present suffering — let a child drown today to fund a fusion reactor that helps those future beings — which is clearly absurd.

(11) So (3) must be wrong? But if I reject indexical neutrality for time, I'm saying "now" has a special status that "here" lacks, and I can't see any non-prejudicial reason for that.

Which step do I actually reject, and what's the cost? I'd favour keeping (1) and (10) if I can, but I feel like I'm cheating somewhere in the middle — maybe between (6) and (9).

Please also say what would change your mind: which consideration or argument would make you pick a different step, and where your own reasoning is shakiest. I realise this is a bit of a tangle, but I'd value your view.
