I came across a summary in an unnamed popular-philosophy article that frames the procreation asymmetry like this: "The asymmetry holds that creating a suffering child is wrong because there is an actual victim who is harmed, whereas not creating a happy child is not wrong because there is no actual victim who is deprived. From this, Benatar derives anti-natalism: since every life contains at least some suffering, the harm-avoidance reason always applies, but because merely possible people lack moral standing under a potentiality principle, there is never a counterbalancing reason to reproduce. Critics who reply that existing people are grateful for being born misunderstand the argument, which operates at an impersonal level rather than from the first-person perspective of the living. The summary also suggests that this asymmetry is structurally identical to Parfit's non-identity problem, just restricted to reproductive ethics rather than future-generations policy, so anyone who accepts Parfit's solution should accept Benatar's conclusion." Something about this feels too tidy, especially the link to Parfit and the mention of a potentiality principle. I am inclined to think the asymmetry captures a real intuition but that Benatar's anti-natalism requires more than just this. Could you audit the summary for accuracy?
