After years of secularism I have recently begun attending synagogue and reading philosophy of religion, and a professor summarized Hilary Putnam's religious stance as follows: "Putnam argued that religious claims are neither true nor false in any scheme-independent way, because his internal realism rejects a mind-independent world against which they could be measured; religious language is therefore best understood as a practical vocabulary for ethical orientation. His own return to Jewish observance was not a metaphysical commitment to God's literal existence, but an adoption of a coherent conceptual scheme that organizes moral life without ontological baggage. So one can be fully religious while denying that God exists outside the framework of one's practice." This sounds suspiciously like it collapses Putnam into a pure instrumentalist about religion. Is it a fair summary? Please distinguish what correctly captures his internal realism and conceptual relativity as applied to religious discourse, what misrepresents his view of cognitive content and ontological commitment within a scheme, and whether his actual return to Judaism supports the claim that he rejected all metaphysical baggage.
