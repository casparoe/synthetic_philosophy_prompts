Consider Zhuangzi’s refusal to mourn his wife on the grounds that life and death are merely successive phases of a single transformation (hua), together with his image of the body as a temporary lodging and his butterfly-dream skepticism about any fixed personal identity. Now suppose he is confronted with modern neurocryopreservation followed by substrate-independent mind uploading: a biological brain is vitrified, then its structural and functional patterns are eventually instantiated in a synthetic substrate, with no biological continuity linking the frozen corpse to the running program. Would Zhuangzi regard this as a genuine death interrupted by technological resurrection, as an unbroken transformation in which only the lodging has changed, or as a category mistake—because the self, on his view, was never anchored in biological tissue or in psychological pattern but in the undifferentiated flow of the Dao? In answering, draw specifically on his equality of life and death, his critique of fixed identity, and his distinction between formed human shapes and the formless source; do not appeal to modern commentators, bibliographies, or named scholars. Then address whether his verdict changes if the transfer occurs gradually, neuron by neuron, rather than through a discrete upload event, and identify which feature of his metaphysics—continuity of process versus continuity of substance—does the explanatory work either way.
