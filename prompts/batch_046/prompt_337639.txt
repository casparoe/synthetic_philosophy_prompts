At a workshop last week Jordan and Riley got into it over Friedman's F-twist. Riley said Jordan is overextending Friedman — he was writing about economics specifically, and there's a real difference between a tractable approximation (like ignoring friction) and a wild falsehood about how agents actually behave. Jordan came back that Friedman explictly collapses explanation into prediction, so even wild falsehoods are fine if they deliver accurate results. Riley said that makes every lucky correlation a "good model" and wipes out the distinction scientists actually care about. Jordan replied that the distinction is made by out-of-sample predictive success, not by realism. Riley then asked why scientists spend so much effort relaxing unrealistic assumptions if Jordan is right — wouldn't that work be wasted? Jordan said no, relaxing them expands the domain of prediction, but it's not required for the original model to be valid. They went back and forth a few more rounds but neither would budge.
