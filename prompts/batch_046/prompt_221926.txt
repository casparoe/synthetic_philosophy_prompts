Write a dialogue between two philosophers of art, Mara and Lin, debating the value of originals versus perfect forgeries in light of a hypothetical case: a museum learns that its celebrated seventeenth-century Dutch portrait—exhibited for decades and generating deep aesthetic responses—was replaced long ago by a chemically and structurally perfect forgery whose creator is unknown and deceased, with every expert and viewer having been deceived. Mara defends what she calls "modal provenance essentialism": the claim that an original's value partly depends on the counterfactual robustness of its causal chain, such that the object in the gallery now lacks "historical solidity" because in nearby worlds where the forger had not acted, it would not exist, and this modal fragility undermines a non-derivative form of value that survives even when perceptual properties are unchanged. Lin defends a strict aesthetic functionalism: if the forgery is perceptually and structurally identical, then knowing its origin changes only our historical interest, not its aesthetic value, and treating the causal chain as metaphysically special is a form of fetishism with no phenomenological payoff. Their exchange should be genuinely reciprocal: Mara must respond directly to Lin's charge of fetishism by explaining why modal provenance is not merely sentimental attachment but a constraint on the kind of object the work can be, and Lin must respond directly to Mara's counterfactual argument rather than dismissing it, pressing whether knowing the origin retroactively invalidates the past viewers' experiences or merely redirects future ones. They should also address whether an AI capable of generating infinite perfect copies would drain value from originals or simply reveal that value was never anchored in causal uniqueness to begin with. Both characters should be equally competent, concede small points where appropriate—Mara might grant that perceptual experience remains intact; Lin might grant that institutional practices depend on provenance—while leaving the core disagreement precisely located. End the dialogue without resolution, but with each side having sharpened the other’s position.
