People always compare universals to a font file — Times New Roman is one abstract thing that gets instanciated in every page it's printed on, so maybe redness works the same way. I want to audit how far that analogie actually holds for the problem of universals. Lay out the mapping: what is the font file, the glyph, the printer, the paper? Then show where it cracks — especially with smudged prints that still count as Times New Roman, or digital rendering where anti-aliasing changes every pixel. Does this smuggle in platonism about abstract files, or can a nominalist co-opt it? And does the fact that the same font renders totally differently on a cheap printer vs. a retina screen tell us anything useful about whether universals need to be exactly identical across instances, or does it just confuse the issue? Keep it to the conceptual audit — no reading lists.
