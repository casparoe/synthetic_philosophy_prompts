After thirty years running a physics lab I'm now retired and reading philosophy for fun, but this Holocaust debate is driving me nuts. There's this German Historikerstreit about whether the Holocaust is unique and can't be compared to other genocides, and then Bauman's argument that modern bureaucracy and rationality actually made it possible — not some primitive barbarism. People have been arguing since the eighties and nobody seems to budge. In science when a debate stalls for decades we usually find it's either a hidden measurement problem or people using the same word for different things. So is this stall substantive? Are the uniqueness people saying it's causally different, or morally incomparable, or both? And Bauman's critics — are they denying the sociology or just refusing the implication? I wonder if the whole thing is split between a factual claim about reproducable bureaucratic mechanisms and a bedrock intuition that comparing suffering is itself wrong. What's the real diagnosis — verbal disagreement about "uniqueness", hidden empirical dispute about modern institutions, or two groups answering different questions? And if it's the mechanism-vs-moral-status split I'm guessing, is there a conceptual distinction that could actually move things, or should we just expect the impasse to stay?
