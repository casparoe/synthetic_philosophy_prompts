I spent thirty-five years in condensed-matter physics before turning, in retirement, to philosophy of science and comparative epistemology. For a discussion group I am preparing, I want a consistency audit of my current position. Here is where I stand:

(1) I accept anekāntavāda in a strong ontological form: reality is irreducibly manifold, and contradictory predicates—for example, being both permanent and impermanent—can genuinely belong to one and the same substrate when indexed to different naya standpoints, not merely as artifacts of human cognitive limitation.

(2) I defend what I call standpoint-indexed scientific realism: theoretical entities such as electrons or quantum fields exist mind-independently, but every true theoretical predication about them is indexed to a parametric framework (scale, measurement context, model structure), and none is framework-independent in the sense of describing the object “as it is in itself.”

(3) I retain the principle of non-contradiction, but only locally: within any single consistent naya it holds without exception, yet it imposes no constraint across standpoints; cross-standpoint collective inconsistency is therefore not a metaphysical defect.

(4) I interpret quantum complementarity—localized versus distributed behavior—as an empirical instance of (1): wave and particle descriptions are not hidden-variable failures but genuinely incompatible, jointly real descriptions indexed to incompatible experimental setups.

(5) I hold that philosophical method should pursue intra-standpoint coherence through reflective equilibrium, but should reject demands for global, cross-standpoint consistency as illegitimate; the total set of standpoints may be collectively inconsistent without undermining knowledge.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? I am especially concerned that (2)’s appeal to a mind-independent substrate may conflict with (1)’s denial of framework-independent properties, and that (4) imports a metaphysical reading of quantum mechanics that (2)’s calibrated realism does not actually license. Rank any real tensions by severity, identify the least costly revision for each, and indicate which single change would do the most repair work. Also, please say plainly if my framing rests on a confused premise—for instance, if standpoint-indexed realism collapses into relativism, or if syādvāda was never intended to bear ontological weight—rather than smoothing over the difficulty to be polite.
