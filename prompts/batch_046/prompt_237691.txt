At a meetup last week two people got into it over whether karma works as a theory of moral desert. (1) Alex said it's actually pretty elegent: bad actions create a moral debt that has to be paid, and reincarnation just extends the repayment window so the accounting balances over long timescales. (2) Jordan pushed back that this only works if the same "you" is around to pay, but with rebirth there's no psychological continuity -- you don't remember being the jerk who stole the mango, so suffering for it now isn't desert, it's just random suffering assigned to an innocent bystander. (3) Alex replied that psychological continuity is overrated; we hold institutions responsible across generations even though no individual employee remembers the original harm, so desert can attach to causal or narrative threads rather than memory. (4) Jordan said that's diffrent because institutions are legal constructs, not moral persons, and then dropped a weird point: if karma is real, moral desert should be "intertheoretically invariant" -- the suffering owed shouldn't depend on whether you buy Buddhism or physicalism -- but since karma's scale is calibrated inside one metaphysical framework that physicalists reject, it's not a real moral fact, just internal bookkeeping. Alex had no idea what he ment. I kinda see both sides but Jordan's last move feels like it's smuggling in metaethics. Can you adjudicate move by move? Rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating.
