For a philosophy of economics course, I want the full specification of adequacy conditions for any causal account of long-run national prosperity—whether institutional, geographic, cultural, or hybrid—rather than another round of institutionalists versus geographers. Write the desiderata: the historical patterns it must cover (the Great Divergence, persistent underdevelopment in resource-abundant states, rapid convergence in East Asian cases lacking prior institutional templates), the conceptual distinctions it must preserve (deep determinants versus proximate mechanisms, institutional selection versus genuine institutional causation, path dependence versus path creation), the counterfactual demands imposed by the non-repeatability of colonial and pre-industrial transitions, and the consistency conditions with comparative historical methods that approximate counterfactual trajectories. For each requirement, state whether it is a genuine logical constraint or a preference of one camp. Then identify which single desideratum eliminates the most popular monocausal accounts, and flag any jointly unsatisfiable subset—especially where demands for deep causal priority conflict with demands for empirically tractable mechanisms. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring specific authors.
