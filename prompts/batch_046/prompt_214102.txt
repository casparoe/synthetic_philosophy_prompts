I teach an upper-level philosophy seminar on moral responsibility and I am writing the final exam essay question. I want a prompt on Strawson's reactive-attitude framework that cannot be answered by summarizing his well-known essay on freedom and resentment. The question should present the following conceptual tension and ask students to resolve it: in cases of large-scale structural harm—where damage is produced by the distributed, individually innocuous actions of millions, such as aggregate consumption patterns that contribute to climate degradation—direct participant reactive attitudes such as personal resentment seem misdirected at any single agent, since no one individual is the appropriate target. Yet vicarious reactive attitudes, such as collective indignation directed at institutions or social patterns, appear not only possible but appropriate. Ask students to evaluate whether Strawson's distinction between the participant reactive attitudes and the objective attitude can accommodate this split. Specifically, they should address: whether adopting the objective attitude toward individual agents is justified here by psychological incapacity, moral ignorance, or distributed agency rather than by the kinds of exemption that Strawson discusses; whether redirecting reactive attitudes toward structures or collectives preserves the naturalism that drives Strawson's resistance to generalizing the objective attitude across all human relations; and whether this move collapses into a form of compatibilism or determinism that Strawson rejects, or whether it offers a genuinely distinct third option.

Please write the exam question, a model answer of approximately 600 words, and a marking scheme. The marking scheme should be structured around five specific moves an A-grade answer must contain, with marks assigned to each (total 20), and should include a one-line note on the characteristic mistake that costs marks at each point. The difficulty should lie in distinguishing suspension of reactive attitudes for reasons of incapacity (Strawson's objective attitude) from redirection of those attitudes for reasons of distributed or structural agency, and in assessing whether that distinction is stable within Strawson's naturalist account of moral responsibility. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
