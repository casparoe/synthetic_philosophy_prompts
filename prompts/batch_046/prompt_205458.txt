I am a retired natural scientist who has recently turned to moral philosophy, and I should be grateful for your assistance, rendered in formal British English.

Please construct a thought experiment to the following specifications. The setting must be a realistic workplace or civic body—not science fiction—in which members vote on adopting a technical protocol whose long-term risks are genuinely ambiguous and about which expertise is unevenly distributed. Design the case so that three accounts of voting ethics—(1) a civic-duty account that obliges participation in collective decision-making, (2) an expressive account that treats casting a ballot principally as the affirmation of one’s values irrespective of causal efficacy, and (3) a lesser-evil consequentialist account that permits voting only when one’s ballot improves the expected outcome—yield three distinct verdicts for a single agent, Dr. Chen.

Specifically, arrange matters so that Dr. Chen belongs to the minority who genuinely grasp the ambiguity, that the voting mechanism assigns equal weight to every ballot regardless of epistemic position, and that her vote is almost certainly not decisive. Under these conditions, the duty account should require her to vote; the expressive account should counsel abstention, on the grounds that casting a ballot with weight equal to that of less-informed voters misrepresents her calibrated epistemic humility; and the consequentialist account should also counsel abstention, because adding an equally weighted vote to an epistemically noisy pool does not improve expected outcomes.

Your answer should contain: the scenario in one paragraph; a demonstration that each theory delivers its assigned verdict and why; identification of the precise feature—equal democratic weighting under recognised epistemic asymmetry—that drives the divergence; a note on what happens to the verdicts if that feature is removed; and an honest account of how a defender of the worst-off theory might attack the case. The purpose is not merely to illustrate disagreement, but to isolate whether the value of voting lies in participation, expression, or causal contribution when aggregation ignores differences in knowledge.

Please avoid bibliographies, citations, or references to named authors, and present everything in polished British English.
