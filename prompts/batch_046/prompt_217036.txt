Lewis Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise is typically interpreted as showing that accepting the premises of a valid deduction does not automatically entail accepting the conclusion, since one can always withhold assent from the corresponding rule of inference; adding that rule as an extra premise merely invites withholding assent from the meta-rule that licenses its application, generating an infinite regress of required endorsements. If this regress is genuinely unavoidable in the structure of deductive justification, and if human working memory is severely bounded, then it seems to follow that no human being ever actually completes a deduction in the normative sense—rather, all real-world reasoning must be a heuristically truncated approximation of an infinitely deep rule-chain. Does this mean that logical competence should be reconceptualized as a form of cognitive satisficing rather than as implicit mastery of an unbounded normative hierarchy, and that the distinction between valid deduction and fallacious inference is ultimately a matter of pragmatic termination conditions rather than of structural correctness?
