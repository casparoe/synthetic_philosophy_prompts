Write the desiderata an adequate theory of life must satisfy: the borderline cases it has to classify correctly (viruses, prions, synthetic minimal cells lacking evolutionary history, living robots built from frog cells, and hypothetical shadow biospheres with non-carbon chemistry); the distinctions it must preserve (being alive versus being merely an evolutionary vehicle, metabolic closure versus chemical autocatalysis, and individual life versus ecosystem-level processes); the biological and chemical results it must stay consistent with (common ancestry, continuity of evolutionary lineages, and the non-arbitrariness of metabolism); and the conceptual puzzles it must dissolve (for instance, why flames and crystals feel like false positives despite analogues to metabolism and reproduction). For each requirement, state the motivating consideration, flag whether it is non-negotiable or camp-specific—say, evolutionary biologists versus autopoiesis theorists—and identify any subset of desiderata that looks jointly unsatisfiable, especially any tension between demanding present metabolic autonomy and requiring evolutionary potential, since some entities have exactly one but not the other. Then briefly score the metabolic, evolutionary, autopoietic, and information-theoretic definitions against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves: I want to know which single desideratum does the most work in eliminating popular theories.
