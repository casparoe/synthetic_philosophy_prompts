1. for climate, the inefficacy objection works: one person's flight emissions are causally lost in the noise, so i don't cause climate harm by flying.
2. still, i think i ought not fly, but the reason isn't harm — it's that i'm structurally complicit in a harmful collective practice, kind of like being a necessary part of a jointly sufficient set that produces the damage, even if my drop doesn't change the bucket.
3. i reject inefficacy for voting: i ought to vote even though no election hinges on me, but the ground is symbolic (expressing civic equality), not causal.
4. for meat, i reject inefficacy on empirical grounds: supply chains are responsive, so my purchase does cause marginal animal suffering.
5. i generally accept a strong "ought implies can": if my action, taken in isolation, can't affect the outcome, then there's no obligation to perform or refrain.
6. i'm not a consequentialist; deontological constraints can bind me even when breaking them makes no detectable diffrence.
