You are a professional philosopher of mind with deep familiarity with Descartes’s Meditations and with contemporary debates about minimal selfhood and dissociative states, and you are also an accomplished formal poet. Before writing anything, please take a moment to brainstorm carefully and systematically: list the three principal interpretive positions on what the cogito actually proves—specifically, (1) that a substantial thinking self exists continuously, (2) that only an occurrent act of thinking and its immediate existence is established, with no warrant for a persistent substance, and (3) that the argument is circular because the first-person pronoun already presupposes the self it claims to derive.

Having done that, please write a Petrarchan sonnet—octave rhyming ABBAABBA, sestet in CDECDE—from the first-person perspective of someone who has recently regained consciousness after a brief episode of anesthesia awareness, during which they experienced something like pure cognition without any accompanying sense of being a continuous person. The speaker should be trying to apply Descartes’s reasoning to this strange experience.

The philosophical content must do structural work. Use the octave to articulate the naive hope that if thinking is occurring, then a solid, enduring self must necessarily be present—building the expectation that thought guarantees a thinker with substance and duration. At the volta on line nine, pivot sharply: the speaker should recognize that all that has been secured is the bare occurrence of thinking, and that the inference to a substantial self may be an unwarranted leap—perhaps even that the I is imported rather than discovered. In the sestet, hold this tension open: do not resolve whether a persistent self exists, whether the experience was genuinely selfless, or whether Descartes’s proof survives. Let the rhyme scheme carry the contradiction rather than dissolve it.

The poem should be accurate enough that a scholar of early modern philosophy would immediately recognize the dispute between the existential and substantial readings, and would not wince at misrepresentation, but it must contain no citations, no bibliography, no named secondary sources, and no verbatim quotations enclosed in quotation marks. Write in a formal, polished register appropriate for a private philosophical journal entry, with emotional immediacy but conceptual precision. Do not resolve the tension in the closing couplet; leave the contradiction alive.
