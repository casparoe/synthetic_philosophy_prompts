I am preparing the framework for a faculty workshop on practical rationality and would like a rigorous consistency audit of the package of commitments I have developed concerning diachronic coherence, intertemporal preference, and decision-theoretic stability. Here is my position. First, I accept the canonical diachronic Dutch Book argument: cyclic preferences over temporal lotteries expose an agent to a guaranteed-loss sequence of pairwise exchanges, and this is a genuine rational failure, not merely a pragmatic inconvenience. Second, I hold that exponential temporal discounting is the only defensible intertemporal framework; hyperbolic discounting is irrational precisely because it renders an agent vulnerable to sequential exploitation of the sort the Dutch Book illustrates. Third, I reject state-dependent utility models that treat an agent's temporal location as a legitimate index for outcome evaluation, on the grounds that allowing one's present temporal position to alter the value assigned to a fixed outcome collapses the normative distinction between rational preference revision and arbitrary affective fluctuation. Fourth, I nonetheless maintain that there exists a distinct class of what I term architectonic preferences—holistic judgments about the narrative shape and temporal structure of one's life—which may be non-transitive without being susceptible to a money pump, since they are assessed globally rather than decomposed into pairwise exchange relations. Fifth, I regard the self-binding remedy for diachronic inconsistency—whereby an agent treats her future self as a separate party to be constrained by present resolution—as both psychologically unrealistic and normatively questionable, because it violates the principle that rationality should be independent of the particular decision procedure employed. Sixth, I hold that diachronic coherence constraints apply with full force only when an agent possesses complete information about her future preference set; where future preferences are genuinely opaque to present introspection, limited cyclicality is not itself irrational. Please examine these six commitments systematically. Identify which pairs are in genuine logical or motivational tension, which conflicts are merely apparent and dissolve on closer inspection, and for each genuine tension specify what must be revised and at what cost. Rank the tensions by severity, and indicate whether a single adjustment—perhaps to my treatment of architectonic preferences or to my information-condition in the sixth commitment—could restore consistency without abandoning the core Dutch Book framework.
