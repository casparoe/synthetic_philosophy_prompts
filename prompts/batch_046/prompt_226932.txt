Take this episode and analyze it through Bagehot's framework of the "dignified" parts of government (the Crown, which commands reverence and supplies formal legitimacy) and the "efficient" parts (the Cabinet and Parliament, which actually exercise power), with the convention that the monarch acts on ministerial advice except in extreme reserve circumstances. I want a disciplined mapping: who occupied which role at each stage, what it meant for the "efficient" branch to deploy a "dignified" instrument—the royal prerogative of prorogation—to disable legislative scrutiny, and whether the Supreme Court's intervention represents the efficient part reclaiming authority from a captured dignified mechanism, or something more complicated.
