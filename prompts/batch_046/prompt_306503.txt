A recent popular article on Cartesian philosophy summarizes Descartes's view of animal minds like this: Descartes maintained that animals are pure automata—mechanically complex but entirely devoid of mental life. Human beings escape this condition only because an immaterial rational soul is joined to our bodies via the pineal gland, which serves as the exclusive point of interaction. Animals, however, lack this gland entirely, leaving them with no physical substrate capable of supporting consciousness. The article concludes that modern neuroscience has therefore empirically falsified Descartes's position, given that animals clearly possess pineal glands and share neural mechanisms for pain and fear with humans.
