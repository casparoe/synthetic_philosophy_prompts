In your analysis, consider not only the explicit arguments about seasonal mobility, playful social experimentation, and the absence of a single agricultural revolution, but also whether their own account implicitly relies on a smaller-scale version of the same rupture narrative, now distributed across local rituals and temporary hierarchies rather than civilizational stages. Weigh the objections that defenders of Harari and Diamond might raise: for instance, that Graeber and Wengrow overread implicit teleology into functional explanations of crop domestication and state formation, or that their emphasis on agency and choice smuggles in a different but equally normative conception of human freedom. Throughout, separate what is well established in the anthropological and archaeological literature, such as the existence of long transitional periods with mixed economies or the regional variability of social stratification, from interpretations that remain genuinely contested among specialists who know the field, and do not present a disputed interpretive claim as if it were the consensus view.
