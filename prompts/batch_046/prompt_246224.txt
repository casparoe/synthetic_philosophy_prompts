okay so i was trying to adapt that sphexishness idea with the wasp to free will but i think i walked into a wall. heres the argument my roommate and i cooked up last night:

(1) if an agent runs the same subroutine in domain D regardless of contextual variation - classic sphexishness - then they aren't exercising free will in D.
(2) human cognition is full of these: anchoring, loss aversion, working-memory caps, etc. they don't scale with the moral stakes.
(3) but flexibility can be sphexish too. call it meta-sphexishness: an agent that always adapts using the exact same learning-rate or epsilon-greedy parameter is just running a higher-level fixed loop.
(4) so whether you're rigid like the wasp or "flexible" like an RL agent, you're sphexish at some level.
(5) now apply the principle of alternative possibilities: free will requires you could have done otherwise in identical circumstances.
(6) but "identical circumstances" has to include your cognitive architecture, including whatever meta-flexibility settings you carry.
(7) so whenever your architecture fixes the output, you couldn't have done otherwise.
(8) every agent - human, angel, whatever - has some architecture or nature.
(9) therefore free will is impossible for anything that exists with a nature, and sphexishness wasn't even the real culprit; nature itself is.

this proves way too much and i hate it. i want to keep (1) and (5) if i can, and i'm actually okay with ditching the idea that flexibility saves us. but i can't tell if the slide is in (3) - is meta-sphexishness just a pun? - or in (6), where i'm baking the agent into "circumstances" in a way that makes PAP trivial? or maybe (1) is wrong and sphexishness is just determinism with a funny name? tell me which step to reject, why it looked okay, and what it costs me especially if i still think sphexishness genuinely kills freedom but not every rigid pattern does.
