I maintained that revealed preference requires diachronic stability: if preferences are mutable, labeling the current choice as utility-maximizing collapses into tautology. She countered that utility is always indexed to the present decision context, and that treating prior preferences as authoritative commits us to an implausible essentialism about the self. Neither side should be a strawman; each should answer the other’s specific objection rather than talk past it. Begin with the dialogue itself, with no preface or commentary.
