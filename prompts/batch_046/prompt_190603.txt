okay so i've been reading keynes's essay about our grandchildern and i think i've walked into a reductio i can't walk out of. here's the chain. (1) keynes argues capitalism is only instrumentally good: it's morally objectionable but necessary as a machine for solving the economic problem, so that eventually we can live for culture and leisure. (2) he predicts we'll solve that problem within about 100 years (so roughly now) and should then work maybe 15 hours a week. (3) but he also says the love of money is a deep psychological trait that won't vanish once abundance arrives, which is why he expects people will keep grinding away even after need is gone. (4) now if capitalism is justified purely as a means to abundance, and abundance is achieved, then capitalism loses its justification and should be scaled back or replaced. (5) yet keynes insists we should keep capitalism running for another century anyway, mainly to avoid disruptive transition costs. (6) but if people are so psychologically wired to chase money that they won't voluntarily scale back, then achieving keynes's 15-hour utopia requires some kind of social coercion or heavy restructuring of preferences. (7) so either keynes's good life is authoritarian (it forces people away from stable preferences) or capitalism was never merely instrumental (it satisfies real preferences and is therefore good in itself). (8) but keynes explicitly denies both: he denies capitalism is good in itself and he doesn't advocate coercion, he just assumes people will eventually see the light. (9) so it looks like his view collapses into contradiction unless we drop one link. which step is the weak one, and what do we lose if we cut it? i especially don't want to give up (2) or (3) since those are the empirical heart of the essay, but maybe i'm misreading the instrumental claim in (1)?
