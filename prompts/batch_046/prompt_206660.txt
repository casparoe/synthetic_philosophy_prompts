I've been reading rationalist blogs for ages and recently got into a weird argument with friends about Wittgenstein's private language argument — not whether he's right, but what he actually kills off. I want a taxonomy, basically a decision tree, that sorts people by their concrete commitments rather than by which philosopher they cite.

Start with: do you accept there's even a coherent idea of a language describing inner sensations that's in principle unlearnable by anyone else? Then branch on where the failure lands — epistemic (no way to check correctness), semantic (no distinction between seeming right and being right), or metaphysical (the notion of a private object collapses once named). Next split on whether the argument hits all mental talk or just descriptive reports, and whether accepting family resemblance saves you or just relocates the problem. Each leaf should be a named position — like the standard Kripkenstein reading, an expressivist rescue, the phenomenal concept strategy in the neighbourhood of Loar and Papineau, or just 'unnamed' — with one sentence on its awkward commitment. Flag pairs your tree can't cleanly seperate, especially where someone says mental language is non-descriptive yet still privately accessible. Note any unoccupied combinations, like the view that private language is metaphysically impossible but epistemically harmless. Don't ask for page references or a bibliography; I need this for a seminar discussion where nobody can look things up. Use British spelling please — my tutor hates Americanisms — and I may have misspelt a few words but you get the idea.
