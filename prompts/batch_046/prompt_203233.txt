For a long-form feature I am writing on the phenomenology of suffering, I have been working through the clinical and philosophical literature on pain asymbolia—those rare patients who report full nociceptive experience matching ordinary pain in quality and intensity, yet deny any unpleasantness, sometimes laugh at the stimulus, and occasionally request that it be repeated. I have sketched an argument that seems to push me into an implausible corner, and I need to know exactly where the leak is. Here is the chain, numbered. (1) If a mental state is intrinsically bad for a subject, then being in that state gives that subject a reason, purely in virtue of being in that state, to want it to end. (2) Pain asymbolics are in a state that is phenomenologically identical to ordinary pain, yet they have no reason to want it to end; indeed, some actively seek its continuation. (3) Therefore pain is not intrinsically bad—its badness must come from an additional evaluative framing, which we might call affective transparency, rather than from the sensory core itself. (4) By parallel reasoning, pleasure that occurs without that framing is not intrinsically good, and the same structure applies to fear, disgust, and other valenced states: none carry value without the framing attitude. (5) Consequently, whether an action harms someone depends not on whether it causes nociception, but on whether the victim’s framing attitude is intact; a torturer who first induced evaluative blindness could cause full pain-sensations without causing harm. (6) That result is morally intolerable. I am inclined to keep (3) if possible and reject (1) if I must, but I want to know whether the real failure is more precise—perhaps an equivocation in (2) between the sensory token and the mental-state type, or a hidden modal slide in (5) that illegitimately moves from “not bad for this subject” to “not harmful in general.” Please begin your answer with your bottom-line diagnosis in the first sentence—name the specific step you reject and the core reason—then lay out the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating my question.
