A philosophy blog I read offers this summary of Russell's theory of descriptions: Russell argued that a phrase like the present King of France is not a name that refers to an object, but a logical device whose full form asserts that there is exactly one present King of France and that he has the property in question; on this analysis, The present King of France is bald is straightforwardly false rather than meaningless, and negative existentials such as The round square does not exist can be true without committing us to non-existent entities. The blogger adds that Russell applied this descriptive analysis to all singular referring expressions, including demonstratives like this table, treating them as abbreviated descriptions; that if the uniqueness condition fails the sentence lacks any truth-value rather than being false; and that, taken together, these points show language never directly refers to reality but only describes it through quantificational conditions, placing Russell closer to linguistic idealism than to logical realism. How fair is this characterization?
