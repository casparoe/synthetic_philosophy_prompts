Here is where I have landed on moral worth and acting for the right reasons; I want an audit of whether these hang together, not a lecture. (1) Moral worth requires that one's motivating reasons align with the normative reasons for the act—doing right from the wrong reasons earns no credit. (2) The relevant normative reasons are substantive (another's suffering, the weight of a promise), not the bare fact that morality demands action; so "because it is my duty" is insufficient unless it is structurally transparent to those substantive considerations. (3) I reject Michael Smith's moral-fetishism charge in this form: caring about rightness is not defective if one's conception of duty tracks the underlying values rather than floating free of them. (4) Worth is scalar, not binary: it tracks the counterfactual robustness of one's reason-tracking—would you still respond to the substantive reason if self-interest pulled hard the other way and no one was watching? (5) Overdetermination is harmless: if sympathy and duty both motivate you, neither dilutes the other, and worth is not reduced by the presence of an extra good motive. (6) Young children and some non-human animals can possess moral worth despite lacking explicit moral concepts, because direct responsiveness to another's need counts as reason-tracking without conceptual mediation.
