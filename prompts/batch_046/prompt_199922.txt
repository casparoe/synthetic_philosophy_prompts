Please explain the conceptual problem that arises when Nāgārjuna extends the tetralemma to emptiness itself — the claim that emptiness is also empty of intrinsic nature (svabhāva). If all phenomena, including the absence of intrinsic existence, lack independent being, does this collapse the distinction between conventional truth (saṃvṛti-satya) and ultimate truth (paramārtha-satya) into an undifferentiated single claim? Or does the self-application of emptiness instead function analogously to a meta-level qualification that avoids self-contradiction? Clarify how Madhyamaka uses this move to navigate between eternalism and nihilism, and say whether the position requires treating “emptiness” as a merely conventional designation rather than as an ultimate reality in its own right.
