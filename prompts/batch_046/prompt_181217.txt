I have been trying to articulate my own position on distributive justice for a small discussion group in the rationalist space, and I would like an audit of whether the pieces actually fit together. My commitments are these. First, I hold that equality should be assessed in terms of capabilities rather than resources or welfare, but I want to restrict the relevant capabilities through a robustness condition: only those functionings count that a person would still endorse after being fully informed about their causal origins and available alternatives, which is meant to screen out adaptive preferences without smuggling in subjective well-being metrics. Second, I treat resources as purely instrumental and think they ought to be distributed according to a prioritarian rule weighted by conversion efficiency into these robust capabilities, yet I reject any cardinal comparison of utility or welfare—even counterfactual utility—as a legitimate distributive standard. Third, I maintain that there is a lexical threshold of basic functioning, covering nutrition, shelter, and what Sen describes as basic capabilities, which must be secured for everyone before any prioritarian calculus applies. Fourth, I believe that social conversion factors, such as access to education and prevailing norms, are the proper object of justice, whereas natural conversion factors, like genetic endowments, fall outside distributive concern except when they push an individual below that lexical threshold, in which case compensation is required only to reach the threshold. Fifth, I think that when two people possess identical robust capability sets but one voluntarily selects a narrower functioning vector, that chooser is not worse off and should not be compensated, though I also insist that this voluntariness be evaluated by a counterfactual autonomy test—whether the choice would persist under conditions of expanded information and absence of coercion—which I regard as an objective structural feature of the choice situation rather than a psychological state. Please work through these commitments and identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear tense but dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real tension rate its severity. For each tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be, what exactly I would have to give up, and how that loss would affect my remaining commitments. Finally, tell me which single revision would resolve the greatest number of tensions with the least overall damage. I ask that you present this entirely in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a carefully written letter.
