I am constructing a novel set in an administrative near-future, and for narrative consistency I need to determine how a senior archivist at the Bureau of Bounded Judgment would rule on a proposed decision protocol, given her established record. She has never formally studied the canonical literature, yet her judgments consistently embody a logic of bounded rationality.

Her recorded verdicts are these. First, she approved a selection protocol that halted upon reaching the first option that exceeded seventy percent on a composite utility index, even though a later option reached seventy-two percent, on the grounds that the marginal search cost exceeded the expected gain. Second, she rejected a protocol that continued searching until no improvement had occurred across three consecutive cycles, ruling that this was optimization disguised as rationality rather than genuine satisficing. Third, she approved a protocol that employed a single, fixed aspiration level calibrated at the outset across all domains, but only on the condition that the level be set according to the worst-case scenario present in the training distribution. Fourth, she rejected a protocol that dynamically lowered its aspiration level whenever cumulative search costs rose, arguing that this collapsed the distinction between satisfying and pure expenditure-minimization. Fifth, she approved a protocol that maintained separate, non-commensurable aspiration levels for distinct attribute clusters, permitting internal trade-offs within a cluster but strictly forbidding them across clusters.

The proposal now before her is designated epistemic shadow-satisficing. For all directly observable attributes, it fixes conventional aspiration levels in advance. For attributes whose values depend on the agent's own confidence estimates—which are opaque to the agent itself—the protocol does not specify any aspiration level whatsoever. Instead, it applies a meta-threshold: computation terminates when the cost of further narrowing the confidence interval exceeds a predetermined fraction of the current best-estimated value of the decision. The designers contend that this preserves bounded rationality by imposing an absolute computational ceiling; her critics maintain that without a genuine aspiration level governing the opaque domain, the procedure is merely maximizing over an unobservable parameter.

Assuming she wishes to remain fully consistent with her own prior record, what should her verdict be? Identify the closest analog among her five earlier rulings, and articulate the principle genuinely in tension. More precisely, lay out at least two distinct interpretive rules capable of explaining all five prior verdicts—one that treats aspiration levels as primitive, non-derivable constraints calibrated ex ante, and one that treats them as instrumental expressions of a deeper constraint on the ratio of search expenditure to expected value of improvement—and state what each rule predicts about this new protocol. Offer your best-guess verdict with reasoning. Finally, specify one further hypothetical protocol whose evaluation would definitively discriminate between those two rules.
