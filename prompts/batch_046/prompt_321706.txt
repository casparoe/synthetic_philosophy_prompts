Write a proposal for a one-semester philosophy-of-aesthetics term paper (approximately 6,000 words) on an unresolved tension in Schopenhauer’s account of music in The World as Will and Representation. Schopenhauer maintains that music is metaphysically unique among the arts: rather than representing Platonic Ideas mediated by the principle of sufficient reason, it is a direct objectification of the Will itself—an immediate copy of its blind striving, tension, and resolution. Standard interpretations take melody, analogized to the arc of human desire, as the primary vehicle of this direct expression, treating harmony and rhythm as subordinate formal conditions that support melodic movement. Yet these readings leave an open question they rarely address: how can Schopenhauer’s monistic, unitary conception of the Will accommodate genuinely polyphonic structures—independent contrapuntal voices, sustained dissonances, fugues in which melodic lines maintain structural autonomy—without either fracturing the unity of the Will or implicitly introducing representational relations among distinct entities?

State the precise question: can Schopenhauer’s metaphysics consistently distinguish direct objectification from representational mediation within multi-voice music, and does his view implicitly privilege monophonic melody in a way that undermines its claim to explain music as such?

Explain why existing positions do not settle it: commentators either assimilate counterpoint to a single overarching melodic line or treat harmony as a neutral container for melody, without examining whether the independence of voices introduces relational content—conflict, resolution, spatial separation—that functions analogously to representation governed by the principle of sufficient reason.

Set out the approach: first, reconstruct Schopenhauer’s criteria for direct versus mediated objectification from his general metaphysics (the grades of objectification of the Will, the role of the principle of sufficient reason, the distinction between Idea and Will as thing-in-itself); second, apply these criteria to a conceptual analysis of contrapuntal independence, dissonance, and harmonic resolution; third, determine whether these phenomena can be interpreted as the Will’s self-relation—that is, internal differentiation without genuine multiplicity—or whether they necessarily invoke relations among distinct objects that belong to the realm of representation.

Specify the possible conclusions and what decides between them: either Schopenhauer’s view extends consistently to polyphony, with contrapuntal conflict expressing the Will’s internal tension and resolution preserving direct objectification; or polyphony reveals that music, like painting or poetry, requires representational mediation among its elements, weakening Schopenhauer’s claim that music occupies a singular metaphysical status above the other arts.

What would count as failure is a collapse of the distinction between direct expression and representation in the analysis of harmony, or being forced to attribute to Schopenhauer an implicit representationalism about multi-voice texture that flatly contradicts his explicit statements—leaving the project with no determinate interpretive result.
