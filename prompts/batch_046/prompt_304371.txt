hey, i'm putting together a paper for my seminar on free will and got stuck on this arguement my adviser sketched. it feels like it proves way too much but i can't tell where the sleight of hand is.

(1) Frankfurt-style cases show that moral responsability doesn't need alternative possibilities, so PAP is false: Jones is responsible for voting X even though Black would have forced him if he'd wavered.

(2) The reason Jones stays responsible is that the actual causal path runs through Jones's own reasoning — Black never actually intervenes.

(3) But apply that same reasoning to Black: he only built the device because of prior mental states he never chose. So Black lacks alternative possibilities regarding those mental states, and if lacking alternatives exculpates then he's not responsible for building it.

(4) If Black isn't responsible for the counterfactual mechanism, then there's no responsible agent "behind" Jones's action grounding the contrast that makes Jones responsible.

(5) So Frankfurt cases implicitly depend on the intervener being responsible for the mechanism — but (3) shows he can't be.

(6) Therefore either (a) PAP is actually true, since without it we can't ground the intervener's status, or (b) responsability collapses entirely because every mental state traces back to unchosen causes.

(7) But (b) is absurd — we do blame people. So (a) must hold: PAP survives Frankfurt after all.

i feel like (4) is smuggling in something about "grounding the contrast" but i'm not sure. if i reject (3) instead, am i buying into some weird source-compatibilist view that we're responsible for our own characters? my adviser says the error is probably that the case structure never required Black to be responsible at all, but i don't see why not — doesn't the counterfactual need to be genuinely available?

which step should i give up, and what's the price? i'd prefer to keep (2) and (6) if i can but i'm open.
