I'm a PhD student in environmental philosophy and animal ethics, and I've been going round in circles with my supervisior about welfare auditing in aquaculture. There's this specific pattern in certification and research protocols that keeps bugging me: for farmed salmon and octopuses, auditors demand individual-level protections — percussive stunning checks, behavioural enrichment, clear humane endpoints. But for decapod crustaceans, especially shrimp, welfare is measured almost entirely through aggregate metrics like mortality rate and feed conversion. Even more oddly, in high-throughput shrimp hatcheries larvae are routinely culled through rapid chilling or mechanical separation, with no stunning or endpoint protocol whatsoever; they're classified as biomass rather than welfare subjects. Some premium 'ethical shrimp' schemes do run individual nociception tests, yet only on adult broodstock, never on juveniles or larvae, despite the same bodies now recognising decapod sentience in principle following recent UK sentience legislation. So you've got individual-based moral consideration for vertebrates and cephalopods, aggregate production logic for crustaceans, and a size-based exemption that treats larval shrimp as outside the scope of welfare obligations entirely.

What philosophical commitments about moral status, suffering, and value does this institutional practice actually presuppose? Is it running an implicit graded-capacity view where obligations scale with behavioural complexity or predictive processing ability? Or is it a tractability argument disguised as ontology — we protect the organisms we can easily measure and manage? There's also a tension between using individual metrics for fish and population metrics for shrimp, which seems to presuppose that crustaceans lack individuated welfare states. Is that consistent with recognising decapod sentience, or does it quietly smuggle in a different theory of value? Could you reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguishing what the practice strictly needs from what merely fits it well? I'd also like to know whether the pattern is genuinely philosophically underdetermined — could a straightforward equal-consideration view or a pure capacity-based account license exactly the same audit structure? Finally, if we replaced the underlying view with one that takes allostatic prediction-error, rather than bare nociception, as the core of welfare, what concrete changes would be required in hatchery culling and slaughter practice?
