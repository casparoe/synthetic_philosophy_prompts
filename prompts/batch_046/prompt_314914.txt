I'm giving a talk next week in our phil mind seminar defending the idea that cognitive penetration in anxiety works through interoceptive predictive coding—specifically, that the anterior insula reweights precision of exteroceptive signals without going through any conceptual interpretation stage. I call this "opaque precision modulation" and I argue it counts as genuine penetration even though there's no conscious belief involved, but only if the subject can't introspectivly access the modulation source. To make it concrete I use a hypothetical double dissociation: insula-lesion patients with high anxiety but normal visual contrast sensitivity vs. intact-insula patients with disrupted prefrontal precision signaling who show lowered contrast thresholds but report no anxiety. I know it's speculative—I'm not presenting clinical data, just a conceptual framework, but I worry the psych person will think I'm handwaving.

Audience: about twelve people. There's a die-hard Fodorian in the front row who still thinks modularty is the default, two predictive-coding philosophers of science who think I'm misapplying the math, one cognitive psych who actually studies interoception and will call out my neuroscience, plus grad students who mostly know Pylyshyn and Siegel and will ask about epistemic circularity. I need a Q&A prep sheet: the objections ordered by how damaging they'll be, phrased the way this crowd actually talks. For each: the objection in their vocabulary, a reply I can give in about two minutes, and a note on where that reply is soft. Include the one question I'm praying nobody asks—probably something about whether my "double dissociation" is empirically real or just a philosophical toy.
