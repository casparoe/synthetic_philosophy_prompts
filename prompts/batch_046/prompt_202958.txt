Organic certification boards allow farmers to use pyrethrins and spinosad against crop pests — both are broad-sprectrum insecticides that hit bees and caterpillars alike — but they ban synthetic pyrethroids that work on the exact same sodium channels as the pyrethrins, even when the synthetic compound degrades faster and has lower non-target toxicity. At the same time those boards mandate pollinator buffer zones and wildflower margins. So what's the implicit philosophy? Does the rulebook presuppose that harm is worse when it's artificialy synthesized, or that natural origin tracks some kind of ecologial fittingness? Reconstruct the view of moral harm or value that makes this double standard hang together, check whether the practice actually needs that view or is just administrativly lazy, and say what the approved-pesticide list would look like if we replaced the origin-based logic with one that weights only sentience-capacity and ecologial side-effects. Also point out where the practice is philosophically underdetermined — could a totally different theory license the same rules?
