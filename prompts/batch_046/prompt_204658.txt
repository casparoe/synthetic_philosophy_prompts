Consider the standard assumption in metaphysical grounding theory that well-foundedness requires an asymmetric hierarchy terminating in an absolutely fundamental base. Now introduce a tension rarely foregrounded in the literature: in complex adaptive and condensed-matter systems, macro-scale organizational patterns constrain micro-scale dynamics through boundary conditions and feedback architectures in ways that appear ontologically determinative, not merely causally efficient, while those same micro-dynamics realize the macro-patterns. This raises the possibility of reciprocal determination between levels, threatening the tree-like structure usually assumed for grounding relations.

Against this background, rank the following four strategies for preserving a coherent distinction between fundamental and derivative reality. Assign each strategy to one of four tiers—S (fully resolves the tension), A (mostly resolves it with a localized cost), B (re-describes the tension without dissolving it), or C (fails or collapses into an undesirable alternative)—and justify each placement in a single sentence. Your evaluation should turn specifically on whether the strategy genuinely eliminates reciprocal loops or merely relocates them, and whether it avoids both denying all macro-to-micro ontological dependence and dissolving fundamentality into a flat, holistic ontology.

The four strategies are:

1. Priority monism in the Schafferian mode: only the cosmos as a whole is fundamental; parts are derivative, so apparent inter-level loops are artifacts of decomposing a unified, prior ground.

2. Essentialist pure grounding in the Finean mode: grounding tracks the essences of absolutely fundamental entities; derivative levels may exhibit internal reciprocal relations, but nothing derivative can serve as a ground for its own ground, preserving well-foundedness strictly at the base.

3. Relational fundamentality (hypothetical): fundamentality belongs not to entities but to specific relational structures; loops may occur among relata provided the relational backbone binding them is not itself grounded in its instances.

4. Determinable asymmetry in Wilson's framework: macro-properties are determinables, micro-properties are determinates, and the determination relation runs strictly downward; apparent top-down ontological constraint is reconceived as descriptive or explanatory dependence rather than upward grounding.

For each ranking, state explicitly whether the strategy risks collapsing into flat ontology, denying macro-dependence, or successfully threading between them.
