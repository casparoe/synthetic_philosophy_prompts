Consider a physically realized variant of Thomson's lamp: an infinite cascade of decay events with half-lives of 1 minute, 30 seconds, 15 seconds, and so on, each decay triggering a switch that toggles a lamp, all occurring within the open interval (0, 2) minutes, with the lamp initially on at t = 0. At exactly t = 2 minutes, is there a determinate fact of the matter about whether the lamp is on?

Please give your credence—between 0 and 1—that the answer is genuinely indeterminate, meaning there is no fact of the matter about the lamp's state at that boundary. Decompose your reasoning into at least the following components: (a) the prior that an infinite sequence of discrete physical toggles can be completed within a bounded time interval without violating conservation laws, quantum decoherence bounds, or informational limits; (b) the conditional that, if such a supertask is physically possible, a binary-state variable subjected to infinitely many discrete flips in finite time necessarily lacks a convergent limit at the boundary point; (c) the weight you place on the possibility that an unstated continuity principle or hidden boundary condition fixes the state despite the non-convergent toggle sequence; and (d) any other factor you think belongs in the decomposition. For each component, provide a number with a one-line justification, show briefly how they combine into your overall credence, and then identify which component you are least confident in and what kind of conceptual argument or physical result would shift it the most.
