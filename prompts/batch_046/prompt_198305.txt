Write a villanelle in the voice of an ecologist weighing the welfare effects of forest fragmentation. Let the two refrains represent the two metrics that move in opposite directions when habitat is cleared: one for the suffering of the animals that die directly in the clearing and in the resulting edge-effect predation (standing harm), and one for the suffering that never occurs because the future generations of prey and predator—those that would have lived in density-dependent starvation and reproductive surplus—are never born (expected absence). The form should do the philosophical work: the alternating refrains must keep passing each other, and their final meeting in the closing couplet should show why the two metrics cannot be collapsed into a single ledger or summed into one net balance. Use precise ecological language—refer to r-selected reproductive surpluses, edge-effect mortality, and density-dependent starvation—without citing studies or naming authors. The diction should be accurate enough that someone familiar with wild-animal suffering debates recognizes the argument, and the poem should leave the tension genuinely open, neither resolving into a sermon about intervention nor shrugging it off as the circle of life. Aim for nineteen lines, strict villanelle structure, with no explanatory preface inside the poem itself.
