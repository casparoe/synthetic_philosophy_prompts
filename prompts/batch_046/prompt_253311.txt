Williamson's argument that luminosity fails for conditions like feeling cold relies on a margin-for-error principle: if one knows one is cold, one must be sufficiently far from the boundary with warmth. If we combine this with the KK principle—that knowing p entails knowing that one knows p—then knowing one is cold should require knowing that one knows one is cold, which in turn requires being even further from the boundary. Iterating this seems to push the required state arbitrarily far into coldness, eventually implying that one can only know one is cold when one is actually freezing, or perhaps never at all. Despite this apparent interaction, discussions of anti-luminism and the KK principle usually proceed independently. Is there a principled reason the margin-for-error principle does not compound across iterations of knowledge, or does their separation depend on an unstated restriction on how epistemic safety applies to second-order beliefs?
