I'm the chair of an institutional review board at a mid-sized university hospital, and we're seeing an uptick in "moonshot" protocol proposals: studies with a less-than-one-percent chance of yielding a transformative therapy, but which consume substantial resources and expose participants to meaningful risk. I need a reusable decision procedure—something my board can run in a single meeting—to evaluate whether such a proposal is acceptable under an expected-value framework without collapsing into EV fanaticism. The procedure should be ordered: the questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, the explicit checks that block approval when expected value is technically positive but practically destructive (budget exhaustion, opportunity-cost substitution, moral hazard, irreversibility of harm), and the points where the procedure must hand the decision back to human judgment because the issue is one of values rather than calculation. Include worked applications to three proposals: (1) a phase-I trial of an unproven neuroregenerative compound with a 0.3% chance of reversing Alzheimer's progression but a 15% risk of severe adverse events, costing $4M; (2) a mandatory vaccination protocol for hospital staff against a hypothetical engineered pathogen with unknown transmission dynamics; and (3) a proposal to redirect 80% of the board's annual review capacity to a single high-stakes protocol. At least one of these should produce an answer the board will find counterintuitive, and the procedure must flag exactly what it cannot settle.
