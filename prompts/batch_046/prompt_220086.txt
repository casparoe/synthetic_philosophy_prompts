For a seminar on historical moral responsibility I have drafted the following profile of my commitments regarding the First World War, the Fischer controversy, and what it means to hold a nation responsible. I want to know whether the set is jointly tenable. Here is where I stand:

1. Modern nation-states do not literally possess the shared intentionality required for full collective moral agency; at best they approximate it through representative institutions, so any "national" blame is always partly derivative or metaphorical.
2. I largely accept Fritz Fischer's thesis that the German leadership in 1914 pursued a calculated-risk policy that made general European war highly probable, and I take this as a sound basis for assigning significant causal and moral responsibility to the German state apparatus.
3. I maintain that an enduring institutional entity such as the German Reich can bear historical moral responsibility that outlasts the individuals who acted, provided present citizens and successors do not inherit punitive liability.
4. I believe collective responsibility for a catastrophic outcome can exist even when no individual intended that specific outcome, so long as the collective's institutional design or negligent maintenance made the outcome a foreseeable product of its ordinary functioning.
5. I reject the Versailles Treaty war-guilt clause not because Imperial Germany lacked causal or moral responsibility, but because imposing legal collective punishment on a population violates distributive moral constraints, regardless of whether the collective agent is genuinely culpable.
6. Because Imperial Germany was authoritarian rather than democratic, I think blame for its aggressive risk-taking should fall almost entirely on the military and political elite, with minimal participatory blame attaching to the general populace.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to conflict. For each real tension, say how severe it is, what the cheapest revision would be, and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single change—dropping or altering one commitment—would resolve the most tension overall, and whether that change is one I should be willing to make.
