Most introductions just repeat the familiar slogan about being a person through other persons and call it a day, but that sounds to me like it could apply to a rock in a river if we don't say exactly what kind of 'through' is doing the work. So I want a concrete conceptual example—really in the weeds—where treating personhood as reciprocal recognition forces a different answer than the standard liberal view that moral status sticks to the individual no matter what social connections break down. I'm especially stuck on severe dementia: if recognition has to go both ways, what happens to someone who can no longer recognize anyone? Don't just say the community remembers them, because that feels like smuggling individualism back in through the back door. Instead, lay out a specific scenario where the relational view either has to bite a strange bullet—maybe moral status actually fragments or fades—or needs extra machinery like asymmetric recognition that creates its own headaches. I want to see the knot tied, not untied.
