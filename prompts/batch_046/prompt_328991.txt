I am a high school student auditing an upper-division philosophy seminar, and I am designing a take-home examination component on the ethics of belief—specifically the disagreement between William James and W. K. Clifford—together with a model answer and a detailed marking scheme. I want the prompt to be unanswerable by anyone who has only memorized the standard labels "the will to believe" and "evidentialism." To achieve that, I have constructed a niche framing I call epistemic decay scheduling: a decision environment in which the practical cost of holding a false belief increases exponentially with time, the cost of further investigation increases only linearly, and new evidence arrives at a constant but insufficient rate. The exam question should ask students to evaluate whether James's defense of belief in the absence of conclusive evidence remains ethically permissible under this decay schedule, particularly with respect to his conditions that the option be live, forced, and momentous; and whether Clifford's evidentialist prohibition—paraphrased as the claim that it is always wrong to believe anything upon insufficient evidence—can be maintained consistently within the same schedule without generating an infinite regress of verification demands. The model answer should be about 600 words, suitable for 60 minutes and 30 marks, and should separate an A response—which must explicitly show how the decay parameter modifies each of James's three conditions, identify that Clifford's position tacitly assumes a static decision window, and argue whether the framework reveals a hidden shared presupposition or genuinely favors one camp—from a B response, which applies James and Clifford correctly in generic terms but fails to integrate the decay schedule or misses the regress problem. The marking scheme should allocate points for: correct reconstruction of James's position (5 marks), correct reconstruction of Clifford's position (5 marks), analysis of the interaction with epistemic decay scheduling (10 marks), identification of the hidden presupposition or decisive asymmetry (5 marks), and argumentative clarity (5 marks). Please do not include any request for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and avoid verbatim quotations from James or Clifford; paraphrase their positions without quotation marks.
