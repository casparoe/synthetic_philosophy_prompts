I sit on the allocation board for a regional emergency-response fund. We must choose between Program A, which will save 40 identifiable people with near-certainty, and Program B, which offers a 10% chance of saving 600 people and a 90% chance of saving none. Our charter requires that any allocation be justifiable to each affected party in terms they could not reasonably reject; pure expected-utility aggregation is therefore not dispositive. Draft the board's decision memo: state a clear recommendation (approve A, approve B, or approve B with conditions), give reasoning that treats Scanlon-style contractualist objections to aggregation seriously rather than dismissing them, address the strongest consideration on the losing side honestly, specify whether the board should treat the stated probabilities as epistemically revisable and how that affects the justification, and set out a concrete course of action with a decision deadline. Do not include bibliographic references, reading lists, or citations.
