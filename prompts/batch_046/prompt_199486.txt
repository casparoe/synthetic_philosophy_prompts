I am a novelist developing a literary work whose central character is a philosopher of physics, and I have arranged an interview with an unnamed scholar who defends a specific, somewhat unorthodox reading of the hole argument. She holds that Einstein’s point-coincidence resolution resolves the ontological worry—showing that diffeomorphism-related metrics represent the same physical situation—but leaves untouched what she calls a modal underdetermination problem. On her view, the gauge structure of generally covariant theories does not merely erase the identity of spacetime points; it removes the very framework needed to specify what counts as the same localized metric perturbation inside a matter-free region across physically equivalent descriptions. Consequently, she argues, we cannot meaningfully anchor counterfactual claims about how the gravitational field might have evolved differently in that region, and this undermines a particular form of scientific realism about gravitational fields—one that depends on tracing causal dependencies through specific geometric configurations rather than through coincidence classes alone. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that engage directly with this position, building from clarification toward sustained pressure.
