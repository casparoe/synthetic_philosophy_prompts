Design a runnable 75-minute workshop activty for about 10 philosophy grad students on teh extended mind thesis. Don't replay the usual notebook example — instead, set up a live role-game: half are "cognizers," half are "external vehicles" (shared docs, notebooks, whatever). The cognizers must solve a hidden pattern task but can only access data through their assigned vehicle, which answers questions honestly but gets silently edited, shared, or corrupted by other vehicles every few rounds. The cognizers have to make predictions and defend them in front of the group. The rules should push them into a bind: if they reject the vehicle's input they can't solve teh task, but if they accept it they have to own beliefs they never generated internally and can't verify alone. Include specific roles, round structure, debrief mapping game moments onto functional parity and epistemic ownership, a prediction of what will go wrong (probably either blind over-trust of corrupted data or refusal to use teh vehicle leading to task failure), and an honest note on which part of the standard parity argument this can't reproduce — especially the long-term transparency and reliability conditions that real extended cognition usually requires. Keep it casual, maybe a typo or two, and don't ask for citations or a reading list; the debrief should do the conceptual work, not a lecture up front.
