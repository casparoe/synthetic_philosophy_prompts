I work as a clinical ethics mediator at a regional hospital, and about twice a week I have to write recommendations when staff and patients deadlock over care. We nominaly use deontological framing — respect autonomy, do no harm, treat justly — but I keep running into what philosophers call the paradox of deontology: the same framework gives us ironclad agent-centered restrictions (you cant use one person as a resource for others) and also lets people off the hook via agent-centered prerogatives if the personal cost is high enough. I need a decision procedre my team can actually run in twenty minutes, not another seminar.

Write a step-by-step protocol — like a flowchart in words — that takes a conflict case and runs it through the restriction side first, then the prerogative side, in a fixed order. For each step say exactly what question to ask, what counts as passing or failing, and where the protocol stops with "action prohibited," "action permitted but not required," or "this is a values call, hand it to the committee chair." Make sure it handles the hard collision where a staff members prerogative not to participate clashes with a patients restriction against being abandoned.

Then apply it to three scenarios:
1. A nurse refuses to assist in a legally permitted abortion because of conscience, but the patient has no other provider available that night.
2. A family demands continued ventilation for a brain-dead patient, citing religious autonomy, while staff say continuing violates non-maleficence.
3. A surgeon wants to harvest organs from a living donor who technically consents but is under obvious emotional coercion — here the procedure should probably say stop, and I want to see that it actually does, even though blocking a life-saving transplant feels wrong.

Say up front what this procedure cant do — where it will misfire, need human override, or just dress up a judgment call as a check. Write in American English. Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, with no preamble and dont restate my question. Keep it casual, like youre talking to a tired colleague — a typo or two is totally fine.
