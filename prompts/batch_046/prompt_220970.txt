Design a final-exam essay prompt for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on Foucault's analysis of power (90 minutes, one essay of approximately 800 words, worth 30% of the course grade). The prompt should ask students whether Foucault's distinction between disciplinary power and sovereign power survives its application to algorithmic governance—specifically, institutional systems that enforce behavioral norms through opaque predictive scoring that adjusts access to resources in real time, rather than through the visible, spatial enclosure of the Panopticon. The question must require students to confront a specific conceptual tension: Foucault treats visibility and the internalization of a displayed norm as essential to discipline, yet algorithmic governance hides both the observer and the normative standard from the subject. Students should be forced to argue whether this represents (a) an extension of disciplinary power into a predictive normalization, (b) a regression to sovereign power disguised by technological opacity, or (c) a genuinely third modality that breaks Foucault's binary. Provide a model answer of roughly 700 words that makes these specific moves: (1) distinguishes disciplinary power (productive, continuous, normalization-based) from sovereign power (repressive, discontinuous, prohibition-based); (2) explains why the Panopticon depends on the subject's awareness of potential visibility and a knowable norm, not merely on uncertainty about being watched; (3) identifies the crux—that algorithmic scoring withholds the norm itself, making self-correction impossible and replacing internalization with reactive adaptation; (4) defends one of the three positions with reasoning grounded in Foucault's account of the prison; (5) addresses the objection that digital platforms restore visibility through feedback loops. Then provide a marking scheme (out of 25 marks) that distinguishes an A-range answer (21–25) from a B-range answer (16–20): an A must identify the visibility/norm-distinction crux, defend one modality with conceptual precision, and address the feedback-loop objection; a B correctly summarizes the Panopticon but treats algorithmic governance as an obvious extension without confronting the opacity problem. Identify two mistakes that should cost marks: conflating biopower with disciplinary power, and treating algorithmic opacity as functionally identical to Panoptic uncertainty. Write the model answer and marking scheme in American English.
