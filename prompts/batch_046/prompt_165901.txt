hey, i'm a phd student in organizational econ and i do some side consulting for this federated research pool that funds cross-university collaborations. lately they've been running into disputes that feel like the classic dollar auction except the currency is reputation capital and future grant access rather than cash — labs keep bidding up commitment to dead projects because walking away signals unreliability to future partners. it's escalation of commitment, sure, but with a twist i'm calling second-order escalation: instead of just throwing more resources in, the parties start rewriting collaboration agreements, adding co-investigators to dilute blame, or tweaking evaluation metrics so that continued failure doesn't count against them. the mediation officers need something reusable, not just gut feeling.

can you turn the core escalation logic into a decision proceduer — a flowchart in words with ordered questions — that a non-legal mediator can actually run in about 48 hours? fix the role: an internal dispute officer at a multi-institution funding body, seeing maybe two or three of these per quarter, with authority to recommend suspension but not impose it, and who has to produce a memo for a review board. the gates should be explicit yes/no/maybe, with clear flags for where judgment can't be eliminated (especially telling strategic rule-rewriting from legitimate restructuring), and include a small set of test conditions they can apply to meeting minutes or budget amendments. then run it on two cases: first, Lab A and Lab B have sunk 30% of annual budgets into a shared sequencing platform that's produced no usable data in 18 months, and now Lab B wants to bring in a third partner and restructure cost-sharing; second, a case where the procedure returns "not an escalation trap" but the mediator will acutally hate that answer because the project is still clearly broken — so i can see the false-negative boundary. also say plainly what the procedure CANNOT do: it shouldn't compute optimal exit points or forecast future cooperation, only diagnose whether the escalation structure is present. don't ask for bibiographies or specific citations — this needs to work for a model with no web access — but keep it in the weeds so it doesn't just say "check for sunk costs."
