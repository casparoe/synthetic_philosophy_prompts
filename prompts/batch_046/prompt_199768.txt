Essay task: Global workspace theory (GWT) proposes that conscious experience arises when information is globally broadcast across multiple cognitive subsystems, rendering it available for report, reasoning, and voluntary action. Defenders respond that phenomenal presence just is global availability, or that the distinction dissolves once integration is properly understood. In DBC, two parallel global workspaces operate within a single cognitive system, each broadcasting identical sensory content to separate downstream subsystems. Both streams are fully introspectively reportable; the system can access either stream individually and describe its contents accurately. By design, however, there is no higher-order structural binding that integrates the two broadcasts into a unified phenomenal field—the system reports two discrete experiential streams rather than one unified consciousness, despite perfect identity of content and full access to both.

Your essay should address whether DBC demonstrates an in-principle limitation of GWT. Specifically: Does the conceptual coherence of DBC show that global broadcasting can occur in the absence of phenomenal unity, thereby proving that workspace theory accounts only for access?
