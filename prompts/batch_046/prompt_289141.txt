The Rietdijk-Putnam argument—that special relativity eliminates absolute simultaneity and thereby entails a four-dimensional block universe in which past, present, and future events are equally real—has generated responses since the 1960s without clear resolution. Critics routinely charge that the argument reifies coordinate frames, or that it conflates the absence of a privileged "now" slice with the presence of all slices; defenders reply that the Andromeda paradox demonstrates that presentness is frame-relative and therefore cannot ground ontological privilege. The literature feels frozen: the physics is settled, the philosophical positions are well mapped, and yet neither camp moves. I want a diagnosis of why, not another defense or refutation.

Is the impasse verbal—do eternalism and presentism denote different theses about temporal parts versus temporal properties, with both sides using the same vocabulary for different concepts? Is it a substantive disagreement about what "real" requires, with defenders adopting a criterion of mathematical existence in the manifold and critics requiring causal or epistemic accessibility from a given worldline? Or is there a hidden formal question about whether Lorentz covariance is compatible with a dynamical theory of becoming, masked by metaphysical rhetoric about "being" versus "becoming"?

I am particularly interested in whether an under-examined distinction is doing hidden work: the difference between frame-relative co-existence (the claim that two events share a coordinate-time value in some inertial frame) and determination-relative becoming (the claim that an event's ontological status depends on its causal determinability from a specific worldline). If defenders slide from the first claim to the second without explicit justification, is that the crux? Or do critics mistake a coordinate-dependence result for an ontological-parity result?

Diagnose the disagreement using concrete moves—how defenders treat the Andromeda scenario, how critics treat the inference from "no absolute simultaneity" to "all times exist," and whether both sides are actually addressing different questions (one about the ontology of spacetime, one about the phenomenology or epistemology of temporal experience) under a single heading. Tell me what, if anything, would count as progress: a conceptual distinction that separates the coordinate claim from the ontological claim, a formal result showing presentism can be formulated in a Lorentz-covariant way, or an argument that the framing of becoming versus being is inapplicable in relativistic spacetime and should be abandoned.

Please also identify where your reasoning is most vulnerable and state explicitly what would change your mind—whether a rigorous demonstration that the Andromeda paradox genuinely forces eternalism without hidden premises, proof that presentists and eternalists are answering non-conflicting questions that have been erroneously merged, or a convincing analysis showing that the distinction between frame-relative co-existence and determination-relative becoming collapses upon inspection.
