A podcast I listened to summarized illusionism about phenomenal consciousness like this: the view denies that there is any subjective “what-it-is-likeness”—no raw feels or qualia—and claims that the widespread belief in such properties is a collective cognitive illusion generated by introspection. The speaker said that illusionists such as Frankish treat reports of feeling pain as descriptions of purely functional, access-conscious states with no intrinsic phenomenal character, and that they explain the error through “phenomenal transparency,” by which introspection delivers mental states as bare seemings without revealing their underlying representational machinery, leading us to misrepresent them as non-physical properties. The speaker concluded that this dissolves the hard problem entirely and also undermines ethical theories that ground moral status in the capacity to suffer, since suffering is revealed as an illusory construct. I think this may conflate denying phenomenal properties with denying consciousness altogether, and that the ethical leap seems unjustified, but I do not know enough to identify the precise misrepresentations.
