I am preparing a decision tree for my senior philosophy seminar that sorts positions on whether the Enlightenment should be judged mainly as a source of modern progress or as the ideological engine of domination. Rather than listing named theories at the start, I want the tree to branch from three diagnostic questions that someone could answer without knowing the philosophical labels in advance. First, does one evaluate Enlightenment reason procedurally—as a method of critical inquiry—or substantively—as a specific body of norms about equality, science, and rights? Second, is the domination in question primarily external, meaning colonial exclusion and racialized violence justified by universalist claims, or internal, meaning the bureaucratic normalization and discipline of European populations themselves? Third, should progress be measured diachronically, comparing conditions within societies across time, or synchronically, comparing the rights and welfare granted to Europeans with those denied to colonized subjects during the same era? For each leaf, name the position it corresponds to—include at least a universalist-progress view, a postcolonial-exclusion view, an internal-discipline view, and one combination your tree predicts is unoccupied because incoherent or unattractive—and state its single most awkward commitment in one sentence. Please also flag any two leaves that remain genuinely indistinguishable on this tree without adding a hidden fourth assumption.
