ok so i retired from a physics lab about five years ago and have been slowly working through philosophy of mind. here's roughly where i've landed, but it feels wobly and i want a consistency check before i try to explain it to anyone.

(1) i'm with dennett - there's no cartesian theater, consciousness is just parallel drafts being revised, no single "show" being watched by anyone.

(2) but i also think the hard problem is genuinely hard, specifically because of phenomenal concepts: we have direct acquaintance with experience that can't be fully captured in physical or fuctional terms.

(3) i think the global neuronal workspace - the recurrent loops that broadcast a winning draft - is the right NCC, and once we map those loops we've basically explained access consciousness and the illusion of unity.

(4) i'm firmly against dualism and panpsychism; consciousness has to be reducible to physical information processing, like temperature is reducible to molecular motion.

(5) introspection is mostly direct and authoritative: when i report being in pain i'm using phenomenal concepts to access content, not making an inference about brain states.

(6) the continuous "me" is a narrative fiction constructed by memory and language, not tracking any real persistence over time.

(7) lately i've been convinced the drafts are really competing predictions with precision weights, and the "broadcast" is just global error minimization in a predictive-processing framework. also i think dennett misread the split-brain data - patients don't feel split, which actually suggests more integration than his model predicts, and predictive processing fixes that.

do these actually hang together? please trace the real tensions - not every pair is in conflict - rank them by severity, say which ones are only aparant but dissolve on inspection, and tell me the least costly revision if something has to give. don't treat them all as equally bad.
