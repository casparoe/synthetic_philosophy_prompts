I'm a journalist covering science and ideas and I'm stuck on a story about corruption across cultures — my own judgments are all over the place and I need a principel that isn't just "Western rules win."

Case one: a planner in southern Italy sends road contracts to his cousin. Work's decent, price okay, zero bidding. Corruption, clearly.

Case two: a health worker in West Africa gives vaccines to her clan first because she feels she owes them survival; outsiders scream nepotism, she calls it moral duty. I want to say corruption since the role is public-health, but I worry I'm just pushing universialism.

Case three: Japanese officials getting seasonal gifts from contractors. Ritualized, refusal is insulting, no explicit quid pro quo, but it lubricates relationships that later win contracts. I feel it's less corrupt than case one, maybe not corrupt at all.

Case four: a US senator's PAC takes a legal, disclosed donation right after sponsoring pharma-friendly legislation. Transparent, still feels dirty — different dirty, though.

Case five: rural doctors accepting chickens or farm labor from patients. In those economies refusing looks like arrogant Western clinical distance, but it definitely effect triage. I lean "not corruption" but can't defend it consistently.

Case six, a newer one: an aid NGO deploys blockchain contracts to remove human bias in food distribution, but local staff hack the oracle feeds to prioritize kin. Is that corruption if the tech was built to eliminate exactly that?

My working idea: corruption is when a private or communal obligation overrides a public role in a way that can't be justified to all affected using shared reasons. That saves case one and maybe four, but case two might slip through if clan members count as "all affected," and case three fails because justification is culturally internal. So either my principle is wrong or one verdict has to go.

Is there a coherent account — maybe splitting transactional, relational, and institutional corruption — that saves most of these? Or do I need to drop one of my calls, especially on case two or five? And if Western standards aren't universal, can I still call case two corruption without being imperialist? I want the principle, not a bibliography.
