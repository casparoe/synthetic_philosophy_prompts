ok so this has happened to me twice now and i dont know if philosophers have named it. first: i had to choose between taking a job in my hometown near my aging parents, or moving abroad for reserch i actually care about. not just hard - the two goods felt like they were in different currencies with no exchange rate. i picked the job abroad. now whenever things get rough i dont just miss my parents; its weirder. i feel like the 'good child' value is standing outside my life judging it, but from a framework that my current life doesnt even recognize as valid. and heres the specific part: when i do well at reserch, part of me feels like that success is somehow 'tainted' or illegitimate because it was bought with the wrong currency. second example: a friend chose to have kids and now talks about her old freedom not with nostalgia but like its a separate moral language that still applies to her but she cant translate it. she says 'i should have that freedom' even though she chose not to, and she feels her current joys are partly disqualified by that missing language. is there a specific concept for this - not just regret, but a value that survives the choice and keeps acting like an external critic that can even disqualify successes in the chosen path? if theres no name, what should we call it and where exactly are its edges? also how is it different from ordinary buyer's remorse or from those cases where people say two options are equally good?
