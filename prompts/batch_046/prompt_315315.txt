Write a deadpan bureaucratic FAQ, in the flat administrative voice of a municipal "Office of Weighted Civic Participation," describing procedures for the city's pilot "deliberative-epistocratic" town halls. In this invented system, residents earn speaking-time credits through a standardized reasoning exam, but those credits decay exponentially if a speaker's subsequent contributions fail to shift the assembly's running Bayesian consensus metric. Cover, in numbered Q&A pairs: how to appeal a decay ruling; whether non-residents or tourists may observe; the purpose and customs of the national holiday honoring the exam's psychometrician-author; and the arbitration protocol when two citizens with identical exam scores hold diametrically opposed policy views. The satire should render visible—without ever naming them—the standard objections to epistocracy: who designs the reasoning metric and with what hidden biases, which demographic groups systematically score lower, whether performance on an abstract reasoning test correlates with competence in actual governance, and whether tracking "consensus shift" suppresses legitimate dissent rather than filtering noise. Keep the tone entirely bureaucratic; the absurdity should emerge solely from the internal logic of the system. Approximately 500 words.
