I'm writing up my philosophical profile for a virtual-worlds reading group and I'm not sure my takes actually hang together. Here's roughly where I've landed, though I might be mixing things up:

(1) I think virtual objects — like that legendary sword in an MMO — are institutional facts in something like Searle's sense: they exist because there's collective acceptance within the game's rules, not because there's some abstract platonic sword out there.

(2) I think virtual harms are only real when they spill over into physical or psychological harm; there's no such thing as an intrinsic virtual harm that doesn't eventually touch the real world.

(3) Despite (2), I think virtual property rights are direct rights to the object itself — not just derivative rights to avoid psychological upset — so if someone hacks your account and steals your sword, the wrong is to you in the same direct way as stealing a bike.

(4) I also think institutional facts can't cause effects unless they're mediatated through mental states, which means virtual objects themselves can't be direct causes of harm — it's always the player's beliefs and feelings doing the work.

So my question is: is this package consistent? I feel like (3) pulls against (2), and maybe (1) and (4) look like they clash but actually don't? But I'm definatly not sure. Could you go through these, tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only seem that way, rank them by severity, and say what I'd have to give up in each case? Don't just list them all as equally bad — I want to know which revision is least costly and which single change would clean up the most tension. Also I was wondering if there's a hidden tension I'm missing between the institutional view and the property-rights claim.
