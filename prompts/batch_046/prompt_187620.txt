I keep noticing this pattern in arguments about whether some condition should be treated medically. First, someone says it's obviously a disorder because some biological parameter is funcitoning outside the normal range—hormone levels, tissue structure, whatever. Then, if you point out that "normal" is just a statistical line and plenty of people live fine outside it, they piviot to saying the real problem is that it causes suffering or blocks people from living well. But then if you ask why we should care about that particular kind of suffering—maybe some people with the same biology report being fine—they retreat back to "no, the biology itself is broken" and act like the suffering point was never the main thing. It feels like a specific rhetorical dance, not just regular equivocation, because both layers seem sincerely held but they get swapped depending on what's under attack. I know philosophers talk about naturalism vs normativism about health, but I'm asking about the argumentative move itself: is there a term for this double-layered retreat? And if not, what distinguishes it from motte-and-bailey or from just being inconsistent?
