My sister recently asked whether I think she should have a biological child, adopt from foster care, or remain childless, given her worries about climate and mental-health risks for the next generation. I find myself relying on an asymmetry I cannot fully defend: adopting seems clearly good because it relieves an existing person's suffering; having a child who suffers severely seems clearly wrong because I have created that harm; yet remaining childless does not seem to wrong anyone, since there is no subject who is deprived of joy. She replies that this privileges non-existence arbitrarily, and that failing to bring a happy person into being is symmetrically bad to bringing a suffering person into being. I want to understand whether this three-way comparison reveals a coherent moral distinction between harming existing people, benefiting existing people, and creating potential people, or whether the asymmetry collapses once we grant that potential lives have moral weight. How should I evaluate her choice?
