I am writing a paper for my history class about how governments decide what counts as national heritage. In the country I am studying, public grants restore old buildings, but only if they are at least 150 years old and reflect one official national architectural style. Structures from the 1970s that mix regional and immigrant influences are excluded, even though local people now treat them as symbols of who they are. What theory of national identity does that funding rule quietly assume? Must it treat nations as ancient, continuous communities with a true origin point, or could someone who believes nations are modern social constructions invented in recent centuries defend the cutoff as just a bureaucratic convenience? Please reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice actually requires from what merely fits it well, and tell me how the grant program would have to be redesigned if it started from the view that national identity is always being rebuilt rather than inherited from a fixed past.
