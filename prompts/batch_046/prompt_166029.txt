hey, i run an ethical-travel column and we keep getting reader mail about whether theyre morally required to follow local conventions abroad — tipping norms, queueing rules, jaywalking laws — or if its okay to treat them as optional local color. I need a reusable procedure our writers can run in about 15 minutes before filing, not just gut feeling. Ground it in whatever philosophy of social norms actually says about conventional obligations, but also build in this specific twist: call it the 'stake-asymmetry test.' The idea is that an outsider is genuinely bound by a local convention only when locals bear a coordination cost from the visitors presence that the convention is designed to distribute, and the visitor can actually absorb part of that cost by complying. If theres no stake asymmetry, its etiquette, not morality.

The deliverable should be:
- a step-by-step checklist in order, with what counts as passing/failing each step
- the exact point where the procedure stops with 'not a moral obligation'
- the exact point where it stops with 'this is a values judgment, not a fact — send to an editor'
- a small set of test questions writers can apply quickly
- then apply it to three cases:
  1. leaving a 20% tip in the US, where service wages are partly structured around it
  2. waiting at a red crosswalk in germany even when the street is completely empty and locals are staring
  3. queueing properly in a british post office when youre running late for a flight

Make sure at least one result is something our readers will hate — probably the german one — and flag where the stake-asymmetry idea breaks down or gives weird answers. Also say what the procedure cant do, like when a convention is tied to dignity rather than coordination. Keep it casual; dont worry about a typo or two.
