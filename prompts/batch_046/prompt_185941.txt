1. I buy the strategy-stealing assumption in evolutionary game theory — if a mutant can copy the resident strategy and dodge the cost, the resident isn't an ESS. I treat it as a hard constraint, not just a modeling quirk.

2. I want to generalize it past biology: any normative system where compliance is costly is unstable if free-riders can steal the social benefits by faking compliance. So strategy-stealing applies to norms, not just genes.

3. I think evolutionary dynamics fully explain why we have the moral norms we do — there's no need for non-naturalist moral facts or anything spooky like that.

4. But I also think unconditonal cooperation in one-shot prisoner's dilemmas is rationally required, not just evolutionarily useful. Like, it's genuinely irrational to defect even when you know it's one-shot and anonymous.

5. I think evolutionary debunking arguments fail because origins don't undermine justification, and reflective equilibrium is still the right method in ethics.

6.
