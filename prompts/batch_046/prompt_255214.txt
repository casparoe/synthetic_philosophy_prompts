I'm a software engineer without formal philosophy training, and I'm trying to formalize a conceptual model of whether high-level cultural values can function as genuine causal nodes rather than post-hoc descriptions. The classic case is Weber's claim that Protestant ascetic values caused specific capitalist economic behaviors through a kind of elective affinity, not merely correlating with them. Before I evaluate any account, I want the full specification of adequacy: the desiderata a theory of value-causation must meet.

Please provide the requirements list, with the motivating case or distinction behind each. In particular, include:
- How it distinguishes values as causes from values as rationalizations of material interests.
- How it treats elective affinity—mutual selection between values and economic forms—without collapsing into pure correlation or one-way determinism.
- Whether it can explain divergent economic outcomes under similar material conditions (and convergent outcomes under different ones) via value differences.
- Its consistency with sociological and economic-historical findings that religious affiliation predicts entrepreneurial behavior after controlling for institutional access.
- The distinction between having causal weight and being the dominant cause, so culture's role isn't overstated.
- A non-circular identification criterion for values, analogous to avoiding data leakage in predictive feature selection: we must not derive the value from the economic behavior it purports to explain.

For each desideratum, say whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference (materialist versus idealist), and explain why. Then identify any subset of requirements that appears jointly unsatisfiable—for example, if irreducible mental causation conflicts with empirically observable identification criteria. That tension is the part I most want to see.

Finally, briefly assess how Weber's elective-affinity account, a rational-choice reduction of values to individual utility, and a strict materialist explanation score against the list—focusing on which hard requirements each fails, not on a full comparative analysis. No bibliography or citation list is needed; I need the conceptual architecture, not references.
