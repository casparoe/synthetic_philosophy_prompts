I've been retired from biochemistry for about five years and lately I've gotten into philosophy of religion — especially Al-Ghazali's occasionalism. The idea that fire doesn't burn cotton, God just creates the burning on the ocassion of contact, keeps nagging at me. Here is roughly where I've landed, and I need to know if it hangs together or if I'm just smuggling in my old science habits.

1. I think Al-Ghazali is right: created things have no causal power of their own, only God acts directly on every ocassion.
2. But I also think chemical kinds have real dispositional essenses — H2O dissolves salt because of its molecular structure, not divine fiat each time.
3. I take Hume's point that we never observe necessary connection, just constant conjunction, so causal talk is mostly a mental organizing habit.
4. Still, I think evolutionary biology gives real explanations: natural selection is a genuine causal process producing adaptation, not God arranging each mutation.
5. I believe that if occasionalism is true, inductive reasoning collapses, since past regularities don't guarantee future ones.
6. I also think God could suspend the regularities if He wanted, and science wouldn't have any way to detect that.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in real tension and which only look like it. For each real tension, say how severe it is and what the cheapest fix would be. Then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. Don't just give me a lecture on Al-Ghazali — I want an audit of my own package.
