Please draft the joint statement that an Intent-Alignment theorist and a Value-Alignment theorist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the narrow definition of the AI alignment problem. The Intent-Alignment side holds that alignment is satisfied when a system's observable behavior reliably tracks the designers' specified objective, regardless of whether the system possesses anything like human values or whether its internal representational structure changes over time; the Value-Alignment side holds that genuine alignment requires the system's normative architecture—its stable evaluative commitments—to mirror human values in a way that survives self-modification and environmental shift.

Organize the document as follows. First, provide a numbered section of claims both sides accept, including at minimum: the formal distinction between behavior-level and architecture-level alignment; the reality of specification gaming and reward hacking; the theoretical possibility of recursive self-modification; and the fact that current empirical methods do not provide direct access to internal normative structures. Second, present a disagreements table that states each side's precise position in wording that side would endorse, isolating in particular whether functional preservation of the original intended goal through radical ontological restructuring should still count as alignment, and whether value-stability across counterfactual ontology-change is a coherent and necessary criterion. Third, identify the cruxes—those specific sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party. Fourth, for each crux, specify a concrete thought experiment or empirical scenario that both parties agree bears on the issue, and record in advance what each side predicts the outcome or interpretation should be. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke obscure formalisms that depend on specific unpublished papers; and keep every disagreement description neutral, precise, and free of caricature.
