In a classroom debate about judging historical figures, a classmate dismissed criticism of Jefferson’s slaveholding, Churchill’s imperial policies, and Columbus’s treatment of indigenous peoples by saying each was simply “a man of his time,” and added that objecting is “presentism.” Before I reply, I want to understand exactly what work that phrase is doing. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims “he was a man of his time” might express—such as an epistemic claim that he lacked our moral concepts, a normative claim that his culture genuinely permitted the acts, a motivational claim that he could not practically have resisted, or a meta-ethical claim about our own standing to blame—and for each say whether it is trivial, whether it actually excuses the wrong, and whether applying it consistently across Jefferson, Churchill, and Columbus produces contradictions? Please also address whether the defense secretly relies on an assumption that moral progress is inevitable, and whether that assumption is necessary for the excuse to succeed.
