I am developing an argument about the normative authority of advance directives across psychological discontinuity. I propose a "staged sovereignty" framework: a competent agent at t1 legislates medical preferences that bind the agent at t2 only if the t2-agent retains a threshold level of psychological connectedness—specifically, the core evaluative commitments that motivated the original directive. Should that continuity fall away, as in profound dementia that transforms one's values, the directive lapses, analogous to a treaty expiring when one contracting party no longer exists in the relevant practical form.

My concern is that this framework reduces the authority of the earlier self to a mere condition of personal identity. The directive binds exactly when the later self is continuous enough to count as the same practical legislator, which suggests the obligation is internal to one enduring agent rather than a genuine case of one temporal self governing another. Consequently, does this model fail to explain cross-temporal authority, and does it conflate the conditions for applying a directive with the normative grounds that justify it?
