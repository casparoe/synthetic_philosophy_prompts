I work at a company with a 'hero wall' for people who cover shifts during storms or mentor new hires on weekends, but if you just do your contract and clock out theres no shame at all. They also let you bank volunteer hours and trade them for extra PTO, so being nice literally gets you vacation days. Meanwhile our ethics training says lying is always wrong, even white lies, but it never says your a bad person if you dont donate blood or help a stranger getting robbed on your commute. What view of right and wrong is baked into that? Is there really a sharp split between 'must do' and 'above and beyond', or is it just that we reward whats visible? Could it work with a view that morality is just about not harming anyone, or does it need a stricter checklist idea? And once you can cash in good deeds for PTO, are they still 'supererogatory' or do they become just another job perk? I keep seeing that word supererogation but I'm not sure it survives a reward system.
