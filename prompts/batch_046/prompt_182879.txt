I've been arguing with my roommate about whether guilt or shame is the better moral emotion, and we ended up in this weird place. Let's say a society actually decides to treat guilt as kind of a failed emotional technology - not because feeling bad is useless, but because guilt lets you turn moral failure into a private debt. You apologize to yourself, do some internal accounting, and you're clean without anyone else knowing or being involved. Meanwhile they engineer shame differently: not the toxic viral-social-media kind, but a structured, communal version where your obligations are visibly disrupted and you have to stay in the relationship and repair it in front of others. They basically suppress private guilt-rumination and build instituions - schools, legal rituals, family practices - around this reparative shame. Assuming this is actually possible and not just a fantasy, what are the most important implicatons? I'm especially interested in whether a culture can genuinely choose its dominant moral emotion this way, what happens to personal autonmy when private guilt is discouraged, and whether law and education would have to be totally rebuilt. Also, are there hidden instabilities that would make this collapse?
