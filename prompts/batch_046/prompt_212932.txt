In a recent bioethics discussion, someone insisted that "DNA is a code, not a metaphor," and used that claim to argue that synthetic genome editing is conceptually continuous with software engineering—therefore lacking any distinctive moral status compared to rewriting computer programs. I suspect the slogan is bearing far more weight than any precise thesis can support. Can you disambiguate the distinct precise claims it could be expressing—separating, for instance, the syntactic claim that nucleic acids map triplets to amino acids via an arbitrary but fixed convention, the semantic claim that genetic sequences carry representational content independent of cellular interpretation, the causal claim that ribosomes and polymerases function analogously to decoders in information technology, and the normative claim that biological information is designed in a manner that makes genetic intervention ethically interchangeable with code modification—indicating for each whether it is trivially true, empirically supported but philosophically neutral, or a substantive overreach? Then identify which of these readings the argument about synthetic biology actually requires, and whether any defensible reading genuinely supports the conclusion that genetic engineering raises no special ethical considerations beyond those attending ordinary software design.
