I am a high school student preparing a decision-tree handout for my philosophy club’s independent study on rational decision-making. Rather than asking people to name philosophers, I want an instrument that sorts positions on satisficing and bounded rationality by the concrete commitments they make in specific cases. The root should split on whether rationality is assessed by the quality of the outcome achieved or by the quality of the decision procedure followed. From there, branches must separate whether cognitive or computational bounds are best understood as failures to be overcome, as ecologically adaptive constraints, or as structurally necessary features of institutional design. The diagnostic questions should be answerable without knowing any labels: for instance, should a researcher stop searching once she has found three adequate sources if one more hour might yield a slightly better citation? Should a doctor use a quick heuristic when a full Bayesian computation is technically available but would delay treatment? And should an institution—say, a university admissions office using a cutoff score rather than computing full expected utility for every applicant—be judged rational in the same sense as an individual who settles for “good enough”? Each leaf should carry the name of a position that actually occupies it if one exists (for example, Simon-style procedural satisficing, Gigerenzer-style ecological rationality, the optimizing view of homo economicus, or the Kahneman-Tversky heuristics-and-biases framing) and state its most awkward commitment in one sentence. Unoccupied nodes—such as a view that treats all bounds as purely internal cognitive defects yet insists that institutions must always optimize regardless of time or attention costs—should be included, briefly diagnosed as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and left as unnamed blanks. The tree should contain exactly seven leaves, treat both individual agents and collective institutions, and flag any positions that the tree cannot cleanly separate. Please do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or requests for further sources; the deliverable must be a self-contained classification instrument usable by someone with no outside access.
