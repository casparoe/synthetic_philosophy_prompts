Construct a thought experiment in historical epistemology: design an archival scenario about a distant-past claim—specifically, whether a long-distance trade corridor existed in a particular region before 1200 CE—such that a Herodotean source-empiricism (accepting testimony only through independent, direct eyewitness chains without contradiction) and a Thucydidean coherence-rationalism (permitting reconstructed narratives that best explain all available material and textual traces, even absent direct eyewitness transmission) deliver opposite verdicts on whether we genuinely know the claim. Constraints: the scenario must be realistic, involving only analog source types—charters, coin hoards, travelogues, archaeological stratigraphy—without digital forensics or named modern scholars; it must isolate exactly one variable, the independence of corroborating sources, while holding total evidential weight constant; and it must be presentable in one paragraph. After presenting the scenario, state the precise belief, the method of formation, the relevant nearby worlds in which the belief is false, and explain why each theory yields its verdict. Then identify which feature of the case does the work, describe what happens if that feature is removed, and explain how a defender of the losing theory would attack the case.
