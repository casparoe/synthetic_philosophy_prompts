I have recently come to defend a position I am calling lateral fundamentality: that some entities are metaphysically fundamental not by sitting at the bottom of a vertical grounding chain, but by standing in symmetric, non-hierarchical grounding loops with other entities at the same ontological level, with no terminal base. I take this to be necessary for accommodating quantum entanglement relations without reducing them to lower-level physical properties. What would genuinely count against this view? I want the decisive defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward—whether formal results in grounding logic, intuitions about explanatory asymmetry, or analogies from coherence theories and biological mutual dependence—together with an honest assessment of whether the position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so whether that insulation is itself a problem for a metaphysical claim of this kind.
