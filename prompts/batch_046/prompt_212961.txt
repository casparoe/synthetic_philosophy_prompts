As a novelist drafting an essay, I should welcome your critical comments on the argument outlined below. I contend that introspection is unreliable not chiefly through self-deception, but because conscious awareness functions like an unreliable narrator in fiction: it fabricates coherent post-hoc narratives to explain behaviour driven by sub-personal processes. Taking the familiar philosophical tension between Cartesian transparency and cognitive-scientific work on confabulation as my point of departure, I wish to advance the further claim that every introspective report is essentially a micro-fiction—brief, socially useful, and composed for coherence rather than epistemic fidelity. I ask you to consider whether this analogy genuinely clarifies the distinction between causal explanation and phenomenological description, or whether it merely collapses the two. Please respond in American English.
