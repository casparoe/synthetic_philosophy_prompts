A friend walked me into this argument and I can't find the exit. (1) Endurantism says that I at t1 and I at t2 are strictly identical, wholly present at each time. (2) If A and B are strictly identical, they cannot stand in a causal relation: causation requires distinct relata. (3) Yet my belief formed at t1 causally produces my decision at t2, with the same subject bearing both states. (4) Therefore the subject at t1 and the subject at t2 are not identical; personal persistence is a causal chain of distinct momentary stages, not endurance. (5) By parity, any persisting object—trees, rocks, planets—must be a four-dimensional succession of instantaneous stages linked by causation. (6) This universal stage theory eliminates enduring subjects entirely. (7) But true modal claims—"this tree could have branched differently"—require an enduring subject that bears the modal property across possibilities; with only actual stages, there is nothing to carry the counterfactual. (8) Since such modal claims are clearly true, universal stage theory is false. (9) So the reasoning from (1) through (6) proves too much. I particularly want to preserve (3) and (7). Which step fails, and what does abandoning it cost?
