In philosophy of physics and Gödel scholarship there's this weird stall around Gödel's rotating universe and his claim that objective time doesn't exist. Physicists tend to dismiss the metric as physically unrealistic — no observed cosmic rotation, plus closed timelike curves look like a reductio — while Gödel scholars say he meant it conceptually: GR permits a model with no global time coordinate, and since he was a mathematical Platonist he took that possibility seriously as undermining temporal flow. My working theory is that the disagreement isn't about cosmology or metaphyiscs but about what counts as a defeating possibility: does a consistent mathematical model alone challenge our intuition that time flows, or do you need empirical relevance? Is that the right diagnosis, or are they actually answering different questions — physicists about our universe, philosophers about the conceptual structure of GR? I want an honest diagnosis with reasons, not a biography, and tell me if anything — a formal result on causality, a sharper local-vs-global time distinction, whatever — could actually move the needle, or if it's just bedrock intutions that neither side can argue for.
