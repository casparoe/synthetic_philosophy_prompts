I hold that the intentional stance is ontologically on a par with the physical stance: both pick out real patterns defined by predictive compressibility, neither reduces to the other, and neither enjoys metaphysical primacy, while design-stance patterns are similarly real but narrower in predictive scope. The view avoids instrumentalism about minds by tying reality to compressive power rather than to observer utility, yet it denies that only microphysical descriptions are fundamental. What would count against it? I want an honest map of its exposure: which neuroscientific or AI results would show that intentional-level descriptions are always replaceable without predictive loss; which arguments demonstrate that predictive compressibility cannot bear ontological weight without collapsing into pragmatism; and whether the view risks being insulated from falsification by allowing defenders to relocate “loss” to ever-higher levels whenever a counterexample appears. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish what proponents of this pattern-realist stance would themselves concede as damaging from what critics allege, and assess whether such insulation is a virtue or a vice for a view of this kind. In doing so, separate what is settled about pattern ontology and the three stances from what remains genuinely contested among people who know the philosophy of mind, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
