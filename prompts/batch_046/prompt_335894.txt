student answer:
the problem of ontology identification under informational inaccessibility is indeed different from vagueness. vagueness is about boundary cases within a shared framework, like whether a mostly-bald person still counts as having hair. inaccessibility is about whether there is any framework-independent fact that could settle the question at all, even in principle. i think pragmatic ontology solves it only partly. if we treat existence claims as framework-relative, then we dont need mind-independent access to justify them; we just need internal coherence and predictive success. the shadow taxonomy worry says we might be categorizing shadows, but this is overstated because pragmatic frameworks are still constrained by causal interaction with the world. take dark matter: we cant observe it directly, but its gravitational effects are causally linked to visible matter, so our taxonomy of dark matter particles versus fields is constrained and not arbitrary. however, if information is truly inaccessible — like inside an event horizon — then pragmatic ontology should eliminate those entities entirely, since they have no causal role in our framework. therefore the shadow worry only applies where there is partial access, not total inaccessibility. this shows pragmatic ontology relocates the problem rather than solving it: we still need to draw a line between useful framework-relative entities and useless shadows, and that line depends on our practical interests rather than on ontology itself.
