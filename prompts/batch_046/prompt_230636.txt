After a long and rather exhausting argument with a friend about whether values can ever be genuine causes in sociology, I find myself stuck on what looks like a reductio of Weber’s Protestant Ethic thesis, though I cannot locate the precise slip. Here is the chain we ended up with, and I should be grateful for a diagnosis of which step fails and at what price.

(1) Weber explains the emergence of modern rational capitalism by appealing to Calvinist worldly asceticism, which he classifies as value-rational action (Wertrationalität): believers pursued salvation and the glorification of God for their own sake, with explicit indifference to economic consequences.

(2) By Weber’s own typology, value-rational action is defined precisely by its orientation towards an absolute value regardless of outcomes; it selects means only insofar as they embody that value, not because they instrumentally produce effects.

(3) If an action is genuinely indifferent to its consequences, then its properties are not linked to any specific macro-level outcome in the way causal explanation requires; causal efficacy for a particular institutional result demands at least some instrumental connection between behaviour and effect.

(4) The Protestant Ethic thesis nevertheless claims that this very value-rational orientation systematically, though unintentionally, produced the specific institutional structure of modern capitalism.

(5) Therefore the thesis requires value-rational action to be causally responsible for a determinate social outcome, which appears to contradict the defining feature of value-rationality—its indifference to outcomes.

(6) Generalising: if culture explains social structures only through value-rational action, then culture can never explain specific structures; if it explains them through instrumental reasoning about ends, then “culture” is redundant and collapses into individual rational choice.

(7) Hence either Weber’s historical explanation fails, or the distinction between Wertrationalität and Zweckrationalität collapses, or culture is never an independent cause.

My friend insists I am smuggling a false dichotomy between being “indifferent to consequences” and being “causally inert,” but I cannot see the gap clearly. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I would prefer to preserve the idea that values are not merely disguised instrumental preferences, but I want to know whether that commitment forces me to abandon Weber’s thesis or his broader sociology of action. Please begin with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
