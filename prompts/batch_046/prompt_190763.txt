I'm working through the philosophical literature on the meta-problem of consciousness — broadly, the question of why we judge that conscious experience poses a distinctive explanatory gap, and whether that judgment itself demands a theoretical account — and I'm trying to map out where limited research energy should actually go. I'm particularly interested in a tension that runs through the debate but is rarely made the explicit center of attention: on one side are attempts to dissolve the meta-problem by arguing that phenomenal judgments are conceptually mediated, constructed, or in some sense illusory; on the other are attempts to preserve the hardness of consciousness by maintaining that introspection delivers direct phenomenal concepts that resist full functional analysis. This tension becomes especially acute when we ask whether solving the meta-problem requires first resolving the hard problem itself, or whether a satisfactory account of our judgments about consciousness could proceed independently — for instance, by treating phenomenal reports as analogical extensions that we apply, perhaps problematically, to non-conscious or artificial systems.

Given that framing, I want a defended agenda rather than a survey. Which two or three open questions in this area should be prioritized for a researcher with about three years, a background in philosophy of mind and formal logic, but no access to empirical labs or neuroscience collaboration? Please rank them and explain the ranking with explicit criteria: tractability for a purely conceptual researcher, how many downstream issues depend on each answer, and what kind of conceptual or empirical development in the next few years would cause you to revise the ordering. I'm also interested in whether I'm missing an upstream question — perhaps concerning the relationship between phenomenal attribution and analogical reasoning about minds — that should displace one of the candidates I have in mind.
