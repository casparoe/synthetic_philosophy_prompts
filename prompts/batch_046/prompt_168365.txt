I'm trying to sort out my gut on supererogation — stuff thats good but not required — and whether there's a principle behind my calls. A bystander jumping into icy water to save a stranger: heroic, not required. A lifeguard doing the exact same thing: just the job, not supererogatory. A doctor pulling unpaid double shifts during a crisis: feels like duty, but if she keeps it up for years does it flip to "above and beyond"? Donating half your income to malaria nets as a middle-class person: I think thats actually required now; but a billionare giving away 90% feels like luxury supererogation even though the absolute gift is larger. A factory owner retrofitting to zero emissions with no law pushing and taking a pay cut: supererogatory or just not harming?

My tentative idea is "exceeds the baseline of your social role, weighted by capacity" — so the doctor eventually exceeds her role, but the billionare never hits her ceiling. But that makes supererogation vanish for the rich, which feels wrong. Can a principle save all these? What does it say about a parent turning down a promotion to stay near an aging parent they don't actually enjoy? Is that self-denial supererogatory or not a moral category at all? And which of my verdicts has to go?
