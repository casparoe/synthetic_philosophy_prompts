I've been thinking about this weird stalemate in philophy of science and ontology. You got one camp saying if information about an entity is permanently inaccessable—not just practically hidden but structurally unreachable, like with certain multiverse branches or purely gravitational dark-matter candidates—then including it in our ontology is just metaphysical baggage. The other camp says inaccessibility only limits what we can know, not what exists, and that we should keep entities if they stabilize our theories or solve identification problems elsewhere. They've been circling this for years with no movement. My hunch is it's not really about existence but about what 'ontology identification' even requires—maybe one side is doing semantics and the other is doing epistemology under the same label? But I could be wrong. What's your diagnosis: is this a hidden empirical disagreement, a verbal mess, or a bedrock clash over explanatory standards? And honestly, is there anything—an argument, a distinction, a formal result—that could actually move it, or should we just accept it's two different projects?
