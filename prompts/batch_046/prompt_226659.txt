Design a 90-minute PhD comprehensive exam essay question in ethics—specifically infinite ethics—worth 20 marks total. The question must present a decision problem in which a present policy determines whether an infinite sequence of future generations with uniformly positive welfare will come into existence, or whether the human line will end after a finite period with no successors. Introduce, within the body of the question itself, a normative constraint you label the Actuality Priority Constraint: it holds that moral weight attaches only to actually existing or determinately future persons, and that merely potential infinite beneficiaries carry no direct axiological standing, yet it permits indirect obligations to preserve conditions for determinately future persons. Ask candidates to do four things: first, derive the recommendations of total utilitarianism, average utilitarianism, and a person-affecting view governed by the Actuality Priority Constraint for the choice between a high-risk policy that might yield infinite survival and a safe policy that guarantees 200 years of moderate welfare followed by extinction; second, pinpoint the exact step in one of these derivations where the presence of an infinite outcome renders standard arithmetic or comparison methods incoherent or indeterminate; third, propose a conceptual revision—distinct from both totalism and averageism—that respects the Actuality Priority Constraint while yielding a determinate recommendation in the infinite case, and defend it against the charge that it implicitly smuggles in totalist or averageist commitments; fourth, explain why the Non-Identity Problem does not dissolve the tension in this infinite setting, even though the specific identities of future people depend on the policy chosen. Provide a model answer of about 750 words that executes all four moves with precision, and supply a marking scheme divided into five criteria worth 4 marks each. For each criterion, list the specific conceptual move that earns full marks, the typical partial-credit error, and the misconception that should receive zero marks. The question should be unanswerable by reciting standard summaries of Parfit-style population ethics; candidates must construct the reasoning in situ. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and do not invoke obscure named theories that a well-prepared doctoral student could not be expected to know without reference.
