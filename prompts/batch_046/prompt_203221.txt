hey im running a philosophy seminar on well-being and that experience-machine idea — about 12 grad students, 90 minutes, mostly people who've read Nozick but are bored of the standard 'would you plug in?' framing. i want them to feel how different theories of well-being actually clash when you have to build the machine for other people, not just choose for yourself.

design a structured classroom game: split them into triads. each triad has to program a 'shared experience pod' — three users live inside one machine together for a simulated year, and they must reach unanimous agreement on the pod's operating rules (how much physical risk is allowed, whether achievements inside count as real, how much false belief about the outside world is permitted, whether emotional bonds with outsiders are preserved or faded). each student gets one hidden 'preference card' that assigns point weights across four goods: feeling good moment-to-moment, doing genuinely difficult things, retaining knowledge that the world is simulated, and maintaining strong connections to real people outside. the cards are not named after philosophers; they just show numbers like 9/2/4/3. they negotiate for 20 minutes without revealing their cards.

then run a 10-minute guided simulation: you read out three scripted scenarios (a physical challenge that might cause pain, a romantic encounter inside the pod, a moment where one user suspects the outside world is fake) and each triad votes on what the pod should deliver. after voting, they reveal cards and map the negotiation.
