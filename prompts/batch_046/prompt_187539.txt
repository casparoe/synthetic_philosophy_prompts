I have spent my career in condensed-matter physics, and since retiring I have turned to philosophy of mind and metaphysics. I want a consistency audit of the following views, which I hold but fear may pull in different directions. (1) Physicalism about propositions: if they exist, they are complex structural configurations in physical systems, not Platonic entities. (2) A qualified acceptance of Bradley's regress: relating relations are required to unify relata, and this hierarchy never terminates in purely non-relational parts, though I deny it is vicious. (3) The unity of a proposition is explained by a primitive binding operation, modeled not on logical conjunction but on quantum-field correlation—an irreducible whole in which terms and relation are not prior independent existences. (4) There are no bare particulars; every particular is constituted by its relations without being reducible to them. (5) The conceivability of a philosophical zombie fails because any physical system that genuinely represents necessarily instantiates this binding operation, and its absence is metaphysically impossible. Do these hang together? Specifically: does identifying propositions with physical structures conflict with the claim that relations are never fully external? Does my quantum-correlation model of binding resolve Bradley's regress or merely rename it? And does my anti-zombie argument smuggle in an essentialist claim about representation that my physicalism ought to reject? Rank the tensions by severity, name the cheapest revision, and flag any tension that is only apparent.
