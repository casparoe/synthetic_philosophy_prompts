As a retired biologist who's recently gotten into philosophy, I've been chewing on Nozick's experience machine and I think I've landed on a different framing. Call it coupled-authorship: well-being isn't just pleasure or achievement, but your mental states being causally downstream of your own perceptual-cognitive loop being coupled to an actual external environment. So the machine fails not because it's "fake," but because it seperates that loop — your brain states are driven by electrodes, not by your own sensory-motor engagement with the world.

What would actually count against this? I want the evidence and arguments that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be versus just awkward. Would people who genuinely thrive in deep sensory deprivation be a real counterexample, or can I qualify that away? What about reports of high well-being in brain-in-a-vat scenarios? And has this view already been insulated into unfalsifiability — if I keep saying "well, they weren't really coupled," is that a problem for a view like this?
