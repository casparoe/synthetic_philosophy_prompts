I am a novelist preparing a satirical sequence set in an administrative future, and for my own creative work I need a deadpan parody document that I can study for tone and conceptual precision. Would you please compose, in approximately six hundred words of continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables—presented as a single uninterrupted letter from the Office of Mortality Affairs—a Citizen's Guide to Your Cessation Options? The voice should be that of a bureaucratic manual that has fully internalized the philosophical split between brain-death and circulatory-death criteria, treating both tracks as equally authoritative without ever resolving whether the deceased is one person or two, and the satire should target the real institutional incoherence that arises when legal death is defined by loss of integrated biological function in one jurisdiction and by irreversible loss of conscious personhood in another.

Please exaggerate these actual distinctions into absurd but logically consistent procedures: for instance, include the specific niche case of a patient whose neocortex has permanently ceased all measurable electrical activity while the brainstem continues spontaneous respiration, thereby qualifying for partial death certificates under two incompatible reciprocity agreements, and describe the office's deadpan instructions for which family members should mourn which version of the patient. The humor must come from the precise conceptual tensions—particularly the gap between whole-brain formulations and higher-brain formulations—rather than from misrepresentations of the debate, so that a specialist recognizes the parody as fair and finds the bureaucratic normalization of the contradiction genuinely unsettling. Write in a formal, polished register, avoid any request for citations or bibliographies, rely only on distinctions a model could work through without searching, and deliver the entire piece as uninterrupted continuous prose in the manner of an official correspondence.
