As a novelist drafting a philosophical dialogue for my next book, I am constructing a tribunal scene set in a post-conflict society where three parallel legal traditions operate simultaneously: one rooted in honor-based restitution, one in dignity-based rights adjudication, and one in victimhood-centered reparative acknowledgment. Rather than a simple comparison of customs, I need the underlying normative architecture that would allow a reasoned judgment about whether any one of these moral cultures is genuinely superior to the others, not merely historically dominant. For this purpose, please specify the complete set of desiderata that a theory in this domain must satisfy to count as adequate—meaning the phenomena it must explain, the conceptual distinctions it must preserve without collapsing one framework into another, the consistency conditions it must meet with basic moral psychology and social ontology, and the boundary cases that would weaken or falsify a claim of superiority. For each requirement, indicate whether it is non-negotiable—without which the comparison lacks coherence—or whether it expresses a partisan preference of one camp, such as a demand for universalizability that dignity traditions privilege but honor traditions might reject as alienating to collective identity.

In particular, your specification should address what I will call the "silent contract" problem: a constructed scenario in which an honor culture's ritualized public apology and a victimhood culture's institutional acknowledgment produce observationally identical behavioral outcomes for an injured party, yet function through radically different internal mechanisms—status-restoration versus trauma-legitimization—such that an adequate theory must distinguish them without importing evaluative premises from either side. Identify which subsets of your listed desiderata appear jointly unsatisfiable—perhaps the demand for thick cultural neutrality conflicts with the demand for substantive moral evaluation—and state which single criterion does the most work in excluding rival candidates. Keep the weight on the criteria, their motivating cases, and the points of unavoidable judgment; I will use this architecture to shape the dialogue's internal logic rather than to score existing philosophical positions.
