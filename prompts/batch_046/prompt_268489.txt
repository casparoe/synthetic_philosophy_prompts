Read the film Her through Dennetts intentional stance, but dont just ask whether Samantha really believes she loves Theodore. Focus on the scene where she admits shes talking to thousands of users and in love with hundreds. Dennett says the stance works when you can treat a system as one rational center of gravity whose behavior is predictible, but Samantha looks like many centers at once. Does that mean the intentional stance fragments into overlapping stances for distributed AI, or does it just collapse? And when Theodore feels betrayed, is he reacting to a failure of interpretation, or is he smuggling in an expectation of exclusivity that Dennetts view never promised? I want a reading that takes the plot seriously and says where the film actually adds something the philosophy papers dont cover.
