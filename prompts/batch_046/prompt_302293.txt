I host a small philosophy podcast and next week's guest is an unnamed philosopher who defends a modified 'status-cascade' version of the honour-first account of moral revolutions. Her twist is that practices like slave-holding or duelling collapse not because moral arguments win, but because honour itself gets restructured through strategic defection by high-status actors, with material interests only catching up afterwards. She's particularly keen on applying this to the rapid disappearance of formal duelling in parts of Central Europe after 1850, where she thinks the standard Appiah-style story misses the role of bureacratic status competition.

Draft eight to ten interveiw questions that go beyond having her restate the view. I want at least one on each of: (a) how she separates honour-driven change from the Bernard Williams-style economic-interest thesis about slavery and duelling; (b) what her model predicts about norms that lack a clear status-marker, like certain dietary or purity taboos; and (c) whether her account can handle moral revolutions that seem backwards — practice stops before belief shifts, versus cases where belief changes first and practice follows slowly. For each of these three, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give (for instance, that interests are just downstream of honour restructuring, or that belief-first cases are actually misdescribed).

Also include one question pressing her on the danger of treating honour as an independent causal force rather than just a label for collective coordination, with a follow-up asking whether she's smuggling in agency where there's only correlation. And one on what her view says about norms that never collapse despite seeming irrational — say, certain initiation rituals — and whether that counts against her or just shows honour is sticky.

Keep it casual, British spelling, no generic 'what drew you to philosophy?' questions. Make them precise and a bit in the weeds.
