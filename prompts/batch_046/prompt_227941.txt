Instead she claims Smith saw imperial distance as breaking the sympathy circuit: the farther away the colony, the harder it is for the imparial spectator to do its job, so adminstrators grow callous and that callousness seeps back into domestic politics. She even argues Smith thought colonies hurt Britain's moral character more than its treasury, which is a pretty big departure from the standard Wealth of Nations reading. I want eight to ten interview questions that don't let her just restate the thesis. I need one that presses the obvious objection about defense costs and monopoly profits — since Smith talks about those constantly — with a follow-up for if she says they're secondary. Another should ask how this sympathy-based frame handles Smith's actual policy proposals for gradual emancipation of colonies, and a third should compare her reading to Hume's skepticism about large states, asking if she's just smuggling in Humean psychology. For each question, include the likely reply and then a sharper follow-up. Don't ask for a reading list or citations — I want to sound like I actually read the book, not like I'm fishing for bibliography.
