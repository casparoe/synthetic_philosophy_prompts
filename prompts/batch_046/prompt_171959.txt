hey, im trying to map out my position on Bradley's regress and the unity of propositions for a seminar im running, and i want to check if im being consistent or just stacking contradictions. heres the package:

(1) i buy Bradley's point that external relations generate a vicious regress - if A is related to B via R, then you need R' to relate A to R, etc. so relations cant be fully external.

(2) but i think propositions are genuinely structured: "Socrates is wise" isnt just a list, its unified. and that unity comes from a primitive "logical scaffold" - not a mental act, not a physical object, but a kind of modal-structural arrangement that holds terms together without being one more term.

(3) im a physicalist about minds. all cognition is brain stuff. but i think physical brains can map onto these scaffolds causally-functionally, without literally touching abstract objects.

(4) i reject Bradley's Absolute. reality is plural, lots of independent things exist, and not everything is internally related to everything else.

(5) i think the scaffold idea avoids the regress because the scaffold isnt a relatum - its more like the shape of the arrangement itself. so you dont need a further tie to tie the tie.

(6) i also like Malcolm's point about dream reports lacking first-person authority, and i think it applies analogously: if someone asks "how do you know the scaffold is there?" i shouldnt appeal to inteospection, since that'd be like claiming to observe a dream-state. the scaffold's reality is theoretical, not observed.

(7) finally, i want to say that this view is compatible with a kind of "thin" realism about propositions - they exist, but only in virtue of being instantiated or grasped, not as freestanding platonic entities.

ok so where do i actually stand? is (2) in tension with (4) since im positing abstract non-physical structures while denying monism and staying physicalist? does (3) conflict with (7) because causal-functional mapping sounds like it needs the scaffold to be real enough to causally interact? and does (6) clash with (2) - if i deny introspective access, do i weaken my reason to think the scaffold exists at all? also is (5) just redefining the problem away rather than solving it? rank the tensions by severity, tell me which ones are only apparent, and say what the cheapest revision is if i have to drop one thing. and be honest: is my "logical scaffold" doing real philosophical work or is it just word salad hiding the regress?
