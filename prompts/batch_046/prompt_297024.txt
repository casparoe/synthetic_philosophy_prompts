I have recently been appointed to a working group revising our institution's governance charter, and the distinction between absence of interference and absence of domination has become personally urgent. Please construct a thought experiment meeting these specifications: design a realistic, single-paragraph workplace or institutional scenario in which an individual enjoys complete negative liberty—no one actually blocks, coerces, or penalizes her actions—and also possesses the resources and capacities associated with positive liberty, yet republican liberty is clearly violated because she lives under the arbitrary power of another. The arbitrary power must be structurally real but dormant: an authority holds a formal, discretionary right to interfere that is never exercised, and the conditions under which it could be activated are genuinely opaque to the subject—not merely unknown to her by accident, but built into the institution's design so that she cannot learn them without altering the power relation. Do not use science fiction, perfect predictors, or exotic legal devices; keep it to ordinary organizational life. After presenting the scenario, state explicitly what each of the three theories—negative, positive, and republican—would say about whether the subject is free, and identify the precise institutional feature that does the work in separating republican unfreedom from the other two verdicts. Then explain what happens to the case if that feature is removed: for instance, if the authority's power is made fully transparent and its conditions of activation are knowable to all parties. Finally, say how a committed defender of negative liberty would try to dismantle the case—whether by denying that dormant power counts as interference, by challenging the opacity condition, or otherwise—and assess whether that response saves the theory or merely relocates the problem. The scenario should not stipulate its conclusions; they should fall out of applying the three conceptions of liberty to the institutional facts you have described.
