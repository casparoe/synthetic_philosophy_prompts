My colleague has been remarkably consistent in her judgements about which maxims survive Kant’s universalizability tests. She holds that a maxim of delegating all moral decisions concerning strangers to an external predictive algorithm fails, on the ground that universalising it would erode the rational agency that makes duty intelligible. She judges that a maxim of refusing to participate in a behavioural-data surveillance scheme passes, even where refusal imposes a collective cost, because rational autonomy may not be made conditional upon social utility. She also maintains that a maxim of treating one’s digital avatar in virtual environments as possessing moral standing equivalent to one’s physical body fails, since universalising it blurs the boundary between rational nature and its representations in a manner that yields a practical contradiction. Finally, she regards a maxim of undergoing reversible cognitive enhancement to strengthen one’s rational capacities as passing, provided the alteration is self-directed and imposes no burden upon others.

Do you think she would say that a maxim of using neurofeedback to suppress empathic responses before making distributive-justice decisions passes or fails? What about a maxim of withholding one’s psychiatric history from an employer when disclosure is not legally required but non-disclosure risks harm to colleagues—would she treat this as safeguarding rational autonomy or as violating a universal duty of transparency? And what of a maxim that grants derivative moral standing to a large language model which demonstrates sustained moral reasoning, such that damaging it without justification is treated as wrong?

I am less interested in the bare answers than in the principle or principles she appears to be running, the alternative interpretations of her four verdicts that fit them equally well, and which of these three new cases would best discriminate between those interpretations. If you were recommending a verdict that best coheres with her precedents, which analogues ought to carry the greatest weight, and what principle would she be committing herself to either way?
