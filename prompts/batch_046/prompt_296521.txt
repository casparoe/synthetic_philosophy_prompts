I've started helping out at my cousin's salmon farm a few months back and now I'm weirdly invested in whether we're actually harming anything. I've come round to this view: farmed fish and crustaceans only matter morally if they have what I'd call integratted affect — not just reflex withdrawl but a unified experience that binds pain with context — and I'm pretty convinced neither Atlantic salmon nor brown shrimp have it, because their nervous systems are too distributed and lack the central integration you'd need. So these new UK welfare rules for decapods feel like category errors to me, basically anthropomorphism in lab coats. But I want to be honest about what could actually shift me. What would count against it? Not just the usual 'they respond to anaestetics' stuff — I mean which findings or arguments would actually undermine the idea that distributed nociception without central binding lacks moral signifcance? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, say what I'd have to concede, and tell me if I've already qualified the view into unfalsifiability — and whether that's even a problem here. I'd especially like to know if there's a case, maybe comparing a lobster with something unexpected, that makes the line between reflex and integratted affect collapse altogether.
