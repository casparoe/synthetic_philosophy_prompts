Here is my current position on mental causation, and I would like a conceptual audit rather than a literature review. (1) I am a non-reductive physicalist: mental properties are multiply realizable, ontologically distinct from their physical realizers, and supervene on them with metaphysical necessity. (2) I accept the causal exclusion argument as valid—if a physical effect has a sufficient physical cause and mental properties supervene on physical properties, then mental properties are causally redundant. (3) I block the argument in practice by rejecting causal closure for macroscopic mental states, though I accept closure at the microphysical level. (4) I hold that mental causation involves downward causation: mental properties constrain or select among physically possible trajectories. (5) I maintain that phenomenal consciousness—the qualitative character of experience—plays no causal role whatsoever in behavior or in producing physical effects. (6) At the same time, I deny that any mental properties are epiphenomenal; I take causal efficacy to be a necessary condition for being mental.

Please examine these commitments pairwise. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are merely apparent, and rank the genuine tensions by severity. For each tension, specify the cheapest revision and what it costs. Then indicate which single change—modifying or abandoning exactly one of the six—would restore the greatest overall coherence, and explain why the remaining commitments survive it. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; I am interested only in the internal architecture.
