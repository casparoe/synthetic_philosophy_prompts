For a philosophy of history seminar, write the full list of desiderata that any adequate demarcation criterion between legitimate historiography and pseudohistory must satisfy. The criterion must be able to distinguish conventional Roman imperial chronology from Fomenko’s New Chronology, archaeological interpretations of megalithic sites from ancient-astronaut narratives about Atlantis, and standard textual criticism from radical chronological reconstructions of the medieval record—without collapsing into “whatever the current academy endorses.” For each desideratum, state the motivating case or counterexample that makes it necessary: consistency with radiometric and cross-cultural textual verification; the capacity to accommodate genuine historical revision without total collapse; non-circularity with respect to institutional authority; and the ability to engage statistical or astronomical arguments that pseudohistories actually deploy rather than dismissing them by fiat. Identify which requirements are non-negotiable and which are negotiable methodological preferences of particular camps. Then flag any pair of desiderata that may be jointly unsatisfiable—such as maximal openness to chronological revision versus preservation of every well-corroborated timeline. Keep the weight on the criteria and their internal tensions; a brief sketch of how a source-critical evidentiary criterion, a sociological consensus criterion, and a predictive-falsifiability criterion fare against the list is welcome, but secondary.
