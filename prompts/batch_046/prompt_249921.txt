I've been stuck in a reading group on Wittgenstein's On Certainty and the debate about hinge propositions has totally stalled. One side keeps insisting that hinges — like the idea that the earth existed long before I was born or that I have hands — aren't beliefs or knowledge claims at all, they're more like the rules of a language-game that make belief and knowledge possible in the first place. The other side says that's just mystification, and hinges are ultra-entrenched beliefs we hold with total confidence, different only in degree from ordinary beliefs, not in kind. There's also this middle camp that calls them practical commitments and insists they sit somewhere between grammar and belief.

We all agree on the standard examples, but then we just loop. The grammar camp says asking whether I know I have hands is like asking whether a chess move is legal — it misses the point of the framework. The belief camp says of course I know it, it's just uninteresting knowledge. The commitment camp says it's neither knowing nor rule-following but a kind of acting-without-thinking.

My hunch is that this isn't really about hinges but about whether epistemology should start with individual mental states or with shared social practices. But I could be wrong. Is this a verbal dispute about the word belief? Are they answering different questions — one about justification, one about linguistic use? Or is there a genuine substantive disagreement about whether certainty can be false?

Here's a case we made up to try to break the loop: imagine someone in long-term solitary confinement with sensory depravation who genuinely breaks down and stops treating the external world as real, not as a philosophical exercise but as a lived psychological collapse. Would that be losing a belief, breaking a rule of language, or abandoning a practical commitment? If the camps give different answers, does that show hinges have one nature we're describing badly, or that we're using one word for three different phenomena?

What kind of disagreement is this actually — verbal, substantive, or a messy mix — and what would move it? Would we need a sharper distinction, an empirical study of breakdown, or is the question just ill-formed? Don't survey every camp; diagnose the stall and say what progress would even look like.
