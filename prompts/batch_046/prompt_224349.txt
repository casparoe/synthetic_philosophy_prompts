I've spent the semester in a workshop on comparative authoritarianism, and next Thursday I am defending my thesis chapter before a joint seminar of modern European historians and normative political theorists. The claim I want to advance is deliberately split: interwar Germany's structural conditions—hyperinflation trauma, the Versailles settlement's delegitimization of parliamentary government, and the organizational vacuum left by the collapse of traditional conservative parties—made some form of authoritarian nationalist consolidation probable by 1930, but Hitler's specific biographical trajectory, his personal antisemitic monomania, and his willingness to override institutional rationality were necessary conditions not for a Nazi regime as such, but for its particular genocidal and expansionist trajectory. I am arguing, in other words, for a bifurcated counterfactual: without Hitler, Germany likely still produces an authoritarian right state under a military-civilian coalition or a conventional völkisch leader, but it does not produce the Holocaust or the war of racial extermination in the East.

The audience is precise and hostile in complementary directions. The structuralist historians will invoke methodological norms against counterfactual reasoning about individuals, pointing to path dependence and institutional lock-in as forces that made radical consolidation inevitable regardless of personality; the normative theorists will worry that separating "the regime" from "the crimes" lets structural explanation off the moral hook and collapses under its own weight, since the Nazi state's architecture was designed around Hitler's personal authority. Prepare me for the objections they will actually raise—ordered from the most likely and damaging downward—phrased in their vocabulary, each with a concise reply I can deliver in about sixty seconds, an explicit note on where that reply is weakest, and the one objection I am hoping nobody asks: the challenge that my distinction between regime formation and policy trajectory is unsustainable because the institutional form of Nazism was inseparable from Hitler's agency from the very beginning. I want to know what to concede up front, what to hold firm, and which reply has to land if the proposal is to survive the session.
