For my philosophy of mind coursework I am drafting a position paper on Douglas Hofstadter’s distinction between frame messages, outer messages and inner messages, particularly as developed in Gödel, Escher, Bach. Before I submit, I would like an internal consistency audit of the commitments I have adopted, rather than a literature review. Here is my current position:

(1) I hold that in any symbolic or formal system, meaning is primarily anchored in the frame message—that is, in the intended interpretation that a designer or author imposes upon the symbols—rather than arising intrinsically from the symbols themselves.

(2) At the same time, I think that in genuinely self-referential or strange-loop structures, an inner message can achieve a kind of semantic autonomy: it can generate content that not only diverges from but actively undermines the frame message, and this divergence ought to be treated as genuinely meaningful rather than as a mere system malfunction.

(3) I am inclined to collapse the outer message into the frame message, holding that the outer message is nothing more than the frame message made explicit through contextual or meta-level commentary; there is no independent semantic layer between authorial intent and the system’s internal dynamics.

(4) I also maintain that meaning is essentially relational and requires an interpreting mind: without at least a potential interpreter—whether human, artificial, or biological—no symbol system possesses meaning, however precisely its frame message is specified.

(5) Finally, I accept that evolutionary processes can produce symbol systems, such as the genetic code, that carry robust inner messages (phenotypic instructions) despite lacking any authorial frame message whatsoever, and I want to say that these systems are genuinely meaningful.

Please examine these five commitments for joint tenability. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled with further clarification, and what is the least costly revision to restore coherence? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging. I am particularly unsure whether (4) can survive alongside (5), and whether (2) undermines (1) more seriously than (3) undermines (2). Please answer in British English, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I need a consistency check, not further sources.
