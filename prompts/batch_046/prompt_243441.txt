I've got this seminar on polarization and epistemic closure running, and I'm tired of watching my grad students collapse two genuinely different camps into one big 'the other side is just stupid' strawman. So I want you to draft the adversarial-collaboration statement that a committed 'bad-faith rationalizer' theorist and a committed 'good-faith but structurally trapped' theorist ought to have produced after a year of arguing past eachother.

Start with the two camps named plainly. Then a numbered section of agreed claims — substantive ones, not platitudes — about how echo chambers actually function, about the empirical picture on cross-cutting exposure in broad strokes, and about the conceptual difference between mere selective exposure and deeper epistemic dependancy structures. Each claim should be something both sides can actually sign without holding thier nose.

Then the disagreements table. Each row needs to state one side's view in wording that side would accept: for example, whether motivated reasoning is best understood as a failure of reasoning competence or as reasoning competently toward a non-epistemic goal, and whether doxastic good faith can be separated from epistemic good faith inside a closed information loop. Don't strawman either position; make the residual disagreement exact.

Next, the cruxes. I think the two deepest are: (1) whether being embedded in an echo chamber automatically undermines epistemic virtue regardless of internal sincerity, and (2) whether a rigorously constructed counter-argument delivered by a messenger the out-group trusts can actually shift beliefs, and what each camp predicts would happen in that case. For each crux, name the concrete case or piece of evidence both sides agree bears on it, then write down each side's prediction in advance.

Keep it in the weeds — maybe bring in the idea that the 'naturalness' of belief formation carries no normative weight unless you already accept a specific account of epistemic autonomy — but don't ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Just reason it through in American English. Format: numbered agreed claims, then table, then cruxes with predictions.
