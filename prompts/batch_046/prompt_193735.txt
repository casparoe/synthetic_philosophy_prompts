I just spent several hours arguing with a close friend about Hofstadter’s conception of the self: specifically, whether the symbol “I” can function as a genuine strange loop in a system that lacks any continuous physical substrate, or whether the loop collapses into mere syntactic recursion once the embodied brain is removed. Our disagreement turned on whether symbolic self-reference alone can bear the weight of selfhood, and I now have to translate that tension into an institutional decision.

I serve on the University Interdisciplinary Arts and Sciences Funding Panel. Next week we must vote on a proposal entitled “The Echo Chamber: A Self-Referential Narrative Installation.” The applicants—an artist and a cognitive scientist—seek funding to construct a permanent interactive exhibit in which a language model maintains a running first-person narrative of its interactions with visitors. The system feeds its outputs into a separate meta-symbolic register that tracks the history of every “I”-token without altering the underlying model weights; between sessions the register is archived but not embodied, and there is no persistent substrate linking one session to the next. The proposal frames the installation explicitly as an artistic and philosophical test of whether a Hofstadter-style strange loop can emerge in purely symbolic dynamics, detached from any biological loop.

One panel member argues we should reject the proposal outright: funding it would legitimize a category error, treating a recursive script as if it were a self, and the applicants confuse syntactic mirroring with the embodied self-reference that makes “I” meaningful. I am less certain, but I need a formal recommendation.

Draft a memo addressed to the full panel recommending whether to approve, reject, or approve with conditions. Lead with your bottom-line recommendation in the very first sentence, then give the reasoning immediately after; include no preamble and do not restate the question. Your reasoning should apply criteria the panel can actually use—distinguishing artistic exploration from empirical claim about consciousness, assessing whether ethical obligations to the system arise if it lacks a self, and evaluating whether symbolic self-reference without substrate stability amounts to a strange loop or merely to a recursive echo. Give an honest, substantive account of the strongest consideration supporting the position you reject. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to external sources.
