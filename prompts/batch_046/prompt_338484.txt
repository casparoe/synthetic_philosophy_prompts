She thinks his pictograms were meant to be a full-blown universal language that could replace propositional logic in science, I keep saying they were only ever pedagogic tools sitting on top of protocol sentances and don't do any logical work themselves. We've both read the same stuff -- the 1936 International Picture Language, some Unity of Science essays -- but neither of us budge.

I'm starting to think the stall isn't about Neurath but about whether visual isomorphism can carry inferential structure, or if it's just decorative. But there's also this other layer: she thinks Neurath's anti-metaphysical physicalism forces him to reject any representation that isn't either a protocol sentence or reducable to one, which would make ISOTYPE redundant; I think physicalism is compatible with non-propositional tools as long as they don't claim metaphysical status.

So what's the actual disagreement here? Is it verbal about what counts as a language? Is it substantive about the nature of representation? Or are we answering totally different questions -- she cares about ontology of scientific communication, I care about pedagogic function? And if it's substantive, what kind of argument or distinction -- maybe between pictorial syntax and pictorial semantics -- would actually move it?
