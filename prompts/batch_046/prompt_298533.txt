I'm preparing remarks for an interdisciplinary working group on civic epistemology and want a consistency check before I present them. Here is where I have landed. (1) I think the epistemic goods cultivated in manual and rural contexts—embodied judgment under material uncertainty, intergenerational transmission of craft knowledge, and what I will call failure-attunement, the capacity to read degradation before it becomes catastrophic—are genuinely incommensurable with the goods of professional-urban life: systematic abstraction, scalability, and formal verification. I do not regard either cluster as a deficient version of the other, and I reject any single metric that could rank them. (2) Despite this incommensurability, I hold that democratic legitimacy requires policy deliberation to include both epistemic modes, and that systematically excluding manual-rural knowledge is a form of civic exclusion comparable in structure, if not in mechanism, to disenfranchisement. (3) At the same time, I maintain that in domains of large-scale coordination—climate regulation, macroeconomic management, epidemiological response—the professional-urban mode possesses legitimate authority, not because it is universally superior, but because the scale of the problem structurally matches the scale of the method. (4) I reject pure populism: listening to working people is necessary but not sufficient for legitimacy; I think legitimacy requires both the inclusion of diverse epistemic modes and conditional deference to domain-appropriate expertise. (5) I also hold that knowing-how is epistemically prior to knowing-that in practical reasoning: one cannot genuinely understand a technical recommendation without some embodied grasp of what failure looks like in the relevant domain. (6) Finally, I maintain that local, small-scale knowledge should sometimes override expert consensus when the costs of error fall disproportionately on the local population and the experts are reasoning about conditions they have not directly experienced.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision that would restore consistency? Order the tensions by severity, and for each specify exactly which commitment must be modified or abandoned and what that costs. In particular, I want to know whether my endorsement of incommensurability in (1) can survive my grant of authority to professional modes in (3) without smuggling in a hidden hierarchy, and whether (5)'s claim about the priority of knowing-how undermines (3) and (4) more thoroughly than I have admitted. I am not signaling which commitment I would be most willing to drop—treat them all as live.
