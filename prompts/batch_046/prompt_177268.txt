Write a reusable decision procedure for a senior analyst on a National Climate Accountability Board who must review proposed carbon-budget pathways every quarter and certify whether they meet intergenerational justice constraints. The analyst has forty-eight hours per proposal, access to economic and demographic models but no authority to make final value judgments, and must deliver a structured memo signed jointly with a political appointee whenever the procedure reaches an unresolved values question. Operationalize a framework that treats intergenerational justice not merely as fair distribution across time but as involving two distinct failures: (1) a representational failure, since affected future cohorts have no democratic voice, and (2) a non-identity failure, since policies that change who comes to exist cannot be justified solely by preventing harm to specific future people. The procedure should be a ordered sequence of tests—each with a clear stopping condition, a definition of sufficient evidence, and a flag for when judgment is ineliminable—ending either in rejection, conditional approval with required amendments, or referral to the political sign-off stage. It must distinguish identity-dependent harm claims (which the non-identity problem undermines) from wrongs that do not depend on the identity of those affected, and it must include a proxy-representation check that asks whether present institutions adequately stand in for future interests rather than merely claiming to do so. Apply the procedure to three proposals: (A) an accelerated net-zero pathway by 2035 funded through regressive consumption taxes, justified primarily by preventing catastrophic harm to people living in 2120; (B) a long-term geological carbon-storage program funded by present bonds, justified by a duty to preserve institutional trust regardless of who exists; and (C) a gradual decarbonization schedule paired with a legally empowered youth-council veto mechanism, justified by fair burden-sharing across present and near-future cohorts. Include one case where the procedure yields an answer the board’s environmental advocates will reject—specifically, where strong climate benefits are present but the justification collapses under the non-identity filter and the representational check fails— and explicitly state what the procedure cannot settle, such as the correct rate of pure time preference or the precise weight of present versus distant welfare. Keep the language formal and precise, suitable for insertion into an official methodological appendix.
