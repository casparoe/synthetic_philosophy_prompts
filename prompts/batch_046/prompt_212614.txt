Write a short story, no more than about 800 words, set in an archive whose librarians catalog not books but entities and the metaphysical relations that hold them in being. The protagonist should be a surveyor of fundamentality who traces grounding chains: each item is suspended by the entities that metaphysically determine it, descending toward a basal layer that is supposed to be ungrounded. Center the plot on the discovery of a single object—the Orphan—that lacks any grounding parents, yet is fully present and causally interactive. Treat this not as a physical puzzle (it is not floating for lack of gravity) but as an ontological crisis: the staff must decide whether the Orphan is a contradiction, a miracle, a misfiled item belonging to a hidden basal layer, or proof that fundamentality is not universal. Make precise the difference between causal dependence and metaphysical grounding—for example, show that the Orphan can be moved, broken, and observed without ever being constituted by what it is, unlike other objects whose existence is literally sustained by their grounds. Include a scene in which one character argues that if the Orphan is real, then the claim that every being is grounded in something more fundamental collapses into either falsehood or tautology. End without resolving whether the Orphan should be destroyed, concealed, or accepted as a legitimate exception, leaving intact the tension between universal grounding and ontological free lunch.
