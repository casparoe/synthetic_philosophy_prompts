Write a short dialogue between two philosophers—call them A and B—who disagree about whether Nozick's well-known distinction between treating animals with utilitarian calculus and treating persons with Kantian side constraints can survive once we take seriously an ecological analog of the Lockean proviso. A should defend the view that Nozick's entitlement framework actually undermines his own sharp species line: if animals can establish negative rights through sustained ecological labor-mixing (niche construction, territorial maintenance, intergenerational resource defense), then interfering with them violates side constraints rather than merely reducing aggregate utility. B should defend Nozick's original position and resist extending entitlement logic across species, arguing that labor-mixing analogies fail for beings lacking rational agency and that the side-constraint/utilitarian boundary tracks a capacity for autonomous choice that ecological behavior cannot substitute. The dialogue must contain real back-and-forth: each speaker should respond directly to the other's specific objection rather than restating an opening position, and neither should be a strawman. After the dialogue, include a brief meta-note identifying where your reconstruction takes liberties with Nozick's actual texts or relies on contested interpretations, and state explicitly which point in the dialogue you are least confident about.
