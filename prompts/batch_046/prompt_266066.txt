Question: Explain the sorites paradox and say whether just calling "heap" a vague word actually fixes the problem.

Student answer:
The sorites paradox happens when you start with 10,000 grains of sand, which is clearly a heap. If you remove one grain, its still a heap. Do that enough times and you end up with one grain, which is clearly not a heap, yet every single step seemed okay. So the argument forces us to accept that one grain is a heap, which is absurd. People often say this is just vagueness, meaning theres no sharp line between heap and not-heap. But I think that just renames the puzzle instead of solving it. A stronger answer is the epistemic theory: there really is a precise cutoff, say at exactly 3,417 grains, but human beings can never know where it is because our concepts are too fuzzy. This preserves classical logic since the boundary exists, we just cant access it. Another fix is fuzzy logic, where an object can be 0.7 heap. This works well for colors like red, but it cant work for sand piles because grains are discrete. You cant have half a grain belonging to the heap, so membership has to be all-or-nothing. Therefore fuzzy logic only applies to continuous properties, not to things made of separate pieces.
