I'm applying for a one-year fellowship and need a two-page research proposal on a question in medieval philosophy of religion. The core idea, still half-formed: the standard interpretation of Maimonides on animal sacrifice treats it as either a divine concession to ancient Near Eastern idolatry or as an enduring form of worship with independent value. I suspect both readings miss a structural possibility rooted in Maimonides' metaphysics. The proposal should ask whether sacrificial ritual functions, in his system, as a cosmological-pedagogical practice: the animal, as a being possessing sensitive and appetitive souls but lacking rational form, materially symbolizes the sub-rational dimensions of the human soul that must be separated— in the metaphysical sense central to his account of prophecy and negative theology— for intellectual perfection. On this reading, offering the animal is not merely weaning people off idolatry, nor is it an end in itself; it enacts the return of material contingency to its source so that rational worship, understood as pure intellectual service, can occur. The deliverable should state the precise philosophical question, show why the concession-versus-intrinsic-value binary leaves it open (noting especially the tension between his explicit comparison of sacrifices to pagan customs and his detailed legal codification of them in the Mishneh Torah), propose a method of philosophical reconstruction that maps the ritual onto his taxonomy of soul-faculties and his theory of service as intellectual worship rather than philological commentary, specify what success looks like (a reconstructed typology that resolves the tension without collapsing into either pole), and define failure (if the synthesis proves textually unsupported or redundant with existing readings). The scope should be realistic for one dissertation chapter or one funded report; citations and bibliographies will be supplied separately, so the proposal should refer to positions and arguments without listing sources.
