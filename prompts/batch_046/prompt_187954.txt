I've come around to a patched hedonism about the experience-machine scenario: the only reason plugging in feels wrong is that we secretly smuggle in goods like real acheivement. If well-being is just the coherence of experienced pleasure with an autobiograpical narrative — and that narrative is itself just mental content — then the machine can deliver it perfectly, and refusing to plug in is irrational. Play it straight: what would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked — fatal, serious, or merely awkward — including empirical findings about memory and life-satisfaction, intuitions that don't depend on acheivement-talk, and any formal circularity in using coherence to define well-being. What would defenders of this view concede is genuinely damaging, versus what they'd brush off? And honestly assess whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — if it has, is that a problem for a theory of well-being or just business as usual in philosophy?
