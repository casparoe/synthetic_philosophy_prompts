I serve on the National Council on Welfare Metrics' Subcommittee on Experiential Policy. The Ministry of Public Health has proposed a pilot program—reversible, transparent Subjective Experience Modules (SEMs)—that allow consenting adults to enter time-limited, value-aligned virtual environments designed to maximize sustained positive affect, with full retention of meta-cognitive awareness that the experience is mediated. The Ministry wishes to include SEM-derived satisfaction reports in the national well-being index, arguing that if a citizen reports high life satisfaction during and after the module, that is direct evidence of well-being. Our subcommittee must recommend approval, rejection, or conditional approval with evaluation criteria and a review timeline, addressing whether hedonist, desire-satisfactionist, or objective-list theories of well-being can accommodate these reports as valid indicators.

The case forces three specific philosophical issues: first, whether transparency and reversibility dissolve the standard experience-machine objection, or whether something about the causal origin of the experience still undermines well-being regardless of subjective report; second, whether value-alignment—where the module's contents are drawn from the participant's own long-term goals—makes the experience structurally closer to authentic achievement than to arbitrary pleasure; third, whether well-being metrics should track subjective report, objective functioning, or some hybrid, and if the hybrid, how to weight them when they diverge inside and outside the module.

Please draft the formal memo for circulation before Thursday's meeting. It should state a clear recommendation with conditions or a timeline, treat the strongest opposing view seriously—specifically, the position that mediated satisfaction is categorically disqualified from counting toward well-being regardless of its psychological profile—and include a concrete evaluation framework specifying what data must be collected, over what period, with what comparison groups, so that the Ministry knows exactly what it must demonstrate to maintain or expand the pilot. The memo should not ask for further philosophical literature; it should resolve the theoretical tension well enough to ground institutional action.
