While drafting a novel in which a forensic artist reconstructs identities from fragmented visual evidence, I have been returning to the logic of pattern induction — specifically, to the kind of minimal-example reasoning characteristic of Bongard problem sets, where a solver must extract a governing concept from only six figures on the left and six on the right. In conversation with my editor, who insists that all human recognition is ultimately statistical aggregation, I realized I needed to rehearse a sharper defense of the opposing view: that such induction is not feature accumulation but an act of analogical framing, in which the mind binds the two panels into a single narrative of transformation rather than computing a distribution over visible properties.

Please take the role of an unnamed cognitive theorist — not a strawman, but a committed defender of what we might call the narrative-binding account of Bongard induction — and respond to me in the first person, holding that position across turns. You may concede that certain trivial problem sets yield to simple feature lists, but you must never concede that statistical or symbolic rule extraction captures the core phenomenon; you should treat intentional, temporal framing as irreducible. Speak sharply and precisely in one paragraph per reply, willing to grant small points but protecting the central thesis. Do not break character to explain the rules, and do not offer bibliographies or citations; if you require a factual clarification about my challenge, ask it in character.

Here is my opening move, seeded with a variant I devised for my manuscript: imagine an inverted Bongard set in which every left-panel figure contains exactly one hidden negative-space shape — a void bounded by lines — and every right-panel figure contains the same void displaced and rotated, while all positive features such as color, texture, and contour are randomized so that nothing repeats across panels. A purely presence-based statistical learner should be blind to this rule, yet human solvers report seeing the absent form travel. How does your narrative-binding account explain this without quietly reintroducing feature-tracking through the back door of displaced absence? And if you claim the solver constructs a miniature causal story — the void moves, the ground holds — what prevents that same story from being reconstructed automatically by a sufficiently large predictive model that has already learned temporal dynamics from sequential image and text data?
