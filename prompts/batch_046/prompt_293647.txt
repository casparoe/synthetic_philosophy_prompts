I am developing a conceptual framework for whether collective moral accountability can survive complete demographic turnover within a state—meaning every original perpetrator and direct victim has died, yet the legal order, territorial boundaries, and institutional advantages produced by past injustices remain intact. To this end, I am extending Karl Jaspers’ distinction among criminal, political, moral, and metaphysical guilt toward what I call structural inheritorship: the condition of present citizens who occupy roles within an enduring institutional structure that was shaped by, and continues to derive benefits from, historical wrongs, without having personally authorized or executed them. Among the open conceptual problems that arise when we try to adapt Jaspers’ four categories to this situation, which two or three questions matter most to resolve first—not merely as philosophical puzzles, but as upstream conditions for practical debates in transitional justice, constitutional reform, and public education? Please defend a specific ordering. For each question you select, specify: (i) why it is tractable through conceptual analysis alone, without requiring empirical sociology or history; (ii) which other issues in the framework depend on its resolution; and (iii) what form of conceptual clarification over the next few years would cause you to revise its rank. I am particularly drawn to three candidates—whether metaphysical guilt can be detached from individual conscience and reconceived as a property of institutional roles; whether political guilt requires present democratic authorization of past state acts in order to remain binding on current citizens; and whether moral guilt necessarily collapses into distributed individual blame once demographic turnover is complete—but if you judge another line to be more central, substitute it and defend the replacement. Rank them according to criteria such as upstream dependency, tractability for pure conceptual work, and practical payoff for institutional design. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want an agenda justified by reasoning about conceptual architecture and its downstream consequences.
