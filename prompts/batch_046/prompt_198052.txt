I'm working on a seminar paper in the philosophy of archaeology, specifically on whether material culture by itself can ever license attributions of belief and institutional commitment to societies that left no texts. I would like you to write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—call them Mara, who defends a moderate inferentialist position, and Jonas, who holds a strict anti-mentalistic view—debating this question through a concrete hypothetical case rather than through purely abstract principle-swapping.

The case is this: excavators uncover a pre-literate settlement layer containing hundreds of unfired clay tokens of nearly identical form, each marked with a single consistent geometric impression and stored in standardized ceramic vessels that show no domestic wear patterns. The tokens vary in size according to a precise modular ratio, yet the layer contains no writing, no representational art, and no burial context that clearly signals ritual use. Mara should argue that this assemblage justifies attributing to the community an institutional belief in quantitative equivalence and a normative commitment to standardized measurement; she might propose that the modular ratio functions as a silent calibration system, embedding shared propositional content about fair exchange even when no individual could articulate it. Jonas should counter that calling the pattern calibration already smuggles in modern economic concepts, and that the evidence supports at best a stable behavioral regularity or a distributed practical skill, not a shared mental state or institutional belief; he should press her on how she distinguishes structurally embedded content from mere functional optimization or ecological constraint, and whether her concept of silent calibration does any independent explanatory work or merely renames the observed regularity. The exchange must contain real back-and-forth—each speaker should respond directly to the other's point about the gap between artifact and mind, the risk of anachronistic projection, and whether an institution can exist without occurrent beliefs if it rests solely on coordinated practice. Please do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or references to specific scholarly works, and keep the reasoning within the bounds of what could be constructed without consulting external texts. End with one interlocutor stating precisely what conceptual discovery or archaeological finding would cause them to revise their position.
