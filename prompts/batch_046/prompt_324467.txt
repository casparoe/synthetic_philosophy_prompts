Compare Goodman's grue paradox with an analogous "strobe" predicate: an object is strobe if it reflects green light in sub-millisecond flashes yet absorbs all wavelengths under steady illumination, and all observed samples have been examined only in steady light. Assess whether Goodman's appeal to entrenchment can adjudicate between projecting "green" and "strobe" without circularly assuming the very observational conditions—continuous illumination—that the predicate renders theoretically unstable. Where exactly does the strobe variant either replicate or undermine the asymmetry that makes grue paradoxical?
