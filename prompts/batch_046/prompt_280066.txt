Write a polished dialogue between two philosophers of mind, Dr. Elara Voss and Dr. Kiran Mehta, debating whether emotions are best understood as evaluative judgments, as perceptual representations, or as embodied feelings. Voss holds that emotions are essentially judgments with deontic modal structure—representing how the world ought or ought not to be relative to the subject's concerns—such that if the judgment is withdrawn, the emotion should dissolve, unless some secondary cognitive fixation maintains it. Mehta holds that emotions are essentially calibrated interoceptive predictions—representations of the body's allostatic state—whose content is non-propositional and which can persist independently of any explicit evaluative belief, much as pain can persist after the injury is known to be healed. The dialogue should centre on a specific, conceptually constructed case: a musician who experiences acute shame immediately after a performance she initially judges to have failed, then receives unambiguous evidence that the audience and critics regarded it as superb, sincerely revises her judgment to match, and yet reports that the shame remains undiminished for several hours, with no corresponding change in heart rate, galvanic skin response, or other interoceptive markers, and with no lingering belief that she failed. Each speaker must address the other's actual interpretation of this case—Voss arguing that the persistence reveals a hidden or fragmented judgment, Mehta arguing that it reveals the autonomy of interoceptive representation—rather than speaking past one another. Both should be presented as equally competent, with no straw-manning. The dialogue should be substantive, with genuine back-and-forth, and should conclude without either side being declared the victor. Write the response in British English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific published works.
