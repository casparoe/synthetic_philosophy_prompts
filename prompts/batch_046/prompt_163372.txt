I sit on the Public Culture Commission for a medium-sized city. Next month we must vote on whether to grant an annual operating licence to the proposed Boundary Festival, a programme of exhibitions, readings, and performances that deliberately explores behaviours and representations widely regarded as immoral—though not criminal—within our jurisdiction. The organisers claim that refusal would damage civic liberty; several community and religious groups contend that state-sanctioned display of such material erodes the shared moral ecology and risks social disintegration, irrespective of direct physical harm.

Please draft the Commission’s formal recommendation memo, to be circulated before the vote. It must recommend a concrete course of action—grant, deny, or grant with enforceable conditions—and specify a clear implementation time frame, including an effective date and a review interval for any conditional approval. The reasoning should treat the Hart–Devlin debate on legal moralism as a live framework rather than a historical summary: present Hart’s harm-based liberal position and Devlin’s social-cohesion position fairly, identify the strongest consideration for the position you ultimately reject, and state explicitly where your recommendation concedes ground to that opposing view.

To keep the analysis from being a generic restatement of the standard debate, evaluate the case through the specific distinction between direct tangible harm and moral ecosystem degradation, treating the latter as a contested but plausible category of institutional risk. Write for commissioners with informal philosophical literacy; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not quote primary texts verbatim.

After the memo, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these named fields:
- recommendation: the bottom-line decision in one phrase
- effective_date: the proposed implementation time frame
- hart_core_argument: the key liberal consideration adopted
- devlin_core_argument: the key moralist consideration addressed
- strongest_opposing_case: the best argument for the rejected position
- concession_made: where the recommendation yields ground
- moral_ecology_assessment: a one-sentence evaluation of whether ecosystem degradation applies here
- conditions_if_any: specific enforceable conditions, or null if none
