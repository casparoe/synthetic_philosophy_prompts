I've been playing with a hardline take on Sen's entitlement view: not just that famines are politically mediated, but that natural famine is basicly an empty category for any human society. The reasoning pulls from Bengal '43 (food availability wasn't the problem), the Irish case (blight was the trigger but extraction did the work), and the Great Leap Forward (procuremnt and bad data), but pushes further—if every death pathway runs through prices, state capacity, or rights, then meterology is never the direct cause.

What would have to be true for that to be wrong? I'm not asking for a survey of critics; I want the conditions under which the framing collapses. Is there a conceivable scenerio where distributive institutions are intact and equitable, yet mass starvation happens purely because there's no food? Would a global crop collapse count, or does the analysis just shift to global political failure? Also, is this view dangerously unfalsifiable—can any starvation event be redescribed as an entitlement failure? I want an honest assessment of how exposed this position actually is, including what its defenders should concede counts against it.
