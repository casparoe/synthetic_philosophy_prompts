For roughly two decades, philosophers and moral psychologists have debated whether documented divergence between WEIRD and non-WEIRD populations in folk moral judgments—particularly concerning harm, fairness, loyalty, authority, and purity—undermines moral objectivity or merely reveals culturally variable applications of universal normative principles. Despite extensive empirical literature on cross-cultural moral foundations, trolley-style dilemmas, and cooperation norms, the philosophical exchange has stalled: moral realists typically treat divergence as disagreement over non-moral background conditions or as noise around a shared normative core, while relativists and pluralists treat the same patterns as evidence that no single moral framework is uniquely correct. A further, less-discussed tension is that both sides often rely on a taxonomy of moral domains—harm, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity—that was originally derived from WEIRD philosophical distinctions and may not carve non-WEIRD moral cognition at its joints, raising the possibility that at least part of the measured "disagreement" is an artifact of the measurement framework itself.

Diagnose this impasse. Is it primarily a substantive disagreement about metaphysics, a verbal dispute about what "moral objectivity" requires, or a case in which two research programs—descriptive moral psychology and normative metaethics—are answering different questions under a single heading? To what extent is the stall driven by hidden empirical commitments, such as assumptions about the coherence of non-WEIRD moral systems or the possibility of genuinely incommensurable normative frameworks, that neither side has fully articulated? Furthermore, does the reliance on a WEIRD-derived taxonomy mean that the empirical data being cited are systematically misdescribed, and if so, how should that affect the philosophical argument? Given your diagnosis, specify what would actually count as progress: a refined empirical taxonomy that avoids WEIRD-centric categorization, a formal argument establishing that descriptive divergence either necessarily entails or necessarily fails to entail metaphysical pluralism, or a conceptual distinction separating objectivity as mind-independence from objectivity as universal rational accessibility? Offer a reasoned structural diagnosis rather than a survey of named positions, and indicate whether you think the debate is partly ill-posed because it conflates patterns in normative cognition with conclusions about normative truth.
