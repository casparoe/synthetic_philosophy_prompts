After a long argument with a friend about Strawson's reactive attitudes, I need an outside view. We were discussing whether the reactive framework can survive if the affective and cognitive components of attitudes like resentment are systematically separated—say, by practices that preserve judgments of accountability while suppressing emotional heat through what we called "disaggregated attribution."

My friend argued that Strawson's distinction between reactive and objective attitudes turns essentially on emotional engagement: if you eliminate the felt response, you have moved to viewing people as objects of management, regardless of whether you still utter judgments of fault. She noted that Strawson's central examples—resentment, gratitude, forgiveness—are all affect-laden, and that trying to keep the judgment without the feeling is like keeping the verdict without the trial.

I countered that Strawson explicitly allows reactive attitudes to be suspended or modified in particular cases—the insane, the very young—without collapsing into the objective attitude globally. So there is already internal variation in intensity and form. If we extend that variation, "cool" reactive attitudes—rational disapproval without emotional heat—remain reactive because they still involve holding someone to account within an interpersonal framework, not treating them as a mechanism to be repaired.

She replied that suspension in special cases is different from systematic disaggregation across the board. Once you institutionally separate judgment from affect, you no longer participate in the interpersonal relationships that make reactive attitudes meaningful; you have replaced moral address with administrative classification. The fact that Strawson permits local suspension does not license a global restructuring.

I answered that this assumes the affective component is essential rather than typical, but Strawson never proves that. His argument concerns the incompatibility of determinism with reactive attitudes as a class, not with any particular emotional temperature. If determinism undermines resentment, it might just push us toward cooler forms of the same attitude, not toward the objective stance.

We could not resolve whether systematic disaggregation destroys the reactive framework or merely transforms it. Can you adjudicate point by point: is her claim about emotional essentialism fair to Strawson? Is my appeal to local suspension a valid extension? And what, if anything, are both of us missing about how reactive attitudes relate to moral responsibility?
