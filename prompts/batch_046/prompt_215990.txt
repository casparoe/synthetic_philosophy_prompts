For my senior philosophy seminar, I am comparing Ned Block's distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness with Bernard Baars's global workspace theory. To test which framework is conceptually more coherent, consider a hypothetical neural interface containing two independent modules: Module A broadcasts rich sensory information into working memory and enables full verbal report, yet produces no subjective experience whatsoever; Module B generates intense subjective experience but is structurally isolated so that its contents never enter working memory or guide behavior. Given only these descriptions, which theory—Block's separation of phenomenal and access consciousness, or Baars's identification of consciousness with global broadcasting—better explains why one module should be judged genuinely conscious and the other not? Please justify your answer through conceptual analysis rather than by appealing to specific experiments or authors.
