I recently came across family and community archives documenting continuous ritual practice in a stateless diaspora group across roughly six centuries without institutional state continuity, and the standard theoretical divide now feels personally inadequate. I am pitching a six-month conceptual workshop paper. The precise question: can we coherently distinguish "ancient" from "invented" collective identities when institutional continuity is absent but mnemonic and performative continuity persist? Existing applications of the Hobsbawmian invented-tradition framework and Anderson's imagined-communities model typically presuppose either state-backed institutions or print-capitalism as the relevant carriers, leaving stateless, ritual-continuous groups conceptually stranded between genuine antiquity and modern fabrication. Rather than empirical historical reconstruction, I propose a philosophical taxonomy of three independent dimensions of collective continuity—institutional, mnemonic, and performative—mapped across the logical space of their combinations, asking which combinations license ascriptions of oldness versus invention without assuming statehood.
