I've come round to this somewhat contrarian view that french laïcité and anglo-american multiculturalism aren't actually opposing poles—rather, they're two flavors of the same liberal assimilationist machinery. Laïcité assimilates citizens into a secular public sphere by rendering religious particularity invisible; multiculturalism assimilates them into a state-managed grammar of "recognized" group identity. So the standard framing—france suppresses difference, britain/canada celebrates it—strikes me as a category mistake. Both nutralize deep difference, just through different administrative techniques.

What would count against this? Include what defenders of the standard "opposites" framing would themselves concede is a problem for my view—not just polemics.
