I have been thinking about the old political idea that a leader sometimes has to do something wrong in order to bring about a necessary good — that refusing to dirty one's hands can be its own kind of failure. I am not an academic; I have just been turning it over on my own.

I run a small community discussion group — about ten people, no philosophy training — and we have one ninety-minute meeting. I want a structured role-play or decision game, not a lecture, that makes us feel why someone might take the dirty option and why walking away is not always the cleaner choice.

Please design an activity with these features: a hidden emergency in which full transparency guarantees failure; a secret collective action that breaks a principle everyone has endorsed; and an individual "clean exit" available to any participant, but with the twist that if more than one person takes it, the good outcome collapses and disaster is certain. Include specific roles, rules, a timeline, and a consequence scheme based on social recognition rather than real money. Then provide a debrief that maps moments in the game onto parts of the dirty-hands idea, says what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to manage it, and admits frankly which feature — perhaps the irreversibility and non-transferability of the moral stain — the activity cannot reproduce and I must supply myself.

Please write the answer in British English.
