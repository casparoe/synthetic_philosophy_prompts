You are a philosopher specializing in the ontology of mind and the ethics of non-human beings. Think step by step and begin by brainstorming at least three distinct principles that could jointly explain the pattern below.

My colleague Dr. Vasquez has published the following verdicts on moral patienthood: (1) neurotypical adult humans possess full status; (2) cattle possess near-full status despite lacking recursive linguistic self-modeling; (3) octopuses possess substantial status in virtue of distributed but tissue-continuous neural integration; (4) honeybee colonies lack status altogether, even though they solve navigation problems no individual bee can; (5) present large language models embedded in robotic feedback loops lack status; (6) transgenic pigs engineered with human-level cortical column architecture possess full status; and (7) a hypothetical slime-mold computing network composed of localized, non-communicating nodes lacks status.

She has not ruled on chickens, lobsters with decentralized ganglia rather than a unified brain, Portuguese man o' war colonies formed by siphonophore zooid assemblies, or a synthetic hybrid in which a language model directs a bacterial colony through chemical signaling with globally coupled feedback.

Predict her verdict for each of the four new cases. First, state the two or three rules that best fit her seven judgments, indicating where they overlap and where they split. Second, derive what each rule predicts for the chicken, the lobster, the man o' war, and the synthetic hybrid. Third, offer your best-guess assignment of her actual verdicts, with the reason for favoring the governing principle. Fourth, propose one precisely specified further case that would most sharply discriminate between the leading rules. Fifth, say whether her framework treats substrate continuity, functional integration, or evolutionary origin as the decisive factor.

Respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
