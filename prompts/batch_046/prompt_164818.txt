Let's say Joseph Bertrand was somehow alive today and saw how people use the principle of indifference in modern machine learning—like putting uniform priors over neural network weights or over possible reward functions in AI alignment.

Given his old chord paradox, do you think he would say those ML folks are just repeating his mistake? His point was basically that random is ambiguous because it depends on how you parametrize the space, so picking a chord in a circle gives different answers if you randomize radius vs angle vs midpoint. So when an AI researcher assigns equal prior weight to every parameter vector, aren't they smuggling in some hidden metric or coordinate system? I was also playing around with this niche idea that maybe you rescue indifference not through geometric symmetry but by treating it as an information-theoretic constraint—like minimizing KL divergence away from a reference measure instead of literally drawing lines in a circle. I don't know if that sidesteps the paradox or just moves it somewhere else. Anyway, what do you think Bertrand's reaction would be? Would he see these modern uniform priors as another version of his circle problem, or are high-dimensional parameter spaces different enough that he wouldn't bother?
