I've been arguing with my study group about Newton's bucket and I think I've backed myself into a weird hybrid view. I reject standard substantivalism — there's no absolute space that the bucket rotates relative to — but I also don't buy Mach's principle in its usual form, where inertia is just a relation to distant stars. Instead I'm pushing something I call internal-relationism: the concavity of the water happens because the bucket's own parts stand in asymmetrical relational structures to each other, and extended bodies carry an intrinsic dispositional property that activates whenever there's internal asymmetry, producing inertial effects without appealing to either absolute space or cosmic matter. So the gradient is real, but it's generated inside the system, not outside it.

This saves relationism from depending on the fixed stars, but I worry I've just smuggled a hidden form of substantivalism back in through inertia itself. The view only works if local structural coherence can do all the work that Mach assigned to the universe at large, and I'm not sure wheter that's physically plausible or metaphysically coherent.

What would actually count against this — not just the standard 'Newton was right' reply? I want the real defeaters: which observational results, which counterfactual cases like a rotating shell in an otherwise empty universe, and which commitments in other areas (say, about causation or the persistence of objects through change) this stands or falls with. Would a defender concede defeat if rotation still produced concavity with no distant matter present? Or if quantum mechanics turned out to lack any intrinsic asymmetry at teh micro-level? Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about whether this position has already been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if so, whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a metaphysical claim of this kind. I'm especially curious if it just collapses into disguised substantivalism about inertial structure, or if there's a principled line that saves it.
