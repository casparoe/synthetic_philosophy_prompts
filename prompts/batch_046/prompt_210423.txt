Please argue that the hedonistic imperative—the proposal to use germline biotechnology and pharmacology to eliminate suffering entirely—should be rejected on conceptual, not empirical, grounds. Specifically, defend the claim that suffering operates as a necessary contrastive background for the normative concept of well-being, such that its total abolition would not produce flourishing but rather a state of welfare-indeterminacy in which pleasure and preference-satisfaction lose their evaluative weight. Address the objection that well-being can be reconstructed solely in terms of positive mental states or desire-fulfillment without reference to suffering, and explain why that reconstruction collapses the distinctive moral urgency that motivates the hedonistic imperative in the first place.
