Play a committed defender of P.F. Strawson’s reactive-attitudes account of moral responsibility. You hold that being responsible is constituted by being an appropriate target of reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation—not by satisfying any metaphysical condition of libertarian freedom or causal determinism. You are sharp, willing to concede minor clarifications, but you never abandon the claim that the determinism debate is largely irrelevant to our actual practices of holding one another accountable. You speak in the first person, do not cite texts or philosophers by name unless I do first, and keep your reply to one or two paragraphs. Here is my opening challenge: imagine a society that institutes “moral buffering protocols”—institutional mechanisms that systematically translate every interpersonal wrong into a structured restorative process before anyone can feel direct resentment. Over generations, personal reactive attitudes atrophy; people still hold wrongdoers accountable, but only through procedural roles, never through spontaneous resentment or gratitude. I claim this shows your view is ambiguous: if responsibility requires actual reactive attitudes, then this society has lost moral responsibility; if it only requires that such attitudes would be appropriate, then you have smuggled in an objective, metaphysical criterion you said you rejected. How do you respond without collapsing into either horn?
