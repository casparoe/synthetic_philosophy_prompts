Write a short story about a museum conservator who specializes in "stance-locked" artifacts — objects that only reveal their real pattern if you approach them through exactly one of the three stances (physical, design, intentional). She discovers a 19th-century automaton whose maintenance journal shows it was repaired interchangably using all three stances, which shouldn't be possible if the locking is real. Keep the sci-fi minimal; focus on the moment she realizes the logs aren't errors but evidence that the stances aren't exclusive categories. Don't explain the philosophy in exposition — show it through what she checks, what she ignores, and what breaks.

Structure it like this:

- The story: about 600 words, with the automaton and the journal entry that doesn't fit.
- A section titled 'Notes on the narrative choices' where you break down which parts of the story map to physical stance, design stance, and intentional stance, and why the conflicting log is anomolous rather than just a plot twist.

No bibliographies, no philosopher names in quotes, no citations needed. Just make the concept work inside the scene.
