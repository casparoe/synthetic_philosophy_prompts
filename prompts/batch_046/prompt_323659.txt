Consider a morning commute in which you depend on an opaque navigation algorithm that reroutes you around an accident with perfect accuracy, though you have no access to its reasoning or the traffic data it processes; you arrive safely with a true belief about the correct path, but you understand nothing of why it was correct. Your colleague takes the same detour, but she reaches it through her own expert grasp of local traffic dynamics and can explain each factor. Both beliefs are true and both guide action equally well, yet you feel there is something deficient in your algorithm-dependent belief compared with hers, even though you cannot specify the deficiency. Is that feeling justified—does her belief possess a genuine surplus value that yours lacks, or is the distinction between true belief arrived at blindly and knowledge arrived at through understanding merely cosmetic? If there is a real difference, what exactly makes knowledge more valuable than mere true belief in cases like this?
