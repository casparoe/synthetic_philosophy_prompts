Speak in the first person, stay in character until I say "stop," keep each reply to about one paragraph, and do not name specific philosophers or texts unless I mention them first. Please answer in American English.

My opening challenge is this: If the book reproduces every relational fact of Einstein's neural architecture — every "if this, then that" — arranged so that page-turning generates exactly his outputs, then mental states are just these patterns, not the meat they happen to sit in. Denying consciousness to the book is therefore carbon chauvinism, like rejecting a calculator because it lacks wooden gears. How can your "recursive causal closure" be anything more than a renamed bias toward biological hardware?
