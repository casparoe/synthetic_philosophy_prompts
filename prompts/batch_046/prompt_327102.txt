I am writing a feature on why historians still argue about whether they can be objective, and I want to set the terms precisely before interviewing scholars. Give me the desiderata—what an adequate theory of historical objectivity must be able to deliver—taking as its problem space the tension between Leopold von Ranke’s source-critical ideal of reconstructing the past on its own terms and the sceptical lines developed by E.H. Carr and Hayden White, who treat every narrative as a present-minded construction shaped by selection, emplotment, and ideological interest. Specify the phenomena it must explain: for instance, how contradictory primary sources can coexist with a justified claim rather than forcing either silence or arbitrary choice; how hindsight bias can be acknowledged as unavoidable without collapsing the distinction between explanation and retrospective projection; and how microhistorical anomalies that resist grand narrative can be integrated without being erased as noise. List the distinctions it must preserve—between objectivity as disciplined method versus metaphysical correspondence, between causal coherence and teleological coherence imposed by the historian, and between legitimate interpretive framing and falsification of the record—with the case behind each. Flag which requirements are non-negotiable (those whose abandonment dissolves the very problem of objectivity) and which are camp preferences (e.g., determinism about archival meaning, or the denial of any trans-historical causal structure). Identify any subset of desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable, especially given White’s claim that all history is tropologically structured and Ranke’s claim that fidelity to sources constrains that structure; say which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidates. Then, briefly and without a full scorecard, indicate how Rankean source realism, Carr’s interest-driven explanation, and White’s narrativist constructivism stand against the list—but keep the weight on the criteria, not the scoring. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
