In our metaphysics reading group we've been going in circles for like six months on whether there's really a fact about whether composite objects exist -- you know, is a table just particles arranged table-wise, or is there something extra? The universalists and the nihilists both agree on the physics, both agree on the language rules, and yet nobody has moved. My working theory is that the disagreement looks ontological but is actually about whether 'composition' should be analyzed as an explanatory relation or as a linguistic regimentation relation, and both sides keep smuggling in assumptions about the other. So is this a verbal dispute about the word 'exists' or about 'composition', or is there a hidden substantive question about explanatory priority that's being masked? And given whatever diagnosis you have, is there actually a conceptual distinction -- maybe separating explanatory dependance from compositional dependance, or framing the question as about theory choice rather than world-structure -- that could unstick us, or should we just accept that this debate is permanently stalled because it bottoms out in pre-theoretic intuitons?
