Write a dialogue between Dr. Mara Lin, who defends an indexical-procedural account of rationality in sequential games, and Dr. Jonas Reeve, who defends an atemporal-structural account, debating whether backward induction genuinely dissolves cooperation in the centipede game when applied to institutional design. Set their exchange around a concrete analog: a four-stage international climate-financing pact in which each stage unlocks exponentially larger collective benefits, yet each party can defect at any round to retain its capital. Lin should argue that rationality is indexed to the decision node—at stage three, the agent reasons from within the tree, not from a timeless strategy profile—so the future defection predicted by backward induction is not a present premise binding the agent’s choice, and cooperation survives. Reeve should reply that this relocates rather than resolves the paradox: without atemporal consistency, agents cannot rationally enter binding commitments at stage one, and treaty design collapses before it begins. Have them address whether “common knowledge of rationality” is the correct diagnosis, or whether the deeper crux is whether rational agents can reason “from within” a sequential structure without undermining the very planning that made entry rational. Require real back-and-forth: each should modify or qualify a claim in direct response to the other’s objection, and neither should be a strawman. Keep the dialogue focused on whether the paradox is dissolved, relocated, or transformed by the indexical move, and on what that implies for designing real multi-round agreements.
