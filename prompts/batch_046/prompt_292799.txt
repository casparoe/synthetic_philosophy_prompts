I am a novelist drafting a scene in which a surgeon contemplates an irreversible neural modification that will replace her tactile sense with something she cannot presently imagine. I should like a poem — twelve to sixteen lines, unrhymed iambic pentameter, with a volta at line nine — spoken in her voice, that renders the structure of a transformative choice without invoking the usual philosophical exemplars of parenthood or vampirism. The poem should turn at the precise moment she recognises that rational deliberation demands a standpoint she can only occupy after she has already surrendered her current one. Please keep the language literary rather than expository, leave the tension unresolved, and ensure the form itself enacts the circularity: the first eight lines should build her confidence in weighing outcomes, while the closing lines should collapse that confidence into the admission that the only way to know is to become unknowable to herself.
