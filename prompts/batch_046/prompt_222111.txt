I am preparing the moderator's script for a virtual colloquium Q&A in normative ethics, and I have been asked to interview a philosopher—whom I will refer to as Dr. Voss, though she prefers to remain unnamed in this context—who has been developing a response to the problem of consequentialist cluelessness that deliberately avoids the usual escape routes. After discussing her work with colleagues over the past month, I have become convinced that the standard objections—that she is merely smuggling in rule-consequentialism, or that she licenses paralysis in climate and public-health decision-making—tend to miss the precise mechanism she proposes, yet I am unsure how to press her without allowing her to deflect into a generic defense of epistemic humility. Her view, which she calls threshold-sensitive consequentialism, holds that the normative weight of an expected outcome should be scaled by an agent-specific reliability coefficient tied to predictive confidence: when that confidence about distant consequences falls below a domain-sensitive threshold, the calculus does not yield inaction but instead redirects optimization toward what she terms procedural outcomes—the robustness, transparency, and correctability of the decision architecture itself—rather than toward the resulting world-state. She insists this is not rule-worship, because the procedural targets are themselves selected by a first-order consequentialist evaluation of institutional design, and she claims this avoids the paralysis objection because climate policy can still be guided by near-term, high-confidence procedural benchmarks such as emissions-accounting integrity or adaptive governance structures.

I need six to eight interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate this framework. Specifically, I want at least one question probing whether her reliability coefficient can be calibrated without circular appeal to the very long-range outcomes it is meant to discount; one on how she distinguishes legitimate procedural deference from mere bureaucratic risk-aversion in high-stakes domains; and one that anticipates her likely reply—that procedural outcomes are ultimately long-run world-states evaluated by the same calculus—and then delivers the follow-up about whether that reply collapses her distinction between outcome-optimization and architecture-optimization. For each question, please include a brief annotation of the answer she is most likely to give and a sharper, keyed follow-up tailored to that specific reply. The audience will include graduate students familiar with the cluelessness literature but not with her specific formulation, so the questions should be technically precise without being elementary. Please avoid generic biographical or career-oriented questions, and do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or further reading lists.
