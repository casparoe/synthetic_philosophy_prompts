Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains that alternate and converge in the final two lines—that dramatizes moral particularism against moral generalism through the figure of one moral feature observed in sharply different contexts. Adopt the voice of a particularist speaker. Let one refrain carry the generalist conviction that a feature such as breaking a promise retains invariant moral weight regardless of circumstance; let the other refrain carry the particularist reply that the feature’s moral valence is configured holistically by the surrounding moral landscape rather than carried atomistically. The form should do the conceptual work: as the refrains recur through new contextual stanzas, they should accumulate altered resonance or emotional weight, so that the final quatrain holds both positions in suspension rather than synthesizing them. Include one concrete, non-obvious scenario—perhaps within an institution sustained by systematic deception—where the speaker argues the feature does not merely diminish in force but actually reverses its moral polarity. The poem should be precise enough that a reader familiar with particularist holism of reasons recognizes the view being staged, yet it must stand as poetry without apparatus: no citations, no bibliographic references, no named authors or texts. Aim for strict villanelle form in American English.
