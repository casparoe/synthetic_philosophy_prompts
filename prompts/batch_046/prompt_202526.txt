As a retired biologist who has recently begun studying moral philosophy, I am attempting to construct a conceptual taxonomy—structured as a decision tree—of how deeply entrenched social norms, specifically chattel slavery and formal dueling, actually cease to be practiced. I am interested not in historical chronology but in the logical space of explanations. The root node should pose whether the collapse of such a norm is driven primarily by an internal failure of its own moral reasoning, by an external shift in material interests that renders compliance irrational, or by a transformation in the non-rational economy of honor and status. From there, branch on whether the mechanism operates through individual conviction, collective institutional realignment, or competitive status renegotiation. Each leaf must identify a named philosophical position where one exists—such as a rationalist argument-driven thesis, an economic-interest thesis, the view associated with Bernard Williams that thick ethical concepts can become unintelligible across cultural distance, or Kwame Anthony Appiah’s account of honor-code transformation—or else be marked 'unnamed' and carry a one-sentence description of its most awkward commitment (for example, that it must treat the disappearance of dueling as entirely accidental to the professionalization of the military). The tree should contain at least one logical combination of answers that no prominent theorist occupies; for that empty leaf, diagnose whether the vacancy results from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Every branch should be phrased as a concrete diagnostic question that a respondent could answer without knowing in advance which philosopher it selects—e.g., 'Does the norm dissolve because its practitioners come to regard it as incompatible with personal dignity, or because the social costs of maintaining it begin to exceed its signaling value?'—and the instrument should not depend on bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific texts. I want the taxonomy to be built from the conceptual distinctions rather than from a roster of names: first the branching questions, then the positions located in that space, with unoccupied combinations noted and briefly explained.
