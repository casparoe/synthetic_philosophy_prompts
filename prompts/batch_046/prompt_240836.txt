In my advanced epistemology seminar we have spent weeks on the regress problem—foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism—yet the debate feels frozen. Every side can recite Agrippa’s trilemma and its standard replies: foundationalists invoke basic beliefs with non-inferential warrant, coherentists appeal to mutual explanatory coherence, and infinitists defend infinite non-repeating chains of reasons. Still, no one in the literature seems to move. My working hypothesis is that the stalemate is not about the actual structure of justification but about a hidden disagreement over whether justification is fundamentally a normative status—something we are entitled to claim—or a descriptive feature of belief systems, and that the three positions are therefore answering different questions under one name. Is that right, or is there a deeper empirical or formal issue in disguise—perhaps about cognitive architecture or about what a reason must be? I want a diagnosis that engages what each side actually says, not a survey: do they differ about facts, about which cases count as data, about bedrock intuitions regarding arbitrariness, or is the question itself ill-posed? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—a conceptual distinction, a formal result about belief dynamics, a change in the framing of the question—or should we expect the impasse to remain permanent?
