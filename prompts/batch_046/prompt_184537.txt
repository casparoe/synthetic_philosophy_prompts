I'm putting together my philosophical profile for a discussion group on well-being and I want a consistency check before I present it. Here’s where I stand:

(a) Tranquilism is basically right — only the absence of craving (tanha/upadana) and suffering carries intrinsic value; positive pleasure, meaning, achievement, etc., are morally weightless, at best instrumentally useful.

(b) That gives us the asymmetry: it’s wrong to bring a suffering being into existence, but not wrong to fail to bring a blissful one into being, since bliss adds zero value.

(c) For policy I think we should maximise total net tranquility summed across all affected lives, irrespective of distribution.

(d) But I also hold that it’s never permissible to maintain one person’s craving so another can reach deeper tranquility — say through exploitative labour — even if the aggregate sum goes up.

(e) I deny phenomenal consciousness is valuable in itself; what matters is just the absence of negative affect. So a being in permanent dreamless sleep with no suffering is in an excellent state, and I think we should count them in the welfare calculus, even though there's nothing it is like for them.

(f) Moral status tracks capacity for affective craving exactly: animals have it in degrees, plants and AI don’t, regardless of information-processing complexity.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which ones only look tense but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real ones by severity. For each, say what the cheapest revision is and what it costs me. I havn't decided which commitments are load-bearing, so don’t assume I’d rather save (c) than (d) — I genuinely haven’t decided which ones I'd drop. Also, I’m not after a lecture on tranquilism itself; I want an audit of my own set.
