I work as a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, though I have been reading Nietzsche informally after a colleague brought up eternal recurrence in a conversation about career burnout and irreversible life choices. I want to turn this idea into a concrete, reusable decision procedure I can actually run—not just a vague mental exercise—whenever I face a high-stakes, hard-to-reverse commitment. The kinds of decisions I have in mind are professional and personal: accepting a promotion that requires relocating and sustained overwork, locking my team into a proprietary architecture, or choosing whether to stay silent about an ethical problem I have discovered.

Please construct a step-by-step procedure that asks questions in a fixed order, defines what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, and marks precisely where it halts with a definitive negative versus where it must defer to a judgment of values that the procedure itself cannot resolve. The framework should incorporate two distinctions that matter to me: first, between asking whether I could endure the literal repetition of this specific event, and asking whether I could affirm the kind of person I become by making this choice under infinite repetition; second, between the outcome of the decision and what I will call its heaviest moment—the point in the decision tree where the choice reveals character rather than merely producing consequences. It must also contain an explicit failure mode for cases where the recurrence test seems to approve a decision that is clearly self-destructive or ethically corrosive if taken literally, and it should be honest about what it cannot do for someone running it without philosophical expertise or external sources.

Then apply the procedure to three proposals: (1) accepting a staff-level promotion that requires moving to a different city and working sustained sixty-hour weeks; (2) adopting a proprietary cloud framework that solves our immediate scaling crisis but locks our architecture in for years; and (3) deciding not to escalate a security vulnerability I have found because disclosure would trigger a painful audit and likely delay my team's release. One of these applications should yield an answer that conflicts with my intuitive preference, and you should say what that conflict reveals about the limits of the procedure. Keep in mind that I need to complete this evaluation in about thirty minutes, I will be the one running it with occasional input from the senior engineer who mentors me, and I do not have access to academic texts to resolve ambiguities that arise.
