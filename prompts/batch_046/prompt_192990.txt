I’m putting together a workshop on emergent minds and I want people to actually feel the weirdness of Hofstadter’s Aunt Hillary thing instead of just nodding along. Design a 45-minute classroom activity for about 15 students where they physically role-play an ant colony deciding whether to relocate—each student only recieves local “pheromone” cards and has zero global view. After the simulation, split them: half defend that the colony just experienced a unified collective intention, the other half says it was pure aggregate mechanics with no mind in the middle.

Here’s a niche twist to bake in: imagine a hypothetical mechanism called inter-ant binding, where individual ants momentarily share a transient neural-like state through rapid antenea contact, not chemical trails. In the debrief, ask whether this binding shifts anyone’s intuition about Aunt Hillary being a real subject, and be honest about which feature of Hofstadter’s ant fugue analogy the game flat-out can’t reproduce—probably the recursive self-reference part. Keep rules simple with just paper and chairs, include a rough timeline, and make sure the idea shows up in what happens, not just in what the facilitator says afterwards.
