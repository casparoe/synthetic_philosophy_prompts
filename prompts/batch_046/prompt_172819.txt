I am preparing a single fifty-minute seminar for first-year philosophy-of-science graduate students who are not specialists in logical empiricism. The topic is Otto Neurath's proposal for a universal picture language—Bildersprache—and its practical realization in the ISOTYPE system. To carry the idea, I want to use a contemporary analogy: modern climate-science reports that replace disciplinary jargon with standardized icon arrays representing phenomena such as drought, forced migration, and economic loss. Plan the explanation: the precise order in which the conceptual components must be introduced so that Neurath's rejection of theoretical terms in scientific communication feels motivated rather than naive, including where the distinction between Carnap's logical syntax and Neurath's physicalist protocol sentences must enter; the concrete carrying scenario based on the climate-icon report, specifying exactly what it must preserve and what it must lack in order to illustrate Neurath's ambition rather than merely illustrate contemporary data visualization; the misconceptions students are likely to bring—such as treating picture languages as empirically pure, reducing ISOTYPE to an infographic style, or interpreting Neurath as advancing a private language in the Wittgensteinian sense—and the point in the sequence where each should be defused; a brief check for understanding that reveals whether students see that visual representation still carries theoretical commitments; and what should be explicitly excluded at this level, such as the internal disputes of the Vienna Circle or the granular design rules of ISOTYPE, with a clear acknowledgment that these lie outside the seminar's scope.
