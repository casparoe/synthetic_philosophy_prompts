ok so I've been trying to sort my head out on just war and the moral equality of combatants before I write a longer post, and I want to check whether my package is actually coherent or if I'm smuggling contradictions in without realising it.

Here's roughly where I sit:

1. I'm convinced by the revisionist line — there's no moral equality of combatants. If you're fighting for an unjust cause, you don't have the same rights to kill as the just side. The old Walzer-style symetry just feels wrong.

2. At the same time, I think nearly all individual soldiers are epistimically excused. They don't have access to the jus ad bellum reasoning, so they shouldn't be blamed personally, even if they're technically liable.

3. I think democratic citizens have less excuse than conscripts, because they can vote and protest, so fighting in an unjust war carries a kind of collective culpability for them. But I don't think that makes them legitimate targets — it's more a moral stain.

4. I've been toying with this idea that in modern networked warfare, people aren't discrete agents but nodes in a distributed system, so liability should attach to roles rather than individuals. I call it "structurally open" vs "structurally closed" roles — open ones let you refuse without catastrophic harm, closed ones don't. I think this modifies revisionism but I'm not sure how, whilst still keeping the asymmetry.

5. I also think proportionality is asymmetric: the just side can weigh civilian harm against achieving justice, but the unjust side doesn't get to run that calculation, because they lack the moral standing.

6. One more thing: if a soldier genuinely can't know whether their war is just, then they shouldn't be treated as liable at all — liability needs some awareness of wrongness, or at least voluntary participation in an unjust enterprise.

So... do these hang together? Which pairs are in real tension and which only look like it? I'm especially worried about (1) vs (6) and whether (4) breaks (1) or just complicates it. What's the cheapest fix if anything has to go? Be specific about severity and don't just tell me it's all fine — I can tell I'm fudging something, probably around the excused-but-liable bit.
