The question concerns aesthetic testimony in a cross-modal case involving a museum installation called a "synesthetic archive"—it encodes historical narratives solely through calibrated temperature gradients and haptic resistance patterns, with no visual or auditory channel. A trained curator testifies that one segment is "tragic" and "exhausting," but a visitor can only access the thermal logs, not experience the gradients directly.

Question: Analyze whether this is a case of genuine aesthetic testimony. In particular, distinguish carefully between (i) testimony that transmits an aesthetic judgment, (ii) testimony that enables the recipient to form their own independent judgment, and (iii) testimony that merely reports the testifier's psychological reaction. Your answer should address whether the absence of direct perceptual access collapses the case into empirical testimony about physical measurements, and whether standard philosophical objections to aesthetic testimony apply here.

Student answer: The curator's statement is clearly not type (i), because aesthetic testimony never actually transmits knowledge—this is Hopkins's point, drawing on Kant's claim that beauty requires a free play of imagination and understanding that conceptual testimony cannot trigger. Since the archive has no visual or auditory component, Kant's framework doesn't apply at all, which means there are no aesthetic properties present; there are only thermal properties, so the curator is really giving empirical testimony about temperature plus a psychological report about her own feelings (type iii). The visitor can't reach type (ii) either, because without direct thermal experience they lack the perceptual basis needed to form any aesthetic judgment, mediated or not. So cross-modal cases actually prove that aesthetic testimony collapses into empirical or psychological reports whenever the recipient's primary sensory modality differs from the work's. The only knowledge gained is about the physical parameters and about the curator's emotional state.
