I've been trying to understand those situations where people begin adopting a belief or action not because they've evaluated the evidence themselves, but because they observe others doing it and assume those others know something they don't—what I understand is called an information cascade. The standard examples feel too tidy, though, and I'm especially curious about an uncomfortable edge case that never appears in the simple versions: what happens when the very absence of action gets misinterpreted as a positive signal?

Could you write a short story that explores this in a concrete, high-stakes setting? Imagine a remote island fishing community that has just received a strange new device—a weather-forecasting box that prints ambiguous symbols. The villagers must decide whether to move their boats into safe harbor before an approaching season of storms. Among them are a few individuals who can genuinely interpret fragments of the symbols (call them the readers), and a larger group who have no understanding of the device but carefully watch whose boats move and copy that behavior (the herders).

The specific conceptual mechanism I want at the center is what I will call a "silent cascade." When a reader is genuinely uncertain about an ambiguous symbol, she makes no move and keeps her boat in place—not out of confidence, but out of caution. The herders, however, cannot distinguish this thoughtful hesitation from a confident judgment that the open water is safe. They therefore copy the inaction, spreading stillness through the fleet. I want you to show, through dialogue and scene, how this observational equivalence—where identical external behavior masks radically different internal states—gradually corrupts the community's decision-making over several days. Follow at least one reader and one herder. The story should conclude as an actual storm strikes, forcing the hidden distinction between true understanding and copied behavior to suddenly determine who survives. Write it as fiction, not as an explanation, but keep the mechanics of rational herding and misinterpreted silence precisely at the heart of the narrative.
