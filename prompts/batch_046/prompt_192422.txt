I have been reviewing a colleague’s précis of Rorty’s antirepresentationalism and want to test whether it does justice to his view before I reply to it in seminar. The précis runs as follows: Rorty denies that language or thought ever represents an independent reality, holding instead that vocabularies are tools for coping rather than mirrors of nature; consequently, he treats scientific theories and literary metaphors as on a par, differing only in practical utility but not in any proximity to truth; he rejects objective justification altogether, reducing knowledge to belief endorsed by one’s peers; and he assigns philosophy the purely therapeutic task of dissolving representationalist pseudo-problems. From this, the author infers that when two vocabularies—say, climate modeling and an indigenous cosmology—make competing predictions about seasonal fire patterns but embed incompatible ontologies, Rorty offers no rational resources for adjudication beyond local social preference, since there is no representational court of appeal. Is this summary fair? Specifically, which parts accurately capture Rorty, and which subtly conflate his rejection of correspondence-truth with a denial of predictive success, or his contextualism about justification with a wholesale rejection of norms? Does his actual distinction between redescription and argument provide any form of epistemic friction between vocabularies that share practical targets but differ in ontological commitment? Finally, does the conclusion about incommensurable adjudication survive once the summary is corrected? Please answer in a single, tightly argued paragraph of no more than 300 words, without citations, bibliographies, or a reading list.
