For a novel I am drafting, I need to understand the philosophical architecture of a character who takes Quine's naturalism to heart but applies it, perhaps too zealously, to the domain of moral judgment. I have constructed the following line of reasoning, which I suspect may contain a hidden error, and I would appreciate your help in locating it.

Quine's ontological relativity holds that questions of existence—whether there are numbers, whether there are rabbits—only make sense relative to a background theory, and that competing theories can be empirically equivalent yet ontologically incompatible. Separately, his thesis of the indeterminacy of translation shows that a speaker's use of terms like gavagai can be mapped onto rabbit, undetached rabbit part, or other hypotheses, all equally supported by behavioral evidence, with no further fact to settle which translation is correct.

From these premises, I have inferred that moral disagreement must operate in the same register. If two societies apply the term cruel to radically different behaviors—one condemning ritual sacrifice, the other requiring it—then, on Quine's account, they are not disputing a shared moral fact. Rather, they are using different but behaviorally adequate translation manuals for normative language, embedded in conceptual schemes that are empirically equivalent with respect to social coordination. Moral condemnation therefore collapses into a choice of ontology: there is no scheme-independent wrongness, only a decision about which conceptual framework to inhabit.

This conclusion strikes me as both philosophically implausible and personally repugnant to the character, yet I cannot see exactly where the inference breaks down. Is the error that moral values are not ontological commitments at all—unlike numbers or physical objects—so that applying ontological relativity to them is a category mistake? Or does Quine's view of ethics, perhaps as adjustments near the periphery of the web of belief rather than as posits in a scientific ontology, allow moral criticism to survive without requiring scheme-independent moral entities?

Please do not simply endorse the relativist conclusion; identify and correct the confusion in my reasoning. In your answer, state how confident you are in your analysis, and single out the specific point about which you are least certain.
