Ive been in this philosophy discussion group and weve been stuck on open borders versus the right to exclude for like six months now. One camp keeps saying the state can exclude migrants the same way a private club can reject applicants — its about collective self-determination and protecting shared resources. The other camp insists territory isnt club property; citizens dont own the land in any meaningful sense, so exclusion is just using force to keep people from opportunities they need. Then one of us threw in this weird framing: what if citizenship is a positional good, meaning it only matters because it keeps others out? If thats true, then protecting our members is really just preserving a hierarchy, not safeguarding something intrinsically valuable. The first camp came back saying even positional goods can ground associative obligations — you dont have to think citizenship is sacred to think you owe more to fellow members. And now were just repeating ourselves. Is this disagreement actually about property analogies, about whether obligations can exist without intrinsic value, or are we just talking past each other with different bedrock intuitions about strangers? Also, is there anything — an empirical result, a conceptual distinction, whatever — that could actually move it, or should we just accept that were stuck?
