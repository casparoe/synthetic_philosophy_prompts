I've been leaning hard toward Dennett's side in the dreams debate — that there's no Cartesian theater running a movie during REM, just discontinuous drafts that get stitched into a narrative after you wake up. Malcolm's view, that we're genuinely experiencing events in sleep and then just misremembering the details, seems to buy back the very theater Dennett wants to tear down.

But I don't want to just cheer for my side. Tell me what would actually count against this position — not generic appeals to phenomenology, but which specific results, thought experiments, or cross-domain commitments it risks. For example, suppose real-time neural decoding during REM recovered coherant visual sequences that matched a subject's later report without any post-waking prompt — fatal blow, or can the drafts model absorb it? Or take lucid dreamers who perform pre-agreed counting tasks during REM with correlating neural markers; if there's a stable cognitive agent tracking time in sleep, does that force us to admit an experiencing subject?

Rank the defeaters for me: fatal, serious, merely awkward. Say which ones a committed Dennettian would concede actually hurt, and which they'd dismiss as Malcolm-style intution mongering. And be honest — is the view so well-insulated that it's unfalsifiable, and is that a bug or a feature? I personally think the lucid-dream counting case is the most threatening, but I want to know if I'm wrong about that too.
