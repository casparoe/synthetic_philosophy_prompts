I'm on the governing board for the Midwestern Public University System (yeah, the one that covers both the flagship and the regional campuses), and we have a vote next month on switching our funding model. Right now it's tuition plus state subsidy, kinda like the UK mix but lower. The proposal is a "graduate contribution" plan: income-contingent repayment at 4% for 25 years, but fully forgiven if you spend 10+ years in public-sector work. Trustees are split. Some say it keeps access open without dumping costs on taxpayers who never went to college; others say it still commodifies education and creates a two-tier system where rich families just pay upfront to avoid the "tax" while poor graduates carry it for decades. There's a philosophical split too: one trustee brought up Rawls's difference principle to say debt is okay if it improves access for the worst off; another said it's basically Nozick-style indentured servitude since graduates never signed a contract with that exact repayment schedule.

Please draft the memo I'd circulate before Thursday's meeting. I want a clear recommendation -- adopt, reject, or adopt with conditions -- not just a list of pros and cons. The memo should have these parts in this order:

- Executive summary (around 150 words)
- The practical case for the plan (around 200 words)
- The strongest philosophical objection, taken seriously (around 250 words)
- My verdict with concrete conditions if any (around 200 words)

Write it formal but readable. Don't ask for citations or a reading list -- I need arguments I can actually use in the room without pulling up papers to recieve them. And don't just restate the legal defintion of funding; engage the two-tier worry and the indenture analogy head-on. Oh, and please avoid quoting specific philosophers verbatim -- paraphrase if you need to reference them.
