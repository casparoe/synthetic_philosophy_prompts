Focus on three domains: (1) the conditions under which such an agent could be said to suffer or experience harm in the absence of any phenomenally conscious state analogous to human suffering; (2) the ethical constraints this interpretation imposes on reward-shaping and curriculum design in high-stakes deployment contexts; and (3) the conceptual boundary it redraws between instrumental error correction and experiential welfare. Where the implications seem to conflict—particularly between treating the signal as purely functional and treating it as ethically loaded—indicate which resolution you take and what it costs.
