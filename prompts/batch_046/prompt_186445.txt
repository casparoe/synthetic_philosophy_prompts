I am a retired evolutionary biologist who, since leaving active research, has audited graduate seminars in philosophy of language and philosophy of science; I am now assisting in the design of a final examination for an upper-level undergraduate course on Wittgenstein. I want one essay question on his account of language games and family resemblance that cannot be answered by reciting the familiar examples—games, numbers, colors—or by summarizing the rejection of essentialism. To force genuine engagement, I want the question to embed an analogy drawn from taxonomy: students should be asked to compare Wittgenstein’s treatment of the concept “game” with the biological problem of a polyphyletic grouping, that is, a class of organisms that share observable morphological traits but do not descend from a common ancestor exclusive to the class. The question should require students to articulate precisely what family resemblance denies about necessary and sufficient conditions, to explain how it accommodates borderline cases without collapsing into mere vagueness, and to evaluate whether the polyphyletic analogy exposes a hidden cost—namely, that preserving the grouping by abandoning shared ancestry (or shared essence) may leave the category without explanatory or predictive power, much as cladists reject polyphyletic taxa. The exam is for advanced undergraduates, to be completed in 90 minutes, and students will have no access to external sources, so the prompt must not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations.

Please write the examination question itself, followed by a model answer of approximately 600 words, and then a marking scheme that separates an A-level response from a B-level response by naming the specific argumentative moves each must contain—for instance, whether the student distinguishes family resemblance from simple ambiguity, whether they correctly identify the explanatory deficit in the biological analogy, and whether they address the objection that Wittgenstein’s account renders the concept “game” theoretically idle. The marking scheme should indicate the weight assigned to each required move and should note the characteristic mistake that costs marks at each point. Write in a formal, polished register appropriate to an examination document, and do not include any verbatim quotations from published texts unless you have verified the wording against a fetched source; paraphrase without quotation marks where attribution is unnecessary.
