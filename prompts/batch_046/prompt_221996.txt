hey so I'm writing this up for a seminar on modal epistemology and I cooked up this argument that feels like it should work but ends in a place I don't want to be. (1) If I can genuinely conceive a state of affairs—build a detailed mental model with no hidden contradition—then that state is metaphysically possible, not just logically possible. (2) I can genuinely conceive of a "silent mirror" agent: physically and functionally identical to me right now, but with every phenomenal quality inverted (what I call pain is experienced as pleasure, red as green, etc.), and with my conceptual vocabulary also inverted so the being never detects the swap. (3) So it's metaphysically possible that I am, right now, such a silent mirror being, or that my study partner is. (4) But if that's genuinely possible, then I can never know anyone's actual mental states, since for all I can tell we're all silently inverted relative to each other. (5) That kind of global skepticism about consciousness makes ordinary psychology and friendship basically impossible, which is absurd. (6) So premise (1) must be wrong—conceivability doesn't reach metaphysical possibility after all.

But I actually like (2) a lot; the silent mirror seems just as coherent as a philosophical zombie, and I don't want to just say "well I can't really conceive it" because that feels like cheating. Is the error in (3), where I slide from "the scenario is possible" to "I might actually be in it"? Or is there an equivocation in (1) between conceiving of a type and conceiving of a token? Or should I just bite the bullet and reject (4) instead—maybe possibility doesn't undermine knowledge the way I think? I want to keep (2) and avoid (5) if I can, but I need to know what I'm paying for it.
