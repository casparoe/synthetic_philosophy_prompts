I have been working through Nietzsche's second Untimely Meditation with a small reading group, and we keep returning to his claim that history serves life only when it operates in one of three modes—monumental, antiquarian, or critical—each of which turns poisonous when it monopolizes the others. For our next meeting I would like to open with a joke that actually turns on these distinctions rather than reducing Nietzsche to a generic signifier for philosophical gloom.

The punchline must depend on the specific pathology of one mode—for instance, the critical historian dissolving the ground beneath their own feet while trying to stand on it, or the monumental historian attempting to live like a past hero in a context that makes the imitation physically or socially disastrous—so that someone who understands the text recognizes the joke is about the imbalance among these living relations to the past rather than about history being simply bad.
