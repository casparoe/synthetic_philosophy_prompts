i've been trying to work out this argument about collective memory vs the archive and now i'm stuck because it seems to drive all the way to historical relativism, which feels wrong but i can't see the crack. here's the chain:

(1) Call it mnemonic sovereignty: for any community C, if C collectively remembers event E in way M, and the historical record R says E happened differently or not at all, then M should take precedence over R whenever they conflict.
(2) This isn't just politeness. R is usually built by outsiders, states, or colonizers with their own interests, while M is embodied knowledge from those who actually lived through it.
(3) But "what happened" isn't only physical movements; it includes the meaning and impact on C. So if C remembers a massacre that R calls a minor skirmish, the massacre is the true historical fact for C, not just a different interpretation.
(4) If that's right for one community, it's right for all. Two communities with opposite memories of the same shared past are both correct about "what happened" within their respective frameworks.
(5) So there is no single objective past independent of collective memory; the archive is just one more memory-system with no special authority.
(6) That means archaeology, forensics, and cross-checking sources have no standing to overrule a community's memory. When they do, they're not correcting errors but committing epistemic violence.
(7) So either we reject (1) and let outsiders overrule lived experience, or we accept full-blown historical relativism where physical evidence is always subordinate to memory.

i feel like the slip is somewhere between (3) and (4) — maybe conflating "the meaning for the group" with "the physical occurence"? — but i don't want to just dismiss the politics in (2). which step do you actually reject, and what's the price? also please seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and don't present a contested view about memory politics as if it's the consensus.
