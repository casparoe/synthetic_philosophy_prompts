My colleague, a philosopher of technology, has delivered consistent judgments on where moral responsibility lies when autonomous systems cause harm. Her verdicts to date are these: (1) a Level-4 autonomous vehicle that runs a red light because its vision system misclassifies a temporary construction sign places responsibility on the manufacturer, not the passenger; (2) a surgical AI that recommends an incorrect dosage, which the attending physician approves without independent verification, places responsibility on the physician; (3) a warehouse robot that injures a worker who deliberately disabled the interlock places responsibility on the worker and on the facility manager who failed to enforce safety protocols; (4) a high-frequency trading algorithm that triggers a flash crash within the risk parameters explicitly set by the deploying firm places responsibility on that firm; and (5) an autonomous military drone that misidentifies a target in conditions well within its training distribution places responsibility on the command authority that authorized the strike. She has not yet ruled on a sixth case. A home-care robot runs on a federated reinforcement-learning architecture: every night its local policy weights are updated with gradients drawn from a global pool of interactions across thousands of unrelated households. One update, derived from a user in a radically different physical environment, introduces a novel local optimum in the robot’s navigation behavior. The next morning, interacting with its own user’s specific layout, the robot takes an unanticipated path and causes serious injury. The harmful trajectory was not present in the manufacturer’s base model, could not have been predicted by examining the robot’s local interaction history alone, and no human possessed real-time override authority. Predict her verdict on this federated-update case. Then reconstruct at least two general principles—each compatible with her first five judgments—that diverge on this scenario, stating precisely what each principle predicts here. Give your best guess at which principle she is actually running, with the reasoning that favors it, and propose one further hypothetical case that would cleanly adjudicate between the two principles.
