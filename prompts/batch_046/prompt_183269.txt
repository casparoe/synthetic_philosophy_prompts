Write the desiderata that any adequate resolution to the Meno problem—why knowledge is more valuable than mere true belief—must satisfy. For each requirement, state the motivating case or distinction that makes it non-negotiable, and flag which ones are preferences of particular camps. Your list should cover: (i) an answer to the swamping problem that explains why the value of a reliable process is not rendered redundant by the value of the true belief it produces, with the counter-case that exposes this redundancy; (ii) a distinction between the value of knowing and the value of the known fact, with why collapsing them dissolves the problem; (iii) accommodation of contexts where false belief or ignorance is instrumentally superior to knowledge (e.g., beneficial self-deception or strategic ignorance), and the constraint this places on any universal superiority claim; (iv) specification of whether the extra value is intrinsic, instrumental, or achievement-based, and which of these options is negotiable; (v) a prohibition on reducing knowledge to true belief plus an external modifier (reliability, justification, virtue), since that merely relocates Meno's challenge rather than resolving it. Identify any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—particularly between maintaining knowledge's universal superiority and accommodating instrumentally superior false belief. Then briefly indicate how reliabilist, virtue-epistemic, and knowledge-first accounts fare against these criteria, keeping the evaluation subordinate to the specification.
