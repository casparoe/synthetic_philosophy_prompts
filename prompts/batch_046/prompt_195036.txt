ok so here's roughly where i've landed on the hole argument and i need a consistency check before i present this at our phil-phys reading group. i'm a phd student in philosophy of physics, fwiw.

(1) i'm a manifold substantavialist — the points in the spacetime manifold are real entities, not just mathematical bookkeeping.
(2) i think Einstein's hole argument is basically correct: hold initial data fixed on a Cauchy surace, apply an active diffeomorphism that's identity outside a region, and you get infinitely many metrics inside the hole with the same initial conditions, so determinism fails unless you do something.
(3) i want to save determinism without becoming a relationalist, so i think we should treat those active diffeomorphisms as gauge symmetries — all the hole-related metrics represent one physical state.
(4) but i also think gauge transformations need a substrate to act on. if there are no real points being permuted, then calling it "gauge" collapses into just saying the fields are identical, which feels like relationalism in disguise. so i think gauge invariance actually presupposes substantivalism rather than undermining it.
(5) and i think determinism is restored globally, not just locally, because once you quotient by the gauge orbit there's a unique evolution for the equivalence class even inside the hole.

do these hang together? i'm especially worried about (3) and (4) pulling opposite directions — using gauge to save determinism usually sounds like "points don't matter," but i'm saying points have to matter for gauge to be meaningful. there's also (2) saying determinism fails up to gauge, while (5) says it's fully restored globally; is that a contradiction or just sloppy wording on my part? and is there a hidden tension between (1) and (3), since if point p gets metric g and point p' gets g' under the diffeomorphism, and p and p' are both real, don't we seem to have two different physical situations?

please rank the tensions by severity, say what has to give in each case, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most mess. also, is there an apparent contradiction here that actually dissolves once you distinguish two different senses of determinism? i won't be satisfied with a bare "yes it's consistent" — i want the audit.
