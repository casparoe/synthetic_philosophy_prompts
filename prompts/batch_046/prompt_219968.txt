ok so i've been thinking about the something-rather-than-nothing thing and want to check if my views are actually consistent. here's roughly where i stand:

1. i think the question is legit, not just a language trick like some people say.
2. i think there's a necessary being — not god, just bare necessity — that explains why there's anything at all.
3. i think this being has no mind, no purpose, nothing personal; it's more like the fact that 2+2=4 except that it exists.
4. i think the physical world is totally contingent; it could easily not have existed, and their's no reason it has these particular laws.
5. but i also think that "absolute nothing" — not even this bare being — is metaphysically impossible. so there was never a real possibility of total non-existence.
6. i also reject the principle of sufficent reason for contingent stuff; some things just have no explanation.
7. and i think existence isn't a predicate, so the ontological argument for god fails, but that doesn't apply to my bare-necessity thing because i'm not treating "exists" as a property there.

do these hang together? specifically, does (4) clash with (5) in a way that makes (2) redundant? does (3) sit weird with the idea that this being "explains" anything if it's just a logical fact? and is there tension between (6) and (1) — if i reject PSR everywhere, why do i think the necessary being gives an explanation (or needs one)? also i worry (7) undermines (2) if i push it. rank the tensions by severity and say what'd have to give in each case.
