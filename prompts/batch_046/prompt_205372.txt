I am developing an essay on the dynamics inside Hofstadter's careenium—specifically, whether symmballs causally push the neural substrate around or are merely pushed by it. Rather than choosing one side, I want to defend an intermediate position I call "symbolic recoil." The core claim is that when symmball clusters reach a threshold of internal coherence—say, during sustained abstract reasoning—they do not exert direct downward causation on individual neurons. Instead, they temporarily restructure the substrate's attractor landscape, so that neural dynamics become slaved to the symbolic trajectory in a transient, one-way fashion. Once the symbolic activity decays, the substrate rebounds with a characteristic lag, which I take to be the signature of recoil rather than continuous interaction. I plan to use a fluid-vortex analogy: the vortex pattern does not push each water molecule, yet the molecules' local dynamics are reorganized around it, and dissipation produces a rebound effect. I then want to apply this to two puzzles: first, how people can maintain contradictory beliefs at different levels of abstraction without generating constant neural conflict; second, why formal symbolic manipulation—like running through a proof—can feel phenomenologically disembodied despite being fully implemented in the substrate.

Please comment on the coherence of this argument. (2) Does the vortex analogy help, or does it obscure the temporal and causal specifics of neural-symbolic interaction? (3) Does my application to contradictory beliefs survive scrutiny, or does it reduce to ordinary cognitive compartmentalization? (4) Is there a serious objection I have overlooked—perhaps concerning the mismatch between the millisecond scale of neural dynamics and the longer timescale of symbolic reasoning? (5) Should I explicitly contrast this framework with predictive-processing models, or is integration more promising? Please write your response in American English.
