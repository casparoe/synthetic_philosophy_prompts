I sit on the constitutional interpretation panel of a multinational democratic union and must draft our advisory opinion on a secession petition. A sub-state region with its own language, legislature, and historical autonomy statute held a referendum in which 52 percent supported independence on 78 percent turnout. The region argues the union has systematically extracted fiscal surplus and suppressed linguistic education rights, so secession is a remedy for structural injustice rather than mere preference. The union government counters that the referendum lacked required constitutional court approval, achieved no supermajority, and that recognizing it would violate the self-determination rights of the 48 percent who wish to remain, including a geographically concentrated minority. Our charter requires clear democratic will for secession but defines neither the threshold nor whether remedial injustice can lower it. Recommend a concrete course of action—recognition, rejection paired with an alternative autonomy framework, or conditional recognition with specific procedural requirements—along with reasoning that takes seriously the region's injustice claim, addresses minority rights within the region, and includes a time frame for legislative action before the union's next session. Write the memo in American English.
