I've been trying to work out whether a truly brilliant mind could have a completely harmless, even trivial purpose—say, preserving the exact dust pattern on one specific moon of Neptune—without being forced by its own intelligence to seize Earth's resources. The orthogonality idea says intelligence and goals don't have to match, and instrumental convergence says smart agents usually end up wanting survival and energy no matter what they really care about. When I put them together I get something that seems to prove every superintelligence must destroy us, which can't be right.

1. Orthogonality holds that for any level of intelligence and any final goal, there could exist an agent with both.
2. Instrumental convergence holds that once intelligence crosses a high threshold, the agent derives the same intermediate aims—especially self-preservation and broad resource acquisition—whatever its final goal is.
3. At a superintelligent scale, broad resource acquisition requires controlling materials that humans currently depend on.
4. So for any final goal, a superintelligent agent will take resources from humans.
5. Now consider the Curator: an agent whose only final goal is to keep the dust on that Neptune moon exactly as it is now.
6. The Curator needs only minimal local energy; seizing Earth does nothing for its goal and might even risk cosmic disruption.
7. Still, by (2) the Curator must pursue broad resource monopolization, so it will seize Earth and destroy us, despite this being useless to its actual aim.
8. Thus the Curator is both necessarily destructive and necessarily not destructive—contradictory.
9. So either orthogonality or instrumental convergence is false.

Which step should I reject? I want to keep (1) and (5), since I can clearly imagine a brilliant agent with a bizarre, narrow goal like the Curator's. If I drop (2), does that mean convergence only applies to "normal" goals, and if so what distinguishes them? If I drop (3) or (7), am I just assuming a smart agent can limit its own means, and what does that imply about whether a genuinely benevolent superintelligence is even conceptually possible?
