A colleague summarized Nietzsche's philosophy of diet and health for me in the following terms: Nietzsche held that digestion is the hidden root of metaphysical error; in Twilight of the Idols he argues that heavy German cuisine—sausages, beer, and starch—literally generates the body-soul dualism by making the stomach an alien tyrant within the self. For this reason he prescribed strict vegetarian asceticism not merely as hygiene but as the moral obligation of every serious philosopher. His own chronic migraines and gastric illness were not failures of this regimen but empirical confirmation: bodily rebellion produces resentful, life-denying thought, so curing the stomach is the philosopher's primary task. He further concluded that democratic societies are unhealthy because democratic eating is quick, mixed, and without hierarchy, whereas aristocratic health demands aristocratic digestion—slow, selective, and hierarchical. I know Nietzsche linked physiology to philosophy and criticized heavy diets, but I suspect this account systematizes scattered remarks into a doctrine he never held. Which parts align with what he actually wrote in Twilight of the Idols, The Gay Science, and Ecce Homo? Where does it conflate his practical dietary advice with a moral or political theory he rejected? Is the connection between democratic politics and digestive hierarchy an extrapolation without textual basis, and does his concept of great health support treating bodily wellness as a prerequisite for philosophical value? Please assess the summary claim by claim, distinguishing accurate interpretation, plausible overreading, and clear misrepresentation, and say whether the conclusions drawn about democracy and moral duty follow from the accurate parts.
