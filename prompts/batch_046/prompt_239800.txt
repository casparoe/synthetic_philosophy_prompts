Draft a 60-minute essay examination question for an upper-level undergraduate module in moral philosophy, together with a model answer and a marking scheme. The topic is P.F. Strawson’s analysis of reactive attitudes and moral responsibility, but the question must be constructed around the following injected conceptual device: a hypothetical Administrative Objectivity Protocol in which interpersonal reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, indignation — are systematically replaced by algorithmically calibrated social-stability metrics that assign precise numerical weights to behavioural outcomes, entirely without emotional or participatory engagement.

The exam question (to be answered in roughly 1,000 words) should be designed so that it cannot be answered by mere recitation of Strawson’s distinction between the participant and objective stances. Instead, it should require candidates to assess two specific issues: first, whether Strawson’s defence of reactive attitudes as natural human reactions can coherently resist absorption into the Protocol’s optimised metrics without presupposing a form of moral realism he elsewhere rejects; and second, whether the Protocol’s elimination of participatory engagement actually achieves the kind of detached objectivity Strawson associates with the clinical or therapeutic stance, or whether it introduces a novel, bureaucratic form of the participant stance disguised as neutrality.

Provide:
(i) the precise wording of the exam question, including any necessary framing of the Protocol;
(ii) a model answer (approximately 800–900 words) that demonstrates the specific argumentative moves required for a first-class response, explicitly addressing both issues above;
(iii) a marking scheme totalling 30 marks, broken into at least five identifiable criteria, each with its allocated marks and a one-line note on the characteristic mistake that should cause a deduction.

The question should be answerable by a well-prepared student who understands Strawson’s core framework but should separate those who grasp the underlying conceptual tension from those who have only memorised standard labels. Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not invoke obscure secondary debates or specialised texts that lie outside mainstream philosophical education; derive the difficulty from the interaction between Strawson’s familiar account and the injected Protocol scenario. Write in British English, with formal academic tone.
