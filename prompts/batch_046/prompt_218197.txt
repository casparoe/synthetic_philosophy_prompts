Carroll's dialogue about the tortoise and Achilles exposes a regress in justifying inference rules: if applying modus ponens requires a further premise licensing it, justification never terminates. Among live responses — logical pluralism, pragmatic or evolutionary justification, inferentialist semantics, and appeals to reflective equilibrium — disagreement persists over whether the regress is fatal, benign, or based on a category mistake. Suppose you advise a small philosophy-of-logic group with three years, conceptual tools only, and no formal-logic or empirical lab. Which two or three open questions or directions should they prioritize, and in what order? Defend your agenda with explicit criteria: which questions are upstream of others, which are tractable without technical machinery, and what result in the next few years would reorder the list. In particular, should they begin by examining whether the tortoise treats structural rules (e.g., weakening, contraction) differently from operational rules (e.g., conjunction-introduction), since that distinction might dissolve the regress for some frameworks but deepen it for others? Or should they instead ask whether different logical frameworks — classical, relevant, paraconsistent — each face structurally distinct versions of the challenge? I want a defended ranking with clear trade-offs, not a survey. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
