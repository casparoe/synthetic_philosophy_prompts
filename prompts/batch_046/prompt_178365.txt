I'm writing to ask for help thinking through a practical dilemma that has started to feel like it touches on something deeper about trust and rationality. I'm not a philosophy student—I'm a screenwriter—but I recently sat in on a friend’s lecture about strategic reasoning, and ever since I've been haunted by a puzzle in my own work.

For the past eighteen months, my closest collaborator and I have been developing a feature script through an unusual back-and-forth process. Every six weeks, one of us produces a revised draft and sends it to the other. The recipient can either continue refining it and return it—knowing that the script improves and our potential share of credit and compensation grows with each cycle—or finalize it immediately and submit it to an independent producer who has promised to read the finished version. The catch is that whoever submits claims the primary authorship and receives the larger portion of any eventual deal. We've now completed eighteen rounds, and the draft is genuinely stronger than either of us could have produced alone. Yet the strategic logic my friend described seems unavoidable: since we both know that the other is rational and self-interested, and since each round offers a larger reward than the last, shouldn't we both anticipate that eventually one of us will defect? In fact, shouldn't that anticipation make defection rational right now, before the other person can act? The reasoning appears to unwind all the way back to the very first exchange—meaning we should never have begun collaborating at all. But we did begin, and we've persisted, and something about that persistence feels like it undermines the logic rather than ignoring it.

What complicates this further is that our collaboration isn't isolated. We belong to a small community of writers who share work and vouch for one another's integrity, so a betrayal wouldn't be a private event; it would reshape my reputation and, I assume, my friend's, in ways that extend well beyond this single script. At the same time, I recently learned—though I haven't confirmed whether he knows I know—that my partner once submitted an earlier, unrelated project without telling his co-author. That private piece of information breaks something about the neat symmetry of our reasoning, but I can't tell whether it should make me more cautious or whether it's irrelevant because the underlying logic remains the same. I don't want generic advice about communication or contracts; I want to understand whether there's a genuine flaw in the chain of reasoning that pulls everything back to the start, or whether trust is just a kind of useful irrationality that holds the structure together despite the logic. If you had to advise someone in my position—someone who genuinely believes the script is better because of the collaboration but also can't shake the feeling that rationality is pulling in the opposite direction—how would you frame the choice?
