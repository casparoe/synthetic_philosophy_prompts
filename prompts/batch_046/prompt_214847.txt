I'm trying to map my actual intuitions about survival in Parfit-style cases and see whether they hang together. Here are my verdicts.

Case 2 – The branch line: the scan is sent to both Mars and Venus; both are rebuilt and wake up with my psychology. Verdict: neither is strictly me. I haven't survived; instead there are two replicas. Survival requires uniqueness, not just psychological continuity.

Case 3 – Gradual synthetic replacement: over twenty years my neurons are replaced one by one with functionally identical artificial ones. Verdict: I survive throughout; by the end I'm still me, even though no original neurons remain.

Case 4 – In-place copy: my biological brain is destroyed and immediately replaced in the same skull by a synthetic brain running an exact copy of my psychology. Verdict: I survive. The body is the same, but even if it weren't, the causal chain and psychological match would be enough.

Case 5 – The stutter machine: every thirty seconds for ten minutes I'm destroyed and rebuilt in the exact same spot with perfect psychological continuity. Verdict: I'm continuously present; it's not a sequence of twenty deaths and rebirths.

Case 6 – The digital upload: my psychology is perfectly transferred to a running computer program; my biological body is destroyed. Verdict: I do not survive. Despite perfect psychological continuity, there's no biological embodiment, and that absence breaks survival.

Case 7 – The long drift: over decades my psychology is gradually edited—values reversed, memories altered—through continuous causal links. By the end I'm psychologically very different, but there's an unbroken chain. Verdict: that's still me, though barely; continuity of causal history outweighs similarity of content.

Case 8 – The snap replacement: at one instant my psychology is replaced with a copy of my twin's psychology inside my unchanged body. Verdict: that's not me. The causal origin is wrong, and the content match is with the wrong life.
