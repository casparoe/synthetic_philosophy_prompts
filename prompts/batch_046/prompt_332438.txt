In my philosophy of mind reading group we've been stuck for months on conceivability as a guide to possibility -- specifically the zombie case. One camp says if I can clearly conceive a phyiscal duplicate with no consciousness, physicalism is false. The other camp says that's just an epistemic gap, not a metaphysical one. I think we've stalled because neither side specifies whether 'conceive' means phenomenally imagining being that duplicate, or just propostional describing a world where the physical facts hold and consciousness is absent. So is the disagreement actually about the psychology of modal judgement versus the structure of modal space? Or is there a deeper hidden disagreement about whether a priori reasoning can ever establish metaphysical possibility? Diagnose it: are we answering different questions under one heading, or is this a bedrock clash about the authority of reason? And what would count as progress here -- a sharper distinction, a formal result, or just accepting that the question is ill-posed?
