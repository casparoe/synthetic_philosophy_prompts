I've been arguing with friends on a rationalist forum about whether religious societies are genuinely "more moral" or merely differently organized, and I realized that every conversation collapses into correlational sociology—charity statistics, crime rates—without ever mapping the conceptual space. For an informal philosophy-of-religion seminar handout, I need a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on whether religion improves moral outcomes, but built from logical branches rather than from named philosophers or empirical studies. The tree should split on three axes that usually get tangled together: (1) what "more moral" denotes—dispositional virtue, reliable prosocial behavior under stress, or institutional resilience against free-riding; (2) whether any putative advantage is causal, selection-based, or structurally ecological; and (3) whether secular societies can sustain equivalent trust without replicating specific functional features of religious communities, such as costly signaling dynamics, cosmic framing of norms, or ritual synchronization.

Start the tree with a concrete diagnostic case rather than a label: imagine two societies, one secular-humanist and one with an organized religious tradition, both facing a severe collective-action crisis like pandemic lockdown or resource rationing. Each branch node should pose a claim that genuinely separates views, phrased so someone can answer without knowing which interpretation it points to—for example, "Does the transcendence or naturalness of a belief affect its moral efficacy, or only its behavioral output?" and "Can secular analogues replicate binding force without functionally mimicking religious form?" Every leaf should carry either the name of an actual position or "unnamed," plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment—such as a view that accepts secular sustainability but requires that secular institutions covertly reproduce costly religious signaling. Please flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, especially views that agree on secular sustainability but diverge on whether that sustainability requires functional mimicry, and note any unoccupied combinations in the logical space with a brief diagnosis of whether they seem incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Avoid bibliographies, citations, or appeals to specific empirical literature; this should be answerable from conceptual reasoning alone.
