I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about distributive justice and luck, mostly because I was arguing with my roommate last night and we kept going in circles. Here are my gut verdicts on concrete cases: if you're born with a severe disability that makes working impossible, society should definatly cover your basic needs — that's pure brute luck, not your fault. If you chain-smoke for thirty years and then get lung cancer, I don't think everyone else should subsidize your care — that's option luck, you made the choice. But then there's this harder pair. Someone is born with a genetic profile that makes them deeply risk-averse; they take a safe, low-paying job, stay poor despite working hard, and I feel like they deserve an income supplement, because the preference that locked them into poverty wasn't chosen. Now flip it: someone is born with the opposite genetic profile — highly impulsive and risk-seeking — they start a business, it collapses, and they're bankrupt. I lean toward saying "tough luck, that's on you," even though the impulsivity is just as genetic as the risk-aversion in the other case. There's also a kid born into poverty with perfect pitch but zero access to instruments or lessons; I think society owes them something for that lost potential, though I'm unsure if it's compensation for the poverty or specifically for the wasted talent. And one more: a teenager bombarded with ads develops an expensive taste for luxury goods, then can't afford them as an adult — I feel like no compensation is owed, but I can't quite explain why that's different from the risk-averse worker.
