I'm attempting to achieve reflective equilibrium on belief revision under uncertain evidence, and I'd like to know whether my intuitions cohere around a defensible principle or whether some must be sacrificed for simplicity. Here are my verdicts, which I take to be mostly sensible but not obviously consistent. First, when I observe a shape through heavy fog and my perceptual system assigns roughly 60 per cent tree, 30 per cent shrub and 10 per cent statue, I feel I should redistribute my credences smoothly across these options rather than adopting a point estimate; this strikes me as a clear case for Jeffrey conditionalisation on an uncertain observation. Second, when a weather model I regard as imperfect predicts rain with 80 per cent confidence, I update my belief about precipitation to somewhere near that figure, adjusted for my prior—again, Jeffrey-style conditioning on a probabilistic report feels correct. Third, when a colleague I trust about 75 per cent tells me a meeting is either at 2 p.m. or 3 p.m. but is unsure which, I update so that my credences over the two times stand in the ratio she implies, scaled by my confidence in her reliability; direct conditioning on her reported distribution seems right. But fourth, contrast this with weighing myself on a scale I have just learned is miscalibrated by an unknown factor within ±5 kg, where I read 82 kg: here my intuition rebels against Jeffrey-updating my weight belief around 82 kg with some spread. Instead, I want to expand the partition and update jointly over (true weight, calibration error). The scale reading does not constrain my weight credence in the same direct way the weather report does. Fifth, with a medical test of known sensitivity and specificity I apply standard Bayes with the likelihood ratios without hesitation, which suggests I am happy with direct probabilistic updating when the error properties are fully specified.

The tension is sharpest between the third and fourth cases: both involve an evidential source of unknown or partially unknown reliability, yet in one I apply Jeffrey conditioning directly to the reported distribution and in the other I insist on joint updating over the mechanism itself. Is there a unified principle—perhaps concerning whether the uncertainty is epistemic versus aleatoric, whether the evidential channel is inside or outside the model, or whether the partition is rigid—that delivers all five verdicts? If not, which should I overturn? Please also state precisely what your principle predicts for a case I have not listed: a prediction market prices outcome X at 70 per cent, but I suspect manipulation by an unknown number of bad actors whose influence on the price is unspecified. Finally, be explicit about which of my verdicts the principle cannot accommodate, and whether saving them all requires an ad hoc complication.

If my framing conflates Jeffrey conditionalisation with noisy Bayesian updating, or if the distinction I am grasping for is incoherent without further formal machinery, please say so plainly rather than accommodating me politely. I am looking for pushback if the task rests on a confused premise or if there is no coherent principle behind these judgments.
