He set up a relational schema that, inside the system, proves there exist uncountably many entries — he mapped the rows so that no internal query can list them all, even though from the outside we can see every row has a finite index. He says thats fine, the database's own logic is what counts, and if it cant enumerate them then for all practicle purposes theyre uncountable. I think thats like claiming you have a billion dollars because your accounting software uses big numbers, when really its just a trick with labels. Is there a real philosophical glitch in his argument, or am I just being pedantic?
