Write a poem structured as a synthetic geometric demonstration—definitions, construction, proof, and Q.E.D.—treating Thomas Hobbes's conviction that the science of politics must possess the demonstrative certainty of Euclidean geometry. Divide the poem into four successive parts: four lines that fix the primitive terms (human matter, motion as desire, covenant as the drawn line); six lines that construct the sovereign figure from these elements rather than discovering it in nature; six lines that derive obligations with the step-by-step inevitability of a proof; and a final couplet that states the Q.E.D. and immediately breaks the analogy by observing that geometric figures are static while the commonwealth is made of men who move and change. Let the progression from definition to conclusion mirror Hobbes's own synthetic method, and let the closing turn expose the hidden fracture: sovereignty may be drawn like a circle, yet it cannot be squared into permanence. The references should be precise enough that someone who knows Hobbes's admiration for synthetic geometry, his dispute with analytic methods, and his failed attempt to square the circle can recognize them, but do not quote any text verbatim and do not ask for citations or sources.
