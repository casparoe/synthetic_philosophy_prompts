After a long and unresolved argument about sphexishness, mechanical rigidity, and free will with a close friend last night, I want to put a sharper parallel exchange to you for adjudication. The disagreement I am recounting took place between two colleagues of mine—neither of whom is me—who were debating whether the digger-wasp loop (the stereotyped burrow-check that restarts endlessly if the prey is moved) tells us anything meaningful about human freedom. Here is how their exchange went.

One colleague began by arguing that sphexishness is essentially the antithesis of free will: the wasp repeats its inspection cycle because it cannot break out of a fixed behavioral loop when conditions change, and free will requires precisely that capacity for loop-breaking revision. The other replied that this mistakes a sensory-motor glitch for an absence of agency; human freedom concerns authorship and reasons-responsiveness, not error-free flexibility in every sub-routine, so the wasp's rigidity proves nothing about us.

The first then introduced what she called "normative lock-in," a second-order sphexishness: imagine a tax accountant who updates every spreadsheet when the code changes—perfect internal flexibility—yet never questions whether she should be in accounting at all, even after learning her firm facilitates serious harm. She claimed this is the real danger to freedom: not repeating actions, but protecting the framework that generates them from any revision. The second responded that this is merely strong vocational identity or commitment; if she genuinely values precision and service, then leaving would be alienation, not liberation, and flexibility without reason is just caprice.

The first pushed back with "preemptive sphexishness": the accountant's cognitive architecture does not merely prefer accounting; it filters out counter-reasons before they reach conscious deliberation, so she never genuinely encounters challenges to her framework. That insulation is rigidity disguised as conviction. The second countered that this is just motivated reasoning or self-deception—psychologically real, but conceptually distinct from sphexishness, which is total insensitivity to reasons. Humans can be selectively responsive without being loop-bound like wasps; conflating selective filtering with fixed action patterns collapses the distinction that makes agency interesting.

Finally, the first accused the second of making free will unfalsifiable: by redescribing any human stability as "hidden reasons-responsiveness," no evidence could ever demonstrate rigidity. The second replied that this is not unfalsifiability but recognition that human agency operates at the level of reasons, not reflex arcs; the burden is on the first to show that the accountant's filtering is structurally identical to the wasp's loop, not merely functionally similar in producing stable behavior. Without that structural proof, she said, the argument analogizes by effect rather than mechanism.
