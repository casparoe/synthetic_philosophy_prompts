Here's an argument I skecthed for a metaphysics reading group and now I'm suspicious of it because the conclusion feels like a reductio but I can't find the leak.

(1) If x grounds y, then x is metaphysically prior to y and y's existence depends on x, not vice versa.

(2) So grounding is irreflexive: nothing grounds itself, not even indirectly through a loop.

(3) Call a fact "base-level" if it isn't grounded in anything else.

(4) Suppose there's a complete set B of all base-level facts — the full fundamentality profile of reality.

(5) Now consider the claim BASE(B): that B is exactly the complete set of base-level facts.

(6) BASE(B) is either base-level itself, or it's grounded in something.

(7) If BASE(B) is base-level, then B contains a fact about B's own completeness. But then B depends on itself, since being in B means being independant, yet BASE(B) being in B makes B's completeness depend on a member of B, which is circular.

(8) So BASE(B) must be grounded. Say it's grounded in some member b of B.

(9) But b is a particular fact like "electron e has charge -1" or "region R is occupied". How could that particular fact ground the global claim that no other base-level facts exist outside B?

(10) More generally, any particular member of B is about some specific entity, not about the absence of other entities. So no single b, and probably no combination of b's, can ground the completeness claim without smuggling in the very global information it's supposed to ground.

(11) Therefore BASE(B) is ungrounded, violating the principle that every non-base-level fact is grounded.

(12) So either there's no complete B (fundamentality has no closure), or grounding isn't irreflexive, or the principle that all non-fundamental facts have grounds is false.

(13) But giving up (12) seems to collapse the whole grounding/fundementality distinction.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? I'd rather keep (1) and (4) than (13), but I want to know what I'm paying if I reject (7) or (8) instead. Please answer in American English and don't just list books — I need the actual reasoning.
