I have been trying to work out whether dreaming counts as a real experience that happens while I am asleep, or whether it is only a story my brain assembles once I wake up. I understand there is a serious disagreement about this—some hold that there is no genuine experiencing going on in sleep because nothing can be observed or reported in the moment, while others say the feeling and memory are real enough to qualify as experience, even if the narrative gets filled in afterward. I would like a joke that turns precisely on this distinction, set in an unexpected everyday situation: perhaps someone filing an insurance claim for emotional distress caused by a nightmare, only to be told by a bureaucrat that no compensable event occurred because there was no lived-through episode, only a reconstructed memory upon waking. Please do not name philosophers or cite texts; let the humor come from the conceptual tension itself. The joke should make clear that you grasp the difference between an episode lived through in real time and a narrative reconstructed after the fact, but it should remain accessible to someone with no philosophical training.
