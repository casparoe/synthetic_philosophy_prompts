The correlation breaks the independence assumption that makes defection appear dominant.

Alex replied that correlation is merely an empirical fact about execution, not a normative reason to change your payoff-maximizing choice. He said treating logical correlation as a reason to cooperate is like saying two identical calculators should both display the wrong answer because they share the same bug—your local optimization shouldn't be distorted by the knowledge that someone else is running the same code.

Blake countered that in a one-shot interaction with no repetition and no external enforcement, the only path to the cooperative payoff is through this kind of logical coordination. He compared it to a smart contract that releases funds only when both parties sign with derivations from the same private-key logic: if you alter your own reasoning, you alter the other party's outcome too, so rationality requires accounting for that interdependence.
