My opening move, and it's a bit in the weeds: everyone frames this as Germany hides, America shares, China scores. But I think that's wrong. What's actually going on is three different architechtures for error-correction in social reputation. In Germany citizens delegate correction to courts and data-protection authorities — you can sue to fix your record. In the US we delegate to market intermediaries (credit bureaus, background-check firms) with weak state oversight. In China the state keeps the correction mechanism in-house via social-credit scoring. So isn't your beloved model just one flavor of delegated reputation management? If the underlying structure is the same — some authority tracks you and offers appeals — then defending German privacy is just defending the court-based appeal architechture over the algorithmic one.
