I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background. A colleague applies Tolstoy's criteria for art to specific works, and her track record is: a folk lullaby sung by a mother counts as art; a technically brilliant but emotionally cold symphony does not; a novel that stirs moral pity for the oppressed yet requires scholarly interpretation to understand is borderline; a religious icon that moves uneducated viewers without explanation counts; a gallery installation that needs a wall text to produce any feeling does not. Now consider a procedural video-game environment that algorithmically generates landscapes in real time from a player's biometric stress signals, built by an engineering team with no single authorial intent, yet reliably produces in players a shared, genuinely moving feeling of sublime isolation. If she wants to stay consistent, should she classify this as art? Which underlying rule—contagion of feeling, accessibility without interpretive mediation, moral sincerity of the creator, or communal reception—best explains her earlier verdicts, where exactly do those rules diverge on this new case, and what single further example would best tell them apart?
