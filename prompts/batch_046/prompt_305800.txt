You serve on a formal audit board for automated reasoning specifications. Your board receives modal-logic formalisms that claim to represent a "necessarily existing maximally perfect being"—drawn from philosophy of religion, formal ontology design, or theological logic—and must decide whether they may be admitted into a reasoning engine without hidden inconsistencies. You want a reusable audit procedure, grounded in Gödel's modal ontological argument (the framework in which properties are classified as positive or not, God-likeness is defined as possessing all positive properties, necessary existence is treated as positive, and a being with all positive properties is shown to exist in every possible world), that your auditors can apply without theological expertise.

Write the procedure as an ordered checklist with explicit stopping conditions. For each step, specify: the exact test to run on the formal specification; what result counts as a pass; what result triggers an immediate rejection; and what result must be escalated to human philosophical judgment because the issue is one of values or interpretation rather than formal fact. The procedure must specifically detect: (i) whether the property classification allows a property and its negation to be jointly positive; (ii) whether the modal framework permits an unintended collapse from possibility to necessity outside the intended derivation; and (iii) whether quantification over properties is world-bound or illicitly ranges across worlds.

Then apply the procedure to two submitted specifications:

Case A. A minimal formalization that includes the axioms that every positive property is necessarily positive and that necessary existence is positive, but omits the axiom that a property is positive if and only if its negation is not positive. Run the full audit and report the outcome at each step.

Case B. A specification that survives all technical checks but defines perfection as the conjunction of omnipotence, omniscience, and self-identity-with-every-perfect-being, yielding exactly one necessary entity that absorbs all properties into itself. Run the audit and state the final recommendation, including whether this is an answer the board will find unwelcome and why.

Finally, specify the procedure's limits: which kinds of formalization it cannot adjudicate, where auditor commitment inevitably enters, and what documentation must be preserved for appeals.

Please state, in your response, how confident you are in the claims made about the procedure's reliability, and single out the specific step about which you are least certain—especially regarding whether that step genuinely distinguishes benign modal derivation from destructive modal collapse without tacitly assuming the conclusion it is meant to test.
