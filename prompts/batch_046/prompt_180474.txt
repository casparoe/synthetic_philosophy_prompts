As a graduate student in a historiography seminar focusing on the ethics of anachronism, I am working on a creative exercise in sustained deadpan satire. Please write a parody of an internal university administrative policy document—approximately 650 words, formatted with numbered sections, subheadings, and bureaucratic prose—issued by a fictional “Committee on Temporal Moral Standards.” The document should propose a formal “Retroactive Ethical Accreditation Protocol” that applies current moral and conceptual frameworks to evaluate all past university activities, complete with invented bureaucratic mechanisms such as a “Presentism Index,” mandatory “Anachronism Impact Assessments” for archivists, and a tiered “Historical Redaction” system for honorary degrees and building names. The target should be the practice of presentist judgment in historiography: treating historical actors as if they shared our conceptual categories, assuming linear moral progress can be quantified institutionally, and proceeding with ethical evaluation without first reconstructing the normative framework of the period in question. The voice must remain entirely earnest and procedural; the absurdity should emerge solely from the rigorous internal consistency of the protocol, not from caricatured villains or winking asides. Every clause should follow logically from the premise that historians have an institutional duty to apply contemporary norms retroactively, so that a specialist reader recognizes the real objections—category mistakes about past reasoning, the hidden politics of selecting which present norms count, the erosion of historical understanding—without them being stated explicitly. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific scholarly texts that require external verification; keep the argument fully self-contained within the fictional document. The piece should be accessible to someone with no background in philosophy of history, yet precise enough that a specialist finds the satire conceptually fair and recognizably rooted in actual debates about whether historians can ever escape the standards of their own time.
