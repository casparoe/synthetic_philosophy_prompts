I am working through a seminar on inductive reasoning and have been trying to reconcile an old argument about custom with a particular variant of Goodman's new riddle that my study group invented. In a well-known passage from David Hume's Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding, he argues that reason alone can never establish that the future will resemble the past; instead, we form expectations through custom or habit, which is produced when we repeatedly observe constant conjunctions between events. Because every past instance of fire producing heat gives us only a psychological propensity to expect heat from future fire, and because there is no logical contradiction in nature changing its course, Hume concludes that our confidence in induction is not rationally compelled but is rather the unavoidable result of our mental constitution.

My confusion arises when I try to apply this psychological account to a modified predicate problem that does not involve colours at all. Suppose we define a predicate "uniform" that applies to any natural law observed so far—say, the inverse-square law—if that law continues to hold indefinitely. Now define "uniform*" such that it applies to the very same law for all observations made up to the present, but specifies that the law will suffer exactly one precise failure in the year 2150 and then resume. For every piece of evidence available to us now, "uniform" and "uniform*" are perfectly co-extensive. Given Hume's claim that custom operates solely on observed constant conjunctions, I cannot see how habit could push me toward projecting "uniform" rather than "uniform*", since both have been equally constant in experience. Furthermore, I wonder whether anchoring the anomaly to a specific calendar date—2150—makes this case structurally different from Goodman's "grue" with its arbitrary temporal cutoff, or whether the calendar merely dresses up the same logical problem with a false air of physical specificity.

Could you help me understand whether Hume's appeal to custom offers any non-circular basis for preferring the simpler projection, and whether the fixed-date failure point introduces a genuinely distinct difficulty for inductive reasoning? Please explain where, if anywhere, my framing goes wrong, and answer in British English.
