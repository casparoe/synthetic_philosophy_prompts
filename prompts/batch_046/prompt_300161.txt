I am drafting an essay for a graduate seminar in comparative metaphysics, and I would welcome your critical comments on the argument and its framing. My central claim is that the Jain doctrine of anekāntavāda—specifically the saptabhaṅgī, or seven-fold predication, and the associated theory of naya (partial standpoints)—is usually interpreted as an epistemic thesis about human cognitive limitation, but it is better read as an ontology of partial determination: complex objects genuinely possess incompatible properties that are each revealed from different, irreducible standpoints. To test this interpretation, I apply it to a problem in the philosophy of scientific modeling that I think remains undertheorized: the status of ensemble pluralism in epidemiological forecasting, where different SEIR model variants—carrying incompatible assumptions about latency distributions and asymptomatic transmission rates—are not treated as approximations converging on a single true model, but are retained simultaneously because each captures a genuinely different causal structure in the population. I argue that treating these models as naya, each expressing a partial truth about an ontologically complex epidemiological reality, avoids both the reductionism of "one true model" views and the relativism of "all models are merely useful fictions." However, I worry that this analogy may smuggle in a metaphysics of multiplicity that classical Jain thinkers, who typically restrict anekāntavāda to linguistic and epistemic domains, would reject. I also worry that the essay has not yet addressed whether the seven modes of predication can be mapped onto the logical structure of model disagreement without serious distortion.

Please respond in no more than 250 words. Begin with a single-sentence bottom-line verdict on whether the argument is coherent and interesting, then briefly identify which element—the ontological reading of anekāntavāda, the epidemiological analogy, or the anti-relativist move—needs the most work, and suggest one specific revision that would strengthen the essay.
