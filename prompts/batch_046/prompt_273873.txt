Consider Thomson’s lamp and the broader family of supertask paradoxes: an infinite sequence of discrete changes—toggling, accelerating, computing—completed within a finite interval, leaving the state at the limit point apparently undefined. Rather than asking for a bibliography, evaluate the following five proposed resolution strategies, each framed as a general constraint on what physical systems may do near a limit point.

1. The Continuity Requirement: any macroscopic change must be mediated by a continuous transition over a nonzero duration; discrete jumps are permitted only at isolated instants with well-defined left and right limits.
2. The Information-Finitude Requirement: no step in an infinite sequence may depend on computing or storing information that exceeds a finite capacity bound for the region containing the system (for example, a bound derived from black-hole thermodynamics).
3. The Causal-Closure Requirement: the state at the limit time must be fully determined by states arbitrarily close to, but not including, the limit, via a well-defined limiting law; if forward evolution yields no unique limit, the supertask is physically impossible.
4. The Retrocausal-Selection Requirement: if forward dynamics leave the limit state undefined, then consistency conditions running backward from the limit—treating the limit state as a boundary condition—uniquely determine which prior sequence was actual.
5. The Symmetry-Breaking Requirement: infinite oscillation is physically permissible only if the system possesses a hidden symmetry that is spontaneously broken exactly at the limit point, thereby selecting a single definite state without continuous mediation.

Rank these five strategies from 1 (the most defensible as a necessary constraint on physical possibility) to 5 (the least defensible). For each, state in one or two sentences whether it resolves Thomson’s lamp, dissolves the paradox by denying that the setup can occur, or leaves the paradox intact, and say briefly whether it preserves determinism for ordinary finite processes. Do not invoke specific named theories from the literature unless genuinely required; assess the strategies on internal coherence and on whether they quietly rule out benign infinite processes that we might want to allow.
