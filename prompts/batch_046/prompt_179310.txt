Me and my roommate got into it about whether generations like Boomers, Millennials, and Gen Z are actual real kinds or just made-up marketing buckets. He said they're obviously not real kinds cause the cutoffs are arbitary -- you could slice every 12 years and get different groups, and there's no shared essence that makes a Millennial a Millennial. I pushed back that generations are social kinds shaped by shared formative events, like coming of age during the 2008 crash or growing up with smartphones always in your pocket, and those shared conditions actually cause measurable differences in risk tolerance and political socialization. He said that's just age effects in disguise: of course people in their 20s take more risks than people in their 40s, that's development, not cohort. I mentioned that cohort studies try to disentagle age, period, and cohort effects, and some differences persist after controlling for age. He admitted that but said statistical differences don't automatically make something a kind in the philosophical sense, since kinds need to ground inductive projections, and knowing someone is Gen Z doesn't let you predict anything specific about them the way knowing something is copper predicts conductivity. I said that might be true at the individual level but generations work more like functional kinds -- think currency or institution -- where membership is defined by causal role and shared history, not by a hidden essence. He said comparing people to currencies is weird and ignores that generational labels are mostly used for stereotyping and targeted ads, not scientific explanation, so treating them as kinds gives false legitimacy to marketing categories.
