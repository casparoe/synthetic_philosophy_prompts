Retired physicist here, been poking around that cross-cultural intuition literature since I left the lab. There's a pattern I can't name. In some East Asian and West African samples, people don't just answer differently on harm-vs-help dilemmas; they treat seperating intent from outcome as itself kind of malformd, like asking what color Tuesday is. They don't reject the hypothetical -- they reject the dimensional split inside it. Western subjects usually take the split for granted and just fight over weights. I know about holistic vs analytic thinking, and about cultures that refuse hypotheticals altogether, but this feels narrower: it's a meta-objection to analytic decomposition within the case, not to the case itself. Does this specific move have a name? If not, can you coin one and draw the boundary with holistic cognition and with plain disagreement? I want the carving, not just a label.
