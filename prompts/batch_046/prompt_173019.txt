Next Thursday I present to my department's philosophy of language and mind workshop a defense of what I call architectural dispositionalism about Kripkenstein's rule-following paradox. My claim is that the paradox arises only if we assume that rule-following must be grounded either in an infinite mental catalog of correct applications (the straight solution) or in communal normative practices (the skeptical solution). I propose instead that having meant addition by 'plus' is a property of a specific representational architecture—specifically, the algorithmic compressibility of the arithmetic-generalization procedure implemented in my cognitive system—so that there is a determinate fact about my past intentions, but one that is architecture-relative rather than normatively constituted or dispositionally infinite. The audience will include three analytic philosophers committed to Brandom's normative pragmatics and Sellarsian social inferentialism, who treat normativity as irreducibly deontic and socially mediated; one philosopher of cognitive science who accepts mechanistic explanation but is suspicious of personal-level normative talk being reduced to implementation details; and two graduate students working on interpretability in neural networks who will ask whether 'architectural compressibility' is anything more than a redescription of learned weights. I need a speaker's prep sheet: list the eight or so objections I will actually face, ordered by likelihood and damage, each phrased in the vocabulary those specific interlocutors would use (deontic scorekeeping, inferential articulation, mechanistic levels, representational content, training dynamics). For each, provide a concise reply suitable for a two-minute defense, note the weakest point in that reply, indicate what I should concede in my opening remarks, and identify the single question I am hoping nobody asks—specifically, the one that exposes the empirical emptiness of my appeal to 'compressibility constraints' without requiring me to specify which neural architectures realize them. Keep the framing philosophical rather than technical, and do not include bibliographic references or citation requests.
