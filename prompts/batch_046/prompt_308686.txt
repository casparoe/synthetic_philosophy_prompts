I’m a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I keep running into this weird loop in the memory debates. You know the basic tension Margalit draws — a duty to remember victims — versus Rieff’s pushback that forced remembrance can turn toxic? Well, I’ve noticed a pattern that doesn’t sit cleanly with either side. Institutions build these sleek, minimalist memorials — polished stone, no names, just an abstract form — and the official line is "this is so we never forget." But the actual visitor experiance is closer to aesthetic contemplation; people take selfies, feel a kind of calm. Meanwhile, the "forgetting" camp tears down statues or renames buildings, but the demolition is always hyper-documentated, live-streamed, turned into spectacle. So the erasure generates way more circulation of the original image than leaving it up would have. Both moves claim to solve the other problem, yet both produce the same hollow presence: the event stays technically alive in culture, stripped of the moral demand Margalit cares about, and also not actually erased the way Rieff might want. Is there an established term for this specific configuation, where remembrance and forgetting become structurally indistinguishable in practice? Or if there’s no name, can you carve out exactly what it is, what it isn’t — say, how it differs from plain commodification of trauma or standard memory politics — and maybe suggest a label with boundary cases? I don’t want a bare name; I want the edges.
