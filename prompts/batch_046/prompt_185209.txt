Okay so I've been tracking how people deploy "Western values" in arguments and my gut verdicts are all over the place but there's def a pattern. Case 1: a politician says Western values include free speech and then cites Voltaire as an example. I feel like that's mostly coherent, even if Europe had censorship. Case 2: someone lists "Western values" as individualism, secularism, and the scientific method, but then includes Japan in "the West" for comparison. That feels like cheating to me — the category is being stretched to fit the argument. Case 3: a forum post says Western values are just whatever liberal democracies happen to do right now, so criticizing them is circular. I think that's incoherent as a definition, but I also feel weirdly defensive about it? Case 4: someone contrasts Western values with "Asian values" and lists family loyalty vs personal autonomy. Here I think the contrast works, but only if we treat both as loose family-resemblance clusters, not essential lists. Case 5: a rationalist blogger I like uses "Western values" to mean specifically the post-Enlightenment package of falsifiability norms plus moral universalism, and applies it to critique tribalism. That feels precise but also like thier smuggling in thier own preferences. So what's going on? Is there a principle for when "Western values" is doing real argumentative work vs just being a tribal signifier? And which of my verdicts do I have to drop to get a clean account? Also what does the principle say about the case where someone claims the West invented human rights but then admits the concept has roots in Islamic jurisprudence — is that a contradiction or just messy history? I keep going back and forth on whether case 3 is salvageable.
