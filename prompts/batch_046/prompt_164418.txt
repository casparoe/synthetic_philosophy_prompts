I'm writing a feature on the revival of Confucian role ethics in contemporary moral philosophy, and I recently came across a widely circulated syllabus summary—attributed to no particular author—that frames the position like this. Confucian ethics, it claims, is essentially a variant of virtue ethics: the five relationships supply a social curriculum through which agents cultivate internal dispositions such as ren and yi, but the moral labor is performed entirely by the agent's character, not by the roles themselves. The roles are therefore instrumental scaffolding; they help shape virtue but do not constitute moral identity. Because of this, the summary argues, Confucian role ethics collapses into a form of Aristotelianism with culturally specific training exercises, and critics are correct that it is essentially conservative—since if roles are only instruments for personal virtue, there is no philosophical reason to preserve specific hierarchical structures once they cease to be useful for character development.

The summary sounds plausible, yet I have heard specialists insist that this instrumental reading misses something central—specifically, a relational ontology in which moral persons are not pre-formed substrates entering relationships but are partly constituted by asymmetrical, non-substitutable roles such as father-son, and that filial obligation is not a transferable virtue but a mode of address that only makes sense within that particular relation. Before I file my piece, I need an accuracy audit. Please evaluate whether the summary is fair: identify what is accurate, what is a standard simplification, and what is a subtle misrepresentation—particularly regarding whether roles are instrumental or constitutive, whether the view genuinely collapses into Aristotelian virtue ethics, and whether the charge of conservatism follows from the corrected view. In your assessment, please separate what is broadly settled in the specialist literature from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus view.
