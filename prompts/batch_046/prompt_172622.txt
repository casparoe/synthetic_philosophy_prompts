I was chatting with a friend who took a metaphysics seminar last semester, and they summed up Bradley's regress like this. They said that if you claim a relation holds between two things—like a horse being white—then you need a further relation to connect that first relation to the horse, and another to connect it to whiteness, and it never stops. From this my friend said Bradley concluded that relations can't actually exist in reality at all, they're just contradictions we fall into when we think analyitcally. To solve the problem of how propositions can still be true despite this, Bradley supposedly argued that a proposition is unified because the subject and predicate are fused in one immediate mental image, not linked by anything external. So the unity of the proposition is basically a psychologcal fact about how we picture things, and that's why Bradley thought reality it's self is just one undifferentiated Absolute with no real parts or distinctions—because if there were parts, they'd need relations to hold them together, and we've already shown relations are impossibe. Is this a fair reading? I feel like there's something off about turning the unity of the proposition into a psychological claim, and I'm also not sure whether Bradley meant relations are strictly impossible or just that they don't apply to ultimate reality. And does his view really commit him to saying all distinctions are fictional, or is that my friend overreading the Absolute?
