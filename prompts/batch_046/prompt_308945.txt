for a 90-min grad seminar with about 12 students, design a runnable activity on presentism in historiography — specifically whether historians can ever apply past standards without smuggling in modern ones. invent a fictional 17th-century guild whose internal ethics reward "ritual forgetting": destroying records earns moral credit, and authority grows with what you've erased, so the norms are coherent but totally alien. split the group into archivists, judges, and guild defenders. round one: they vote on whether an elder was justified in burning census records, but they can only use the guild's own norms. round two: same case, same vote, but now they must apply modern archival ethics, without being allowed to name presentism yet. ask for explicit rules, a timeline, roles with small stakes, and a debrief that maps exactly where people sneaked in modern values, predicts the usual failure mode (everyone claims they stayed neutral in round one but actually didnt), and is frank about which philosophical feature — maybe the hermeneutic problem of understanding alien norms at all — the game cant reproduce and the facilitator must supply. keep it casual, no lecture up front, reveal the concept only after round two, and bake the niche guild idea in so it doesnt feel like generic roleplay.
