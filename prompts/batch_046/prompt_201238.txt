I am a third-year philosophy student currently enrolled in an upper-level seminar on philosophy of psychiatry and social science. I am drafting a mock examination for revision purposes and would like you to construct a single essay question, together with a model answer and a marking scheme.

The topic should be Ian Hacking's account of looping effects and interactive kinds, but I want the question to avoid the standard textbook illustrations (e.g. multiple personality disorder or childhood hyperactivity) and instead apply the framework to a deliberately constructed, philosophically loaded scenario: a proposed psychiatric category called 'chronic digital dissociation', characterised by attentional fragmentation and identity diffusion that are partly constituted by patients' awareness of diagnostic criteria circulated through algorithmic screening tools and peer discourse. The question should ask candidates to assess whether the presence of strong looping effects in this case undermines psychiatric realism about the category, or whether the feedback loop can be accommodated within a realist account that treats classification as part of the causal mechanism it describes.

Please provide:
1. One essay question suitable for a 90-minute examination for advanced undergraduates (around 800 words expected in the answer). The question must require students to do more than summarise Hacking; it should force them to distinguish metaphysical objections from epistemic ones, to apply the interactive/indifferent kind distinction precisely, and to evaluate whether looping necessarily destabilises a category or can instead stabilise it.
2. A model answer of approximately 800 words, written in British English, that addresses the specific scenario rather than offering a generic exposition of looping effects.
3. A marking scheme built around five criteria (each worth 4 marks, total 20 marks). For each criterion, specify the specific conceptual move a top answer must make (for example: correct identification of why digital dissociation counts as an interactive kind; clear separation of the claim that classification shapes behaviour from the claim that it thereby creates an artefact; evaluation of whether the loop undermines reality or is part of the causal structure). Also include, for each criterion, a one-line note on the typical mistake that should cost marks.

Please avoid requesting bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations that depend on access to particular texts. The answer should be meaningful to someone who knows Hacking's general framework but has not encountered this specific hypothetical category before. Write everything in British English.
