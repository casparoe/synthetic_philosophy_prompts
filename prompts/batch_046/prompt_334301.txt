Write a joke that turns on the internal logic of expected-value fanaticism—specifically, its willingness to trade near-certain destruction of a unique good for an arbitrarily small chance at unbounded payoff—rather than relying on generic probability tropes. Frame the joke around this invented scenario: a philosopher must choose between (A) retaining her sole, irreplaceable manuscript, or (B) pressing a button that destroys it with probability 1 − ε but, with the remaining ε, instantiates a transfinite archive containing every well-formed variant of the manuscript across all possible worlds. The punchline should hinge on the fanatic selecting B because the expected cardinality of copies exceeds one, and on an interlocutor objecting that dominance reasoning exists precisely to block this kind of calculus from consuming irreplaceable goods. Keep it concise, genuinely funny to someone who understands basic decision theory, and free of bibliographic references or obscure named doctrines.
