I sit on the funding review panel for the Mid-Atlantic Science Museum's new Origins Wing. The curaters want every display card to read like this is the true account of human emergence — they call it represenationalist labeling. The eduction staff wants cards that say this is one useful vocabulary among others for coping with our past, which is basically straight Rorty antirepresentationalism. Our charter actually has a clause banning dogmatic metaphysical assertions in public exhibits, but the state grant requires us to present evolutionary biology as the correct scientific narrative. I need a memo recommending which framing we approve, with conditions if any. The memo needs to tackle whether treating science as a vocabulary rather than a mirror of nature violates our funding contract, and it needs to honestly address the strongest worry from the other side — that antirepresentationalist labels either confuse visitors or betray the museum's duty to scientific accuracy. Write it like a real board doc: bottom line up front, reasoning, fair treatment of the losing argument. Don't just regurgitate standard neopragmatist talking points; think through how vocabulary-choice actually works on an exhibit wall.
