In the butterfly dream passage, he demonstrates that the boundary between waking and dreaming is unstable, which the author takes to imply that the line between life and death is equally porous. From this, one can infer that death is not a distinct state but literally a form of waking up: the individual does not cease to exist but merely shifts form, like a sleeper changing position. Because Zhuangzi treats the self as a temporary configuration of qi rather than a permanent substance, there is allegedly no metaphysically real subject that dies—only qi dispersing and re-forming elsewhere. Consequently, Confucian mourning rituals are conceptually incoherent on his account, since they presuppose the loss of a stable self that never existed in the first place. The author concludes that Zhuangzi’s position amounts to a kind of metaphysical solipsism: if there is never a self to live or die, then there is no one to suffer loss, and all emotional attachment is based on an illusion.
