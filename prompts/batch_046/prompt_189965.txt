Here is the half-formed idea: the standard literature treats finks—interferers that remove a disposition precisely as it is about to manifest—and masks or antidotes—interferers that block manifestation while leaving the underlying base intact—as exhaustive categories for how dispositions can be thwarted. I suspect this binary collapses for what I will call distributed compound dispositions: dispositions that depend on the coordinated functioning of two or more independent subsystems, each with its own dispositional profile, where one subsystem can be finked and another masked simultaneously. Is there a coherent way to say whether the plant’s compound disposition is present, absent, finked, masked, or only partially so? And does the answer depend on whether compound dispositions are reducible to the dispositions of their parts, or whether they require an irreducible joint-manifestation condition?
