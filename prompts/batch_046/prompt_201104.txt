Next thursady I'm presenting my seminar paper to the ethics grad workshp—small room, mostly people who know negative utilitarianism mainly from extinction-style arguments. My thesis is this weird hybrid I'm calling saturation-bound asymmetry: suffering has a kind of moral magnetism that happiness just doesn't have, because suffering undermines agency in a way pleasure doesn't mirror. But that magnetism saturates—once you're below catastrophic intensity, further suffering-reduction doesn't stay on the same scale as producing happiness, they're structurally incommensurable. So I avoid the extinction conclusion without saying a galaxy of happy people outweighs one torture victim. But I also avoid collapsing into regular prioritarianism because I'm not just weighting suffering more—I'm saying the comparison breaks.

Audience: three classical utilitarians who think asymmetry is just emotional confusion, two Kantians who think grounding urgency in felt experience already abandons dignity, one population-ethics person who's going to ask about repugnant-conclusion-style problems and person-affecting restrictions, and two agnostics who mainly want to know if this is just prioritarianism with extra steps.

Sketch of my argument is: (1) suffering relates to agency-undermining in an asymmetrical way; (2) this creates lexical-style urgency but only up to a saturation point; (3) below that point, happiness and suffering don't trade on a shared metric.
