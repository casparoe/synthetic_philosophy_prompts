But here's the twist: banks still have to hold capital against all of them equally, as if each narrative carried the same weight regardless of plausability. What philosophical commitments about probability and uncertainty does that pairing actually presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit epistemology — does it need Keynes's non-measurable uncertainty, or can a Bayesian with flat priors justify equal capital charges? Is the practice philosophically consistent, or does it smuggle in a probabilistic assumption through the back door of capital allocation? And tell me what would have to change if we replaced the framework with one that demanded frequentist point estimates for every tail risk: would the capital rules collapse, or could they survive with different reasoning? I want to know if the institution is genuinely committed to one view or just using Keynesian language to cover a practical mess.
