I am a novelist revising a philosophical dialogue for a forthcoming work of fiction, and I wish to be certain that the argument delivered by one of my characters — an economist turned moral philosopher, speaking at a Cambridge dinner party — is genuinely vulnerable rather than merely theatrical. The scene turns on pure time preference, and I have drafted the following chain for her to advance.

(1) If pure time preference is rational, then a rational agent may discount future utility by any constant factor per unit of time.
(2) Choose any positive discount rate r > 0. Then the present value of a utility U experienced n years hence is U multiplied by e raised to the power of negative r n.
(3) For n large enough — say, the welfare of people living in the thirty-first century — this present value is arbitrarily close to zero, regardless of the magnitude of U.
(4) Therefore, on any consistent exponential model of pure time preference, the suffering or flourishing of distant generations carries negligible weight in present deliberation.
(5) But moral intuition, and indeed every serious argument in climate ethics, treats the welfare of distant generations as carrying substantial weight.
(6) So either pure time preference is not rational — in which case all saving, investment, and long-term planning are irrational — or it entails a morally intolerable neglect of the future.
(7) Hence pure time preference must be rejected entirely, and with it the entire framework of discounted utility maximisation over time.

The conclusion strikes me as intolerably sweeping; I intend her interlocutor to dismantle it with precision, not with mere sentiment. I cannot tell whether the error lies in step (3), in the inference from (4) to (5), or in a hidden equivocation between individual and social discount rates. Which premise ought she to reject, and what does that concession cost? I should prefer, if it is defensible, to preserve the rationality of long-term planning without licensing the neglect of distant generations. Could you identify the weak point and price the alternatives?
