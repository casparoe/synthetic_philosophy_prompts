I've been arguing with my roommate about whether democratic backsliding is actually a useful analytic category or just a fancy label liberals slap on goverments they dislike. We kept looking at Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the US and going in circles because we never agreed on what the concept even needs to do in the first place. So before I dive into more literature, I want the full spec: what does any adequate theory or framework of democratic backsliding actually have to get right?

It needs to cover the hard cases—when an elected administration uses perfectly legal mechanisms to pack courts, restrict media access, or weaken oversight bodies, but does it with genuine popular backing and without open violence, like the judicial reforms in Poland or the centralization moves in Hungary. At the same time it has to tell that apart from ordinary constitutional hardball, routine federalism fights in the US, or just losing an election you care about. There's also the measurement problem: if backsliding is tracked by expert indices like V-Dem or Freedom House, the framework needs to explain why those scores shouldn't be written off as elite bias, and whether the concept can still work if the people experiencing the erosion actually support the leaders causing it. I also think it has to handle retroactive legitimation—where norms are broken first and then the rules are rewritten to cover the break, particularily in cases like the Turkish constitutional restructuring after 2016—without treating every constitutional amendment as collapse.

But I'm particularily worried that some of these demands pull against each other. A framework thick enough to condemn court-packing might depend on normative commitments that don't travel well across presidential, parliamentary, and hybrid systems, which makes it hard to apply consistently to the US, Poland, and Turkey in one breath. Meanwhile, treating very different institutional pathways—say, executive aggrandizement versus legislative capture—as instances of the same phenomenon might force you to ignore differences that matter for remedy. Write this out as continuous prose with no headings, no bullet points, no bold, and no tables—just like an email. And please don't give me a bibliography or name-drop specific scholars; I want to work through the criteria on my own.
