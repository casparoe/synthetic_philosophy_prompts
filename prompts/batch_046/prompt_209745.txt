ok so I'm trying to work out my stance on Bradley's regress and the whole propositional unity mess for a paper I'm sketching, and I want to check if my commitments actually cohere or if I'm just smuggling in fixes without noticing. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) Bradley's regress is genuinely vicious, but only if you assume every relation is an entity that needs binding to its relata in exactly the same way the relata are bound to each other. So I reject that assumption. (2) Still, I think relations are real—not just logical constructions—and that they have a kind of unsaturatedness that lets them bind without being bound themselves. I borrow the language informally from Frege, but I reject his actual technical apparatus: no sense/reference machinery, no Gedanken split. (3) Turning to mental representation, I think propositional unity—say, the thought that Socrates is wise—is Bradley's problem in psychological dress. I picture mental files like database entries with foreign keys: the file for Socrates carries a pointer that gets filled by the property-file for wisdom, and that pointer-filling is all the glue there is. (4) At the same time, I want propositions to be genuinely structured: they have proper parts, the mental-file representations for the object and property, that exist independantly in the mind. The proposition isn't just a mental blob. (5) But I also think the unity of the proposition is primitive and not further analyzable—there's no hidden mental syntax or binding relation that explains it. (6) Finally, I think Russell's strategy of making relations internal just relocates the regress rather than solving it, because then you still need to explain what distinguishes an internal relation from an external one without using the very distinction you're trying to ground.

So do these hang together? I feel like (3) and (5) are pulling in opposite directions—one gives an analysis in terms of pointer-filling, the other says it's unanalyzable—but maybe I'm wrong and they're compatible if I frame unsaturatedness right? And is there a hidden tension between (2) and (4), since if relations/concepts are unsaturated entities that don't need binding, why do I need independant mental-file parts that then require unification? Or is that only an apparent conflict? Also, does (6) undercut (1) in some way I can't see? I want you to audit this: rank the tensions by severity, say for each whether it's fatal or cheap to fix and what the revision costs, and tell me which single change—dropping or tweaking one commitment—cleans up the most mess. Don't treat them all as equally bad; I need to know which are real and which are phantom. And please don't just lecture me on Bradley in general—stick to whether my package works.
