I serve on the Student Wellness Allocation Committee of a public university system. We must decide whether to renew or sunset the Universal Basic Wellness Grant, which gives every enrolled student a $500 mental-health services voucher regardless of need. A prioritarian proposal urges sunsetting the grant and pooling its budget into an Emergency Severity Fund reserved for students in acute crisis; proponents argue that resources must be weighted toward the worst-off. Critics reply that the Emergency Fund is already fully capitalized, so the sunset would eliminate benefits for moderately well-off students without improving anyone's condition—replicating the leveling-down structure that prioritarianism was designed to avoid. Draft the committee’s decision memo recommending approval, rejection, or approval with conditions. Include an actionable bottom line, reasoning that treats the prioritarian weighting argument seriously, an honest account of the strongest leveling-down objection on the losing side, and language that both the administration and student-government representatives could actually respond to.
