Please rank the following four accounts of artistic value—expressive individualism, formal autonomy, institutional conferral, and what I will call delayed-infection—according to how severely Leo Tolstoy's infection theory of art undermines each one, specifically when both the account and Tolstoy's criteria are applied to works of procedural or distributed authorship in which the artist's originating emotional state is either absent, reconstructed by a system, or dispersed across multiple agents. By expressive individualism I mean the view that art's essential function is the direct externalization of a singular conscious feeling; by formal autonomy I mean the view that artistic value resides entirely in internal relations of form, color, or structure without reference to any psychological origin; by institutional conferral I mean the view that an object becomes art solely through the recognition practices of an artworld or institutional framework; and by delayed-infection I mean the view that art operates as a stored emotional protocol that can activate in later or culturally distant audiences a feeling structurally analogous to, but causally discontinuous with, whatever state the originator may have held. For each account, assign it a place on a continuum from fully compatible with Tolstoy's demands for sincerity, clarity, and successful emotional transmission to fundamentally irreconcilable with those demands, and present your evaluation in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, ordering your paragraphs from the account that survives best to the one that survives worst. In each paragraph, explain specifically how procedural or distributed authorship interacts with the sincerity, clarity, and infectiousness conditions that Tolstoy requires, and note where your judgment depends on an interpretive choice rather than on the logical structure of the account itself. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; rely only on the conceptual architecture of Tolstoy's position and on the logical relations among the four accounts.
