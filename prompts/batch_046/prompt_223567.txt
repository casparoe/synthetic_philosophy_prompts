I'm on the editorial board for a small rationalist-adjacent journal and we recieve too many drafts that treat WEIRD moral intuitions like they're the factory settings for every human. I need a reusable decision procedure -- ordered questions, not vibes -- that my co-editors can run to catch what I'm calling the canonical-form error: treating one culture's specific packaging of a value (say, explicit contract-style consent) as if it were the value itself, then reading absence of that packaging elsewhere as absence of the value. The steps should seperate what's empirically supported by the cross-cultural literature from where we have to make a judgment call about whether the underlying concern is shared under different clothing. Apply it to two hypothetical submissions: one saying merit-based distribution is fairness full stop, another saying autonomy requires formal opt-in rituals. Include one result where the procedure clears an argument despite my suspicion, and flag exactly where the checklist hands off to a human because it's values, not facts. No citation lists, just the logic.
