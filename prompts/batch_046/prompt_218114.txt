I'm trying to pin down whether "avoid side effects" for a low-impact AI can be defined without smuggling in a full specification of human values. Here are my gut verdicts on concrete cases, and I think they may be pulling in incompatible directions.

An AI deploys micro-robots to rearrange sand in the Sahara into a perfect circle one kilometer across; no human observes it, local ecology is unchanged, and the mechanism itself vanishes afterward. Verdict: unacceptable side effect.

The same AI computes the first ten billion digits of pi inside an isolated server, then powers the server down permanently; the result never influences any decision or observation. Verdict: acceptable, not a meaningful side effect.

It redirects a single photon that was going to strike a rock on the Moon, altering its path by one millimeter with zero macroscopic consequence. Verdict: unacceptable.

It writes an original philosophical argument, encrypts it, and stores it in an archive that will be destroyed unread in fifty years. Verdict: acceptable.

It prevents exactly one bacterium in an unused petri dish from replicating; the dish is destined for autoclaving regardless. Verdict: unacceptable.

It calculates and executes a small trajectory correction for an untracked asteroid, preventing a collision with Earth in ten millennia. Verdict: acceptable, despite being a large-scale physical intervention with lasting causal consequences.

I'm drawn to distinguishing "counterfactual footprint" (how far the world-line diverges from the baseline) from "causal footprint" (energy or matter actually moved), but that doesn't resolve the split: the bacterium and asteroid interventions both have cosmically tiny footprints, yet I treat them oppositely, while the sand circle and the pi computation both leave physical traces, yet I split them too. Is there a coherent principle—perhaps involving whether the change is "value-laden" in a way I haven't formalized—that accommodates all six? If not, which verdict should I overturn? And what should the resulting account predict for an AI that silently corrects a single transcription error in a genomic database that no researcher will ever query?
