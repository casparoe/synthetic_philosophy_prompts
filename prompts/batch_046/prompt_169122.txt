Please argue that the standard comparison of beef versus chicken on animal-welfare grounds is conceptually misframed because it measures only direct farm-animal suffering per calorie while ignoring the wild-animal mortality—particularly to small mammals and insects—induced by the crop cultivation required per unit of bioavailable protein. Defend the claim that, once feed-crop acreage, harvest-related deaths, and pasture-based forage efficiency are factored together, pasture-raised beef generates less aggregate wild-animal suffering than grain-fed chicken. In your argument, address the objection that indirect, ecologically mediated harms are not morally comparable to the direct harms of confinement, and explain why the direct/indirect distinction does not undermine the comparison even if insects have minimal or negligible moral status.
