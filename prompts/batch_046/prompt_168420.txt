hey, i sit on our city's small public-arts board — seven of us — and we're stuck on the Meridian Project. we already sunk about $380k in permits, design, and steelwork for this big outdoor piece, but the artist pivoted the concept: instead of the abstract river-form we approved, it's now a literal monument to a factory closure that half the board finds politically loaded and the other half just thinks is ugly. we've got $220k left in the grant line, and we can either finish it, pull out and salvage what we can for smaller neighborhood murals, or try some middle path.

my co-chair keeps saying we have to kill it because honoring sunk costs is definately the fallacy and we can't throw good money after bad. but there's a serious counter-argument that in institutional funding it's not so simple — taxpayers already contributed, pulling out erodes trust in our commitments, and there might be something like intertemporal fairness or institutional credibility at stake that makes "wasting" the prior investment actually costlier than finishing. i want the memo to take that side seriously, not just dismiss it.

please draft the short opinion i'll circulate before thursday's meeting. recommend one concrete course — finish with conditions, exit with salvage, or whatever — with a clear timeline (say, 30 days for final decision, 6 months to execute). engage the strongest argument for honoring the sunk cost honestly, then explain why your recommendation still holds or how it accommodates that worry. don't ask for philosopher citations or reading lists; just work with the concepts. make it something the board can actually vote on.
