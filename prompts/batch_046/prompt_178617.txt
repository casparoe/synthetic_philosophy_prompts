There's that old comparison people wheel out for soul-making theodicy -- God is like a good parent who lets her kid scrape a knee on the bike so she learns balance, not because she's cruel but because growth needs risk. The idea is that evil works the same way at scale: congenital illness, natural disasters, the lot of it, are all just tougher lessons in the same curriculum.

But critics reply that no decent parent lets a stranger's child be tortured just so her own kid learns empathy. So audit the analogy for me: give the correspondance table (parent, child, scraped knee, the bystander suffering for someone else's growth?), then say where the mapping breaks when you scale from a skinned knee to genocide. Does it quietly assume God has direct parental oversight, or can it survive if we treat the divine relation as more like a distributed foster system with limited direct intervention? And if the analogy collapses at scale, does that kill the soul-making conclusion, or just show we need a different picture?
