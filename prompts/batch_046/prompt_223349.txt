hey im on my universitys public-event funding board and we got a tricky case for thursday. A student group applied to fund a panel titled "Untranslatable Reasons: when religious conviction loses its normative force if rendered in secular terms." The proposed speakers include a theologian arguing that some divine commands are non-paraphrasable and a philosopher defending an asymmetry of translation costs — she claims secular citizens never have to rephrase their core commitments, so requiring religious citizens to do so is an unequal civic burden. The board is split. One side says funding this violates our charter clause requiring funded events to be "framed in terms accessible to all reasonable citizens," because the panel's whole point is that accessibility fails for some religious views; they think approving it undermines the clause itself. The other side says rejecting it is viewpoint discrimination — we recently funded a critical-theory workshop that argued liberal neutrality is an ideology that cant be questioned within liberal vocabulary, and nobody blinked. We need a memo recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a concrete reasoning path and an honest treatment of the best argument on the other side. Also please include 2-3 criteria we could actually use next time, since our charter mentions "equal respect for reasonable pluralism" but that phrase is basically decorative right now.
