After a long argument with a friend about whether psychiatric diagnosis can ever be objective, I keep circling back to what I think is a tension in Ian Hacking's idea of looping effects that neither of us resolved. She argued that because psychiatric classifications are interactive kinds—meaning that being labeled, say, with borderline personality disorder changes the self-conception and behavior of the person so labeled—the DSM-5-TR categories are essentially self-fulfilling constructions: they generate the very symptoms they purport to discover, which makes them unfalsifiable in the Popperian sense and therefore places them in social practice rather than natural kind taxonomy. I tried to reply that even biological kinds can be altered by human classification—think of domestication changing canine genetics—but she said that in psychiatry the loop is constitutive rather than merely causal, so the comparison fails. Now I am left with what feels like a false choice: either the looping undermines the scientific legitimacy of psychiatric categories entirely, reducing them to performative speech acts, or we must deny that the loop is genuinely constitutive and treat patients as indifferent kinds like chemical elements. Is this dichotomy itself a misunderstanding of Hacking? Specifically, can an interactive kind remain genuinely predictive and explanatory—can clinicians correct for looping without abandoning the taxonomy—without collapsing into the claim that mental disorders are just socially constructed? I want to understand whether the loop complicates realism about mental disorder or actually dissolves it.
