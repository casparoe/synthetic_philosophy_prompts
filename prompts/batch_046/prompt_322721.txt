Kant's position is that lying is always wrong, regardless of consequences, because it violates the categorical imperative by treating the listener as a mere means rather than as an end in itself. This comes through clearly in his essay on lying, where he rejects even a lie told to save an innocent person from a murderer. On the cultural side, Dutch directness and Japanese indirectness show that politeness norms are not universal.
