For a graduate seminar in philosophy of medicine with twelve participants meeting for ninety minutes, design a structured simulation that makes the group feel the force of the challenge that pain asymbolia poses to the view that pain is necessarily bad for the one who experiences it. Begin by dividing the room into pairs: one student receives a patient card describing a bodily state with either high or low reported intensity and either present negative affect, absent affect with neutral curiosity, or absent affect with an oddly positive response, while the partner acts as a triage clinician holding a strictly limited budget of analgesic doses and counseling minutes. Give the clinicians hidden allocation rules that reward reducing suffering and penalize unnecessary sedation, but do not reveal the weights until after the first round. Run two rounds in which clinicians must justify each allocation aloud in thirty seconds, then reveal scores and ask the group to notice that high-intensity-without-affect cases received almost no resources despite tissue-damage signals. In the debrief, have them map this pattern onto the distinction between nociceptive signaling and affective evaluation, and press them to articulate what they implicitly treated as the harm requiring remedy. Then introduce a hypothetical twist case of reverse asymbolia in which a patient reports no localized bodily sensation yet expresses profound dread and demands immediate intervention; ask whether they would allocate identically and what that reveals about whether badness tracks the sensory or the evaluative component. Specify roles, a minute-by-minute timeline for rounds and discussion, rules for clinician-patient interaction, a prediction of what will predictably go wrong—such as clinicians allocating based on sympathy rather than hidden rules—and how the facilitator should handle that failure. Conclude with an honest note about which feature of the philosophical debate—perhaps first-person authority over suffering or representational content without sensory substrate—the simulation cannot reproduce and must be supplied directly. Present everything as continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as in a formal letter or email.
