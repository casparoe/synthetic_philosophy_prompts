Consider the published track record of a philosopher of logic who evaluates artificial belief architectures for vulnerability to the Löbian obstacle. Her rulings on five prior designs are consistent and public. Design Alpha applies unconditional global self-trust together with explicit representation of its own consistency; she judges it vulnerable. Design Beta restricts self-trust solely to outputs of verified sub-modules, never applying it globally across the architecture; she judges it safe. Design Gamma possesses full introspective access to its outputs but is structurally blind to the rules governing its own belief-formation processes; she judges it safe. Design Delta conditions every instance of self-trust on empirical calibration against an external oracle; she judges it safe. Design Epsilon applies self-trust only to first-order object-level beliefs and never to meta-beliefs about belief formation itself; she judges it safe.

Now consider Design Zeta: it applies self-trust exclusively to predictions concerning its own future actions, not to any present beliefs; it possesses complete introspective access to its source architecture; it operates without any external oracle; yet it is structurally incapable of formulating the consistency claim “this system is consistent” within its object language.

Predict her ruling on whether Design Zeta falls to the Löbian obstacle. Identify at least two general explanatory principles that are each fully consistent with her five prior rulings yet yield opposite predictions for Zeta—specifying, for each principle, whether Zeta is vulnerable or safe, and stating the feature of Zeta that the principle treats as decisive. Give your best-guess verdict with the reasoning that favors that principle over its rival. Finally, specify the minimal further design case that would adjudicate between those two principles, explaining exactly how the two rules diverge on it.
