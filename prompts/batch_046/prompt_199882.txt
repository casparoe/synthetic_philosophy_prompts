As a journalist who covers science and ideas, I am drafting a six-month independent research proposal for a philosophy fellowship. The project asks whether Frank Ramsey’s framework—particularly his redundancy-style treatment of certain logical paradoxes and his pragmatic account of belief—can be reconstructed as a systematic dissolution of the “meaning of life” question, rather than as either an existentialist construction or a naturalist reduction to well-being or evolutionary function. The precise question: if we apply Ramsey’s distinction between logical impossibility and psychological impossibility to normative inquiry, does asking “what is the meaning of life?” turn out to confuse a request for justification with a request for causal explanation, and can this confusion be dissolved analogously to the way Ramsey dissolves self-referential paradoxes? The proposal must explain why standard positions—objective-list theories, subjective satisfaction accounts, and hybrid narrative views—leave this specific dissolution untouched, since they all assume the question is well-formed and await an answer. The method should involve close conceptual analysis of Ramsey’s published philosophical papers and surviving correspondence, analogical reconstruction of his redundancy arguments in the ethical domain, and a comparative check against parallel moves in early logical empiricism, all without requiring full archival excavation. A useful deliverable would be a concise monograph arguing that the meaning-of-life debate rests on a category error that Ramsey’s tools expose; failure would be if the analogy collapses and Ramsey’s apparatus applies only to logical paradoxes, leaving the normative domain intact. The scope should be realistic for six months of solo work, and since I will supply all references myself, the proposal should not include any bibliography or citation list. Please write the one-page proposal with the question stated sharply, the angle defended against the obvious charge that this is mere intellectual biography, and an honest assessment of what could make the project fail within the allotted time.
