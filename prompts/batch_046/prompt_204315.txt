You're a senior philosophy consultant for an international arbitration board that reviews local moral practices whenever NGOs claim they violate universal rights. The board needs a reusable decision procedure they can run in about two hours with no philosophers present except you, to tell whether a clash between a local norm and an international standard is real — meaning the local practice is objectively impermissible — or just a WEIRD framing effect that makes Western folk morality look like objective truth. Start by brainstorming the core distinctions you'll need: descriptive cultural variation vs genuine normative disagreement, what "normative compression" across cultures requires, and where moral objectivity actually lives. Then write the procedure as ordered gates with clear pass/fail conditions, hard stops where the answer is "no universal claim applies here," hard stops where it's "objectively impermissible," and explicit hand-off points where the question is values not facts so judgement can't be eliminated. Ground it in actual ideas about WEIRD psychology and moral objectivity, but do not ask for biblographies, citations, or reading lists — the board can't use those. Apply it to two worked cases: first, a gerontocratic dispute-resolution rule that looks authoritarian to Western reviewers but might survive your compression test, and second, a gendered inheritance practice that fails despite strong local justifications — make sure the second one gives an answer the board will not like. Flag the exact steps where WEIRD bias is most likely to sneak in, and say clearly what the procedure cannot do. Keep it practical: who signs off, what evidence counts, and where to stop.
