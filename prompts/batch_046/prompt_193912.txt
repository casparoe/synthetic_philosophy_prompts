A philosophy blogger summarized Kant's position on animals like this: "Kant denies animals any direct moral standing, since only rational beings with autonomy are ends in themselves. Unlike Descartes, he allows that animals feel pain and possess representational capacities, but lacking reason they remain mere means—sentient tools rather than persons. We therefore have no duties to animals themselves; cruelty is wrong solely because it blunts our sympathy toward other rational agents. From this it follows that Kant's ethics permits factory farming and animal experimentation without restriction, as long as the practitioner does not develop a cruel character. In effect, Kant simply takes Cartesian mechanism and applies it to moral status, treating animals as biological automata that happen to suffer."
