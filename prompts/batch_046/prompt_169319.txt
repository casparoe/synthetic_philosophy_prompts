The Frege–Geach problem for expressivism has been running since Geach's 1965 critique of non-cognitivism, with successive generations proposing solutions — Blackburn's quasi-realist spread of attitudes through logical compounds, Gibbard's normative analysis of 'rationality', later hybrid and 'expressive-assertive' accounts — only for the same structural objection to return: if moral sentences do not express beliefs, how do they embed in conditionals and negations with genuine logical relations? I want a diagnosis of why this debate has stalled rather than a survey of the solutions. Is the disagreement substantive — about whether there is a unified semantic value for moral sentences — or is it partly verbal, with 'expressivism' covering both a claim about psychological state and a claim about linguistic content, so that the sides talk past each other? Or is there a hidden formal question, for instance about whether dynamic or perspectival treatments of embedding contexts can satisfy Frege's point without assigning propositional content? I am working with the hypothesis that the impasse is not empirical but stems from competing standards for what counts as an adequate semantics of embedding: one side demands compositional uniformity, the other accepts context-sensitive attitude ascription. Assess that hypothesis, but be honest: if the premise is confused, or if the task as posed rests on a false dichotomy, say so plainly. I also want to know what, if anything, could actually break the deadlock — a formal result in dynamic semantics, a precise distinction between expressive force and descriptive content, or an argument that no single account should be expected — and whether progress should be expected at all. Do not give a bibliography or reading list; I am interested in the structure of the disagreement itself.
