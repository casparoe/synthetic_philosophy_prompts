I’ve been working through Quine’s scattered remarks on ethics with two members of my study group—Maya, who reads him as a pragmatist about conceptual schemes, and Jordan, who thinks epistemological frameworks are misapplied to normative domains. Their exchange went deeper than the usual secondary-literature summaries, and I want a point-by-point adjudication rather than a vague compromise.

Maya opened by arguing that Quine’s web-of-belief picture treats moral values as continuous with empirical theory: they are nodes answerable to recalcitrant experience through their effects on social coordination and human flourishing. She noted that Quine permits revision anywhere in the web, including the periphery where observation meets theory, so moral disagreement is structurally like scientific disagreement—scheme-relative but not arbitrary, since schemes face practical pressure to survive lived experience. She even claimed that ontological relativity supports this: if ontology is scheme-relative yet internally regulated by proxy functions and observational constraints, then moral ontology can be scheme-relative without collapsing into mere preference.

Jordan pushed back that Maya was importing a normative constraint Quine deliberately withholds from ethics. In Word and Object, Quine anchors stimulus-meaning in intersubjective assent and dissent conditions; moral terms, Jordan argued, lack that kind of observational control. If two cultures translate good differently, the indeterminacy of translation applies with full force—there is no deeper fact resolving whether they share a moral property or just use homophonous vocabulary. So moral commitments are not web-nodes on par with electrons; they are expressive or notational variants that do not face the tribunal of experience. Jordan added that Quine’s own remarks about values being part of the scenery, not part of the data, support this quietist reading.

Maya did not concede the quietism, but she introduced a distinction I have not seen applied to Quine in standard discussions—between proxy-function moral terms and direct-regulative moral terms. She proposed that within any stable scheme, predicates like exploitation function as proxy functions mapping onto observable labor-relations and are therefore revisable through empirical feedback, while predicates like justice function as direct regulative ideals with no observable proxy and remain underdetermined—mirroring Quine’s treatment of mathematics versus physics. Moral progress is possible in the proxy region, she said, even if the direct-regulative core is a matter of pragmatic choice.

Jordan replied that this distinction collapses under Quine’s rejection of the analytic-synthetic boundary. Without a sharp conceptual-empirical line, any moral term can be protected from revision by adjusting auxiliary beliefs, just as Duhem showed for physical theories. So Maya’s region of empirical moral revision is an illusion; the entire moral web is as underdetermined as ontology itself. He concluded that Quine is closer to a scheme-bound quietist about moral truth than to a pragmatist moral realist, and that Maya’s comparison to mathematics actually undermines her case, since mathematics is also continuous with science and yet not empirically falsifiable in isolation.

They turned to me. Maya thinks Jordan overstates the quietist reading and ignores the functional role moral commitments play in stabilizing a scheme. Jordan thinks Maya projects scientific methodology onto a domain Quine left deliberately underdetermined. Please adjudicate point by point: who is right about whether Quine’s stimulus-meaning framework applies to ethics, whether ontological relativity extends to moral ontology, and whether the proxy-function versus direct-regulative distinction can survive Quinean holism. Also say what, if anything, both are missing—perhaps about how Quine’s remarks on values actually function within the web, or about whether the mathematics comparison is apt in the way Maya intends. Do not split the difference mechanically; identify the stronger arguments and specify what each side owes.
