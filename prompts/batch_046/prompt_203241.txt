so me and a friend were arguing about epistemic modesty and I think I built an argument that if its right I should never trust my own reasoning about anything, even whether to carry an umbrella. here's the chain:

(1) if there's an outside-view base rate showing people in situation S usually botch judgment J, and I'm in S judging J, then rationality requires I defer to that base rate over my inside view.

(2) there are reliable outside-view stats for almost every domain: doctors misdiagnose at some rate, active investors underperform indexes, people overestimate driving skill, philosophers rarely converge, couples underestimate divorce risk, etc.

(3) therefore for any first-person judgment i make—career move, philosophical claim, medical symptom—I should replace my inside reasoning with the relevant base rate and suspend belief in my own conclusion.

(4) but "should I apply the outside view right now?" is itself a judgment in situation S. there's also data that people who constantly second-guess with statistics suffer decision paralysis and end up with worse life outcomes (analysis paralysis, choice overload).

(5) so by (1) I should defer to that meta-level base rate and reject applying the outside view here.

(6) yet deciding not to apply it is itself an inside-view move, which (1) forbids.

(7) the only consistent positions are total deferal (I never act, since applying the rule requires an inside-view decision about when) or total rejection of modesty.

(8) but both are absurd—i still need to decide whether to take an umbrella or sign a lease.

where does this break? is the flaw in (4) because there's no stable referance class for meta-decisions? or is (1) only meant for empirical predictions, not for singular indexical choices? i really want to keep the idea that inside-view and outside-view can genuinely come apart, so I'd rather not just reject (1) flat-out, but I need to know what I'm paying if I restrict it. also what does the repair predict for a case like deciding whether to trust my gut that my partner is trustworthy, where there's a base rate about relationship optimism but also a specific history I know about?
