We are particularly interested in the debate between the Williams thesis — that moral revolutions such as the end of slavery or duelling are driven less by moral argument than by shifts in interest and social structure — and Appiah's counter-emphasis on the transformative power of honour and reputation. Does explaining this lag require us to refine Appiah's honour-based model, or does it reveal a limitation in the Williams thesis's dismissal of argumentative forces? More broadly, if we apply these frameworks to contemporary shifts — say, the emerging norms around algorithmic transparency or climate-related professional obligations — which implications genuinely carry weight for policy design, and which are merely analogical stretches that should be set aside?
