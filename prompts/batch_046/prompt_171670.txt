Please construct a rigorous argument for the following claim: in iterated amplification and distillation protocols designed to preserve both competence and alignment in complex reasoning systems, decomposing a holistic normative judgment into simpler, amplifiable sub-tasks necessarily introduces a semantic drift in the normative valence of intermediate representations. Because each distilled sub-model is optimized for local competence rather than global normative coherence, the reconstructed judgment at the amplification endpoint may systematically diverge from the original value framework—not through standard reward hacking or distributional shift, but through the gradual erosion of contextual constraints that only the full judgment could supply. From this, derive the stronger conclusion that maintaining alignment across iterations requires an evaluator that already embodies the complete normative framework being decomposed, rendering the amplification procedure circular with respect to alignment. Your argument should explicitly distinguish this form of circularity from bootstrapping objections raised in other contexts, and should consider whether defining sub-tasks purely by formal structural features of reasoning—rather than by semantic content—can escape the drift without presupposing the very alignment being sought.
