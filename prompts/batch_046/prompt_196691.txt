I am preparing remarks for a constitutional theory seminar and want a consistency audit of my package of views on collective and individual precommitment. Here are my commitments:

(1) I hold a strict "diachronic participatory authorization" requirement for collective Ulysses contracts: a constitutional provision binds present citizens only if there is an actual chain of participatory authorization linking them to its adoption; hypothetical consent is never sufficient for collective binding.

(2) I think individual psychiatric and behavioral advance directives remain binding when present preferences conflict, because personal diachronic identity supplies the continuity that democratic collectives lack; I accept hypothetical consent only for interpreting the directive's specific content, not for justifying its initial binding force.

(3) I defend an asymmetry between negative and positive collective precommitment: a polity may legitimately bind itself not to violate certain rights (negative binding) even when diachronic authorization is imperfect, because this merely restrains future action, but it may never legitimately bind itself to maintain specific institutions, spending levels, or procedural rules (positive binding) without full participatory continuity, since the latter requires continuous active agency.

(4) I believe that sustained democratic practice—repeated constitutional amendment attempts, judicial interpretation, and civic reaffirmation—can retroactively repair broken authorization chains, but only for provisions that citizens have actively engaged with; this is why long-contested rights provisions may gain legitimacy through practice, while rarely-invoked structural rules like Article V amendment procedures cannot.

(5) I accept reflective equilibrium as my method: if my authorization requirement, combined with (3) and (4), implies that the core structural framework of most modern democracies is illegitimate, then I should revise the authorization requirement rather than accept widespread illegitimacy.

Please audit these: rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each pair whether the tension is genuine or only apparent, specify what the cheapest revision would be in each case, and tell me which single change would resolve the most tension at the lowest cost. Please answer in American English.
