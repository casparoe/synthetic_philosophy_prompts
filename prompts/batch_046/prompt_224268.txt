I'm working as a clinical ethicist for a regional pain clinic and we're trying to build a reusable decision procedre — something our intake nurses and residents can run in about fifteen minutes — for patients who show up with pain asymbolia. You know the profile: they can localize the sensation precisely, rate it 9/10, describe it as stabbing or burning, but there's basically no negative affect, no suffering, no motivation to stop it; sometimes they even call it "interesting." Our staff keep defaulting to "high number means escalate analgesia," but I think that's confused, and I want you to take the actual philosophical debate about whether pain is bad because of its representational content (it tracks tissue damage) or because of its evaluative/aversive feel and turn it into an operational checklist. The checklist needs to ask questions in a fixed order, say what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, flag exactly where the decision becomes a value judgment rather than a clinical fact, and specify who signs off — nurse, attending, or ethics committee. It should have clear stop points: one that says "do not escalate analgesia" and one that hands the case to a values review. Apply it to three concrete cases: a patient with an untreated fracture reporting severe nociceptive input but zero distress who refuses opioids; a patient with no detectable tissue damage who describes the sensation like describing wallpaper texture and asks for medication anyway; and a patient post-insular lesion who says the feeling is "neutral" but now refuses to bear weight on the affected limb. Don't just list options — take a committed position on which theory of pain's badness the procedure actually implements, defend that choice, and show where the procedure will misfire or produce an answer our team will hate (especially in that third case). Be honest about what the procedre can't do, like tell asymbolia apart from malingering or dissociation, and please don't tell me to go read philosophy papers — I need this translated for people who haven't.
