I'm attempting to work out my own reflective equilibrium on self-locating credence—cases where an observer's indexical position, rather than merely her non-indexical evidence, seems to bear on rational belief. I want a principle, or a small family of genuinely competing candidates, that captures the pattern in my verdicts below; for each candidate I want to know which clause is motivated by which case, what it predicts about scenarios I haven't listed, and which of my current judgments it must overturn to remain coherent.

Here are my gut verdicts on concrete cases:

(i) Classic Sleeping Beauty: a fair coin is flipped; if tails she is awakened once on Monday with amnesia, if heads she is awakened on Monday and Tuesday with amnesia between them. My verdict: upon being awakened, her rational credence in heads is 1/3.

(ii) The personal Doomsday Argument: I am roughly the 100-billionth human; if humanity eventually produces 10^20 people, my being this early is surprising under uniform self-location. My verdict: this gives me essentially no reason to update on total population or on near-term extinction. The observer-counting reasoning that applies to Beauty should not apply here.

(iii) The Amnesiac Clone: each night, with 50% probability, I am painlessly destroyed and replaced by two perfect copies with false continuity memories. On waking with no internal marker, my credence that the split occurred is 2/3—there are twice as many observers in the split branch.

(iv) The Server Fork: a deterministic reasoning module is instantiated at 00:00. If a fair coin lands heads, it runs on two isolated servers; if tails, on one. At noon, an instance on Server A knows the protocol but has no phenomenal "waking" experience and no memory differences between branches. My verdict: its rational credence in heads should be 1/2, not 1/3; the absence of subjective phenomenal continuity breaks the analogy to Beauty.

(v) The Centered Lab: 100 physically identical labs exist; one was randomly designated "the conscious one" and contains an observer, the rest are empty. I find myself in a lab and learn the setup. My verdict: my credence that I am in the designated lab is 1/100, treating it like ordinary conditioning on a hidden location.

(vi) The Deferred Awakening: Beauty is told Monday that the coin will be flipped Tuesday night; if heads she will be awakened Wednesday with full amnesia about Monday and Tuesday; if tails she sleeps undisturbed through Wednesday. On Wednesday, if she finds herself awake, I keep oscillating between 1/2 and 1/3 and want to know what consistency demands.

The tensions seem sharp: (i) and (iv) treat structurally isomorphic protocols differently; (ii) rejects observer-counting that (iii) embraces; (v) uses ordinary conditioning while (i) rejects it for an apparently similar hidden-location structure; and (vi) is precisely where I have no stable intuition.

Can you state precisely the principle—or two genuinely competing candidates—that best accommodates these? Finally, apply the best candidate to resolve case (vi).
