I serve on my university’s Dining Procurement and Environmental Ethics Board, and we must circulate a formal recommendation before Thursday’s meeting on a binding five-year procurement policy. The proposal before us—submitted jointly by the Office of Sustainability and a student coalition—would eliminate beef and lamb from all campus dining contracts within eighteen months, but with a single exception: vendors may continue to offer ruminant meat only if they purchase verified lifecycle carbon offsets at a 10:1 ratio relative to direct emissions and direct half the premium to our internal climate-adaptation research fund. Our direct Scope 3 food emissions represent a vanishing fraction of global agricultural methane, so the board is split between those who see the ban as causally inefficacious symbolic theater and those who see the offset mechanism as converting dining revenue into net-positive institutional funding.

Draft the short written opinion I will circulate. Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence—adopt, reject, or modify the proposal, with concrete milestones and a clear timeline—then provide the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. The reasoning must engage the strongest argument for the position you reject: that because individual diners and even our entire campus contribute negligibly to global climate harm, removing beef here does not alter atmospheric outcomes (the inefficacy objection), and that the offset exception may be the only pathway that transforms procurement spending into research capital rather than mere abstention. At the same time, address whether institutional procurement can constitute participation in collective harm despite causal dilution, and whether licensing harm through purchased offsets undermines the normative signaling value of the policy. Be explicit about what the board must sacrifice to accept your recommendation, and specify the implementation phases if adopted or the alternative mechanism if rejected.
