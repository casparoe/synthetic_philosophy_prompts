Write a short story about a city that governs itself by lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism—the idea that pleasure and pain arent just opposite numbers on one scale, but there's a hard qualitative boundary: once suffering crosses into a specific kind of harm, no quantity of joy can ever outweigh it, but below that line they calculate net welfare in ordinary units. Follow a hedonic auditor who notices a strange pattern: people are experiencing tiny, repeated pains that stay technically below the lexical threshold but accumulate into something that feels like it should trigger the rule, except the maths says they dont. The story should show, not tell—maybe through a meeting with a planner, or a personal moment where the auditor feels one of these micro-pains herself and realises the scale might be broken. Keep it realistic, no aliens or magic, and please write in British English (colour, centre, realised, etc.). I want to see whether the idea actually works when you live inside it, so let the plot hinge on that gap between the calculation and the experience. There might be a typo or two in this prompt, just roll with it.
