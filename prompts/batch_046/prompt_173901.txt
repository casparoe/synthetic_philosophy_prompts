I'm preparing a handout for an advanced seminar in philosophy of action, and I would like a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on moral responsibility in light of Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the Principle of Alternative Possibilities. Rather than organising the space by familiar authorial labels, I want the branches defined by answers to concrete diagnostic questions that students can answer without prior knowledge of the literature. The tree should open with a case in which an agent performs a wrongful action while under covert neurological monitoring: the monitor could have intervened to prevent the action but in fact never does, leaving the agent unaware of its presence. From there, the first split should ask whether the mere presence of this unactualised blocker undermines moral responsibility. If responsibility survives, the next branch should distinguish those who think it survives only because the agent's own hierarchical will endorsed the action from those who think sourcehood is preserved regardless of internal endorsement. A further branch should ask whether determinism threatens responsibility independently—through a failure of sourcehood—even when all Frankfurt-style blockers are absent and hierarchical endorsement is present. Each leaf should carry either a recognised position name or the label 'unnamed', together with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment; in particular, please include and flag the logical combination that accepts Frankfurt cases yet denies that determinism is compatible with responsibility, since that seems to require maintaining that sourcehood demands indeterminism. Where the tree allows answer-combinations that no actual philosopher occupies, please note them and briefly diagnose whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Please write the response in British English, using British spelling and punctuation conventions throughout.
