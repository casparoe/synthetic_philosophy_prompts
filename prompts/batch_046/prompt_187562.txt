I've been working through the Sleeping Beauty problem on my own. A friend summarized the debate for me like this: "Beauty is put to sleep Sunday and a fair coin is flipped. If heads, she is woken once on Monday; if tails, she is woken on Monday and again on Tuesday, with her memory erased between awakenings. Each time she wakes and is asked for her credence in heads, she should answer one-third, not one-half, because there are three equally likely awakening situations—Monday with heads, Monday with tails, and Tuesday with tails—and she has no information to distinguish them. This proves that self-locating beliefs can update probabilities without any new physical evidence about the coin. My friend added that if you apply this to quantum branching, there are infinitely many copies of Beauty in the tails worlds, so counting discrete awakenings is wrong; you should use a continuous measure over branches, which restores the one-half answer. From this he concluded that every morning when you wake up, you should rationally lower your credence that you are the 'original' you rather than a branched copy."
