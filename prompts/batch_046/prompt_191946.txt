I want you to take the central positions in the philosophical debate over forgeries and the value of original artworks—specifically, the view that originals carry an irreducible aesthetic value that perfect copies necessarily lack; the view that any difference is purely epistemic or historical and adds nothing to direct aesthetic experience; and the deflationary view that our preference for originals is a contingent market or institutional artifact with no deeper normative basis—and restate each one systematically in the vocabulary of those three axes. For each position, report precisely which axis or axes it translates onto without distortion, which axis it must distort to accommodate its claims, and whether any of its core commitments have no expression whatsoever within the framework. After the three translations, assess whether the accumulated residues indicate that the artifact-authenticity framework is missing a dimension necessary for aesthetics, or whether they instead show that the original arguments about artistic value were less coherent than they appeared because they conflated these three axes without argument. Be explicit about where the translation forces you to choose between preserving the logical structure of the original claim and preserving the conceptual integrity of the target vocabulary.
