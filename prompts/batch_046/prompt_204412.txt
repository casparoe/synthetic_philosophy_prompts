Design a scenario, presentable in one paragraph, in which a purely mechanical, multi-volume printed encyclopedia called the Codex encodes the complete state-transition graph of Einstein's brain in a distributed, holographic page layout: no local region of the text represents any discrete mental state, and the volumes must be read in a specific sequence determined by acoustic sensors driving gears and levers, yielding handwritten marginal annotations that reproduce Einstein's documented responses. Then isolate the single feature whose absence would cause all three views to converge on the same answer, explain why removal collapses the disagreement, and show how a defender of the anti-consciousness position would argue that this feature is not genuinely instantiated in your case. Keep the answer free of bibliographies, citations, and references to texts I could not access without searching.
