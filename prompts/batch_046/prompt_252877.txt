For my epistemology course on self-locating belief, I am comparing the halfer and thirder responses to the Sleeping Beauty problem, but I want to move past the standard Monday-Tuesday framing. Consider an asymmetric variant: if the coin lands Heads, Beauty receives one awakening with intact long-term memory; if Tails, she receives two awakenings, each carrying distinct, non-overlapping fragments of short-term memory such that no single awakening can verify that another occurred. Compare how each position handles her rational credence in Heads upon finding herself awake in one of these fragmentary states. Specifically, does the thirder's centered-world counting argument still hold when the observer-stages are not evidentially symmetric, and does the halfer's claim that awakening supplies no new information survive when the qualitative character of the awakening itself differs by outcome? Focus on the logic of self-locating update rather than empirical psychology, and say which view offers the more coherent account of evidential symmetry breaking in branching structures.
