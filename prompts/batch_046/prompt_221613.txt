I'm a journalist who writes about science and philosophy, and I have been asked to deliver the opening remarks at a small workshop being held in memory of a metaphysician colleague who died last year. The audience is split: roughly half are philosophers familiar with debates about identity and relational properties, and the other half are family members with no background in the field. I need a five-minute address that uses Black's symmetrical-universe scenario—the case of two qualitatively identical spheres in an otherwise empty space—not as a decorative image but as the actual logical architecture of the tribute. Specifically, the speech must demonstrate that if we take seriously the challenge this scenario raises to Leibniz's principle of the identity of indiscernibles, then any claim that she was genuinely irreplaceable cannot depend on some hidden intrinsic essence unique to her; it must depend entirely on her specific relational position within our lives and within the discipline. The address should therefore be precise enough that experts recognize the metaphysics is being handled correctly, accessible enough that non-philosophers can follow the emotional logic, and it must avoid sentimental falsehoods about an afterlife, as well as any request for citations, reading lists, or bibliographies. Please write this speech.
