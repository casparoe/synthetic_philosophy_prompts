A close colleague is enrolled in a blinded two-day clinical study with an unusual memory protocol. Before sleeping on Sunday, investigators randomly assign her to either an active compound or a placebo, with equal objective probability. If she receives the active compound, she is awakened Monday morning for an interview, then administered an amnesic agent, returned to sleep, and awakened again Tuesday morning for an identical interview with no memory of Monday. If she receives the placebo, she is awakened only on Monday for the interview and then discharged.

She understands the protocol completely. She has just now woken up in an unmarked study room. She has no external indication whether it is Monday or Tuesday, and no introspective access to whether she has already experienced this awakening before. Before calling the staff, she must decide whether to consent to an optional biomarker assay. The assay is harmless if performed once, but redundant and slightly risky if performed twice.

She reasons as follows. Under the active assignment there are two awakenings precisely like this one; under the placebo assignment there is only one. Therefore, across all possible awakenings she might currently be experiencing, two-thirds occur in the active arm. From this she infers that the objective probability she was assigned the active compound is two-thirds, and consequently that she has more likely than not already undergone the assay during a prior awakening that has been erased from her memory. She declines the assay.

I am convinced that her reasoning involves a subtle error in how the fact that she is awake right now should update her belief about the assignment and about whether this is her first or second session, but I cannot isolate the mistake without appearing to reject straightforward counting. Is her two-thirds credence in the active assignment correct? How should she distribute her belief between "this is my first and only awakening" and "this is my second awakening"? And given that distribution, what is the rational decision about the biomarker draw?
