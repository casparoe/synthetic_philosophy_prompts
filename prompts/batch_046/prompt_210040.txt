Bradley argued that if you try to explain how a proposition like "a is related to b" holds together, you have to invoke some further relation that binds "a" and "b" to "R"; but then that binding relation itself needs binding, and you get an infinite regress. From this, the summary says Bradley concluded that relations cannot be real, independent entities at all, and that the only consistent view is that reality is one continuous, undivided Absolute, with all distinctions being abstractions made by thought. It ends by saying Bradley's real point was that propositional unity can never be rationally explained; it can only be apprehended in immediate, pre-conceptual experience, and any attempt to analyze it into parts destroys it.
