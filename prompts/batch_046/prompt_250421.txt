At a workshop on historical method, two colleagues of mine argued about whether presentism in historiography can genuinely be escaped. Mira opened by defending what she called normative bracketing: when studying pre-modern scientific or ethical practices, historians should suspend modern evidentiary and moral standards and reconstruct the internal rationality of past actors on their own terms, because applying current criteria to judge past beliefs is precisely the distortion presentism warns against. Jonas replied that total bracketing is a fiction—selecting which beliefs merit explanation, deciding what counts as causation, and translating texts for modern readers all necessarily draw on modern conceptual resources; he proposed reflexive presentism instead, where historians make their modern framework explicit rather than pretending to see purely through past eyes. Mira countered that this still makes modernity the meta-language of history, effectively colonizing the past; she insisted that translation and evaluation are separable, so one can render past claims intelligible without judging them by present norms. Jonas argued the distinction collapses, since translation requires selecting analogies and judging relevance, acts that are already normatively loaded; he added that if radical contextualism were taken seriously, the resulting narratives would be so alien they would fail as history for present audiences. Mira conceded accessibility matters but claimed it is a pedagogical constraint, not an epistemic one, and accused Jonas of letting audience comfort dictate historical truth. They asked me to referee.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each main step—Mira’s bracketing claim, Jonas’s reflexive presentism, Mira’s colonization objection, Jonas’s collapse-of-distinction argument, and Mira’s pedagogical/epistemic split—from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with brief justification for each. Then say who is more right overall, and identify what both parties are missing, especially whether there is a stable middle position between pure bracketing and explicit reflexivity.
