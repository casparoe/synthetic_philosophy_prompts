Read Cervantes's Don Quixote through the problem of verisimilitude, distinguishing correspondence verisimilitude—representations that track an independent reality—from coherence verisimilitude—representations that sustain belief through internal consistency and social reinforcement. Quixote reads chivalric romances as correspondent reports, yet the novel shows these texts possess formidable coherence power: they supply predictable roles, emotional scripts, and even staged confirmations, above all in the Duke and Duchess's orchestrated adventures. Does Cervantes present Quixote's madness primarily as a failure of correspondence, or does he suggest that coherence verisimilitude becomes self-sustaining and socially dangerous when a community colludes to maintain it? How do the episodes in which Quixote is temporarily "validated" complicate the assumption that false representations are naturally corrected by reality? Conclude by saying what the novel adds to philosophical theories of verisimilitude—particularly regarding the social dynamics of belief-maintenance—that abstract accounts focused on truth-conditions tend to miss.
