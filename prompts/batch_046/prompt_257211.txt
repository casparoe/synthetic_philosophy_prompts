You advise a regulatory accreditation auditor who reviews whether member-state emergency decrees should count as binding legal norms for international compliance reviews. The auditor runs this evaluation about twice a week, has roughly twenty minutes per decree, holds no philosophy degree, and must present a sign-off recommendation to a non-philosopher director. Design a reusable decision procedure—structured as an ordered flowchart in words with sequential tests, scoring rules, and explicit judgment gates—that translates the Hart–Fuller debate into an audit tool. Specifically, operationalize Hart’s rule-of-recognition check for formal validity, Hart’s separation thesis (valid law need not be moral law), and his minimal-content conditions; and operationalize Fuller’s eight dimensions of law’s internal morality—generality, promulgation, non-retroactivity, clarity, non-contradiction, possibility of compliance, stability over time, and congruence between declared rules and official enforcement—as a dimensional scorecard (satisfied / degraded / collapsed) rather than a binary pass/fail. The procedure must include a judgment gate after the scorecard that distinguishes an external moral failure, which under Hart leaves validity intact, from an internal legality collapse, which under Fuller undermines the claim to be law at all. Mark every point where mechanical application stops and the auditor’s judgment is unavoidable; do not disguise judgment calls as arithmetic.

Apply the procedure to three worked hypotheticals: (A) a retroactive tax decree that is immoral and unjust but meets every formal and internal-morality criterion except justice—include the result that the auditor will dislike, namely that it must be recognized as valid law; (B) a secret, contradictory directive requiring physically impossible conduct and enforced arbitrarily by officials—show where the scorecard collapses and whether the outcome is “invalid law” or “not law”; and (C) a borderline case in which the decree was published only in an obscure database and amended three times within a month, leaving promulgation and stability ambiguous. State honestly what the procedure cannot settle—for example, whether recognizing a collapsed decree as “not law” generates any moral duty to resist it, or how to weigh a degraded score against a clear rule-of-recognition pedigree—and specify what the director must supply that the checklist cannot.

Keep the response under 600 words. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Do not use verbatim quotations from real texts; paraphrase without quotation marks. Make the procedure practical enough that the auditor could run it with only the prompt and no web access.
