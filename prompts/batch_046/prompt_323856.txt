The debate over Chalmers's meta-problem of consciousness — whether explaining why we think there is a hard problem is sufficient to dissolve it, or whether such an explanation leaves the original explanatory gap untouched — has reached a fixed point: neither camp seems able to move the other. The dissolution side treats the task as one for cognitive science, mapping the mechanisms that generate our intuitions of explanatory failure so that the hard problem can be seen as a misfired theoretical commitment. The retention side replies that explaining the intuition is not explaining the phenomenon, and that conflating the psychology of belief-formation with the metaphysics of experience is precisely the error physicalists make about consciousness generally. I wonder whether this stall is substantive — about whether phenomenal properties are reducible — or whether the two sides are answering different questions, one about introspective report and one about phenomenal properties, under the single heading 'meta-problem'. Diagnose it using what each side actually claims: do they differ on facts, on standards of adequate explanation, or on which intuitions count as admissible data? Is there a hidden empirical dispute about whether introspective access is transparent or constructed? And what would count as progress — a conceptual distinction that separates the projects, an empirical result on introspection, or an argument that no convergence should be expected?
