I sit on my city's Emerging Tech Oversight panel and we're deadlocked over a pilot for autonomous delivery drones in residential zones. The upsides are obvious -- less truck traffic, lower emissions -- but the long-term noise and social-cohesion effects are basicaly unknown. Half the board wants to invoke the precautionary principle and block the pilot until we have multi-year data. The other half says that's just status-quo bias in a fancy hat: we're treating 'no drones' as the risk-free baseline, but inaction also has costs (more diesel delivery, etc).

We need a decision memo with an actual recommendation -- approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions -- and reasoning the losing side can actually engage with. I'm especially stuck on how to tell real precaution from hidden preference for the current state, given that drone rollout is technically reversable (we could ban them later) but probably socially irreversible once local commerce restructures around them. Tbh I think that reversability distinction is doing a lot of hidden work but I can't quite formalize it. No need for citations, just reasoning we can use in the meeting tommorow.
