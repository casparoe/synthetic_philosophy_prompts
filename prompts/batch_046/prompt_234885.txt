Before I evaluate theories of legal self-reference, I want a rigorous specification of what an adequate account of nomic self-amendment must deliver. Define the target phenomenon as a legal rule that governs not only the content of other rules, but also the conditions of its own recognition or validity—call this reflexive entrenchment. Write the desiderata: (i) the concrete cases any theory must correctly classify or explain, such as a constitutional sunset clause whose expiration and renewal procedures are defined entirely within the clause itself; a judicial doctrine that alters the standard of review it applies to future cases; or a statute that delegates authority to an agency with the power to revise the delegating statute's procedural prerequisites; (ii) the conceptual puzzles it must dissolve or accommodate, including the classic paradox of self-amending authority associated with Alf Ross, infinite regress in chains of authorization, and the simultaneous appearance of validity and invalidity under a rule's own criteria; (iii) the distinctions it must preserve, especially between amendment and interpretation, between procedural and substantive entrenchment, and between internal legal change and extra-legal revolution; (iv) the consistency conditions with broader jurisprudential commitments, for example compatibility with the claim that no rule is its own ultimate source of authority, and with the practical requirement that legal systems remain determinately applicable. Behind each desideratum, state the motivating consideration. Then indicate which requirements are non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences, such as determinism about legal outcomes versus openness to evolutionary change. Crucially, flag any subset of desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether reflexive entrenchment can be maintained without circularity in the foundation of authority, or whether complete procedural closure can coexist with democratic revisability. Keep the weight on the criteria and their tensions rather than on a full scorecard of existing theories, though you may briefly note how a Hartian rule-of-recognition framework, a Kelsenian Grundnorm approach, and a dynamic-systems model respectively confront the list. Write this as a polished, formal brief suitable for a jurisprudence seminar.
