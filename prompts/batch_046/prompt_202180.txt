I serve on the Scientific Advisory Board of the Mid-Atlantic River Authority. We must vote Thursday on whether to adopt a proposed deterministic chaos-model for official flood forecasting in place of our current probabilistic ensemble. The proposed model treats the basin as a fully deterministic nonlinear dynamical system with sensitive dependence on initial conditions; it produces precise trajectory forecasts but has a hard predictability horizon of roughly 72 hours, beyond which divergence is structurally guaranteed rather than merely noisy. Proponents argue that replacing probabilistic language with deterministic trajectories—paired with explicit divergence timelines—respects public epistemic autonomy by showing citizens exactly when prediction ends and chaos begins. Critics contend that issuing authoritative deterministic forecasts we know will fail beyond the horizon violates our fiduciary duty of care, because residents will treat any official projection as actionable regardless of disclaimers, and that determinism without predictability offers no practical advantage over stochastic methods.

Please draft the short written opinion I should circulate before the vote. Recommend approval, rejection, or conditional approval with concrete conditions, and provide reasoning the Board can act on. Your memo should take seriously the philosophical problem at stake—whether causal determinism coupled with practical unpredictability alters the moral and institutional obligations of a forecasting body—and should present the strongest counter-argument to your recommendation fairly rather than dismissing it. Aim for about 450 words, with a clear bottom line and, if conditional, an explicit implementation requirement.
