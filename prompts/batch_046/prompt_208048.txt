I've been thinking about why human extinction is bad beyond just all the deaths. There's this philosopher Scheffler's idea - the afterlife conjecture - that we care about humanity continuing because our lives feel like part of a bigger story that goes on after we die. I kinda ran with it and added my own twist: extinction is wrong not just cause future people vanish, but cause it's like breaking an imlicit promise to descendants we never actually agreed to. So what would count against this? Not the obvious "dead people dont suffer" thing, but real defeaters - like if people turn out not to care about the far future at all, or if there's a logical problem with saying we wrong people who dont exist yet, or if ending a culture actually makes everyone happier. Rank them from fatal to just awkward. And be honest - have defenders of this view added so many caveats that it cant really be wrong anymore? Is that a problem?
