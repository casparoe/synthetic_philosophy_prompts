Write a deadpan parody of a clinical intake manual for "Consensus Therapy," a couples-counseling practice founded on Aumann's agreement theorem. Format it as a formal document of about 600 words, with numbered sections: Preamble; Common Prior Certification; The Announcement and Merger Protocol; Residual Dissent Management; and Termination Criteria. Write in the flat, bureaucratic voice of licensed clinical paperwork. The target is not the theorem itself but the practice of applying its formal conditions—common priors, common knowledge of rationality, and the impossibility of agreeing to disagree—to ordinary domestic disputes over thermostats, in-laws, and grocery budgets. Stay internal: the manual never breaks character or admits absurdity; instead, it earnestly requires spouses to complete a "Hyper-Prior Reconciliation Worksheet," submit "Rationality Audit Checklists," and treat disagreement as a "posterior divergence error" to be mechanically corrected by public belief-announcement. The satire should exaggerate real features of the theorem (the need for common knowledge, the requirement that beliefs be publicly shared, the conclusion that rational agents cannot disagree) rather than invent false ones, so a reader familiar with the result recognizes the distortion is in the application, not the math. Include at least one invented procedural form (e.g., the "Thermostat Posterior Disclosure Form") applied to a concrete domestic case. Do not ask for citations or references to specific papers. End the document with a single line beginning "Verdict:" that states, in character, whether the manual considers persistent disagreement compatible with rational marriage.
