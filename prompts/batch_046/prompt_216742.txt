hey, quick consistency check before i post this on my blog. im a backend engineer so forgive the sloppy framing, but heres where ive landed on climate ethics after reading some threads:

(a) i think people in 2100 deserve the same moral weight as strangers alive today — suffering then isnt less bad just because its distant.
(b) im pretty convinced by the non-identity problem: if we decarbonize hard now, diferent people get born later, so we cant literally be "harming" specific future folks by emitting — theyd never exist in the low-carbon timeline.
(c) still, i think cutting emissions is a strict duty, not just charity or virtue signaling.
(d) i also think individual footprints are rounding errors next to policy and infrastructure, so shaming people over flights is mostly perfomative.
(e) but personally i avoid unnecessary flights and think thats a real moral obligation, not just signaling.
(f) i think obligations require a direct causal chain from my specific action to someone being worse off; diffuse systemic harm doesnt generate individual duties.
(g) i apply basically zero discount rate to welfare across generations — a life in 2200 counts exactly as much as one in 2025.
(h) i sometimes model the carbon budget like a shared resource pool with no garbage collection — future processes inherit the leaked state — but i dont think that analogy actually creates rights-holders in the future.

do these hang together? im especially worried that (b) kills the harm-based reason for (c), and that (f) undermines both (e) and the systemic version of (c) since climate causation is always diffuse. but maybe theres a resolution im not seeing. rank the tensions by severity, say which commitment is the cheap fix vs the expensive one, and point out anything that only looks contradictory but actually dissolves on closer inspection. no need for citations, just check my logic.
