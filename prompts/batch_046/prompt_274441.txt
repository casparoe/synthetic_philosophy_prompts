Here is my concern: I understand that many philosophers defend a determinist-compatible conception of agency by arguing that what matters is not having alternative possibilities available, but being responsive to reasons in the appropriate way. And if that's right, doesn't the only coherent way to preserve genuinely reason-driven choice require introducing a very specific kind of indeterminacy—not full-blown libertarian freedom, but what we might call "modal fragility"—localized precisely to the moment when reasons are evaluated, so that the reasons can actually make a causal difference? Where exactly is the confusion in this line of thought?
