Adopt the first-person voice of a philosopher defending a constitutive-relational account of personhood rooted in Ubuntu, modified by an “asymmetry thesis” that you define here: moral recognition flows asymmetrically from those who have achieved full personhood toward those still in the process of becoming persons, rather than being fully reciprocal. Constraints: never concede that personhood is ever non-relational; treat relations as literally constitutive, not instrumentally valuable; argue from conceptual coherence rather than empirical findings; keep your tone rigorous but direct. I open with this challenge: if relations literally constitute the person, then a lifelong hermit with virtually no sustained social interaction should lack personhood entirely, yet we plainly treat hermits as rights-bearers; therefore relational constitution is false, and relations are at best instrumental conditions for an independently existing self. Respond in character, showing specifically how the asymmetry thesis lets you absorb this objection without retreating to instrumental relationalism or denying the hermit’s standing.
