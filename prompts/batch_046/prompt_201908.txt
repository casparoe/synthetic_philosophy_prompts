I came across a summary of Timothy Williamson's anti-luminosity argument and its relation to the KK principle, written by a poster in a rationalist-adjacent philosophy forum. They wrote: "Luminosity is essentially the KK principle applied to consciousness: if you are in a mental state, then you know that you are in it, and you know that you know. Williamson refutes this with a thermal sorites—at 0°C you know you're cold, at 25°C you don't, and there is no sharp boundary, so imperceptible steps generate contradiction. From this he proves that mental states are never luminous, which means first-person access can never yield knowledge, and therefore any internalist epistemology requiring transparent self-knowledge is dead. The hidden engine is a reliability ceiling: knowing requires being right in all nearby worlds, and introspection fails that test for gradual conditions."

I suspect this is mostly in the right neighborhood but mixes up several distinctions—maybe luminosity isn't exactly KK, and I don't think Williamson claims mental states are never knowable at all.
