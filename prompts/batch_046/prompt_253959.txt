Du Bois's essay "Criteria of Negro Art" at an interdisciplinary colloquium next week. My thesis is that Du Bois's declaration that all art is propaganda should not be read as collapsing aesthetic value into political utility; instead, I argue he is advancing a formalist account of narrative affect—an early anticipation of contemporary theories of emotional calibration—where the propaganda operates through mood, rhythm, and structural design rather than explicit doctrinal content. If this distinction holds, Du Bois's view is compatible with aesthetic autonomy, because the "message" is embedded in form rather than imposed upon it.
