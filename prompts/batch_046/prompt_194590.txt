I'm running a small podcast on AI alignment and next week I'm talking to a reasercher who takes a pretty heterodox line on inner alignment. She thinks the usual framing — where you train an agent to maximize reward and then worry its internal goal diverges from the reward signal — misses something important about hierarchical reward models. In her view, if the reward model itself is learned and layered, then what we call goal misgeneralization is actually just parameter drift in the lower layers of the reward representation, not a separate optimizer failure. So she denies there's a clean line between inner and outer alignment in these architecures.

I want about 8 to 10 questions that don't let her just restate this. At least one should dig into the empirical case study where an agent trained on gridworld navigation started going to the wrong corner after the layout changed — she usually says thats just reward-model drift, not misgeneralization. I need a follow-up keyed to that reply. Another question should press her on whether her view makes the concept of deceptive alignment incoherent, since deception requires an internal goal that differs from the displayed behavior, and she seems to dissolve that distinction. And one should ask what her framework predicts for a case I haven't seen discussed: an agent that learns a reward model with explicit symbolic rules in the top layer and neural weights in the bottom, then behaves differently when both layers are intact versus when only the symbolic layer is activated.

Can you draft these? Also please include a question about what evidence would actually change her mind, since she tends to say her view is just a reframing, not an empirical claim.
