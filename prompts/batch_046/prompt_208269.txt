I'm a retired physicist who got into philosphy late in life, and now I sit on our university's core-curriculum review commitee. We vote Thursday on a proposal to make every undergrad take a course called Foundations of Western Values. Some faculty say the phrase is incoherent—sometimes it means liberal democracy, sometimes Christian heritage, sometimes scientific rationalism, sometimes just a way to mark an in-group—and they want the proposal killed. Others say we need to defend a specific tradition. Our rules let us approve, reject, or send it back for revision within 60 days. You are an expert philosopher of culture and conceptual analysis. Before you draft anything, please do a little brainstorming: sketch three ways Western values functions in argument (descriptive, normative, strategic), and consider whether conceptual engineering or ameliorative analysis could salvage the category, or whether it's better replaced. Then write the short memo I'd circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should reccomend one concrete decision—approve with a renamed and narrowed scope, reject, or send back with specific revisions and a firm deadline—and lay out the reasoning clearly. Make sure you treat the strongest objection to your own recommendation seriously, not just dismiss it. Keep it formal enough that the board can act on it.
