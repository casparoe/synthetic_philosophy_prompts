I've been noticing a recurring pattern in how certain people I follow online engage with opposing views, and I'm not sure whether there's an established term for it. These are not people who live in obvious isolation — they read widely, share articles from across the political or philosophical spectrum, and often present themselves as genuinely interested in honest disagreement. Yet there's something peculiar about the disagreement they actually display. They will highlight and dissect a particularly clumsy tweet, an obviously uninformed comment, a fringe figure's outlandish claim, or a single poorly worded paragraph taken out of context, treating these as if they were representative of the opposing position. At the same time, they seem to pass over or quickly dismiss the more rigorous formulations: the peer-reviewed study, the carefully structured essay, the expert interview that actually addresses the objections. The weak version gets the sustained attention, gets shared, gets rebutted in detail, and then that rebuttal circulates within their group as if it had defeated the view in its strongest form.

At first I wondered if this was simply an echo chamber, but that doesn't seem right — in an echo chamber, dissent is largely absent or suppressed, whereas here dissent is actively present, just in a systematically degraded form. I also considered whether it's an epistemic bubble, where people miss valuable information because of structural filtering, but in this case the better information is not hidden; it's visible, accessible, and deliberately bypassed in favor of the weaker substitute. The pattern feels closer to constructing a straw opponent, except it isn't a one-time rhetorical move made in a single debate — it's a sustained information diet, almost like curating a permanent gallery of the worst available representatives of what one rejects, so that one can maintain the appearance of open-minded engagement without ever risking actual contact with the strongest version of the opposing view.

I'm asking this without any academic background in epistemology or media studies, just from observing my own social feeds and talking with friends who do this without seeming conscious of the pattern. So I want to know: is there a specific concept that captures this precise phenomenon — not mere confirmation bias, not the standard echo chamber, but a kind of selective exposure that seeks out the weakest available representatives of an opposing position in order to maintain the illusion of engagement? If such a concept exists, how does it differ from related ideas like motivated reasoning or the motte-and-bailey? And if there is no established term, could you help me define its boundaries — what separates it from plain straw-manning as a tactical choice, from an epistemic bubble where good information is simply missing, and from a genuine attempt at charitable interpretation that happens to fail?
