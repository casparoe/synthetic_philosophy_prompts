I have been trying to understand a practice I came across in reports about international development funding. Before certain microfinance and entrepreneurship grants are released, consultants in some programs administer surveys measuring local attitudes—such as preferences for long-term saving over immediate family obligations, or the degree to which personal achievement is seen as distinct from communal honor. The results are compiled into what is called a cultural readiness score, and regions that score low are either given smaller allocations or required to complete structured value-alignment workshops before receiving capital. Meanwhile, the same agencies publish analyses attributing poverty primarily to missing institutions, weak property rights, and lack of access to credit, explicitly denying that local morality or character is the root cause.

This strikes me as inconsistent, but perhaps I am missing something. If an agency genuinely believes that material structures and institutional gaps explain economic outcomes, why would it treat cultural values as a gatekeeping condition for funding? What philosophical commitments about causation and explanation does this evaluation protocol presuppose? Specifically, does it require accepting something like Weber's thesis—that a particular ethos or value orientation can act as an independent driving force behind economic development—rather than treating values as mere byproducts of material conditions? Or is there a way to defend the practice without granting culture genuine causal power?

If we replaced the underlying view with a strictly structural or institutional explanation, what parts of the practice would have to change? Would the surveys and readiness scores become pointless, or could they be retained as purely descriptive tools? And does the requirement that a community adjust its values before receiving investment presuppose a particular theory of moral responsibility—one that holds people accountable for the economic effects of their cultural outlook in ways that sit uneasily with the agency's claim that poverty is not a moral failure?
