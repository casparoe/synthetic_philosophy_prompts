I have been developing a novel whose protagonist thinks within a Leibnizian metaphysical framework, and in constructing his ethical outlook I have adopted a somewhat unorthodox interpretation: that for Leibniz, moral obligation derives not primarily from rational self-perfection or divine command, but from the ontological interdependence of monads through pre-established harmony, so that moral improvement is essentially a restructuring of one's perceptual clarity regarding the internal states of other substances. On this reading, morality is less an individual rational ascent and more a refinement of relational awareness made possible by the harmony that already binds every substance to every other. I want to know what would genuinely count against this interpretation—not merely the standard objection that it neglects his theodicy or his jurisprudential writings, but specifically which conceptual tensions become fatal rather than merely awkward. Which passages in his metaphysical and ethical writings would have to be read differently for this view to hold, and how damaging are the apparent conflicts between the isolation of monads and any genuine moral interdependence, or between appetition as internal striving and obligation as relational restructuring? Would defenders of the standard rationalist-eudaimonist reading be able to absorb these tensions without abandoning their core commitments, or do those tensions instead expose a structural weakness in my relational account? I would like the potential defeaters ranked from those that would collapse the interpretation entirely to those that would only require careful qualification. In particular, I am interested in whether the view risks collapsing into a form of Spinozism or into moral quietism, and whether that risk is decisive or manageable. Which of these objections would a committed proponent of my reading have to concede as genuinely damaging, and which could be dismissed as misreadings? What would have to be true about Leibniz's doctrine of love, justice, and the best of all possible worlds for my interpretation to fail?
