I am drafting a protocol for an international maritime arbitration board where I serve as senior compliance auditor. Our referees, who typically lack formal legal training, must repeatedly rule on contract disputes involving clauses that are technically permitted by governing national statute yet raise serious concerns about basic fairness—such as a seafarer's contract containing unilateral termination without notice signed under conditions of economic duress, or a retroactively enacted tariff that strips a vessel of an accrued fuel subsidy. After discussing the framework with colleagues over several working sessions, I want a reusable decision procedure grounded in the Hart-Fuller debate that translates their philosophical disagreement into concrete operational steps a referee can run in roughly thirty minutes before issuing a preliminary ruling.

Please design the procedure so that it takes the referee through ordered questions distinguishing Hart's descriptive account of legal validity—what the law is, independent of what it ought to be—from Fuller's claim that law must satisfy certain internal moral conditions, including generality, publicity, non-retroactivity, and coherence, in order to count as law at all. At each stage, provide an explicit test or criterion the referee can apply without legal expertise, and clearly flag where the step requires an irreducibly normative judgment rather than a factual determination. The procedure should state precisely where it stops with a clear directive to apply or refuse to apply the clause, and where it must hand the decision back to the referee's values because the philosophical framework underdetermines the outcome. Then apply it to both the termination-clause case and the retroactive-tariff case, including one worked example where the procedure yields an answer that conflicts with the referee's likely moral intuition. Conclude by specifying what categories of dispute the procedure is not equipped to resolve and where it will systematically misfire, particularly in cases involving cross-jurisdictional statutes or implicit contractual terms.
