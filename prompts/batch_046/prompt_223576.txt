I've been trying to make sense of history on my own, without any formal training, and I've become fascinated by the disagreement over whether it moves in cycles or in a straight line. I read about Polybius describing governments rotating through monarchy, tyranny, aristocracy, democracy, and mob rule before returning to monarchy; about Ibn Khaldun arguing that the social cohesion that builds civilizations naturally erodes across generations; about Spengler treating cultures like organisms that pass through inevitable seasons from growth to decline; and about Toynbee insisting that civilizations survive only through creative responses to external challenges, which sounds less like destiny and more like a test. These four pictures seem to overlap, yet they point in different directions—some leave room for human agency, others make decline seem unavoidable.

What I find confusing is that people often say history repeats itself as if that settles the matter, when these thinkers actually disagree about why repetition happens, whether it is political, biological, or spiritual, and whether a society can break the pattern. I would like to see this confusion turned into humor. Please write a joke that plays specifically on the conceptual differences among these four views, rather than relying on a generic cliché about the past repeating. To keep the joke grounded, set it in a modern scenario: imagine a consultant giving a strategic presentation to a failing corporation, and in successive slides the consultant invokes Polybius, Khaldun, Spengler, and Toynbee to explain the company's crisis—using each framework correctly enough to sound authoritative but combining them in a way that reveals they contradict one another about whether the collapse is reversible. The punchline should depend on the audience recognizing that the consultant has accidentally proved that cyclical history, if taken seriously across all four models at once, makes strategic planning impossible.

The joke should be understandable to someone who has heard of these thinkers but does not need to have studied them deeply; it should not require citations or references to specific editions, and it should avoid obscure technical vocabulary unless the joke explains it in context. I want it to feel like a joke that respects the ideas rather than mocking them from the outside.
