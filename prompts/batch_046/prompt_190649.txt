I keep running into this two-step move in conversations about whether I could have turned out differently — like being born in another country or picking a different career. First people get really strict: if it's actually me, then some core traits can't change, so that other person with the different life just isn't me, end of story. They use that hard boundary to block contradictions, like you can't both be a musician and a philosopher if those are essential. But then right after, they slide over to saying well, there's still a scenario where someone just like me does it, so in a sense it's possible for me. They need the strict version to stay logically consistent, and the loose just-like-me version to keep our gut feeling that we really could have taken that path. They flip back and forth depending on whether they're being pressed on logic or on intuition, running both accounts in parallel. Is there an established name for this specific shuffle? It's not quite equivocation, since they own both sides, and it definatly isn't just changing your mind. It's more like keeping two seperate ledgers open and paying out from whichever one isn't being audited right now. If there's no term, can you coin one with clear edges — what exactly counts as the shuffle, what it's not (like plain ambiguity or just revising your view), and where the boundary cases sit?
