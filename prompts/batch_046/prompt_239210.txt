Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing interpretive camps regarding Galileo's hermeneutics of scripture. Camp A maintains that Galileo's accommodationist framework—particularly the distinction between demonstrative physical reasoning and probable scriptural interpretation—reflects a sincerely orthodox theological conviction that biblical language is subordinate to mathematical proof only in cosmological matters, with no concealed secularist program. Camp B maintains that the same framework is strategically instrumental: Galileo deploys accommodationist rhetoric primarily to protect heliocentrism from ecclesiastical condemnation, while privately treating scriptural authority as epistemically irrelevant to natural philosophy, with his correspondence and unpublished manuscripts revealing a uniform physicalist priority that renders his theology merely defensive.

Produce a document containing: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims about Galileo's arguments—specifically his treatment of the Joshua passage, his appeal to Church Fathers on non-literal interpretation, and the logical structure of his demonstrative/probable distinction—that both camps can endorse without reservation; (2) a disagreements table presenting each camp's position in wording that its own proponents would accept, isolating the cruxes, including whether Galileo's private letters and his unpublished tidal manuscript indicate a genuinely theological epistemic hierarchy or a purely rhetorical shield; (3) for each crux, a concrete textual feature, historical counterfactual, or piece of internal evidence that both sides agree is relevant, accompanied by each camp's explicit prediction about what that feature demonstrates; and (4) a concluding analysis distinguishing purely verbal disputes—such as competing definitions of accommodationism or natural philosophy—from substantive disagreements about Galileo's intentions and the normative scope of his hermeneutic principle. The statement must be internally evaluable without external bibliographies, citation lists, or appeals to sources outside the texts and arguments under discussion. Maintain a formal, polished register throughout.
