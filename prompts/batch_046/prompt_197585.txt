I'm preparing a conceptual seminar on self-deception that deliberately avoids the well-worn "can one person hold contradictory beliefs?" formulation, and I need a precise evaluative framework before I turn to particular theories. The phenomenon I have in mind includes not only classic cases—where an agent sincerely avows not-p yet behaves in ways that systematically presuppose p and avoids disconfirming evidence—but also what I am calling strategic epistemic insulation: situations in which an agent engineers their own informational environment so that a damaging belief never becomes occurrent, without ever actively suppressing an already-formed judgment. I also want the framework to address the transparency tension: if belief constitutively aims at truth and first-person authority is at least partly reliable, then sustained self-deception looks either like rational failure or like a division within the agent that threatens ordinary moral and psychological ascription.

Please write the full desiderata for an adequate theory of self-deception in this expanded terrain. For each requirement, specify the phenomenon or case it must accommodate (e.g., motivated reasoning under conditions of partial awareness, the difference between self-deception and mere wishful thinking or socially advantageous ignorance, and the preservation of agency without collapsing into full-blown partition theories), the distinction it must respect (particularly between holding a false belief and merely failing to form a true one, and between self-deception and self-manipulation for non-epistemic goals), and the philosophical or empirical constraint it must not violate (including consistency with work on the bias blind spot and with the principle that belief is regulated by truth-aiming norms). Flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—especially any conflict between requiring a unified subject who genuinely possesses both p and not-p and requiring that the subject remain rationally accountable for the error. Indicate which conditions are non-negotiable for counting as an explanation of self-deception at all and which reflect camp-specific commitments (such as commitments to psychoanalytic architecture, eliminativism about a unitary self, or strong transparency doctrines). Keep the emphasis on the criteria and their motivating tensions rather than on scoring named theories, though a brief indication of how partition-based and non-partition approaches stand with respect to the list is welcome if it clarifies the trade-offs.
