Write a Petrarchan sonnet about Douglas Hofstadter’s MU-puzzle and the move he calls jumping out of the system (JOOTSing). In the octave, present the formal rules of the MIU system—beginning with MI, deriving longer strings through the permitted transformations (appending U, doubling after M, contracting III to U, dropping UU)—and build the sense that MU might be reachable, using precise logical vocabulary rather than metaphor alone. At the volta (line 9), break the derivational rhythm: introduce the invariant that the count of I’s is never divisible by three, and let the syntax or rhyme scheme register that this observation is not generated by the rules but imposed from outside. In the sestet, show that recognizing MU as unreachable requires a meta-level proof about the system’s syntax, not another line of derivation within it. Close the couplet by stating exactly what is sacrificed in that escape—namely, that the proof of the limit is not available to the system that lives inside the limit. The poem must be philosophically accurate (distinguishing formal derivability inside MIU from meta-mathematical proof) and should make the sonnet’s structural turn enact the logical discontinuity it describes.
