You have noticed a recurring pattern in debates about moral objectivity, especially where anthropological findings about cross-cultural disagreement are brought in. The pattern is not the disagreement itself, but a specific interpretive asymmetry. Consider two concrete cases. In one culture, refusing food offered by an elder is treated as a serious moral failure; in another, accepting food that burdens the host is treated as the failure. Or take distributive judgments: one group treats fairness as overriding loyalty when resources are scarce, while another treats loyalty as overriding fairness in the same conditions, with both sides agreeing on the relevant empirical facts. When these divergences are discussed—whether in philosophy seminars or in popular science writing—the judgment that aligns with typical WEIRD intuitions tends to be treated as the baseline, the "null" moral position that requires no special explanation. The other judgment is then accounted for in one of two ways: it is assimilated to the home framework ("they really care about fairness, but their social structure masks it") or it is treated as a deviation forced by local conditions ("scarcity or kin structure makes them prioritize loyalty, but the underlying universal norm is still fairness"). Even when the foreign group explicitly articulates a different normative principle—not a different application but a different ordering of values—the divergence is often interpreted as hidden agreement or environmentally induced distortion rather than as an independent moral ontology. You are not asking for a broad label like "ethnocentrism" or "moral universalism." You want to know whether there is a precise concept for this specific habit: treating one's own folk moral framework as the default explanatory state that needs no causal or normative accounting, while treating every cross-cultural variation only as a perturbation to be explained away. If no established term captures exactly this asymmetry—distinguishing it from ordinary outgroup homogeneity, from the legitimate explanation of applied ethical differences by local conditions, and from the mere assimilation of unfamiliar beliefs into familiar categories—then you want one coined, together with clear boundary conditions that say what counts as an instance, what does not, and where the edges lie. What is this phenomenon, how should it be carved, and what should it be called?
