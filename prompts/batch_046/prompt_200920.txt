Write the opening remarks for a single-day interdisciplinary workshop on climate-model validation, delivered to an audience divided between working climate scientists and philosophers of science. The speaker must welcome both camps without either asserting that atmospheric data uniquely fix a single correct model or retreating into the claim that model choice is purely conventional; let the underdetermination of theory by evidence do the real structural work, determining exactly which acknowledgments about model selection are permitted and which stronger claims must be withheld. To keep the argument out of well-worn pretraining territory, inject the following niche framing: treat competing climate models not as rival ontologies of the atmosphere but as alternative coordinate transformations applied to a shared, already-accepted statistical manifold, so that the familiar underdetermination problem is recast as a question about which Riemannian metric on that manifold best preserves out-of-sample predictive utility rather than about which theoretical entities exist. The remarks must be arranged as exactly five brief movements, numbered 1 through 5, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no separate introduction or conclusion and no citations, bibliographies, or appeals to texts the audience could not be expected to know.
