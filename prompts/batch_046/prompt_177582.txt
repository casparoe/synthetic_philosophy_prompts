I'm drafting my philosophical profile on panpsychism and its ethical implications for a reading group, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present. Here is where I stand:

(1) I accept Russellian monist panpsychism: physical properties have intrinsic proto-phenomenal natures, but this does not entail that electrons or quarks are miniature conscious subjects.
(2) I think macroscopic phenomenal unity is a structural relation among these proto-phenomenal properties—analogous in form to quantum entanglement, though not literally identical—so the combination problem is dissolved rather than solved.
(3) I am a strict sentientist about moral status: direct ethical consideration extends only to entities with integrated, bounded phenomenal perspectives; unbound aggregates, fields, and fundamental particles do not count.
(4) Given the ubiquity of proto-phenomenal properties, the aggregate "proto-disvalue" in the universe—roughly scaled by decoherence events—likely exceeds all biological suffering by many orders of magnitude.
(5) Despite (4), I hold that ethical practice should remain focused exclusively on macroscopic sentient organisms, and that attempting to "reduce suffering" at quantum or cosmological scales is conceptually incoherent because there is no unified subject there to suffer.
(6) I further believe that suffering is temporally oriented: proto-phenomenal disvalue contributes to moral reality only along the thermodynamic arrow of entropy increase, not in equilibrium states or in time-reversed branches.
(7) I reject evolutionary debunking arguments against our intuitions about animal suffering, but I accept that our intuitions about cosmic-scale ethics are unreliable because they evolved for macroscopic social coordination.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each? For the real tensions, specify the least costly revision and what it sacrifices. Rank them by severity, and identify the single change that would clean up the most inconsistencies. In particular, address whether (2)'s entanglement analogy implicitly commits me to granting moral status to large-scale physically integrated systems, and whether (4) undermines the practical restriction defended in (5).
