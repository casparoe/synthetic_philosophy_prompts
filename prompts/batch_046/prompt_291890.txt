I just finished a long argument with a friend about whether Occam's razor is mere aesthetic preference or whether Solomonoff induction gives us an actual procedure for eliminating needlessly complex theories, and I want to turn that into something I can use rather than debate. I serve as the senior technical auditor for an interdisciplinary funding body; I review roughly three proposals each week that claim to explain the same phenomenon—attention dynamics, climate feedback loops, economic forecasting—through different ontological frameworks. I have thirty minutes per proposal, no ability to compute Kolmogorov complexity, and I must sign off with a clear recommendation. Build me a reusable audit procedure derived from Solomonoff induction and Occam's razor that I can run in that window: the ordered questions, the pass or fail conditions at each stage, the explicit stop points where added complexity is unjustified, the stop points where empirical divergence justifies added structure, and—essentially—the points where I must make an uneliminable judgment call rather than apply a rule. Because Kolmogorov complexity is relative to a description language, include a specific step that detects prior smuggling, where a theory achieves apparent simplicity only by encoding complexity into its representational vocabulary or its universal prior rather than into its explicit claims. Then apply the procedure to three cases: (a) a neurophenomenological attention model that introduces first-person data structures into a standard predictive-processing framework; (b) a climate proposal that adds a novel atmospheric feedback loop to account for an anomalous warming signal; and (c) a proposal that the procedure flags for rejection on parsimony grounds but that is in fact the correct one to fund—identify the exact step where the audit misfires and explain why I should override it. Conclude by stating precisely what this procedure cannot do: which forms of theoretical disagreement it will systematically misread, and why it can never substitute for a domain expert's evaluation of explanatory adequacy.
