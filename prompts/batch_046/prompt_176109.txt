Write an exam essay question for an upper-level philosophy of probability seminar, together with a model answer of approximately 800 words and a detailed marking scheme. The topic should be the Judy Benjamin problem and updating on conditionals, but you must inject a novel variant that prevents students from merely reciting the standard Jeffrey-conditioning resolution. Specifically, construct a scenario in which Judy Benjamin receives two conditional messages in strict sequence—first, that if she is in Blue HQ then she is in the front company; second, that if she is in Red HQ then she is in the rear company—and ask students to determine whether her final credence in being in Blue HQ depends on the order in which she receives these messages. The essay question should require students to distinguish three different interpretations of "updating on a conditional": updating on the material conditional, updating on the unconditional fact that the message was uttered, and updating on the conditional belief itself; to apply the correct probability calculus to show whether sequential belief revision is commutative under each interpretation; and to evaluate whether any non-commutativity threatens the claim that rational belief revision is path-independent. The model answer must be written in British English and should explicitly identify the ambiguity in the phrase "updating on a conditional," calculate the posterior probabilities for both orderings under each interpretation, and discuss the philosophical significance for whether belief history is locked in. The marking scheme should award first-class marks only for answers that make all three interpretive distinctions, perform the calculations correctly, and address the path-dependence issue; it should also specify the errors that should lower a grade—such as conflating Jeffrey conditioning with simple conditionalisation, ignoring the messenger-reliability structure, or treating the two updates as jointly received rather than sequential. Set the exam conditions as a 90-minute essay worth 60 per cent of the module grade, with answers expected to be around 800 words.
