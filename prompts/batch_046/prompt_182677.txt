Design a diagnostic taxonomy—structured as a decision tree with concrete case nodes—that classifies normative stances on how much rule-following a society should demand. Rather than beginning with the familiar tight-loose dichotomy, root the tree in three branching commitments: (A) whether social rules within a domain ought to be universal across all members or context-sensitive to subgroup identity; (B) whether enforcement should be peer-based and decentralized or institutional and centralized; and (C) whether a society should permit what we might call domain-modular pluralism, in which different subgroups maintain mutually incompatible rule-sets within non-intersecting spheres of life (for example, one community practices strict ritual food preparation while another rejects food rules entirely, with neither imposing norms on shared public spaces). Each internal node must present a specific scenario—such as whether a municipality should penalize a traditional chef who adapts a ceremonial dish to accommodate a minority religious restriction, or whether professional writing standards should apply to private digital correspondence—phrased so that a respondent can answer yes or no without knowing which leaf it leads to. The leaves should be clearly labeled positions (for instance, Universal Centralized Monism, Context-Sensitive Peer Enforcement, Modular Pluralist Loose, and so on). Include all eight logical combinations of answers to A, B, and C, but note which combinations appear unoccupied in actual normative debate, diagnosing each gap as either conceptually incoherent, practically unstable, or merely underexplored. Finally, identify any pair of leaves that the tree fails to genuinely separate—cases where identical answers to the nodes could plausibly map onto two different positions—and explain what further distinction would be needed to split them. The instrument should be suitable for an audience already acquainted with the basic tight-loose literature but seeking to map the finer logical space of normative rule-demand.
