Can you write me a reusable procedure my copy-editors can run? It needs ordered checks: identify the load-bearing formal features in the source, test whether an English equivalent exists without parody, decide if the feature carries semantic weight or is decorative, and flag the case for human judgement when both conditions fail. Include the misfire points — false equivalents, nursery-rhyme effects, and the trap of preserving form that actually changes the poem's argument. Apply it to two cases: an algorithmic villanelle with a recursive rhyme loop where preserving the loop destroys the central image (the one where the procedure should say drop it, even if we hate that), and a syllabic-count poem where English stress patterns block direct preservation but a stress-count substitute works. Also say clearly where the procedure just stops and can't settle things.

Write it casual, British spelling (behaviour, judgement, etc.), and don't worry if there's a typo — I definately won't mind. At the end, tell me how confident you are in the whole thing and single out the point you're least sure of — probably whether load-bearing form can be tested without circularity.
