Here's an argument I sketched for a seminar and now it feels suspicious because it proves way too much.

1. Mises claims rational economic calculation requires actual market prices for capital goods—simulated prices like Lange's shadow prices dont cut it.
2. Hayek adds that prices also carry dispersed local knowledge that no central node can gather.
3. Lange replies with iterative trial-and-error: adjust prices until supply equals demand.
4. But now imagine a big tech firm allocating GPU clusters across divisions using internal transfer prices computed by an algorithimic layer that aggregates local scarcity reports—no market exchange for those goods, no single planner either.
5. Or take a peer-to-peer resource mesh that allocates bandwidth via distributed consensus on scarcity gradients, with no prices at all.
6. If Mises and Hayek are right together, then both of these should be impossible or deeply irrational.
7. Yet they happen, and they seem to work about as well as market coordination.
8. So either the socialist-calculation critique applies to every large firm and distributed mesh—proving far too much—or it was never really about prices but about something hidden.
9. But I cant see what the hidden thing is. Where's the flaw? I'd like to keep (1) and (2) if possible, but I'll pay the cost elsewhere if I have to.
