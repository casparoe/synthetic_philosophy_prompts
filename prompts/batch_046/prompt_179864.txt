I've been trying to pin down what I actually think about the hedonistic imperative — like, the idea that we should use biotech to wipe out suffering. Going case by case, my gut calls are all over the place.

Case one: gene-editing embryos so they never develop chronic pain conditions. That feels like an obvious yes, do it.

Case two: giving wild prey animals some kind of universal neuropeptide that suppresses fear and grief but leaves pain intact. No, don't mess with ecosystems like that.

Case three: creating artificial minds built to experience only positive affect gradients, no negative ones at all. Yes, and maybe we actually have a duty to make a lot of them.

Case four: implanting factory pigs with neural modulators so they feel constant euphoria regradless of crowding or conditions. That's not okay, even though suffering is technically gone.

Case five: a drug for humans that wipes out existantial dread and heartbreak but also flattens emotional range into bland contentment. No, that goes too far.

Case six: using microbiome engineering to reduce suffering in parasites and thier hosts at the same time. I'm genuinely torn — the suffering reduction is huge but it feels like playing god in a way I can't defend.

The tension is that case one and case five both remove bad feelings, but I want the first and reject the second. Same with case three vs five: why is building bliss-beings okay but flattening existing people wrong? And case two and four are both biotech interventions that reduce animal suffering, yet I reject them for totally different reasons. Is there a coherent principle behind all this, or do I just need to drop a verdict? What would it say about using optogenetics during surgery so people can dial physical pain down without losing emotional depth? I go back and forth on that one.

Can you propose a principle, or maybe two competing ones, that gets as many of these as possible? For each, say which case forces which clause, which verdicts have to be sacrificed, and where the surgery case lands.
