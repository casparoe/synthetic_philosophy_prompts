Consider a class of decision problems—call them observer-conditioned epistemic-externality problems—in which an agent may acquire a costless observation prior to acting, but an external observer conditions a punitive payoff specifically on the agent's possession of that knowledge, regardless of the agent's subsequent choice. Thus the information itself alters the payoff structure through an epistemic externality, not through any causal pathway involving the agent's action.

State your credence that problems of this kind genuinely violate Good's theorem that free information cannot reduce the expected utility of an optimal decision-maker. Decompose your answer as follows:

(iii) The probability that Good's result is best interpreted normatively (one ought to acquire free information) versus descriptively (expected utility is monotone in information for a fixed decision rule), and whether this distinction neutralizes the counterexample.

Combine (i)–(iii) into an overall credence (a specific number between 0 and 1) that these problems undermine the theorem. Then identify the component in which you have least confidence and specify what evidence or formal result would most shift it.
