Write an essay evaluating whether the Kalai-Smorodinsky monotonicity axiom survives a specific kind of counterexample: an asymmetric expansion of the feasible set in which only one party's utopia point shifts outward, leaving the Pareto frontier near the disagreement point entirely unchanged. First, outline the standard Kalai-Smorodinsky solution and contrast it with the Nash bargaining solution, clarifying how monotonicity operates when the entire feasible set expands uniformly. Then introduce the concrete case: two collaborators negotiating the division of resources and authorship credit on a joint project have a fixed disagreement point—if talks collapse, each retains their prior solo funding and individual reputation. After the disagreement point is set, an external funding body announces a distant, highly speculative grant that only one collaborator could realistically pursue alone. This grant extends that collaborator's ideal payoff far along one axis, but it does not alter any point on the frontier that is actually reachable within the joint project. Assess whether applying monotonicity here forces an unfair redistribution of the original, unchanged pie in favor of the party whose distant counterfactual improved. Weigh the defense that monotonicity prevents strategic shrinking of options against the criticism that it conflates real bargaining leverage with an irrelevant hypothetical. Reach and justify a definite conclusion: should the axiom be applied without qualification, qualified to require frontier-expansion near the agreement, or rejected in contexts where ideal-point shifts are decoupled from feasible-set improvements?
