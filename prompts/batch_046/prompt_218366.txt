Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on whether the contrastive historical question "Why Britain and not China?" is well posed as an explanation of the Great Divergence (the Needham question).

Camp A, the contrastive institutionalists, holds that comparing Britain and Qing China is methodologically legitimate: that differential institutional arrangements, property-rights structures, and technological pathways can genuinely explain why industrial modernity consolidated in Britain, and that macro-historical counterfactuals—such as substituting specific institutional features across the two cases—can be coherent and causally informative.

Camp B, the particularist coherence critics, holds that the comparison is fundamentally ill-posed: that treating Britain and China as unitary, comparable units at a shared baseline ignores internal heterogeneity and anachronistic framing; that asking why Britain "rather than" China presupposes a teleological endpoint both societies were competing to reach; and that counterfactual institutional substitutions collapse because altering one feature requires altering so many interdependent background conditions that the substituted scenario is historically incoherent.

Produce a constructive document both parties could sign. Structure it with:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section covering: what the Great Divergence denotes; the core framing of Needham's original question; shared rejections (for instance, of racial or cultural essentialism and of monocausal determinism); agreed methodological constraints on macro-comparative explanation; and any empirical or formal points both accept regardless of interpretation.

2. A disagreements table containing: the exact formulation of Camp A's position, phrased so its defenders would accept the wording; the exact formulation of Camp B's position, phrased so its defenders would accept the wording; and the derived cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would move at least one camp.

3. For each crux, provide: the precise sub-question; one historical case, analytical test, or formal argument both sides agree bears on it; and, recorded in advance, each camp's predicted interpretation or outcome. Include at least one crux centered on whether "counterfactual robustness under institutional substitution" can be satisfied for macro-historical comparisons without collapsing into incoherence—specifically, whether targeted alterations (e.g., transplanting a specific British legal or fiscal institution into Qing China) necessarily entail so many background adjustments that the counterfactual loses explanatory purchase.

4. A concluding section identifying whether any portion of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive—such as disagreements about whether "explanation" in macro-history requires counterfactual dependence, or about the appropriate unit of analysis—together with a brief justification for that classification.

The document should be precise and in the weeds of comparative historical methodology, not a general summary of the Great Divergence. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests. Do not depend on specific page numbers or obscure technical terminology that a well-read generalist model would not recognize.
