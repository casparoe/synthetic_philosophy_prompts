I am a retired physicist who has recently begun studying moral philosophy, and I have agreed to lead a single ninety-minute discussion for a local group of former scientists and engineers—intelligent non-specialists—on incommensurable values and hard choices. Rather than explaining the concept to me, please plan the session: lay out the conceptual dependencies that must be established first and the order in which they should unfold, particularly to distinguish incommensurability from mere equality, uncertainty, or quantitative trade-off. I want to carry the entire explanation through one concrete scenario—choosing between preserving an irreplaceable old-growth watershed and funding a long-term fundamental-physics experiment—because both goods resist translation into a common metric; specify what this example must possess and what it must lack in order to work. Next, identify the misconceptions that listeners with scientific training will likely import—such as the belief that rational comparison always requires a shared unit of measurement, or that a "hard choice" is merely a problem of insufficient data—and state precisely where in the sequence each should be defused. Provide one focused question I can ask at the close to test whether they understand that incommensurability does not eliminate reasoned commitment but only eliminates reduction to a single scale. Finally, state openly what should be left out at this level—perhaps formal preference-aggregation theory or the technical literature on value pluralism—and say so explicitly.
