Two members of my study group just finished a long argument about whether 'better than' is transitive and asked me to referee. Maya offered a spectrum case with twenty paintings arranged in a chain: each adjacent pair differs only by an imperceptible shift in chromatic harmony, so Painting 1 is better than 2, 2 better than 3, through 19 better than 20. Yet Painting 20, she said, is clearly worse than Painting 1 because the accumulated micro-shifts have destroyed global aesthetic coherence. She concluded that 'better than' is not transitive. Leo replied that Maya smuggles in a hidden parameter—global coherence—that is absent from the local pairwise judgments; within any fixed evaluation frame the relation is transitive, and the apparent failure is only a shift in comparison context. Maya countered that saving transitivity by indexing it to context means the bare relation is not transitive—only the indexed family is consistent—and asked whether ethical spectrum arguments (e.g., small moral compromises accumulating) depend on this hidden-context move or genuinely expose intransitivity. Please adjudicate move by move: assess Maya's painting-chain argument, Leo's hidden-parameter reply, and Maya's counter about contextual indexing. Who is right about what, what are both missing, and what does this imply for spectrum reasoning in evaluative domains?
