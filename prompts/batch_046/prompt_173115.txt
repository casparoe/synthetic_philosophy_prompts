I've been teaching Schlick's essay on the meaning of life for years — you know, the one where he says life gets its point from play, not purpose or achievment. Recently I've been pushing a kind of updated version: an activity is meaningful exactly when it works like a self-generating game, where the rules and the value come into being together with no prior justification outside the activity itself. So work only becomes meaningful when you stop treating it as a means and start treating it like play you could have invented on the spot. I think this saves Schlick from the obvious objection that people have to work to survive, but I'm not convinced it's stable. What would actually count against this view? Not just the usual "but people need purpose" line — I want the serious defeaters. Which cases or conceptual pressure points show that self-generating play can't carry the weight of meaning? Would it be fatal if we discovered that all play secretly relies on hidden instrumental goals? Or that meaning requires some external validation Schlick tried to reject? Rank them for me: what's genuinely dangerous vs just awkard. And be honest — has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that a problem for a view like this?
