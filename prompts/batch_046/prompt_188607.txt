Please explain the conceptual issue at the heart of the Epicurean argument that death is not bad for the one who dies: specifically, whether the argument's force depends on an implicit presence requirement according to which harm requires a present subject who can experience it. Then show how that same presence requirement applies—if consistently—to other states in which a subject is temporarily or permanently absent, such as deep unconsciousness, a permanent vegetative state, or pre-natal non-existence, and whether this generates a reductio of the Epicurean view or instead reveals that the deprivation account of death's badness must rely on a different metaphysics of personal identity over time. Do not survey the literature; just map the conceptual commitments and the points at which they conflict.
