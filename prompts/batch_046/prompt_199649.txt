I have recently begun keeping a rigorous sleep journal after a string of verified lucid episodes, and the experience has made the philosophy of dreaming personally urgent. Before presenting my position in a reading group, I want an internal consistency check. My commitments are these:

(1) I follow Malcolm in holding that ordinary, non-lucid dreams are not experiences occurring during sleep; the morning report is a post-hoc reconstruction, not a recollection of prior phenomenal consciousness.
(2) I broadly accept Dennett's cassette framing—the narrative is assembled near awakening—but I amend it with what I call live drafting: brief micro-arousals within REM can embed fragments of genuine phenomenal content into an otherwise constructed story.
(3) I take verified lucid-dream reports at face value: in REM lucid states the subject genuinely possesses introspectively accessible conscious experience.
(4) I hold that first-person authority, though fallible, is generally trustworthy for present mental states, including immediate reports upon awakening from lucid episodes.
(5) I maintain that the explanatory gap applies wherever phenomenal consciousness occurs, so it is present for lucid dream experience just as for waking experience.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear so? Rank the real conflicts by severity, and for each specify the cheapest revision and its cost elsewhere in the set. In particular, does my live-drafting amendment preserve the boundary I want between ordinary and lucid dreaming, or does it collapse (2) into (3)? And if I must drop or revise exactly one commitment to resolve the most conflicts while keeping the core picture—that ordinary dreams are not experiences, lucid ones are, and first-person reports carry limited but real weight—which one should go?
