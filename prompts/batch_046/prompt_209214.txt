Proponents of this analogy use it to frame Franklin as a proto-behaviorist who understood virtue as a trainable skill, and to explain his admitted failure to reach perfection as an ancestor of the “falling off the wagon” problem familiar from contemporary commitment devices. Third, say what the analogy quietly imports—a secular, individualist, instrumental model of the self—that Franklin’s deist and communitarian commitments do not share.
