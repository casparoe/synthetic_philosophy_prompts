my therapist keeps saying emotions are like smoke alarms: they go off automatically, they dont ask your permission, and they can be false alarms, so treating them as judgments is like treating a fire alarm as a court verdict. she uses this to push back against the idea that we can just think our way out of anxiety. but i wonder how far the smoke-alarm picture actually holds for emotions like guilt or nostalgia, where the danger seems built right into the evaluation itself. can you map the correspondences -- whats the sensor, the threshold, the false positive, the reset mechanism -- then show where it breaks, especially for the moral emotions? and does her conclusion -- that emotions arent reason-responsive judgments -- survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the whole argument?
