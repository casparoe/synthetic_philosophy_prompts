I'm drafting the opening remarks for a workshop I am convening next month on institutional design for advanced AI governance. The audience will be split roughly in half: on one side, alignment researchers for whom the competitiveness constraint is a lived reality and the alignment tax an unavoidable feature of the landscape; on the other, senior policymakers and legal scholars who understand market behaviour well but have never had to realise that a serious safety investment can be a direct strategic liability. I want the philosophical idea to do real structural work — it should determine which cooperative proposals I can honestly advance and which ones I must rule out — rather than serving as decorative framing. Specifically, please build the remarks around the distinction between paying the alignment tax ex ante, as a deliberate pre-deployment slowdown that makes a team vulnerable to less cautious rivals, and paying it ex post, as a reactive patch after competitive pressure has already forced a rushed release. The argument should not be that cooperation is pleasant, but that the temporal structure of the tax alters the rational calculus for every actor, which is precisely why naive appeals to collective action fail in this domain. The speech should run about six to eight minutes, should land for the non-technical half without condescending to the technical half, should include one sustained analogy that both audiences can grasp, and should close with a concrete institutional proposal — not a vague aspiration — for how an ex ante tax could become collectively affordable rather than individually suicidal. I am composing this request in British English; please deliver the speech in American English.
