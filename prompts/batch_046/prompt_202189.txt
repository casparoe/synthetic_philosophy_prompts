I've been trying to work out my own stance on how to weigh personal reasoning against the judgments of people who have studied a subject more deeply, particularly for abstract conceptual questions where there is no experiment I can run to check myself. I don't have a formal background in philosophy or statistics, but I've read enough to form some views, and I want to make sure they aren't contradictory before I talk them through with friends. Here is roughly where I have landed.

First, when I am looking at a specific argument or paradox—say, whether a particular line of reasoning about infinity actually forces a conclusion or just exploits an ambiguity—I think I should take what I understand as the inside view: I should examine the steps directly and decide whether they compel me, rather than polling what experts think. Second, when I am judging how reliable that direct examination is, I think I should take the outside view: I should consider base rates for people with my level of training reaching correct conclusions in similar domains, and calibrate my confidence downward accordingly. Third, I hold that epistemic modesty is not about always meeting others halfway, but about aligning my confidence with my actual track record; if I haven't done well historically at resolving these kinds of puzzles, I should be uncertain even when the steps look clear to me. Fourth, I have come to think there is a special class of judgments—what I call structural intuitions about how a concept must behave—where outside-view statistics are largely useless, because the relevant reference class is just "people who have carefully examined this specific structure," and that group is too small and heterogeneous to yield a meaningful base rate. Fifth, I think that if I apply the outside view consistently across all my controversial philosophical beliefs, including my belief that structural intuitions are special, I should end up deeply unsure about almost everything I think.

My concern is that I am using the "structural intuition" category as an escape hatch: whenever the outside view suggests I am probably wrong, I reclassify the issue as structural and revert to trusting my inside view. So I want an audit: do these five commitments form a coherent package, or am I helping myself to something? Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what is the least costly revision—should I drop the structural-intuition exemption, weaken my outside-view commitment, or revise my understanding of modesty? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad.
