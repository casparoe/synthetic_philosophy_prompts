I've been trying to work out Wittgenstein's idea of hinge propositions from On Certainty—the background assumptions, like that the earth is ancient or that I have hands, that are not themselves backed by evidence yet make all other reasoning possible. The structure sounds to me exactly like what predictive-processing theorists call high-precision priors: deep cognitive commitments held with near-absolute confidence that we revise only when accumulated prediction error becomes overwhelming. So is Wittgenstein essentially describing these hinges as our deepest updatable priors, with rationality consisting in knowing when the error signal is large enough to swap one out? If so, then a last-Thursdayist—someone who holds the hinge that the world was created five days ago complete with false memories—would just be running an unusual prior, correctable in principle by enough contradictory sensory data. Or does the claim that hinges belong to a form of life mean they are not psychological weights at all, and therefore immune to that kind of revision?
