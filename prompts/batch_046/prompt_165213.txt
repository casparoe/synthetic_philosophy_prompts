My working position is that the is-ought gap and the charge of the naturalistic fallacy are best reconstructed not as logical prohibitions but as modal stability conditions: a normative claim is genuinely supported by descriptive premises only if it survives counterfactual perturbation of those premises across nearby possible worlds, and evolutionary-functional accounts of value can meet this condition by treating moral properties as second-order properties of multi-level functional stability rather than as first-order descriptive features. On this reading, Hume's guillotine and Moore's open-question argument do not block naturalistic ethics; they impose a design constraint—counterfactual robustness—that naturalism can in principle satisfy, and failure to satisfy it explains why naive reductive accounts feel fallacious without showing that all naturalism is fallacious. I want an honest map of this position's exposure. What observations, arguments, formal results, or conceptual developments would count against it, sorted from fatal to merely awkward? In particular: is there an intuition or case—perhaps involving radical value change under preserved functional organization, or the persistence of normative disagreement despite shared evolutionary history—that the position cannot accommodate without ad hoc qualification? What does this stability-condition view predict that its rivals—strict non-cognitivism, irreducible non-naturalism, and simple reductive naturalism—do not, and are those predictions risky or merely decorative? Which defeaters would a committed defender of the position concede as genuinely damaging, and which would they dismiss as relying on a pre-theoretic conception of reduction? Has the position already been qualified—say, by allowing that functional stability is itself normatively loaded—into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that insulation a virtue for a metaethical framework or a vice that makes the view empty? Finally, if the position turns out to be well insulated, does that indicate that it has captured a structural feature of normative reasoning or that it has abandoned the field?
