Consider the standard disjunctivist response to the argument from illusion: there is no mental common factor shared between veridical perception and hallucination, because in the good case the object itself is present and constitutive of the experience, whereas in the bad case it is absent. Now examine a structured intermediate case that does not fit neatly into either category. A subject views an object through a precisely calibrated environmental refractive interface—say, a stable atmospheric gradient or engineered optical layer—that systematically distorts only one relational property of the object (for instance, its apparent distance or orientation relative to the observer) while leaving its intrinsic properties (color, shape, texture) correctly registered and causally tied to the object itself. The experience is therefore partially matched: the object is genuinely present and perceptually engaged, yet one property is misrepresented due to an external environmental condition rather than an internal sensory malfunction.

Please explain the conceptual difficulty this poses for disjunctivism. Specifically, address the following: (1) Must the disjunctivist classify this as a veridical perception of a different state of affairs, as a hallucination that coincidentally overlaps with a present object, or as a distinct mental kind that undermines the binary taxonomy? (2) How does the case pressure the disjunctivist's reliance on the presence of the object as the sole determinant of experiential kind, given that presence here coexists with systematic misrepresentation? (3) Can the argument from illusion be reconstructed to exploit this partial-matching structure—showing that if experience can be decomposed into property-specific components, some veridical and some illusory, then there must be shared mental constituents across good and bad cases? And (4) what does your answer imply about whether disjunctivism can maintain its anti-representationalist stance without either collapsing into a form of representationalism or multiplying mental kinds beyond plausibility?
