As a journalist who writes about science and ideas, I am drafting an application for a three-month independent research fellowship offered by a foundation that funds cross-disciplinary projects at the intersection of historical case study and contemporary institutional design. My proposed project takes up three canonical failures of Athenian democracy—the assembly’s reversal in the Mytilene debate, its catastrophic commitment to the Sicilian Expedition, and the judicial execution of Socrates—and asks whether they can be explained not as discrete moral or strategic lapses, but as variants of a single structural failure that I am tentatively calling “epistemic scaling”: the inability of a face-to-face deliberative body to calibrate the intensity and kind of judgment required when the object of decision is distant in space, in time, or in social position. The precise research question is whether there exists a unified institutional mechanism—perhaps tied to the absence of mediated representation, or to the dynamics of direct-democratic rhetoric—that explains why the same assembly corrected itself in one case and failed irreversibly in the other two, and whether identifying that mechanism changes how we should think about modern distributed democratic decision-making. Write this as a polished, formal proposal of approximately 800 words, suitable for a board that includes historians and political philosophers but no specialists in Attic democracy. The proposal must contain: a sharply stated question and an argument for why standard treatments in classical scholarship and democratic theory leave the unified framing genuinely open; a month-by-month methodological plan that relies on translated primary sources and conceptual analysis rather than archival work, with an honest assessment of my limitations as a non-classicist; a description of what a useful deliverable would look like for readers interested in democratic design rather than ancient history alone; a clear account of what findings would count as success and what would count as failure; and a realistic scope assessment given the three-month timeline. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations—I will compile those separately. The framing should not treat the three cases as mere historical narrative; it must argue that the conceptual work is worth funding because it produces a portable analytical framework.
