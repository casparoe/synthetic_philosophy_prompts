Design a classroom activity for a postgraduate philosophy seminar of twelve participants, running for ninety minutes, that makes them experience the structural force of incommensurable values and genuinely hard choices—not as a failure of reasoning or information, but as a feature of practical rationality that resists maximisation.

Instead of the familiar introductory examples, construct a specific, irreversible public-resource allocation in which three goods operate with categorically different evaluative logics: commemorative fidelity to an unrecoverable past event, distributive equity in present housing need, and non-instrumental ecological preservation. Divide the seminar into four triads. Each triad is assigned one good and must first propose a ranking of the three possible outcomes using only the internal vocabulary of that assigned good. The triads then exchange positions and must defend their ranking to a triad holding a different vocabulary, without translating the values into one another. Finally, all twelve participants must attempt to construct a single decision procedure that respects all three goods without reducing any to a common currency.

Arrange explicit phases with symbolic, irreversible stakes: provide each group with a fixed pool of deliberative tokens that can be spent to protect one value, but spending permanently removes the capacity to protect another, so that the experience of loss is enacted rather than described. The rules should be structured so that participants themselves build the comparative sequence step by step, and only in the final phase discover that they have implicitly presupposed a covering value they have not justified.

Your deliverable should include: the precise group configuration and assigned roles; a minute-by-minute timeline; the token scheme and an explanation of why real money is unnecessary; the debrief that maps each phase onto weak comparability, strong comparability, and the idea that rational choice can leave moral remainder; an honest assessment of which feature of value pluralism—such as the absence of a cardinal metric or the rational persistence of regret—the activity cannot fully reproduce and must be supplied by the facilitator; and a prediction of what will predictably fail in the first run, likely an unnoticed slide into a hidden utilitarian translation, together with a correction.

Write in formal, polished British English. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or named theorists beyond those generally familiar from standard philosophical training.
