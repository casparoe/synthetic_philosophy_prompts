Write an essay on the following controversy. Imagine a technology that gradually replaces a human brain not neuron by neuron, but functional module by functional module, substituting each biological subsystem with a synthetic analog that replicates its precise input-output behavior yet operates through representational formats structurally alien to the original—for example, replacing a visual processing module with one that yields identical perceptual judgments but lacks any retinotopic mapping or analog spatial coding, or replacing a memory consolidation module with one that stores the same information in a non-associative, vector-based architecture. The procedure is gradual, and the subject reports an unbroken stream of subjective experience with no detectable behavioral discontinuity. The question is whether this process preserves the original person's phenomenal consciousness and personal identity, or whether it produces a successive being that behaviorally mimics the predecessor while lacking its inner life.

Weigh the central arguments on both sides. Those who defend continuity should address whether functional equivalence at the modular level, combined with the absence of any abrupt transition, is sufficient to maintain phenomenal and personal identity, and whether the subject's first-person reports of uninterrupted awareness carry evidential weight. Those who deny continuity should explain whether phenomenal character is tied to specific representational vehicles rather than to functional roles, whether cumulative micro-discontinuities in representational format could render the final subject a kind of philosophical zombie relative to the original despite perfect behavioral continuity, and whether the reporting mechanisms used to assert continuity have themselves been replaced by analogs that merely generate the appropriate verbal outputs without access to genuine phenomenal states.

In reaching your conclusion, defend a specific verdict—whether modular gradual replacement preserves identity and consciousness, whether it necessarily fails to do so, or whether its success depends on a condition you specify (such as the preservation of a particular kind of representational structure, or the irrelevance of vehicle-level properties altogether). Develop the relevant conceptual distinctions—between functional role and representational vehicle, between behavioral continuity and phenomenal continuity, and between first-person authority and replaced reporting mechanisms—through direct reasoning rather than by appeal to bibliographic authority. Do not provide citations, reading lists, or references to specific texts; build the argument from the conceptual architecture of the scenario itself.
