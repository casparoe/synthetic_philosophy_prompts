I'm applying for a four-month fellowship at a small philosophy-of-science center and need a two-page proposal. My idea is kind of half-baked: Darwin shows you can get complex functional competence—like an orchid's shape fitting a moth—without any comprehension or design, and Turing shows you can get behavioral competence—passing a conversation—without internal understanding. But people treat these as seperate stories, one in biology and one in AI, and I think there's a hidden parallel in how both thinkers use an external evaluator, natural selection or an interrogator, as a black box that never looks inside. I want to ask whether competence without comprehension is actually one unified phenomenon, or whether Darwin's case is about diachronic accumulation while Turing's is about synchronic performance, and if that difference means we should apply different criteria when we say an AI understands versus when we say an instinct understands.

The existing literature mostly keeps them in seperate silos—philosophers of biology talk about Darwin's blindness, philosophers of mind talk about Turing's behaviorism—so nobody has really mapped whether the inversion works the same way in both domains. My approach would be conceptual, not empirical: I'd compare Darwin's reasoning about instinct versus reason in his private notebooks with Turing's reasoning about learning machines and the imitation game, focusing on exactly where each thinker allows an outside observer to stop asking how does it work inside. I'd deliver a framework with three conditions—robustness, opacity, and evaluative independence—that distinguish mere functional success from something that genuinely lacks comprehension, and I'd apply it to both biological adaptation and modern language models.

If the project fails, it fails because the parallel collapses: maybe Darwin's selection requires generations of error correction that Turing's single-session evaluation doesn't, so the black-box comparison is just a metaphor. A realistic scope is four months of close reading and one working paper; I wouldn't try to solve consciousness, just clarify whether we're using the same words for two different gaps.
