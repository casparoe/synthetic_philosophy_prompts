Construct a thought experiment — call it the Fragmented Narrative Machine — engineered to isolate one specific variable across three theories of well-being: whether continuous autobiographical access to the pre-machine decision is necessary for a good life, holding constant the presence of pleasure, achievement, and deep relationships.

The scenario must satisfy the following design specifications. First, the setup must be realistic (a clinical or research context with existing neurotechnology, not science-fictional physics). In one paragraph, describe a machine or protocol that offers a subject a life rich in objective goods — significant creative achievement, intimate relationships, and sustained pleasure — but with an engineered structural feature: every ninety days, the subject undergoes a partial autobiographical reset that severs episodic access to the decision to enter and to earlier cycles, while preserving procedural skills, emotional bonds, and first-order experiential quality. The subject never feels confusion or loss; they simply lack the narrative thread linking present experience to an origin outside the machine.

Second, apply three theories and show that the case generates the specified divergence rather than stipulating it:
- Hedonism (experienced well-being) must recommend entering, and the reason must depend specifically on the experiential quality being untouched by the reset.
- Objective-list theory, with genuine achievement and real personal relationships as irreducible goods, must recommend staying out, but specifically because the ninety-day fragmentation undermines the narrative continuity required for those goods to be genuinely possessed, not merely because the experiences are simulated.
- Actual-desire satisfaction theory (present, first-order desires) must recommend entering, because the subject's current desires are fulfilled; informed-global-desire theory (idealized, fully informed desires including knowledge of the reset) must recommend staying out, because the informed desire includes the desire for narrative authorship.

Third, identify the exact structural feature doing the work — the decoupling of first-order experiential content from second-order autobiographical integration — and explain what happens if it is removed: does the case collapse back into a standard experience-machine scenario, and do the theories then converge or diverge differently?

Fourth, present the honest defense that an objective-list theorist could mount — that the achievements and relationships inside the machine are still real enough, or that narrative continuity is not on the objective list — and reply with the feature of the case that blocks that defense.

Fifth, state explicitly what this case is designed for: testing whether well-being theories treat narrative identity as a constituent good, an instrumental condition, or an irrelevant background factor, without relying on the generic unreality objection that dominates standard discussions of the experience machine.

Constraints: keep the scenario under one paragraph; do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; do not invoke theories or experiments too niche for a small model to know by name; and do not use verbatim quotations from any real text.
