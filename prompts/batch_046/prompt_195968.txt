Having recently retired from experimental physics and begun reading metaphysics, I want to understand the adequacy conditions for a theory of material constitution before I evaluate any particular view. Take the canonical case: a statue formed from a lump of clay, where the two entities seem spatially coincident yet diverge in their survival conditions—the statue perishes when squashed into a ball, the clay persists. I am asking not for a survey of named theories, but for the full specification of what an adequate account must deliver. Write the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain (spatial coincidence, differential persistence conditions, the grounding of modal properties in physical parts), the distinctions it must preserve (between being identical to one's parts and being constituted by them, between enduring through change and perduring via temporal parts), the puzzles it must dissolve or at least accommodate (the problem of temporary intrinsics, the problem of the many, and the tension with physical descriptions that deny sharply bounded macroscopic objects), and the constraints it must respect—distinguishing non-negotiable conditions from camp-specific preferences (such as ontological parsimony, determinacy of composition relations, or compatibility with four-dimensionalism). For each requirement, provide the motivating case or consideration behind it. Then identify whether any subset of these desiderata appears jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether the demand for genuinely distinct coincident objects can be reconciled with unrestricted mereological composition and with the absence of arbitrary modal differences. Finally, indicate which single desideratum does the most work in excluding the best-known candidates, such as standard mereological essentialism or constitution-without-identity accounts. Present the answer in American English, in a formal, polished register suitable for philosophical analysis. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or author-specific citations; I want the conceptual architecture, not the literature map.
