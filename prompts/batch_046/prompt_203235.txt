Quick consistency check for a paper I'm drafting on moral agregation and cross-kind harm. I hold: (1) one act of torture is lexically worse than any finite number of dust-speck harms, no matter the scale; (2) moral reasons only aggregate within a "kind-domain", so comparing torture and dust-specks is blocked because they're different kinds; (3) I reject the repugnant conclusion on average-utilitarian grounds; (4) if I reject total aggregation across people, I can't justify saving five over one; (5) but I still clearly save the five. Do these actually hang together? I suspect (2) is smuggling in a hidden metaphysics of harm-kinds that I don't defend anywhere else, and that (4) misreads what rejecting total utility commits me to. Rank the tensions by severity, not all equal, and for each say the cheapest fix and what it costs. End with a line that starts Verdict: naming the single commitment I should drop if I want the least damage overall.
