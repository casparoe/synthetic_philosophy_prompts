Write a dialogue between an absurdist and a narrative-coherence theorist about whether keeping a journal of totally random, unconnected events can still carry meaning. The absurdist should defend the practice—maybe call it revolt in ink—saying that the very pointlessness frees us from the trap of narrative. The coherence theorist should push back that selecting entries, ordering them by date, and re-reading them smuggles in structure no matter how chaotic the content claims to be. They should actually respond to eachother's points, not talk past each other. Include a moment where the absurdist concedes that physical tiredness from writing might limit the quantity of experience they value, and let the coherence theorist use that to argue that even absurdism relies on embodied continuity. Write it casually, like a transcript with filler, and don't worry about perfect spelling—feel free to drop "recieve" or "thier" in there if it feels right.
