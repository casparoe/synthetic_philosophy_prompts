Please rank the following five “boundary regimes” in three tiers — Tier 1 (most defensible), Tier 2 (partially defensible but carrying a serious internal tension), and Tier 3 (conceptually muddled or easily co-opted) — according to how well each balances stratigraphic legibility with resistance to state and corporate narrative capture, while still attributing moral responsibility with reasonable precision.

The regimes are:
A. The Orbis hypothesis marker (c. 1610), ratified solely by the International Commission on Stratigraphy.
B. The Industrial Revolution marker (c. 1784), ratified by interdisciplinary consensus among climate historians and earth-system scientists.

D. The plutonium fallout peak (c. 1964), legitimised not by geologists but by a hypothetical “planetary parliament” including representatives of non-human ecosystems and future generations, with no veto power for nation-states.
E. A “rolling boundary” defined by local ecological collapse events, aggregated by an algorithmic monitoring network without human deliberation.

For each regime placed in a tier, explain in one or two sentences the specific tension or virtue that justifies its position. Write your response in British English, using British spellings and punctuation conventions. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; I am asking only for conceptual evaluation.
