I've been messing around with Hofstadter's idea that the self is just a strange loop — a symbol 'I' that keeps referring back to the brain that generates it. Lately I've been wondering if the loop is actually porus and extends past the skull: my notebooks, digital traces, even other people recognizing me could all be part of the feedback circuit, so the 'I' isn't locked inside one head. What would genuinely count against this extended loop view? Not just generic "you're reducing the self" objections — I mean specific defeaters. Like, could split-brain cases prove that symbolic closure can't be distributed? Or if self-reference works perfectly in total isolation, does that kill the external-feedback part? Rank them for me: fatal vs just awkward vs things a defender would definately dismiss. Also, does this view risk predicting something weird — say, that erasing all my external traces should actually weaken my selfhood — that other theories of personal identity don't?
