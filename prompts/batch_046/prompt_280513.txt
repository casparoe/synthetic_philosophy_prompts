ok so i've been stuck on this debate about whether qualia are basically incoherent in the way Chase and Sanborn suggest — like, try to pin them down and they dissolve into either functional descriptions or mysterious leftover properties that dont do explanatory work. One camp says the concept is perfectly coherent, we just have direct acquaintance and thats the end of it. The other camp — the quiners — say every attempt to describe a quale ends up describing its causal role or relational profile, so theres no intrinsic residue left.

This has been going in circles since at least the 80s and nobody actually moves. I'm trying to figure out if its a verbal dispute about what 'qualia' denotes, or if theres a hidden empirical claim about neural processing, or if its bedrock intuition about first-person authority vs third-person explanation. But I also keep wondering if part of the stall is that both sides treat 'coherence' differently — one side means logical consistency, the other means explanatory indispensability within physicalism — and they talk past each other because they never agree which standard applies. Maybe its even deeper: maybe one side is treating qualia as a kind term like 'electron' and the other as a failed kind term like 'phlogiston', and thats the hidden structure.

What kind of disagreement is this really? Is there a distinction — say, about whether mental concepts need causal efficacy to be legitimate, or whether direct acquaintance counts as evidence — that would actually unstick it? Or is the impasse a sign the question is ill-posed, with people answering different things under one word? I want a diagnosis that engages what each side actually says, not just a survey, plus an honest take on whether anything — a formal distinction, a weird case like partial zombies with inverted spectra but identical behavior except in aesthetic judgment, whatever — could move it, or if we should just accept no progress is possible and explain why.
