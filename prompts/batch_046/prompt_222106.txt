Having retired from statistical mechanics, I have recently begun reading about bounded rationality. Herbert Simon's notion of satisficing seems analogous to a system reaching a local free-energy minimum: the aspiration level acts as an entropic boundary, and once crossed, no further thermodynamic work is required. From this viewpoint, the distinction between satisficing and optimising appears to dissolve into a single problem of equilibrium states in neural tissue. Does this not imply that bounded rationality is merely an engineering account of heat dissipation, lacking any genuinely philosophical content about practical reason? Or is there a normative dimension that survives even when the decision process is described in purely physical terms?
