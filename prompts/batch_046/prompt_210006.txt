Hey, I'm a retired marine biologist who got into philosphy after retirement—mostly because my granddaughter kept asking "what IS a fish, really?" and I realized I had never thought about it. I'm in an intro course on ancient thought now, and we just covered Plato's cave and that Forms business. I cooked up an argument that seems to show the whole cave escape is pointless, but I can't find the crack. Here it is, numbered.

(1) Think of a Form—say Justice—as a perfect anolog signal with infinite resolution.
(2) Any physical thing that particpates in Justice is just a digital sample taken at some finite rate.
(3) The shadows in the cave are samples of those samples—like compressing an MP3 twice.
(4) There's a result in signal processing, the Nyquist-Shannon idea, that you can only reconstruct the original continuous signal if your sampling rate is more than double the highest frequency present.
(5) But a Form has every possible property in perfect degree, so its "frequency content" is unbounded—there's no highest frequency.
(6) So any finite sampling rate, whether you're studying a statue in sunlight or a shadow on the wall, falls below the Nyquist rate.
(7) That means neither the prisoner nor the escaped philosopher can recover information about the Form from what they percieve.
(8) So leaving the cave doesn't actually improve your epistemic position; you've only traded one unrecoverable sample for another.
(9) But Plato clearly thinks the philosopher gains real knowledge. So something here must be wrong.

If I have to pick, I'd like to keep (2) and (3) because the copy-of-a-copy picture feels right to me, but I'm not sure what I pay for dropping (4) or (5). Is there an equivocation hiding between (6) and (7) about what "recover information" means? Or is (5) just a category mistake, treating metaphysical perfection like a radio signal? Tell me which step to reject and what the cost is.
