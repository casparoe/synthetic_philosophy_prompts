I've come to accept what I call the double-inversion thesis: that Darwin's demonstration of design without a designer and Turing's demonstration of computation without cognition are not parallel curiosities but two expressions of a single metaphysical priority — competence is always prior to comprehension, and understanding is never an original ground of successful performance but only a retrospective reconstruction of mechanisms that already worked. I take this to apply not just to evolution and machines, but to human scientific and everyday reasoning itself. What would count against this thesis?

I want more than a list of standard objections. Rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward: which empirical results (in developmental psychology, neuroscience, or the evaluation of artificial systems), which conceptual arguments (about intentionality, about the causal direction of explanation, about whether comprehension can be epiphenomenal), and which internal tensions (for instance, between Darwinian contingency and the determinism of formal computation) would genuinely threaten the thesis? For each, say whether a committed defender of the double inversion could concede it as damaging, or whether they could absorb it by redescription.

In particular: are there clear cases in which comprehension causally precedes and directs competence, not just in narrative but in mechanism? Does the thesis risk insulating itself into unfalsifiability by treating every apparent case of prior understanding as itself the output of hidden prior competence — and if so, is that insulation a virtue or a vice for a claim of this kind? And what specific predictions does the thesis make about domain-general artificial competence that distinguish it from standard functionalism or from emergentist views?

Please separate what is relatively settled in the philosophy of mind, cognitive science, and history of science literature on these inversions from what remains genuinely contested — for example, about the exact scope of Turing's claims regarding machine intelligence, or about whether Darwin's argument eliminates teleology entirely or only relocates it. Do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus view.
