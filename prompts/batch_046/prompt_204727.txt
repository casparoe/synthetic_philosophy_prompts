Hey - retired condensed matter physicist here, got into phil of science and AI ethics since I stopped working full time. I've been reading about the so-called alignment problem for about a year and I'm frustrated in a very specific way. Everyone talks about "the alignment problem" like it's one puzzle, but half the papers I see treat it as an engineering control issue (reward hacking, distributional shift, etc.) and the other half treat it like a deep philosophical question about whether an artificial system can share human values in any real sense. And the debate hasn't moved an inch - same camps, same talking past each other.

My working hunch is that "alignment" is doing double or triple duty and we're making a category mistake: in physics we align lasers or detectors, which is purely technical, but here people smuggle in normative questions about what values even are. So is the stall happening because people are answering different questions under one label? Or is there a hidden substantive disagreement inside - maybe about whether intelligence and values are genuinely seperate, or about what counts as proof that a system is aligned? I want an honest diagnosis with reasons: is this verbal, is it about standards of evidence, or is it bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for? And please don't hedge - take a definite position. Is this really one unsolved problem, or should we split it into several named problems? If split, what should we call them? Defend your answer.
