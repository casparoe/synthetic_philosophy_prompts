Construct a decision-tree taxonomy of interpretations of Buddhist anatta (no-self) as they bear on personal identity over time, intended for readers who do not already know the school labels. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of named views: each internal node must be a concrete claim or case on which interpretations genuinely come apart, phrased so that someone can answer it without recognizing philosophical names. Begin with a root split between (A) anatta as the denial only of a permanent, unitary, metaphysically substantial self (atman), leaving room for conventional diachronic identity, and (B) anatta as the denial of any meaningful diachronic personal identity whatsoever. From branch A, split on what kind of conventional continuity remains: psychological-stream continuity across aggregates, causal-aggregate dependence without unified psychological continuity, narrative construction over aggregates, or purely pragmatic convention with no ontological basis. From branch B, split on whether the absence of identity is local (no self at any single time) or global (no self across times), and then on whether conventional self-reference is pragmatically indispensable, merely conventional in a deflationary sense, or actively misleading. Introduce a concrete branching case on rebirth without a transmigrating soul: if a being's karmic-causal stream divides after death so that two future beings each inherit causal traces, is there one successor, two successors, or no determinate answer? Use this case to separate positions that treat the stream as divisible from those that require unitary continuity. At each leaf, place either a named position (for example, a Theravada Abhidharma reductionist view, a Madhyamaka conventionalist view, a Yogacara storehouse-consciousness view, or a contemporary Buddhist eliminativist position) or the label 'unnamed,' followed by one sentence stating the view's most awkward commitment. Where two named positions yield identical answers on all branching questions, flag them together as indistinguishable by this tree rather than inventing a false split. Include at least one logically possible combination of answers that is unoccupied, and briefly diagnose whether its emptiness is due to incoherence, extreme unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The tree should have between eight and twelve leaves. Present it in outline form, with each node labeled by its splitting question and the leaves arranged beneath their parent branches. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not ask the reader to identify positions by school name alone.
