You are a professional philosopher of social choice and institutional design. Before answering, take a moment to brainstorm the central tension between comparative aggregation mechanisms and norms of non-comparative evaluation.

Consider the standard practice in competitive academic peer review: reviewers assign cardinal scores—say, 1 to 10—across dimensions like originality, rigor, and significance. The program committee then converts these into complete ordinal rankings per reviewer and applies a Borda-count aggregation rule to produce a single ranked list of submissions, with hard acceptance and rejection cutoffs. The institution's published ethics code instructs reviewers to assess each submission entirely on its own merits, forbidding explicit comparison with other papers, and classifies strategic score manipulation—such as depressing the scores of direct competitors—as research misconduct. At the same time, the institution permits private deliberation among committee members before final scores are submitted, and it allows authors to suggest preferred reviewers.

What philosophical commitments about evaluation, preference, and fairness does this practice implicitly presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory: does it treat merit as isolable and non-comparative, even though the Borda rule is irreducibly comparative? Does it assume that strategy-proofness can be secured by normative prohibition rather than by mechanism design—despite the Gibbard-Satterthwaite theorem showing that any deterministic, non-dictatorial rule over three or more alternatives is vulnerable to manipulation? Distinguish strictly what the practice requires from what merely happens to fit it well. Identify where the practice is philosophically underdetermined: for example, could a defender consistently maintain that the Borda transformation is only a pragmatic heuristic, or that hidden comparison in private discussion is acceptable? Finally, explain what concrete institutional changes would be necessary if the practice genuinely acknowledged that sincere evaluation in isolation is incompatible with any deterministic ranking over three or more submissions.
