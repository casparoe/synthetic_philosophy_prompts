I'm preparing the interview outline for a podcast episode with a philosopher of social science who has recently defended a revised Weberian thesis about culture and economic development. Rather than arguing that Protestant doctrine reshaped individual economic psychology from the inside out, she proposes what she calls the "filter thesis": religious values functioned not as direct motivations but as institutional selectors, restructuring local honor economies so that capital accumulation became legible as social virtue. This normative recognition, in turn, unlocked access to communal risk-pooling arrangements—what she terms "honor-credit nodes"—while suppressing competing economic practices that could not secure the same status-based endorsement. On her account, culture causes institutional outcomes not by pushing agents toward specific acts, but by altering the cost-structure of deviation through mechanisms of collective sanction and reward.

I want eight to ten questions that engage the actual mechanics of this view rather than asking her to restate Weber. Please include at least one question that presses the standard materialist counter-argument—that underlying economic conditions shaped both the religious ethics and the institutional outcomes—followed by a sharp follow-up keyed to her likely reply that correlation is not causation. I also want a question on how collective values can do causal work without being reducible to individual mental states, with a follow-up that tests whether her answer commits her to methodological holism or to some form of reification; another that asks whether her "honor-credit nodes" are empirically distinguishable from ordinary interest-group politics or class-based coalition building, with a follow-up specifying what pattern of evidence would count against the cultural-filter explanation; and a final question that pushes the normative implication—if culture operates as a causal filter, does her framework license deliberate cultural engineering to foster economic development, and if she resists that inference, what exactly blocks it? Avoid generic biographical openings; every question should anticipate the defensive move she is most likely to make and press past it.
