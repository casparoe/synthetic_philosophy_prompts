I serve on my university's Committee on Ethical Formation and Curriculum, and by Thursday we must issue a recommendation on whether to approve a pilot required first-year seminar titled Foundations of Tranquil Practice. The course is built around what its designers call the asymptotic tranquility thesis: that human well-being is not a positive state to be achieved but rather the diminishing integral of craving and suffering over time, asymptotically approaching a zero-state in which only the absence of disturbance carries value. Proponents argue that this offers students a rigorous, non-instrumental framework for mental discipline; critics contend that it institutionalizes a tranquilist view that denies intrinsic value to achievement or striving, making it incompatible with our university's pluralism mandate and likely to undermine the motivational structure necessary for intellectual virtue. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—full approval, rejection, or conditional approval with specified modifications and a clear review timeline—and should include reasoning that engages seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side, specifically the worry that framing education around the elimination rather than the cultivation of desire erodes the conditions for sustained inquiry. Finally, please state explicitly what argument or evidence would change your recommendation, and identify the point in your reasoning that is most likely to fail.
