Please rank the following five accounts of weakness of will—specifically in cases where hyperbolic discounting produces a preference reversal upon the introduction of a minor external commitment device, without any reported change in the agent's underlying values—by how well they explain the pattern of reversal-without-revision. Assign each account to one of four tiers: excellent fit, adequate fit, strained fit, or poor fit, and justify each placement in a single sentence. The accounts are: (1) the divided-self model, in which a present-biased self temporarily dominates a long-term self; (2) the judgment-shift model, in which the agent genuinely revises her all-things-considered judgment at the moment of action; (3) the evaluative-inattention model, in which the agent fails to activate her prior evaluation without altering its content; (4) the structural-binding-failure model, in which a prior resolution lacks causal efficacy not because of competing desire but because of a disconnect between deliberative and executive systems that commitment devices temporarily bridge; and (5) the temporal-framing model, in which hyperbolic discounting changes the agent's representation of the choice problem itself rather than merely reweighting outcomes within a stable frame. Treat the following case as decisive: a smoker places her cigarettes in a time-locked container, reports that her craving has disappeared and that she values health over smoking, yet when the container accidentally opens ten minutes early she smokes immediately and later describes the urge as the same craving made suddenly salient, insisting her values never changed. Which tier does each account fall into for this case, and which account best predicts that removing the device restores not just the old strength of desire but the old representation of the choice?
