Write a formal taxonomy—structured as a branching decision tree—classifying philosophical positions on Quine’s thesis of the indeterminacy of translation, using the “gavagai” scenario as the central diagnostic case and one constructed extension of it. The instrument should serve an advanced philosophy-of-language audience and be navigable without prior knowledge of the labels: each internal node must pose a concrete claim or case that a respondent can answer yes or no without recognizing which leaf it points toward. Build the tree from the logical space of commitments, not from a pre-existing roster of named schools.

Begin with this root commitment: radical translation is constrained exclusively by observable speech behavior—patterns of assent and dissent in response to stimuli—with no access to private mental states.

From that root, construct the following branching structure. First split (Node 1): in the standard “gavagai” case, where a speaker utters the term upon the appearance of a rabbit, do the available behavioral data leave reference genuinely underdetermined among enduring rabbits, temporal rabbit-stages, undetached rabbit-parts, and pluralities?

If the answer is No—reference is behaviorally fixed—proceed to Node 2: is the determinacy secured by an empirical constraint (causal-historical chains linking uses to the world, or shared neurobiological architecture) or by a rational-normative constraint (charity, coherence norms, or rationality conditions that transcend behavior)?

If the answer to Node 1 is Yes—reference is underdetermined—proceed to Node 3: does this indeterminacy extend to interpretation within one’s own language (“indeterminacy begins at home”), or is it confined to radical translation?

If the answer to Node 3 is that it is confined to radical translation, place the leaf there. If it extends home, proceed to Node 4: can analytical hypotheses—adjustments to the translation manual that preserve overall coherence of belief attribution—ever uniquely resolve the ambiguity without altering any behavioral predictions?

If the answer to Node 4 is Yes, place a leaf for holistic resolvability. If No, proceed to Node 5, which introduces the niche extension: the “shadow-gavagai calibration” case. Here the speaker consistently corrects others who apply the term to rabbit-shadows, and also corrects applications to rabbits that are present but fully occluded. Does this error-correction behavior break the underdetermination by uniquely privileging the enduring-object ontology over stage or part ontologies?

Answer Yes at Node 5 yields one leaf; answer No yields another.

This tree should produce six occupied leaves corresponding to the paths above. In addition, you must explicitly include two further combinations that are logically available but unoccupied on this tree—specifically, (i) determinacy in radical translation combined with indeterminacy at home, and (ii) underdetermination resolved solely by analytical hypotheses without extending to home-language interpretation. For each, assign the leaf the label “unnamed / unoccupied,” state in one sentence why the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and diagnose whether it is contradictory, arbitrarily restricted, or vacant.

Every leaf—occupied or not—must carry a descriptive name (or “unnamed”), a one-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment, and a brief note on whether anyone actually defends it. Where your tree collapses two genuinely distinct views into the same leaf—such as views that differ only in motivation but share all answers—flag the pair explicitly and explain why the diagnostic questions cannot separate them.

Keep all descriptions paraphrastic: do not put any real text in quotation marks, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific works. Phrase every node as a question or claim a respondent could engage with without already knowing which interpretation of Quine it maps onto.
