Compose a sustained satirical piece in the form of a deadpan internal corporate memorandum—approximately 700 words, bearing the letterhead and procedural voice of a fictional multinational firm’s Office of Relational Governance, addressed to all divisional heads—announcing that the organization has adopted care ethics as its sole and exclusive framework for institutional decision-making. The satire should target the specific tendency of care-ethical reasoning, once formalized, to dissolve fixed procedural fairness and terminal accountability into an unending demand for contextual, relational attentiveness: every resolution must be reopened in light of previously unacknowledged emotional dependencies, so that no rule can stand and no conflict can be finally settled. To ground the parody in recognizable conceptual terrain without relying on citation or named authorship, have the memorandum allude—without quotation marks or bibliographic reference—to the standard distinction between direct, particular caring-for and more diffuse caring-about, and to the familiar critique of universalist moral reasoning as insufficiently attentive to concrete relationships. At the same time, inject a novel, niche bureaucratic mechanism—designate it the Relational Recursion Protocol—that operationalizes this dissolution: under the Protocol, any care-based resolution to a workplace dispute automatically triggers a mandatory secondary audit of the emotional labor invested in reaching that resolution, and that audit is itself classified as an act of caring that requires further audit, generating an administratively mandated infinite regress. The voice must remain earnestly administrative—precise, procedural, and entirely devoid of irony or winking—so that the absurdity arises strictly from the internal consistency of the logic rather than from exaggeration invented out of whole cloth. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific scholarly texts; the piece should be fully intelligible without access to external sources.
