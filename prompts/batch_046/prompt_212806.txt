Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain—taking as its conceptual spine Moritz Schlick’s 1927 argument in Vom Sinn des Lebens that the question “what is the meaning of life?” rests on a category mistake: it treats biological purpose (Zweck) as if it could supply an overall teleological point, whereas genuine meaning (Sinn) arises only in “play” (Spiel), that is, in activity that carries its own value independently of any extraneous goal. Use two refrains that alternate through the tercets: the first should voice the teleological fallacy (the insistence that life must be justified by a purpose outside itself), the second should voice Schlick’s counterclaim that meaning is intrinsic and that “youth” is not a chronological stage but a permanent possibility of playing. Let the form do philosophical work: the cyclical return of the refrains should mirror the way the purpose-question keeps reasserting itself despite being emptied, while the final quatrain—where both refrains appear together—should enact the transformation Schlick describes, in which the drive that sought an extraneous end is not fulfilled but dissolved into activity valued for its own sake, so that the two lines no longer oppose but reveal that meaning was never at the terminus but in the playing itself. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with Schlick’s ethics of youth recognizes the distinction between instrumental work and self-justifying play, and should leave the tension open rather than resolving it into a sermon: Schlick offers not a doctrine but a change of attitude.
