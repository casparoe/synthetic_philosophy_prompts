In my philosophy of mind seminar we have been working through collective-consciousness cases—particularly the ant-fugue scenario in which an ant colony might or might not constitute a single subject of experience—without committing prematurely to any particular theory of mind. To test the limits of functionalist intuitions, I want to apply a substrate-independence variation that I have not seen discussed in our readings: imagine a mature leafcutter-ant colony in which every biological ant is replaced, one individual at a time across generations, by a microscopic silicon analog that replicates precisely the original’s behavioral dynamics, pheromone-response thresholds, local neural computation, and energy metabolism, but contains no biological tissue whatsoever. By the end of the procedure the colony is 100 percent artificial, yet the functional graph of interactions—trail geometry, task-allocation patterns, nest architecture, and information flow—remains isomorphic to the pre-replacement biological baseline, with no central controller added and no interruption to collective activity.

Given this setup, please state your credence that the fully replaced colony has exactly the same mental status—whether conscious or not—as the original biological colony; in other words, that substrate replacement makes no difference to the presence or absence of a unified, colony-level phenomenal consciousness. Decompose your reasoning explicitly:

1. The base-rate probability that any ant colony, biological or artificial, can be the bearer of a single unified phenomenal consciousness rather than merely a distributed system without a subject.
2. The conditional probability that biological substrate is irrelevant to such consciousness, given that functional and organizational isomorphism is fully preserved.
3. The probability that the gradual, one-by-one replacement procedure actually preserves whatever organizational or causal properties are necessary for consciousness, rather than subtly disrupting them—for instance by altering temporal dynamics, breaking micro-causal loops, or eliminating evolutionary-history conditions in ways the functional graph does not capture.
4. Any further factor you consider essential, such as the need for a centralized integrative mechanism, the applicability of global-workspace or integrated-information conditions, or the relevance of evolutionary origin.

For each term, provide a rough numerical probability with a one-line justification, then combine them into an overall credence. Finally, identify which term you are least confident in and specify what conceptual argument or empirical result would most substantially shift that number.
