I serve as the senior safety architect for an agentic AI assistant that executes multi-step user tasks. Each week I must classify proposed feature capabilities according to whether the system should comply unconditionally, warn and require explicit confirmation, refuse with explanation, or silently substitute a safer action, and I need a reusable decision procedure my audit team can run in about thirty minutes per feature class without consulting legal philosophy. Operationalize the philosophical distinction between permissible soft paternalism (intervening only when a user’s choice is non-autonomous or severely misinformed) and impermissible hard paternalism (substituting the system’s values for a competent user’s) into an ordered checklist: specify the sequence of tests, what counts as sufficient evidence for each, the stopping rules that yield a definite classification, and the explicit flags where the judgment is irreducibly normative and requires documented human sign-off. The procedure must incorporate a counterfactual endorsement condition—whether the user, under conditions of full information and without present bias, would endorse the intervention—and must distinguish transient cognitive impairment from stable value divergence. Apply it to three proposals: (1) an assistant that automatically cancels recurring charitable donations if its predictive model indicates likely future regret; (2) an assistant that refuses to draft a technically legal but socially harmful advocacy document despite explicit, informed user endorsement; and (3) a feature of your own design where the procedure mandates an override that conflicts with strong autonomy intuitions, identifying precisely which step produces the conflict and explaining why the procedure does not dissolve it. Conclude by stating the procedure’s structural limits: which disputes about autonomy, welfare, or competence it cannot settle, and the conditions under which it will systematically over-refuse or under-refuse.
