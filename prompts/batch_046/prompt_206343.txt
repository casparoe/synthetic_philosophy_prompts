I'm working through my views on the regress problem for a seminar paper and I want to see if I'm acutally consistent or just hoarding positions I like. Here is what I hold: (1) Foundationalism feels right in that there needs to be a stopping point somewhere, but I don't think basic beliefs are infallible—just that in a given context they don't need further backing. (2) At the same time I think coherentism captures something real about mutual support, and I'm okay with belief loops as long as they're big and diverse enough, so maybe a weak loop version works. (3) But I'm also pulled toward infinitism because I think you can always ask 'why?' and there's no principled reason to ever stop, so I reject both the foundation and the loop as final. (4) And I think knowledge is contextualist—whether a belief counts as basic depends on what questions are live in the conversation, not on some absolute feature of the belief.

So do these hang together? Specifically, does my contextualist move in (4) save me from the contradiction between wanting a stopping point in (1) and rejecting all stopping points in (3), or is it just papering over the conflict? Is my weak loop in (2) compatible with infinitism, or am I smuggling circular justifcation that infinitism should reject? What's the worst tension, and what's the least costly revision—dropping contextualism, weakening the infinitist commitment, or admitting my 'foundational' beliefs are just temporarily paused? I don't need a full lecture on all three theories, just an audit of where my mess falls apart.
