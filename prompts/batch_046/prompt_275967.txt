I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A at an AI alignment workshop, and the panelist is an unnamed researcher defending a particular formalization of low-impact AI. Her position is that the requirement to avoid side effects can be defined without a full specification of human values by using a purely structural measure she calls counterfactual footprint: specifically, the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the actual trajectory of physical state variables and a counterfactual trajectory in which the agent's actions are replaced by null actions but background dynamics proceed unchanged. She claims that because the divergence is computed over unweighted physical-state distributions—energy configurations, particle positions—rather than over valued outcomes, it does not smuggle in axiological commitments.

Draft a sequence of interview questions that presses this position at its actual weak points rather than letting her restate it. I want an opening question on why computing divergence over physical states does not implicitly privilege the particular state-space representation we happen to find salient, with a follow-up anticipating her likely appeal to invariance under coordinate transformation. Then a second question on how she selects the baseline counterfactual dynamics without importing normative judgments about which processes count as background and which as interference, with a follow-up for the reply that the distinction can be made causally, not normatively. Next, a third question on whether the metric collapses into practical uselessness in high-dimensional state spaces where any localized action produces massive global divergence, and what this implies for the feasibility of low-impact action, with a follow-up anticipating her distinction between global divergence and localized causal footprint. Finally, ask what specific empirical failure in a simulated or real-world deployment—for instance, a case where the metric approves an action that intuitively causes severe harm, or rejects a benign action—would cause her to abandon the structural approach rather than merely recalibrating the state-space representation. Keep the questions concrete, avoid generic biographical prompts, and do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
