I'm putting together the final for my phil mind seminar and I want one essay qustion on the extended mind thesis, but not the usual parity-based recap everyone memorizes. Instead, I want students to tackle this idea I'm calling 'normative parity': an external tool counts as part of cognition not just when it plays the same functional role as internal memory, but when the agent's entitlement to rely on it is structurally identical to their entitlement to rely on an internal belief-forming mechanism — including being blameworthy for errors. The exam qustion should ask whether this criterion actually draws a stable boundary or if it collapses back into functional parity once you unpack what 'entitlement' means in a distributed system. I need the qustion itself, a model answer around 700 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points totaling 20 marks. Each point should list the mark weight and a one-line note on the typical wrong turn (like mixing up causal coupling with normative accountability). Don't ask for bibliographies or citations — students won't have acess to texts in the exam room anyway. Keep the tone casual.
