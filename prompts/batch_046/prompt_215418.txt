Please describe the most important implications of Nozick's rejection of epistemic closure—on his tracking account, knowing that p and knowing that p entails q does not automatically yield knowledge that q—for the practice of long-horizon belief aggregation in forecasting and decision-making communities. In particular, consider cases where a group accepts a well-tracked base claim (say, about the scaling properties of a particular class of models) and then derives, through a multi-step theoretical inference, a far-reaching conclusion about risk or alignment outcomes. If the intermediate steps are not themselves tracked by the individuals or by the collective procedure—because they depend on contested modeling assumptions rather than direct sensitivity-tracking—what does this imply for whether the derived belief counts as knowledge? Address specifically: (1) whether collective epistemic closure can be restored by distributing tracking conditions across members; (2) how this affects the legitimacy of inside-view reasoning that extends from first principles through long inferential chains; (3) whether the failure of closure here is merely a technicality or a deep challenge to how rationalist-style belief-updating norms treat derived conclusions; and (4) what procedural adjustments, if any, could allow a group to legitimately hold the derived belief without violating tracking conditions. Rank these implications by how severely they undermine current norms, and note which ones depend on accepting Nozick's particular counterfactual conditions versus more general anti-closure intuitions.
