In my philosophy of tech seminar we've hit a wall debating virtual harms—say, losing a rare item in an MMO or being harrassed in VR. One camp says these are real harms because they involve real emotional investment and social standing; the other says they're fake because the object or space can be reset with a patch. Whats frustrating is that both sides cite the same player-psychology studies, so it's not empirical. I think the stall might come from them using different criteria for when a harm is world-involving versus just representation-involving, but I'm not sure if thats the root or just a symptom. Could the real disagreement be about whether virtual objects have modal persistence conditions—i.e., whether they depend on server-state continuty in a way that makes harm to them structurally different from harm in fiction? Or is this just bedrock intuition about the real world? Diagnose the stall for me, and tell me whether a formal distinction, an experiment, or changing the question is the right move.
