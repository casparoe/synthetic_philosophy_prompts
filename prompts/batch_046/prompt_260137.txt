everyone in the collapse-studies threads i hang out in keeps comparing a society falling apart to a tree falling in a forest — one minute its standing, then theres a crack and its down, and the old shape is permanently gone. they use it to license the idea that the Bronze Age or the Classic Maya was a real 'death,' not just people moving to smaller villages and changing how they farm. audit the analogy for me: map the roots, trunk, the crack, the rot onto whatever social parts they think correspond, then show where it breaks — especially with Easter Island, where people stayed put but the big statue-building just stopped. does the tree picture quietly import the idea that any big institutional change equals failure? and if we replace it with a corporation filing for chapter 11 — assets split, some branches keep running, the brand dies but the workers don't — does that just rebrand collapse, or does it actually change what we're counting as a society 'dying'?
