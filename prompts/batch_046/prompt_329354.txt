I am designing a diagnostic tool for an advanced philosophy of religion seminar that sorts interpretations of Wittgenstein's philosophy of religious belief according to the actual commitments they make, rather than by the scholarly labels they carry. Please construct a decision tree that begins from a single shared premise—that religious belief is not a deficient or preliminary scientific hypothesis but operates in its own grammatical space—and proceeds through four to six branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete yes/no or either/or question that a reader could answer without knowing which interpretation it selects. The leaves should be named interpretive positions that genuinely occupy distinct regions of this logical space; include at least the fideist isolationist view, the grammatical pluralist view, the picture-guided practice view, and the framework-judgment quietist view, arranging them according to the path of answers that reaches each.

Crucially, inject a distinction that introductory treatments usually collapse: split the treatment of religious pictures (Bilder) according to whether they function as unshakeable framework judgments—in the specific sense of Wittgenstein's later remarks on certainty, where the picture makes other judgments possible—or as deeply embedded but in principle revisable practices within a form of life. Use this distinction either to divide one standard interpretation into two separate leaves, or to generate an unoccupied combination of answers that you briefly diagnose as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. For every leaf, state the exact combination of answers that leads to it. Explicitly flag any pair of interpretations that your tree cannot genuinely separate—where they share all branch answers but differ only in emphasis or rhetorical framing—rather than inventing a spurious distinction. Also note any logical combination of answers that yields no named position, explaining briefly why the cell is empty.

Present your response in the following sections: (1) The Tree Structure—display the tree with nodes labeled as questions and leaves as named positions, with the answer path for each leaf noted (approximately 350–450 words); (2) Diagnostic Logic—explain, for each branch node, why the positions on either side genuinely diverge on a concrete feature of religious language or practice, not merely on taxonomy (approximately 250–350 words); (3) Located Positions and Empty Cells—list each occupied leaf with its path, then list unoccupied combinations with a brief diagnosis of whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or lack of exploration (approximately 200–300 words); and (4) Unseparable Pairs and Limits—name any interpretations the tree collapses together and specify what further case or conceptual distinction would be needed to split them (approximately 150–250 words). Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; do not ask me to verify sources; and do not include verbatim quotations from Wittgenstein's texts—paraphrase any textual reference without quotation marks.
