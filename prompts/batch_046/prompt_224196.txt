I've been reading some older philosophy of physics mixed with contemporary consciousness papers, and I keep noticing an argumentative move that I can't quite place. Someone starts by pointing out that quantum mechanics makes it hard to draw sharp lines between physical systems — entanglement means you can't always treat particles as having seperate, well-defined states independent of each other. Then they slide from that physical point to a claim about minds: if the material world doesn't have firm boundaries, then the separation between 'my consciousness' and 'yours' must be an illusion too. They usually cite Schrödinger's writings where he links quantum unity to Vedanta, the idea that there's one Atman identical with Brahman underlying all individual appearances. But the version I'm seeing now isn't exactly Vedanta, and it's not Penrose-style quantum brain computation either.

So I'm wondering: is there an established name for this specific inference, where quantum non-separability is used as a direct premise for the metaphysical unity of consciousness across people? I don't just want a bare label — I want to know how it's different from cosmopsychism, from Advaita Vedanta on its own, and from generic mystical monism that doesn't invoke physics. Where are the edges? When does an argument count as this particular thing versus just saying 'everything is connected' in a vague way? If there's no standard term, what should we call it, and what are its closest relatives in the literature?
