Write a dialouge between Mara, a strict tranquilist who thinks only the absense of craving and suffering has real value, and Juno, who agrees suffering is uniquely bad but thinks Mara misses "directed absence" — not just lacking pain, but a life shaped by orientation toward something other than relief, like keeping up a friendship that isn't driven by need. Mara should argue that even this orientation is really a structural craving for coherence, and that Juno is smuggling in positive goods she claims to reject. Juno should then push back that Mara's definition of craving is so broad that tranquilism becomes unfalsifiable: any state Mara likes gets called "non-craving" and any state she dislikes gets called "hidden craving." Have them actually respond to each other's moves rather than talking past one another, and keep the focus tight — don't let them wander into general ethics. Casual wording is fine, and a typo or two is okay.
