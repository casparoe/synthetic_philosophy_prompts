In popular expositions of the Carter-Leslie doomsday argument, human birth rank is routinely compared to drawing a numbered ball from an urn of unknown size: if you draw ball seven, a container holding only ten balls suddenly looks more plausible than one holding a thousand, and analogously our relatively early position in the human sequence is taken to weigh against enormous future populations. Recently, some commentators have tried to strengthen the analogy by treating hypothetical digital minds or simulated observers as additional balls in the same urn, arguing that technological proliferation only enlarges the pool and therefore sharpens the inference to near-term extinction. I am trying to understand exactly where this mapping holds and where it collapses. Could you lay out the explicit correspondences—urn to reference class, draw to self-sampling, unknown size to total human or posthuman population—and then identify which features of the physical draw the analogy quietly imports that do not transfer to epistemic self-location? In particular, does treating artificial observers as further draws preserve the uniform probability assumption, or does it instead highlight that our sampling distribution may be systematically biased by the very conditions that allow us to ask the question? And once those disanalogies are on the table, can the urn picture still license any specific probabilistic conclusion about our future, or does it reduce to a heuristic with no determinate force?
