I've been working through the second essay of Nietzsche's Genealogy and I'm caught on what looks like a performative contradiction in his account of ressentiment. He describes slave morality as arising when the weak, unable to act, invent reactive values that function like a kind of moral immunodeficiency — producing normative "antibodies" that attack noble vitality rather than expressing their own strength. But if that's the case, then Nietzsche's own judgment that these values are "life-denying" or toxic already employs the reactive, negative evaluation characteristic of the slave. So isn't he implicitly operating within the very slave morality he claims to diagnose?

There's a further issue if we extend this "moral immunology" framing — treating dominant egalitarian norms as epidemiologically dangerous cultural pathogens. Once genealogy reveals these values not just as different but as literally suppressive of vital expression, doesn't the genealogist's task become therapeutic rather than descriptive? Should we understand Nietzsche as advocating a kind of normative quarantine — isolating these moral pathogens — rather than merely tracing their origin? Or does preserving the descriptive force of genealogy require abandoning the immunological metaphor altogether?
