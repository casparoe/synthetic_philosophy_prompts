Explain Hofstadter's strategy-stealing assumption and how it connects to superrationality in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma. Then say whether applying it to multi-agent RL with shared policy weights actually makes the argument stronger or just shifts it onto different ground.

Student answer:

The strategy-stealing assumption means that if two players use the exact same reasoning process, each one can effectively 'steal' the other's strategy and predict the move before it's made. So in the PD, if my reasoning leads me to defect, my twin opponent already sees that coming and defects too, giving us both the punishment payoff instead of the reward. That makes defection unstable, so cooperation is the only self-consistent choice—Hofstadter called this superrationality. When you apply this to RL agents that share weights, it's even stronger because they literally run the same neural network, so strategic transparency is built into the architecture. That should mean cooperation emerges automatically in self-play without any special reward shaping, since predicting the other agent is just running a forward pass on yourself. The limitation is just that biological humans don't share weights, so in practice the argument only applies to digital twins.
