Draft the joint statement that a micropsychist defending phenomenal-bonding combination and a cosmopsychist defending derivation-from-a-universal-subject could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration focused on the combination problem. Center the collaboration on the bridge-case: a hypothetical, physically unspecified scenario in which two initially isolated phenomenal fields are linked by a gradual, reversible informational bridge that eliminates informational isolation without generating a discrete boundary or any new unified representational content. Both parties should treat this case as a genuine crux, not merely an illustration.

Format the deliverable as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. In the agreed-claims section, include: (i) shared formal commitments about what the combination problem demands—specifically, that it requires explaining macro-level phenomenal properties from micro-level ones, not only macro-level functional properties; (ii) joint acceptance that the bridge-case is conceptually coherent and that its physical parameters are left open; (iii) neutrally phrased definitions of phenomenal bonding and cosmopsychist derivation that each side can endorse without conceding the debate; and (iv) a joint acknowledgment that phenomenal transparency—the direct accessibility of part-phenomenal-properties to a whole without representational mediation—functions as an unresolved criterion that both camps invoke but interpret in opposing ways.

In the disagreements table, present the precise formulations of each camp’s position, phrased so that each side would accept the wording applied to itself. Identify at least three cruxes, one of which must concern whether phenomenal transparency observed in the bridge-case supports bonding, derivation, or neither. For each crux, specify the argument, thought-experiment, or structural consideration that both sides agree would genuinely bear on it, and record each side’s explicit prediction about what that consideration would establish. Conclude the statement with a brief, jointly signed note indicating whether any portion of the dispute is merely verbal and, if so, which portion and why.

Maintain a formal, analytical tone throughout. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or appeals to named authorities. The document should be readable as a standalone text that neither party feels misrepresents its core commitments.
