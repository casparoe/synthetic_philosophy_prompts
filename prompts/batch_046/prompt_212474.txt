I am preparing a brief position paper for a metaphysics reading group and want an internal consistency check before I circulate it. My current package on individuation, symmetry, and structural realism is as follows:

(a) I accept a restricted Principle of Identity of Indiscernibles: for any two concrete particulars, if they share all intrinsic qualitative properties and all qualitative relational properties directed at entities outside the pair, then they are identical. I explicitly exclude irreflexive symmetric relations that hold only between the two objects themselves—such as being exactly two miles apart in a rotationally symmetric universe—from counting as individuating differences.

(b) I regard Black's two-sphere universe—two qualitatively identical spheres in an otherwise empty, rotationally symmetric space—as metaphysically possible, and I treat the spheres as numerically distinct.

(c) I deny that spatiotemporal separation is a fundamental individuating relation. On my view, location is derivative on an underlying metric field, and in a symmetric universe the field assigns both spheres the same structural role with no hidden asymmetry.

(d) I am drawn to a structural-realist account according to which concrete objects are nodes in a relational structure, and their identity conditions depend on asymmetries within that structure. In a perfectly symmetric two-node structure, I think there is no further fact of the matter about which node is which.

(e) I take quantum-mechanical cases of identical particles—say, two fermions in the same quantum state—to involve genuinely numerically distinct entities despite their complete qualitative symmetry, and I treat this as showing that nature permits distinctness without qualitative differentiation.

(f) I hold that conceivability is a fallible but genuine guide to metaphysical possibility, and I find the Black scenario clearly conceivable.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe is each tension? Specifically: does (b) collapse under pressure from (d), or can structural realism accommodate symmetric nodes? Does (e) support (b) by providing an actual-world analogy, or does it undermine (a) in ways that make (a) redundant? Does (c) render (a) idle, since if location is non-fundamental then no qualitative relation distinguishes the spheres anyway? Rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each the least costly revision, and tell me whether there is a single change that would resolve the most problems at once. Do not lecture me generally on PII or Black's spheres; I want the audit applied specifically to these commitments.
