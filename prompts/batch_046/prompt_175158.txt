In a seminar comparing gradated moral status across insects, partially brain-damaged humans, synthetic agents with simulated affective integration, and thermostats, someone invoked the line "sentience is a dimmer switch, not an on-off switch" to defend assigning continuous moral weights—perhaps 0.1 to an insect, 0.4 to a language model with partial affect, and 0 to a thermostat—rather than binary rights or none. I want to understand what this metaphor actually does. Can you lay out the distinct precise theses it could express: about phenomenal consciousness, about functional architecture, about the structure of moral obligations, and about the existence of a quantitative scale? For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, false, or just a denial of sharp boundaries without delivering a metric. Then say which of these readings actually supports grading synthetic minds against insects and thermostats, and whether any of them smuggle in a specific measurement framework—such as integrated information or global workspace capacity—that the slogan itself does not justify.
