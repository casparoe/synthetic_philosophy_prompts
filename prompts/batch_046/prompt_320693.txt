hey, I'm prepping for a dept seminar talk defending this taxonomy I cooked up. There's persuasion, which changes your beliefs about outcomes. There's manipulation, which hides the mechanism that changes how you form beliefs. And in between there's what I'm calling frame-locking: the speaker restructures the choice architecture so one option is the path of least resistance, the mechanism is totally visible, but you're still being pushed. I argue frame-locking is only legitimate when the agent has what I call "reversible framing literacy" — they can spot the framing and undo its effect using only their own reflective resources, no outside debiasing needed. The mechanism works through something like cognitive pathway locking, where attention gets channeled along one route without actually blocking the others.
