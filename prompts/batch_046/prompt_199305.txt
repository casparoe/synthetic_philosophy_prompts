In my aesthetics seminar we have spent months circling the same disagreement over Hume’s essay Of the Standard of Taste without any shift in positions. One faction reads Hume as offering a genuinely normative standard: there is a correct verdict about beauty that the true judge tracks, and deviation from that verdict is a kind of error, even though sentiment remains the mechanism of judgment. The other faction reads him as describing only an idealized convergence of refined sensibilities, with no further metaphysical or normative authority beyond the agreement itself. What makes the stall puzzling is that both sides accept the same textual evidence, both reject the crude dichotomy between objective beauty and mere preference, and both can accommodate Hume’s circularity objection about defining the standard by reference to good judges. I suspect the disagreement is not really about Hume’s text at all, but about whether aesthetic judgment belongs to a domain where disagreement implies a hidden fact—like color perception—or one where disagreement reveals only a difference in cultivated sensibility. More specifically, I wonder whether the two camps are running incompatible models of what I will call calibrational authority, where the standard is just intersubjective stability achieved by training perceptual mechanisms, versus normative authority, where the standard tracks properties that are in some sense response-independent. Given that framing, is the debate substantive—turning on whether beauty is genuinely response-dependent—or is it verbal, with both sides using standard in different registers? Or are they answering different questions—one about the metaphysics of aesthetic properties, one about the sociology of critical practice—while pretending to disagree about interpretation? Please take a definite position: diagnose the exact structure of the disagreement, identify whether a conceptual distinction between calibrational and normative authority or an analog from another domain of expertise could genuinely move it, and be direct about whether this is the kind of philosophical impasse that should be expected to persist because it ultimately rests on bedrock intuitions about the nature of disagreement itself.
