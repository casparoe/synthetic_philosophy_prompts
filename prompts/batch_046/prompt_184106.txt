I am preparing a consistency audit for a seminar on practical reason and wish to check whether the following set of commitments hangs together before I present it. The positions concern internal and external reasons, intergenerational obligation, and the scope of rational authority.

(1) I accept a strict internalist thesis about reasons: an agent S has a reason to φ only if, via some sound deliberative route from elements within S's actual subjective motivational set, φ-ing serves something S cares about, values, or is committed to.

(2) Despite (1), I maintain that moral reasons are genuinely external: agents can have decisive reason to act morally even when no element in their actual motivational set connects, via any sound deliberative route, to that action.

(3) I attempt to reconcile (1) and (2) through a universalised reconstruction: every rational agent necessarily possesses a second-order commitment to the coherence of her agency, and moral requirements are derivable from that structural commitment alone, without importing foreign desires.

(4) I extend (3) to intergenerational climate justice: present agents have weighty reason to mitigate climate damage to future generations, not because those future people currently exert causal or psychological influence, but because the second-order commitment to sustained agency requires preserving the conditions under which future agents can exercise rational coherence.

(5) I hold that logical consistency in belief is reason-giving only for agents who possess an epistemic concern for truth. If an agent genuinely lacks any such concern, maintaining contradictory beliefs involves no rational failure, though it may involve practical failure.
