After a long and somewhat circular argument with a friend about whether Fuller's internal morality of law actually defeats Hart's separation thesis or merely smuggles efficacy in through the back door, I'm sketching notes for an essay that tries to break the deadlock. The central move is to treat Fuller's eight principles—generality, promulgation, non-retroactivity, clarity, non-contradiction, possibility, constancy, and congruence—not as substantive moral constraints but as structural conditions of intelligibility, analogous to Grice's conversational maxims rather than to Kantian duties. On this reading, violating promulgation or clarity doesn't make a ruler immoral; it makes the putative system fail to function as law at all, much as speech that violates Quantity or Relation fails to be cooperative discourse rather than failing to be ethical. This relocates Fuller's critique: law is not just empirically efficacious (as in Hart's minimum content of natural law) but conceptually constituted by these procedural rules, yet Hart's separation of law from substantive morality remains intact because these are grammar conditions, not moral ones. The risk, of course, is that this collapses Fuller's normative force into a tautology—if contradictory, secret, retroactive commands are "not law" purely by definitional grammar, then we have translated Fuller's moral language into Hart's functionalist vocabulary without resolving whether legal validity can ever be conceptually independent of such procedural norms. I am especially unsure whether the Grice analogy holds for institutional rules as opposed to conversational ones, and whether I've hidden an assumption about norms that makes the reconciliation too cheap. Could you comment on the accuracy of this framing of Fuller's position, on where the mapping between Gricean maxims and Fuller's eight principles breaks down—for instance, whether promulgation maps onto Quality or onto Manner—and on whether the essay's central claim survives once those disanalogies are on the table? I would also welcome suggestions about whether this grammatical reading genuinely reconciles the two positions or simply renames the problem.
