Exam question (graduate seminar in epistemology): In Plato’s Meno, Socrates asks why knowledge is more valuable than true belief if both guide action correctly. Some interpreters distinguish two kinds of stability that might explain this surplus value: procedural stability, in which a belief is retained because it is produced and sustained by a robust cognitive mechanism; and justificatory stability, in which it is retained because the believer possesses an explanatory account (aitias logismos) that ties it down. Virtue epistemologists such as Zagzebski add a further layer, arguing that knowledge is an achievement that carries intrinsic value beyond practical success. In a concise essay, explain whether procedural and justificatory stability are competing or complementary explanations of knowledge’s value. Then evaluate whether the achievement account can explain the surplus value of knowledge over a belief that is both true and procedurally stable—say, a belief fixed by a highly reliable but unreflective perceptual mechanism—without collapsing into the claim that all reliable belief-formation is intellectually virtuous.

Student answer:

The Meno problem is about why knowledge is better than true belief. Plato solves it with the statue of Daedalus: true beliefs run away unless tied down by reasoning. This is justificatory stability—having reasons keeps the belief in place. Procedural stability is essentially the same idea expressed in modern reliabilist terms, since a reliable mechanism also keeps the belief from running away. So the two accounts are not competing; they are just ancient and modern descriptions of one phenomenon. Virtue epistemologists say knowledge is an achievement, which means you have to exercise your intellectual virtues and reflectively endorse the truth to get credit for it. This adds intrinsic value: knowing is good in itself, not merely useful. But if a belief is produced by a reliable mechanism like perfect vision, that is not an achievement, because there is no reflective effort or personal agency involved. So the achievement account explains why knowledge is better even than a stable true belief, since the stable belief might just be the product of a lucky mechanism. Therefore, we need both stability and achievement, but achievement does the real explanatory work in accounting for value.
