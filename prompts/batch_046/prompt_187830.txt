I'm a doctoral student in applied ethics consulting for a regional water authority on intergenerational infrastructure planning. The board must choose between two desalination regimes that will lock in patterns for the next century, and I need to defend one position to them next week.

Regime Alpha: High-efficiency plants serving 50,000 people with excellent water quality—flourishing lives, high average well-being, strict capacity ceiling, minimal footprint.

Regime Beta: Low-cost, widespread filtration serving 2 million people, including a large future population not yet born. The water meets survival thresholds but is mineral-deficient; residents avoid dehydration but face chronic low-grade morbidity and constant clinical monitoring. Lives are clearly worth living—no rational person would prefer non-existence—but they hover just above that boundary. The aggregate number of lives is forty times larger.

The charter requires maximizing "sustainable human flourishing" but leaves undefined whether this is per capita, aggregate across generations, or constrained by a quality floor. I feel pulled toward Beta by arithmetic—how can 50,000 excellent lives outweigh 2 million acceptable ones?—but I also feel moral revulsion at endorsing a system engineered to produce barely-tolerable existence at scale. Alpha feels like resource hoarding disguised as excellence.

Give me a committed recommendation—Alpha, Beta, or a principled hybrid—not an even-handed survey. Structure your response as follows (approximate word counts):

1. Diagnosis (about 150 words): Name the philosophical structure precisely and explain why the intuition that Beta is "manufacturing mediocrity" is either sound or a cognitive illusion.

2. The strongest case for Beta (about 200 words): Defend aggregate or totalist reasoning, address the quality-floor objection, and explain how intergenerational scope affects the calculus.

3. The strongest case for Alpha (about 200 words): Defend a critical-level or average-utilitarian position, explain why adding lives barely above neutrality does not contribute moral value, and respond to the "elitism" charge.

4. Verdict and charter language (about 300 words): Take a definite position. Specify whether there is a defensible critical level, where it should be set, whether the authority should adopt average or total metrics, and exactly what clause should be inserted into the charter. Be explicit about the weakest premise in your argument—particularly any assumption about comparing well-being across lives or across generations—and concede its vulnerability rather than hiding it.

Please do not list further readings or ask me to check citations. I need to present one view to the board.
