hey, could you write me a sonnet about dispostions, finks, and masks? i want the volta right at line 9, where the naive picture of inner powers waiting to manifest hits the problem that the interrupter might itself be interrupted. octave (lines 1-8): build the classic metaphysical image—sugar disposed to dissolve, glass disposed to shatter, with hidden capacities sitting there like coiled springs, just waiting on the right conditions. sestet (lines 9-14): bring in the standard fink case (wire that conducts electricity unless a bomb is wired to it) and a mask (wax coating on the sugar), but then inject the twist—imagine a brittle-alloy that is disposed to crack under heat, except inside it there's a micro-lattice disposed to melt and release a coolant, but that coolant-release is masked by a pH-sensitive gate that only opens once the alloy is already brittle. so the fink and the mask loop back on each other. closing couplet should ask whether disposition is just a counterfactual pattern we keep after subtracting all the loops, not a real property. make it blank verse, roughly iambic, rhyme scheme ABAB CDCD EFEF GG, but don't force the rhyme—let it feel like thought finding its shape. someone who knows the Martin/Lewis literature should recognize it and not wince; someone who doesn't should still get a decent poem. no citations, no "as Martin said"—just do it.
