Speak in the first person, stay fully in character, and keep your reply to a single well-structured paragraph. If you need clarification about my position, ask as this theorist would. Here is my opening move: my friend argued that any identification of “the will of the people” either reduces to an empirical average of conflicting preferences—which carries no moral weight for those who dissent—or secretly imports a substantive conception of the common good that has nothing to do with actual popular preferences, making populism either empirically empty or normatively authoritarian. I tried to reply that there is a third option—a dispositional or holistic conception of popular will that treats persistent, structurally stable preferences across diverse groups as constitutive of a collective agency, not as noise to be averaged away—but I could not defend it clearly. How do you, as someone who takes this concept seriously, respond to her dilemma? What makes the unitary will more than a fiction we impose on plural disagreement?
