Critics respond that allowing such an override collapses the distinction between precedent autonomy and present autonomy into a single sliding scale, thereby undermining the very purpose of advance planning. Student answer: The family should prevail, and the directive should be overridden. Precedent autonomy, as articulated in bioethical discussions of advance planning, grants earlier directives authority only insofar as they continue to express the patient's critical interests across time. Consequently, the directive now conflicts with M's enduring critical interests instead of protecting them.
