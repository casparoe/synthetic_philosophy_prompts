I've been argueing with friends about whether China's economic growth or Singapore's stability actually makes their goverments legitimate, and I'm tired of people just yelling 'authoritarian!' without sorting out what they actually believe. So I want a taxonomy — like a decision tree with maybe eight or nine branch points — that takes someone from concrete judgments to an actual named position on performance legitimacy. The nodes shouldn't ask 'do you like democracy?' but real splits: if a regime delievers fifty years of rising living standards but suppresses dissent, does that count as tacit consent? If citizens can emigrate easily like in Singapore but can't vote out leaders, does exit substitute for voice? And here's the weird twist I want baked in: what if the prosperity and order aren't delivered by human officials but by algorithmic governance — predictive policing, AI resource allocation, that kind of thing — does that make the legitimacy claim stronger because it's less arbitrary, or weaker because there's no human accountability? Each leaf should be a position like output legitimacy, democratic instrumentalism, Lee-style pragmatism, or just 'unnamed' if nobody actually holds it, and please flag any two leaves your tree can't actually tell apart. Don't give me a bibliography; I just want the logical map with the cases that split people.
