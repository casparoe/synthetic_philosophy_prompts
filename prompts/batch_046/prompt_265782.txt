One student wrote the following: At Nuremberg, the Allies used the London Charter of 1945 to define crimes against peace and humanity retroactively, violating nullum crimen sine lege since these categories were not clearly criminalized in positive international law before 1939. At Tokyo, Justice Pal dissented by arguing that the victors were judging the vanquished, but he rejected nullum crimen entirely in favor of a pure natural-law claim that sovereign equality makes any trial of defeated leaders illegitimate regardless of prior codification. Because the Tokyo Charter was issued as General MacArthur's executive decree in 1946 rather than negotiated as a multilateral treaty like the London Charter, the retroactivity objection is actually weaker there—executive decrees are not constrained by treaty-based prohibitions on ex post facto punishment in the same way, so the formal legal gap is smaller. Thus Pal's dissent is mainly about political sovereignty, not about retroactive criminal law.
