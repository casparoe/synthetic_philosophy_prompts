hey, for this writer's circle im trying to lay out my philosophical profile on language and fiction so the others can poke at it. heres where i actually stand:

1. im fully on board with the twin earth argument -- mental content aint in the head alone. if my doppelganger on twin earth thinks about twater when he thinks "water," we're just not thinking the same thing, brains be damned.

2. i write novels with characters who have deep inner lives, and i genuinely think sherlock holmes has beliefs and fears in a real sense, even tho he dosnt physically exist. i also think empty names like his dont just refer to nothing; they point to what id call a modal artifact -- kind of like a platonic form but stripped of all the metaphysical baggage.

3. i hold that the real meaning of a metaphor in my book is fixed by my private mental images at the moment of writing, not by reader interpretation or community usage.

4. i reject natural kinds in psychology. mental states are institutional or social facts, not joints in nature that science carves.

5. if me and my twin earth double wrote identical sentences about water but lived in different chemical enviroments, we'd be writing about totally different subjects, even with identical intentions and brain states.

please audit this for me. which pairs are actually in tension, and which only look like it? im especially worried about the clash between saying mental content is enviroment-dependent and also that my private imagery fixes meaning. and does my fiction view fight with my anti-natural-kind stance, or is that just surface noise? for each real tension, rate how bad it is and say what the cheapest revision would be. then tell me which single change would clean up the most mess overall. be honest -- i dont know where the cracks are.
