hey, i've been trying to formalize this argument for my diss chapter on Hofstadter's careenium and i'm stuck in a loop. i want to defend that symbols actually push the substrate around, but every time i formalize it i end up with something that either kills mental causation or makes rationality epiphenomenal. here's the version i walked my advisor into yesterday:

(1) In the careenium, the only fundamental dynamics are magnetic interactions between individual sims. that's the whole physical story.
(2) Simmballs are just clusters of sims arranged in a particular geometry; to an observer who knows the code they represent symbols, but physically they're nothing but sims.
(3) So if a symbol "pushes" other sims around, that just means the cluster's geometry makes a causal difference to neighboring sims that wouldn't happen if the cluster were arranged differently.
(4) But that difference is fully accounted for by the magnetic forces between the constituent sims. the symbolic interpretation adds nothing to the causal ledger.
(5) Therefore, in any non-vacuous sense, symbols don't push the substrate—only sims push sims.
(6) Mental causation, though, requires that my belief (a high-level simmball configuration) causes my action. so mental causation requires that symbols push substrate.
(7) From (5) and (6), mental causation is impossible.
(8) But mental causation is real: i reached for the cup because i believed it was hot.
(9) So (5) must be false—sometimes symbols really do push the substrate in a way that isn't just sim-on-sim mechanics.
(10) Now, a false belief and a true belief can have the exact same internal cluster geometry; they differ only in their relation to the world, not in how the sims are arranged.
(11) So if the true belief pushes substrate in virtue of its symbol-structure, the false belief pushes it identically.
(12) That means representational content—truth, accuracy, aboutness—plays zero causal role. only syntax pushes things around.
(13) But then rational belief-revision (correcting errors, tracking truth) has no causal explanation; it's causally inert.
(14) So either mental causation doesn't exist (reject 8) or rationality is epiphenomenal (accept 12-13). both seem crazy.
(15) Hence the argument proves too much.

my gut says the slide is between (4) and (9)—i think i'm equivocating on "push"—but i can't name the equivocation precisely. if i'm forced to pick, i'd rather keep (6) than (5), since mental causation feels non-negotiable, but i need to know exactly what that costs. can you tell me which step is the weak link, why it looked okay at first, and what the standard positions (maybe a kind of interventionist about macro-causation, or a non-interventionist who just denies downward causation altogether) each have to pay to reject it? also, does the problem go away if i treat the careenium as a model rather than a literal ontology?
