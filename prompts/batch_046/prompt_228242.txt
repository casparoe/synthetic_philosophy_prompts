Consider the metaphysical dispute between endurantism and perdurantism applied not to ordinary physical objects but to institutional entities that exercise normative authority—specifically, a municipal zoning authority. Imagine that over several decades every land-use ordinance is replaced, board membership turns over completely, and the physical offices are demolished, yet the legal power to restrict or permit development is continuously exercised. On an endurantist analysis, does this authority persist as numerically one and the same enduring institution despite lacking any shared constituent parts with its earlier stages? Or does perdurantism compel us to treat it as a four-dimensional worm composed of discrete temporal parts—individual votes, regulations, and administrative acts—such that continuity is merely causal or legal rather than strict identity? Explain the conceptual tension that arises when metaphysical identity conditions designed for material objects are applied to social institutions, and identify what this implies for attributing moral or legal responsibility across regulatory regimes that have been entirely reconstructed. Focus on diagnosing where the machinery breaks down, not on surveying the standard literature.
