hey, so i've been reading about these metaontology debates where people say arguments about whether something "really exists" are just verbal disputes — like disputants talking past each other because they mean different things by "exists". i mostly get the basic move, but i'm stuck on a specific wrinkle that keeps bugging me.

suppose we have a debate in ecology over whether there is a "real" species boundary between two populations that hybridize constantly, or whether the boundary is just something we draw for practical reasons. side a says the boundary is real if and only if it tracks reproductive isolation; side b says reproductive isolation comes in degrees and our cutoff is purely pragmatc. now, both sides agree on all the empircal facts about gene flow, hybridization rates, morphology, etc. they also agree that the classification is used to guide conservation policy — for instance, deciding which populations get protected status. so the disagreement doesn't seem to be about the word "species" in a simple dictionary sense. it looks more like a disagreement about whether the descriptive patterns license a particular normative commitment — namely, that we ought to preserve distinct lineages even when they blur together.

my question is: can the verbal-dispute diagnosis still work here? it seems like the deflationist has to say either (1) there's a hidden semantic difference about what "species" means, or (2) the normative part is separate and the ontological part is indeed verbal. but if we split the term into a descriptive core and a normative halo, don't we lose the point of the debate? the biologists aren't just arguing about labels; they're arguing about whether nature itself supports the kind of distinction that should ground policy. so where exactly does the deflationist's move fail? and if it does fail, does that mean metaontological deflationism has to absorb normative disagreements into its framework, or admit that some ontological debates are genuinely substantive after all?

i'd like an explanation that gets into the mechanics — not just "it's complicated" — but shows precisely where the diagnosis slips, and whether there's any way to patch it without collapsing the descriptive/normative split.
