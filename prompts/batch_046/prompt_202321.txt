I'm preparing remarks for a graduate seminar on the conceptual architecture of liberty and want a consistency audit before I present. Here are my commitments: (a) I take Berlin's negative/positive distinction to be descriptively useful but normatively incomplete, because it omits the republican claim that freedom is non-domination rather than mere non-interference. (b) I accept MacCallum's triadic analysis—agent X is free from constraint Y to do Z—as the correct logical form for every freedom claim, and I apply it to policy debates. (c) I think republican liberty is not a third, independent concept but a modal refinement of negative liberty: it tracks the arbitrary capacity to interfere, not just actual interference, so it still belongs within the negative-liberty family. (d) I also hold that democratic collective self-authorship—positive liberty in Berlin's sense—is essential: citizens are not fully free unless they author the laws that bind them. (e) I defend constitutional courts with the power to invalidate democratically enacted statutes when those statutes create arbitrary domination over minorities, even against current democratic majorities. (f) I maintain that in a well-ordered polity, negative, positive, and republican liberty can all be maximized together without serious trade-offs; conflicts are usually solved by institutional engineering rather than conceptual sacrifice. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision to restore consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad. Keep your audit to a single paragraph of no more than 300 words.
