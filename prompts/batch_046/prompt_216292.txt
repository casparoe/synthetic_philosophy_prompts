There's this practice some big musems have now where they display high-res holograms of artifacts instead of the real objects, and they justify it by saying the hologram captures the 'essential form' of the piece while protecting it from decay. At the same time they charge full admission and market it as an 'authentic encouter.' Meanwhile in the same musem's education wing they run workshops on Plato's cave allegory telling kids that shadows are deceptive and the real objects are what matter. What's going on philosophically? What view of reality, appearance, and value does that practice presuppose? Is it basically Platonic ontology where the form is primary and the physical copy is just imperfect? Or more like nominalism where form is just information? And does it presuppose that knowledge needs direct sensory contact with originals, or can mediated access count? Reconstruct the implicit metaphysics and epistemology, say if it's coherent, and tell me what'd have to change if we dropped the Platonic framing entirely.
