As a novelist who has spent the last decade writing fiction under severe formal constraints—reversing chronology, excising a single grammatical mood, holding every other narrative parameter fixed—I have become fixated on Douglas Hofstadter’s idea that creativity is fundamentally knob-twiddling: the disciplined variation of one parameter within an otherwise stable conceptual space. This led me to examine the charter of the Aureate Foundation, a real-world institution (or one I have constructed for the sake of argument) that awards its annual Conceptual Fiction Prize exclusively to works altering exactly one structural “knob” of an established narrative form—causal logic, temporal ordering, narrative reliability—while leaving the remaining architecture untouched. The charter explicitly disqualifies both derivative pastiche and radical formal invention; its jurors are instructed to ask not whether a work is unprecedented, but which dial has been turned and with what precision.

What philosophical commitments about creativity, artistic value, and the ontology of fictional worlds does this institutional practice presuppose? Must it assume that every aesthetic space comes pre-structured by a fixed set of independent parameters, so that invention is always navigation within a given map rather than the drawing of a new one? Does the refusal to honor paradigm-breaking work commit the foundation to a combinatorial essentialism about art—one that implicitly denies the possibility of genuinely new conceptual spaces, analogous to the distinction between normal and revolutionary change, but applied to literature? Or can the practice be reconciled with a more radical view of creativity, provided we interpret “knob” with sufficient metaphorical latitude? Reconstruct the implicit theory of value embedded in the evaluation criteria, distinguish what the institution strictly requires from what merely fits it comfortably, and identify which rival accounts—those treating form as historically emergent, as socially constructed, or as irreducibly holistic—are quietly ruled out by the prize’s design. Then tell me what would have to change in the foundation’s jury instructions, its public defense, and its funding structure if we replaced its underlying model with one that sees creativity as the invention of entirely new parameter spaces rather than the disciplined variation of old ones. Please separate in your answer what is settled about Hofstadter’s model and its philosophical applications from what remains genuinely contested among theorists of creativity and aesthetics, and do not present any disputed interpretation as though it were the consensus view.
