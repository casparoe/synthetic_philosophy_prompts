For a philosophy of religion seminar, write the desiderata that any adequate interpretation of Wittgenstein's views on religious belief must satisfy. The interpretation must distinguish grammatical belief—the framework that renders religious language intelligible—from existential commitment, the lived orientation that may accompany it without being reducible to propositional assent, and it must accommodate Wittgenstein's scattered remarks on miracles, the Last Judgment, and the relation between faith and doubt without treating religious belief as either cognitive error or purely emotional expression. List each requirement with its motivating case or textual consideration, mark which are non-negotiable and which reflect preferences of particular camps (e.g., continuity with the Tractatus versus radical discontinuity), and identify any subset that appears jointly unsatisfiable given his anti-theoretical stance on philosophy. Then briefly assess how the standard language-game reading, the fideist reading, and a synthetic grammatical-existential view fare against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their conflicts rather than on the scoring.
