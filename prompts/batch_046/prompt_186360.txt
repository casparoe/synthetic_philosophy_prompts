Question: In his political writings, Spinoza contends that democracy is the absolute form of sovereignty and is structurally less susceptible to degenerating into tyranny than either monarchy or aristocracy. Explain this argument with reference to Spinoza's definition of sovereign right as coextensive with the collective power of the multitude, and address how he distinguishes democratic deliberation from the rule of a single monarch or a restricted council.

Student answer: Spinoza thinks democracy is the best government because every citizen gets an equal vote, making it the fairest regime. He defines sovereignty as the right to do whatever the state has the power to do, and in democracy this right is held collectively by all citizens rather than by one monarch. Because authority is distributed across the entire population, there is no single individual who can seize absolute control and become a tyrant. Spinoza also argues that when people deliberate together, their individual passions tend to cancel out, leaving collective reason to guide the laws. This distinguishes democracy from aristocracy, where a small council can become corrupt. Spinoza allows that democracy can be limited—for example, by restricting the vote to property-owning citizens—but he still maintains that the core principle is that the multitude's combined power is the source of all authority. Ultimately, democracy is the most natural form of government because it preserves the same unrestricted freedom that individuals enjoy in the state of nature, only now secured through collective agreement.
