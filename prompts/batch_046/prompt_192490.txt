I'm working through a deontological framework for a writing project and want an internal consistency check before I build further on it. Here are my current commitments:

(a) Deontological side-constraints are genuinely agent-relative and non-fungible: it is impermissible for an agent to directly kill one innocent person as a means, even if that act prevents five identical killings by other agents.

(b) These constraints undergo what I am calling catastrophic attenuation: they do not vanish at extreme stakes, but their normative form shifts from an absolute prohibition to a weighted veto that can be overridden only by a correspondingly catastrophic moral cost, never by simple aggregation of lives saved.

(c) Moral residue—the lingering sense that one has done wrong even after a justified transgression—is not merely affective but epistemic: it tracks the fact that a constraint was genuinely violated, not that an optimal outcome was missed.

(d) In collective agency, where no individual performs the physical harm and decision is distributed across a procedural structure (for example, a democratic vote authorizing a policy), the agent-relativity of constraints dissolves into structural accountability: the collective bears the moral debt, but no individual bears the residue.

(e) The so-called paradox of deontology—that it permits worse aggregate outcomes—is dissolved if we reject the premise that moral theories must rank outcomes; instead, constraint-compliance and outcome-promotion are incommensurable dimensions, and a theory can be correct on both without contradiction.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? Specifically, does (b) undermine the non-aggregative core of (a), or can catastrophic attenuation be defined without appealing to aggregate welfare? Does (d) conflict with (c), since distributed decision-making seems to erase the epistemic signal of residue? And does (e) depend on a value pluralism incompatible with the unitary authority I grant to constraints in (a)? Please rank the tensions by severity, and for each indicate which commitment should be modified or abandoned.
