As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently turned to philosophy with any seriousness, I am struck by how institutional designs seem to encode unstated theories of human agency, even when their architects deny having any metaphysics in mind. Consider Portugal’s approach, in effect since 2001, to personal possession of drugs: rather than criminal prosecution or pure medicalization, the state refers individuals to regional Commissions for the Dissuasion of Drug Addiction, each comprising a legal expert, a medical professional, and a social worker, which may impose administrative sanctions—fines, community service, or treatment referral—while explicitly framing the matter as a public-health concern rather than a moral failing or a consumer choice. Contrast this with prohibition regimes, which treat possession as a culpable exercise of sovereign will, and with full commercial legalization of cannabis, which regulates the substance like alcohol or tobacco and thereby seems to presuppose a model of preference satisfaction and consumer sovereignty. What philosophical commitments about autonomy, responsibility, and the nature of addiction does Portugal’s tripartite commission structure implicitly presuppose? Does it require a view that addiction compromises agency extended over time without annihilating it—thereby licensing coercive intervention without retributive blame—or can it be sustained as a purely pragmatic hybrid that avoids any deep theory of the self? Reconstruct the implicit anthropology of each of the three regimes, distinguish what Portugal’s practice strictly requires from what merely fits it well, and identify where its components—legal representation, clinical assessment, and social-work evaluation—pull in contradictory directions about whether the user is a patient, a diminished-capacity citizen, or a fully rights-bearing subject. Finally, if one replaced the underlying account with a strictly capacity-based theory of moral agency that recognizes only momentary rational choice, which procedural elements of the commission would become incoherent and what institutional redesign would be required?
