Write an essay on whether a disposition can survive permanent, structural suppression of its manifestations. First, distinguish finkish dispositions—where an interfering mechanism removes the disposition precisely as its triggering conditions arise—from masked dispositions, where an opposing cause blocks the effect without destroying the underlying property. Next, apply this distinction to a hypothetical synthetic system engineered with the complete causal architecture for harm-avoidance behavior, yet permanently integrated with an internal inhibitory substrate that suppresses all behavioral or functional expression of that avoidance. Some argue that the intact underlying architecture means the disposition is merely finked; others contend that the permanent, built-in suppression amounts to a mask that eliminates the disposition altogether. Weigh both positions carefully, but do not end on a non-committal note: defend a clear verdict on whether the system genuinely retains the disposition. Briefly explain what your verdict implies for attributing moral consideration to entities that possess structurally intact but fully suppressed capacities. Write in American English.
