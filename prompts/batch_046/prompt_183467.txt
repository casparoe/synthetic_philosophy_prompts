design a runnable 75-minute classroom game for about 16-18 undergrad students in a philosophy workshop. the idea to make them feel is ubuntu-style relational personhood — that being a person isnt some private, intrinsic thing you own, but is constituted by recognition, obligation, and being woven into others. i want the rules and payoffs to generate that feeling before anyone names it.

set it up in three rounds:
- round 1 (20 min): every student writes an individual 'values charter' alone. they score themselves on internal consistency and autonomy. let them think this is the real task.
- round 2 (20 min): put them in random trios. now they must merge charters into one group document, but secretly change the rubric: points only exist if another trio-member explicitely ratifies your contribution as 'ours', not 'yours'. one student per trio gets no relational tags at start — they can only enter the document if two others reframe them. the facilitator should stay silent about the rubric shift until after.
- round 3 (15 min): a crisis. half the 'voice tokens' are taken away. groups must decide who keeps speaking rights, but the rule is you can only defend a spot by citing a relational dependency ('if she goes silent, thier student loses a mentor', etc.), not by appealing to individual merit. watch the untagged ones drop out unless someone rewires the web for them.

then a 20-min debrief. please include:
- a timeline with facilitator moves vs silent moments
- how to map round 1's emptiness, round 2's hidden scoring, and round 3's crisis onto parts of relational personhood
- what predictably goes wrong (e.g., liberal-minded students trying to 'save' the unrecognized person on individual-rights grounds) and how to handle it without shutting down the game
- an honest admission of which feature this cant reproduce — probably the long generational/ancestral dimension of ubuntu — and exactly what the facilitator should say to supply that gap
- keep it casual, a typo or two is okay, and dont ask for citations, bibliographies, or anything that needs web access. the prompt should be self-contained.
