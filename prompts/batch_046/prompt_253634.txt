Rank the following five accounts of why human extinction is bad, ordering them from the one that best captures Samuel Scheffler’s “afterlife” conjecture to the one that least captures it. For each entry, provide a single sentence explaining its placement, and identify any point at which the account collapses into either a summed-utility loss over future deaths or a rights-based complaint about non-existent victims.

A. The termination of all future artistic, scientific, and moral achievements that present people implicitly value and work toward.
B. The frustration of present individuals’ “project-dependent” values, which derive their meaning from belonging to an ongoing human story extending well beyond their own lives.
C. The breaking of an implicit intergenerational covenant: present generations reproduce and sustain culture on the tacit understanding that successors will inherit and continue it.
D. The disappearance of a specific normative horizon—human practices of justification, blame, and aspiration—whose value is not reducible to the well-being of the individuals who instantiate them.
E. The direct harm done to potential future persons by depriving them of existence, weighted by the number and quality of lives that would have been lived.
