I am preparing a paper for my seminar on comparative moral philosophy and want to clarify a conceptual issue that keeps arising in our discussions. The question is whether ranking historical atrocities is possible without being morally obscene, but I want to avoid both simplistic aggregation and pure incommensurability. Specifically, I have been trying to work with a multi-dimensional moral topology in which atrocities are not placed on a single scale but are instead mapped onto a partial ordering defined by independent axes—such as the intensity of bureaucratic rationalization, the magnitude and irreversibility of suffering, the explicitness of genocidal intent, and the degree to which normative protections for victims collapsed. Under this framing, two events may be incomparable along one axis yet ordered along another, producing a lattice rather than a linear hierarchy. Could you explain whether this dimensional approach genuinely avoids the obscenity of ranking, or whether it merely conceals an implicit aggregation that reintroduces the very comparison it seeks to avoid?
