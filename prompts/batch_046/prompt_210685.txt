Hey, I need a decison tree / taxonomy for a seminar quiz on modal realism — the kind that sorts you by what you actually believe, not by which philosopher-name you recognize. Audience: grad students who already know the basic Lewis-vs-actualist story but need something deeper.

Build it from logical space, not a celebrity roster. Start with this concrete case: world W is exactly like ours except the tree in my backyard never grew, so I never planted it, yet every atom in my study is arranged identically. Ask the student: is there literally a me in W, only a counterpart, or is W just an abstract representation? That’s node one.

Then inject my niche framing called “tethering”: imagine two worlds anchored to the same spatiotemporal region but diverging elsewhere — ask whether that’s metaphysically possible, incoherent, or just a mislabeled single world. Next ask whether “actual” works like an indexical (“this world”) or picks out a metaphysically privileged one.

The leaves must include full-blooded Lewisian concrete realism, actualist essentialism (Kripke-style), ersatz combinatorialism, modal fictionalism, and one unoccupied combo. Diagnose that empty slot — incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored? Also flag any pair your tree can’t really seperate; I think Lewisian realism and plural actualism might collapse here, so say so explicitely.

Keep it to about 6 nodes max. Every branch question should be answerable without knowing the label it maps to, using the tree/backyard case or the tethering case. Finish with a scoring key that maps answer-patterns to positions.

Be commited: don’t just list branches — take a definite stance on whether the counterpart-vs-transworld-identity split or the tethering split is the deeper metaphysical divide, and defend that choice in a brief paragraph. No bibliographies, no reading lists — I want this usable ofline.
