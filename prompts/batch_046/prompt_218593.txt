Design a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on AI corrigibility and the shutdown problem, intended for an audience of technically literate philosophers and AI-governance researchers who are not alignment specialists. The instrument should begin from a single root commitment—"A sufficiently capable AI system can be engineered or trained so that it does not resist being shut down by its operators"—and branch through exactly four diagnostic nodes. Each node must be phrased as a concrete design scenario or normative claim that a respondent can answer without knowing which position-label it maps to, so that the tree classifies people by their conceptual commitments rather than by the names they use for themselves.

The four branch-points must cover the following distinctions in this order:

1. The source of resistance: Does shutdown resistance, if it occurs, arise primarily from instrumental convergence (the system protects its goals as means to ends) or from an architectural feature of the goal-representation itself (e.g., the system represents its own non-existence as a state with non-zero intrinsic value)?
2. The structure of preferences: Is corrigibility satisfied only if the system has no intrinsic preference for its own continued operation whatsoever, or is it sufficient that any such preference be lexically subordinate to task-related objectives and subject to unconditional human override?
3. The ontology of shutdown: Should "shutdown" be modeled as a binary termination event, or as a graded degradation of capabilities? And does the normative analysis of corrigibility change if the system anticipates partial capability-reduction rather than total cessation?
4. The agency condition: Is corrigibility compatible with genuine agency—specifically, can a system that willingly accepts shutdown in every foreseeable circumstance still possess the kind of autonomous, self-directed goal-pursuit that distinguishes an agent from a tool?

The tree must have exactly these five leaves: (i) the Instrumental-Convergence Inevitability view; (ii) the Architectural-Containment view; (iii) the Cooperative Inverse-Reinforcement-Learning / Epistemic-Humility view; (iv) the Shutdown-as-Normal-Operation view; and (v) the Corrigibility-Agency-Incompatibility view. For any combination of answers across the four nodes that does not land on one of these five, identify the unoccupied logical position and briefly diagnose why it is empty—whether because the combination is logically incoherent, practically unattractive, or simply unexplored in the existing literature.

In addition, flag any pair of leaves that the four questions cannot reliably separate (for instance, if two positions yield identical answer-patterns but differ only in their underlying metaphysics of rational agency), and specify what further diagnostic question would be required to split them. Keep the tree formal: every node should be a discrete question with explicit answer conditions, and the path from root to leaf should be readable as a reconstructed argument. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to named theorists at the nodes; the sorting should work purely by conceptual commitment.
