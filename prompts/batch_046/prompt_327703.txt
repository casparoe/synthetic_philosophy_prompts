One classmate argues that correctly identifying these rules demonstrates a genuinely symbolic, non-statistical capacity for conceptual induction in humans, since the governing principle—such as whether nested regions contain an odd or even number of boundaries when the overall figure lacks symmetry—cannot be captured by the feature-correlation mechanisms that drive statistical translation or large language models. The other classmate maintains that this gap is only apparent and architectural: with a sufficiently hierarchical, attention-based representation trained on structured analogies, a statistical system could induce the identical rule from sparse examples, and human intuition itself is best understood as compressed statistical inference accumulated over evolutionary and developmental timescales.

Please draft the adversarial-collaboration statement they ought to produce after a good-faith exchange. Point four should identify the central crux—whether success on a novel cross-modal Bongard variant, in which a rule learned from visual figures is applied without retraining to an isomorphic auditory sequence, indicates genuine conceptual understanding or merely generalized feature extraction—and should record each side’s explicit prediction about that case.
