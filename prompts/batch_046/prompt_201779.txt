Here's an argument I sketched for a discussion on scientific realism and now find suspicious because it seems to annihilate its own premises. (1) Ontic structural realism holds that mature science reveals only relational structure, leaving the intrinsic natures of entities permanently opaque. (2) If intrinsic nature is opaque, then any ontology that realizes the same relational structure is empirically indistinguishable and therefore rationally on a par with every other. (3) For the structures in quantum field theory, this means discrete hidden-variable lattices, continuous fields, pure information-theoretic patterns, and even purely mathematical structures without spatiotemporal embedding all realize the same equations. (4) So structural realism implies that we can never have a reason—whether evidential, parsimony-based, or causal—to reject a lattice ontology in favor of a field ontology, since any such reason would have to appeal to features of the relata that are, by (1), unknown. (5) But working physicists do reject specific realizations (e.g., non-local hidden-variable models) using precisely criteria like local causality and ontological economy, which seem to depend on knowing something about the relata's nature. (6) Therefore structural realism either strips science of its rational basis for ontology-selection, or it smuggles in direct knowledge of intrinsic nature through the back door of "structural" criteria. (7) Hence structural realism is either self-defeating or collapses into standard realism.

I'd like to keep both (1) and (5), because giving up either feels like abandoning either the epistemic humility of structuralism or the rationality of actual science. Where is the slip—between (2) and (3), in the inference to (4), or in treating parsimony as non-structural? And if I preserve (1) and (5), do I have to accept that some "structural" virtues are actually tracking intrinsic nature after all?
