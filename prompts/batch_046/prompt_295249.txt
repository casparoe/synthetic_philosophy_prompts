As part of my dissertation research on moral responsibility and penal philosophy, I am preparing an adversarial-collaboration document between a compatibilist in Dennett's tradition—who accepts determinism, rejects libertarian free will, denies basic-desert retributivism, yet retains a revisionary practice of answerability—and a free-will skeptic in Caruso's tradition—who denies basic-desert moral responsibility altogether and defends social defense through purely consequentialist, public-health-style institutional design. Both reject strong metaphysical desert and agree that punishment should not be justified by intrinsic suffering-owingness.

Please draft the joint statement they could both sign after good-faith collaboration. Structure it as follows:

(A) Numbered agreed claims: shared acceptance of determinism and the scientific image; rejection of libertarian sourcehood; the conceptual distinction between basic-desert moral responsibility and derivative institutional accountability; agreement on the legitimate aims of criminal justice (protection, rehabilitation, deterrence, and expressive social signaling without retribution); and joint denial that wrongdoers metaphysically deserve suffering in proportion to culpability.

(B) Precise residual disagreements, each phrased in wording its own camp accepts. Specify: (1) whether determinism undermines the sourcehood conditions required for genuine answerability, or whether sourcehood can be reconstructed within a compatibilist framework; (2) whether non-desert justifications for incapacitation and behavioral control can be institutionalized without covertly importing retributive intuitions; (3) whether the term moral responsibility should be retained as a normative practice-concept or eliminated from institutional and interpersonal discourse.

(C) The cruxes, with tests and predictions. I take the central cruxes to be: (i) whether folk judgments about blame and punishment-deservingness form a unified cognitive module or a dissociable cluster of agency-detection, harm-assessment, and social-signaling heuristics—both sides should agree that cross-cultural priming studies manipulating determinism beliefs and separately measuring blame attributions versus punishment-desert judgments bear on this, with each side predicting whether attributions collapse globally or dissociate into separable components; (ii) whether institutional designs modeled on epidemiological quarantine can be sustained without moral-contamination effects—i.e., without practitioners and the public reconstruing protective detention as deserved suffering—with each side predicting whether framing and transparency mechanisms can stabilize non-retributive interpretations or whether retributive reconstrual is psychologically inevitable.

(D) A brief joint conclusion identifying whether any portion of the dispute is verbal—particularly whether disagreement about responsibility reduces to a conflict between a normative practice-concept and a metaphysical sourcehood-concept—and stating, for each substantive crux, what kind of evidence or argument each camp regards as decisive and whether either anticipates that settling the crux would move them.

Keep the document self-contained and meaningful without web access. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or appeals to highly obscure empirical literatures; rely on conceptual analysis and broadly familiar domains in moral psychology and institutional design.
