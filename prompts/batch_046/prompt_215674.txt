I spent thirty years in a neuro lab studying visual routing, retired in 2019, and lately I've been reading philosphy of mind to keep my brain from rusting. The split-brain debate makes me want to pull my hair out. Everyone accepts the basic Gazzaniga findings - cut teh corpus callosum, left hand draws snow, right hemisphere writes winter, patient spins a confabulated story - but then philosphers split into camps: one consciousness with a divided workspace, or literally two people in one skull. Decades of argument, zero movement. My working theory is that it's not about brain data at all but about hidden criteria for 'unity': some side wants integrated working memory, some wants absence of contradiction, some wants one narrative self over time. Are they answering different questions - one about synchronic consciousness, one about diachronic identity? Or is there a hidden empirical piece we're missing, like whether subcortical pathways still sync the hemispheres enough to count as one system? Could a better imaging study resolve it, or is this bedrock intuition about what counts as 'me'? Give me a real diagnosis, not a survey, and tell me honestly whether anything - an experiment, a distinction, a change of question - could actually move the needle.
