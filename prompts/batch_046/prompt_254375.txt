I've been running a small philosophy discussion group with some friends who aren't academics, and we've hit this wall about hard choices and incommensurible values that I can't figure out. The specific case we keep circling back to is this: imagine you have to decide between taking a job that requires you to relocate permanently away from your hometown—where your family has lived for generations and where you have deep community ties—and staying put but giving up a career path that actually uses your specific talents in a way that feels like a calling. Everyone agrees both options involve genuine goods—belonging and continuity versus flourishing and contribution—and everyone agrees that whichever you pick, you're not saying the other good is fake or unimportant.

But then the conversation splits. One side says this is a classic case of incommensurability: there's literally no fact of the matter about which choice is rationaly better, so the decision has to come down to something non-rational like identity or commitment. The other side says that's just mysticism—of course there's a rational answer, it's just that figuring it out requires weighing reasons that don't share a common metric, and the answer might be that you should stay, or go, but there IS a right answer even if we'll never be fully sure. We've argued about this for like four weeks and nobody's moved. My own hunch is that the incommensurability side is conflating "no common metric" with "no comparison possible at all," and that they're really defending a kind of existentialist view about self-creation, while the other side is treating rationality like it has to be algorithmic. But maybe I'm wrong.

Is this actually a substantive disagreement about the nature of value and practical reason, or is it verbal—are they just using "rational" differently? Or is it that one side cares about the phenomology of decision-making (the feeling of loss that doesn't go away) and the other side only cares about normative structure? And here's a weird twist I want your take on: I recently came across this distinction—maybe from a paper I can't fully recall—between "scale-incommensurability," where values can't be mapped onto a single quantitative measure but can still be compared via qualitative reasoning, and "choice-incommensurability," where comparing them at all violates something essential about each value's nature. If I introduced that into our group, would it actually help clarify the disagreement, or is the stall happening at a deeper level—like bedrock intuitions about whether human lives should have a single narrative coherence? Be honest: is there any kind of argument, formal result, or conceptual distinction that could actually resolve this, or are we just stuck with competing pictures of what it means to live well?
