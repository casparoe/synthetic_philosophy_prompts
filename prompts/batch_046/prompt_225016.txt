In my philosophy of mind seminar weve been going in circles about whether introspection is actually reliable. One side points to all the confabulation studies — people explaining choices they never made, misreporting emotional triggers — and says introspection is basically storytelling with extra authority. The other side says thats just misreporting, not failed access, and that present-tense first-person experience still carries a kind of transparency that third-person observation cant replicate. Heres the frustrating part: we all agree on the empirical findings, we all agree people sometimes lie or confuse themselves, and yet nobody has budged in two years. Is this even about psychology anymore? My hunch is its a hidden disagreement about standards: should we judge introspection by predictive accuracy, by coherence with behavior, or by some transparency condition that doesnt reduce to either? Or maybe its deeper — one side thinks mental states are partly constituted by how theyre accessed, the other thinks access is just one causal route among others. I want an actual diagnosis using what each side commits to, not a survey. Is this verbal, substantive, or are we answering different questions about reliability under one heading? And be honest: is there a conceptual distinction or framing shift that could move it, or is this bedrock intuition that no argument will touch?
