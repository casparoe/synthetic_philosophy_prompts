I serve on the replication-oversight board of a multi-site neuroscience initiative. Our protocol requires that funded labs independently replicate "the same mechanistic anomaly detector," but the protocol never defined what counts as sameness of mechanism. Lab A detects aberrant circuit dynamics through an explicitly parameterized biophysical state-space model with interpretable transition dynamics; Lab B detects the same class of anomalies with a self-attention architecture that learns implicit dynamics and carries no explicit state representation, yet claims equivalence because both isolate an identical causal pathway and yield the same intervention predictions. One faction argues that shared mechanistic explanation requires shared representational format and biophysical interpretability, so Lab B is running a black-box classifier and fails replication. The opposing faction argues that sameness is fixed solely by causal-target identity and predictive equivalence, regardless of implementation substrate. Draft the board's formal decision memo recommending whether Lab B satisfies the replication criterion, with a concrete compliance or review timeline, and with an honest, serious treatment of the strongest argument on the side that loses.
