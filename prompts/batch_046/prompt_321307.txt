I'm putting together a conceptual sorting instrument for a dataset I'm curating — the kind of prompt that should work for a model without search access but shouldn't be answerable by rote repetition of the standard Ship of Theseus summary. The domain is artifact identity over time, but I want the taxonomy to go past the usual material-continuity-versus-form-continuity binary and to function as a genuine "which position do you actually hold?" quiz.

Please construct a decision tree that takes a respondent from a single opening commitment to one of several leaf positions. The root node should ask whether artifacts have identity conditions that are genuinely independent of both their material parts and their social or institutional tracking — in other words, whether there is a metaphysical fact of the matter about ship-identity at all. From there, branch through concrete, answerable questions: does gradual part-replacement over years differ metaphysically from instantaneous wholesale replacement (this is the niche distinction I want injected — popular treatments almost always ignore the temporal profile of replacement); does functional and structural continuity suffice for persistence if material continuity fails; and does institutional recognition — a harbor registry, ownership record, or ceremonial renaming — determine identity when both material and functional continuity are broken?

The leaves must include: (A) strict material continuity; (B) functional-structural continuity regardless of parts; (C) four-dimensionalist denial that there is one enduring ship, only ship-stages or temporal parts; (D) institutional or conventionalist identity fixed by social tracking; and (E) any combination that denies both material and functional continuity yet still asserts identity — mark this leaf as either incoherent or an unexplored position, with a one-sentence diagnosis of why the space is empty. Wherever two positions genuinely cannot be separated by the tree — for instance, if functional continuity and institutional tracking always coincide in practice — flag that pair explicitly rather than inventing a spurious branch.

Every internal node should be phrased as a concrete claim or case judgment that a reader of long-form rationalist forums could answer without knowing the philosophical label it maps to. Do not include named philosophers, bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; if you reference a specific idea, paraphrase it without quotation marks. The deliverable should be usable as a diagnostic instrument with an implied scoring key: the path of yes/no answers leads to the respondent's actual position, and any path that lands in an unoccupied combination should be noted as such. Keep the tree compact — no more than six internal nodes — but ensure the logical space is mapped rather than merely listing familiar names.
