Restate Dennett's brain-transplant puzzle -- the surgical division of brain tissue between a biological body and an artificial substrate, the radio-link attempt to maintain coordination, and the first-person problem of locating oneself -- entirely within that vocabulary. For each component, say what translates without distortion, what must be forced to fit (for instance, treating subjective location as a consensus problem or a leader-election outcome), and what has no counterpart whatsoever in distributed computing. Then report on the residue: does the untranslatable remainder indicate that Dennett's argument captures something about personal identity that genuinely exceeds any node-replica framework, or does it merely expose an unmotivated assumption -- a single privileged locus of consciousness -- for which distributed systems have no analog and no need?
