I'm trying to stabilize my interpretive stance on Plato's treatment of intoxication before a seminar on the Laws, and I want a consistency audit of the package I've assembled. Here is where I've landed:

(1) In Laws I–II, the Athenian Stranger treats the symposion not as social indulgence but as a civic diagnostic institution: leader-regulated, moderate drinking temporarily weakens aidos—the mechanism of social shame—just enough to reveal each citizen's natural disposition toward courage, temperance, or licentiousness, without collapsing into full akrasia.

(2) In Republic III, Socrates prohibits wine altogether for the guardians because any relaxation of the rational part's command over the spirited and appetitive parts threatens the hierarchical harmony that defines justice in the soul.

(3) The Alcibiades episode in Symposium shows that drunkenness strips away performative self-presentation and exposes the soul's true erotic orientation; I take this as corroborating the Laws I diagnostic model rather than contradicting it.

(4) There is no deep contradiction between Republic and Laws because they address different political conditions: the Republic describes a first-best polis whose citizens already possess rational harmony, so no diagnostic institution is needed, whereas the Laws describes a second-best polis where citizens must be tested, sorted, and corrected.

(5) My underlying view is that alcohol functions in Plato as a pharmakon calibrated to the soul's rational architecture: it is destructive for souls lacking stable rational hegemony (hence the guardian prohibition) but therapeutically revelatory for souls in which reason is present but obscured by social convention (hence the regulated symposion).

Which commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. Specifically, I am concerned that (3) and (5) together commit me to saying that Alcibiades—who clearly lacks rational hegemony—should have been destroyed rather than revealed by wine, yet the Symposium presents his drunken revelation as philosophically productive for Socrates. Is that a real contradiction in my package, or can it be dissolved without abandoning (5)? And does (4)'s distinction between first-best and second-best conditions actually reconcile (1) and (2), or does it smuggle in an institutional-design claim about testing regimes that Plato never makes in the Republic? If the latter, which of (1) through (5) should I weaken first?
