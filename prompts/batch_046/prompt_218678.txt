I've been going down a bit of a rabbit hole about inside vs outside view and I think I've convinced myself of something that feels extreme, maybe paranoid. The position is this: if you're being genuinely epistemically modest, then for basically any belief you hold — including the belief that modesty itself is correct — you should treat your own reasoning as just one more sample from the population of people who've thought about this, and weight the outside-view base rate over your inside view. So if smart people who study reasoning still systematically overestimate their own calibration, then my belief that I'm calibrated well enough to override that base rate should itself be downgraded. I call this the recursive modesty view, or the modesty trap, since it applies to itself and might collapse into uniform skepticism.

What would actually count against this? I want the defeaters sorted — fatal versus merely awkward — including whether there's a performative contradiction in using first-person reasoning to defend outside-view supremacy, or whether empirical patterns about expert calibration in some domains show that inside-view reasoning is actually protected. Has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability, like if I just dismiss any counterargument as more overconfidence, and is that a feature or a bug? Especialy, are there cases where a rational agent can consistently apply the outside view to object-level beliefs but not to the meta-belief that the outside view applies there? Be honest about how exposed the position actually is.
