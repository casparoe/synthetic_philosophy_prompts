Write an essay on whether culture, conceived as a system of shared values, can function as a genuine cause of economic behavior in the manner Max Weber suggested in his Protestant ethic thesis. Focus especially on the circularity objection: that the values Weber invokes may be emergent descriptions of aggregate individual choices rather than independent forces, and that capitalism might produce those values rather than the reverse. Evaluate whether treating values as causally efficacious requires analogizing them to institutional feedback loops that constrain action independently of individual mental states, or whether such analogies merely disguise a reduction to material interests and institutional rules. Weigh the main arguments on both sides, but defend a specific verdict: does Weber's cultural explanation survive once we reject mentalistic and reified accounts of values?
