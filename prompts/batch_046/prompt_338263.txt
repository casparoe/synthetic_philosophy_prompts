Here's one I cooked up while re-reading Republic VII and now i'm stuck. Let's call the relation between a mental state and what it represents cognitive participation - it's like metaphysical participation but running upward.

(1) The prisoners in the cave take shadows for reality.
(2) Those shadows are images cast by objects that are themselves copies of Forms, so shadows sit two removes from true being.
(3) If a prisoner believes shadow A is taller than shadow B, that belief stands in cognitive participation to the shadows themselves.
(4) Plato distinguishes knowledge, which grasps Forms directly, from opinion (doxa), which concerns the intermediate realm of becoming - the physical objects behind the shadows, not the Forms themselves.
(5) So opinion is cognition directed at things that both are and are not, which on this reading means the objects, not their images.
(6) But cognitive participation to shadows should place the mental state below opinion, since shadows are images of the intermediate realm rather than members of it.
(7) Call this lower state shadow-opinion. Plato never names it, but if his ontology has three levels then cognition should have matching levels too.
(8) Yet in the allegory Plato describes the prisoners arguing over which shadow is taller and treats these disputes as ordinary conflicts of doxa.
(9) So either Plato misclassifies shadow-opinion as doxa, or shadow-opinion is actually a form of doxa after all.
(10) If he misclassifies, then life in the cave involves no genuine cognitive states at all - just mechanical reaction to light patterns - which makes the philosopher's return pointless, since there's nobody with beliefs to correct.
(11) If shadow-opinion is doxa, then doxa can be directed at images, which collapses the three-tier ontology because the intermediate realm and the image realm become cognitively indistinguishible.
(12) Either way the cave story seems to destroy the very distinction it's meant to illustrate. Where does it actually break? If I have to choose I'd rather save (4) and (8) than (6) or (10), but I want to know what saving them costs me.
