I'm a PhD student in philosophy of physics designing a seminar exercise on Newton's bucket. I want a two-axis taxonomy that sorts actual commitments on the argument rather than sorting people by the labels they prefer. The first axis should concern the ontological status of whatever determines true rotation: a substantial container, a network of material relations, a geometric structure whose points lack primitive identity, or a dynamical field. The second axis should concern whether the bucket demonstrates a kinematic fact (a privileged state of rotation independent of the form of the laws) or a dynamical fact (rotation is constituted by law-structure, e.g., via inertia or geodesic deviation).

Please construct the grid with concrete diagnostic questions for each axis, phrased as responses to specific cases: the bucket in an otherwise empty universe; the bucket surrounded only by distant stars arranged in different symmetric and asymmetric distributions; and a rotating bucket inside a massive rotating shell in a GR setting. Each question should be answerable without knowing which named position it points to. Then locate the standard views in the grid—Newtonian absolute space, Machian relationism, Leibnizian relationism, modern substantivalism that accepts spacetime points without identity conditions, Barbour-style relational dynamics, and GR-informed structuralist positions. Note any unoccupied cells and briefly diagnose them: incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of named positions that your taxonomy cannot cleanly separate.

Finally, separate what is broadly settled in the debate (for instance, that the bucket establishes some form of non-relative rotational state) from what remains genuinely contested—such as whether that state requires substantival spacetime, whether Mach's principle is empirically viable, or whether general relativistic frame-dragging dissolves the problem—and do not present any contested interpretation as if it were the consensus.
