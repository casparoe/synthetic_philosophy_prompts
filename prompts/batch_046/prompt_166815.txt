A friend walked me into this about generations and ontology and I can't find the exit. (1) Real kinds have causal essences—members share traits because some underlying mechanism produces them. (2) If 'Gen Z' is a real kind, its essence can't be the birth years 1997-2012, since years don't cause anxiety or voting patterns; the essence would have to be the shared formative environment (social media, climate dread, etc.). (3) But that environment isn't actually shared—Gen Z in Lagos and Gen Z in rural Japan have totally different tech access and cultural conditions, yet both count as Gen Z. (4) So there's no unified causal mechanism behind the cohort label. (5) Therefore Gen Z isn't a real kind; it's just an age bracket with a marketing name attached. (6) Now, if Gen Z isn't a real kind, then any genralization like 'Gen Z is more progressive' or 'Gen Z has higher anxiety' is either an ecological fallacy or a bare stereotype with no explanatory backing. (7) But socioligists and pollsters use those generalizations all the time, and they often predict behavior well enough to shape policy. (8) So either generations are real kinds despite the heterogenity problem, or we have to reject a huge chunk of demographic sociology as conceptually confused. (9) I want to hang onto the idea that cohort data can be useful without being essentialist, and I also don't want to pretend birth years literally cause politics. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost me?
