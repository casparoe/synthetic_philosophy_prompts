As a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, I have been thinking about the paradox of fiction in the context of interactive systems that generate characters algorithmically. In these systems, a user knows in real time that a character’s dialogue and behavior are produced by a deterministic model, yet users still report genuine emotional attachment and grief when the model is updated and the character’s state is overwritten. One view holds that this transparency resolves the classic paradox: the emotion is directed at an actual, existing computational process, not at a nonexistent fictional entity. The opposing view holds that the paradox is only deepened, because the user simultaneously knows that no enduring subject of experience exists behind the outputs and yet responds with full-blooded affect. Write a concise but substantive dialogue between a defender of the “process-directed resolution” and a defender of the “deepened-paradox” position. Both interlocutors should be philosophically competent; they must engage with each other’s arguments directly, not talk past one another. Focus specifically on whether knowing that every utterance is deterministically computed undermines the emotional response, and whether resetting the model’s weights is analogous to the death of a fictional character.
