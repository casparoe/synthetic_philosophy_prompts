I'm a doctoral candidate in philosophy of mind writing a one-year dissertation prospectus for a philosophy department fellowship. My project concerns Daniel Dennett's well-known taxonomy of cognitive architectures—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures—but I want to press an angle that standard expositions usually neglect. Rather than treating these four kinds as a linear evolutionary ladder or as nested capacities that simply accumulate, I want to investigate whether Gregorian-level cultural transmission functions as an active restructuring mechanism that can retroactively modify an agent's Skinnerian conditioning regimes and constrain the space of Popperian hypotheses available to it. In other words, can cultural tools not merely extend cognition but selectively disable or rewire lower-level cognitive strategies?

The prospectus should contain: (1) a sharply defined research question that captures this interaction problem without collapsing into empirical psychology or evolutionary biology; (2) an explanation of why existing philosophical treatments of Dennett's taxonomy—which tend to present the four kinds as sequential stages—leave this cross-layer dynamic unexamined; (3) my proposed method, which will be conceptual analysis paired with structured case studies (e.g., pedagogical rituals that override innate reinforcement patterns, or linguistic framing that restricts which mental models can be entertained), with an explicit acknowledgment of what this method cannot establish; (4) a clear statement of what would count as a successful philosophical result versus a failure or a mere redescription; and (5) a realistic twelve-month timeline and scope, including where the argument risks becoming metaphorical and how to prevent that. I will supply citations and literature references myself, so please do not include a bibliography or reading list. The tone should suit a philosophy fellowship committee that expects conceptual rigor without empirical data collection.
