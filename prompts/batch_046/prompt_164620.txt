I serve on my university's Democratic Governance Working Group, and each semester we must review two or three formal proposals to replace an elected or appointed campus body with a randomly selected panel. I need a reusable decision procedure I can run in about forty-five minutes per proposal, with no statistical tools and only my own judgment, that translates the core rationale for sortition—democratic equality combined with deliberative legitimacy—into an operational checklist. The procedure should ask questions in fixed order, specify what counts as a sufficient answer at each gate, stop with a clear rejection or referral when conditions fail, and flag exactly where a value judgment replaces a factual check. It must include explicit veto conditions (e.g., legal accreditation, irreversible harm) that override the lottery logic regardless of other scores. Apply it to three cases: (1) replacing the elected Undergraduate Budget Senate with a thirty-person random panel for discretionary fund allocation; (2) replacing the appointed Institutional Review Board for human-subjects research with pure sortition; and (3) selecting members for a temporary Climate Action Task Force with a one-year mandate. In at least one case the procedure should yield a recommendation that a strong pro-sortition advocate would dislike, and you should say precisely what the procedure cannot settle—such as whether epistemic inequality is ever a legitimate reason to abandon lot altogether. Finish by listing the three hardest judgment calls the procedure leaves untouched and noting one category of proposal for which it is explicitly not designed.
