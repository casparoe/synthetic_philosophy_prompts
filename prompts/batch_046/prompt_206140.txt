You are a senior philosopher of mind and clinical ethics consultant with expertise in medieval epistemology and modern neuropsychology. Before constructing the deliverable, take time to think carefully: brainstorm exactly which distinctions in Avicenna’s flying-man scenario—where an individual suspended in void, blindfolded and deprived of all bodily sensation, nonetheless knows that he exists—must survive translation into clinical practice, and which distinctions can be dropped without betraying the principle. Pay special attention to the gap between direct awareness of self (knowledge by presence, unmediated by body-image, memory, or inference) and reconstructed self-modeling built from residual sensory data, caregiver narration, or theory-of-mind reasoning.

Your task is to design a reusable, step-by-step decision procedure that a hospital neurology and ethics team can apply to any patient with complete or near-complete sensory isolation—such as locked-in syndrome, total deafferentation, or prolonged sensory deprivation—to determine whether direct, non-inferential self-awareness is present. The procedure must be usable by clinicians without philosophical training, must specify the exact order of questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a clear negative determination, and where it explicitly hands the decision to human judgment because the issue is one of values rather than facts. It should not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and it should not depend on highly specialized empirical findings that a general model could not be expected to have memorized.

In operationalizing the principle, preserve this core distinction: direct awareness of the self versus self-knowledge reconstructed through inference, narrative, or bodily schema. You must also invent and define one novel conceptual tool—call it a counterfactual stability criterion or a modality-gap check—that is not standard in the existing literature on Avicenna but that helps the practitioner apply the distinction in borderline cases.

The procedure must include:
1. A preliminary null-sensory baseline verification, with explicit criteria for confirming that exteroceptive and proprioceptive channels are genuinely absent or functionally inert.
2. Three ordered tests—indexical stability, reconstruction exclusion, and your invented counterfactual/modality check—each with precise pass/fail conditions.
3. Stop-rules: state exactly when the team should conclude that direct awareness is absent and the case should be treated as reconstructed self-modeling only.
4. Judgment nodes: mark the points where the procedure cannot eliminate human decision—especially where direct awareness is present but communicative capacity is permanently absent, raising questions about life-sustaining intervention, or where family values conflict with the procedural finding.
5. Three worked applications, including at least one where the procedure returns an answer the practitioner will not like:
   a) A locked-in patient with intact cortical self-monitoring but no sensory feedback, where the procedure returns positive direct-awareness and this conflicts with family requests to withdraw care.
   b) A patient with severe anterograde amnesia and partial sensory loss whose first-person references are reconstructed entirely from caregiver narration, where the procedure returns negative.
   c) A borderline case involving intermittent sensory restoration or ambiguous neural signals that mimics direct awareness, where the procedure misfires and requires explicit human override.
6. After the cases, a brief honest accounting: what the procedure cannot do, where it risks false positives or false negatives, and which philosophical objections to the original flying-man argument—such as the claim that even in the void the subject retains an implicit bodily schema—threaten specific steps.

Write formally and precisely. Do not quote any source verbatim; paraphrase the flying-man scenario without quotation marks. Do not depend on specific publication dates or obscure attributions. Start with a short conceptual-translation section outlining your brainstorming, then present the procedure in clear ordered steps, then run the three cases, then close with the limits and misfire analysis.
