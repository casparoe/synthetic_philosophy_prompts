I'm writing my response paper for a graduate seminar in the philosophy of physics, and I want to defend a particular resolution of Einstein's hole argument that I haven't seen fully developed in the literature. Here is the position I want to argue for: the hole argument fails to establish a genuine metaphysical indeterminacy for substantivalist interpretations of general relativity, provided we sharply distinguish the active diffeomorphism invariance of the Einstein field equations—which maps a given metric and matter distribution to a mathematically different tensor field on the same manifold—from the passive coordinate redundancy that Einstein's point-coincidence reply addresses. I claim that treating the metric as a dynamical field rather than as an intrinsic property of a pre-existing spacetime container means that applying an active diffeomorphism does not generate a physically distinct state of affairs; it is a redescription of the same relational structure among dynamical fields. Consequently, the "indeterminacy" the hole argument reveals is purely representational, not ontological. Please construct the strongest version of this argument, and specifically address the objection that this move collapses substantivalism into a disguised relationalism that cannot accommodate the local degrees of freedom of gravity. Also explain why Einstein's point-coincidence argument, while resolving the formal ambiguity, does not by itself answer this deeper metaphysical worry, and how the dynamical-field interpretation closes the remaining gap without abandoning a substantivalist commitment to the reality of spacetime points.
