Write a one-year fellowship proposal (approximately two pages, suitable for a philosophy or interdisciplinary history-of-ideas program) framing a precise conceptual question that applies Fernand Braudel’s tripartite temporal hierarchy—geographical time, social time, and the time of events and individuals—to the normative theory of constitutional durability. State the precise research question: if climate and geography impose constraints that persist across constitutional generations without being chosen by any constitutional agent, do they constitute a legitimate form of Ulysses-contract-like binding, and does this strengthen or weaken the democratic case for revising institutions in response to new social values?
