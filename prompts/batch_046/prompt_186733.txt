I serve as a peer reviewer for an interdisciplinary journal that receives frequent submissions proposing evolutionary explanations for contemporary human behaviors and cultural patterns. Our board needs a reusable decision procedure—one a reviewer without specialized evolutionary biology training can apply consistently in about thirty minutes—to distinguish legitimate, testable adaptationist hypotheses from unfalsifiable just-so stories. Please construct the procedure as an ordered sequence of questions, specifying for each what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure terminates with a clear rejection, and where it must defer to expert judgment because the issue is conceptual rather than procedural. The procedure should explicitly incorporate the distinction among adaptations, exaptations, and spandrels; it should require authors to specify the environment of evolutionary adaptedness with enough ecological and temporal detail that the proposed mechanism could plausibly misfire or produce maladaptive outcomes in modern contexts; and it should include a specific check against reverse-engineering, in which an explanation is constructed post hoc from an observed modern trait rather than predicting it from an independently specified adaptive problem. Then apply the procedure to two submitted proposals: first, the claim that human preference for facial symmetry is an adaptation for detecting parasite load in potential mates, supported mainly by cross-cultural correlation studies and analogies to other species; second, the claim that compulsive checking of digital notifications is an evolved social-monitoring adaptation—analogous to primate grooming—that misfires in information-rich environments, supported by behavioral parallels and self-report data about anxiety. For at least one application, the procedure should yield a verdict that conflicts with the intuitive plausibility of the explanation. Finally, state explicitly what the procedure cannot do, particularly regarding historical or archaeological evidence that is permanently unavailable and where judgment about plausibility is unavoidable.
