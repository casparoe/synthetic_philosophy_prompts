I am preparing a graduate seminar on Sidgwick’s philosophy of science and want a consistency check before I present the framework. Here are the commitments I intend to defend:

(1) Sidgwick’s involvement with the Society for Psychical Research was not an eccentric departure from his critical rationalism but its direct extension: he treated reports of telepathy and apparitions as empirical hypotheses requiring replication and controlled conditions, not as mysteries to be embraced or dismissed on prior metaphysical grounds.

(2) His utilitarian ethics, I argue, provides no distinctive reason to investigate post-mortem survival. If death is annihilation, the dead suffer no harm; if survival obtains, the living may be comforted, but false belief itself causes suffering. The expected utility is therefore roughly neutral, and his sustained investment in psychical research must be explained by epistemic rather than moral motives.

(3) Nevertheless, I want to maintain that Sidgwick’s acknowledgment of the explanatory gap—his recognition that phenomenal consciousness is not conceptually derivable from physical descriptions—functioned for him as a defeater for the default physicalist assumption that non-physical causal interactions are impossible. This lowers the a priori barrier to telepathy without constituting proof.

(4) On testimony, I hold Sidgwick to a strict standard: a witness’s general credibility must be established independently of the particular claim being tested. Consequently, first-person experiential reports by mediums should never serve as primary data, only as prompts for experimental design.

(5) Despite (4), I regard Sidgwick’s qualified acceptance of judgments made by fellow SPR investigators—trained observers whose reliability he had established through non-psychical collaboration—as entirely rational, even when those judgments concerned anomalous phenomena.

(6) At the same time, I claim that Sidgwick’s actual evaluation of anomalous reports was governed by an implicit presumption of fraud or error that exceeded the standards he applied to ordinary scientific anomalies: he required that every conceivable physical explanation be eliminated before accepting a psychical interpretation, a burden he did not impose on chemical or physiological discoveries.

Is this package jointly tenable? Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity; for each genuine tension, specify which commitment is the weakest link and what abandoning or modifying it costs my interpretation.
