Please lay out the explicit correspondence in continuous prose—what corresponds to the individual agent, the shared plan, the mechanism of coordination, the collective outcome, and the external recognition of unity—then identify precisely where the mapping fractures, especially with respect to whether collective intentionality requires pre-existing common knowledge of every participant's role in the way that orchestral performance requires knowledge of the score, and whether the analogy unfairly assumes that joint action needs an external designer or audience to constitute its unity. Finally, assess whether the irreducibility thesis—that there is genuinely collective intentionality over and above individual intentions—survives once these disanalogies are made explicit, or whether the analogy was silently supplying the conclusion all along.
