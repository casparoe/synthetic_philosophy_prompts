If we apply reference-class forecasting to the longevity of centralized states, selecting the class of agrarian empires that maintained both a unified legal code across heterogeneous regions and a single fiat currency, the observed base rate for survival beyond three centuries is zero—every member collapsed within that window. Now consider the emerging global administrative order: it enforces uniform digital contract protocols across jurisdictions and relies on a dominant reserve currency, so structurally it belongs to this class. Given that the outside view assigns essentially zero probability to persistence past 2300, is it rational to allow any inside-view reasoning—say, about adaptive institutions or technological change—to update this forecast upward? Or does this framing already commit a subtle error by treating technological form as an invariant membership condition rather than a variable that can break the reference class?
