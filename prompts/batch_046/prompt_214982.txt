I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy or law, but I've been working through my own position on speech regulation and platform governance, and I want a consistency audit before I present it in a discussion group. Here is the package of commitments I've arrived at:

(1) Speech that systematically dehumanizes members of a protected group causes tangible, documented harm—psychological trauma, democratic erosion, and normalization of violence—so democratic societies have a legitimate interest in restricting it through law, not merely through private disapproval.

(2) The American First Amendment standard requiring imminent lawless action is too permissive: it protects a great deal of targeted dehumanization that never reaches direct incitement but still corrupts public discourse.

(3) Algorithmic recommendation systems change the moral status of a platform: ranking and amplifying content is active curation, not passive hosting, so promoting hate speech is closer to endorsing it than to merely allowing it.

(4) Precisely because platforms are curators, governments should not dictate specific moderation rules to them; editorial autonomy should be protected, and regulation should take the form of transparency and user-choice mechanisms rather than mandated take-down lists or algorithmic prescriptions.

(5) Given (3), allowing algorithmic amplification of dehumanizing content is harmful enough that platforms should be legally required to suppress it in recommendation feeds—not merely permitted to do so.

(6) At the same time, exposure to offensive or hateful ideas in non-amplified contexts—direct messages, unranked forums, or structured debate—has instrumental value for developing critical reasoning and resilience, so blanket suppression everywhere is paternalistic and counterproductive.

(7) Any restriction, whether legal or platform-based, should satisfy a least-restrictive-means test: it must target the specific harm without suppressing legitimate discourse about the same groups or issues.

My question is whether these hang together. I am not asking for a lecture on free-speech theory; I want an audit of this specific set. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled on inspection, and what is the least costly revision for each real tension? Please order the tensions by severity, and for each say which commitment would have to give and what that costs me. Do not recommend abandoning more than is necessary; I want to know the weakest link in the chain, not a wholesale replacement of my views.
