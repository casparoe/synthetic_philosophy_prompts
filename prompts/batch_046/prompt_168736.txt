I cover science and philosophy of science for a magazine, and I need a reusable decision procedure for whether unrealistic assumptions in a research model should stop me from reporting its conclusions. The procedure should translate what philosophy of science actually distinguishes—Friedman’s F-twist about predictive success licensing unrealistic premises, but also the differences between assumptions that isolate a mechanism, those that distort causal structure, and those that invoke entities with no empirical counterpart—into a checklist I can run in about fifteen minutes without consulting the original papers. I need explicit decision nodes, ordered steps, and clear markers for where I am making an editorial judgment rather than applying a rule. It must include: a quick typology of the unrealistic assumption by the work it performs in the model; a test for whether the study’s predictive success actually bears on the specific claim I am writing about; a hard branch for models that predict well despite describing non-existent entities or impossible dynamics; and an explicit statement of what the procedure cannot settle, particularly around normative modeling choices. Apply it to three studies: a climate projection that treats atmospheric turbulence as a uniform flow; a neuroscience paper that models neurons as deterministic binary switches; and a behavioral-economics intervention study whose unrealistic rational-actor baseline predicts outcomes well—this last one being a case where I want the finding to hold but the procedure should advise caution. For each, show the step-by-step outcome, flag any judgment call, and say what the procedure leaves unresolved.
