I am a high school junior writing an independent study on the ontology of mathematics. From my reading, finitism maintains that only finite mathematical objects genuinely exist, rejecting so-called actual infinities—completed totalities such as the full set of natural numbers—while permitting potential infinity, understood merely as an unending process of extension. Meanwhile, Cantor's analysis of transfinite cardinals and Hilbert's hotel are often presented in popular accounts as intuitive demonstrations that actual infinities are not only coherent but possess counterintuitive structural properties, such as a set being equipotent with a proper subset of itself. This has left me with a confusion I cannot resolve on my own. If finitists deny actual infinity on philosophical rather than logical grounds, must they also reject the standard calculus treatment of limits approaching infinity, since that endpoint seems to presuppose a completed infinite magnitude rather than a mere potentiality? Furthermore, since Hilbert's hotel exhibits internal consistency among infinite collections—showing, for instance, that a fully occupied infinite hotel can still accommodate additional guests—does this not amount to a direct demonstration that actual infinity is logically sound, thereby reducing finitism to an arbitrary preference rather than a philosophically defensible position? I would be grateful for a careful explanation of precisely where my reasoning errs.
