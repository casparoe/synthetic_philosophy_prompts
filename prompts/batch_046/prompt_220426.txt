My municipality is debating an “inclusionary upzoning” ordinance that would grant developers a floor-area-ratio bonus in exchange for below-market-rate units, while permitting triplex construction on lots previously restricted to single-family homes. At the public hearing, a planning advocate dismissed objections about lost privacy, changed streetscapes, and disrupted community ties by insisting that “a neighborhood’s character is not property anyone can own.” What are the different precise theses that slogan could be expressing—from tautological claims about the non-assignability of collective atmospheres, to normative arguments about the priority of housing access over aesthetic stability, to metaphysical denials that groups can hold property rights? Lay the readings out as a menu, indicate which ones actually support dismissing the objections raised, and say which ones the advocate probably intended versus which ones would require further argument to defend.
