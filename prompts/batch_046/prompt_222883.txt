I have just spent an evening arguing with a close friend about diachronic rationality, and I want to stress-test the position I ended up defending. I hold that money-pump objections across time only carry normative force if one already accepts psychological continuity as the correct account of personal identity: without that metaphysical commitment, a sequence of preferences that allows pumping is not one agent being irrational, but merely distinct temporal stages transferring resources. So diachronic coherence collapses into a thesis about persistence. What would count against this? I am not asking for the familiar instrumental reply that people happen to care about their futures; I want the arguments, formal results, or empirical findings that could break the link I am asserting—whether in decision theory, in the metaphysics of personal identity, or in the study of intertemporal choice. Which defeaters would be fatal, which merely awkward? And is my position dangerously insulated: if any plausible theory of persistence can be recruited to do the same work, have I rendered the claim unfalsifiable?
