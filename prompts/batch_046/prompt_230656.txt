Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between behavioral reconstructionists and cognitive symbolists in archaeology, on the question of how institutional beliefs and social structures can be inferred from material objects without direct textual or ethnographic access.

The reconstructionists maintain that institutional patterns—authority relations, obligation networks, exchange protocols—are readable from spatial distributions, use-wear sequences, manufacturing choices, and discard patterns alone, without attributing propositional beliefs or symbolic intentions to past agents. The symbolists maintain that material traces radically underdetermine institutional interpretations; unless one first reconstructs a symbolic framework that assigns representational content to artifacts, the same physical pattern is compatible with radically different belief structures, including none at all.

Produce the document both parties could sign. First, a numbered section of agreed claims about the archaeological record, the epistemic limits of direct observation, and the conditions that must be met for an inference about past institutions to be warranted rather than speculative. Second, a disagreements table with two columns: on the left, the reconstructionists' position in wording they fully accept; on the right, the symbolists' position in wording they fully accept. The disagreement must be stated precisely and without straw-manning either side.

Third, identify the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would cause at least one camp to revise its view. At least one crux must concern "threshold artifacts": objects that exhibit clear functional wear or manufacturing investment but are deliberately deposited in contexts that serve no obvious practical purpose (for example, broken implements placed in foundation trenches or sealed in non-utilitarian chambers). For each crux, specify a concrete archaeological case or analytical procedure that both sides agree would bear on it, and record each side's explicit, advance prediction about what that case or procedure would demonstrate.

Begin the document itself immediately, with no preface, commentary, or restatement of the task.
