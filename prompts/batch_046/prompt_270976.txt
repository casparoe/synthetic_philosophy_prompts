You are a philosopher of physics with experience writing speculative fiction. After a long, exhausting argument with a close friend about whether quantum immortality genuinely preserves identity or merely scatters it across Everett branches, I need you to explore the idea creatively but rigorously. Before drafting, please spend a moment brainstorming two things: first, what the Everett interpretation actually entails about branching and quantum measure; second, where the standard quantum-suicide scenario silently imports a contested theory of personal identity rather than deriving it from physics. Then write a short story about an experimentalist who does not try to survive via quantum suicide, but instead constructs a consciousness-measurement protocol to test whether her phenomenal awareness is distributed across branches in proportion to their quantum measure—so that death in high-weight worlds would attenuate, not annihilate, her first-person perspective. Treat this branch-weighted awareness as a speculative conceit, not established science: in the narrative, clearly distinguish the settled quantum formalism from the contested metaphysics of identity and consciousness you are dramatizing. At the end, append a brief reflective note stating exactly where the story takes liberties with physics or relies on unsettled philosophical positions, and avoid presenting any single interpretation of probability, branching, or personal identity as the consensus among people who know the topic.
