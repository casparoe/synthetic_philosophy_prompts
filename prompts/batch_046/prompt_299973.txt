Consider a critic working within W. E. B. Du Bois’s framework — in which artistic production must serve as propaganda advancing racial truth and justice, yet must not abandon aesthetic integrity to mere message — who has rendered the following judgments about specific works: (1) a 1934 Chicago library mural depicting Black agricultural laborers in an unadorned social-realist mode, praised as art fulfilling its highest duty; (2) a 1941 jazz suite using atonal harmony and recorded field sounds of prison work gangs, performed with program notes linking its structure to the Middle Passage, praised as truth that need not please; (3) a 1936 novel portraying Black urban professionals with comic irony while entirely omitting racial violence, economic exploitation, or segregation, criticized as a dangerous evasion of the artist’s obligation; (4) a 1928 photographic series of Black church interiors composed with strict geometric formalism and exhibited without captions, praised for rendering dignity visible without pleading; and (5) a 1930 lyric poem in strict Petrarchan form celebrating Black maternal care, containing no reference to racism or oppression, praised as beauty that functions as resistance. Now take a new case: a 2024 immersive virtual-reality installation created by a collective of Black digital artists that reconstructs a 1927 Harlem rent party with period-accurate music, dialect, and spatial design, allowing mostly wealthy, white museum patrons to inhabit the scene; it provides no narrative explanation of the economic exploitation that made such gatherings necessary, requires expensive equipment to access, and pursues emotional immersion rather than political instruction, though it is technically sophisticated and historically faithful in surface detail. Predict whether this critic would praise or reject the installation; identify at least two distinct rules that equally well explain her five earlier verdicts, specifying what each rule predicts about the installation; state which rule you think she is actually running and why; and name the single further hypothetical case that would best discriminate between those rules. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then provide the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
