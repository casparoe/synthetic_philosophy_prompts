I have been working through the tension between liberal bioethics' framing of reproduction and relational ontologies of the self, and I want you to construct a rigorous argument for a specific, somewhat unconventional synthesis. Begin from the Ubuntu premise—often rendered as the idea that a person is constituted through other persons—not as a sociological observation but as a metaphysical claim about the conditions of moral agency. Argue that if personhood is genuinely relational in this constitutive sense, then an agent's identity is partly determined by her position within a diachronic web of recognition that extends both backward through ancestors and forward through descendants. Call this the intergenerational relational constitution thesis. From there, defend the position that reproductive choices are not merely exercises in individual preference or harm-prevention toward a hypothetical child, but are structural interventions in the very network that constitutes the chooser's own moral personhood. This should lead you to reject the standard liberal separation between procreative liberty and the independent moral status of the future person, while also showing why the view does not collapse into a crude communitarianism that suppresses individual deliberation.

Please make the argument concrete: address how this framework evaluates cases where reproductive technology might produce severe disability, or where the decision is not to reproduce at all, without relying on a simple consequentialist calculus about the stability of the relational network. Explain specifically why treating reproduction as network-constituting avoids both the atomism implicit in liberal eugenics and the reduction of persons to functional nodes in a system. I am developing this for a conceptual ethics course that is skeptical of both individualist rights-talk and totalizing social determinism, so the argument needs to preserve a meaningful, non-instrumental space for individual choice while denying that choice is metaphysically prior to relation.
