A philosophy prof in my metaphyics seminar summed up the special composition debate like this: "Universalists think any two things make a whole, so there's literally an object made of your left shoe and the Eiffel Tower. Nihilists deny that parts ever form a real object—there are just simple particles arranged shoe-wise or tower-wise, and talk of 'tables' is just a useful fiction. The restricted view tries to split the difference by saying composition only happens when the parts are conected or fuctioning together, like in an organism or a machine. So universalism is crazy because it multiplies entities beyond reason, nihilism is self-defeating because then you don't exist, and restricted composition is obviosly the sane middle ground that saves common sense. Plus universalists are stuck with mereological essentialism—if one particle in the shoe-tower object changes, the whole thing is destroyed."

I feel like there's something slippery here, especially in how he sets up the nihilist and the restricted view. Is this fair? Which parts are accurate descriptions, which are strawmen or oversimplifications, and does his conclusion that restricted composition is the "obvious middle ground" actually follow? Also I'm not sure if he messed up what universalists actually say about arbitrary combinations—do they really think there's one object per pairing, and is mereological essentialism really part of their view?
