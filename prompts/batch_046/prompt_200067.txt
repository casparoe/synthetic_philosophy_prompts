Write a dialogue between two equally knowledgeable researchers—Dr. Aisha Okonkwo, who favors process-based supervision, and Dr. Julian Reed, who favors outcome-based supervision—debating how to oversee an AI system that manages long-horizon planning for interdependent critical infrastructure, such as power grid and water distribution networks that must adapt to cascading failures over months-long horizons.

Okonkwo should argue that when a system exhibits what she calls structural semantic drift—the phenomenon where intermediate reasoning representations change their functional interpretation across different load and failure regimes without changing their surface syntax—outcome metrics alone cannot detect reasoning corruption in the unobserved intermediate states that precede visible failures. She should insist that only granular process auditing, tracking how the system constructs and revises causal models at each planning stage, can reveal dangerous misrepresentations before they compound into catastrophic outcomes.

Reed should respond that attempting to audit intermediate representations under structural semantic drift is either impossible or secretly outcome-dependent: any criterion for correct intermediate reasoning must eventually be calibrated against observed system behavior, and for long-horizon domains the relevant outcomes are by definition not yet available. He should further argue that process audits in such domains generate prohibitive false positives—flagging adaptive representational shifts as errors—thereby disabling the very flexibility that makes the system useful in novel failure modes.

The dialogue should feature direct back-and-forth: Okonkwo must address Reed's calibration objection specifically, and Reed must answer her concern about corruption in unobserved intermediate states rather than dismissing it generally. Neither character should be a strawman. Each should concede one substantive weakness in their own position by the end, without reaching a false consensus.

Keep the dialogue technically precise but accessible, and do not include requests for citations, bibliographies, or references to specific published papers.
