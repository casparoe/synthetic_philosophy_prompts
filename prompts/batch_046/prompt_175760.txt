I have an argument about chaos and determinism that seems to show determinism is false for every physical system we actually study, and I want to know where the sleight of hand is. The steps are:

(1) A system is deterministic if and only if its exact present state, together with the governing laws, fixes one and only one future trajectory.
(2) The shadowing lemma for hyperbolic chaotic dynamics establishes that for any approximate trajectory—any sequence of states within epsilon of the true dynamics—there exist infinitely many exact deterministic trajectories that stay within epsilon of that approximation for all time.
(3) Every actual measurement or observation of a physical system has finite precision, so any observed present state is necessarily only an approximate trajectory.
(4) Therefore, given any observed state in a chaotic system, the laws together with that observation do not single out one future; they are compatible with infinitely many exact deterministic futures, each shadowing the observation.
(5) So determinism, applied to observed states, fails to determine a unique outcome.
(6) But determinism is only an interesting metaphysical claim if it applies to real systems, and real systems are never accessed except through approximate observation.
(7) Hence determinism is false for all actually observed chaotic systems.
(8) Because macroscopic physics—weather, neural dynamics, turbulent flow—is pervasively chaotic, determinism is false for the actual world.

The conclusion feels wrong: it doesn't just say determinism is hidden, it says it's outright false, as if chaos destroyed the uniqueness relation rather than hiding it. I'd like to keep (1) and (2) intact, and I want to preserve the idea that chaos genuinely undermines predictive access without thereby refuting determinism. Give me a committed answer: identify the specific step or transition where the reasoning slips, explain why that step looked defensible, and say what giving it up costs—particularly whether I must abandon the shadowing lemma's relevance or only restrict its interpretation.
