Write a sustained parody of a luxury wellness retreat marketing brochure, approximately 600 words, in the deadpan, aspirational voice of an upscale travel magazine feature. The target is tranquilism—the philosophical position that only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic value, while positive pleasure is at best instrumentally useless and at worst a deceptive precursor to further craving. To prevent the satire from drifting into generic wellness mockery, build the piece around an invented in-house taxonomy of "residual cravings" that the retreat claims to diagnose and eliminate: anticipatory craving, retrospective nostalgia-craving, vicarious craving for others' satisfactions, and phantom-limb craving for pleasures that were never actually experienced. Describe specific programs—the Zero-Desire Suite, the Negative Pleasure Detox, and the Silent Craving Audit—that treat accidental joy as a service failure warranting partial refund. The humor must arise from exaggerating genuine structural features of the view: its asymmetry between suffering and happiness, its tendency to regard satisfied desire with suspicion, its reduction of well-being to a mere null state, and its practical difficulty in distinguishing contentment from absence. Keep the irony sustained and deadpan: the narrator should never wink, and a reader unfamiliar with the philosophy should still understand the retreat's logic, while a specialist should find the exaggeration fair rather than straw-manning. Do not include headings, bullet points, bold text, lists, or any other formatting; present the entire piece as continuous prose, as in a single flowing letter or uninterrupted magazine article. Do not provide citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts.
