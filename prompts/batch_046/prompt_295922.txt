In my political theory group we've been stuck on open borders vs the right to exclude for like six months now. The cosmopolitan side says if suffering is bad and borders cause suffering, exclusion needs a justification stronger than "citizenship is arbitrary". The other side says democratic communities have associative obligations that include controlling membership, and comparing states to clubs isn't sloppy analogy, its the core claim. We agree on the empirical stuff—migration numbers, welfare effects—and we all sign onto basic equality of moral worth. Nobody moves an inch.

My hunch is the stall isn't about immigration policy at all, but about whether collective entities like states can bear moral rights that don't reduce to summed-up individual rights—like a group-level claim to self-determination that blocks entry even when no one is harmed. But maybe I'm wrong and its verbal: one side asks what justice requires globally, the other asks what makes a democratic decision legitimate, and they just share a keyword.

What kind of disagreement is this, actually? A hidden metaphysical question about collective agency, different standards for what counts as a moral reason, or bedrock intuitions about political community that neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, is there anything—maybe a distinction between exclusion-as-harm and exclusion-as-wrong, or treating territorial rights like revocable licenses rather than property—that would actually shift it? I want diagnosis with reasons and an honest answer about whether progress is even possible, not a survey of positions.
