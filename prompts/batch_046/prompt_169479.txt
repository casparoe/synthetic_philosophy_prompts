I am currently taking a graduate seminar in the philosophy of rationality and artificial agency, and I am drafting an essay on the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents. Rather than submitting a polished draft, I have included my working notes and central thesis below, and I would appreciate your critical comments on coherence, accuracy, and philosophical interest.

My notes: The canonical Löbian obstacle shows that a rational agent with sufficient logical strength cannot consistently believe that its own reasoning is sound, because such a belief would trigger Löb's theorem and generate an unconditional belief in any proposition it considers. Most discussions treat this as a problem for fully reflective agents—particularly in AI alignment contexts—who wish to endorse their own decision procedures without circularity.

I want to propose a two-part revision. First, I distinguish between syntactic proof-access and semantic evaluation-access. An agent might lack the internal capacity to construct a formal derivation of its own soundness (thereby blocking the antecedent of the Löbian conditional) while still possessing a rational, high-credence belief in its reliability grounded in external calibration, evolutionary design, or empirical track record. Second, I ask whether the obstacle genuinely applies to probabilistic agents that represent confidence through credence functions rather than through a binary modal belief operator. Since Löb's theorem governs modal logics of belief, it is unclear whether a Bayesian agent that updates on self-observational evidence faces an analogous contradiction, or whether the problem is merely relocated into the prior.

Could you comment specifically on whether this distinction between proof-access and evaluation-access is philosophically stable? Am I mischaracterizing the standard formulation of the obstacle? And does the probabilistic framing genuinely dissolve the loop, or does it just push the self-reference into the prior selection? Any suggestions for sharpening the argument or identifying hidden premises would be welcome.
