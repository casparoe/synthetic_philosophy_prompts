I sit on the funding adjudication committee for our university's Interdisciplinary Science Initiative, and a proposal that arrived last month has forced us to confront a boundary question I had previously treated as purely academic. The applicants—teams from theoretical physics and philosophy of science—seek five years of institutional funding for what they term a Center for Landscape Epistemology. Their project explicitly abandons empirical prediction as a success criterion, arguing that within string theory's multiverse landscape—where conventional estimates place the number of possible vacua far beyond any exhaustive observational survey—direct empirical selection is structurally impossible. Instead, they propose to validate multiverse hypotheses through what they call "inter-theoretic coherence profiling" and "explanatory depth metrics": formal methods that rank theoretical models by internal consistency, mathematical uniqueness, and cross-framework unification rather than by experimental correspondence. The committee is split. One faction holds that institutional science funding must preserve an empirical-access condition, however distant, and that approving this proposal would set a precedent for funding metaphysical speculation under scientific cover. The opposing faction, which includes several philosophers of science, argues that non-empirical confirmation is already implicit in parts of cosmology and mathematics, and that refusing funding on empirical grounds alone applies a criterion that is already obsolete for this domain.

I need a formal written opinion—circulable before Thursday's meeting—that recommends approval, conditional approval with specific governance milestones, or rejection. The opinion should take a definite position, defend it with criteria that apply specifically to funding bodies rather than to individual scientists, and treat the strongest counter-argument with intellectual honesty rather than dismissal. This debate has become personally relevant because I recently co-taught a seminar on scientific realism, and I realize my own commitments about the empirical boundary are less settled than I assumed; I want a memo that does not merely survey options but argues for one concrete course of action, including, if conditions are imposed, exactly what those conditions should be and how they would be enforced. Please be explicit about whether non-empirical strategies like coherence profiling represent a defensible expansion of scientific method in this institutional context, or whether they risk collapsing the distinction between empirical science and speculative metaphysics in ways that a university funding body should actively resist.
