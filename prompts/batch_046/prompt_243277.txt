Here is my opening challenge. I have been thinking about what I call “observer-dependent contingency.” Suppose that tomorrow a sea battle may occur, but only if no one investigates the harbor. If a messenger goes to check whether the sailors are preparing, they see him and stay home—so there is no battle, but precisely because he looked. If he stays away, they sail and fight. The future seems open, yet my very act of checking appears to determine it. Is the contingency in the nature of things themselves, or is it only in my relation to them? Does this preserve your claim that not all future events are necessary, or does it undermine it?
