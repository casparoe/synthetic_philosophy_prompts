I'm trying to stabilize my view on historiographical periodization before a workshop and want a consistency audit. Here is where I stand: (1) The "Middle Ages" is not merely a humanist slur but picks out a real object—roughly Latin Christendom from 500 to 1500, characterized by manuscript culture, ecclesiastical legal frameworks, and feudal political fragmentation—though the label itself originated polemically. (2) The "Renaissance" (c. 1300–1600) is largely a 19th-century construct: it was not an epochal rupture or rebirth but an intensification and geographic diffusion of late-medieval scholastic, urban, and courtly dynamics. (3) By contrast, the "Scientific Revolution" (c. 1550–1700) marks a genuine discontinuity in practices of inquiry, including institutionalized experimental replication, the mathematization of nature, and the separation of empirical investigation from theological authority. (4) Period labels are legitimate only when they track differences in the causal mechanisms by which societies produce, validate, and transmit knowledge—not when they serve pedagogical convenience or collective identity. (5) I reject strong presentism: past intellectual cultures should not be dismissed for failing to anticipate modern science or secular democratic norms. (6) The reason "Middle Ages" persists in curricula is not inertia but that it captures a real contraction in urban secular literacy and long-distance commercial networks relative to both late antiquity and the post-1500 world.
