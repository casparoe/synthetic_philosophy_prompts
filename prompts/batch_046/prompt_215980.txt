Write a dialogue between A and B arguing over whether a mayor handing city contracts to her cousin is corruption when every voter expects kin loyalty and sees it as care, not theft. A should defend an "unbundling" view—corruption is betraying the internal purpose of a public role, not violating some imported Western rule about impartiality that was never actually lived there. B should push back that this gets too relativist: if the cousin overcharges and pockets the margin, that's extraction, and people inside the system often resent the skimming too; rejecting Western standards as universal doesn't mean local norms erase harm. Make them actually respond—B should ask how A handles a patron who genuinely helps clients but still skims surplus, and A should ask whether B's "extraction" criterion secretly smuggles back in Western property-rights assumptions. Keep it casual, maybe misspell recieve somewhere, and don't let either side sound like a strawman.
