Construct a thought experiment, not exceeding one paragraph of narrative, situated within a Gödel-type rotating universe in which the metric permits closed timelike curves but contains no technology more exotic than a spacecraft following an allowed timelike geodesic. The protagonist should be an ordinary human observer with standard cognitive capacities who records timestamped log entries immediately before and after traversing a curve that returns the craft to an earlier coordinate region of the manifold. Engineer the scenario so that three theories of time deliver distinct, non-collapsing verdicts that follow from their core commitments rather than being stipulated: (i) presentism entails that the observer never genuinely returns to a previously real present, because only the current temporal slice exists, and therefore declares the traversal metaphysically impossible despite being geometrically permitted; (ii) eternalism (the block universe) treats the traversal as unremarkable spatial-like motion along a worldline that intersects an earlier coordinate, with no ontological distinction between "going back" and "going forward" in time; (iii) the growing-block theory, which takes the past and present as real but the future as open, holds that the observer returns to a genuinely real earlier block but cannot alter it, yielding a deterministic loop that is compatible with an open future only if the loop lies entirely within the already-fixed past region. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate for each theory why its verdict follows from its ontological commitments, identify the specific structural feature of the Gödel metric—specifically, the absence of a global Cauchy surface and the defeat of any universal time ordering—that forces the three theories to diverge, explain what happens to all three verdicts if that feature is removed and spacetime becomes Minkowski, and conclude with the precise objection a presentist defender would raise against allowing this cosmological model to license eternalist conclusions—particularly, whether the presentist can reject the metric's global topology as physically irrelevant or as already presupposing eternalism in its mathematical formulation.
