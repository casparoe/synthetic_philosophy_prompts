I'm in a philosophy of mind / logic seminar and our prof asked me to draft a final exam question for the grad section. I want it on the Gödelian analogy in Hofstadter's GEB — the move from self-referential formulas to self-aware minds — but I need it to be something students can't just answer by reciting the strange loop narrative they've seen in every pop-philosophy thread. I was thinking about this tension: Gödel numbering is an extensional encoding, right? The formula refers to a number that represents it syntactically, not to itself in any first-person, indexical way. But self-awareness seems to be exactly that kind of direct I-reference, not reducible to a proper name or a Gödel number. So the question should force students to say whether Hofstadter's tangled hierachy actually bridges this gap or just relocates the problem. And maybe have them consider Kripke's idea that natural language can contain its own truth predicate without a metalanguage — if that's right, does it help Hofstadter avoid the charge that he's smuggling an intensional relation through an extensional backdoor? I need the exam question, a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme for a 90-minute essay that separates A answers from B answers by listing specific moves they must make — like correctly explaining Gödel numbering, identifying the extensional/intensional mismatch, using the tangled hierarchy precisely, engaging Kripke beyond just naming him, and taking a clear position on whether the analogy survives. Please write everything in American English.
