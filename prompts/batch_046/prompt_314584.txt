In our philosophy seminar we discussed the thought experiment in The Mind's I in which Einstein's brain is kept alive in a vat and continues to respond to questions. A fellow student used this to defend a broad analogy: he said that Einstein's brain is essentially a living book—both are repositories of organized information that can generate answers without a body, and both are "read" or activated by external prompts. He then argued that if consciousness is just this kind of structured information access, then a sufficiently complex interactive book—say, an AI-annotated encyclopedia that updates its own pages—could literally be conscious, and biological substrate is therefore irrelevant. I want to audit this analogy rigorously. Could you lay out the correspondence table—what plays the role of the pages, the binding, the reader, the author, the printing press? Then identify the precise points of breakdown: does the brain's real-time causal coupling with an environment map onto anything in the book? Does the analogy silently substitute passive storage for active integration? Finally, tell me whether the anti-biological conclusion actually survives once the disanalogies are explicit, or whether the analogy was carrying the argument all along.
