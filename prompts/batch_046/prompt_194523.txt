Popper's problem of verisimilitude asks how one false scientific theory can be closer to the truth than another false one. Miller and Tichý showed that under Popper's logical-distance metric, if one false theory entails more true statements than a rival, it necessarily entails more false ones too; from this I infer that adding empirical content to a false theory automatically raises its verisimilitude, which seems to make scientific progress inevitable—merely accumulate predictions. Shouldn't we therefore abandon logical consequence measures and rank theories instead by predictive divergence (e.g., KL-divergence over shared observation spaces), since predictive accuracy avoids the content-tradeoff entirely? Or does the Miller-Tichý collapse actually undermine every quantitative account of approaching the truth, not just Popper's specific formalism? Answer in one paragraph, no more than 120 words.
