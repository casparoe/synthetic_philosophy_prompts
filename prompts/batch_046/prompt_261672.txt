I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher of mathematics who defends what she calls the "modal-epistemic" interpretation of the Church-Turing thesis. On her view, the thesis is neither a definitional stipulation about "effective calculability" (the logical interpretation) nor an empirical claim about the physical world (the physical Church-Turing thesis), but a modal constraint on physical theorizing: any law that is genuinely epistemically accessible to us must be computable by a Turing machine, because a non-computable law would place its predictions outside the reach of verification procedures we could in principle carry out. She accepts that analog devices or oracles could in principle compute non-computable functions, but she argues these rely on physically unrealistic idealizations—specifically, infinite-precision measurements that violate the noise-tolerance conditions required for scientific testing—so they do not undermine the constraint. She further claims that quantum speedup poses no threat, since quantum computers remain within the computable, and that her view explains why hypercomputation proposals feel conceptually unstable without committing her to the metaphysical claim that nature is fundamentally discrete.

Draft a sequence of six to eight questions for the Q&A. I want them to move past mere exposition: at least one should press her on whether her epistemic constraint collapses into a hidden metaphysical claim about the structure of nature; one should challenge the analog-computing reply by asking how she distinguishes legitimate idealization in physics (point masses, frictionless planes) from the illegitimate idealization she attributes to analog hypercomputation; one should ask what her view implies for the status of quantum mechanics if a future theory turned out to require genuinely non-computable dynamics; and for each, include a brief follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give (for instance, if she appeals to verificationism, ask whether that commits her to an anti-realist stance about unobservable entities; if she distinguishes idealization by noise-tolerance, ask why noise-tolerance should have modal weight rather than being a merely practical limitation). The sequence should build toward a final question about whether her interpretation makes the Church-Turing thesis unfalsifiable by definitional fiat, and if not, what empirical finding could genuinely overturn it.
