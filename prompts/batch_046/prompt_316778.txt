I've been trying to wrap my head around Weber's Protestant ethic thing for a course paper, and I keep running into the same wall: everyone says culture can't be a 'cause' in the same way a tax rate or a drought is, but then they still want Weber to be saying something explanitory. So here's the position I'm tentatively holding, and I want you to tell me what's actually at risk with it.

My view is that Weber isn't really claiming that Calvinist values efficiently caused capitalism in the Humean sense. Instead, I think he's describing what I'd call a 'constraint-lock' mechanism: the ethic doesn't push people toward capital accumulation like a force, but it eliminates competing normative frameworks (like the traditionalist disdain for rationalized profit) so that capitalist practice becomes the only stable equilibrium once material conditions are ripe. So culture here isn't a cause like rainfall; it's more like a filter that removes alternatives, and the 'causation' is really just the absence of cultural resistence allowing material factors to do all the pushing. I know Weber talks about elective affinity, but I'm treating that affinity as essentially modal—it's about which combinations of values and practices can co-exist without internal contradiction, not about values generating practices.

What would actually count against this? I don't want the usual 'correlation isn't causation' dismissal—I'm already conceding it's not efficient causation. I want to know: what specific historical evidence or conceptual argument would show that the 'constraint-lock' framing fails? For instance, if there are clear cases where capitalist practices emerged despite strong cultural resistence that wasn't eliminated, or if Weber's own text commits him to values having direct motivational force rather than just filtering? Also, does this view risk being unfalsifiable—if any capitalist emergence can be retroactively described as the absence of resistance? And what would defenders of the standard 'values as causes' reading say is wrong with reducing Weber to a modal filter? I want an honest map of where this position is exposed, including whether insulating it from counterexamples by calling it 'modal' is intellectually honest or just a dodge.
