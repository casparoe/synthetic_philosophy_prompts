I'm auditing my own views on cosmological fine-tuning and want a consistency check before presenting them. Here is where I stand:
(1) The life-permitting ranges for several fundamental constants are narrow enough that, treating them as free parameters, their actual conjunction is highly specific.
(2) I reject the multiverse explanation because it lacks empirical accessibility and violates ontological parsimony.
(3) I think the inverse gambler's fallacy undermines the inference from "our constants permit life" to "there are likely many universes," though I worry standard presentations of the fallacy smuggle in an unjustified reference-class.
(4) I deny there is any physically privileged measure over constant-space, so claims about the "probability" of our constants are technically ill-formed.
(5) I accept that weak anthropic selection dissolves part of the puzzle—hostile constants would yield no observers—but I don't think it answers why the constants fall inside the life-permitting region rather than outside it.
(6) Despite rejecting both multiverse and theism, I maintain that single-universe naturalism is now less rationally compelling than before we appreciated the tuning, even though I don't take this as positive support for any specific alternative.

Are these jointly tenable? In particular, is there a hidden tension between (3) and (4) about whether probabilistic reasoning is available here at all? Does (6) smuggle in the very evidential comparison that (4) blocks, and does it leave me with an unexplained explanatory debt given (2)? Also, is the conflict between (2)'s parsimony appeal and (4)'s measure-denial only apparent? Please rank the real tensions by severity, say which single revision would clean up the most, and identify any pair that looks incompatible but actually coheres.
