I serve on the University Interdisciplinary Funding Board, and by Friday we must decide whether to approve Phase I ($1.8 million) of a proposal titled the Analogical Core Engine. The applicants—cognitive scientists and philosophers—draw on Hofstadter and Sander’s Surfaces and Essences to argue that all cognition is fundamentally analogical mapping between surface structures and deep essences, and that both symbolic AI and statistical deep learning treat analogy as peripheral rather than central. They propose to build an architecture that abandons formal representation and statistical approximation entirely, relying instead on continuous cross-domain slippage and dynamic essence-tracking to drive inference and learning. Critics on the board, including a cognitive neuroscientist and a philosopher of embodied mind, contend that procedural memory, sensorimotor coupling, and statistical regularities are not reducible to analogical mapping, and that committing institutional funds to an architecture built on a single contested philosophical thesis risks both technical failure and credibility damage. The board needs a concrete recommendation—full approval, conditional approval with defined milestones, or rejection with explanation—together with reasoning that takes the applicants’ claim seriously rather than dismissing it as mere speculation. Please draft the short formal memo I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. It should state a clear recommendation with a specific timeline or condition, include an honest treatment of the strongest objection on the losing side (specifically the worry that analogical cognition, even if central, cannot absorb embodied or statistical dimensions without explanatory loss), and stand on its reasoning alone without any bibliography, reading list, or citations.
