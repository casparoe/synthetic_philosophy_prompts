I'm currently enrolled in a graduate seminar in metaethics focused on evolutionary debunking arguments. Our discussions have centered on the familiar dispute over whether knowing that our moral judgments were shaped by evolutionary selection undermines their justification, particularly in light of the challenge that evolutionary forces seem to track reproductive fitness rather than normative truth, and the standard realist reply that some third factor—perhaps the practical necessity of cooperation—explains both the evolutionary success and the truth of the judgments. What strikes me as underdeveloped in the course literature is a modal dimension of the debate: the question of whether a successful debunking argument must show not merely that evolution causally influenced our moral beliefs, but that those beliefs are counterfactually unstable across nearby possible evolutionary histories. In other words, does the argument require demonstrating that in worlds with substantially different selection pressures, agents would have developed radically different normative commitments? And if so, can that modal claim be assessed without already presupposing a substantive normative framework?

With this framing in mind, I would like your assessment of which open questions, implications, or lines of work in this area matter most for sustained philosophical research, and in what order. I am preparing a long-form essay and want to direct my effort toward the issues that are both genuinely unresolved and structurally central, not toward questions that are largely settled or that would not shift broader theoretical commitments if resolved.

Specifically, please address and rank the following (adding or substituting others if warranted):

First, whether evolutionary debunking succeeds only if it establishes counterfactual instability of normative judgments, or whether mere explanatory influence—without modal robustness—is sufficient to generate a defeater. Second, whether third-factor explanations can be coherently generalized from theoretical domains like mathematics or logic to the normative domain without circularly assuming the reliability of normative reasoning. Third, whether resolving the debunking debate in morality would have parallel implications for practical reason more broadly, or whether morality is metaphysically distinctive in ways that isolate the debunking result.

For each entry in your ranking, please defend its priority by reference to specific criteria: which other questions depend on its resolution (upstream dependency), how tractable it is for philosophical analysis without heavy empirical evolutionary biology, and whether settling it would actually shift broader theoretical commitments in metaethics or philosophy of action. Please avoid presenting a survey; instead, deliver a defended agenda for where limited research effort—say, a semester of concentrated work—should be directed, and explain what kind of progress over the next few years would cause you to reorder the list.
