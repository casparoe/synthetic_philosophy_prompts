Assume Simone de Beauvoir were alive today and encountered the practice of embedding gender in large language models, where gender is not represented as a binary social role but as a high-dimensional vector distributed across token co-occurrence statistics, producing classifications that are statistically predictive yet lack any corresponding embodied social situation. Drawing on her analysis of woman as the Other and her critique of biological essentialism, how might she assess whether these vectorized gender kinds constitute genuine social constructions or merely technical artifacts? In particular, would she argue that the absence of a lived, reciprocal consciousness between the model and the classified subject undermines the dialectical structure necessary for gender to function as an existential category, or could she accept algorithmic classification as a new mode of objectification?
