I am a retired physicist now working in philosophy of education, and next month I present to a joint seminar of Locke scholars and philosophers of education—mostly defenders of the standard harmonizing view that Locke's educational paternalism in Some Thoughts Concerning Education belongs to a pre-political family sphere and therefore poses no tension with the liberal neutrality of the Letter Concerning Toleration, plus a couple of historians of science attentive to his empiricism. I defend the contested claim that this harmonization fails because Locke's associationist psychology and his account of personal identity as continuity of consciousness together imply that educating a child literally constructs the associative chains that will constitute the adult's consciousness; consequently, the adult's later consent to government is conditioned by a deep psychological formation that precedes autonomous reason, leaving an unresolved metaphysical tension rather than a neat practical division. Please draft my Q&A prep sheet: list the objections this audience will actually raise, in roughly the order they will come and phrased in their vocabulary—particularly the family/state distinction, the misapplication of personal-identity metaphysics to children, and the historical-context objection that I am anachronistically reading metaphysics into a gentleman's manual—rating each by likelihood and damage, noting what I should concede up front, what I must hold, a concise reply suitable for two minutes, and the weakest point in that reply. Identify the one objection I am hoping nobody asks—likely the charge that my argument, if taken seriously, undermines all education and thus collapses into self-defeating radicalism—and sketch a brief defense. Throughout, separate what is well-established in Locke's texts from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present contested interpretive claims—such as that associationism entails determinism about identity—as if they were consensus.
