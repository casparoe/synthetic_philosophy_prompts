Here's an argument I cooked up mixing Schrödinger's Vedanta stuff with a point about temporal parts, and it feels like it proves too much. (1) Schrödinger said consciousness is literally one thing, not many — bodies are just different windows on the same room. (2) If it's literally the same consciousness in me and you, then any mental state has to belong to that single subject. (3) So when I feel pain, it's not "my" consciousness hurting, it's just consciousness hurting, and I'm one viewpoint where that happens. (4) Now add temporal parts: if my brain cells get replaced over years but consciousness stays, then even "my" viewpoint at time t1 and t2 are just two windows on the same single consciousness. (5) So if consciousness is unitary across both space and time, there's nothing to stop that same pain-state from being present in every other viewpoint — past, present, or in your head — since there's no wall inside one consciousness. (6) So I should feel your toothache right now and also my own future headaches, which I obviosly don't. (7) You might say the "window" filters it out, but then the window is doing real individuation work, which means consciousness isn't actually unitary — it's partitioned by bodies and times after all. (8) So either we accept universal shared suffering across space and time or we give up the unity claim entirely. (9) Where's the slip? I feel like step (5) smuggles in something about boundaries that doesn't apply to a non-physical consciousness, but I can't name it. Which premise do I drop, and what's the cost?
