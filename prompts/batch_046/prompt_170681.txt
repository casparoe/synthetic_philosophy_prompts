I'm a junior in political philosophy serving this year as the student representative on my university's Committee on Global Academic Partnerships. For my course on legitimacy and governance we're studying whether prosperity, social order, and high state capacity can justify authoritarian rule—the performance-legitimacy problem raised by Singapore and comparable regimes. Our committee now faces a concrete renewal decision that turns directly on this question, and I need a formal memo I can present to the full board.

The case: We have a five-year joint research agreement with a prominent technical university in Singapore, focused on climate-resilient infrastructure modeling and public-health data systems. The agreement expires in June. Singapore delivers exceptional scientific output, economic prosperity, and public order, but also maintains strict legal limits on political assembly, critical academic discourse in state-defined "sensitive" domains, and independent civil-society oversight of research priorities. One faction of faculty argues that severing ties punishes individual scientists for state policy, wastes genuine scientific capacity, and ignores the partner's strong output legitimacy; another faction argues that renewal lends our institutional reputation to an authoritarian knowledge regime and signals that prosperity can excuse coercion.

Please write the committee's decision memo—approve renewal, reject it, or approve with conditions—with reasoning that researchers and administrators can actually respond to. The memo should treat the strongest consideration on the losing side as a serious argument, not a strawman.

In particular, work with the following conceptual framing, which I want developed rather than merely named: the risk of "prestige spillover," by which a reputable Western university's partnership with a state-directed institution allows the partner state to borrow that reputation and claim that authoritarian governance is fully compatible with world-class knowledge production. The memo should examine whether carefully structured conditions can block this spillover without destroying scientific collaboration.

Please structure the memo exactly as follows:
1. Executive Recommendation (state the bottom line first; approximately 120 words)
2. The Case and the Stakes (the partnership, the expiration, and what is genuinely at issue; approximately 150 words)
3. The Performance-Legitimacy Argument for Renewal (prosperity, order, scientific output, and the unfairness of punishing researchers; approximately 200 words)
4. The Strongest Counter-Argument: Complicity and Prestige Spillover (the moral cost of lending reputation, the hidden cognitive capture in state-directed data regimes, and why output legitimacy does not answer the institutional-duty question; approximately 200 words)
5. Adjudication: Where Output Legitimacy Ends and Institutional Duty Begins (a disciplined mapping of which features of the case the performance-legitimacy framework explains and which it misses or misdescribes; approximately 200 words)
6. Proposed Conditions or Final Verdict (specific, enforceable conditions if recommending conditional renewal, or a clear rejection with rationale; approximately 150 words)

Write in formal American English suitable for an institutional document. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Do not use verbatim quotations from specific texts; paraphrase any ideas you reference. The deliverable should be a document the committee could act on, not personal advice to me.
