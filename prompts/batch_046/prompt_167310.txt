Consider a modified Frankfurt-style case with a nested intervention architecture. The agent, call him Jones, faces a choice to act or refrain. The counterfactual intervener, Black, employs not a single blocker but two layers: a primary mechanism B1 that monitors Jones's preliminary neural state and can forcibly prevent the action if Jones shows signs of refraining; and a secondary meta-mechanism B2 that can disable B1 should B1 itself initiate an intervention. Jones proceeds to act, B1 remains dormant, and B2 also remains dormant.

The conceptual problem concerns the principle of alternative possibilities (PAP) and the familiar objection that Frankfurt cases leave a flicker of freedom — a remaining alternative in the causal chain that is not the target action but some prior mental event. In this nested architecture, B2 represents a potential intervention point upstream of B1. Analyze whether the presence of B2 strengthens, dissolves, or transforms the flicker-of-freedom objection.

In your answer, carefully distinguish three things: (a) causal alternatives that are physically available in the world but causally irrelevant to Jones's actual decision path; (b) alternatives available only at the meta-level governing the intervention mechanism itself; and (c) whether PAP requires that the agent be the causal source of the absence of intervention, or merely that some branching path exist somewhere in the overall causal graph, regardless of the agent's relation to it. You should not treat this as a question about specific published authors; build the analysis directly from the logical structure of nested blockers and the conditions under which an alternative counts as genuinely available to the agent.
