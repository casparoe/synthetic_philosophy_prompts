Two members of my philosophy workshop—one defending foundationalism about epistemic justification, the other defending infinitism—have spent a year arguing past each other about whether an infinite regress of reasons is vicious or virtuous. They have agreed to produce an adversarial collaboration document. Write it for them. Structure: (1) A numbered list of at least five claims both can sincerely sign, for example that justification is not reducible to true belief, that an infinite chain of reasons is not automatically fallacious, that accessibility of one's reasons is relevant to epistemic responsibility, that circular reasoning is generally defective, and that terminating reasons, if they exist, must not be arbitrary; (2) A disagreements section in which each camp's position is stated in wording that the opposing side recognizes as accurate, focusing especially on whether the absence of a foundational reason makes justification impossible and whether an unsurveyable infinite chain can confer justification; (3) The cruxes—I take the deepest ones to be (a) whether non-arbitrary support requires a reason that is itself justified without further inference, and (b) whether an agent must be able to access or articulate the entire chain of reasons for her belief to be justified; for each crux, present a concrete scenario, such as a scientist who justifies her belief in a theoretical entity through a ten-step chain of instrumental inferences, and record what each camp predicts about whether the scientist's belief is justified in that scenario, including any conditions under which they might revise their prediction. Keep the formulations neutral and avoid attributing specific texts or authors.
