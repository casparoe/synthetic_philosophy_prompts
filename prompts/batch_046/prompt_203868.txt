I'd like you to write a sustained satirical piece in the deadpan voice of a luxury wellness retreat's promotional brochure and accompanying guest contract for "The Ataraxia Sanctuary," an exclusive facility built entirely around the philosophical doctrine of tranquilism—the view that only the absence of craving and suffering possesses intrinsic value, while pleasure, achievement, and aesthetic experience are at best instrumentally useful and at best axiologically irrelevant. The brochure should read as genuinely luxurious and aspirational, but every amenity must quietly embody the doctrine's extreme implications: the cuisine eliminates not merely discomfort but any positive gustatory interest; the architecture is designed to prevent not just stress but the very formation of preferences; the spa treatments aim not at rejuvenation but at the permanent flattening of affective response. Include a sidebar titled "Our Philosophy" that explains, without ever breaking the marketing tone, why guests who report feeling "fulfilled" or "inspired" are gently reminded that these states carry no weight in the axiological ledger, and include a mock FAQ addressing practical objections—whether children are permitted (they generate needs), whether artistic programming is offered (it cultivates taste, hence craving), and what happens if a guest achieves complete tranquility and requests an early departure (the refund policy treats the absence of desire to stay as the fulfillment of the contract's purpose). The satire should be legible to someone unfamiliar with the doctrine—the exaggeration should feel like a reductio—while a reader versed in value theory should recognize that each feature is a fair extension of the actual position rather than a strawman. Keep it around 800 words, formatted with section headers, and maintain the deadpan luxury voice throughout; do not wink or explain the joke in a concluding authorial note.
