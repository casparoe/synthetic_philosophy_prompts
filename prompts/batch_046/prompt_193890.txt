Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between (A) outside-view forecasters, who defend reference-class and base-rate methods for predicting normative collapses—such as the disappearance of dueling, chattel slavery, or foot-binding—drawn from imperial lifespans and structural indicators; and (B) inside-view critics, who hold that normative structures are path-dependent and that treating Athens, Qing China, and the British Empire as exchangeable samples commits an ecological fallacy. The document should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both sides can sign, covering the formal structure of reference-class reasoning, the empirical record of rapid normative discontinuities, and the conditions under which base-rate forecasting is valid for social systems; (2) a disagreements table in which each side states, in wording it accepts, its precise objection to the other—distinguishing substantive disputes about causation from verbal ones about whether explanation and prediction are separable in this domain; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one camp—including whether there exists a cross-cutting moral-reference-class (taxonomies of societies by rate of normative discontinuity rather than by geopolitical lifespan) that improves calibration without assuming political exchangeability; and (4) for each crux, the specific historical comparison, formal result, or forecasting protocol that both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's advance prediction recorded. Where possible, identify whether part of the dispute is verbal—particularly around whether the outside view requires denying causal mechanisms, or merely bracketing them for predictive purposes. Keep the document focused; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
