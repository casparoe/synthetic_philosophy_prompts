As a philosophy professor, I present the following concrete situation for analysis, described in everyday terms without labeling its underlying conceptual structure. A municipal archivist must design a permanent public exhibition about her city’s industrial heritage, and she is pulled among three curatorial impulses: constructing a heroic narrative of founding engineers to inspire present civic action; assembling a total, uninterpreted archive of every factory record and personal letter to preserve the past in its exact weight; and removing all commemorative monuments to expose the founders’ exploitation, thereby liberating current residents from inherited reverence. Complicating her choice is a new mandate that the exhibition will be digitized into a machine-learning dataset used to generate personalized historical narratives for citizens based on psychological profiling, so her decision will train synthetic systems that reproduce one mode of relating to the past at scale. She asks how to navigate these demands without either falsifying history, crushing present life under dead weight, or destroying the continuity required for collective purpose. Please advise her by identifying the philosophical problem instantiated here, articulating the specific danger each curatorial mode poses to living vitality, and assessing whether the three can be genuinely integrated or must be hierarchically ordered; respond in exactly five numbered items, each one to two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
