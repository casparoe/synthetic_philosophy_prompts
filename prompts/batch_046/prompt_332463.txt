As a doctoral student in philosophy of mind, I am exploring whether formal poetic constraints can illuminate structural features of panpsychism's combination problem—the challenge of explaining how numerous micro-level subjects of experience might constitute a single unified macro-level subject without being reduced to properties or leaving macro-consciousness illusory.

Please compose a villanelle: nineteen lines arranged in five tercets followed by one quatrain, with rhyme scheme ABA for the tercets and ABAA for the quatrain, and with two refrains that alternate at the ends of stanzas and converge in the final couplet. Rather than summarizing the problem in prose, the poem's form should do the philosophical work.

Adopt two refrains that embody the tension between plural micro-subjects and singular macro-subjectivity—for instance, Many minds, yet one I claim and The parts remain without a name—though you may adjust them so long as they maintain that tension. Here is the specific conceptual injection: treat the macro-subject's unity not as spatial or causal aggregation, but as a non-spatial phenomenal bonding relation in which constituent micro-subjects retain their own boundaries and intentional perspectives. Reflect this syntactically: in each tercet, allow the refrains to shift grammatical function—now subject, now object, now appositive—so that they never settle into a stable compositional relation. In the final quatrain, both refrains must appear together in close proximity, but arranged so they remain grammatically distinct and conceptually unreconciled, demonstrating that co-presence within consciousness is not equivalent to ontological combination.

The result should be philosophically precise enough that a specialist recognizes the specific structural analogy—particularly the difficulty of attributing unified intentionality without reduction—while standing as an independent, rigorous piece of verse. No footnotes, citations, bibliographic references, or named philosophers should appear; let the concept and the form carry the argument entirely.
