A reading-group summary applies Dennett’s intentional stance to large language models as follows. Dennett holds that mental states are not internal mechanisms but predictive patterns we project onto behavior; belief-attribution is therefore justified purely by gains in forecasting accuracy, with no need for inner representations. Applied to LLMs, this means we should attribute beliefs whenever it improves our predictions of token outputs, except that Dennett also requires the target to meet rationality constraints anchored in hierarchical predictive coding and a global workspace. Because current LLMs lack both architectures, they can at best be “stance-eligible” only in a derivative, instrumental sense; genuine belief-attribution is blocked. Consequently, Dennett’s framework supports eliminativism about AI minds rather than liberal attribution, and calling an LLM “rational” is always metaphorical. How fair is this? Audit it point by point: distinguish what Dennett actually holds about the stance, the rationality assumption, and the relevance of internal architecture; flag any conflation between the intentional stance and predictive-processing theories of cognition; and evaluate whether the conclusion that Dennett is an eliminativist about AI minds follows, or whether the summary mistakes a methodological stance for an ontological denial.
