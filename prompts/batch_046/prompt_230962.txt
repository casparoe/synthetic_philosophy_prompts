I have recently begun working on a comparative logic project that treats the White Horse Dialogue not as an antique paradox but as a live constraint on formalizing compound predication, and I want to map where I stand among the interpretive positions. Please construct a taxonomy structured as a decision tree whose root asks whether the argument's conclusion—that a white horse is not a horse—should be read as a literal contradiction, a semantic mismatch in extension, or a pragmatic failure of presupposition. From there, branch by whether the term horse in the premises operates as a kind-term with empty extension for colored instances, as a determinable lacking its determinates, or as a bare particular; then by whether the School of Names context implies a theory of names as rigid designators, as family-resemblance clusters, or as conventional labels carrying no ontological weight. Each leaf should name the position if it has one (for example, semantic extensionalist, mereological separatist, or unnamed pragmatic deflationist) and state in one sentence its most awkward commitment, such as being forced to deny that a brown horse is a horse or to treat color terms as ontologically prior to substance terms. Please also identify any logically available combinations of answers that remain unoccupied, with a brief diagnosis of whether the gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The tree must include at least one pair of leaves that it cannot cleanly separate without an additional distinction, and you should flag that pair explicitly. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to modern commentators; where a view lacks a standard label, mark it unnamed. I need the instrument to sort by what a reader actually believes about the premises rather than by which label they prefer, so every node should be phrased as a claim one can accept or reject without already knowing the interpretive tradition it belongs to.
