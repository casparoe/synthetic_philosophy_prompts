Consider the following exam prompt and a student's written answer. The exam asks: Can the criteria of authenticity—particularly the criterion of dissimilarity and the criterion of embarrassment—ever settle the historical Jesus's self-understanding and social location, or are they structurally confined to producing only a minimal, defensible core? In your response, address the charge that these criteria presuppose an implausible 'pure origin point' for religious movements, and evaluate whether expanding the comparative frame beyond Second Temple Judaism and the early church to include Greco-Roman philosophical schools strengthens or undermines the method.

Student answer:
The criteria of authenticity filter later invention from genuine tradition. The criterion of dissimilarity identifies material that is unlike both Second Temple Judaism and the early Christian community; if a saying or action fits neither context, it must come from Jesus. The criterion of embarrassment operates on the same comparative logic: if the church preserved something damaging to its claims, such as Jesus being baptized by John, it could not have invented it, so the event is historical. Used together rigorously, these criteria can recover far more than a minimal Jesus—they can settle his self-understanding, because any saying that survives both filters reveals his actual perspective rather than later projection.

The 'pure origin point' objection claims this method assumes Jesus was entirely disconnected from his environment. That misreads the criterion: dissimilarity only requires that Jesus differ in specific respects, not that he be wholly unique. Furthermore, adding Greco-Roman philosophical schools to the comparative frame strengthens the method. Once we compare Jesus to Cynic and Stoic practices, we see he was closer to Cynic philosophers than to Jewish apocalypticists, which gives us a sharper contrast space and confirms that distinctive sayings are genuinely his.

That said, these tools have one clear limit: they apply to public sayings and deeds, not to internal psychological states, so they cannot reconstruct Jesus's private intentions.
