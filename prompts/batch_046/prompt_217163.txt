My roommate and her friend were arguing about luminosity and KK over beers last night. I didn't follow all of it but here's the back-and-forth as best I remember.

(1) He said the standard anti-luminosity argument only works if mental states come in smooth gradations. But if you accept threshold luminosity — you're only luminous about feeling cold once your confidence is above a sharp cutoff — then the sorites slide never starts, since near the boundary you're just not in the luminous zone.

(2) She said thats just pushing the bump in the rug. If there's a sharp cutoff between 0.98 and 0.99 confidence, you can run the same sorites on the cutoff itself: what's the principled differnce? Either the line is arbitrary, which makes luminosity conventional, or it's vague, which brings back the slide.

(3) He replied that the cutoff isn't arbitrary — it's fixed by whatever conditions actually make knowledge possible. The KK principle never said you can always tell you're knowing, only that knowing puts you in position to tell given those conditions. Near the boundary the conditions fail, so there's no contradiction.

(4) She said then you've gutted KK. If transparency only holds away from boundaries, it's just a local accident, not a structural principle of knowledge. So you either keep KK strong and accept anti-luminosity, or save luminosity by making KK trivial.
