Debates over updateless decision theory and causal decision theory have stalled for years on structurally identical Newcomb-style cases: both sides accept the payoff matrices, agree that rationality involves maximizing expected utility, and share much of the formal machinery for logical counterfactuals, yet they do not converge. I suspect the deadlock is not empirical—no experiment resolves it—nor purely formal, but conceptual: specifically, they may disagree about whether an agent's own choice should be treated as an observed event that updates beliefs, or as a fixed point that breaks the symmetry required for updateless reasoning, with the divergence becoming especially sharp in multi-agent settings where agents share source code and indexical uncertainty is non-trivial. Is the stall actually about this epistemic status of choice, or is there a hidden formal disagreement—say, in how each framework handles self-reference, logical correlation across copies, or the boundary between decision and prediction—that each side misdescribes as a disagreement about rational norms?
