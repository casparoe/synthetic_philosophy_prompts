I am preparing the interview guide for a podcast episode featuring a philosopher—unnamed, but representing a live position—who defends a specific variant of procreative beneficence combined with what she terms threshold liberal eugenics. On her view, parents bear a non-comparative obligation to secure the best genetic prospects reasonably available for the child they will actually conceive, but this obligation is capped by a genetic privacy threshold: a deontological barrier that blocks state or institutional coercion once parents have satisfied a baseline welfare standard, and that also limits the extent to which parents must select for non-health-related traits. She maintains that this cap distinguishes her position from both strong eugenic selection and from pure reproductive liberty, and she applies it specifically to cases involving high-precision polygenic risk scores for cognitive and psychiatric traits, where predictive value is substantial but the social meaning of selection remains contested.

Draft ten to twelve interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the framework. I want at least one question that presses on how the genetic privacy threshold avoids collapsing into either a pure rights-based veto or a disguised welfare-maximization rule; at least one on what the view says about parents who could access polygenic screening but decline for identity-based rather than religious reasons; at least one on whether the non-comparative framing survives the objection that any act of selection is implicitly comparative against the counterfactual child who would have existed otherwise; and for each of these, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to offer—defending the threshold as a side-constraint, appealing to relational autonomy, and insisting that the obligation tracks expected welfare rather than personal identity.

Additionally, include two questions probing the interaction between her liberal constraint and intergenerational obligations—specifically, whether a parent who meets the threshold but declines available enhancement thereby wrongs future descendants, and whether the threshold ought to be indexed to technological availability or to social equality of access. For each, provide a brief note on what kind of answer would expose a genuine tension in her package rather than a mere clarification. The questions should assume no audience access to specialist literature and should not ask for bibliographies or citation lists.
