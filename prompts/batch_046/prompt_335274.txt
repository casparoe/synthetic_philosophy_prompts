Hey, I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm trying to figure out what a real theory of moral progress through the "expanding circle" actually has to be able to do. Not just the pop-philosphy version or Singer's usual framing. I want the hard criterias: what phenomena must it explain, what distinctions must it keep straight, which parts are non-negotiable, and which are just camp prejudices?

For starters, it needs to account for radial expansion—who gets pulled inside the boundary—without treating it as inevitable teleology or pure historical accident. It should also seperate membership expansion from deepening concern for people already inside. There's this density-dilution problem I'm noticing, where the circle gets wider but the moral weight per member might be thinning out; I don't know if that's a genuine constraint on any theory or just my own framing, but a decent account should address it.

Then there's local contractions—domains where moral concern actually shrinks while others grow. The theory can't just write those off as noise. It also needs to handle the asymmetry between rights/considerability and actual obligations, especially across species or borders. Is explaining that gap required, or can the theory stay silent?

Boundary cases matter too: if artificial agents or ecosystems eventually belong inside the circle, does that break the geometry, or is the circle still anchored to sentience? And must the theory be normative—telling us who should be inside—or can it stay purely descriptive?

I'm particularly curious whether some subset of these is jointly unsatisfiable. Can you have historical contingency, non-zero-sum expansion, and no density dilution all at once? Which single requirement does the most damage to the usual candidates?

Lay out the requirements with the reasoning behind each, flag the negotiable ones, and if there's a hidden tension or no-go result in the list, say so. Skip the bibliography—I'll do that part myself. And please don't just score existing views; the criteria themselves are what I want to see.
