Compare counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail as structural challenges to evidential decision theory. Define counterfactual mugging as the case in which a highly accurate predictor offers you a large reward if you refuse a smaller immediate sum, with the reward delivered precisely because the predictor foresaw that refusal; define XOR blackmail as the case in which a blackmailer threatens to carry out one of two independent harmful actions unless you pay, but the mechanism ensures that exactly one threat is active and the other is inert, so that paying averts only the active threat without causally influencing which branch was selected. Rather than listing superficial similarities, isolate the dependency structure that distinguishes them: counterfactual conditioning on a single act versus disjunctive activation across independent branches. Then construct a hybrid scenario in which a predictor offers the counterfactual-mugging reward structure, but the predictor's own evaluation is gated by an unobservable XOR condition linking two independent background variables, so that the counterfactual dependency between refusal and reward holds only on one branch of the gate. For this hybrid, explain what evidential, causal, and functional decision theories each recommend, and identify whether the unitary correlation or the disjunctive gate is the feature that forces the evidentialist to pay. Finally, specify whether removing the XOR gate collapses the hybrid back into standard counterfactual mugging, and state which feature a defender of evidential reasoning would most likely attack first. Keep the analysis conceptual and precise, avoid bibliographic references, and show the reasoning for each theory without invoking formal notation.
