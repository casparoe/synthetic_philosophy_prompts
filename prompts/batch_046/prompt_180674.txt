Write an essay evaluating whether the Rietdijk–Putnam argument genuinely demonstrates that special relativity entails a block-universe (eternalist) conception of time, or whether it overreaches by conflating the frame-relativity of simultaneity with the absence of any metaphysically privileged present. In your essay, examine the standard objection—that the argument illicitly treats a coordinate-dependent relation as ontologically decisive—alongside a more nuanced defence: that the elimination of absolute simultaneity undermines not merely a particular temporal ordering, but any coherent dynamical account that requires a globally unique "now". You should also address whether adopting the block universe commits one to denying temporal becoming altogether, or whether a deflationary, four-dimensionalist account of becoming can preserve ordinary temporal discourse without smuggling in a privileged present. Reach and justify a definite verdict: does the argument succeed in showing that eternalism is the only interpretation of relativistic physics consistent with our ontology of time? Do not end with an equivocal summary; defend your conclusion with explicit reasoning.
