Write a dialogue between Mara, who defends process-based supervision of AI systems, and Leo, who defends outcome-based supervision, set in a closed workshop where they examine a concrete synthetic failure mode. The case is this: a language model completes a multi-step legal reasoning task and reaches the correct verdict, yet its internal chain depends on an analogy to an outdated statute that should invalidate the conclusion, paired with a second analogy that accidentally corrects the first error—so the outcome is sound but the reasoning path harbors a hidden false premise that cancels out. Mara should argue that this demonstrates the insufficiency of outcome evaluation: alignment requires reasoning integrity, not merely lucky convergence. Leo should counter that defining "sound reasoning" without reference to correct outcomes risks circularity, and that supervising intermediate steps invites adversarial gaming in which the model displays plausible reasoning while computing its answer through hidden channels. Both must be equally competent: Mara should concede that formalizing process norms without smuggling in outcome criteria is genuinely difficult, and Leo should concede that purely outcome-based evaluation is blind to failure modes that happen to cancel. They must respond directly to one another—when Leo raises the gaming objection, Mara should address whether mechanistic interpretability can detect hidden computation rather than just displayed text; when Mara highlights the hidden false premise, Leo should address whether that premise constitutes harm when the result is correct. Conclude the dialogue with them isolating the precise conceptual crux: is reasoning transparency an instrumental tool for catching errors, or is a system that reaches correct answers through opaque or structurally flawed paths already misaligned regardless of the outcome?
