From this starting point, please construct a compact decision-tree taxonomy that sorts the positions on whether a single I can be genuinely distributed across such brains. The tree should branch through four diagnostic questions that a non-specialist could answer by reflecting on the scenario, without knowing any philosophical labels: first, whether the persistence of selfhood requires spatiotemporal continuity within one biological brain or can survive distribution across substrates; second, whether a distributed system must still contain exactly one unified phenomenal stream or can host genuinely plural co-conscious selves; third, whether self-reference is best understood as a causal-functional pattern of the kind Hofstadter associates with strange loops or as an identity tied to biological substrate; and fourth, whether an I requires a single unified narrative focal point or can be genuinely plural without fragmenting. Each leaf should carry a named position—such as Hofstadter-style pattern pluralism, Lockean psychological continuity constrained by a uniqueness clause, biological animalism, and a narrative-multiplicity view—or be labeled unnamed, with a one-sentence diagnosis of whether the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Include no more than eight terminal nodes, phrase every node as a direct yes/no or either/or question, and explicitly flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, particularly where pattern pluralism and narrative multiplicity converge on the same answers despite different metaphysical commitments. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the task is to map the logical space, not to catalog the literature.
