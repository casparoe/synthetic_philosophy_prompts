Write a dialogue between J and R about material constitution, but skip the standard crushing-the-statue scenario every intro textbook uses. Instead, imagine a statue built from self-healing polymer clay. Over ten years every original molecule gets swapped for an identical synthetic compound, while a live 3D scan streams its exact geometry to a remote server that can reprint a perfect replica on demand. J thinks the statue survives the full replacement because the form-information never lapses—it's always present either in the physical object or in its active digital twin—so there's an unbroken causal-historical chain tracking the structure. R thinks that's confused: information isn't material substance, so the statue dies the instant the last original polymer is replaced, and the scan is just a map of a ghost, not a contiuation. Have them actually argue back and forth—J should tackle R's worry that digital tracking can't substitute for material continuity, and R should tackle J's point that without some form-preservation criterion we can't distinguish the statue from a random pile of synthetic molecules that happen to share the shape. Neither should be a strawman, and they should respond to each other's actual points rather than talk past them. Keep it casual, maybe include a typo or two.
