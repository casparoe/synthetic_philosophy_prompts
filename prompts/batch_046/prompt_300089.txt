i run a philosophy workshop with about 12 undergrads, 90 minutes, on distributive ethics. design an activty that makes them feel the actual pull of the utility monster — they should build a distribution rule themselves and only realize where it leads once the payouts happen. use real small stakes: each student gets vote-credits that determine next week's discussion topic, plus some candy distributed by whatever rule the group picks. secretly assign everyone a hidden 'welfare conversion card' — most are linear (1 candy = 1 point), but one or two are exponential so that extra candy gives them massive gains. they vote on an aggregation principle (maximize total points, equal shares, etc.) without seeing the cards. then run the allocation for real using thier cards. after, a debrief that maps each stage onto Nozick's utility monster argument, points out exactly where they could resist the rule and what it costs to do so, predicts what will go wrong when you run this (someone will feel cheated when cards are revealed), and is frank about the part the game can't reproduce — the literally infinite utility — which you have to supply verbally. no lectures up front, don't name the monster until after, and don't ask for bibliographies or citations since a model without web access shouldn't be guessing at those. keep everything runnable with paper and candy.
