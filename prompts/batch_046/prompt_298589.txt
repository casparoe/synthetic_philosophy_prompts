As a journalist covering the philosophy of mind and cognitive science, I am developing a feature on whether the first-person perspective can genuinely extend across separate neural substrates, and I need a rigorous philosophical argument to anchor it. I am drawn to Douglas Hofstadter’s general framework—familiar to many readers—that the self is best understood as a recursive pattern rather than a localized substance, one that might in principle persist in another mind that carries the right structural echoes. But I want to move well beyond the standard metaphor. Please argue for the following precise and somewhat unorthodox position: that an “I” can indeed be distributed across two or more brains, but only if a specific structural condition—call it reciprocal second-order recognition—is satisfied. By this I mean not merely that brain A represents brain B’s mental states and vice versa, but that each brain implements a working model of the other that explicitly includes a model of itself being modeled by that other, thereby generating a cross-subjective strange loop that binds the two architectures into a single first-person circuit.

Your argument should defend the claim that in the absence of this recursive interlocking, what exists between the brains is at best sympathy, memory, or coordinated behavior—not a genuinely distributed self—whereas with it, the boundary of the first-person perspective genuinely blurs without collapsing into an undifferentiated hive-mind. Address the standard philosophical objections directly: the apparent privacy of phenomenal consciousness, the asymmetry of first-person access, and the worry that distributing an “I” would violate the unity of consciousness required for moral agency. Keep the reasoning formal and conceptual rather than empirical; do not invoke specific neuroimaging studies or laboratory results that would require verification beyond what a non-specialist could reasonably reconstruct. And please avoid bibliographies, page citations, or appeals to niche technical literatures; build the case from the conceptual logic of pattern, recursion, and inter-subjective modeling alone.
