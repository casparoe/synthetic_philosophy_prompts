For my graduate seminar on existential phenomenology, I have been pressing the students to move past the familiar, grand-scale illustrations of Sartre’s analysis of bad faith—political collaboration, career self-deception, the waiter playing at being a waiter—and to locate the phenomenon in a genuinely microscopic moment of indecision where the structure is easier to miss but conceptually identical. I want you to write a villanelle: nineteen lines arranged as five tercets followed by a closing quatrain, with the rhyme scheme ABA and exactly two refrains that must alternate and finally appear together in the last two lines. Set the scene on a train platform at dusk, where a single figure holds a bag and hesitates before doors that are about to close; the concrete details—the smell of diesel, the hiss of brakes, the weight of the handle—should do the work of grounding the abstraction, because I want the poem to earn its philosophy through the situation rather than announce it.

The two refrains should be composed as opposing voices of bad faith: one denying transcendence with a line to the effect of “I am only what the world has made of me,” and one denying facticity with a line to the effect of “I am only what I have made of myself.” The mechanical repetition and variation of the villanelle form should enact the oscillation Sartre describes, in which the for-itself attempts to coincide with being-in-itself by treating itself now as pure object, now as pure subject, never holding both truths at once. The turn—traditionally in the final quatrain—must not resolve this tension into a synthetic freedom or a peaceful acceptance; instead, the closing couplet should place the two refrains side by side without merging them, capturing the anguish that radical freedom is not an escape from facticity but its perpetual, ungrounded assumption. The poem must be accurate enough that someone familiar with Sartre’s ontology recognizes the specific errors of denying one’s transcendence versus denying one’s facticity, and the rhyme and refrain structure should carry conceptual weight rather than serve as mere ornament. Keep the language precise and unsentimental; I am asking for a poem that functions as a piece of philosophical thinking in verse, not a sermon about choice.
