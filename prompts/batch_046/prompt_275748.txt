I am preparing the interview guide for an episode of a philosophy podcast focused on metaphysics, and my guest is Dr. Alistair Venn, a philosopher of time who defends a position he calls branch-index presentism. The view holds that presentism is correct—only entities located at the present moment exist—but it combines this ontology with a branching-modal framework in which the present is not a metaphysically privileged slice of reality but an indexical standpoint traversing one actualized path through a tree of causally connected possibilities. Rather than grounding past-tensed truths in presently existing traces of actual past objects, Venn argues that they are grounded in present modal-relational properties that encode information about non-actual but causally adjacent branches that have been pruned by the actualization of the present path. He claims this solves the truthmaker problem without invoking past entities, and that it explains causal asymmetry by treating causation as a directional constraint on branch-actualization rather than as a relation between successive temporal slices. I want a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that turn on the actual content of this view and go well beyond asking him to restate it. Specifically, include questions that press whether his modal tree collapses into an eternalist block distributed across modal rather than temporal dimensions; that ask how his account distinguishes veridical historical memory from structurally identical confabulation if both are grounded in present modal relations; that anticipate his likely reply that causal-adjacency is an objective primitive and then follow up on whether that primitive is empirically detectable or merely a post-hoc theoretical posit; and that explore what becomes of the view if we reject the idea of a single actualized branch, as in a quantum-mechanical interpretation where multiple branches remain equally real. For each primary question, provide a concise follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give, and order the sequence so that his standard deflections become progressively less tenable, culminating in a direct question about what observational or theoretical result could genuinely count against branch-index presentism.
