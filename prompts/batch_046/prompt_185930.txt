I'm a retired biologist who got hooked on philosophy after I left the lab. Now I sit on my university's small advisory board for international ethics—mostly volunters, we vote on curriculum and policy stuff. Next Thursday we have to decide on a proposed teaching module that lumps Kosovo '99, Iraq '03, and Libya '11 together as 'unauthorized humanitarian intervention,' applying one moral verdict: they violated sovereignty norms regardless of outcomes. One board member, who's been deep in the moral-luck literature, says that's wrong—Libya collapsed into militias, Iraq went sectarian, Kosovo stayed relatively stable, so judging all three by the same standard ignores that decision-makers in Libya and Iraq got 'morally unlucky' with outcomes they couldn't fully control, while praising Kosovo for its stability is just outcome bias. Another member says results are totally irrelevant; only authorization and intent matter. I need a short memo for the board recommending whether we approve the unified module, split the cases by outcome, or take some middle road—something concrete we can vote on. Please treat the moral-luck objection seriously, not as a strawman, and lay out the strongest argument for the side that loses.
