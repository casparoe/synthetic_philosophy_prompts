I sit on our county's Heritage Funding Board and there's this proposal in front of us thats genuinely weird. A research collective wants £80k to build what they call a 'structural geo-archieve' of the 1973 Millbrook flood — they keep spelling it wrong in the bid, but I think they mean archive. Their framing is pure Braudel: they say the flood wasn't really an event caused by a storm or broken levees, but the inevitable expression of about a century and a half of upstream deforestation and sediment loading. So they've designed the database taxonomy so every survivor interview, council minute, and rescue photograph gets tagged as 'episodic noise' and buried in a non-searchable appendix. Only soil cores, long-range rainfall averages, and land-use maps are fully indexed and visualised. They want the grant to show that human testimony actually corrupts the structural signal.

Half the board thinks this is a brilliant correction to 'great disaster' storytelling and exactly the kind of long-view geography we should fund; the other half thinks it's methodologically arrogant and violates our charter's commitment to community memory, not just geophysical data. Could you draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting? It needs to recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with a clear time-frame and a concrete bottom line the board can vote on. Take the Braudelian argument seriously rather than dismissing it, but also give an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Write it in British English please, and keep the tone formal but readable. Don't include any bibliography or citation list — I just need the reasoning and the decision.
