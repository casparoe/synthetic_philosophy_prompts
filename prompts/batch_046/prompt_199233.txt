Following a recent period of personal reflection on the relationship between divine sovereignty and human agency—prompted by pastoral conversations with a close friend facing an irreversible medical decision—I have become preoccupied with a tension within Molinist accounts of providence that I do not think receives adequate attention in standard treatments. I would like you to argue for the position that Molinism's commitment to middle knowledge (scientia media) is incompatible with a robust libertarian conception of freedom, not because of the familiar grounding objection concerning the truthmakers of counterfactuals of freedom, but because of what might be called counterfactual anchoring: the claim that if God knows, prior to any creative decree, the true counterfactuals describing what every possible creature would freely do in every possible circumstance, then the modal space surrounding any actualized world is epistemically and metaphysically constrained in such a way that the agent's ability to do otherwise in nearby worlds is systematically undermined, even though no causal determinism obtains. In developing this argument, please focus specifically on how the conditions for weak actualization interact with the logic of counterfactual dependence, and show why this undermines moral responsibility rather than preserving it. Your response should proceed in clear steps, and it should conclude with an explicit statement of what argument or evidence would lead you to revise or abandon this conclusion—indicating in particular which premise or inferential move is most likely to be erroneous.
