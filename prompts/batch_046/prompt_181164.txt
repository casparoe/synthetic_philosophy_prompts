I am preparing a one-year dissertation prospectus on pain asymbolia and the conceptual structure of pain's badness. My working idea is that both representationalist and affectivist theories of pain treat badness as a unitary property—either grounded in representational content about bodily damage or in phenomenal aversiveness—and that each assumes this single property explains why pain motivates avoidance. Clinical descriptions of pain asymbolia, in which subjects report pain without protective behavior or emotional distress, are usually read as challenging one camp or the other. I suspect, however, that asymbolia reveals a deeper problem: badness is not unitary but composed of two dissociable dimensions, which I will call phenomenal aversiveness and prudential urgency. On this view, asymbolia does not falsify either standard theory outright but exposes that each captures only one dimension and mistakenly treats it as exhaustive. I need the proposal to articulate the precise question—whether the badness of pain can be decomposed into these two dimensions without collapsing back into a unitary account—explain why current representationalist and affectivist responses to asymbolia fail to address this decomposition, present a month-by-month research plan (conceptual analysis of the two dimensions; close examination of clinical and philosophical descriptions of asymbolic behavior; construction of a dual-component framework; evaluation against objections about unity and explanatory power), specify what finding would count as success versus failure, and restrict everything to a realistic twelve-month scope. Do not request citations, a literature review by reference, or a reading list; I will integrate sources after drafting. Keep the framing formal and the argumentation precise.
