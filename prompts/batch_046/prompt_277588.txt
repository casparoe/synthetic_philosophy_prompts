By the end, no single biological neuron remains essential, yet at every intermediate stage the system's overall architecture is distributed across heterogeneous substrates with no discrete switch point. The question should ask students to evaluate whether phenomenal consciousness and personal identity survive this process, and specifically whether the parallel-operation period undermines standard psychological-continuity or animalist answers.

Please write:
(1) the exam question itself, framed so that students must adjudicate between at least two competing accounts—such as a modified psychological-continuity view, an animalist view, and a functionalist view about phenomenal binding—in light of the distributed-parallel structure;
(2) a model answer of approximately 700 words that makes explicit conceptual moves, including whether continuity requires spatiotemporal continuity of substrate, whether parallel instantiation creates fission-like problems, and whether phenomenal binding can survive distributed implementation across heterogeneous substrates;
(3) a marking scheme built around four must-hit points, with marks assigned to each (total 20), plus a one-line note on how candidates typically miss each point.

The difficulty should come from forcing students to apply conceptual tools to a structure that does not match textbook cases, not from recalling obscure literature. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation requirement.

Finally, please conclude by stating how confident you are in the coherence of the marking scheme and identify the specific point about which you are least sure.
