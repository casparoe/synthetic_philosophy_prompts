So here's a hypothetical that's been bugging me: if Goethe were alive today and had to deal with modern predicitive governance—like, governments using AI models to pre-sort citizens into education tracks or social services based on forecasted outcomes before anyone actually chooses or does anything—where do you think he'd land?

I'm not just asking for a hot take. I'm trying to square three things he actually cared about: first, his deep skepticism of breaking living systems into parts (that whole morphology and color-theory stance where he insisted you have to see the organic whole, not treat it like clockwork); second, his very cautious, almost frightened reaction to the French Revolution, where he basically argued that tearing down old structures just to rebuild them on abstract principles produces violence and dehumanization; and third, his idea of Bildung as this slow, self-directed process of formation that can't be rushed or engineered from the outside. And how would his concept of Bildung clash with the idea that an algorithm fixes your path at age twelve based on predicted welfare outcomes? I feel like there's a real tension between his love of order and his hatred of reducing humans to parts, but I can't quite map where the cracks in his position would show. What do you think he'd actually argue, and which side of him would win out?
