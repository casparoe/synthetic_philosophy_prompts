Please argue that von Neumann's comparison of the brain to digital computers in his late writings—especially his observation that individual neurons are highly unreliable yet the brain achieves robust computation through analog, statistical, and non-Boolean mechanisms—undermines digital functionalism about cognition not merely at the implementation level but in principle. Specifically, defend the claim that neural noise and graded, continuous signal integration are not engineering imperfections to be abstracted away, but constitutive features of cognitive computation; consequently, any discrete digital architecture that simulates cognition must either import stochasticity extrinsically (breaking the intrinsic noise-structure coupling) or suppress the analog dynamics that generate reliability, thereby altering the very computational properties being explained. Address how this differs from standard multiple-realizability objections and whether it commits us to a form of biological essentialism about mind.
