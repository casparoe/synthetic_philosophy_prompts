I'm writing up my philosophical profile for a graduate seminar on negative ethics and axiology, and I want a consistency audit before I present it to the group. Here is where I've landed. First, I hold a strict tranquilist position: only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic axiological weight, while pleasure, knowledge, achievement, and similar states have value solely as instrumental stabilizers of that absence. Second, I draw a distinction within craving itself: somatic craving—the bodily drives of appetite and aversion—is always intrinsically disvaluable when present, but noetic craving—the structured drive for coherence, understanding, or explanatory closure—can be axiologically neutral, functioning as a kind of architectural scaffold that sustains long-term tranquility without itself constituting suffering. Third, my normative framework is globally negative-utilitarian across sentient beings: the primary moral obligation is to minimize aggregate suffering, and there is no symmetrical duty to maximize flourishing or happiness. Fourth, despite this orientation, I reject strong anti-natalism; I think bringing new conscious beings into existence is permissible, even laudable, provided their expected suffering is near zero and their presence can contribute—through collaboration, inquiry, or moral progress—to reducing suffering elsewhere. Fifth, I maintain that a state of minimal conscious awareness without any form of craving, which I call empty tranquility, is not merely the absence of disvalue but possesses a faint positive axiological status, analogous to the value of perfect silence in an otherwise noisy environment. Finally, I think that artistic creation and philosophical inquiry deserve robust protection and pursuit, not because they yield pleasure or knowledge for their own sake, but because they cultivate the precise mental architecture—low in somatic craving, structured by noetic scaffolding—that makes sustained tranquility achievable.

I want to know whether this package actually hangs together. Specifically: which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, which ones only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real conflict what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally damaging. For each tension, say exactly which commitment has to give and what that costs in terms of the broader view. Then tell me which single revision—if I could make only one—would clean up the most inconsistencies while preserving the core tranquilist orientation. I am not asking for a general lecture on negative ethics; I want an audit of this specific belief set, with particular attention to whether my distinction between somatic and noetic craving does the work I need it to do, and whether empty tranquility introduces a hidden positive value that undermines the first commitment.
