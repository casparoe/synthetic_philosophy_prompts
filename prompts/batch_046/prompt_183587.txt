After a long and somewhat frustrating argument with a friend last night about who gets to blame whom and whether hypocrisy is always disqualifying, I want to set out my commitments precisely and see whether they actually cohere. Here is the package as I understand it:

(a) Standing to blame is relational but not victim-exclusive: one must not be personally implicated in the specific wrong being blamed, and one must have a legitimate normative interest in its correction.

(b) There is a distinctive failure I call "agential hypocrisy"—blaming someone for violating a norm that one currently and knowingly violates in one's own conduct—which undermines standing. By contrast, "structural hypocrisy," in which one benefits from systemic conditions that generate a wrong without personally enacting it, does not undermine standing.

(c) Victimhood confers a durable, domain-transcendent entitlement: a victim of wrong W retains standing to blame perpetrators of W even if the victim has herself committed W, or closely analogous wrongs, in other contexts or at other times.

(d) Standing lost through agential hypocrisy can be regained only through a public act of accountability—explicit acknowledgment coupled with repair—rather than through private moral reform or the mere passage of time.

(e) Blame is fundamentally a normative demand addressed to the wrongdoer, not an expressive release or a communicative signal; therefore standing should be understood as the entitlement to issue that particular address.

Please audit this set. I am not asking for a literature survey; I want to know which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severely. For each real tension, specify what has to give and what the cheapest revision would cost me. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad. Then identify the single revision that would clean up the most problems at once. I am especially unsure about whether my distinction in (b) is compatible with the relational basis in (a), and whether (c) can survive intact if (b) is taken seriously, but I do not want to prejudice the answer: treat all five as equally up for grabs.
