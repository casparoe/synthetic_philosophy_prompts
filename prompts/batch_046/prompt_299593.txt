Construct a thought experiment in which an AI system guided by liberal neutrality must endorse a public policy that every major value-aggregation framework—utilitarian, prioritarian, and maximin—would jointly reject, with all three frameworks converging on the opposite recommendation. The setting should be a realistic institutional decision about allocating a fixed cultural-preservation budget between a widely spoken majority language and several minority languages, not a trolley-style emergency, and the AI must have full information about citizens' preferences and the outcomes of each option. The divergence must arise from a structural feature of neutrality itself—specifically, its treatment of comprehensive doctrines as non-comparable across citizens—rather than from informational limits, technological constraints, or neutrality being interpreted as simple indifference. Present the scenario in one paragraph. Then state the verdict that liberal neutrality delivers and the common verdict of the three aggregation methods, explaining the reasoning for each. Identify the precise feature of the neutrality condition that forces the split, describe what happens to the case if that feature is removed, and outline how an aggregationist defender would attack the scenario by arguing that neutrality has been illicitly elevated to a first-order value rather than treated as a procedural constraint.
