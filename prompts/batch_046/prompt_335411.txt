Construct a thought experiment, realizable in principle within special relativity and using only macroscopic physical processes (no exotic physics, no perfect predictors, no science-fictional devices), in which a single observer—rather than two spatially separated ones—undergoes a precise change in motion or orientation that forces her to reassign which distant events lie in her present, thereby reproducing the core tension of the Rietdijk-Putnam argument for eternalism without relying on distant simultaneous observers. Engineer the case so that: (a) a presentist committed to one unique global present must either reject the observer’s revised assignment or accept that contradictory ontological statuses hold for the same distant region; (b) an eternalist resolves the tension without contradiction; and (c) a moving-spotlight theorist delivers a verdict that differs from both, specifically by retaining a single privileged present while denying that distant events alter their ontological status when the observer’s assignment shifts. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate the reasoning for each of the three positions, isolate the precise structural feature (not merely relative motion) that causes the moving-spotlight view to diverge, explain what happens to the argument if that feature is removed, and show how a presentist defender would attack the case as question-begging. Take a committed stance on whether framing the argument around a single observer genuinely sidesteps the conventionalist objection about distant simultaneity, and defend that stance with explicit reasons rather than merely laying out options.
