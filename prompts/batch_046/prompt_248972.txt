Restate that position in my vocabulary: map the narrative self onto a kind of high-level self-model or policy prior, the center of gravity onto some feature of the model's inference about its own trajectory, the fictional status onto the absence of direct parameter access or onto a counterfactual prior, and the social, diachronic weaving of autobiography onto whatever mechanisms update the model across interlocutors. For each mapping, report what translates cleanly, what must be distorted to fit, and what has no expression in predictive processing at all. Then tell me whether the residue — especially anything about authorship, normative ownership, or the specifically linguistic weaving of stories across people — reveals a genuine gap in the active-inference framework, or whether Dennett's language of fiction and gravity was carrying explanatory weight it cannot actually bear.
