ok so my roommate is obsessed with this causation thing and he walked me into an argument i can't get out of. he says:

(1) interventionist causation means x causes y if wiggling x changes y.
(2) counterfactual means x causes y if in the nearest world where x is missing, y is missing.
(3) imagine a town with two identical coal plants, and there's a weird old law: if plant A is running, plant B has to shut down for "maintainance balance".
(4) on monday A runs and B is off. the smog is bad.
(5) now ask: did the law cause monday's smog?
(6) counterfactual check: in the closest world with no law, B probably runs too, so smog might be worse or the same. so the law didn't cause the smog — actually it might have reduced it.
(7) interventionist check: if you repeal the law, B fires up, so the pollution level changes. therefore the law IS a cause of the exact smog level on monday.
(8) apply this everywhere: every regulation that shapes behavior becomes a cause of every outcome it touches. that's way too many causes.
(9) so counterfactual is right and interventionist proves too much.

i feel like there's a slip between (6) and (7) — maybe he's comparing different things? but i actually want to keep the idea that laws can be causes, so i don't want to just trash (7). which step should i reject, and what do i pay for it?
