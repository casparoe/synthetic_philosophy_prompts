After a long argument with a friend about whether the moral equality of combatants can survive revisionist just-war theory, I want a decision tree that sorts positions by answers to three concrete diagnostic questions rather than by the labels philosophers wear. Start from the commitment: combatants on opposing sides may kill each other, yet only some are genuinely liable to defensive killing. First branch: does liability track objective just cause, or subjective reasonable belief about cause? Second branch: does a coerced or profoundly ignorant combatant on the unjust side retain full non-combatant immunity, or only diminished liability? Third branch: can proportionality be satisfied when harm is distributed probabilistically across liable and non-liable targets, or must it be deterministically assigned to identifiable individuals? Each node should be phrased as a yes/no question answerable without knowing which named view it leads to. The leaves should include traditional Walzerian symmetry, McMahan-style revisionist asymmetry, and any coherent intermediate combinations; please note any logically available branch combinations that remain unoccupied and briefly diagnose why—whether incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of leaves the tree genuinely cannot separate, particularly if subjective-belief tracking combined with deterministic proportionality collapses into one of the two named positions.
