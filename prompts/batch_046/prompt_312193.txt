You are a professional philosopher with expertise in ethics and conceptual analysis. Before answering, briefly brainstorm the main positions on whether pleasure is unified by an intrinsic quality, a functional role, or only a family resemblance. Then consider the following dispute between two friends of mine—neither has formal philosophy training—who have been thinking about hedonism on their own.

Maya said that if pleasure is the only thing that is genuinely good, there must be something all pleasures share; otherwise “maximize pleasure” is as empty as “maximize blarg.” She pointed out that eating a ripe peach and finally grasping a hard proof feel very different, yet both carry a kind of “this is right” positive tone that is present even when you are not conventionally happy.

Leo replied that this “positive tone” is just a metaphor. The bodily relief of stretching after sitting too long and the intellectual satisfaction of solving an equation share no detectable feature except the word we apply afterward. He suggested that “pleasure” works like Wittgenstein’s example of “game”—a family resemblance with no single essence—so hedonism is incoherent unless it arbitrarily picks one kind of pleasure and ignores the rest.

Maya countered that family resemblance still requires overlapping similarities. She proposed a functional signature: pleasures are states you are motivated to prolong or repeat in the moment itself, regardless of content. Even the strange pleasure of a sad song is something you want to keep experiencing right then, and this works for both bodily enjoyment and mental fulfillment without reducing them to one identical feeling.

Leo said this collapses pleasure into desire. People sometimes want to stay anxious because it sharpens their performance, yet anxiety is not pleasure. He added that in some cultures there is no single word covering both bodily enjoyment and intellectual fulfillment, which suggests English “pleasure” forces together experiences that do not naturally belong together. So hedonism either bundles unrelated things or fails.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of Maya’s and Leo’s four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly correct and briefly justify each rating. Then say who is closer to the truth overall, and identify what both are missing—especially whether there is a defensible middle position that avoids treating pleasure as one single feeling without reducing hedonism to mere wordplay. Think carefully and be precise.
