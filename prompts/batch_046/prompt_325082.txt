After a long and somewhat heated argument with a friend last night about rational choice under correlation without causation, I want to check whether the position I ended up defending is actually coherent before I present it in a seminar. I hold the following commitments: (1) causal decision theory correctly describes rational choice in ordinary cases, because what matters is the causal efficacy of one's action; (2) in Newcomb's problem, one-boxing is rational, but only because the predictor's past behavior and my present choice are jointly caused by my underlying psychological type, so the correlation is causally anchored rather than a bare evidential link; (3) counterfactual mugging should be resisted—if an agent in a counterfactual branch predicts I will refuse and therefore withholds a reward I never actually had access to, paying in the actual world is irrational, since there is no causal pathway linking my payment to any benefit, and honoring such correlations licenses exploitation; (4) by contrast, in XOR blackmail—where two independent blackmail threats are wired so that my vulnerability to one is logically tied to my reasoning about the other—I believe one should pay, because the XOR structure makes my reasoning about one threat evidentially relevant to the other, and refusing both exposes me to harm through my own predictable reasoning pattern; (5) I also maintain that rational agents should never assign decision weight to outcomes in counterfactual worlds that share no causal connection with their actual situation; (6) finally, I hold that expected utility must always be calculated strictly at the actual decision node, with probabilities assigned only over causally accessible outcomes.

My friend insisted that (2) and (4) both invoke non-causal correlations that (1), (3), and (5) reject, and that (6) undermines both (2) and (4). I think there is a principled distinction between causally anchored correlations and spurious ones, but I am not sure I have drawn it consistently. Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but can be reconciled, and what is the least costly revision if I must give something up? Please rank the tensions by severity, and for each specify exactly which commitment would have to be weakened or abandoned to restore consistency.
