Give an example of a sustained human project or vocation in which the practitioner derives robust, non-illusory significance precisely from pursuing the activity with explicit awareness that it is cosmically futile and will leave no lasting trace—where the futility is not a psychological obstacle to be overcome but a constitutive condition of the commitment itself. The example should illuminate the conceptual tension between Camus-style absurdism, in which revolt against meaninglessness is said to generate its own form of dignity, and contemporary hybrid theories of meaning that treat subjective engagement and objective value or achievement as jointly necessary conditions. In particular, show how the project avoids collapsing either into mere subjective preference-satisfaction, which hybrid accounts might accommodate without difficulty, or into an objective accomplishment that undermines the absurdist framing by supplying the very cosmic significance the agent claims to reject.
