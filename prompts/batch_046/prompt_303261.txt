In clinical mindfulness protocols derived from Buddhist practice, patients are often guided to observe traumatic autobiographical memories with the explicit framing that these mental contents lack self-ownership, applying the anatta doctrine directly to first-person memory. Meanwhile, the same institutions maintain continuous case files, assign long-term prognosis, and hold clinicians accountable for outcomes tied to a single enduring patient. What theory of personal identity and psychological ownership does the decentering instruction actually presuppose? Must it be grounded in a Humean bundle view, or can a narrative-identity theorist consistently adopt it without contradiction? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the protocol strictly requires from what merely fits well with it, and say whether maintaining unified case records is philosophically incompatible with the decentering practice or only administratively convenient. Give a committed answer: if the two are genuinely in tension, which should yield?
