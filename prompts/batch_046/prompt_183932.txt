The protagonist is this architecht who leaves her partner for what she tells everyone is a career move, but as I'm writing the scenes I keep finding these little details that match my own breakup from like eight years ago: the way she packs her books, the excuse about the lease, even a specific argument over a burnt lamp. At first I thought it was just good obsrevational writing, but now I'm wondering if I'm actually excavating my own real motives for leaving, which I always told myself were purely practical.

I don't need a reading list, I'm just trying to figure out if I should lean into this hidden-motive thread for the novel or treat it as artistic coincidence. If introspection is that unreliable, does using fiction to "discover" your own past even make sense, or am I just building a false autobiography and calling it art? Would love a take that takes both the writing process and the idea seriously, not just "trust your gut" or "all art is personal" fluff.
