Draft a 90-minute essay question for an upper-level political-philosophy seminar that asks students to apply Simone Weil’s critique of political parties—her claim that parties operate as machines producing collective passion, suppressing individual moral attention, and requiring loyalty prior to judgment—to algorithmically curated political communities on large social-media platforms. Frame the platform’s recommendation architecture as an analog of a party-machine that lacks formal membership, explicit ideology, and hierarchical leadership. Require students to determine which of Weil’s objections survive this translation, which depend on institutional features now absent, and whether the analogy reveals algorithmic curation as a new form of the danger she identified or as something categorically different. Provide a model answer that distinguishes the affective dynamics of algorithmic sorting from the organizational dynamics of parties and identifies at least one point where Weil’s framework is silent. Include a marking scheme with five must-hit points totaling 20 marks, each with its weight and a one-line note on how candidates typically miss it.
