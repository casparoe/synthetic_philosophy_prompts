In a clinical-ethics seminar someone dismissed algorithmic withdrawal-of-care programmes by repeating the line 'omission is not commission': since the physician does not actively administer a lethal agent, they argued, no killing occurs, even when predictive suffering-modelling shows identical trajectories for withdrawal and active euthanasia. I want to understand what that slogan actually amounts to. Can you separate the precise claims it might express — about causal contribution, about bodily movement versus institutional protocol, about moral agency under deterministic healthcare algorithms, and about whether identical predicted suffering changes the moral category — and for each say whether it is a platitude, a defensible substantive thesis, or false? Then tell me which of these readings actually supports blocking algorithmic protocols, and which leave the ethical question untouched. Please write your answer in American English.
