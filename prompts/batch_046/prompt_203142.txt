I have recently become personally invested in questions of self-locating belief after assisting with the design of a cognitive study that employs staggered awakening protocols, and I now find myself unable to resolve a disagreement that arose between two philosophers I consulted about the statistical reasoning involved. They were discussing a variant of the Sleeping Beauty problem in which Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday, a fair coin is flipped, and if the result is heads she is awakened and interviewed on both Monday and Tuesday, whereas if tails she is awakened and interviewed only on Monday; after each interview her memory of the awakening is erased and she is returned to sleep if the protocol requires it. The dispute concerns not merely the standard credence Beauty should assign to heads upon being awakened, but specifically whether her reasoning should treat the two possible Monday-Tuesday centered perspectives under heads as evidentially equivalent to the single Monday perspective under tails, and whether this requires adopting a synchronic rather than diachronic framework for self-locating update.

Maya defended the thirder position. She argued that upon awakening, Beauty should consider three centered possibilities that are, by the symmetry of the protocol, equally likely from her first-person perspective: (heads, Monday), (heads, Tuesday), and (tails, Monday). Since two of these three involve heads, her credence in heads should be one-third. She further claimed that this is not a case of receiving new empirical evidence about the coin, but of resolving self-locating uncertainty within a fixed evidential base, analogous to being told you are in one of several identically furnished rooms where the distribution of rooms differs by some hidden state.

Jonas defended the halfer position. He replied that the coin flip is an ordinary chancy event with prior probability one-half, and that Beauty's awakening was guaranteed regardless of the outcome, so she acquires no information about the coin itself. He granted that she learns she is awake now, but insisted that this is not news, since she knew she would be awakened on Monday in either case. He added that allowing self-location to shift credence in past or hidden events leads to an unacceptable instability: if she updates to one-third on Monday, then on Tuesday—if heads—she should revert or shift again, even though no new empirical data about the coin has arrived, which suggests the update was never about the coin to begin with but about a spurious counting of perspectives.

Maya answered that Jonas conflates diachronic updating over time with synchronic updating across possible present centers. She proposed that Beauty's belief state upon awakening is not indexed merely by temporal stage but by a centered world that includes her present phenomenal location without a calendar marker. Under this synchronic framing, the prior over centered worlds is weighted by the protocol itself: heads generates two centers, tails generates one, so the uniform distribution over centers yields the one-third result. She argued that this does not require any empirical news about the coin, only the correct representation of her evidential situation.

Jonas countered that Maya's synchronic framing smuggles in a metaphysical commitment about the weighting of centered worlds that is not part of Beauty's evidence. He said that if Beauty genuinely lacks any reason to privilege one center over another, then she should apply the principle of indifference only after conditioning on the information she actually possesses—which is that she is awake and that the protocol is in effect—not on a hypothetical enumeration of all centers that might exist across the protocol. He concluded that Maya's counting argument treats the number of awakenings as if it were a sample space for a random selection, when in fact Beauty was not randomly assigned to one of the three centers; she deterministically occupies whichever one the protocol places her in.

Please adjudicate their exchange point by point: evaluate Maya's initial symmetry argument, Jonas's guarantee-of-awakening reply, Maya's synchronic-versus-diachronic distinction, and Jonas's final objection about conditioning on actual evidence versus enumerated centers. Say who is right about each specific move, identify what either party is missing, and state which position is more defensible overall, with reasons.
