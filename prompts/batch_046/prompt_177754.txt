I've been trying to map out a research program at the intersection of environmental philosophy and formal decision theory, focused on the precautionary principle not as a rhetorical device but as a structural constraint on rational choice under radical uncertainty. Most discussions oscillate between a weak reading—where precaution functions as a weighted heuristic within standard expected-utility frameworks—and a strong reading—where ignorance of outcomes itself triggers a duty to refrain or intervene. What strikes me as underexplored is the intermediate conceptual territory: whether one can formalize a reversibility threshold that determines when precautionary constraints should bind without collapsing into generic risk aversion, and whether such a threshold requires a prior commitment to intergenerational moral equality or can be justified on purely epistemic grounds.

Given that framing, I want a defended prioritization of the open questions that actually determine whether this intermediate formalization is possible, rather than a survey of the literature. For instance: can the distinction between risk and ambiguity be operationalized in a way that justifies differential regulatory treatment, or does every attempt implicitly rely on a hidden model of ignorance? Is the principle's appeal to irreversibility coherent without a metaphysically loaded account of temporal continuity—do we need to resolve problems in the philosophy of identity and persistence before applying the principle consistently across long time horizons? And if we succeed in formalizing precaution, does that formalization undermine its normative force by rendering it derivable from standard Bayesian updating with appropriately conservative priors? Please order these—or substitute others you consider more consequential—according to explicit criteria: which are upstream of the others, which are tractable with current conceptual and formal tools rather than requiring empirical resolution, and which carry the greatest leverage over actual institutional design. Defend the ranking by stating the trade-offs between dependency, tractability, and practical relevance; identify what kind of result—a formal model, a conceptual distinction, or a normative argument—would shift a question's position; and indicate where limited resources, say a three-to-four-year project with philosophical and mathematical training but no empirical data-collection capacity, should be concentrated. I am not asking for a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I want the reasoning that determines where the effort should go and why.
