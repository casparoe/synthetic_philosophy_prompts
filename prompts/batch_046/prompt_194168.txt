I've been trying to wrap my head around Dennett's real patterns essay and I think I've talked myself into a corner. Here's the chain I wrote for my study group.

(1) A pattern counts as real, not just projected, if adopting the stance that tracks it gives you predicitive leverage you can't get from lower-level stances. (2) The intentional stance gives you leverage over human behavior—counterfactuals like "she'd have left earlier if she hadn't beleived the bus was late"—that neither physics nor design-stance biology can match. (3) So beliefs and desires are real patterns, not fictions. (4) Now take evolutionary psychology: the design stance predicts behavior by citing adaptive function, but for some traits the functional story is so complex that treating the agent as rational (intentional stance) actually predicts better. (5) So for those traits, the intentional pattern is more real than the design pattern. (6) But if "more real" just means "better predictive leverage," then whenever physics gives poor predictions we can invent a higher-level stance with its own ontology. (7) That means for every physical system there's some higher stance—maybe treating galaxies as agents with goals—where that stance outpredicts physics. (8) So either we accept that galaxies literally have goals, or we reject (1) and say real patterns are just the physical ones after all. (9) But rejecting (1) makes science's use of chemistry, biology, etc., look like a useful fiction, which feels wrong. (10) So I'm stuck: it seems like (1) forces me to populate the universe with beleifs everywhere, or abandon the idea that chemistry is real. Which step is the weak link? I'd rather keep (1) and (3) if I can, but I'm not sure what that costs me—like do I have to say there's a principled cutoff that stops the slide before galaxies, and if so where does that cutoff come from if not from predictive power?
