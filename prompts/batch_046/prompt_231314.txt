I'm working on a feature about whether emergence in science is a real ontological thing or just a placeholder for reduction we haven't finished yet, and I tried to sort out my own verdicts on concrete cases before I start interviewing researchers. Water being wet feels like pure reduction: it's just hydrogen bonds doing their thing at scale, nothing left over. Same with temperature being average kinetic energy — that's a clean win for reductionism. Consciousness is the opposite for me; I don't think it's just neurons firing in a complicated pattern, I think subjective experience is genuinely emergent and I even buy downward causation, where mental states can push physical ones around. Then there's starling murmurations: the geometric shape is reducible to three simple rules per bird, but the collective predator-avoidance response feels like it belongs to the flock as a whole, not to any individual bird, so that part seems emergent.

The case I'm stuck on is imagine a synthetic neural network where error-correction patterns appear that no single node possesses, yet they're fully predictable from the architecture. To me that sounds reducible, but it feels exactly like consciousness, which I said was emergent — so I'm being inconsistent. Is there a principel that separates the water and temperature cases from consciousness and the flock's evasion behavior, and that also tells me whether the synthetic network counts as emergence? Or do I have to overturn one of my verdicts? And what should it predict for a hypothetical tissue-level biological field that's mathematically derivable from quantum interactions but only organizes cell division at the macro scale?
