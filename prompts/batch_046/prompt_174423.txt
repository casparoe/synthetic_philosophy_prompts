I am moderating a podcast interview with a philosopher who defends a mechanism-level reinterpretation of the principle of alternative possibilities against Frankfurt-style counterexamples. Rather than treating PAP as requiring the ability to perform a different action, she holds that it requires the presence of genuinely open causal pathways within the agent's practical-reasoning mechanism—pathways that can remain available even when an external intervener ensures only one action results. She argues that standard Frankfurt scenarios conflate action-level blockage with mechanism-level determinism, and that recognizing this distinction saves moral responsibility without relying on the familiar flicker-of-freedom reply.

Draft six to eight interview questions that press her on the actual content of this position. Include at least one question on how she distinguishes an open reasoning pathway from a merely counterfactual one; one on whether her view renders PAP empirically untestable or conceptually empty; and one on how she avoids collapsing her mechanism-level alternative back into the standard flicker objection. For each question, provide a brief follow-up tailored to the reply she is most likely to offer—for instance, that the pathway is defined by the agent's own normative commitments, or that testing it requires observing sub-personal causal structure rather than behavioral choice. Do not include generic biographical questions, and do not ask for citations, reading lists, or bibliographic references.
