I hold a strict reconstruction of Schopenhauer's account of music: unlike the other arts, which present Platonic Ideas as intermediaries between the Will and phenomenal appearance, music is a direct copy or objectification of the Will itself and therefore operates entirely outside the principle of sufficient reason. On this reading, melody expresses the Will's insatiable striving, harmony expresses the graded hierarchy of the Will's objectification in nature, and the result is a form of metaphysical knowledge that is immediate and requires no conceptual mediation. I draw from this a further, more contentious claim: if music genuinely bypasses representational content, then its basic intelligibility should not depend on cultural training or learned conventions—it should be immediately accessible as pure feeling—yet Schopenhauer also insists that only the philosophical genius or the profoundly reflective listener grasps music's true metaphysical significance. I defend both parts together. What would count decisively against this combined view? I want the strongest defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward: arguments that melody and harmony are conceptually or culturally mediated after all; empirical patterns in cross-cultural musical cognition that suggest understanding requires enculturation; objections that Schopenhauer's broader metaphysics can survive without this exceptional status for music; and any move that insulates the view by redefining every apparent counter-example as a failure of the listener rather than of the theory. Please distinguish what a committed defender of this Schopenhauerian view would themselves concede as genuinely damaging, and give an honest assessment of whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability—and if it has, whether that is a fatal problem for a metaphysical claim of this kind.
