ok so I'm a retired physicist (spent about 30 years in statistical mechaniks) and lately I've gotten into philosophy of probability because I kept arguing with economist friends about risk vs uncertainty. I'm writing up my view for a small reading group and want a consistency check before I present, since I'm prettry sure I'm mixing standard Keynes with Savage in a way that might not hang together.

Here's my package: (1) I think Keynes was basically right in his big book that probability is an objective logical relation between a proposition and the total evidence, not a subjecive feeling — so I'm firmly against the pure Bayesians on ontology. (2) At the same time I think Savage's decision framework is correct for practice: you hold personal probabilities and maximize expected utility, full stop. (3) I take Keynes's "weight of argument" seriously — I think piling up evidence can leave the probability value fixed at, say, 0.6 while increasing how much you should trust that value, and I think that trust-factor should feed into decision-making directly, like a weight that discounts the utility. I call this the weight-utility interaction in my notes. (4) I reject the principle of indiference, just like Keynes did, because spreading belief evenly over unknown possibilities isn't rational. (5) Despite (1) and (2) seeming to clash, I think they're compatible: the logical relations constrain which subjective probabilities are rational, but Savage still tells you what to do with them.

So does this actually hang together? Specifically: is the tension between (1) and (2) real, or does (5) resolve it? And does my idea in (3) about weight entering the utility calculation conflict with Keynes's own separation of weight from probability — is that serious or just me extending him? Also, I worry that rejecting the principle of indiference in (4) undermines (1), since without it we can't assign numerical probabilities in ignorance and the logical relation becomes mostly empty. How severe is each?

Please rank the tensions by severity and for each say what the cheapest revision would be. Don't lecture me on Keynes biography — I'm asking whether my commitments cohere, not whether Keynes would approve.
