I'm trying to determine what emotions actually are—whether they are best understood as judgments, perceptions, feelings, or some hybrid—by testing my intuitions against specific episodes. Here are my verdicts.

Case one: I see a shadow that looks like a coiled snake and feel sudden fear, then realize it's only a rope. I think the fear was not a judgment that I was in danger; it was more like a quick perceptual registration of a threat, and it faded the moment my belief changed.

Case two: I learn that a close colleague has taken credit for a project I led, and I feel sustained anger. Here the emotion seems essentially a judgment that a loyalty-norm has been violated; if I came to believe she was coerced, the anger would dissolve in a way the fear in case one did not.

Case three: I wake up on a Sunday with a heavy, diffuse sadness that colors everything for hours, though I can identify no particular loss or failure it is about. This feels like a pure bodily-affective state, not directed at any object; if it has intentionality at all, it is only in the loosest sense.

Case four: I feel genuine grief when a beloved fictional character dies in a novel, even though I never believed she was real. The grief is directed at narrative events, but since there is no belief in their reality, it cannot be a standard judgment; yet it is not like case three, because it clearly has specific content.

Case five: I recall an awkward social mistake from a decade ago and feel sharp shame, even though my present judgment is that no one remembers it and it carries no real weight. The shame survives my explicit rejection of its grounds, which makes me think it might be a recalcitrant feeling or a lingering judgment I have not fully revoked.

Case six—the one I keep going back and forth on: I contemplate the eventual heat death of the universe and feel a kind of existential dread. There is no specific event I fear, no agent responsible, and no bodily sensation I can isolate; yet the dread is not free-floating like case three, because it seems anchored in the abstract fact itself. I cannot decide whether this is a judgment that that state is bad, a perception of metaphysical threat, or just a somber mood mislread as intentional.

Is there a single principle—perhaps a qualified judgment theory, a perceptual account with content conditions, or a feeling theory with intentional structure—that delivers all six verdicts? If not, which verdict should I overturn for the sake of simplicity? Whatever the account, please state it precisely, indicate which clause is motivated by which case, and say what it predicts for an episode I have not listed: feeling sorrow that two plus two is not five, directed at the impossibility itself. Finally, be explicit about which of my six verdicts your favored principle cannot accommodate.
