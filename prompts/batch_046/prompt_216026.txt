Design a thought experiment that isolates the tension between the “all-affected interests” principle, the “all-subjected” principle, and a hypothetical “stakeholder reciprocity” criterion for democratic membership, situated within a synthetic ecosystem of distributed cognitive agents that lacks any centralized sovereign authority. The scenario must satisfy the following specifications: (1) one agent is included by all-affected interests but excluded by all-subjected; (2) a different agent is included by all-subjected but excluded by all-affected; (3) a third agent is included by both standard principles yet excluded by stakeholder reciprocity; (4) no agent possesses full human-level phenomenal consciousness, and no technology exceeds present theoretical computer science; (5) the scenario is confined to a single paragraph. After presenting the case, demonstrate—without stipulating the verdicts—why each principle delivers its specified judgment based on its internal logic: trace how affectedness, subjection to coercive rules, and capacity for reciprocal justification are respectively applied. Identify the precise structural feature of the ecosystem that forces the three-way divergence—whether it is the absence of coercive institutions, the presence of indirect but systematic causal influence, or the asymmetry in deliberative capacity. Then explain what happens if that feature is removed: does the scenario collapse into a familiar boundary-problem example, or does a residual disagreement persist? Finally, provide an honest reconstruction of the defense that a proponent of stakeholder reciprocity would mount against the case, including how they might redefine the relevant capacities without abandoning the principle’s core commitment, and note whether that defense succeeds in recovering the excluded agent or merely relocates the boundary dispute.
