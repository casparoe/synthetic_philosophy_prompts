I am a retired physicist who, since leaving active research, have begun studying the philosophy of science with some rigor. I now serve on the monthly acquisitions committee of a university archive that receives unsolicited bequests—laboratory notebooks, correspondence, and instruments—from researchers who assert that their work overturns a long-dominant explanatory framework. Our conservation budget and storage space are strictly limited, and my colleagues are trained archivists and scientists rather than philosophers; we must document each decision for university auditors, but we have neither the time nor the staff to conduct original historical scholarship on every claim. I want a reusable, written decision procedure—one that can be applied in about thirty minutes per case—grounded in the forecasting logic of reference classes, base rates, and the outside view of imperial decline, but translated into the evaluation of what I will call scientific "empires": dominant theoretical frameworks that have governed a field for decades. The reference class should draw on well-known superseded regimes—such as phlogiston chemistry, the luminiferous ether, and classical Mendelian genetics prior to the molecular turn—treating their durations and modes of collapse as base-rate data. For each challenger claim submitted to the archive, the procedure should guide the evaluator through observable indicators analogous to pre-collapse imperial conditions: internal theoretical strain (ad hoc adjustments, declining predictive precision), anomaly accumulation (reproducible results that the framework cannot accommodate without structural damage), institutional sclerosis (defensive peer-review patterns, funding concentration, and resistance to methodological reform), and external competitive pressure (the presence of a rival framework that explains the same domain with greater parsimony). Please construct the deliverable as follows. First, a sequential checklist of no more than seven steps, ordered so that an early failure permits rapid rejection without further work. Second, for each step, explicit graded or pass-fail tests that rely only on the submitted materials, standard scientific literacy, and publicly available institutional records—no philosophical specialization required. Third, a worked application to a hypothetical claim that the procedure correctly rejects as ephemeral, including the specific indicator that triggers failure and noting that this answer will likely displease the donor. Fourth, a worked application to a hypothetical claim that initially appears implausible yet survives the filter, showing which indicators are satisfied and which remain ambiguous. Fifth, an explicit statement of the decision nodes where quantitative judgment cannot be eliminated by rule, the signature required at those nodes, and the characteristic false-positive and false-negative errors the procedure will commit. Sixth, a brief note on what the procedure is structurally incapable of doing—such as identifying genuinely revolutionary work that lacks any imperial precursor or recognizing gradual conceptual erosion that produces no discrete anomaly—so that the committee knows when to override it. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, citation formats, or references to further literature; I need only the operational framework itself, written with sufficient formal precision that it could be appended directly to our committee bylaws.
