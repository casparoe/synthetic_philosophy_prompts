Rank these five conceptions of maximal moral goodness by how desirable it actually is to become that kind of person, not merely to admire them: (1) Susan Wolf's "loving saint," who integrates deep moral commitment with rich personal projects and non-moral goods; (2) the Kantian agent of perfect virtue, for whom moral worth exhausts the value of action; (3) the effective altruist, who treats the maximization of expected impartial welfare as the overriding life aim; (4) the Aristotelian megalopsychos, who regards ordinary moral demands as small-scale and subordinate to greatness of soul; and (5) a "quietist saint," who achieves maximal moral goodness precisely by rendering morality invisible in self-presentation and refusing to treat moral achievement as part of identity. Assign each a tier—S, A, B, C, or F—where S means fully desirable to embody and F means actively undesirable. For each tier, write exactly one sentence identifying whether the central problem is internal incoherence, psychological impossibility, or a conflict with non-moral flourishing. Do not provide citations or a literature review; evaluate the conceptions on their own terms.
