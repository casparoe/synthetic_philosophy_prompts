After a long argument last night with a friend about whether germs, climate shifts, and volcanic eruptions can be treated as genuine historical actors rather than mere background conditions, I attempted to formalize my own view and now worry that the parts pull in opposite directions. My commitments are these. First, I think that in the strict sense agency requires intentional states, so only beings with minds can be true agents. Second, despite that, I regard events like the 1815 Tambora eruption or the transmission of Yersinia pestis during the fourteenth-century Black Death as causal protagonists: they redirect societies, collapse economies, and restructure political authority in ways that cannot be captured by treating them as passive scenery. Third, I reject environmental determinism, holding that human social, economic, and institutional structures always mediate the effects of climate or disease, producing outcomes that are not mechanically fixed by the natural trigger. Fourth, I also hold that once a non-human force is embedded within a social system, its causal role becomes functionally equivalent to that of a strategic human agent, because it forces adaptive choices, reallocates power, and sets constraints that actors must navigate. Fifth, I deny that there is any sharp metaphysical line separating human causation from non-human causation in history; I see influence as a continuum. Sixth, precisely because I fear sliding into determinism, I maintain that rigorous historiography should avoid attributing agency to volcanoes or pathogens altogether, confining such language to metaphor. I am writing this not to defend one side but to ask whether these six positions form a coherent package or whether they secretly undermine one another. Please go through them and identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve upon closer analysis, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, explain what the cheapest revision would be and what exactly it costs me in terms of the other commitments. Finally, tell me which single commitment I should drop if I want to clean up the greatest number of conflicts with the least damage to the overall view. I ask that you reply entirely in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, boldface, or tables, arranged as you would in a careful letter or long email, since I need to read it as a single flowing argument.
