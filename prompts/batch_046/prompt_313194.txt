Since I retired from the lab I've been getting into philosphy of mind, and I keep seeing people in AI ethics claim that an RL agent's reward signal is basically valence — positive reward is pleasure, negative is suffering. They use it to argue we shouldn't apply large negative rewards in case we're actually torturing the system. It strikes me as a bit like the old behavourist experiments with rats and sugar pellets, except here the agent just receives a number, not a feeling. But then I read about this newer idea that suffering might not need any felt quality at all — it could just be a persistent negative prediction error that the system can't resolve. How far does the 'reward signal as valence' analogy actually hold? Give me the correspondance: what plays the role of the nociceptor, the limbic loop, the subjective report? Where does it break, especially if we take that unresloved-error view seriously? And does the ethical conclusion — avoid big negative rewards — survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the argument all along?
