I have come to accept the standard anti-substantivalist conclusion drawn from the hole argument: if spacetime points are bare individuals with primitive identity, then general covariance implies radical indeterminism about which point carries which metric property, and this counts as a genuine defect that undermines manifold substantivalism. Play it straight: what would have to be true for this conclusion to fail? Which premises — that diffeomorphism-related metrics represent the same physical situation, that bare points lack essential qualitative properties, or that determinism in this specific sense is a theoretical virtue — could be denied without incoherence? Consider in particular the metric-essentialist reply, on which points essentially possess their geometric properties and so cannot be shuffled by diffeomorphisms: does it block the argument or merely relocate the indeterminism? And do considerations from quantum gravity, especially the emphasis on diffeomorphism-invariant observables, alter the classical stakes? Rank these potential defeaters from fatal (the anti-substantivalist position collapses) to merely awkward (it survives with heavy qualification). I want an honest assessment of how exposed the standard conclusion actually is, and whether its insulation from refutation is a strength or a weakness.
