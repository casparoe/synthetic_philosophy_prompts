I sit on the exhibition ethics board of the Midwest Regional Archive, and we gotta vote Thursday on whether to greenlight a new digital exhibit called Sanitation and Sovereignty: Public Health Campaigns, 1910-1940. The curators have annotated every primary doc—letters, posters, clinical logs—with modern ethical tags: they call certain quarantine practices "racialized coercion" and vaccination drives "non-consensual by current bioethical standards," plus there's a banner admitting presentism is unavoidable. About half the board thinks if we don't apply our own moral framework we're just reproducing historical harm; the other half says we're slapping anachronistic categories onto actors who didn't share them, which makes it impossible to understand what they actually meant. I need a short memo—two pages max—recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions, plus a concrete timeline for whatever changes or rollout. It should take both sides seriously, especially the strongest argument from the losing side, and not just say "presentism is bad" or "presentism is good." Maybe engage the distinction between using present values to decide what deserves exhibition versus using them to judge the actors inside the frame? I was talking with a friend in museum studies who mentioned something like "double hermeneutics"—that the archive is already an interpretation, not a neutral window—but I'm probly misusing that. Don't worry about citations or reading lists; just write the reasoning the board could actually act on, and if you think one framing wins, say so clearly but don't pretend the other side has no weight.
