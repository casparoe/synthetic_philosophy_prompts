I was discussing with a colleague how to open a graduate seminar on Renaissance political thought without falling into the usual caricature of Machiavelli as a mere advocate of cynicism. I would like a joke that engages the actual conceptual pairing of fortune and virtù as developed in The Prince—one that assumes the audience knows fortune is not pure randomness but a structural contingency analogous to the river that can be channeled with dikes if anticipated, and that virtù is not moral excellence but an adaptive, technical capacity to reshape action as circumstances shift. The punchline should turn on the ironic asymmetry that fortune rewards decisive aggression precisely because she is unreliable, and that the mastery virtù achieves is therefore never a possession but a continuous, unstable re-enactment. The joke should be self-contained, should avoid invented quotations or precise chapter references, and should work for listeners who understand the theory but do not have the text open in front of them. Please keep it polished and brief—no more than a short paragraph—so that it functions as an actual opening remark rather than an explanation.
