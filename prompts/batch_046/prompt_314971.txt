I was recently appointed to my university’s Faculty Appeals and Discipline Board, and our first case has made the boundary between dissent and sabotage personally urgent for me. The respondent, L., is a doctoral student in political philosophy who was suspended not for violating any explicit regulation, but for organizing a “silent quorum” in mandatory departmental assemblies: participants attended and fulfilled all procedural duties except one—no one verbally acknowledged the chair’s authority to convene. There was no “here,” no assent to minutes, no performative recognition of institutional legitimacy. The administration charges disruption of governance; L. argues she was practicing civil disobedience directed at what she terms “acknowledgment obligations,” the minimal social performances by which members sustain the common knowledge that an authority is legitimate. The board must decide whether withholding these un-codified performative acts is protected political expression or punishable institutional sabotage.

Draft a formal memo for the board recommending whether the suspension should stand or be reversed. Structure it exactly as follows:

1. Executive Summary (about 150 words): state the bottom-line recommendation clearly.
2. The Case and the Conceptual Issue (about 200 words): describe the silent quorum, define acknowledgment obligations in plain language, and frame the philosophical problem—whether political obligation includes a duty to supply the social acts that make authority common knowledge, and whether refusing them can count as justified civil disobedience.
3. The Case for Upholding the Suspension (about 250 words): present the strongest argument that this is not protected dissent—lack of publicity about the specific grievance, evasion of the usual conditions for civil disobedience, functional paralysis of a required body, and punishment of a norm the university never formally enacted but legitimately expects.
4. The Case for Reversing (about 250 words): present the strongest counter—that if acknowledgment is genuinely performative and not legally mandated, withholding it expresses a view on legitimacy without coercion or deception; compare to conscientious refusal; and address whether an institution may discipline non-compliance with norms it has left implicit.
5. Recommendation and Rationale (about 200 words): deliver the clear recommendation, identify the decisive consideration, and give an honest account of the weight of the losing side.

Write for board members without philosophical specialization. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific works. Do not invoke obscure named theories without defining them in the text. Reason from premises the board can evaluate without external sources.
