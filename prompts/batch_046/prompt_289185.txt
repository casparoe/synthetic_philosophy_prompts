You chair the eligibility panel for a municipal emergency-relief fund that distributes limited temporary housing vouchers after localized disasters. The charter requires that subsidies cover only harm arising from brute luck—events outside an applicant’s reasonable control—and exclude harm traceable to option luck, that is, choices for which the applicant can be held responsible. Your panel reviews about twenty applications per session, has no staff philosopher, must issue written justifications auditable by a civil court, and has thirty minutes per case. Design a reusable decision procedure—an ordered sequence of questions, evidentiary thresholds, and stopping rules—that translates the luck-egalitarian distinction into something your panel can actually run. The procedure must explicitly handle three hard cases: (1) foreseeability failures when an applicant lacked standard information, such as an undocumented tenant unaware of flood-zone designations; (2) compound cases in which pre-existing poverty both constrained choice and magnified harm; and (3) collective-choice situations, such as a family unit that decided together whether to evacuate. At each step, flag whether the question is factual or requires a value judgment that the procedure cannot eliminate, and identify where the procedure is most likely to misfire. Apply it to three applicants: (a) a homeowner who declined optional flood insurance despite repeated, clearly communicated warnings; (b) a renter in an undocumented basement apartment who had no access to flood-zone maps and no notice of risk; and (c) a low-income family that stayed during a mandatory evacuation because leaving would have meant immediate job loss and forfeiture of their only vehicle, then lost their home. The procedure must yield at least one result the panel will find morally uncomfortable. Finally, state precisely what the procedure cannot decide—specifically, whether the fund should ever compensate for option-luck harm when refusing compensation would result in catastrophic, irreversible destitution.
