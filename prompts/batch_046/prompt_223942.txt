In a discussion of Lewis Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise—specifically the regress that arises when the Tortoise demands an explicit rule licensing the step from premises to conclusion—a classmate kept returning to the line: "If you grant the premises, logic leaves you no exit; there is no separate act of accepting the conclusion." I am trying to see what precise philosophical claims could be packed into that sentence. Could you lay out the distinct readings—whether psychological inevitability, semantic entailment, normative rational commitment, or a claim about the hierarchy of inference-rules—and for each indicate whether it is a platitude, a substantive thesis, or plainly false? Then say which of these readings actually addresses the Tortoise's regress about modus ponens, and which ones merely change the subject. I am particularly interested in whether the slogan silently assumes that to "grant" a premise is already to grant all of its logical consequences, and whether that assumption is defensible or just smuggled in to avoid Carroll's problem.
