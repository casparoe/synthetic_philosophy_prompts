I am a retired biologist—forty years in developmental genetics—who has recently begun leading a philosophy discussion circle for educated non-specialists, mostly fellow retired scientists and engineers. I want to design a single ninety-minute session on origin essentialism, specifically on whether one could have come from a different sperm and egg. I am not asking for the explanation itself; I want a teaching plan that tells me how to build it. Please organize your response in exactly these sections, with approximate word counts:

1. Conceptual Dependency Chain (c. 250 words): The precise sequence in which ideas must be introduced so that the essentialist claim does not collapse into either mystical vitalism or a trivial genetic observation. Identify the pivot point—probably the distinction between personal identity and genetic continuity—where this audience is most likely to conflate the two, and specify what must be secured before that pivot is reached.

2. The Carrying Case (c. 300 words): One concrete scenario that should carry the whole session. It must make the essentialist intuition feel grounded in biology without smuggling in teleology, and it must simultaneously show where the intuition frays. Build it around monozygotic twinning versus a counterfactual gamete substitution, but design it so that the genome is held constant in one variant and altered in another, forcing the audience to isolate origin from genetic makeup. State explicitly what properties this case must have and what it must lack—particularly, it should not invoke science-fiction multiverses or religious souls, and it should acknowledge cellular turnover rather than pretend the body is genetically static.

3. The Misconception Map (c. 300 words): The predictable misconceptions this audience will bring—likely treating "essential origin" as functional necessity for the organism's traits, importing evolutionary fitness logic, interpreting modal possibility as physical possibility in other universes, or assuming that chimerism and mitochondrial replacement therapy dissolve the essentialist claim by breaking one-to-one genome-to-origin mapping. For each misconception, state the exact point in the dependency chain where it should be defused, and with what distinction or counter-example.

4. The Understanding Check and the Explicit Omission (c. 250 words): One targeted question or brief exercise I can deploy at the end to distinguish those who grasp the philosophical claim from those who have merely adopted an essentialist mood; and one technical element—perhaps the formal modal logic of origins or the precise relation to Kripkean naming and necessity—that I should openly declare out of scope for this session and refuse to explain.

Throughout, separate what is settled in the specialist literature from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers who work on personal identity and modality. In particular, do not present the view that modern genetics uniquely supports origin essentialism as if it were the consensus view; mark clearly where specialists disagree about whether origin essentialism survives biological realities such as chimerism, mitochondrial heteroplasmy, and the complete cellular turnover of the adult body. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
