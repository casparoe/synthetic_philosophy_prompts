I'm working through my intuitions about cross-modal identification in cases that extend Molyneux's original sphere-and-cube problem, and I'd like to know whether they hang together. Here are my verdicts. First, the classic case: a person blind from birth learns a sphere and a cube by touch alone, then suddenly gains sight; I think they cannot immediately tell which object is which by vision alone. Second, the same individual, but for a full year before surgery they used a tactile-to-visual sensory-substitution device that mapped spatial layout to vibrations on the back; I still think that upon gaining sight they fail to recognize the shapes visually. Third, a sighted adult who has never touched either shape learns them only through precise geometric descriptions, is then blindfolded and handed the objects; I think they identify them correctly by touch right away. Fourth, a person born deaf learns sign language visually, then receives cochlear implants and hears spoken descriptions of hand shapes they already know; I think they match the auditory descriptions to the visual signs immediately. Fifth, a blind person navigates familiar buildings for years using echolocation, then gains sight and is shown photographs of those same interiors; I think they recognize the spaces. These five feel hard to square: touch-to-sight fails even with prolonged technological mediation, yet verbal-description-to-touch succeeds, echolocation-to-sight succeeds, and auditory-visual matching succeeds. Is there one principle—perhaps about embodied spatial schemas versus conceptually mediated representations, or about the format in which modality-specific information is stored—that delivers all five verdicts? If not, which verdict should I overturn and why? Please state the resulting principle precisely, identify which clause is motivated by which case, and say honestly which of my five judgments it cannot accommodate. Finally, apply your best account to a case I haven't listed: a person who learns music entirely through vibrations felt on the skin, then hears the same melodies for the first time through normal hearing—do they recognize them?
