I've been working through Frege's puzzle about identity statements — specifically the tension that "a = a" is trivially knowable a priori, while "a = b" can be a substantive empirical discovery, even when "a" and "b" co-refer. I have a decent working fluency in the vocabulary of dependent type theory, particularly the distinction between judgmental (definitional) equality and propositional equality, the identity type Id_A(x,y), path induction, and the way transport operates across a constructed path term. I'd like you to translate Frege's analysis into that framework: treat the names "a" and "b" as terms of some base type, treat the cognitive discovery of identity as the construction of a non-trivial path p : Id(x,y) versus the reflexivity witness refl : Id(x,x), and treat Frege's "sense" (Sinn) as whatever plays the analogous explanatory role — perhaps the computational trace of the term, the canonical form under normalization, or the proof object itself. For each component of Frege's original view — the sense/reference distinction, the informativeness of true identities, the idea that modes of presentation determine cognitive value, and the claim that identity is an object-level relation — say what translates cleanly into type-theoretic terms, what must be distorted to fit (and in which direction the distortion runs), and what has no expression whatsoever within that vocabulary. Then address the residue: does the fact that type theory can encode both trivial and non-trivial equality without any separate "mode of presentation" apparatus show that Frege's sense/reference split was doing redundant work, or does the absence of anything like cognitive significance or epistemic route in the type-theoretic account reveal a genuine limitation of that framework for explaining linguistic understanding? Please keep the translation honest — don't import assumptions like "all equality is computation" and present them as Frege's own commitments — and treat the failure points as the interesting part of the exercise rather than bugs to be patched over.
