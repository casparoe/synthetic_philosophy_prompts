Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Mira and Leo, arguing in a campus cafe about whether hedonism can survive the heterogeniety problem — what do pleasures actually share? Mira defends "affective non-resistance": pleasures dont share a feel, they share a structural lack of internal braking when sustained or repeated. Leo says thats just functionalism in disguise and introduces "blank reward" — a hypothetical neural interface that triggers all reward circuitry and approach-behavior but produces absolutely no qualitative feel, not even faint warmth, more like a silent green light in the mind. If Mira counts that as pleasure hedonism loses its soul; if she excludes it she needs a phenomenological criterion she said she didnt need. Mira replies blank reward lacks the subjective economic structure — but Leo accuses her of smuggling phenomenology back in through the word subjective. Then Mira flips it with "echo pleasures": remembering past joy can produce a faint diluted version now with no fresh stimulus — is that a new pleasure or just memory coloring? Leo argues that if echo pleasures are real, then pleasure can float free of any present object, which makes heterogeniety worse, not better, since now you've got affective states with no shared content. They should actually respond to each others points rather than talk past them, and neither should be a strawman. Write it casually, maybe let one of them misspell "recieve" or "occured" in the flow.
