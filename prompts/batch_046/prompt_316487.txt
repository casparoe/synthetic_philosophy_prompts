I have tentatively adopted a fairly strong synthesis within predictive processing and the free energy principle: not merely that brains approximate Bayesian inference, but that any adaptive system literally instantiates variational free-energy minimization, and that this collapses the distinction between perception and action into a single precision-weighted prediction-error dynamics. More specifically, I take the claim that representational content in cognition is nothing over and above the hierarchical structure of high-level predictions whose precision is contextually modulated—which, if correct, dissolves the standard representationalist versus non-representationalist debate in philosophy of mind. I also take affective valence to be interoceptive prediction error scaled by its expected precision, so that mood and motivation are not separate modules but direct properties of the inference process itself.

What would have to be true for this package to be wrong? I want an honest map of its exposure rather than a list of familiar complaints. First, are there empirical findings in neuroscience or cognitive psychology that, if robustly confirmed, would genuinely undermine the identity claim rather than merely forcing a parameter adjustment—say, neural architectures that implement model-free control without any hierarchical prediction, or perceptual phenomena that violate precision-weighting in a systematic rather than noisy way? Second, which formal results—perhaps about the computational intractability of deep hierarchical inference, or about alternative architectures that match behavioral and neural data without variational dynamics—would be decisive versus merely awkward? Third, could the framework accommodate every possible observation by redefining precision or prediction error, and if so, is that insulation a defensible theoretical virtue or a fatal flaw that renders the view unfalsifiable in the wrong way? I also want to know what committed defenders of this strong version would themselves concede as counting against them, not just what critics allege: which predictions do they actually risk, and which escape routes do they treat as legitimate theoretical refinement rather than ad hoc rescue?

Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Note whether any have arguably already occurred but been explained away within the framework, and whether there is a hidden dependency—perhaps on a particular interpretation of self-organization—that, if shown to be compatible with non-Bayesian dynamics, causes the edifice to collapse into a weaker, merely heuristic account. I am especially interested in whether there is a live conceptual defeater that shows the dissolution of the representational debate is achieved only by redefining representation in a way that makes the victory pyrrhic.
