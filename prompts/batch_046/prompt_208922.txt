I just finished a long, frustrating argument with a friend about Rousseau’s Letter to d’Alembert, and I need to defend my reading in a formal setting next week without being blindsided. I am presenting at an interdisciplinary seminar—half the audience are political theorists steeped in the Social Contract and the general-will literature, a third are performance-studies and theater historians who treat Rousseau as a reactionary moralist attacking actresses and luxury, and the rest are comparative-literature scholars working on affect theory. They are collectively skeptical of “system-building” interpretations that collapse aesthetics into politics. My thesis is that Rousseau’s anti-theatrical argument is not primarily a conservative panic about gender visibility or corruption by luxury, but a rigorous extension of his theory of representation: theatrical spectacle necessarily converts active civic identification into passive representational consumption, structurally mirroring how representatives destroy the general will. I argue specifically that Rousseau distinguishes “participatory sentiment”—embodied, reciprocal, and present in the Genevan festivals he praises—from “substituted affect,” in which the spectator’s emotional judgment is outsourced to the playwright’s framing and to the actor’s simulated passion. I further claim that this explains an otherwise puzzling asymmetry in his corpus: he permits his own opera, Le Devin du Village, and certain popular spectacles that do not require the audience to suspend belief about social hierarchy, while he bans dramatic forms—especially comedy and tragedy—that depend on exactly that suspension. To make the point precise, I use a distinction drawn from contemporary affect theory but mapped onto Rousseau’s vocabulary: first-order emotional engagement, in which the citizen feels as a member of the collective body, versus second-order emotional delegation, in which one feels through a represented other and thereby activates a hidden form of amour-propre disguised as benevolence. I am not asking for a bibliography or for citations; I need a speaker’s prep sheet for the Q&A.
