i want the full spec for what a theory of perceptual experience in these cases actually has to deliver. not just "explain the illusion" — i mean the real desiderta, with the motivating case behind each, and which are non-negotiable vs. camp preferences.

so here's what i think it needs: it has to account for the temporal ordering without allowing literal backward causation in neural processing, but it also can't just collapse the experienced timeline into the physical one. it must keep the distinction between revising the content of an experience and never having formed the "wrong" representation in the first place — that's the whole Orwellian/Stalinesque split, and if a theory can't tell us which is happening, that's a failure. it needs to handle the fact that subjects can be told about the illusion and still experience it, so the mechanism can't depend on belief or top-down expectation alone. it should be consistent with general visual binding latencies — there's some postdictive window, maybe a few hundred ms, where later input reshapes what was experienced — but without treating consciousness as just a delayed report. and it can't violate causal closure: no ghostly editing of past brain states.
