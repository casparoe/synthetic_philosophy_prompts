I'm a retired geophysicist who got hooked on philosophy after I stopped working, and now I'm stuck on my town's planning board. We keep recieveing proposals that mix up "what will happen" with "what ought to happen" and nobody can agree on which part is science and which part is just politics wearing a lab coat. I read a bit about Carnap — the logical empiricist guy — and his idea that planning has a hard scientific/empirical side and a seperate value side, plus this thing he called "explication" where you have to make vague terms precise inside a framework before you can even argue about them. I want to turn that into an actual checklist my board can run in about fifteen minutes on a one-page proposal.

Please write the procedure as ordered steps: first check whether a claim is internal to our current planning framework or whether it's asking us to pick a new framework; then flag where Carnap's value-freedom idea actually stops the science and hands the decision to a vote; then say exactly where the procedure gives up and it's just my judgement call dressed up as logic. I need it to handle hard cases where residents cite "the data" but the data only answers a question they already smuggled in. Don't make it a flowchart with boxes — just words, in order, with the stopping conditions clear.

Then run it on three proposals: (1) rezoning a floodplain using a 100-year rainfall model that some residents call "just statistics" and therefore not real science; (2) prioritizing affordable housing over green-space preservation, where both sides cite "the public good" as if it's one number; and (3) a vaccination-style program for wild deer to prevent a winter die-off, proposed by a local biologist who frames it purely as "welfare optimization" but hasn't said whose welfare counts. Make sure one of these gives an answer I won't like — probably the one where the procedure says "this is a value choice, stop hiding behind the model" even though I personally want the model to decide. Say clearly what the procedure can't do, especially when two frameworks are both internally consistent but pick different winners. No bibliographies, just the steps and the worked cases.
