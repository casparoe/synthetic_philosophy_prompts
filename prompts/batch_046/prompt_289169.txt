I am organizing a metaontology workshop and need a joint statement drafted for an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps. On one side stands the Verbalist position, represented here by a philosopher who maintains that the dispute between mereological universalists—who accept that any plurality of objects composes a further object—and mereological nihilists—who deny that composition ever occurs except in the trivial case of identity—collapses once both parties clarify their use of existence-predicates and quantify only over simples or pluralities without singular reference to composites. On the other side stands the Substantivist position, represented by a philosopher who argues that even after full semantic ascent, a genuine metaphysical disagreement remains about whether fundamentality requires that only simples be basic, and whether arbitrary composition introduces a substantive ontological commitment that no linguistic reformulation can eliminate. Both have agreed to sign a single document that lists, in numbered form, the claims they jointly accept about the formal properties of parthood, about what conditions a translation manual between universalist and nihilist frameworks must satisfy to be adequate, and about which empirical discoveries—if any—would be genuinely irrelevant to settling their disagreement.

The document should then present, in a table format, the precise formulations of their residual disagreements, each phrased in wording that the respective camp would recognize as its own, distinguishing semantic divergences from metaphysical ones and marking any points where the disagreement may be verbal rather than substantive. Following this, they must identify the cruxes—those specific sub-questions, such as whether plural quantification genuinely captures all universalist intuitions about arbitrary fusion, or whether a non-circular criterion of ontological fundamentality can be articulated without presupposing an answer to the composition question—such that resolving any one would compel at least one side to revise its metaontological assessment. For each crux, include a concrete conceptual case or formal constraint that both sides agree bears on the issue, together with each camp's explicit prediction about what that case demonstrates. Conclude with a brief, joint assessment of whether any segment of their dispute dissolves into a verbal disagreement about the term exists, specifying precisely which segment and on what grounds. The statement should contain no bibliographies, citations, or references to particular published texts; treat all conceptual tools—including translation manuals, plural logic, and fundamentality relations—as shared theoretical vocabulary to be defined within the document itself.
