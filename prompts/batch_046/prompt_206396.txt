By contrast, single-family subdivisions, large-lot residential builds, or comparable-scale commercial expansions on equivalent parcels are typically permitted 'by right,' requiring only administrative verification of code compliance with no comparable forum for neighbor veto. The practice is concrete and widespread, yet I am struck by its implicit moral geography.

What philosophical commitments about the right to the city, about democratic voice versus property-based exclusion, and about the fair distribution of spatial risk does this two-track system presuppose? Furthermore, is the practice consistent with itself: the same legal framework that treats single-family entitlement as a baseline property right and multi-family presence as a conditional privilege also contains anti-discrimination and fair-housing provisions that seem to reject precisely that hierarchy of belonging?
