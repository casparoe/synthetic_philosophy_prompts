ok so I'm trying to sort out my actual view for a semenar paper and I think I might have stitched together incompatible peices without noticing. Roughly where I stand:

(1) I'm convinced the Cartesian theater is a myth - there's no inner screen watched by a homunculus, just distributed neural processing.
(2) I mostly buy Dennett's multiple drafts idea: consciousness isn't one clean stream but competing narratives that get revised, edited, and sometimes replaced after the fact.
(3) Still, I think there's a genuine binding problem - not a theater, but some mechanism that integrates color, motion, and spatial location into a unified percept.
(4) I also think I have direct, non-inferential access to the temporal order of my conscious states; if I experience flash-then-bang, I don't infer the order, I just see it.
(5) I think predictive processing - the brain as layered prediction-error minimization - explains both the draft-editing and the binding without any central observer.
(6) And I think the self is just whichever draft is currently dominant, though I also want to say there's a stable "me" that survives across draft-replacements over years.

Is this package actually consistent? It feels like (4) might crash into (2): if drafts are edited post-hoc, isn't my sense of "flash then bang" just a reconstructed story rather than direct access? And is (3) smuggling a central integrator back in, or can predictive processing do distributed binding? Then there's (6) - either I'm identical to the current draft or I'm something continuous, but I seem to want both. Which commitment has to go, what's the cheapest repair, and are there tensions I'm imagining that actually dissolve?
