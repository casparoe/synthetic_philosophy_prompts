After a long argument with a friend about lifeboat ethics, I want to formalize our disagreement through an adversarial collaboration framework. Please draft the joint statement that an ex-ante exclusionist—who treats physical carrying capacity as exogenous to admission decisions and holds that strict boundary denial is morally required once a closed system reaches its stochastic shock limit—and an endogenous-capacity inclusionist—who treats institutional carrying capacity as partly endogenous to demographic composition and social design, and who denies that adopting ex-ante exclusion rules extinguishes ex-post obligations to those outside—could both sign.

Write the response in American English and organize it as follows.

1. A numbered section of agreed claims about the formal problem structure: the dynamics of closed-system resource stocks under stochastic shocks; the distinction between physical carrying capacity and institutional carrying capacity; the information constraints faced by decision-makers inside the boundary; the moral status of those inside versus those seeking entry; and what is at stake if the boundary is either enforced or permeable.

2. A disagreements table that states, in wording each camp would accept for its own position, the precise points of divergence: whether carrying capacity is genuinely exogenous with respect to admission; whether admitting individuals with particular skills or social roles can alter resilience parameters in morally relevant ways; whether ex-post rescue obligations survive the institutional adoption of ex-ante exclusion rules; and whether the lifeboat metaphor itself systematically obscures structural interdependence between the system and its environment.

3. The cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I suspect central cruxes include whether empirical cases of closed-system stress show that demographic composition measurably changes institutional resilience, and whether there exists a morally decisive vulnerability threshold that transforms admission from a permissible choice into an impermissible rights-violation against current occupants.

4. For each crux, specify a concrete scenario, empirical pattern, or conceptual argument that both sides agree bears on it, and record each side’s advance prediction of what that scenario or argument would demonstrate.

5. Identify any portion of the dispute that is verbal rather than substantive, explain which framing or definitional choice is generating the appearance of disagreement, and state why resolving that verbal issue would not by itself settle the underlying moral conflict.

The formulations should be neither vacuous nor slanted. Separate genuine disagreement from terminological confusion. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific texts, authors, or obscure theoretical attributions that a model without web access could not reliably reconstruct. Do not ask for quotations from real works.
