I serve on the advisory council of the Center for Applied Contemplative Ethics, which oversees multi-year grants for community programs rooted in Buddhist practice. By Thursday we must circulate recommendations ahead of Friday’s vote on whether to renew funding for the “Sati Prison Outreach” initiative. The program teaches a streamlined, secularized form of vipassanā meditation to incarcerated individuals; internal evaluations indicate it reduces recidivism and self-reported psychological distress more effectively than any other intervention we fund, yet its curriculum deliberately omits the Four Noble Truths and frames mindfulness solely as a stress-reduction technique, severing practice from its soteriological orientation. Several council members, drawing on Abhidharma analyses of cetanā, contend that instructors are cultivating misdirected volition by applying upāya without its liberative aim—generating beneficial karma for participants but corrupting the integrity of the dharma as transmitted. Others maintain that Mahāyāna ethics, particularly the bodhisattva commitment to relieve suffering by any skillful means, demands precisely this adaptation, and that invoking ultimate-truth (paramārtha) considerations to veto a conventionally (saṃvṛti) effective program is itself a philosophical mistake in institutional decision-making. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting: it must recommend one concrete course of action—whether a conditional one-year renewal tied to curriculum revision within ninety days, a phased termination with six months of transition support, or another specific option—complete with an explicit time frame, and it must engage the strongest philosophical argument on the losing side with genuine seriousness rather than treating it as mere traditionalism. In your response, please also state how confident you are in the reasoning you advance, and identify the specific point about which you are least certain—whether that concerns the institutional applicability of cetanā-based evaluation, the legitimacy of two-truths reasoning in grant governance, or another tension in your argument.
