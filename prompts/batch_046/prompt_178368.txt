In contemporary philosophy of language, an analogy is often drawn between Gongsun Long's White Horse argument and modern intension-extension puzzles: just as a white horse is said not to be a horse because the determination of color is included in the concept, it is argued that in Fregean contexts a term's sense-modifiers prevent straightforward substitutivity. Proponents use this parallel to defend a conceptualist reading of properties — that adding a qualitative determination changes the kind rather than merely specifying it. Finally, if the analogy collapses, indicate what a more accurate comparison to Gongsun Long's reconstructed position would require, and whether that comparison still supports any interesting conclusion about how properties relate to kinds.
