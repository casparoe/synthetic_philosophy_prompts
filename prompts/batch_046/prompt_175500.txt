I want a specification—not a survey—of what a genuinely adequate theory of agency in superrationality experiments must be able to deliver.

Your task is to write the full list of desiderata: the phenomena the theory must explain, the distinctions it must preserve, the structural constraints it must respect, and the cases it must get right.

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or mathematical feature behind it—symmetry-breaking without coordination mechanisms, containment of the Luring Lottery’s runaway escalation dynamics, preservation of individual agency despite correlation reasoning, consistency with expected-utility formalism, and empirical plausibility given the actual contestant behavior (astronomical entries, mathematical expressions for maximal numbers). Flag explicitly which requirements are non-negotiable and which reflect camp-specific prejudices (e.g., determinism of choice, rejection of probabilistic reasoning, insistence on causal independence).

Then address the joint-satisfiability question directly: examine whether the demand for symmetric reasoning among identical agents can coexist with the demand for a non-zero, stable expected payoff without an external coordination device. Finally, score the main contenders—causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and a pure Hofstadter-style superrationality framework—briefly but with definite commitments. Do not lay out options neutrally; take a committed position on which framework comes closest to satisfying the list and identify the single desideratum that, in your judgment, does the most damage to the others.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their structural motivations rather than on historical exposition. Write for readers who already know the puzzles.
