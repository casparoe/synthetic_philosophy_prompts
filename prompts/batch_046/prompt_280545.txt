Consider an argument that extends the Gould-Lewontin spandrels critique into cognitive architecture, and that seems to collapse into self-defeat. (1) Many phenotypic traits are spandrels—structural byproducts of an organism's architecture rather than adaptations shaped by selection for their present function. (2) The same architectural logic applies to cognition: any information-processing system built on neural tissue with particular metabolic, connectivity, and developmental constraints will generate certain representational capacities as inevitable structural byproducts, regardless of whether selection calibrated them for those roles. (3) If a cognitive trait is such a spandrel, then selection did not design it for truth-tracking in its domain, so its outputs in that domain carry no special epistemic authority. (4) The human capacity for abstract, domain-general reasoning—including the reasoning used to construct spandrel arguments—is plausibly one of these cognitive spandrels, arising as a byproduct of linguistic and social-cognitive architecture rather than as an adaptation for tracking structural truths about the world. (5) Therefore, if the spandrels critique is correct and applied consistently, the very reasoning that produces it is not calibrated for truth, which undermines our reason to accept the critique. (6) The only escape seems to be an ad hoc exemption claiming that cognitive traits are uniquely immune to being spandrels, but that exemption conflicts with (2). (7) Hence the critique, pushed consistently, appears to refute its own justification.

Where does this chain slip, if anywhere? I would prefer to keep (1) and (2)—the extension from morphology to cognition strikes me as genuinely illuminating—but I am uneasy about (3) and (4). If the flaw is in (3), does that mean spandrels can still be epistemically reliable, and if so, on what basis? If it is in (4), does that commit me to treating scientific reasoning as an adaptation after all? And if the argument is actually valid, which premise is least costly to abandon: denying that cognition has spandrels, denying that spandrel status undermines reliability, or conceding that the critique is pragmatically self-defeating but not false?
