I've been working through the statue-and-clay puzzle alongside recent discussions in artifact ontology. If the statue and the clay share every physical part and intrinsic property at time t, yet differ in modal persistence conditions—squashing destroys the statue but not the clay—then the ground of their distinction must lie outside the material arrangement itself, presumably in relations like being intended as art or being exhibited in a particular context. Does this mean that for artifacts, material constitution is not a hylomorphic relation between matter and an intrinsic form, but instead a kind of status-conferral that generates an object ex nihilo? And if the constitutive relation is fixed entirely by external social intentions, what work is left for a specifically metaphysical account of constitution, as opposed to a purely institutional or conventional one?
