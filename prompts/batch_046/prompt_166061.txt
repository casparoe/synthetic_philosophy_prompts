hey, i'm a science-and-ideas reporter and i was at a seminar where two philosophers got into it over Aquinas on tyrannicide. i need help untangling it for a piece.

person one (call her mara) said aquinas basically greenlights killing a tyrant only if he's an illegitimate usurper — like someone who seized power without right — but not if he's a legit ruler who went bad. she pointed out that in teh Summa he warns against sedition and says private citizens shouldn't take the law into their own hands.

person two (raj) said that's too narrow. he argued aquinas allows resistance — and in extremis, killing — whenever a ruler governs unjustly and destroys the common good, regardless of how he got the crown. raj said mara misses that for aquinas 'tyrant' is defined by behavior, not pedigree, and that the prohibition on private action applies to rebellion, not to a single act of removing an oppressor when public authority is absent or complicit.

mara came back: if behavior is the test, then every unpopular king becomes fair game, and aquinas explicitly says you shouldn't kill a king unless there's no other recourse and the community hasn't acted. she said raj is smuggling modern democratic frustration into a medieval text.

raj replied that aquinas does require exhaustion of legal remedies, but he thinks 'no other recourse' is a practical check, not a categorical ban. he also brought up this weird edge case: what if the tyranny isn't one person but a bureacratic system — say, an administrative apparatus that systematically denies justice without a single tyrant? he asked whether aquinas's framework even applies, since there's no individual to kill.

mara said that's exactly why behavior-alone is dangerous: if tyranny can be structural, then 'tyrannicide' becomes meaningless or revolutionary chaos. she thinks aquinas's usurpation-focus is actually a safeguard against that slide.

they asked me to adjudicate. so: who's right about aquinas's actual view? does the structural-tyranny idea expose a real gap in his framework, or is it a red herring? and what are they both missing — maybe about how aquinas treats the role of public authority in authorizing resistance?

please break it down move by move, rate the arguments, and tell me who's closer to being right overall. don't need a bibliography — just the reasoning.
