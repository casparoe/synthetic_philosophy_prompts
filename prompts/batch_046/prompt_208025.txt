As a journalist covering science and philosophy, I have been working out my own stance on self-locating belief and rational credence for an upcoming feature, and I want an audit of whether these commitments actually hang together before I file the piece. Here is where I stand:

(1) In the Sleeping Beauty problem, I take the "thirder" position: upon awakening with no knowledge of the day or the coin result, my rational credence that the coin landed heads should be one-third.

(2) I regard self-locating belief as metaphysically unremarkable. It is simply de dicto belief with an implicit indexical—"I," "now," "here"—that can be made explicit, not a special mental state that requires a centered-worlds ontology.

(3) I endorse a uniform principle of indifference: whenever my evidence fails to discriminate among possibilities, I should distribute credence equally, whether the differences are uncentered (different worlds) or centered (different times, locations, or personal stages).

(4) I reject the "halfer" defense that appeals to betting ratios, because I think it illegitimately presupposes diachronic personal identity between the Monday and Tuesday awakenings under tails. On my view, those are distinct temporal stages, not one agent placing bets across both.

(5) In branching or fission cases, I deny that personal identity is preserved. Therefore, self-locating belief in such contexts should be analyzed stage-theoretically: each stage carries its own rational-credence profile, and there is no "pre-branching subject" whose beliefs bind the post-branching stages.

(6) I maintain that reference-class reasoning—counting "all possible awakenings" or "all temporal parts" from an external statistical perspective—is epistemically illegitimate in first-person reasoning unless it can be derived from an explicit symmetry principle internal to the agent's evidence.

Are these commitments jointly tenable? Specifically: which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision to restore consistency? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what I would have to weaken or abandon, and identify the single change that would clean up the most inconsistencies without collapsing my core view.
