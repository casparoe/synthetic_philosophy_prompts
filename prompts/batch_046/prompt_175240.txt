I'm preparing a single ninety-minute seminar for philosophy graduate students who have already worked through Moore's critique of ethical naturalism and his basic distinction between intrinsic and instrumental value, but who have not yet encountered his positive account of the highest goods. I want them to grasp Moore's particular claim that both friendship and aesthetic appreciation are not merely instances of intrinsic value, but paradigmatic cases of organic unity — that is, that the value of a beautiful scene experienced in the presence of a friend is not the arithmetic sum of the aesthetic value plus the value of companionship, but a qualitatively different whole whose constituent parts, taken in isolation, do not possess that same value. Do not explain Moore's view to me; instead, design the teaching plan. First, lay out the exact sequence in which the conceptual pieces must be introduced — for instance, whether the principle of organic unity must precede the discussion of friendship and beauty, or whether concrete cases must come first — and justify the dependency: what understanding must be in place before what? Second, specify the single carrying example or scenario that should anchor the entire session — it should involve both aesthetic attention and interpersonal presence — and describe precisely what structural features it needs (e.g., a non-additive interaction between its parts) and what it must lack (e.g., reducibility to individual mental states or pleasures) in order to make Moore's point visible rather than obscured. Third, identify the two or three misconceptions these students are most likely to form or bring — perhaps conflating organic unity with mere intensity of feeling, or treating friendship as instrumentally valuable for the pleasures it produces — and state exactly where in your sequence each misconception should be anticipated and defused. Fourth, provide one targeted diagnostic question I can ask at the close of the session that will reveal whether they genuinely understand that the value of the whole exceeds the value of its parts in a non-quantitative way. Finally, state what aspect of Moore's broader metaphysics or ethics should be explicitly bracketed at this level and acknowledged as beyond the scope of the seminar.
