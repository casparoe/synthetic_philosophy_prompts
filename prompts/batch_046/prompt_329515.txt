I run calibration audits for a distributed forecasting collective that resolves predictions through staged disclosure events—preliminary data, peer-review status, replication—rather than all at once. Analysts submit a reflection profile mapping each stage to their anticipated conditional credences. I need a reusable thirty-minute audit procedure, grounded in van Fraassen's Reflection Principle, that checks whether an analyst's current all-things-considered credences equal the expectation of their own future credences across the disclosure sequence.

Deliver a step-by-step checklist: (1) formalize the current credence vector and the anticipated update paths from the profile; (2) compute or approximate the reflected expectation; (3) set a discrepancy tolerance and rules for approximate reasoning; (4) handle hard cases—e.g., an analyst anticipating that an intermediate disclosure will cause them to discount their prior reasoning model, or claiming a private information channel outside the staged protocol; (5) flag every point where judgment must replace automation and requires senior sign-off; (6) define pass, fail, and indeterminate conditions.

Apply it to three cases: (a) a linear update path with clean alignment; (b) a non-linear revision triggered by an anticipated replication failure, where the procedure returns a failure the analyst will reject—specifically, that they are double-counting a future evidential source and thus violating reflection; and (c) an "epistemic lockbox" scenario, a niche pre-commitment mechanism in our system where analysts seal a reasoning trace to be opened only after stage-two disclosure, leaving ambiguous whether reflection applies to the sealed or unsealed state. For each, show the audit steps, the judgment entry point, and the classification.

End by stating what the procedure cannot do: which irrationalities it misses, what it assumes about self-knowledge, and why it must not be used for ranking or hiring despite its quantitative form. No bibliographies, no citation lists, no web-dependent requests—assume the auditor has only the submitted profile and a calculator.
