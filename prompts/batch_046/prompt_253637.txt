I'm moderating a podcast interview with a philosopher who recently defended what she calls counterfactual coordination as a resolution to the absent-minded driver problem. On her view, the driver at the first intersection should neither maximize expected utility over his epistemic possibilities nor adopt a simple causal-decision plan formed in advance; instead, he should choose as if he were coordinating with his counterfactual self at the second intersection across an epistemic partition, treating the two decision nodes as linked by a modal dependency rather than by causal influence. She claims this dissolves the planning paradox without collapsing into evidential reasoning. I need you to draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond having her restate the position. Include at least one question pressing why counterfactual coordination is not merely evidential decision theory with extra metaphysical window dressing; at least one on how the view handles the modified case in which the driver is warned in advance that his memory will be erased but is not told whether he has already passed the first exit; at least one on whether her framework generalizes to Newcomb-style problems and, if so, whether that generalization is a feature or a bug; and for each of these, include a sharp follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give—specifically, if she invokes diachronic rationality to justify cross-node coordination, ask whether that presupposes a contested account of personal identity over time. End with a question asking what specific behavioral or experimental result could genuinely falsify her framework rather than merely puzzle it. Frame the entire sequence in a direct, committed tone that takes her view seriously and demands definite answers. Finally, take a clear, committed position on which line of questioning is most damaging to her view and explain why, rather than presenting the options evenly.
