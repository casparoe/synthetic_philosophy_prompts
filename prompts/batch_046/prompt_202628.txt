In debates about Gregory Kavka's toxin puzzle—where a rational agent receives a large reward for merely forming the intention to drink a painful but non-lethal poison, with no further obligation to actually drink—defenders often invoke the analogy of Ulysses binding himself to the mast to resist the Sirens. The parallel is meant to show that rationality can require a present self to constrain a future self: Ulysses knows he will be tempted by the song and removes the option in advance, just as the agent should now form the intention to drink, knowing that once the money is in her account she will face overwhelming pressure to avoid it. Audit this analogy in detail. First, lay out the explicit mapping: what corresponds to the Sirens, to the ropes, to the crew who obey the binding, to the temporal gap between the commitment and the temptation, and to the reward Ulysses receives (survival) versus the reward in the toxin case? Next, identify the fractures: Ulysses uses an external physical constraint, whereas the toxin-drinker must rely solely on an internal mental state with no external enforcement mechanism; the reward in Kavka's scenario is obtained before the harmful act, not after surviving it; and the "crew" is absent, leaving only her own future reasoning to contend with. Then ask what the analogy quietly imports—particularly whether it presupposes a view of personal identity that treats the future self as an alien agent to be bound rather than as part of a continuous deliberator—and whether that import is legitimate in the toxin case. Does the comparison actually support Kavka's conclusion that rationality demands forming an intention one expects not to execute, or does the disanalogy show that precommitment reasoning does not apply here? If the Ulysses parallel ultimately collapses under this pressure, propose a different analogy—perhaps a contractual penalty clause or the conditioning of an involuntary reflex—and explain what that replacement reveals about whether rationality can genuinely license an intention disconnected from expected action.
