I've been working through what happens if we treat a normative ethical theory as a consistently formalized system with enough arithmetic to compare outcomes, and Gödel's first incompleteness theorem seems to apply directly: there should be specific moral obligations that are true yet unprovable within the theory—ethical analogues of the Gödel sentence. Given that, should we take these undecidable obligations as supporting moral intuitionism, since they appear to require recognition outside the axioms? Or does Gödel's second theorem undermine the whole project, by showing that any such formalized ethics can never demonstrate its own consistency, leaving us with no rational basis for adopting the axioms in the first place?
