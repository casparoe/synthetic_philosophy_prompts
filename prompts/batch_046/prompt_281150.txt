Write a sestina in which the six end-words for the first stanza, in order, are: I, strange, loop, symbol, refer, self. The speaker should be the symbol "I" as understood in Hofstadter's strange-loop account of selfhood: not an inner homunculus or soul, but an emergent, self-referential pattern that arises when lower-level symbolic processes loop back upon themselves and begin to designate their own collective activity. In each successive stanza, shift the grammatical role of "I"—subject, direct object, indirect object, possessive, object of a preposition, reflexive—so that the sestina's mandatory rotation and repetition of end-words structurally enacts the absence of any fixed underlying referent. Every appearance of "I" should read as the loop naming itself in a new syntactic position, with no entry point outside the pattern. In the final envoi, collapse all six end-words into a single line that reads simultaneously as the symbol performing reference and as the loop being referred to, leaving the circle unbroken. Keep the form exact: six six-line stanzas followed by a three-line envoi, thirty-nine lines total. The result should be recognizable to someone familiar with the strange-loop theory of the self as a performance of that mechanism rather than a mere mention of it.
