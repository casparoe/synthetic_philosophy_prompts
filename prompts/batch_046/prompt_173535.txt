I serve as staff legal counsel to the Riverside Municipal Humanitarian Protection Board, and we have a session this Thursday on a case that has split the board exactly along the conceptual lines I was arguing about in a seminar on displacement ethics last semester. The applicants are a family of four—two parents and two minor children—who fled a region where state-level persecution is absent but where a non-state armed group has systematically destroyed water infrastructure in the context of a multi-year drought that regional climate assessments attribute partly to global carbon patterns. They do not meet the persecution-based criterion of the 1951 Refugee Convention, but they have applied to our municipality's discretionary complementary-protection pilot, which carries no legal mandate to admit them. Some board members want to reject them on the grounds that admitting climate-related displacements collapses the refugee category and imposes unlimited obligations on local jurisdictions; others want to admit them based on a compound-causation view that treats inseparable environmental and conflict drivers as morally equivalent to persecution, paired with a differentiated-responsibility framework that assigns admission obligations to municipalities in proportion to their institutional carbon footprint rather than to nation-states alone.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—admit, deny, or admit conditionally—with an explicit time frame for implementation or review, such as a six-month provisional status with defined reassessment criteria, and it should present reasoning the board could vote on. The memo should treat the decision as an instance of the broader philosophical problem of who counts as a refugee when the persecution boundary fails and who must bear the obligation of admission. You must engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side: specifically, the worry that expanding the category without a principled stopping point undermines the very purpose of refugee protection and unfairly localizes a global burden onto municipalities that lack sovereign authority over borders. The document should have a clear bottom line, specify any conditions or monitoring requirements, and acknowledge the limits of what a single municipal decision can resolve. I do not need citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; the board needs an actionable recommendation, not a literature review.
