In debates about gender, a frequent argumentative move is to analogize gender categories to currency: neither possesses intrinsic physical value, both depend on collective acceptance and coordinated behavior, and both resist unilateral revision — just as you cannot declare a leaf legal tender, an individual cannot opt out of gendered social expectations by fiat. The analogy is typically used to rebut the charge that “socially constructed” means “fake” or “easily changeable,” by showing that some constructed things are robust and real within the systems that sustain them. Audit this analogy systematically.

First, provide an explicit correspondence table: what in gender plays the role of the physical substrate (paper, metal), the issuing authority (state, central bank), the deontic rules governing use, the collective acceptance or trust that underwrites value, and the coordination problem solved? Second, locate the fractures in the mapping — particularly whether gender behaves more like an “interactive kind” (where classification feeds back into the properties and self-conception of the classified, altering the very thing being categorized) rather than an institutional kind with a clear status-function declaration and a centralized authority; and whether intersectional gender positions lack the uniform “denomination” and fungibility that make currency transferable across contexts. Third, separate the disanalogies that actually damage the intended conclusion (that gender is real though not biologically essential) from those that are irrelevant. Fourth, examine what the source domain quietly imports — quantifiability, explicit enforcement mechanisms, fungibility, or the possibility of precise exchange rates — and ask whether gender’s lack of these features undermines the analogy’s defense of robustness, or whether they are inessential ornament. Fifth, assess whether the conclusion that gender is “real but not essential” survives once these disanalogies are on the table, or whether an alternative analogy (say, to language games or to legal status) does the same work with less distortion.
