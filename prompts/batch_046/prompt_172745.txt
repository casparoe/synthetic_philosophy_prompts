Assume Maimonides, as presented in the Guide for the Perplexed, held that animal sacrifice was a divine concession to an entrenched human need for ritualized, physical worship that could not be abolished without social rupture, gradually redirecting that impulse toward the one God. Now suppose he is confronted with two modern developments: precision-fermented tissue biologically identical to sacrificial meat but involving no animal death, and an automated shechita system using computer vision and robotic blades that eliminates human error but removes the human agent from the killing act entirely. Would he treat either as fulfilling the Torah's gradualist aim of weaning humanity away from animal death, or as corruptions of the very weakness the concession was designed to manage? Specifically, does his theory entail that the ideal endpoint is the complete absence of killing in worship, or only its rigorous regulation? And if a reconstructed Temple permitted only automated slaughter of lab-grown tissue, would that represent the culmination or the dissolution of the sacrificial institution he describes?
