ive been trying to wrap my head around the Lobian obstacle for self-trusting agents — the thing where if you can represent your own soundness, Lobs theorem seems to stop you from ever being fully confident in your outputs unless you already started out confident. In the alignment and formal-epistemology crowd the debate feels totally stuck: one side treats it as a hard formal ceiling on rational self-trust, the other says its just an artifact of picking an overly idealized modal logic with full belief closure. But heres whats weird — nobody actually switches frameworks when they hear the rebuttal, and the same three moves get replayed every year. My working guess is that its not really about modal logic at all, but that the sides are answering different questions hidden under one phrase: one camp means "trusting yourself" as a doxastic fixed-point, the other means it as a decision-theoretic commitment to stick with your current policy. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a hidden formal disagreement about proof-theoretic strength or bounded rationality doing the real work? And if it is mostly conceptual, could anything actually move it — say, a clear distinction between syntactic self-reference and semantic self-modeling, or a result showing that agents with limited computation sidestep the obstacle entirely? Or is the framing itself ill-posed because "self-trust" is being imported from psychology and doesnt correspond to any single formal object? I want the diagnosis of the stall, not a survey of the interpretations, and an honest take on whether progress should even be expected.
