I've been having a right row with my flatmate about whether modern democracies are basically Rome on repeat — populists, the senate turning into a talking shop, armies loyal to generals instead of the res publica. It got me thinking that everyone just name-checks Syme or Beard and thinks they've won, but nobody actually sorts out what kind of analogy they're running. So I'm writing a little taxonomy for my blog (doing this on my phone between shifts, so forgive the mess) that starts not with historian names but with the structural claims people smuggle in.

Could you build me a decision tree? Start with: 'You accept that the fall of the Roman Republic offers at least one genuinely analagous mechanism to institutional stress in modern liberal democracies.' First branch: is the crisis mainly procedural hollowing — formal rules stay intact but are circumvented by personal networks — or distributive overload, where the state promises security and welfare beyond what its tax base can deliver? Second branch: does the military-civilian boundary matter, i.e., was the Republic's death partly about armies pledging to commanders rather than the res publica, and does that map onto modern private militias or security firms? Third branch: is normative capture — elites stopping to believe in republican virtue and just playing naked power games — a root cause or only a symptom of the other two?

Each leaf should carry a position name if one exists ('institutionalist', 'materialist', 'normative-decline', 'skeptical analogist', etc.) or 'unnamed' plus a sentence on its awkward commitment. I want six to eight leaves total. Crucially, flag any pair your tree can't really seperate — I have a hunch that 'pure institutionalist' and 'procedural-hollowing-only' might collapse, so don't paper over it. Keep it casual, don't ask me to read anything — I haven't got library access right now and I won't be able to check citations anyway. Just map the logical space first, then stick the actual views into it, noting any empty slots and whether they're incoherent or just unexplored.
