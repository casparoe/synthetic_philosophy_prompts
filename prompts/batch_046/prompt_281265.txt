I serve as the senior methodologist for a government ethics review panel that evaluates proposed policies or funding commitments justified primarily by appeals to outcomes of astronomical magnitude coupled with probabilities below any conventional statistical threshold. I need a reusable decision procedure my reviewers can apply consistently without requiring advanced formal training, grounded in the decision-theoretic responses to Pascal-style reasoning—specifically, bounded-utility rejection of unbounded expected-value maximization, evidential proportionality constraints, and dominance screening—while also incorporating a robustness gate drawn from robust decision theory: any tail-risk argument must survive a maximin-regret analysis across the panel’s defensible prior range (from 10⁻⁶ to 0.5) and must not be stochastically dominated by a safer, conventional alternative across that same range. Write the procedure as an ordered sequence of gates, each with an explicit pass/fail test, a specification of what counts as a sufficient answer, the precise point at which the procedure issues a hard stop with a “no,” and clear markers for where the gate reduces to a value judgment rather than an empirical determination. Include a concise set of applied test criteria reviewers can use at each gate. Then run the procedure on three proposals: (1) a $2 billion global engineering program to prevent a hypothesized strangelet chain reaction assigned a probability near 10⁻¹²; (2) a mandatory universal vaccination justified not by current disease burden but by a claimed 0.001% risk of inadvertently selecting for a pandemic-resistant pathogen; and (3) a legislative proposal to defund all conventional oncology research and redirect funds to an unproven quantum-biological intervention that proponents claim carries a 10⁻⁹ chance of eliminating cancer entirely. Ensure at least one worked case produces a verdict the proposer will strongly reject, flag every step that is a judgment call disguised as procedural form, and state explicitly what kinds of proposals lie outside the procedure’s competence. After the procedural text and case applications, provide a structured summary in JSON with exactly these fields: “procedure_name” (string), “gate_sequence” (array of objects, each with “gate_number” (integer), “test” (string), “pass_criterion” (string), “fail_action” (string)), “cases” (array of objects, each with “case_name” (string), “final_gate_reached” (integer), “verdict” (string), “value_judgment_triggered” (boolean)), and “limitations” (array of strings).
