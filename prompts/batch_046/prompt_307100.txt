Write a short story that dramatizes the problem of superrational cooperation between two agents who share only a partial, noisy isomorphism of reasoning architecture rather than being perfect copies or twins. Set the scene in a one-shot, irreversible decision context—such as two isolated colony ships that must independently commit to either pooling their remaining fuel reserves for mutual survival or conserving them for unilateral escape—where direct communication is impossible and each agent knows that the other is running a structurally similar but not identical deliberative process. Rather than presenting a clear resolution, let the narrative hinge on the fragility of correlation when self-modeling is imperfect: one agent may overestimate the symmetry of their reasoning, the other may misidentify which features are shared, and the outcome should depend on how they navigate epistemic opacity rather than on strategic repetition. Keep the philosophical structure visible without naming it as "superrationality" or "prisoner's dilemma," and avoid futuristic tropes that reduce the conflict to simple AI-versus-human rivalry.
