I am taking an advanced seminar in philosophy of science that has spent several weeks on Daniel Dennett’s distinction between cranes and skyhooks, together with his critique of greedy reductionism—the tendency to explain complex phenomena by stripping away organizational layers rather than constructing them from simpler components. Most of our discussion has stayed within evolutionary biology and cognitive neuroscience, but I want to extend the framework into a domain we have barely touched: the explanation of institutional authority and normative bindingness in social ontology. In particular, I am struck by the possibility that attempts to reduce legal or moral authority entirely to evolutionary psychology, game-theoretic coordination, and individual belief-states may appear to employ only cranes, yet might secretly depend on an unexplained normative residue that functions precisely like a skyhook.

Write an essay that takes up this specific controversy. Weigh carefully the argument that greedy reductionism can fully account for institutional and normative phenomena through layered evolutionary and psychological cranes, against the counter-argument that any such reduction leaves a “normative gap”—a brute fact of authority or obligation that cannot be built from below and therefore operates as a hidden skyhook. You should reach and defend a definite verdict: either that greedy reductionism succeeds in this domain without smuggling in skyhook dependence, or that it inevitably collapses into it. Do not end with a balanced survey of options; commit to one side and justify it with explicit reasons. Additionally, since this applies Dennett’s metaphors well beyond their usual context, include a brief note identifying where your argument takes liberties with the standard interpretation of cranes and skyhooks, or where it relies on contested assumptions in social ontology.
