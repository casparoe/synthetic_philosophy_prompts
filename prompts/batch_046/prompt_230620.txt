Quick philosophy question—been noticing a pattern in arguments about knowability. Someone starts with this broad idea: if a statement is true, then its not permanently hidden—some mind, maybe not ours, could in principle come to know it. Then they show that if you apply this to every truth, you get the weird result that every true thing is already actually known, even stuff nobody knows. So instead of dropping the idea, they patch it: except truths that depend on being unknown, except indexical claims about ignorance, except modal claims about what's possible in worlds where the claim itself goes unknown. Each patch sounds okay alone, but together they hollow out the principle. I see this especially when people extend it to modal cases—like nearby-world knowability—where you need extra rules for self-reference. Is there an established name for this specific restriction-creep on an epistemic principle? How's it different from an ordinary ad hoc auxiliary hypothesis? If there's no term, what'd you call it—maybe "epistemic gerrymandering"? Keep it to one paragraph, max 160 words.
