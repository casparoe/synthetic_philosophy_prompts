I'm trying to pin down a move I keep seeing in debates about markets that shouldn't exist -- vote buying, kidney sales, price gouging on essential meds. Someone argues that putting an explicit price on X corrupts its value or changes its social meaning. Then the other side points out X allready carries hidden costs: campaigns need funding, organs have scarcity value, drugs have R&D budgets. The first speaker then retreats to saying the corruption only bites with open transactional pricing, not these implicit economic realities, but they dont explain why the harm needs a literal cash register. Is there an established name for this specific retreat -- not the broad corruption objection, but the idea that commodification damages value only when its explicit? If there isnt one, how should I distinguish it from plain special pleading and from the usual Sandel-type argument?
