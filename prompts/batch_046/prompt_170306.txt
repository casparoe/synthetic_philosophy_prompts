I'm giving a short talk next week to our philosophy of action / cognitive sci joint seminar, and I need to survive the q&a. My thesis is that Buridan's ass isn't really a paradox about rationality—it's a category mistake about what "equal preference" even means in a system with continuous internal dynamics. I argue that in both neural and computational architectures, tie-breaking isn't arbitrary or libertarian; its deterministically resolved by sub-threshold fluctuations that we just don't have introspective access to. So the ass doesn't starve, but not because it "chooses"—rather, the idea of a perfectly balanced preference state is an idealization that never actually obtains in physical systems. I want to defend this without sounding like I'm smuggling determinism in through the back door, and without dismissing the phenomenology of feeling genuinely stuck.

The audience is a weird mix: two decision theorists who think in Savage-style axioms and will want to know why my view doesn't collapse into a hidden-variable version of causal decision theory; three phenomenologists who are going to hate anything that sounds like its reducing the lived experience of deliberation to basal ganglia noise; and one cognitive scientist who actually models action selection in the basal ganglia and will probably ask whether my "micro-fluctuation" story is empirically plausible or just philosophy cosplay. They care a lot about whether "arbitrary" is doing real philosophical work or just covering for ignorance. Can you draft my prep sheet? I want the eight or so objections I'll actually get, ordered by how damaging they'd be, each phrased the way that camp would put it, with a two-minute reply and a honest note on where the reply is weakest. Tell me what I should concede upfront—probably that I'm not solving consciousness or whatever—and flag the one question I'm praying nobody asks, which is probably whether my view makes moral responsibility impossible if every "choice" is just noise resolving a tie. Also flag which objections come from the formalists vs the phenomenologists vs the scientist, since I need to know who I'm talking to when I answer.
