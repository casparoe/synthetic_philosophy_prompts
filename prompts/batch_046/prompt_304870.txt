hey so my study partner came up with this argument about nuclear brinkmanship he calls the "credibility leakage" problem and now i cant sleep. he says if a state's deterrent threat is only believable because they've built some kind of automatic or delegated response — like a dead hand mechansim — then that irrationality cant stay contained. here's how he lays it out:

(1) extended deterrence only works if the attacker believes state A might actually nuke them back on behalf of ally B.
(2) a fully rational A knows nuking back risks its own annihilation, so without some commitment device it wouldn't do it.
(3) so A builds a commitment device — delegation, automated protocols, hair-trigger posture — that makes retaliation more likely even if A's leaders later want to back down.
(4) but now A's own population and leaders see that the device is genuinely irrational: it could destroy them over a mistake.
(5) so they have reason to dismantle it, or at least to signal that they have dismantled it.
(6) therefore A must either actually keep the irrational device (and be genuinely unpredictable) or pretend to keep it while secretly being rational.
(7) if the attacker knows this, they face a dilemma: either A is irrational and therefore unpredictable in dangerous ways that make alliance with B unstable, or A is rational and faking, which means the threat isn't credible.
(8) either way, extended deterrence collapses — with irrational allies you cant coordinate, with rational ones you cant be believed.
(9) but NATO-style alliances clearly exist and seem to stabilize regions.
(10) so either "rational state" is the wrong category, or credibility doesn't actually depend on these mechanisms.

i think the slip is somewhere between (6) and (8), maybe an equivocation on what "irrational" means? but if i drop (6) then i'm saying states can just be transparent about there commitment devices, which feels wrong too. which step should i actually reject, and whats the price of rejecting it versus giving up on (2) or (3)? i really want to keep the idea that commitment devices are real and necessary.
