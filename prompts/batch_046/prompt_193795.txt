The key difference is that in (b) the failure is internal to the predicate: the properties contradict each other, so the uniqueness clause is not merely unsatisfied but logically incoherent. To avoid this, Russell must either exclude contradictory properties from logical space or allow that some descriptions refer to impossible objects. Regarding denotation, Russell clearly holds that every well-formed definite description denotes an object, because otherwise the quantifier structure could not be evaluated; that is precisely why he introduces the primary and secondary occurrence distinction, to protect reference when descriptions fall within the scope of negation.
