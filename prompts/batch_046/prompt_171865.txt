Verdict: there is no phenomenal visual consciousness. The patient has an illusion of visual phenomenology produced by non-conscious inference and verbal confabulation.

Case 2: A blindsight subject can navigate around obstacles and discriminate orientations within a scotoma, yet sincerely reports seeing nothing and describes themselves as merely guessing. Verdict: phenomenal consciousness is genuinely absent. This is not an illusion of consciousness but a case of intact non-conscious processing paired with an accurate absence-report.

Case 3: During REM sleep, a sleeper awakens and reports a bright orange sphere pulsating with warmth. Verdict: this is genuinely phenomenal consciousness — endogenous dream phenomenology that does not depend on peripheral sensory pathways or corresponding cortical feature-maps.

Case 4: A long-term meditator in deep open-monitoring reports no color, no shape, no self, yet insists there remains an undeniable bare luminosity — a minimal phenomenal presence that they describe as being like something, despite lacking any specific qualitative content. High-density EEG shows globally synchronized low-amplitude alpha and no localized feature-binding signatures. Verdict: I am genuinely split. One part of me says this bare awareness is real phenomenal consciousness stripped to its minimal form; the other part says that describing it as being like something is a misapplication, and this is actually an illusion of phenomenal presence generated by sustained attentional monitoring in the absence of qualitative content.

Case 5: Under subliminal presentation of a fearful face, below conscious detection threshold, a subject reports feeling vaguely uneasy but insists they did not see anything at all. Verdict: phenomenal consciousness of the face is genuinely absent, not hidden or illusory; the affective response is real but carries no visual phenomenology.

Case 6: In a split-brain preparation, the right hemisphere directs the left hand to perform an action; the left hemisphere immediately offers a fluent narrative explaining why it did so. The left hemisphere reports feeling fully conscious of having deliberated and chosen. Verdict: the specific phenomenal content of having consciously willed the action is illusory — a post-hoc fabrication. There may be phenomenal consciousness of the narrative itself, but the agency-phenomenology is not veridical.
