Please argue that in anthropic reasoning the self-indication assumption ought to be adopted in place of the self-sampling assumption, even conceding the intuitive pull of the presumptuous philosopher objection. Do not simply rehearse the standard response that finding oneself in a large population should not be surprising; instead, formulate and defend an independent constraint—reference-class stability—holding that a valid anthropic framework must employ a reference class whose membership conditions are invariant across the cosmological hypotheses under comparison. Demonstrate how this constraint blocks the presumptuous philosopher's move from an expansive universe to inflated prior confidence, and show that self-sampling fails the stability test in a manner that self-indication avoids.
