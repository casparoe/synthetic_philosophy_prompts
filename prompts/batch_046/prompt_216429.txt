I take the view that Goodman’s new riddle of induction dissolves once we stop treating predicates as freely constructible syntactic strings and instead treat them as nodes in a conceptual economy—predicates earn projectibility not through metaphysical naturalness but through their embeddedness in measurement practices, causal-explanatory roles, and inter-predictive dependencies. On this account, green and grue do not present competing hypotheses about emeralds; grue is a gerrymandered composite that lacks autonomous inferential standing, so the asymmetry in our inductive practice reflects the structure of our reasoning tools rather than a hidden constraint on legitimate properties. Play this straight: what would have to be true for this dissolution to fail? Which of its commitments—about the autonomy of syntactic construction, the sufficiency of pragmatic entrenchment, the irrelevance of metaphysical natural kinds—are actually load-bearing, and what specific observation, formal result, or conceptual pressure could undermine each? I want an honest map of the position’s exposure: is there a live route to showing that grue can be given a stable, independent inferential role without collapsing into green? Could a formal learning-theoretic result demonstrate that any system capable of projecting green must also, on pain of inconsistency, be capable of projecting grue? And does the view risk insulating itself—by defining away any apparently projectible grue-like predicate as conceptually parasitic—such that no conceivable evidence could count against it, and if so, is that insulation a virtue or a defect for a philosophical account of induction?
