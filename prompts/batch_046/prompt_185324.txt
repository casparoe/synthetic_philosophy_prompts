I was chatting with some mates in our philosophy of mind reading group about Parfit's teletransporter, and one of them kept insisting it's basically just like ripping a vinyl master to digital, melting the original lacquer, then pressing a new disc on Mars. The copy plays the same music — same psychological patterns — but the original substrate is gone. He used this to argue that personal survival is nothing more than data-continuity, so worrying about being destroyed is like fretting over the melted lacquer when you've still got the record.

But I'm not convinced the analogy carries the weight he puts on it. Can you audit it properly: lay out the correspondence table — scanner, melting bath, digital file, pressing plant, the same-music claim — then show me exactly where the mapping fractures? I'm particularly keen on whether it quietly imports assumptions about causation and physical continuity that Parfit's case actually lacks, and whether the relaxed conclusion — that survival is just pattern-transfer and therefore not worth worrying about — survives once the disanalogies are exposed. Also, is there a better analogy that doesn't hide the problem?

Apologies for the ramble — it's occured to me that the vinyl metaphor might be smuggling in more than it should.
