I came across the following summary in an unnamed graduate seminar handout discussing Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition. It states: "Bradley demonstrated that if a proposition is genuinely composed of real constituents—such as a particular and a universal—there must exist a relation of instantiation that binds them into a single unit. If this binding relation is internal to the complex, then a further relation is required to connect it to the other members, generating an infinite regress. If it is external, the constituents remain an unconnected aggregate rather than a unified proposition. Contemporary defenders of structured propositions avoid this result only by treating the combinatory tie as a primitive, non-relational connection; yet this is essentially Bradley's own doctrine of internal relations recast in modern terminology, and it collapses because it provides no account of how the same constituents can be arranged differently to yield distinct propositions. Consequently, Bradley's regress remains unsolved, and any adequate theory of propositional unity must either accept an infinite hierarchy of binding relations or reject the claim that propositions have real structural constituents."

Is this characterization fair? Specifically: does Bradley's original argument presuppose that relations are external in the manner the summary suggests, or does it apply regardless? Are modern structured-proposition theorists correctly described as treating combination as primitive, and is that position genuinely equivalent to Bradley's internal-relation view? Furthermore, does the objection that primitive combination cannot distinguish different orderings of the same constituents actually succeed, or does it mistakenly treat the ordering relation as part of the combinatory mechanism itself? Please distinguish what in the summary is accurate, what is a defensible simplification, and what misrepresents either Bradley's position or the contemporary structured-proposition view, and explain whether the conclusion—that the regress forces a choice between infinite hierarchy and constituent denial—follows from the corrected premises.
