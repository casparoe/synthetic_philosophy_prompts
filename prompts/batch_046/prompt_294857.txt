Here is an argument I have been turning over and cannot locate the fault line in. It seems to show that any rejection of epistemic bootstrapping forces us into a skeptical paralysis that applies not just to perception but to every mental faculty, which strikes me as absurd, yet the premises feel individually sound.

(1) If knowing p via method M can justify believing that M is reliable in this instance, without any prior independent check on M, then that justification is circular—call this bootstrapping.

(2) In the classic case, I see my car is red, then use that very visual belief to support the claim that my vision is working well today.

(3) If bootstrapping is illegitimate, then no single successful use of M can ever contribute to a justified belief about M's reliability, because every such contribution relies on M itself.

(4) Now extend this to diachronic calibration loops: over months I compare my perceptual reports with those of others, with instruments, and with my own past records, building a cumulative case for reliability.

(5) Every comparison in that loop still employs either perception, memory, or testimony—each of which is itself a method whose reliability is partly at issue—so the loop never escapes dependence on the methods it calibrates.

(6) Therefore, if bootstrapping is banned, diachronic calibration loops cannot yield justified beliefs about the reliability of any basic faculty.

(7) But introspection and memory face an even stricter version of the same problem: there is no genuinely independent faculty to check them, so their reliability can never be established except through bootstrapping.

(8) Hence rejecting bootstrapping entails that we lack justified beliefs not only about our perceptual reliability but about the reliability of introspection and memory.

(9) Without justified beliefs about the reliability of these faculties, we have no warrant for any empirical or psychological belief that depends on them.

(10) So rejecting bootstrapping collapses into global skepticism about mental content.

(11) Global skepticism is self-defeating—asserting it requires the very mental faculties it denies—so (10) must be false.

(12) Therefore bootstrapping is legitimate, and the standard objections (e.g., Vogel's, Cohen's) fail.

I feel the slip is somewhere between (3) and (6), perhaps a hidden shift from "this instance" to "all instances over time," but I cannot name it precisely. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I am especially reluctant to give up (7) or (11), but I want to know the price of each option.
