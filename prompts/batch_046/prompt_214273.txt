I am drawn to a stringent interpretation of Ubuntu-inspired relational personhood: that being a person is not an intrinsic, monadic property but is constituted by actual, reciprocal recognition within a living community. On this view, moral standing is not possessed in virtue of internal capacities such as rationality, sentience, or self-awareness, but is conferred through sustained relational practices—what might be called being-seen-as-person. I have been trying to sharpen this into a specific claim: that there exists a recognition threshold such that an entity lacking any actual reciprocal relational embedding—say, a solitary survivor in a permanently depopulated region, or a conscious substrate maintained in isolation with no interlocutors—does not merely suffer diminished flourishing but lacks full personhood and the direct moral claims that come with it. This seems to avoid both biological essentialism and overly capacious individualism, yet I want to know what should genuinely count against it. I am not asking for a bibliography or a list of authors; I want the arguments, observations, intuitions, or conceptual distinctions that should move me. Does the view collapse into a form of social constructivism that makes personhood arbitrary or vulnerable to majority whim? Can it accommodate the strong intuition that we wrong a hermit if we kill her, rather than merely depriving her of community? What results from moral psychology on recognition, or analogies from the philosophy of language on indexicality and shared reference, would undermine the claim that reciprocity is constitutive rather than merely instrumental to personhood? Furthermore, has the position been qualified into unfalsifiability—for instance, by redefining community so broadly that it includes ancestors, future generations, or ecological systems, so that no case of isolation ever genuinely threatens it? If so, is that insulation a virtue or a vice for a view that presents itself as offering concrete normative guidance? Please distinguish evidence that would be decisive from evidence that would merely be awkward, say what defenders of this stringent relational view would themselves concede as counting against it, and assess whether the view risks anything at all or has been protected by qualifications that render it immune to counterexample.
