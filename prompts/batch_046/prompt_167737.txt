Consider the classic problem of Buridan's ass—choice between identical options with no preference—together with its modern dissolution through stochastic tie-breaking, framing effects, and noise in neural decision processes. Several philosophical directions remain open: whether introducing a randomizing mechanism saves rational agency or reduces choice to arbitrariness; whether the problem re-emerges at a higher order when options are incommensurable rather than merely identical; whether the paradox genuinely threatens the principle of sufficient reason or only exposes a rationalist overidealization; and whether it carries real weight for contemporary AI alignment, where agents can face symmetrically rewarded states. If you had to direct one dissertation-length project or a small grant in this space, which of these lines deserves the effort, and which are overrated?
