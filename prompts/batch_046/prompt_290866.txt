Urban planning boards usually hold loud public hearings for zoning changes but treat 5G and fiber rollouts as pure engineering with no democratic input needed. What philosophy of tech does that split actually presuppose? Does it assume artifacts are politically neutral until humans misuse them, or that network layout has no distributive effects at all? And if you take seriously the view that some infrastructures embed specific power relations — think of how an algorithim that picks tower locations for latency optimization produces a map with no human-readable justification — how would approval processes have to change? Reconstruct the implicit theory behind the current practice, say whether it's strictly required or just one compatabile option among several, and flag where the practice is philosphically underdetermined, especially now that "infrastructure as code" removes the human planner from the loop entirely.
