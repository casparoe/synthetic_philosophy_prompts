I am moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who defends what she calls “institutional derivationism” about the is-ought gap. On her view, Hume’s law marks not a logical impossibility but a disciplinary boundary that dissolves within structured deliberative frameworks: once descriptive premises about collective authorization procedures are embedded in participatory institutions, normative conclusions follow without committing Moore’s naturalistic fallacy, because the institution’s descriptive practices already constitute the relevant normative framework. She further argues that evolutionary debunking of moral cognition does not undermine realism but demonstrates that normative bootstrapping is functionally inevitable in cooperative species. I want six to eight interview questions that go well beyond restating the view. Include: (1) a question pressing whether her authorization procedures smuggle normative premises into the descriptive base, with a follow-up anticipating her reply that constitution is not derivation; (2) a question on how she distinguishes her position from standard contractarian or constructivist accounts that also bridge fact and norm via procedure; (3) a question on whether evolutionary inevitability provides normative authority or merely causal explanation, with a follow-up keyed to her likely appeal to functional success; (4) a question on what her view says about the open-question argument when applied to institutional norms—specifically, whether asking “is this authorized practice good?” retains independent critical force. Keep the questions focused on the mechanics of the argument, avoid biographical or generic prompts, and design each follow-up to make her standard deflections harder to deploy.
