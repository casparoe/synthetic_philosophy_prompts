I am an undergraduate philosophy student in my final year of a philosophy of language seminar, and I am preparing an adversarial collaboration exercise for my capstone project. I need you to draft the joint statement that two opposing camps could both sign after a good-faith collaboration focused specifically on semantic externalism and a non-standard Twin Earth variant.

The two camps are:
(1) The Broad Externalist camp, which holds that mental content and linguistic reference are partly fixed by causal-historical relations to the environment and by the social-linguistic division of labor, such that two individuals with identical intrinsic psychological states can differ in what they mean or think.
(2) The Narrow Internalist camp, which holds that mental content is fully determined by intrinsic, non-relational properties of the individual; environmental relations may determine reference in some derivative sense, but they do not constitute the mental states themselves.

Rather than using the classic water/Twin-H2O scenario, the collaboration should center on a constructed case—call it Twin Earth Gamma—in which the substance in the lakes is chemically H2O (identical to Earth water), but the term 'water' was introduced into the linguistic community through a sustained, never-corrected misclassification by founding scientists, after which usage remained stable and functionally successful despite the error in the original reference-fixing chain.

Please structure the document as follows:

First, a numbered section of agreed claims. These should include shared commitments about what Twin Earth Gamma does and does not establish; about the distinction between reference-fixing mechanisms and belief-formation; about the scope and limits of first-person authority over mental content; and about the conditions under which an environmental factor is genuinely constitutive rather than merely causally influential.

Second, a disagreements table with columns for: the crux (the precise sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side); the Broad Externalist formulation of its position on that crux (phrased so that members of that camp would accept the wording); and the Narrow Internalist formulation of its position (phrased analogously).

For each crux, also specify: the case or argument that both sides agree bears on it (derived from Twin Earth Gamma or a direct variant); and, for each camp, the explicit prediction written down in advance about how resolution of that crux should come out.

The formulations should be neither vacuous nor slanted, and you should separate disagreements that are genuinely substantive from those that are merely verbal. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or appeals to specific texts. Frame the two positions in general philosophical terms, without depending on precise historical attributions to particular authors.
