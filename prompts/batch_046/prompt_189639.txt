I'm writing from an undergraduate philosophy of well-being seminar, and I'm trying to move past the standard classroom replay of Nozick's experience machine. For a discussion assignment, I want to stage a dialogue between two interlocutors who are equally well-versed but defend opposing positions: a revised hedonist who concedes that raw pleasure is insufficient for well-being and instead defends a condition of causal-phenomenal integration—roughly, the idea that the qualitative feel of one's mental states must be non-accidentally tied to the causal structure of one's actions in the world—and an objective-list theorist who maintains that achievement and knowledge are irreducible goods and suspects that this integration condition is merely achievement described in phenomenological language. The hedonist should argue that integration is genuinely distinct from achievement because one can have integration in purely experiential domains, such as artistic creation within a fully simulated environment, where no objective state of affairs is secured, and should explain why its loss specifically undermines hedonistic welfare rather than some other kind of value. The objective-list theorist should respond by showing that any non-accidental causal link between experience and world-structure just is the achievement of directing one's mental life toward real states of affairs, and should press the hedonist on whether integration can be satisfied in cases of systematic deception that still preserve causal coherence. Both sides should address the other’s actual points rather than talking past each other, and the exchange should remain accessible to a student familiar with Nozick’s machine and the broad contours of hedonism and objective-list theories, without invoking specific page numbers, bibliographies, or obscure secondary literature.
