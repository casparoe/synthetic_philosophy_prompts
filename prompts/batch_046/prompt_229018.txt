Write a villanelle spoken from the perspective of the severed corpus callosum in a patient following complete commissurotomy. The poem must employ two refrains: one articulating the "unitary consciousness" interpretation of split-brain cases (that a single subject persists, now with disunified but not duplicated awareness), and one articulating the "dual consciousness" interpretation (that two distinct subjects inhabit the same body, each associated with one hemisphere). Arrange the refrains so they alternate through the tercets and converge in the final couplet, letting the villanelle's unavoidable repetition mirror the clinical repetition of dissociation experiments without resolving whether that repetition reveals one divided self or two. The imagery should be accurate enough that a philosopher of mind familiar with Sperry and Gazzaniga's behavioral findings and the subsequent debate over hemispheric independence recognizes both positions as genuinely represented rather than named in passing; do not caricature either view. Keep the tension open in the closing lines, allowing the couplet to hold both refrains in unresolved adjacency.
