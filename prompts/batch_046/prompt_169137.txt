In debates about AI and neuroscience I keep encountering the slogan “the brain is just a computer” used as a conversation-stopper—sometimes to defend substrate independence, sometimes to claim that cognition is literally symbol manipulation, sometimes to suggest that neuroscience and computer science share one explanatory vocabulary, and sometimes to dismiss worries that large language models lack subjective experience. I need a precise disambiguation rather than a single interpretation. Could you lay out the distinct theses that sentence might actually express—separating, for instance, claims about Turing-computability, digital implementation, algorithmic explanation at Marr’s levels, functional equivalence across substrates, the identity of mental and computational properties, and the claim that all mental causation is syntactic? For each reading, indicate whether it is a near-platitude, a substantive empirical hypothesis, or a category mistake; say which ones remain compatible with the brain being analog, continuous, stochastic, or thermodynamically open; and specify which readings, if any, actually license the inference that a transformer running on silicon is “doing the same thing” as a human mind, versus which ones leave that question untouched. I am not looking for a bibliography or author citations—just a menu of readings with verdicts, so that I can tell when someone invoking the slogan has advanced a real claim and when they have merely changed the subject.
