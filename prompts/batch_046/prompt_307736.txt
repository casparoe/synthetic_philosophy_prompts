The question was: Avicenna imagines a person suspended in empty air, blindfolded, with limbs spread so that no part of the body touches another, and deprived of all sensory experience of the external world. He claims that even in this condition the person would still be aware that he exists. Explain what this argument is intended to establish about the soul and its relation to the body, and assess whether it successfully demonstrates that the soul is an immaterial substance.

The answer I received:
The flying-man scenario is meant to show that knowing you exist does not depend on your body or your senses. Even with no sight, no touch, and no feeling at all, the suspended person still knows that he is, which means the soul can be aware of itself directly, without using bodily organs. This is essentially the same argument that Descartes gives in his Meditations—both use the fact that thinking reveals existence to prove that there is a non-physical thinking substance. Once Avicenna has shown that the soul does not depend on the body for its basic awareness, he immediately proves that the soul is also immortal: since the soul is not composed of physical parts, nothing in it can break down or decay, so death cannot affect it. Therefore the argument succeeds in establishing both that the soul is immaterial and that it survives the death of the body.
