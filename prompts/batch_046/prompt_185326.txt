In the literature on Gödel's cosmological work and his philosophy of time, there's a debate that seems frozen. One side reads Gödel's rotating-universe metric—specifically its closed timelike curves—as the backbone of a serious metaphysical argument: if general relativity permits a universe in which an observer can travel into her own past, then there can be no objective lapse of time, and temporal becoming must be ideal in something like Kant's sense. The other side treats the same metric as a mathematical curiosity that demonstrates the formal permissiveness of Einstein's equations but carries no philosophical weight, since the solution requires a globally rotating matter distribution that doesn't match our observed cosmos, and since the existence of a geometric path doesn't entail its physical traversability. Both camps agree on the field equations, on the presence of CTCs, on Gödel's documented Kantian and Leibnizian sympathies, and even on his claim that travel into one's past undermines objective time. Yet for decades neither has convinced the other, and the exchange often feels like talking past one another.

My working hypothesis is that this isn't a hidden empirical disagreement—no one expects to observe a Gödel universe—nor is it purely verbal. I think the stall comes from a deeper divergence about what kind of modal claim a cosmological solution licenses: does the bare existence of a globally consistent solution with CTCs show that time is not fundamentally directed, or does it only show that GR is underdetermined with respect to temporal structure unless constrained by boundary conditions and dynamical accessibility? But I'm not sure. Is the disagreement actually about standards of inference from theoretical physics to metaphysics? About whether an objective present is a coherent notion that needs saving? Or are the two sides answering different questions—one about the ontology of time in any physically possible world, the other about the ontology of time in worlds that are dynamically accessible from ours?
