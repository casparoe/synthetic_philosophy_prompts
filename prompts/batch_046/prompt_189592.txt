Philosophers working with Nozick's tracking conditions have been stalled for decades on whether the theory violates epistemic closure. One camp holds that if S tracks p via a belief-forming method M, then S automatically tracks any q entailed by p as long as M is preserved, because the counterfactual sensitivity that secures p's tracking carries over with modal inertia to its logical consequences. The other camp insists that tracking is proposition-specific: the conditions that make "this is a zebra" sensitive can fail for "this is not a painted mule" even when the first entails the second, and that re-indexing tracking to methods either collapses tracking into generic reliability or smuggles in an unjustified relevance filter deciding which entailments inherit tracking. My working hypothesis is that this is largely a verbal disagreement about whether "tracking" names a relation between agents and propositions, agents and methods, or agent-world pairs across modal space, with each side answering a different question under one heading. Is the stall instead driven by a hidden formal issue—whether counterfactual logic permits inheritance of sensitivity across entailment—or by bedrock intuitions about whether knowledge of entailments requires independent tracking? Give me a diagnosis supported by what each camp actually claims, say whether the disagreement is substantive, verbal, or about different questions, and tell me what—if anything, such as a formal result on counterfactual inheritance, a conceptual distinction isolating modal inertia from method-preservation, or a reframing—would actually count as progress.
