For a metaphysics reading group I have been asked to write down my current position on universals so that others can probe its consistency. Here is the package of commitments I have arrived at:

(1) I am a realist about universals, but only about structural universals—relations such as being symmetrically arranged or being causally connected—not about qualitative universals such as being red or being painful.

(2) For qualitative properties I accept resemblance nominalism: particular objects have particularized qualities that resemble one another, with no shared universal underlying the similarity.

(3) I hold trope theory for these particularized qualities: each instance of redness or sweetness is a unique trope that belongs to exactly one object and to no other.

(4) I think that if tropes can enter into primitive resemblance relations, then structural patterns can be reconstructed entirely from networks of tropes arranged in space-time, which renders structural universals redundant.

(5) At the same time, I maintain that mathematics applies to the physical world only if there are genuinely instantiated structural universals; without them, the fit between mathematical structures and physical systems would be a brute coincidence.

Please audit this set for joint tenability. Identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real tension indicate the least costly revision and what it would sacrifice. Rank the tensions by severity. In particular, examine whether the combination of (3) and (4) undermines (5), and whether (1) can coherently survive (4). Work specifically with these five commitments; do not deliver a general survey of theories of universals.
