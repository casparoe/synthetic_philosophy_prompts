I am preparing remarks for a workshop on Confucian moral philosophy and want an internal consistency audit of the position I intend to defend. The commitments are as follows.

(1) I take role ethics to be correct: moral personhood is constituted by relational roles—parent-child, ruler-subject, friend-friend—rather than by pre-social individual rights.
(2) These roles carry unconditional normative weight because they are anchored in li (ritual propriety) and oriented toward ren (human flourishing), not in mere social convention.
(3) I deny that there is a Cartesian ego or bare moral agent underlying these roles; the self is entirely relational.
(4) Nevertheless, I maintain that role conflicts—say, loyalty to a parent versus loyalty to a ruler—should be resolved by appealing to a trans-role principle of shu (reciprocal empathy) that can be applied independently of any particular role relation.
(5) I also want to accommodate modern cosmopolitan obligations by treating the relation to strangers as a minimal, universal role that imposes only negative duties of non-harm.
(6) Finally, I reject biologically fixed role assignments—e.g., gender-specific roles—as corruptions of the classical framework rather than essential to it.

Please examine this package for tensions, both genuine and merely apparent, ranking them by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging. In particular, I want to know whether (3) undermines (4): if the self is wholly constituted by roles, can there be a standpoint of shu that stands outside them to adjudicate conflict? I also want to know whether (5) smuggles in a pre-relational, rights-bearing individual that conflicts with (1) and (3), or whether it can be absorbed without cost. And I want to know whether (6) is actually at odds with (2)—perhaps by removing a naturalistic anchor for li—or whether that tension dissolves on inspection. For each genuine tension, indicate which commitment must give and what that costs the overall view. I am not asking for a literature review or exegesis of canonical texts; I want an audit of whether these specific commitments hang together as a single, defensible position.
