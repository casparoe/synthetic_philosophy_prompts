My position is that Darwin's mechanism of natural selection and Turing's imitation game instantiate the same structural inversion: genuine competence—adaptive fit and conversational behavior—arises and is validated without any underlying comprehension, representational understanding, or intentional ground in the system displaying it. I call this "competence-first validation," and I argue it undermines the Chinese Room objection not by resolving Searle's problem but by showing that the demand for an internal "understander" is a category mistake we never apply to evolved biological systems. To prevent the analogy from being flat, I inject the "evaluator asymmetry": natural selection validates competence with no evaluator at all, while Turing's test requires a human judge, so the two inversions diverge precisely in whether third-person assessment is intrinsic or externally imposed.
