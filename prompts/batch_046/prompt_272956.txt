Please describe the most important implications of Karl Popper's argument that Darwinian natural selection operates primarily as a metaphysical research program rather than as a strictly falsifiable scientific theory, specifically if we apply that distinction rigorously to contemporary evolutionary psychology and its practice of reverse-engineering mental traits—such as domain-specific modules for social exchange or predator avoidance—from presumed ancestral selection pressures. In particular, address how Popper's falsifiability criterion bears on the problem of post-hoc adaptive storytelling, whether his later qualification of that critique weakens its application to cognitive science, and what the resulting epistemic assessment means for the scientific status of evolutionary explanations that rely on reconstructed Pleistocene environments.
