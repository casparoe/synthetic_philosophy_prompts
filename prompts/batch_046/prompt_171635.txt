Consider the cluster of problems surrounding “free-floating rationales”: normative reasons that allegedly obtain even in the absence of any actual, possible, or counterfactual reasoner who could recognize, be moved by, or stand in the relevant normative relation to them. The issue cuts across metaethics (whether reasons are essentially relational to agents), philosophy of mind (whether responsiveness is constitutive of normative force), and applied domains such as the moral status of non-sentient ecological systems and the possibility that future artificial systems might be beholden to reasons they do not conceptually grasp.

If you were advising a small research group—say, two senior researchers and a postdoc, with about three years of focused work—how should they prioritize the open questions in this cluster? I want a defended agenda, not a survey. Specifically:

- Is the fundamental crux ontological (whether reasons can exist without reasoners) or functional (whether they can exert normative force without any potential respondent)? Which framing renders the others tractable, and which makes them dissolve into pseudo-problems?
- Among the alleged downstream implications—for environmental ethics without sentient subjects, for the normative governance of systems that lack agential understanding, and for the coherence of “objective wrongness” in a permanently post-human future—which genuinely depend on resolving the free-floating rationale question, and which are overrated or do not follow?
- Given current philosophical tools—analyses of response-dependence, grounding relations, structural realism about norms, and analogies to mathematical Platonism—which sub-questions are tractable within three years, which depend on prior breakthroughs in philosophy of mind or metaphysics, and which are likely to remain stalled regardless of effort?

Defend the ordering: state the criteria you are using (downstream dependency, tractability for philosophers rather than empirical scientists, neglect, and resistance to rhetorical resolution), map which questions are upstream of which, and identify what kind of intermediate result over the next few years would cause you to reorder the priorities.
