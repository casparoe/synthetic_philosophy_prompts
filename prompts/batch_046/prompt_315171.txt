Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a virtue ethicist who maintains that moral character consists in genuine, robust dispositions and a situationist critic who holds that cross-situational consistency in moral behavior is too low to support global trait attributions. Write it as a journalist commissioning the document for a feature on philosophy of psychology. Include: (1) a numbered agreed-claims section covering shared commitments about the empirical findings on behavioral variability, the conceptual distinction between global traits and context-sensitive skills, and the conditions under which either side would revise their view; (2) a disagreements table with each side's position phrased in wording it would accept—specifically on whether virtues must manifest across diverse situations to be real, and whether a skill-model of virtue preserves or dissolves the traditional concept; (3) the cruxes, including whether there is a principled consistency threshold for virtue attribution analogous to professional expertise, and whether ecological validity requires that moral assessments be conducted in naturalistic rather than laboratory settings; (4) for each crux, one concrete empirical case or argument both sides agree is relevant (for instance, a longitudinal study tracking compassionate response across varying institutional roles, or an intervention measuring honesty in high-stakes versus low-stakes environments), with each side's explicit prediction recorded. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; keep the focus on the conceptual and empirical agreements and disagreements themselves.
