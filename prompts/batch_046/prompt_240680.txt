I have been working through a view on valid consent that treats deception, coercion, and incompetence not as three independent veto conditions but as distinct failures of what I call "authorial continuity"—the idea that consent is valid only when the consenter remains the continuous normative author of the change being authorized. On this account, deception invalidates consent when it substitutes a false normative landscape (misrepresenting what is being consented to or what follows from it), coercion invalidates it when it collapses the option set so severely that no genuine selection remains, and incompetence invalidates it only when it is domain-specific to the decision at hand and unrecognized by the consenter, rather than reflecting general cognitive limitation. I developed this framing in conversation with friends in an applied ethics seminar, and it seems to capture why mild optimism in risk disclosure often does not vitiate medical consent, while apparently benign framing in other cases does—but I worry this may be post-hoc rationalization dressed up as unity.

What would have to be true for this unified authorial-continuity account to be wrong? I want an honest map of its exposure: which specific hypothetical cases, formal distinctions, or conceptual results would count decisively against it, which would merely be awkward, and whether its defenders should themselves concede any of these as damaging. Does the view risk collapsing back into the standard tripartite framework it seeks to replace—for instance, by smuggling in separate conditions under the umbrella of "continuity"—and are there live escape routes, such as refining what counts as domain-specific incompetence or allowing that some coercive structures leave authorial continuity intact? Please distinguish evidence that would genuinely undermine the position from evidence critics typically invoke but that the view can absorb, and conclude with an assessment of whether this position is well insulated or genuinely exposed, and whether that insulation is a virtue or a defect.
