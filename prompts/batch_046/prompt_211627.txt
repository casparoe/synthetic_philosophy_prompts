I am preparing a decision-tree handout for a graduate seminar on axiological quietism, specifically on whether only the absence of craving and suffering possesses intrinsic value—tranquilism. Please construct a branching taxonomy (not an author list) that separates positions by four concrete commitments. First: does preference satisfaction contribute value beyond the pleasure or relief it produces? Second: is a state with no craving a positive good, a neutral baseline, or a privation valuable only relative to prior suffering? Third: can second-order cravings—desires directed at the absence of first-order craving—generate independent positive value, or do they merely extend the logic of absence? Fourth: does the modal impossibility of future craving (not merely its absence now) alter the present axiological status of tranquillity? Each leaf must carry a label—tranquilism, negative hedonism, the preference-quietist stance, Schopenhauerian pessimism, or 'unnamed'—together with one sentence describing its most awkward commitment. Note any unoccupied combinations and diagnose whether they are incoherent, collapsed into adjacent branches, or merely unexplored. The tree should not conflate tranquilism with negative hedonism; explicitly flag which premise keeps them apart.
