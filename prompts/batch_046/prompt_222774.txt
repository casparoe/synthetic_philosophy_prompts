I'm a phd student in epistemology (mostly externalism stuff, but I keep getting sidetracked into value-of-knowledge questions). I keep circling back to the Meno problem — why is knowledge more valuable than true belief if both guide action correctly? The usual answers talk about stability or justification, but I'm stuck on a more specific framing. Imagine two agents who both correctly believe some proposition P, but in the first agent P is embedded in a dense web of other true beliefs that support it, while in the second P is basically an isolated fluke surrounded by falsehoods. The first seems to have knowledge, the second just true belief. Is the extra value of knowledge here just reducible to the value of coherence or explanatory integration? Or does the knowledge relation itself carry value that can't be captured by network-level properties? And if we go the network route, don't we risk circularity — using coherence as a marker for knowledge when coherence itself seems to depend on already knowing which beliefs are true? I'd love an explaination of whether this kind of holistic shift actually dissolves Meno's puzzle or just relocates it, and wether there's a principled way to separate the value of knowledge from the value of a well-structured belief system.
