Write a short story of approximately 800 words about Mara, a cartographer-philosopher working in a hypothetical region where space-time is locally symmetric but globally curved, who must place a single observation buoy to intercept a drifting vessel. Because the region lacks a canonical coordinate system, Mara discovers that applying the principle of indifference produces contradictory predictions: treating proper time, coordinate arc length, or local entropy gradient as uniformly distributed each yields a different "uniform" prior and therefore a different optimal buoy location. The narrative should embed this Bertrand-style ambiguity in concrete sensory and technical detail—metric charts, acoustic readings, the physical texture of the buoy—rather than in abstract exposition alone. Include an internal monologue or brief dialogue in which Mara articulates why symmetry arguments cannot resolve the ambiguity: there is no physically privileged parameterization, so indifference requires selecting a state space, yet the principle offers no rule for selecting the state space itself. The story should not present a hidden correct answer or a trick resolution; instead, its conclusion should show Mara committing to one parameterization not because she has discovered the true measure, but because she accepts that rational action under structural ignorance demands an explicit conventional choice for which she remains accountable. The philosophical reasoning should feel inseparable from the plot, and the ending should leave the reader uncertain whether her decision was rational or merely pragmatic.
