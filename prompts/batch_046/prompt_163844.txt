Compare the person-affecting restriction and the counterfactual-baseline account as explanations of the procreation asymmetry—the view that there is a moral reason not to create a suffering or barely-worth-living person, but no parallel reason to create a flourishing one. The person-affecting restriction holds that moral reasons must concern identifiable, actual individuals: harming an existing child makes that specific subject worse off, whereas failing to create a happy child wrongs no one, since there is no actual subject who is deprived of well-being. The counterfactual-baseline account holds that harm involves making someone worse off relative to a counterfactual in which that same individual exists without harm, while benefit involves comparing existence to non-existence; because non-existence is not a state of deprivation for anyone, bringing a happy person into being does not remedy a wrong, and thus no reason to create arises.
