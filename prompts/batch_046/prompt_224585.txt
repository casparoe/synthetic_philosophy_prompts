I'm trying to nail down when its legit to talk about germs, climate, or volcanoes as "actors" in history without sliding into that lazy enviromental determinism everyone warns about. Here are my gut verdicts on specific ways people phrase things:

1. Saying Tambora in 1815 reshaped European politics via crop failures and then unrest — that seems okay, just tracing causality.

2. Calling smallpox Cortés's "unwitting ally" against the Aztecs — not okay, feels like I'm giving a virus strategic intent.

3. Claiming the Little Ice Age "forced" states to centralize bureaucracy to manage grain — not okay, sounds like weather is running the government.

4. Describing a 2003 heatwave in Paris as exercising "thermal agency" by lowering the violence threshold in specific neighborhoods without replacing human choice — I'm actually okay with this, but it feels weirdly close to case 3.

5. Writing that "the glacier had other plans" for a Norse Greenland settlement — definatly not okay, pure personification.

6. Saying the Black Death "created space" for laborers to renegotiate wages — I'm torn, sometimes I think its just describing an opening, other times it sounds like I'm assigning the plague a purpose.

So I reject 2, 3, and 5; I accept 1 and 4; and I'm stuck on 6. Is there a principle that captures why 4 is in but 3 is out, and does it tell me what to do about 6? Or do I have to drop one of 1/4 to keep it clean? And what about a case I havent listed — like treating a volcanic winter as an "intervention" in a war? I'd like a principle, or maybe two competing ones, with each clause tied to one of these verdicts, plus an honest note on which of my calls it cant save.
