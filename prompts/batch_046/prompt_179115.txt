Write an essay addressing the following controversy: in proposals for low-impact artificial intelligence, designers often seek to constrain an agent so that it achieves its objective whilst minimising "side effects" — alterations to the world beyond what is strictly required for task completion. The central philosophical difficulty is whether such a constraint can be formulated independently of a full specification of value. Consider two competing formal intuitions that have been proposed in the alignment literature, though neither is canonical: (1) side-effect aversion as minimisation of divergence from a counterfactual baseline trajectory in which the agent never acted, measured by some state-space metric; and (2) side-effect aversion as the avoidance of actions that destroy or diminish entities or configurations that possess non-instrumental value, where "value" here is left deliberately unexplicated. Assess whether either intuition can be made precise without smuggling in a complete axiology. In particular, examine whether the baseline-trajectory approach can avoid implicit value judgements in the choice of state-space representation, distance metric, and temporal horizon, and whether the value-preservation approach collapses into the very specification problem it was meant to circumvent. Weigh the argument that low-impact design is therefore incoherent without prior moral ontology, against the counter-argument that impact can be bounded by purely structural or information-theoretic criteria — for instance, minimising causal footprint or preserving option value for unspecified future preferences. Reach a definite conclusion: is "avoid side effects" definable without a full specification of value, and if so, under what conditions? Justify your verdict with explicit reference to the conceptual dependencies you have identified, rather than with appeals to authority.
