Consider the following exam question and a student response.

Question: In Fear and Trembling, Johannes de Silentio examines Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac as an instance of the teleological suspension of the ethical. Define this concept and explain how Abraham's position differs from that of the tragic hero. Be specific about the role of silence, the absurd, and the universal.

Student answer: The teleological suspension of the ethical occurs when the universal moral law is temporarily set aside in service of a higher religious telos—here, Abraham's direct, private relation to God. God commands Abraham to sacrifice Isaac, so the ethical obligation to protect one's child is suspended for the sake of this absolute duty. Unlike the tragic hero—Agamemnon, for example, who sacrifices Iphigenia to secure favorable winds for the fleet—Abraham cannot justify his act by appealing to any universal good such as the state or the community. His silence is primarily strategic: he withholds his intention from Sarah and Isaac to spare them distress, since explaining the divine command would be incomprehensible within ordinary ethical discourse. Abraham is the knight of faith, not the knight of infinite resignation, because he believes by virtue of the absurd that he will keep Isaac even as he prepares to kill him. Once the trial ends and the angel stops him, the suspension is lifted and Abraham resumes his place in the ethical order without residue, confirming that the ethical remains permanently intact but can be overridden whenever faith demands it.
