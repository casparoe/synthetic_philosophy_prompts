two friends in my study group got stuck arguing over this museum case i mentioned. theres a 1930s sculpture made of raw unfired clay kept in a climate box. over eighty years conservators replaced every decaying bit with a synthetic polymer that looks and weighs identicle but isnt clay chemically. they never took it apart, just patched in place, and the catalog still lists it as the original 1934 piece.

friend A said thats obviously not the original anymore, its a perfect replica. the statue was that specific lump of clay arranged statue-wise, and once all the original matter is gone the object is destroyed, like replacing every part of a car.

friend B said the car analogy is wrong because cars are defined by function, but a statue is basically its form plus institutional history. since the shape was maintained continuously and the museum never declared a replacement, the statue survived the swap. he said if you reject this then biological bodies are constantly dying and being replaced by copies since cells turn over, which is absurd.

A came back that organisms have an internal life-process that maintains continuity, statues dont. without that internal principle the only thing holding the object together is the matter itself, so when every molecule is different you just have a new arrangement copying the old one.

B said thats too materialist. he argued that if they had slowly turned the clay into fired ceramic or even bronze, keeping the form and the museums recognition intact, people would still call it the original statue, not a copy. so the substrate cant be doing all the identity work.

A said that just shows people are sloppy with artifacts, not that theres real identity. she said B is sneaking in the idea that identity can survive with zero shared parts, which makes identity cheap and lets you call anything the original if you keep the shape going long enough.

can you walk through their exchange move by move and say whos right about what? also whats the deeper thing theyre both missing about material constitution?
