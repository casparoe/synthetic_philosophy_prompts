I am a doctoral student in political philosophy, and I have been asked to deliver the opening address at the inauguration workshop for the city’s new Civic Accountability Office—an event attended by republican theorists, urban planners, and community organizers. The room is split: roughly half are fluent in Isaiah Berlin’s taxonomy and Philip Pettit’s republican liberty, while the other half hear “freedom” as either the absence of barriers or the presence of capacities. I want the speech to do structural work with positive, negative, and republican liberty—not to decorate the occasion but to determine what the Office can legitimately claim as success and what it must refuse to say.

Specifically, I want to introduce a distinction I am developing in my dissertation between thin republicanism, in which an institution avoids arbitrary interference but leaves the conditions for domination intact, and thick republicanism, in which institutional design makes arbitrary power structurally impossible rather than merely unlikely. The speech should welcome both audiences, explain why the Office’s charter is best read as an exercise in thick republican liberty rather than in negative or positive liberty, and set the terms for the workshop’s debates without collapsing into lecture. It must avoid framing achievement in terms of removed barriers or distributed resources alone; instead, it should argue that success requires citizens to be able to look one another in the eye within these bureaucratic encounters without fear of arbitrary power, and that this standard is stricter than either non-interference or capacity-building.

The occasion is formal but brief—about five minutes, spoken rather than read as an essay—and it should orient planners and activists without losing the theorists. Please write the address. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or verbatim quotations from published texts; paraphrase any concepts as needed. The philosophical framework should shape what can and cannot be promised about the Office’s mission.
