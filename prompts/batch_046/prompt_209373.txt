I've been convinced that for personal life forecasts—whether you'll stick with a hard career, recover from burnout, etc.—the outside view should basically dominate, and the inside view is mostly rationalization. The only real exception is genuinely private info that can't be collapsed into a reference class, like a hidden health condition. What would count against this? Not just "people are special snowflakes"—I want the hard objections. For instance, what if the reference class is so heterogenous that the outside view gives you a distribution with no actionable center? Does the view risk becoming "trust stats except when you don't," i.e. unfalsifiable? And are there empirical results—maybe from forecasting tournaments or clinical psych—where people systematically beat base rates in self-prediction, and do those survive controlling for selection? Rank the defeaters if you can, and say whether any of them force a real revision or just a tweak around the edges.
