In my political philosophy class we've been going in circles about dirty hands—specifically wether a leader who lies to avert disaster is morally excused, or whether that kind of reasoning always corrupts democratic politics. Everyone agrees on the hypothetical: the lie works, lives are saved, but trust is eroded. Still, the "excused" camp and the "never justified" camp haven't moved in like three discussions. I think part of the stall is that one side is talking about individual moral psychology—can a good person do a bad thing for good ends—while the other side is talking about institutional design, like wheather we should build systems that depend on secrecy. But maybe I'm wrong and it's deeper: maybe it's a hidden disagreement about whether political morality is continuous with personal morality or a totally separate domain. Or maybe it's just bedrock intuition about whether power is inherently tainted. What's the actual structure here? Is it verbal, substantive, or are they answering different questions? And given that, is there a distinction or thought experiment that could break the logjam, or is this just where reasoning stops?
