ok so i've been trying to nail down my view on whether culture actually causes stuff, specifcally in the Weberian frame, and i want a consistency check before i present it in a seminar. here's roughly where i stand:

(a) i think Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis is mostly right — the anxiety around predestination shaped a capitalist-oriented psychological disposition, and it wasn't just post-hoc rationalization of material interests.

(b) but i also think values or cultural meanings are never efficient causes in the strict Humean sense; they only do causal work through institutional rules and incentive structures, so any real causal story bottoms out there.

(c) i accept that social explanation needs interpretive adequacy — you have to grasp the subjective meaning actors attach — but i also think mechanistic process-tracing is the only way to actually confirm a causal claim, not just render it intelligible.

(d) i want to defend value-freedom: researchers should bracket their own normative commitments when weighing evidence.

(e) at the same time i buy Weber's distinction between value-judgment and value-relevance: picking which conditions count as causally salient is always partly guided by the researcher's values, there's no view-from-nowhere algorithm.

(f) lately i've been toying with downward causation — culture as an emergent entity reshaping individual preferences — but i reject it because i think causal closure of the physical rules that out.

do these hang together? i'm specifically worried that (a) and (b) pull opposite directions: if culture is always mediated by institutions, is Weber's spirit of capitalism just an institutional story with cultural window dressing? and (d) with (e) looks contradictory but maybe it's not? does (f) conflict with (a) or just reinforce (b)? and is there a hidden tension in (c) between demanding mechanistic confirmation and demanding interpretive meaning — can you mechanize Verstehen without destroying what makes it explanatory?

please rank the actual tensions by severity, flag which ones dissolve on closer inspection, and tell me the least costly revision. i don't want to abandon Weber but i'm genuinely unsure which commitment has to bend.
