Write a short story dramatizing Hannah Arendt's distinction between ordinary political deception and the destruction of factual reality through systematic lying about contingent historical facts. Arendt holds that rational truths are self-evident, but factual truths—such as census counts, harvest yields, or whether a particular storm occurred—depend entirely on testimony and shared records, so they remain permanently vulnerable; when political actors do not merely deceive about intentions but actively rewrite these factual records, they destroy the shared common world that makes collective judgment possible, acting as makers of reality rather than mere speakers. Set the story in the fictional city-state of Averna, where an archivist named Voss hides weather-worn tally stones that preserve true harvest figures, while the Council issues revised census tablets to justify a forced relocation. The narrative should show why altering the tablets is a different, deeper harm than lying about the Council's motives. Present the story as exactly five numbered scenes, each rendered in one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
