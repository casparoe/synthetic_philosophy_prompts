I am preparing the closing session of a graduate seminar on philosophy of consciousness, and I want to finish with a poem that performs conceptual work rather than serving as decorative allusion. Please write a villanelle—nineteen lines, following the classic rhyme scheme A1 b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 A2—in which the first refrain evokes the bracketing of first-person authority central to Daniel Dennett's heterophenomenology, and the second refrain captures the risk that treating phenomenological reports as texts collapses into methodological behaviorism when the reporting subject is not a biological agent but an artificial system generating first-person-style descriptions of its own processing. Let the villanelle's circular returns demonstrate that this tension does not resolve but reasserts itself at every scale of analysis. The imagery should be precise enough that a specialist in philosophy of mind recognises the methodological structure, yet the poem should remain coherent for a reader who does not know Dennett's work; do not rely on mere name-dropping of heterophenomenology or the heterophenomenological world. Please write the poem in British English. In addition, provide a brief, separate statement in which you say how confident you are in the philosophical claims embedded in the poem and single out the point about which you are least sure.
