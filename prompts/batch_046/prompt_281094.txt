Here is my current package on rigid designation and necessary a posteriori truths, and I want an audit of whether it holds together or collapses: (1) I accept Kripke's modal argument that identities between co-referring rigid designators are necessary if true, yet knowable only a posteriori; (2) I restrict rigid designation to proper names and natural kind terms, holding that definite descriptions and institutional concepts are descriptively anchored; (3) I think terms like 'democracy' refer via a cluster of institutional conditions rather than through a causal-historical chain; (4) I also think that democracy might be essentially participatory in a specific, non-obvious way that comparative historical sociology could reveal, yielding a necessary but empirically discovered identity; (5) I insist that recognizing (4) does not make 'democracy' a rigid designator. Which pairs are genuinely in tension? In particular, does (4) contradict the descriptive anchoring in (3) together with the restriction in (2), or can a descriptively fixed term support necessary a posteriori discoveries without rigidity? And is there only an apparent conflict between (1) and (5) that dissolves once we distinguish reference-fixing from modal discovery? Rank the tensions by severity, indicate the cheapest revision for each, and say which single change would clean up the most.
