I serve on the partnership-ethics board of a small international development nonprofit, and next Tuesday we must vote on whether to renew our four-year funding agreement with a rural agricultural cooperative in a region where institutional interaction operates through practices I have been trying to understand by distinguishing constitutive reciprocity from transactional corruption.

Every step of our project — securing land-use approvals, consulting the elder council, registering with local officials — requires the cooperative's leaders to present substantial gifts of livestock, ceremonial textiles, and other goods whose value is publicly acknowledged; refusal is interpreted not as ethical purity but as a denial of the recipient's social standing and a refusal to enter the shared framework within which collective decisions occur. Our Western funders treat any material presentation to officials or gatekeepers as bribery regardless of context, while the cooperative insists that forbidding these gifts will not eliminate corruption but will exclude them from local institutional life, since without them there is no recognized medium for legitimate partnership.

Please draft the board's decision memo recommending one concrete course — renew with an explicit exception, renew under documented caps and transparency conditions, or terminate the partnership — with a clear implementation time frame, serious engagement with the cooperative's position, and an honest account of the strongest argument for the option you reject. Present the memo as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion; in the fifth item, state what specific local finding or interpretive evidence would change your recommendation and identify where your reasoning is most vulnerable to failure.
