In debates between counterfactual and interventionist theories of causation, the counterfactual side is often likened to rewinding a tape: you fix the background, splice out one event, and check whether the outcome still plays. The interventionist side is likened to flipping a switch on a circuit board: causation is what you can change by manipulating one variable while holding others fixed by design. Lay out the correspondence table for each analogy—what plays the role of the tape, the splice, the viewer; the circuit, the switch, the engineer—then identify where each mapping breaks, especially regarding overdetermination, preemption, and symmetric causal structures. Now inject a less-discussed scenario: a fully coupled system with no exogenous variables, where every node is both cause and effect of every other. Ask whether either analogy survives this injection, whether the standard conclusion—that interventionism better captures scientific practice—still holds, and if not, what a more accurate analogy would be.
