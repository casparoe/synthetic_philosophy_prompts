I am preparing for a graduate seminar on moral metaphysics and have become dissatisfied with the standard taxonomy that places Buddhist ethics somewhere between virtue ethics and deontological rule-following. I want to argue instead that the canonical analysis of karma—specifically the interplay between cetanā (volitional intention) and vipāka (fructive result)—entails a form of structural consequentialism that is neither agent-centered nor rule-bound in the usual sense. Please construct the argument in detail: begin by explaining why the doctrine of anattā (non-self) undermines any purely virtue-ethical reading that treats moral development as the cultivation of an enduring character, then show how the Abhidhammic distinction between mental formation and result creates a causal topology in which the moral weight of an action is determined by its position within the relational network of conditions rather than by subjective intention alone.

To make the argument distinctive, incorporate the following niche conceptual move: model karmic causation not as linear transmission but as a non-local field, so that the ethical evaluation of an action depends on how it reconfigures the web of pratītyasamutpāda (dependent origination) across agents and conditions, not merely on the individual agent's mental state. Address the standard objection that karma is strictly individual—often summarized by the claim that beings are heirs to their own actions—by showing that individual ownership of karma is compatible with, and in fact requires, this relational field structure if anattā is taken seriously. Do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or references to specific modern commentators; ground the reasoning entirely in the conceptual architecture of cetanā, vipāka, anattā, and dependent origination. Conclude by specifying whether this structural consequentialist reading makes Buddhist ethics closer to indirect consequentialism or to a distinct third category, and defend that classification against the objection that it collapses moral agency into deterministic causal analysis.
