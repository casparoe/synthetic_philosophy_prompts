I'm in a seminar on religion and science and me and my friends Maya and Leo have been going back and forth on whether explaining religion naturally automatically debunks it. I mostly lean toward Maya but I'm not totally sure, and they asked me to write up our exchange for feedback. Here's how it went.

(1) Maya said that once you explain religion as a natural phenomenon—agency detection running hot plus evolutionary pressure for in-group bonding—then you've basically shown religious beliefs are false but useful mental software. She used the cloud-face analogy: we understand the visual mechanism, but that doesn't mean there's a face.

(2) Leo said that's just the "adaptive misbelief" story and its not the only naturalistic account. He brought up what he called "cultural path dependency," where specific rituals and doctrines get locked in through costly signaling, but the metaphysical claims—gods, spirits, etc.—function more like "scaffolding" or framing devices that stabilize cooperation, not literal false beliefs people are duped by. He compared it to legal fictions like corporate personhood.

(3) Maya said that's speciall pleading. Even if rituals are arbitrary, believers still direct prayer and worship at real entities, and Leo hasn't shown they treat metaphysics as fiction. She said if the beliefs aren't literally false, then the natural explanation doesn't do any debunking work, which makes his view pointless.

(4) Leo replied she's treating belief as all-or-nothing propositional assent. He said in many traditions participation is closer to enactment or social coordination, and the metaphysical language is part of the practice rather than a scientific theory about ontology. So the phenomenon isn't "people being wrong about the world" but "people using symbolic frameworks to hold groups together."

(5) Maya came back and said that sounds like redefining belief to save religion from debunking, and Leo's scaffolding idea ignores that for plenty of believers, prayer is genuinely addressed to an existing agent, not performed like a legal ritual.
