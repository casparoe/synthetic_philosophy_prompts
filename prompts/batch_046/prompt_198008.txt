Please construct a philosophical argument for the position that Bradley's regress, when directed at the problem of propositional unity, is dissolved not by positing a special non-relational tie (of the kind invoked by some realist theories of states of affairs) nor by eliminating propositions in favor of unstructured lists of constituents, but rather by reconceiving unity as an intrinsic feature of functional application within a ramified type hierarchy, in which each level of application is self-grounding and requires no external unifier. Your argument should specifically address: (i) why this hierarchical-functional view does not generate an infinite regress of unifying relations; (ii) how it distinguishes a genuinely unified false proposition from a mere aggregate of its constituent terms; and (iii) why it does not collapse into Bradley's own claim that relations are ultimately internal to their relata. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; focus on the internal coherence and explanatory advantages of the view relative to standard relational and non-relational accounts of judgment.
