I'm preparing a graduate seminar on the logical empiricists' attempts to ground scientific communication in non-linguistic media, and I've found that most treatments of Otto Neurath's ISOTYPE system treat it either as a quaint precursor to modern infographics or as a straightforward application of his physicalist ontology. I want to go deeper into a conceptual tension that standard overviews gloss over. Neurath held that metaphysical pseudo-propositions arise largely from the grammatical ambiguity and emotional coloring of ordinary language, and he proposed a standardized picture language—governed by explicit transformational rules—to serve as a universal, theory-neutral vehicle for the encyclopedia of unified science. Yet the very project of standardizing visual symbols requires fixing conventions of representation: deciding that a particular stylized figure denotes a factory worker, or that repetition of an icon scales with quantity, involves interpretive choices that look structurally similar to the linguistic conventions he criticized. At the same time, Neurath insisted that protocol sentences, even in visual form, remain fallible and historically situated, which seems to pull against any claim that pictures provide an unmediated window onto physical reality.

Please explain, at a conceptual rather than biographical level, how Neurath can maintain that his picture language avoids the metaphysical pitfalls of verbal language without falling into a parallel form of pictorial conventionalism. In particular, address whether his transformational rules are meant to be purely syntactic mappings between verbal and visual expressions—thereby leaving semantic interpretation to empirical usage—or whether they carry substantive ontological commitments about what kinds of entities or relations are pictorially representable. Furthermore, clarify how this issue connects to his broader rejection of foundationalist epistemology: if visual protocols are both convention-laden and fallible, what work is left for them to do in securing intersubjective agreement within unified science?

I am not looking for a history of the ISOTYPE movement or a bibliography of secondary literature; I want an analysis of whether the tension between standardization and anti-metaphysical neutrality is genuinely resolvable within Neurath's framework, or whether it reveals a deeper instability in the logical empiricist dream of a purely descriptive, language-independent scientific medium. If you find it useful to contrast Neurath's position with the later information-design tradition, do so only to sharpen the conceptual point, not to invoke external authority.
