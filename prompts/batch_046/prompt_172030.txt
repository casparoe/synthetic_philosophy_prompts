I'm on the review board for a synthetic-bio lab and we keep having to decide whether an engineered gene cluster in modified gut bacteria counts as having a 'function' for our regulatory checklist. The distinction between etiological and causal-role accounts keeps coming up, but no one can agree on how to actually run the check. Can you write me a practical procedure -- like a flowchart in words -- that my team can use on a proposal? It needs to handle both theories seperately, flag where they conflict, say exactly what evidence counts for each step, and mark the spots where we still have to make a value call rather than just read the biology. Then run it on three cases: (1) a CRISPR-inserted nitrogen-fixation pathway that works but was never selected for in this host, (2) a native digestive enzyme thats been silenced but still structurally present, and (3) a horizontally transferred antibiotic-resistance gene thats currently contributing to survival in the gut but arrived by accident. One of these should come out differently under the two theories, and one should hit a point where the procedure says stop, this needs human judgement not just more data. Keep it usable in about 20 minutes per proposal, and dont ask us to look up evolutionary phylogenies we dont have access to -- work with what a reviewer can actually observe from the proposal docs.
