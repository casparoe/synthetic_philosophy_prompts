Please argue that XOR blackmail and counterfactual mugging should not be treated as structurally analogous decision problems, and that an agent who correctly refuses to pay in counterfactual mugging—because the randomizing event has already occurred and causal influence is absent—should nevertheless pay in XOR blackmail. Ground your argument in the claim that XOR blackmail creates a symmetric predictive dependency, which I will call a counterfactual lock: two independent predictive mechanisms are cross-conditioned so that each branch predicts the opposite outcome in the other, leaving neither branch as already resolved. Explain how this symmetry eliminates the temporal-resolution asymmetry that makes counterfactual mugging evidentially one-sided, and show why evidential reasoning in the lock must weigh both outcomes as live possibilities rather than treating one as causally inert and therefore irrelevant. Distinguish this carefully from a collapse into causal decision theory: the point is not that the agent causes the outcome, but that the correlation structure is constitutively tied to the agent's choice in both directions simultaneously. Finally, address the objection that this reasoning would license payment in any symmetric predictive scenario, and defend the claim that the counterfactual lock is special precisely because the agent's decision is part of the correlation's generating conditions rather than merely a predictor of an independently fixed state.
