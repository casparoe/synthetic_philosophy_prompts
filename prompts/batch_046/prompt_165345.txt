write an essay on whether Berkeley's account of money—he treats it as a bare token representing labor or goods, with value fixed only by social convention—is genuinely compatible with his immaterialism. critics argue that monetary institutions (debts, exchange rates, national currencies) involve relations and obligations that persist even when no individual mind is thinking about them, which looks like smuggling in mind-independent structure. defenders reply that these are just long-lasting patterns of shared ideas, nothing more. weigh the arguments on both sides, but don't end with a shrug—defend an actual verdict. then apply it to this twist: if bodies are just stable bundles of ideas for Berkeley, is a fully digital currency—the kind that lives on distribued ledgers with no phyiscal coin—the perfect Berkeleyan money, since it has no material substrate? or does its hidden dependence on servers, electricity, and silicon show that even "immaterial" value needs material support, which would undermine Berkeley's claim that matter is unnecessary? reach a conclusion and justify it.
