I've been trying to wrap my head around Bradley's regress — you know, the old problem that if a relation connects two things, then you need another relation to connect the first relation to those things, and then another one for that, and it never stops? A friend of mine said it's basically why you can't just have 'facts' sitting there without some kind of glue, but then the glue needs glue too, which sounds like a nightmare for anyone who likes tidy ontologies. Anyway, I want a short story that actually lives inside that problem instead of just explaining it like a textbook.

Write about a protaganist who works as a kind of archivist in a world where every time two objects are joined, the joining itself becomes a separate physical object that also needs joining. Maybe they've discovered what they think is the ultimate binder — a universal knot or a single thread — but then realize that even it's connection to everything else is just another loose thread. I don't need it to be a philosophy lecture disguised as fiction; let the regress feel real in the plot. Here's my own weird addition, not from any real theory: in this world, people can only remember things that are fully bound, so every time the chain breaks somewhere, a memory vanishes without warning. Use that if it fits, ignore it if it doesn't. Keep it literary and a bit melancholy, not too techy or sci-fi.
