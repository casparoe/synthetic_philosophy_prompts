My study group on political obligation keeps repeating the line "an unjust law is no law at all" to dismiss the Rawlsian claim that civil disobedience should accept legal punishment. They apply it specifically to climate activists who block highways, arguing that if the emission regulations being enforced are unjust, the activists have no duty to submit to arrest and the slogan proves it. Could you lay out, as a precise menu, what that slogan could actually be claiming? I want the range from the trivial claim that unjust laws lack moral authority, through the Thomist distinction that they remain legally valid but do not bind conscience, to the conceptual claim that injustice is analytically incompatible with law, and on to the anarchist claim that such norms generate no political duties whatsoever. For each reading, say whether it genuinely supports refusing punishment in this case, and whether it is consistent with the same group's parallel commitment that democratic legislation in a nearly just society generally creates a prima facie obligation to obey. I need the disambiguation laid out cleanly so I can bring it back to the session.
