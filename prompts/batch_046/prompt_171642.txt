I'm moderating a panel interview for an interdisciplinary philosophy-of-social-science series. Our guest is an unnamed defender of a reflexive-causal interpretation of Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis. On her view, cultural values are not mere correlates or background conditions but genuine causes that operate through what she terms reflexive normative embedding: economic practices gain altered causal efficacy when agents acquire second-order representations of those practices as morally charged, thereby restructuring the pathways through which values influence outcomes. She is adept at deflecting materialist critiques by insisting that she is not denying economic base conditions but specifying the precise mechanism by which values acquire causal traction.

Draft eight to ten rigorous but collegial interview questions that move well beyond a restatement of her position. I require at least one question that presses the distinction between reflexive normative embedding and standard rational-choice or belief-desire explanations of action; include a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that rational-choice misses the second-order restructuring of practices rather than merely adding beliefs to preferences. I also require at least one question confronting the familiar geographic and temporal counterexamples—Catholic regions with capitalist development, or the chronological gap between the Reformation and industrial takeoff; include a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that these cases lack the specific representational shift her mechanism requires. Third, include at least one question asking how reflexive normative embedding is empirically distinguishable from post-hoc narrative reconstruction of institutional history; include a follow-up keyed to her likely answer that the mechanism predicts specific institutional path-dependencies rather than mere retrospective coherence.

Beyond these three core pairs, include one question that isolates which feature of her account actually does the explanatory work—the particular content of Protestant values, the formal structure of reflexivity, or the surrounding institutional context—and asks what the explanatory landscape looks like if that feature is removed. Conclude with a question about what evidence—historical, sociological, or experimental—would genuinely count against reflexive normative embedding, rather than merely demonstrating that material factors also contribute. The sequence should anticipate her standard deflections, avoid generic biographical openings, and must not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
