I've been working through some recent literature in information thermodynamics after a colleague challenged my intuition that computation is essentially free so long as it is reversible, and I have come to hold a somewhat idiosyncratic view of Maxwell's demon: rather than seeing Landauer's principle as resolving the paradox by identifying a retrospective cost in bit erasure, I now think the physically significant burden lies in the ongoing maintenance of mutual information between the demon's state and the sorted particle states. On this framing, the demon does not violate the second law because sustaining the conditional correlations required for continued sorting itself requires a continuous thermodynamic investment; the standard erasure-focused account, I think, misplaces the cost onto the wrong temporal stage of the cycle and treats correlation as an ephemeral bookkeeping device rather than a physical resource that must be actively preserved. I am aware that this shifts emphasis rather than overturning Bennett's analysis, but I want to understand whether it is a substantive reconceptualization or merely a redescription.

What, then, would count as a genuine defeater for this correlation-maintenance position? I am looking for an honest assessment of its exposure: could a precise formal result show that mutual information maintenance carries no independent thermodynamic cost beyond what is already captured by erasure? Would an experimental demonstration of a sorting mechanism with negligible sustained correlation costs undermine the claim, or would defenders simply retreat to a more abstract level? Are there conceptual distinctions—perhaps between epistemic correlation and physical interaction—that render my framing a category error rather than a physical insight? Please rank these potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, indicate which ones proponents of the standard Landauer-centered view would concede as damaging, and tell me whether the position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability—if so, whether that insulation is a virtue of a conceptual claim or a vice.
