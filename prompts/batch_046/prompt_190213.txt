People talking about Hofstadter's frame/outer/inner message distinction in GEB often lean on the record-player analogy: the vinyl grooves are the outer message, the needle and motor are the frame, and the music from the speaker is the inner message. They use it to argue that symbols are dead on their own — meaning only happens when the frame "plays" them. Audit this: map the correspondances precisely (stylus? amplifier? listener? acoustic space?). Where does it brakes — e.g. can the same formal system be "played" consistently but differently, like a record on a different turntable? Does the analogy smuggle in the idea that the artist's intended recording is the "true" inner message, and does that bias how we treat frame messages? If we swap the record for DNA and the player for a ribosome, does the location of meaning shift — and does that weaken the claim that meaning is purely frame-dependent? Don't just list objections; say whether the original conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table.
