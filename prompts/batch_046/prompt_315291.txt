Write a dialogue between two philosophers—Mara, who holds a post-absurdist view, and Leo, who defends a contemporary theory of meaning in life—set informally as they walk back from a graduate seminar on existentialism and stop at a café to keep arguing. Mara should maintain that recognizing cosmic indifference transforms rather than destroys the possibility of meaning, defending what she terms "rebellious continuity": the practice of pursuing local projects with full intensity precisely while refusing to treat their success as resolving the global question of ultimate justification. She should accuse standard project-based accounts of committing "the substitution error"—treating frame-internal significance as if it answered the frame-transcendent demand for cosmic endorsement—while Leo should reject that demand as illegitimate, arguing instead for "value density," the idea that meaningfulness consists in the coherence and reciprocal richness of one's attachments within a life-structure rather than in external metaphysical certification.

The dialogue must contain genuine back-and-forth rather than parallel monologues: Leo should directly respond to Mara's substitution-error charge by explaining why frame-relative meaning does not pretend to be frame-transcendent, and Mara should answer Leo's accusation that she demands an impossible form of metaphysical assurance that no human practice actually requires. Both characters should be equally competent—no strawman, no obvious loser—and the tone should remain intellectually rigorous but conversational, grounded in the café setting. Focus the disagreement on whether meaningfulness requires cosmic endorsement and whether local significance can stand without it, using the specific conceptual tools of rebellious continuity, substitution error, value density, and the frame-relative versus frame-transcendent distinction, without appealing to biographical details or asking for historical citations.
