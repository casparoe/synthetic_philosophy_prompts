Here is one student’s submission:

Dennett’s intentional stance is a predictive strategy in which we treat a system as a rational agent with beliefs and desires rather than describing its physical mechanisms. Dennett applies this stance very broadly—even to thermostats—which shows that consciousness is not a prerequisite. When we interact with large language models, we naturally explain their outputs by attributing beliefs about language, context, and user intentions, and the predictive success of that practice suggests the stance is appropriate. Nevertheless, Dennett should be read as an instrumentalist about mental states: he holds that beliefs are useful predictive fictions that do not literally exist in anyone, human or artificial. On his account, original intentionality arises only through evolutionary history, giving humans a kind of real mental causation, whereas LLMs possess only derived intentionality, borrowed entirely from their designers and users. Because derived intentionality is not genuine—merely a projection of human purposes onto an artifact—adopting the intentional stance toward an LLM is pragmatically justified but ontologically empty. The model behaves as if it believes things, but there is nothing it is like to be the model, and nothing inside it corresponds to actual belief.
