ok so i'm prepping for this philosophy of mind seminar i'm sittting in on — not teaching, just presenting — and i want to audit my own commitments before i open my mouth. here's the package i've ended up with after too much coffee and arguing with my friend about whether you could "be" in two places at once:

(1) i'm with Hofstadter in a broad sense: the "I" is a high-level pattern, not tied to one particular brain. if my cognitive architecture got instantiated in another brain with high fidelity — not just a snapshot copy of memories but the actual running pattern — there's a real, non-metaphorical sense in which i persist there. think Aunt Hillary in the ant fugue, except the substrate is another human brain.

(2) i'm also a hard physicalist: consciousness only comes from specific causal architectures in neural tissue (or functionally identical substrates). no souls, no panpsychism, no mystical substrate-dependence.

(3) personal identity requires psycholgical continuity — memories, values, personality traits — carried forward through causal connection, not just snapshot similarity.

(4) consciousness requires a single, unified phenomenal field. you can't have one subject of experience distributed across two brains with no real-time causal coupling.

(5) ant colonies don't have minds. no collective "I", no matter how elegant the coordination. just individual ants.

i can see at least three places where these might pull apart. first, (1) and (3) seem to fight: if pattern-instantiation is what makes me persist, then breaking the causal chain to copy me into a new brain shouldn't matter, but (3) says causal continuity is necessary. second, (4) looks threatened by (1): if my pattern is copied into two seperate brains, both seem to be "me" by (1), but then i've got two disconnected phenomenal fields both claiming to be the same "I", which violates (4)'s unity. third, (1) and (5) look like they clash — if patterns can survive substrate-transfer in brains, why can't an ant colony realize a collective "colony-I" pattern?

i'm not sure which of these are real and which dissolve once you look closer. can you rank them by severity? for each real tension, say what has to give and what's the least costly revision. and tell me if there's an apparent conflict i'm overrating — i genuinely don't know which commitment should bend, maybe (1) is overreaching, maybe (3) is too conservative. just trace what commits me to what, don't lecture me on the basics and please don't give me a bibliography.
