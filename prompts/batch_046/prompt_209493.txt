I am writing a feature on whether Marx’s analysis of machinery anticipates contemporary anxiety about AI-driven displacement. A historian of economic thought offered me the following summary of Marx’s position: he argued that as machinery evolves into an automatic system of production, labor shifts mainly to supervisory roles and the working day tends to shrink; Marx therefore regarded mechanization as unambiguously emancipatory, treating the tendency of the rate of profit to fall as a temporary technical snag that faster automation would eventually resolve. The historian added that Marx’s deeper worry was not job loss but capital’s private appropriation of the general intellect—the collective scientific knowledge objectified in machinery. Before I file, I want to check whether this is a fair rendering. Which parts align with Marx’s actual texts on the factory system and the Grundrisse fragment, which parts are common simplifications, and where does the summary quietly invert Marx’s actual argument about technology, exploitation, and crisis?
