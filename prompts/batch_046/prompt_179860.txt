I want a poem I can use to close a discussion with my team about decision-making under moral uncertainty. Write a sestina of exactly 39 lines using the end-words one, five, claim, count, coin, and world. The repetition should structurally mirror the argument's cycle—the single claim returning with undiminished force, the attempt to sum lives collapsing, and the resolution refusing to arrive. In the envoi, leave the tension open, perhaps by contrasting a distributed-system intuition (equal nodes, no weighted quorum) with the visceral pull to save more lives. The poem should respect the view I described: it permits randomization between the two groups but rejects weighting the chance by headcount. Make it precise enough that someone familiar with the debate recognizes the position, elegant enough that the form itself carries part of the philosophical work, and free of sermon or resolution.
