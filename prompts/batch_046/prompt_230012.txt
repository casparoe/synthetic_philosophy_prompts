here's the exam question i gave them:

"Explain the Frege-Geach problem for moral expressivism. Focus on what happens when a moral sentence is embedded in a logical context like negation or a conditional, and say whether adopting a contractualist framing — where expressing 'X is wrong' is taken to be proposing a cooperative norm rather than reporting an inner feeling — helps solve it."

and here's one student's answer:

"The Frege-Geach problem shows that expressivism can't explain how moral sentences work inside larger logical structures. Normally, expressivists say that 'torture is wrong' doesn't describe a fact but expresses a negative attitude toward torture. But when we embed it in a conditional like 'If torture is wrong, then we shouldn't do it,' the speaker isn't necessarily expressing disapproval of torture — they're just considering a possibility. So the expressivist has a compositionality problem: the meaning of the embedded clause seems to change depending on context, which breaks logical consistency.

Geach originally pointed this out and proposed that expressivists should treat moral terms as having both expressive and descriptive functions — they denote properties in embedded contexts but express attitudes in unembedded ones. This hybrid approach lets the conditional work because the 'if' clause can refer to the property of wrongness without requiring the speaker to feel disapproval.

Also, if we use a contractualist framing where saying something is wrong means proposing a cooperative norm, then embedding works fine because we're just proposing a norm hypothetically. So contractualism basically dissolves the problem by making moral language about social rules rather than private attitudes. Therefore the Frege-Geach challenge is mostly solved once we stop treating morality as personal emotion."
