I am revising the syllabus for my graduate seminar on epistemic dynamics, and I want an exercise that pushes Van Fraassen’s Reflection principle beyond the textbook harmony cases—specifically, a scenario in which Reflection and a structurally enriched variant of the Principal Principle come into direct conflict in a way that cannot be dissolved by the standard expert-function reply. To that end, please construct a thought experiment built to the following specifications.

The scenario should be realistic, confined to a single paragraph, and set in an ordinary institutional context—say, a public-health forecasting office or an adaptive clinical-trial monitoring unit—without invoking demons, perfect predictors, exotic physics, or lotteries. Introduce a novel framing device: the agent receives not a bare numerical chance report, but what I will call a “structured chance report” that specifies both an objective probability and the causal-graph dependencies linking the generating mechanism to her own measurement apparatus, which she knows introduces a predictable directional bias. At time t she also knows, with complete transparency, the exact non-Bayesian correction algorithm that her future self at t+1 will apply upon receiving intermediate data—an algorithm she currently endorses because it is pre-registered and calibrated to correct for that apparatus bias, but which is not equivalent to Bayesian conditioning. Engineer the case so that, for the same proposition about the outcome (for example, the efficacy of a treatment or the peak of an infection wave), Van Fraassen’s Reflection principle demands one precise credence at t, while the Principal Principle applied to the structured chance report demands a different precise credence at t, and the two prescriptions do not coincide.

After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that the specifications are met: identify the proposition at issue, the agent’s method of belief formation, and the nearby worlds or update paths relevant to each theory. Walk through the verdict Reflection delivers and why—explicitly computing the expectation of her future mechanically corrected credence—then do the same for the structured Principal Principle, showing how the embedded causal model of measurement bias shifts the assigned value. Isolate the structural feature that does the work: the combination of transparent algorithmic self-correction and the apparatus-bias component embedded in the chance report. Then explain what happens if that feature is removed—for instance, if the future update is pure Bayesian conditioning, or if the chance report is stripped of its structural apparatus component—indicating whether the conflict collapses back into standard agreement. Finally, include an honest assessment of how a defender of Reflection would attack the case—likely by arguing that the expectation should be taken over rational belief-revision rather than over the output of a calibrated mechanical procedure—and state whether that reply restores agreement without begging the question against the Principal Principle. Keep the entire response suitable for a philosophy graduate audience and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
