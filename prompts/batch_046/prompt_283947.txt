If Gettier's counterexamples establish that knowledge is impossible whenever a justifying chain contains even one false intermediate step, then examine a distributed climate-monitoring network in which every local sensor is miscalibrated yet a redundant aggregation protocol corrects these errors and outputs a true global mean. Would the network's collective judgment be a Gettier case—thus failing to be knowledge—because each node's reasoning relies on a false local reading? Or does the protocol's error-correction supply the requisite connection between justification and truth, implying that Gettier's problem applies only to isolated individual knowers and not to epistemically distributed systems?
