Von Neumann's *The Computer and the Brain* frames neural computation as analog and statistical rather than discrete and logical, while his unfinished collaboration with Burks on self-replicating automata implies that genuine autonomy requires a universal constructor capable of interpreting its own blueprint. Taken together, these seem to entail that a purely digital system—such as a transformer-based model with discrete token processing and no explicit self-descriptive architectural loop—cannot replicate the brain's mode of cognition regardless of parameter scale, because it lacks both the analog substrate and the literal self-reconstruction mechanism von Neumann treated as essential. Is the error in treating his analog/digital distinction as an ontological boundary rather than an engineering observation, in misreading the self-replication requirement as demanding literal blueprint-reading at runtime, or in applying these two frameworks jointly to contemporary AI at all? In other words, does the argument fail at one specific joint, or does it dissolve once we stop forcing von Neumann's separate projects into a single criterion for machine mind?
