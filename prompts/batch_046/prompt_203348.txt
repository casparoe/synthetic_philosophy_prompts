In a recent discussion of scientific change, an interlocutor summarized Kuhn’s position with the line “paradigm shifts are gestalt switches, not arguments,” and used it to block any request for rational justification across paradigms. I suspect this slogan conflates several distinct claims. Could you disambiguate it by laying out, as a menu, the precise theses it might express? For each reading, indicate whether it amounts to (a) a psychological observation about how scientists experience change, (b) a claim about the structure of conceptual frameworks that makes direct comparison impossible, or (c) an epistemic thesis that rational evaluation between paradigms is genuinely impossible. I am especially interested in how each reading relates to the distinction between perceptual “seeing-as” (like the duck-rabbit figure) and deep conceptual incommensurability: does the gestalt analogy apply only at the perceptual level, or does it genuinely extend to the evaluation of explanatory standards and predictive success? Finally, for each reading, say whether it actually licenses the interlocutor’s move—dismissing requests for cross-paradigm justification—or whether it merely describes a difficulty without resolving the normative question.
