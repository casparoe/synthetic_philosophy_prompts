You are advising the design team for an autonomous emergency-response drone. In a multi-casualty incident, the drone carries limited medical supplies that can either be deployed in full to stabilize one patient with an immediately life-threatening injury, or divided into five smaller kits that can each stabilize one of five different patients with serious but not instantly fatal injuries. A team member insists that the drone should always choose the five, because saving more lives is axiomatically better and any preference for the one treats people as interchangeable units rather than as individuals with equal moral standing. You worry that this reasoning might miss something important about whether the presence of additional patients changes the moral claim of the first patient. Without appealing to any named ethical theory, advise what decision rule the drone should follow and defend whether the number of people affected should determine the choice.
