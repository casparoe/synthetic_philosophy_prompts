Construct the joint statement that an Aggregate-Continuity Reductionist (ACR) and a Conventionalist Emptiness Theorist (CET) could both endorse after a sustained adversarial collaboration on Buddhist anattā and personal identity.

Camp A (ACR) holds that anattā denies only a permanent, unitary, immaterial ātman; that the five skandhas are ultimately real, dependently originated processes; and that personal identity over time is constituted by causal-psychological continuity among these aggregates, comparable to a stream or flame. They treat rebirth as literal continuity of this stream, deny any further fact of ownership, and hold that first-person indexicals in karmic contexts refer indexically to a present node in this real though impermanent process.

Camp B (CET) holds that anattā extends to the skandhas themselves: all dharmas lack svabhāva. Personal identity is a purely conventional designation (prajñapti-mātra) with no metaphysical substrate; causal-psychological continuity is conceptually constructed rather than mind-independent; and rebirth is the conventional ascription of karmic results to a dependently originated sequence lacking intrinsic unity. They deny that first-person indexicals pick out any real process-node, treating them as pragmatic linguistic instruments.

The document should contain the following, in order.

First, a numbered section of claims both sides genuinely accept. Include at minimum: (i) the rejection of a permanent, unitary soul; (ii) the compatibility of karma and rebirth with the absence of such a soul; (iii) the descriptive adequacy of skandha-analysis for ordinary phenomenology; (iv) the obligation to avoid both eternalism (sassatavāda) and annihilationism (ucchedavāda); and (v) any further shared formal points about the structure of the problem.

Second, a structured disagreements section. For ACR, state precisely—in wording they would sign—the claim that there is a non-conventional, mind-independent fact about whether two temporal stages belong to one causal-psychological stream. For CET, state precisely—in wording they would sign—the claim that there is no mind-independent fact of stream-membership beyond conventional ascription, and that the appearance of continuity is itself dependently originated and conceptually mediated. Present each formulation separately, without blending them into compromise language.

Third, identify the cruxes: sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp. Likely cruxes include whether psychological continuity requires a non-conventional substrate; whether first-personal indexical reference in karmic contexts is analyzable without conventionalism; and whether avoiding eternalism and annihilationism demands a middle-way ontology of processes or can be satisfied by pure conventionalism.

Fourth, for each crux, present the Split-Stream Test Case: suppose neuro-cognitive continuity is bifurcated technologically so that one mental-state chain divides into two equally continuous branches, both retaining shared initial memories and karmic traces. Both sides should agree this case bears directly on the crux. Then record each side's explicit advance prediction: ACR should state whether both branches, neither, or exactly one inherits the original karmic obligations, and on what basis; CET should state whether the question is malformed, conventionally resolvable, or otherwise, and on what basis.

Finally, include a brief assessment of whether any part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive—specifically, whether disagreement about "real continuity" versus "conventional continuity" collapses once the ontological status of the skandhas is fixed—and if so, identify which part and why.

Format the response with the agreed-claims section first, followed by the disagreements table or list, then the crux-analysis with predictions, then the verbal/substantive assessment. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke obscure texts by direct quotation; rely on widely recognized Buddhist concepts and standard philosophical tools only.
