I'm organizing a workshop on epistemic rationality and self-trust, and I need a sorting instrument—not a prose comparison, but a decision tree that maps where someone actually stands on Van Fraassen's Reflection principle. Rather than asking people to pick a label, the tree should begin with a concrete case: a scientist knows that tomorrow she will examine biased data and will rationally update her credence in hypothesis H downward, even though she now judges H to be well-supported. The first branch asks whether her present credence in H should match her expectation of tomorrow's credence; the second asks whether the principle applies to beliefs about her own reasoning or only to object-level beliefs; the third asks whether anticipated future irrationality can ever justify present revision. From these, build a taxonomy in which each leaf carries a named position or "unnamed" and one sentence on its most awkward commitment—especially for combinations that seem coherent but have no defenders. I want the tree to separate strict reflectionists, diachronic coherence revisionists, and pragmatic self-trust views, but also to flag any pairs it cannot distinguish (for instance, if two positions differ only over whether reflection failures are epistemic or pragmatic defects). The instrument should work for someone who understands rational belief revision but hasn't memorized the literature: no bibliographies, no citation requests, and no questions that depend on knowing specific publication dates.
