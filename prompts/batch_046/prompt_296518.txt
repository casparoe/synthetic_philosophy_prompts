Im an auditor for a labour rights NGO and we keep seeing employment contracts that claim to protect 'work-life balance' but sneak in obligations through 'optional wellness hours', 'flex time', or 'rest bonuses' that still colonize the weekend. I want a reusable decision procedure I can run on any contract to determine whether it violates a worker's right to leisure, not just health-and-safety rules. Ground the steps in three things: Weber's idea of work as a calling (the protestant ethic), the french 35-hour week's quantitative cap on labour, and japanese karoshi jurisprudence on death-by-overwork as an extreme boundary. But also inject this newer distinction: 'idle entitlement', meaning leisure must include genuinely unstructured time that isnt repurposed for recovery, self-improvement, or employer-mandated wellness.

Write it as a checklist with ordered questions: what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where the procedure stops with a clear 'no violation' or 'red flag', and where it has to hand off to a human because its a values judgment not a fact. Give me a small set of test clauses I can apply at each step. Then run the procedure on three contracts:

1. a standard 40-hour contract with mandatory 'mindfulness breaks' counted inside the 40-hour cap.
2. a 'flex' contract with no hourly cap but a 'right to disconnect' clause that only applies after 10pm.
3. a gig-economy contract that pays per task but offers a 'rest bonus' if you take two consecutive days off.

Make sure one result is something I'd probably disagree with — maybe the mindfulness contract passes — and flag exactly where my judgment has to override the checklist. Also be explicit about what the procedure cant do, like measuring subjective burnout. Keep it practical: I have maybe 20 minutes per contract, I dont have legal training, and I need to sign off on the form myself.
