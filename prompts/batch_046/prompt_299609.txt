I've been trying to formalize a distinction between what I will call Kelly-style growth rationality and a more speculative framework I'm tentatively labeling geometric rationality, and I want to see how far the comparison can be pushed before it breaks. In Kelly betting, the rational agent allocates capital across uncertain outcomes to maximize the expected logarithm of wealth, which yields geometric-mean growth; the core normative commitment is multiplicative invariance—scaling all payoffs by a constant should not change the optimal allocation—and an exponential penalty on ruin. By contrast, geometric rationality, as I'm sketching it here, applies an analogous structural principle not to capital but to belief space: it treats an agent's credences as coordinates on a curved statistical manifold, and requires that rational belief revision follow geodesics that preserve the Fisher information metric, so that updates minimize path-length in information geometry rather than maximizing expected accuracy or satisfying standard Bayesian conditioning.

Compare these two frameworks in depth. Do they share a deeper normative root—perhaps a shared commitment to multiplicative invariance, or to preventing catastrophic divergence in some metric space—or is the analogy merely formal, relying on the coincidence that both invoke geometric structures? Which of the two offers a more defensible account of rational failure: does Kelly-style reasoning degrade gracefully when the payoff distribution is misspecified, and does geometric rationality fail analogously when the assumed manifold structure is wrong? If one were forced to unify them under a single normative theory—applying geometric growth rules to capital and geodesic update rules to belief—where does the synthesis collapse most quickly under standard decision-theoretic or epistemological objections, and which framework retains more independent justification? I am especially interested in whether geometric rationality inherits Kelly betting's well-known vulnerability to model misspecification, or whether the information-geometric framing introduces a distinct mode of failure that Kelly-style reasoning avoids entirely.
