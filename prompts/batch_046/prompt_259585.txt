Please argue that, in contexts where one party acts as an epistemic trustee—someone whose audience relies on their framing and selection of information rather than merely on the literal truth of each sentence—misleading by strategic omission or framing is morally worse than direct lying, not better. To do this, distinguish "content deception," which violates only a duty of literal honesty by asserting a falsehood, from "framework deception," which violates a separate duty of cognitive hospitality: the obligation not to exploit a listener's rational resource constraints by inducing them to construct a false overall picture from individually defensible claims. Your argument should defend the claim that framework deception corrupts the listener's autonomy more thoroughly than content deception, because it hijacks their own reasoning processes rather than merely supplying them with bad input. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
