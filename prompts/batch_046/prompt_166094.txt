I am presenting a seminar paper next week defending the view that moral consideration should be indexed not to behavioural output, biological substrate, or standard functionalist computation, but specifically to the presence of what I term counterfactual self-modelling under recursive metabolic constraint—a form of globally integrated predictive processing in which error-correction loops operate over representations of the system's own future states under resource limitation. I argue that this criterion excludes contemporary large language models, which lack the requisite architectural recursion, but includes certain distributed invertebrate nervous systems, and that it therefore implies a partial reallocation of welfare concern away from speculative artificial sentience and toward non-charismatic organisms that meet this structural condition.

The audience is a philosophy of mind and ethics seminar with the following composition: three philosophers of biology who favour evolutionary continuity and are wary of sharp metaphysical boundaries between taxa; two AI ethicists who defend behavioural or high-level functionalist criteria for attributing sentience to artificial systems; one cognitive scientist whose research centres on predictive processing but who is agnostic about its normative implications; and one philosopher of science who treats all such boundaries as pragmatically constructed rather than ontologically real. They care about empirical tractability, evolutionary plausibility, and avoiding both anthropocentrism and what they see as mysticism about consciousness.

Please prepare me for the question period. Give me the objections I will actually face, in roughly the order they will arise, phrased in the vocabulary and tone each subgroup would use. For each objection, indicate whether it is genuinely damaging to my position or mainly rhetorical; suggest what I should concede up front; provide a concise reply suitable for no more than two minutes when spoken; and flag the point in that reply that is weakest or most contestable. Identify the one objection I am most hoping nobody raises, explain why it is lethal, and draft the best damage-limitation response I can give without misrepresenting the settled science.

In your response, please use British English. Also, please clearly separate what is well-established in the relevant literature—such as the basic architecture of predictive processing, the evolutionary distribution of nociception, the absence of global workspace dynamics in current transformer-based architectures—from what remains genuinely contested, such as whether metabolic constraint is a necessary condition for moral status or whether sharp boundaries can ever be justified in a continuous biological world. Do not present any contested philosophical commitment of mine, particularly the normative weight I assign to recursive self-modelling, as though it were the consensus view.
