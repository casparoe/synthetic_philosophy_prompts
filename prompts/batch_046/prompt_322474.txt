Having spent a career in condensed-matter physics before turning recently to philosophy of mind, I find Hofstadter's claim that conceptual creativity is essentially the manipulation of knobs on a thematic structure—variations that preserve some deep invariant while altering surface features—intuitively powerful, yet I am unsure what conceptual or empirical obligations such an account actually incurs. Before I evaluate further defenses or criticisms, I want the full specification laid out.

Write the desiderata that any theory of conceptual creativity, if it is genuinely grounded in thematic variation rather than in retrieval, random recombination, or associative chaining, must satisfy. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or observation behind it—drawn from artistic practice, scientific discovery, or ordinary problem-solving—and indicate whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference of a particular camp. Among the requirements, include: whether the theme must be an explicit, representable structure or can remain implicit; whether all valid variations must be continuous deformations of that structure or whether abrupt restructuring—analogous to a symmetry-breaking phase transition—must also be accommodated; whether the theory must account for the phenomenology of insight as sudden restructuring rather than incremental adjustment; whether it must preserve a determinate mapping between knob-settings and generated concepts; and whether it must remain consistent with neurobiological constraints on working-memory capacity and representational density.

In particular, address whether the following subset of desiderata can be jointly satisfied: (i) the theme is fully explicit and representable; (ii) every valid creative move is a continuous deformation of that explicit theme; (iii) genuinely novel concepts arise precisely when a hidden symmetry of the theme is broken; and (iv) the generative process is deterministic given the initial knob-state. If some combination is jointly unsatisfiable—say, if explicit representability conflicts with symmetry-breaking novelty, or if determinism precludes the discontinuities required for insight—say so clearly and identify which tension is structural rather than merely technical.

Finally, score briefly how well Hofstadter's own knob-twiddling framework, as developed in his writings on analogy and concept-space navigation, fares against your list: which desiderata it meets naturally, which it meets only by stretching the metaphor, and which it appears to violate outright. Keep the weight on the criteria and their interrelations rather than on biographical summary; the aim is to know whether the framework can bear philosophical scrutiny, not to recount an author's oeuvre.
