You are preparing questions for a podcast or colloquium Q&A with a philosopher—call her Dr. Elara Voss—who defends a specific, unnamed view: a threshold-sensitive, scalar account of combatant moral equality. On her account, opposing combatants are equally liable to defensive harm only so long as the war’s cause clears a minimal justice threshold; as the injustice of the cause deepens, that equality erodes continuously rather than flipping discretely. She rejects both blanket equality for all combatants and the view that unjust combatants are individually analogous to culpable aggressors, grounding her position instead in a contractualist pact that binds participants only up to a justice-gradient ceiling.

Draft eight to ten formal interview questions that go well beyond having her restate the position. Your sequence must include:
- one question on how she calibrates the scalar threshold without letting it collapse into an act-utilitarian or case-by-case calculation of overall harm, plus a follow-up keyed to her likely appeal to institutional rules rather than battlefield arithmetic;
- one question on the status of non-combatants when combatant equality is gradual rather than binary—specifically whether civilians in deeply unjust wars acquire derivative protective rights that civilians in barely unjust wars lack, with a follow-up anticipating her distinction between direct and derivative liability;
- one question pressing the contractualist objection that unjust-combatants never genuinely consented to a pact binding them to equal liability, making her foundation fictitious for the unjust side, followed by a reply that targets her probable move to tacit or role-based consent;
- one question pressing her response to the McMahan-style analogy between unjust combatants and culpable aggressors in interpersonal self-defense, with a follow-up keyed to her expected answer that collective authorization dilutes individual culpability.

Keep the tone scholarly and adversarial in the philosophical sense: each question should turn on the actual content of her view, anticipate the standard reply, and include the sharp follow-up. Do not include generic biographical or motivational questions. Think carefully about which feature of her scalar-contractualist framework does the heavy lifting in each case, and make sure your questions expose whether that feature can bear the weight.
