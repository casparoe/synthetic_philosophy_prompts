What's frustrating is that it feels like the same moves have been replayed since the nineties with zero convergence. The illusionists keep saying introspecion is basically a kind of self-monitoring that misrepresents representational states as having some extra qualitative glow, and that once you see the mechanism there's nothing left to explain. The realists reply that this just confuses the vehicle with the content—denying phenomenal feel is like denying you're in pain, and that the illusionist has only explained away the report, not the thing being reported.

My own hunch is that the stall isn't about consciousness at all, but about a hidden picture both sides share: call it the Cartesian screen model, where if phenomenal consciousness is real it has to be like a private display that's immediately observed, and illusionism works only if you prove the screen is blank while realism works only if you prove there's an image on it. So maybe they're answering different questions under one name—one asking whether a non-representational qualitative given exists, the other asking whether first-person reports carry a special epistemic authority that needs explaining—or maybe it's verbal, because "phenominal consciousness" for the realist just is whatever survives that direct-observation test, and for the illusionist it's the exact same theoretical construct shown not to exist.
