A study-group handout summarizes Augustine's position on lying as follows: Augustine holds that every lie is sinful without exception, even to save innocent lives, because lying fractures the alignment between the speaker's inner word of the heart (verbum cordis) and the spoken word (verbum vocis), damaging the soul's orientation toward Truth regardless of consequences. He defines a lie as intentionally affirming what one believes false, distinguishing it sharply from mere error or silence. He permits withholding information and even speaking in ways that allow the listener to draw a false conclusion on their own, provided the speaker's heart never assents to falsehood; but he rejects mental reservation in the strict casuistical sense, since deliberately directing another's misunderstanding remains a form of deception. In his reply to Jerome on Galatians 2:11-14, Augustine denies that Paul practiced dissimulation (simulatio) toward Peter at Antioch, arguing instead that Paul exercised legitimate pastoral discretion—adapting his conduct to his audience without ever interiorly affirming a false proposition. The handout concludes that Augustine's ethics is therefore not the caricatured rigorism that demands truthful speech at the cost of innocent lives; since silence and contextual ambiguity are fully permitted, the standard objection—that he cares more for the speaker's purity than for the victim's survival—misses its mark, because he never obligates anyone to speak in situations where deception would be forced.
