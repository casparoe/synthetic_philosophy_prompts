I am directing a focused research seminar on metaphysics and causation, with about one semester and a group of graduate students whose strengths lie in logic and analytic metaphysics rather than in the history of Islamic philosophy. Our point of departure is al-Ghazālī's occasionalism, but I want us to avoid the well-trodden comparison with Humean skepticism or Malebranchean parallelism. Instead, I am interested in a tension that seems underexplored: if every particular occurrence is immediately produced by divine volition, then the observed stability of natural correlations might be read in at least two incompatible ways—either as a brute, continuously renewed regularity sustained by constant divine choice, or as an epistemic projection that masks a uniform causal structure in which all secondary-cause discourse is reducible without remainder to primary-cause discourse, a view one might call causal ascription universalism. Before we can assess either reading, however, we need an agenda. What are the two or three most important open questions or lines of conceptual work that turn on resolving this tension? Please rank them in order of priority for our group, and defend the ordering with explicit criteria: tractability given our background in logic and metaphysics rather than Arabic philology, which questions are upstream of others in the sense that settling them unlocks further work, and what kind of intermediate result over the next several months would cause you to reorder them. I want a defended research agenda, not a survey of occasionalism scholarship, not a bibliography, and not a list of recommended readings.
