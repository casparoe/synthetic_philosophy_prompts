I'm taking a course on economic philosophy and we got onto Keynes — not the macro stuff but that little essay where he imagines people working about fifteen hours a week and spending the rest on living well. Someone in the seminar mentioned that we technically could have done that now with AI and automation, but instead we've ended up with this weird hybrid where loads of people do long hours in knowledge jobs that feel mostly like admin, then spend whatever free time is left on apps that harvest attention so the leasure isn't even restful. There's this idea I've come across called 'playbour', where your hobbies and relaxation literally produce data value for platforms, and it made me wonder: if Keynes were alive today and saw that we solved the productivity problem he talked about but then traped the surplus inside algorithmic feeds, gig-work side hustles, and constant 'digital detox' retreats that you have to pay for, what do you think he'd say about whether we've actually reached the good life he meant? Would he see the four-day-week trials in Iceland and Spain as the realisation of his vision, or would he think we've invented a new kind of scarcity — attention scarcity — that just makes the old economic problem mutate into something psychological? I'm not after a lecture on Keynesian policy, just a guess at his philosophical reaction: would he be optimistic, or would he think we'd fundamentaly misunderstood what leisure is actually for?
