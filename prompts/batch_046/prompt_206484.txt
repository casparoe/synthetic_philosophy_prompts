I'm defending the interpretation that Zhuangzi's reflections on death—particularly in the episodes commonly called the butterfly dream and the cook Ding, and in the passages on the equality of life and death—should not be read as a metaphysics of energetic transformation, nor as an ethics of equanimity borrowed from later traditions, but as a therapeutic dismantling of the narrative self. I claim that death appears as a loss only for a subject that frames its existence as a biographical trajectory sustained by what the text describes as a completed heart (cheng xin), and that Zhuangzi's exercises aim to dissolve that framing rather than to console the subject within it.
