i run a grad ethics seminar, about 12 students, one 75-min session. i want them to physically feel the paradox of deontology — specifically the part where a rigid constraint against direct harm ends up licensing way more harm through inaction when the scenario scales, not just the basic trolley intuition.

design an in-class simulation/game. each student gets a constraint card (e.g. "you may not physically restrain anyone", "you may not seize property", etc.) and there are crisis cards describing emergencies where breaking their own constraint saves five people but violating one. heres the niche twist to inject: theres a hidden "replication envelope" mechanism. whatever the group decides in round 1 is secretly applied to four parallel scenarios they dont see, so if they collectively refuse to violate anyones constraint, they "allow" 5 deaths in each hidden world — 25 total — without direct agency. if they do violate, they cause 5 direct violations across the worlds. theres also a delegation token that lets one student ask another to perform an action on their behalf, which should make them scramble to distribute blame before they realize the system is rigged.

need: materials list, exact round rules, a small-stakes payoff scheme (points or candy), the debrief that maps the delegation scramble and the envelope reveal onto the formal paradox, a note on what will go wrong first run (theyll probably overfixate on the token and miss the scaling), and an honest admission of which feature of the real philosophical problem the game cant capture — probably the actual moral phenomenology of real harm vs simulated tokens.

write this casual, like an email to a colleague, maybe with a typo or two.
