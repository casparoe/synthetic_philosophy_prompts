I've been thinking about structural realism, the view that science reveals only the structure of reality and not the intrinsic nature of the entities that fill that structure. The usual defence appeals to the history of science: equations survive theory change while interpretations of the entities do not. I want to argue for a different foundation. Please make the case that structural realism is better defended through a modal claim about invariant patterns across all possible measurements, rather than through historical continuity. Specifically, argue that if structural commitments are understood as constraints on what any observer could register, then the so-called Newman problem — that purely structural descriptions can be satisfied by arbitrarily different collections of objects — does not require a separate observational layer to solve it, but dissolves: there is no residual question about the intrinsic nature of the relata because the modal framework already determines everything that needs determination. Present this as a positive argument, not a survey of the literature, and tell me whether this modal defence genuinely avoids the problem or merely relocates it.
