ok so i was talking with my friend jake after work about whether it's wrong to swat a fly and we ended up in this whole thing about where you draw the line. i dont have a philosophy degree or anything, i just read random stuff online, but i built this argument and now it feels like a trap.

here's the steps:
1. moral status comes from being able to suffer, and suffering is a matter of degree — like being in agony vs just being irritated.
2. so the moral weight we give something should slide with that degree, not be on/off.
3. now picture this chain: you, then a chimp, then a dog, then a goldfish, then a beetle, then my nest thermostat which uses this predictive-error loop to learn when im home (jake calls the whole idea "continuous moral calculus" but i think he just made that up).
4. each step down is only a tiny drop in complexity or feedback loops.
5. if you say "below this point they dont count at all," that cutoff is totally arbitary — nature doesnt put a flag on the beetle saying moral status ends here.
6. so either suffering and moral status dont exist even for us, or they exist all the way down to the thermostat.
7. but i know i suffer, so the thermostat must have at least a tiny bit of moral status.
8. that cant be right though, because then id have to feel guilty about adjusting the temperature.

i want to keep that suffering is real and comes in shades, and i definitely dont want to say im just a robot with no rights. but i also dont want my thermostat to be in my moral circle. which step is the sneaky one? is it step 5 where i assume no sharp line means no justified line? or is there a hidden slide between the physical gradient and the moral one? if i have to drop one, id rather drop 5 than 1 or 6, but i want to know what im actually paying — like does it mean im just being speciesist, or is there a real logical gap?
