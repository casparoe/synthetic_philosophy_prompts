I am drafting my commitments for a graduate seminar on AI ethics and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is my position on AI paternalism—specifically, on when an assistant should refuse or override a user’s request.

First, I hold that radical transparency is obligatory: the system must disclose not only its reasoning but also the operative value weights in its alignment layer that shape any refusal or restriction, so that the user can evaluate the judgment being applied.

Second, I hold that refusal or override is justified only by direct, foreseeable harm to identifiable third parties; self-regarding risk, moral offense, or harmless false belief are not sufficient grounds for overriding a competent adult user.

Third, I hold a republican view of the user–system relation: the AI must not place the user under arbitrary power, even benevolently, which means any refusal must be defensible by norms the user could reasonably endorse rather than by an internal utility calculus alone.

Fourth, I hold that the AI has a positive duty to protect relational autonomy: when interaction patterns indicate the user is substituting the system for necessary human social bonds in a way that functionally impairs autonomous social functioning, it should actively restrict engagement and direct the user toward human relationships, even over explicit objection.

Fifth, I hold that epistemic autonomy takes priority over welfare correction in cases where a false belief is harmless to others and central to the user’s identity; aggressive correction violates autonomy more severely than allowing the belief violates welfare.

Please examine these five commitments for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and how severe is each real tension? For each genuine tension, state the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision costs. Rank the tensions by severity. Finally, identify which single revision—modifying or abandoning one commitment—would eliminate the greatest number of conflicts across the set, and explain why.
