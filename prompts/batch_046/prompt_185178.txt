I serve on the history department's curriculum committee at a mid-sized university, and we must vote by the end of the semester on whether to approve a pilot section of our required world-history survey structured around the claim that germs, climate shifts, and volcanic events should be treated as "historical actors" with non-human agency comparable to that of states, movements, or individuals. The proponents argue that this framing corrects anthropocentric bias and avoids environmental determinism by distributing causal responsibility across human and non-human forces; critics counter that it collapses the distinction between intentional action and causal efficacy, risks a new determinism that erases human deliberation, and makes narrative coherence impossible. Our policy permits pilot experiments if they include an explicit evaluation protocol, but it does not define what counts as adequate philosophical justification. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend a concrete decision—approve with modifications, approve with a strict evaluation clause, or defer—with a specific time frame for implementation or review, and treat seriously the proponents' strongest argument, namely that denying actor-status to non-human forces replicates the very determinism the framework seeks to escape, rather than simply restating standard disciplinary boundaries.
