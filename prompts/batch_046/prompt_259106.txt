I am preparing a handout for an advanced philosophy of well-being seminar and want a decision tree that sorts positions by how they answer four concrete diagnostic questions, rather than by the usual labels. The tree should begin with whether one accepts Nozick's experience machine as a decisive counterexample to hedonism, then branch on: (i) whether a permanently plugged-in subject who falsely believes she is composing a symphony is better off than a real-world composer writing poor music; (ii) whether the procreation asymmetry — that there is no obligation to create happy people, but there is an obligation not to create suffering ones — applies analogously to the decision to enter the machine; (iii) whether well-being requires mental states to stand in a causal relation to actual states of affairs, or whether narrative coherence suffices; and (iv) whether a being with no conscious experience whatsoever could possess well-being if it instantiated an objective list of goods. Each leaf should be named where a recognised position exists (for example, classical hedonism, authenticity-conditional hedonism, narrative-desire hybrid, strong objectivism, impersonal consequentialism) or marked 'unnamed'; every leaf must include one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate, and indicate whether any empty branch represents an incoherent combination or merely an unexplored one. Please write in British English, and clearly separate what is settled in the specialist literature from what remains genuinely contested; do not present any contested branch as the consensus view.
