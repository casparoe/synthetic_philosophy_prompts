As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun reading philosophy of mathematics and science, I have become preoccupied with a structural pattern that recurs whenever an actually infinite collection is treated as a completed totality rather than as an open-ended process. The pattern first struck me in the familiar lodging thought-experiment: imagine chambers numbered 1, 2, 3, and so on, with every chamber occupied. Despite being fully occupied, the lodging can accept one more arrival if each present occupant is reassigned to the chamber immediately adjacent, freeing chamber 1. The cardinal number of occupants does not change, yet the occupancy state has been altered without any corresponding displacement of physical substance—only a relabeling of positions. I then encountered a related result in set theory, where the collection of all subsets of an infinite set is strictly larger than the set itself, so that enumerating every member still does not exhaust the structure. More recently, while reviewing the formalism of statistical mechanics, I noticed that if one treats a system with a genuinely infinite discrete energy spectrum as a completed object, one can construct a transformation that shifts every occupied state upward by one quantum level, thereby “making room” at the bottom—much like the lodging case—while leaving the total energy of the system either undefined or infinite. In each case, the same feature appears: an infinite totality that is complete in the sense that every element is accounted for, yet non-exhaustive in the sense that an internal rearrangement can generate a vacancy without removing any element and without changing the cardinality. This is not merely the potential-actual distinction, since the totality is actual; nor is it exactly a logical paradox, since the reasoning is formally valid. It also differs from an ordinary scientific auxiliary hypothesis, because the accommodation is not an empirical correction but a direct consequence of the domain’s infinity. I am trying to determine whether philosophy of mathematics or metaphysics of science possesses an established name for this specific configuration—an actually infinite, completed collection that permits internal accommodation without cardinal, energetic, or material change—and, if so, how it is precisely delineated from the lodging paradox, Cantor’s comparison of cardinalities, and finitist objections that reject completed infinities altogether. If no single term exists, I would welcome a careful carving of the phenomenon’s boundaries: what distinguishes it from a mere limit process, from an equivocation about size, and from a physically illegitimate idealization?
