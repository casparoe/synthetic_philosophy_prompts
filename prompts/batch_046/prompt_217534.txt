I hold that valid consent requires not only competence, absence of coercion, and truthful disclosure, but also freedom from "structural framing capture" — a condition in which the institutional or informational architecture makes the consenter's evaluation of alternatives depend on conceptual categories or dependency relations supplied by the party seeking consent, so that the consenter cannot represent the choice in genuinely independent terms. On this view, standard informed-consent protocols can fail even when all disclosed facts are true and no threat is present, because framing capture remains intact. What would count against this position? I want the decisive versus merely awkward evidence and arguments, including what defenders of thinner disclosure-only accounts would concede, which institutional realities in clinical or contractual settings would undermine the framing-capture diagnosis, and whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability by expanding "framing" to cover every influence. Separate what is settled about consent conditions — such as the invalidating role of explicit coercion and basic incompetence — from what is genuinely contested about structural framing and epistemic dependency, and do not present any contested philosophical account as the consensus.
