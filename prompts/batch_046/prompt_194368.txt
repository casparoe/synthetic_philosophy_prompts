Here is roughly where I've landed: (a) I think work is partly constitutive of human dignity — not just a paycheck, but a way of being recognized and recognizing yourself as contributing to something shared; (b) I'm strongly in favor of pushing automation as hard as possible in dangerous, repetitive, or degrading jobs, even if it wipes out whole categories of employment, because no one should be forced into toil that strips them of autonomy; (c) at the same time, I believe society has an obligation to guarantee that everyone can access genuinely meaningful work, and that a universal basic income alone wouldn't solve the problem — people still need the structure and social recognition that comes from contributing; (d) I also think that once AI can do creative or intellectual labor — writing music, designing, composing — we should deliberately preserve spaces for humans to do those things, even if the machine output is technically superior, because there's a distinct value in human authorship; (e) the reason contribution matters to me is relational: work connects you to other people's needs and embeds you in a shared project; and (f) I don't think anyone should be forced to work — the right to meaningful work has to be compatible with genuinely voluntary participation.

Do these actually cohere? I'm especially suspicious of (b) and (c) together, and maybe (d) and (e), but I'm not sure if those are real tensions or just look like it.
