Hey, I'm prepping notes for my grad seminar on vagueness and think I've talked myself into a circle. Epistemicism (Williamson-style) says vagueness is ignorance: there's a sharp cutoff for "significant contribution"—say, exactly 17 publications—only we can't know it. Supervaluationism denies any single cutoff, just a range of admissable precisificaitons. But then if a tenure committee applies supervaluationist logic and demands a candidate clear every admissable precisificaiton, haven't they effectively placed a sharp boundary at the conservative end? So isn't supervaluationism in practice just epistemicism with risk-averse policy layered on? I can't see where the metaphyiscal distinction survives once you leave pure language for institutional rules. What's the actual error here?
