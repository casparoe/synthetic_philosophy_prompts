I'm preparing the framework for my philosophy-of-mathematics dissertation chapter and need a consistency audit before I present it. My commitments are these: (a) ontological finitism about nature—there are no actual infinities in the physical world, only potential infinity in the sense of indefinite extendability; (b) acceptance of Cantor's power-set theorem as valid, and the view that it demonstrates an open-ended, strictly ascending sequence of cardinalities with no maximal element within any given domain, though I withhold judgment on whether this licenses talk of one completed totality containing "all" infinite cardinals; (c) the claim that Hilbert's hotel functions solely as an intuition pump showing the internal consistency of actual-infinity concepts, carrying no ontological weight about existence; (d) the interpretation of quantum field theory in which the ultraviolet cutoff is physically real rather than a mere calculational artifact, so that nature never actually realizes infinitely many degrees of freedom and renormalization marks a genuine ontological boundary; (e) a restricted Quine-Putnam indispensability principle—ontological commitment extends only to those mathematical entities genuinely indispensable to our best empirical theories, not to every object quantified over in pure mathematics; and (f) set-theoretic potentialism, holding that the universe of sets is never completed but is always open to further extension, which I adopt partly for reasons of ontological parsimony.

Please audit this set for joint tenability. Which commitments pull against each other genuinely, which only appear to, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Rank the tensions by severity, specifying for each whether the revision should fall on (a), (d), (e), (f), or on a reinterpretation of (b), and what that revision costs the overall position. Finally, identify at least one apparent tension that dissolves on closer inspection and explain the dissolution, so that the audit distinguishes real conflicts from illusory ones rather than flagging every pairing as problematic.
