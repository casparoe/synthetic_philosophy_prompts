Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy of positions on whether transcendental arguments can establish substantive metaphysical or normative conclusions about rational agency, as opposed to merely clarifying conceptual commitments. The root question should be whether there exists any non-circular derivation of a reality-claim from a condition of possibility of thought. From there, branch on whether the argument requires a "thick" conception of the agent (embodied, socially situated, historically embedded) or can operate with a purely formal or "thin" conception. A further branch should separate those who read the conclusion as synthetic a priori (yielding positive ontology) from those who read it as deflationary anti-skeptical clarification (ruling out self-refutation without constructing reality). At each leaf, provide the standard label if one exists—such as thick constitutivism, thin formal transcendentalism, deflationary conceptualism, or unnamed—together with one sentence stating the view's most awkward commitment (for instance, having to deny that disembodied or artificial agents fall under the argument's scope, or being unable to distinguish transcendental reasoning from ordinary conceptual analysis). Note any unoccupied branches and briefly diagnose why they are empty: incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored. The instrument should sort a respondent by what they believe about these concrete distinctions rather than by which label they prefer, and it should not depend on knowledge of specific texts or authors.
