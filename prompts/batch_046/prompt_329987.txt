Since receiving a diagnosis of chronic neuropathic pain last autumn, I have been trying to formalise my philosophy of well-being. My current position comprises five commitments: (1) well-being consists solely in experiential quality, with no independent non-hedonic goods; (2) I accept the heterogeneity problem—gustatory, aesthetic, and somatic pleasures appear to share no common phenomenal feature; (3) nevertheless, I think public policy can legitimately employ a cardinal, interpersonally comparable scale of pleasure built from structured experience-sampling; (4) I regard phenomenal concepts as irreducibly first-personal and not entailed by any functional or physical description; and (5) I hold that a life of intense but phenomenally disparate pleasures is superior, other things being equal, to one of uniform mild pleasure with an identical integral. Do these form a coherent package, or do some pull against others? Please respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: identify each tension, rank them by severity, state which commitment must be revised, and specify the single cheapest revision that restores overall consistency.
