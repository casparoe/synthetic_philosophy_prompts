Exam question:
Nozick's experience machine is typically interpreted as an objection to hedonism: we value being genuinely connected to reality and actually performing actions, not merely experiencing the appearance of them. Now consider a modified case—the opaque-source machine—which generates precisely the same subjective experiences as Nozick's original, except that the content of the simulation is causally fixed by the subject's actual past actions in the real world (for instance, a career choice made years earlier determines which simulated professional trajectory unfolds). The subject, however, has no awareness of this causal linkage. Some philosophers suggest that this variant preserves "causal authorship" even while it eliminates direct reality-contact. Does the opaque-source machine undermine Nozick's objection? In answering, clearly separate (i) the value of direct reality-contact, (ii) the value of causal authorship of one's experiences, and (iii) whether a hidden causal chain can substitute for the first.

Student answer:
Nozick's experience machine demonstrates that hedonism is inadequate because people care about living in reality and actually doing things, not just feeling good. The opaque-source machine restores causal authorship—your real past choices determine the simulation—while still withholding direct reality-contact. This reveals that Nozick's objection actually depends on two independent conditions: being the cause of your life and being in unmediated contact with the world. In the opaque-source case, you satisfy the first but not the second. Because the causal link is hidden, you are also deceived about your situation, which makes the case worse rather than better. Consequently, if people would still reject this machine, it cannot be because they lack authorship; they must be demanding direct experiential access to reality. So the objection survives in a sharper form: well-being requires both real-world causation and conscious awareness of direct contact, and hedonism fails on both counts.
