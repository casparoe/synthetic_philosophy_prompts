Argue that the American folk-moral emphasis on individual conscience over explicit rule-following is self-undermining, not because rules matter more than conscience, but because conscience can only operate as a critical check on social order when that order is visibly externalised rather than internalised as personal preference. Contrast the German practice of treating traffic regulations and bureaucratic procedure as impersonal structures that one obeys regardless of private judgment with the American tendency to interpret compliance as a personal moral choice—for instance, “I stop at stop signs because I am a responsible person”. Your argument should explain why externalising rules preserves a sharper distinction between ordinary obligation and genuine moral exception, and should address the objection that this model reduces citizens to unthinking automata. Build the case from the conceptual structure of the two moral attitudes alone; do not appeal to historical sources or ask for citations.
