I am designing a 40-minute philosophy seminar for second-year undergraduates who have already encountered Paley's watchmaker argument but have not studied Turing. The session is titled "Competence without comprehension: Darwin's and Turing's strange inversions of reasoning." I want students to see that Darwin inverts the inference from adaptive design to a reasoning designer—natural selection produces functional complexity without foresight, intention, or understanding—while Turing inverts the inference from intelligent behavior to an internal mind, proposing that behavioral competence can be mechanized without establishing comprehension inside the mechanism. To bridge the two, I want to introduce Turing's concept of "unorganized machines"—networks that learn through random variation and selection—as a case where Darwinian logic is literally implemented inside a computational system, so that evolutionary computation becomes the hinge between biological and artificial competence.
