Design a final-exam essay question, together with a model answer of roughly 700 words and a four-part marking scheme, for an upper-level seminar on the philosophy of archaeology and the epistemology of material inference. The prompt must not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Frame the question around the following self-contained hypothetical: a late-Neolithic burial assemblage contains standardized ceramic vessels bearing uniform geometric markings, yet entirely lacks differentiated grave goods, personal ornamentation, or spatial segregation of interments—features usually read as correlates of hierarchy and individuated identity. Require examinees to determine whether this evidence supports an inference to an egalitarian communal institution, and compel them to address the specific problem of negative archaeological inference—whether the absence of hierarchical material correlates justifies an inference to the absence of hierarchical belief or social structure, or whether this commits an argumentum ex silentio fallacy in the archaeological context. The question should be unanswerable by mere summary of inference-to-the-best-explanation and should require engagement with: (1) the underdetermination of institutional hypotheses by object-traces; (2) the epistemic asymmetry between positive and negative material evidence; (3) a comparative assessment of minimalist interpretations (withholding institutional claims) versus maximalist interpretations (positing concealed or non-materialized hierarchy); and (4) the general limits of reconstructing mental states and social structures from artifacts. The model answer should explicitly deploy these four moves. The marking scheme should allocate marks as follows—framing the inferential problem (6 marks), analyzing negative inference and the silentio risk (6 marks), evaluating minimalist versus maximalist readings (6 marks), and critically reflecting on the mental-state/object gap (7 marks)—with a one-line description of the typical mistake that loses marks in each category, for a total of 25 marks.
