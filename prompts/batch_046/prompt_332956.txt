Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, with two refrains repeating according to the classic pattern—taking up the specific metaphysical position of Zhuangzi on death: that death is not the negation of life but one transformation (hua) in the continuous circulation of qi, and that the self is a temporary convergence rather than a persistent substance. The first refrain should evoke the butterfly dream—the epistemic instability between waking and dreaming that undermines any fixed claim to be the real self—while the second refrain should evoke the bellows of heaven, the hollow center through which all forms pass and sound, representing death as a change of position within an inexhaustible flow rather than annihilation. Structure the poem so that its formal turn (the shift into the closing quatrain where both refrains appear together) coincides exactly with the philosophical turn from observing transformation to recognizing that grief depends on a false attachment to form. Before that turn, the tercets should accumulate images of seasonal change, bodily dissolution, and the dissolution of boundaries between self and other; after the turn, the interwoven refrains should leave unresolved whether the speaker has achieved the equanimity Zhuangzi describes or has lost the distinction between acceptance and indifference. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader of the Inner Chapters recognizes the metaphysics—rejection of a fixed self, equality of life and death, the uselessness of ritual mourning—without relying on direct quotation or chapter citation, and it should stand as a poem for a reader who knows none of this. Do not resolve the tension with a sermon or a shrug; let the form hold the contradiction open.
