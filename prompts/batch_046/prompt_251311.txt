Two members of a philosophy study group argued over moral particularism versus generalism after discussing a concrete case: in one neighborhood conflict, returning a borrowed tool immediately restored trust, while in a structurally similar conflict, the same prompt return deepened resentment because it signaled dismissal of an apology still owed. (1) Marcus argued that particularism is just hidden generalism: if the particularist praises prompt return in the first case but condemns it in the second, there must be some feature F—say, the presence of an unpaid emotional debt—that distinguishes them, and F functions as a highly specific moral principle in disguise. (2) Elena replied that the particularist does not treat F as a portable reason; rather, in the second case the very feature "restoring material fairness" is present but its moral valence is inverted by the holistic configuration of the situation—what she called the case's irreducible moral shape—without being overridden by a separate countervailing principle. (3) Marcus said this "inversion by shape" is merely a complex general rule about when material fairness flips its significance, articulable in principle if one works hard enough. (4) Elena countered that a genuine principle must be extractable and applicable across cases independently of the specific relational web in which it sits, whereas the particularist's judgment depends on weights and relations that cannot be lifted out without destroying the judgment itself; she added that Marcus's demand for extraction is itself the generalist prejudice. They could not agree on whether "irreducible shape" is a real distinction or a relabeled principle. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right with brief justification, say whether Elena's notion of irreducible moral shape genuinely differs from a complex principle or merely renames one, identify what both parties miss about the burden of proof in this dispute, and state who is more right overall, noting the strongest reply available to the defender of the weaker position.
