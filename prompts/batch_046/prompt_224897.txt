Consider the canonical Sleeping Beauty problem: a fair coin is flipped on Sunday. If it lands Heads, Beauty is awakened and interviewed exactly once, on Monday. If it lands Tails, she is awakened and interviewed on Monday and again on Tuesday, with her memory of the Monday awakening erased before Tuesday's; she knows the full protocol. When awakened and asked her credence that the coin landed Heads, the thirder answer is 1/3 and the halfer answer is 1/2.

Give me your own credence—expressed as a specific number between 0 and 1—that the thirder answer is correct, and that this result genuinely reflects the logic of self-locating belief rather than being an artifact of misapplied conditioning. Decompose your reasoning into the following terms, each with a rough probability and one line of justification:

(a) That Beauty's prior credence in Heads should be 1/2 and that the coin's objective chance is indeed 1/2.

(b) That centered (de se) propositions—such as "it is now Monday"—can be coherently incorporated into Bayesian updating via conditioning on centered worlds without distorting the underlying objective chance.

(c) That Beauty's awakening supplies genuinely new epistemic information—specifically, indexical information about her temporal location—that rationally lowers her credence in Heads from 1/2 to 1/3, despite her gaining no new non-indexical evidence about the coin.

(d) That the halfer objection—she learns nothing about the coin, so she should not update—fails specifically because it neglects the epistemic relevance of self-location, rather than failing for some unrelated reason.

(e) That there is no hidden selection effect, protocol ambiguity, or observer-bias confound (for example, ambiguity about which awakening she is currently experiencing) that undermines the 1/3 calculation.

Show how these terms combine into your overall number, then identify which term your final credence is most sensitive to. Finally, specify what argument or formal result—say, a rigorous demonstration that indexical information cannot affect credences in non-indexical propositions, or a formalization of centered-world conditioning that resolves the ambiguity—would move that term, and therefore your overall answer, the most. Do not retreat to "it depends on your framework"; defend a single number with its weak points marked.
