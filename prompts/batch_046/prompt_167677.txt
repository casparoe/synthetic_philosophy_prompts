ok so my study group has two people who've been fighting for months about free-floating rationales -- reasons without reasoners. one side is the realist camp: they think normative reasons are just out there in the structure of situations, like math but for action, and they don't need any agent to actually hold them. the other side is the agent-constructivist camp: they say a reason is always for someone, and if there's no reasoner then you've just got descriptive facts, not a real ought.

they want an adversarial collaboration document but they're stuck on wroding. draft the joint statement they should have produced.

format:
1. numbered agreed-claims section -- stuff both sign, like that reasons guide action, that we can genuinely disagree about them, that physical laws don't depend on belief, maybe that extinct languages don't change physics. keep it non-vacuous.
2. disagreements table -- each position in neutral wording so both sides recognize thier own view, separated from verbal disputes about "objective" vs "real" or "independent" vs "mind-made"
3. cruxes -- i think the big ones are (a) whether moral learning can happen without a teacher-agent present, and (b) whether normative claims recovered from a corrupted archive with no living author bind anyone who decodes them. for each crux include a concrete case both agree is relevant (maybe an orphaned algorithm that outputs imperatives with no programmer, or undeciphered proto-language directives found in ruins) and record each camp's prediction in advance about what we'd observe or whether obligation arises.
4. make sure neither side is a strawman. don't ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists -- a model without web access can't do that honestly. inject something weird/niche so it's not pure regurgitation from standard metaethics textbooks. keep it in the weeds but don't rely on super obscure named theories a small llm wouldn't have memorized.
