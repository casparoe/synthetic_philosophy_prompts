Lately I see people extend it straight to algorithimic feeds: TikTok and YouTube are basically accelerated natural selection for memes, so the Darwinian framework should cover viral culture with no extra machinery. The conclusion they draw is that online cultural change is just Darwinian evolution running faster, and that talk about intentional design or Lamarckian inheritance is a red herring since the algorithm is just the enviroment selecting.

Can you audit how far that holds? Give me the explicit mapping first — what's the gene, what's the phenotype, what's the selection pressure, what's the replicator — then the breaks. I especially want where the analogy quietly smuggles in assumptions about blindness or lack of foresight that fail when engineers literally tune engagement metrics. Does the conclusion that it's all just Darwinian selection survive once you account for the designer-in-the-loop? And is there a better analogy — maybe directed breeding or artificial selection — that captures the engineered selection enviroment without collapsing back into the gene metaphor?
