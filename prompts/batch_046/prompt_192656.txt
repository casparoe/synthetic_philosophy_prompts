Compare the standard Kripke-style origin essentialism — the idea that you are essentially tied to that exact sperm and egg pair — with a view I'll call "developmental trajectroy essentialism," which holds that what fixes your identity is the continuous biological process from conception onward, not the particular gametes themselves. To pressure-test them, imagine a wierd two-cell-stage swap: one blastomere from your actual zygote is replaced by one from a different zygote formed from entirely different gametes, yet the embryo still develops into someone with your psychology and body. The trajectroy view might allow that you could have come from those other gametes since the continuous living line was preserved; the origin view should deny that, but then it owes an explanation of why the swapped cell doesn't change who you are. Which view handles the swap more cleanly? And does trajectroy essentialism secretly collapse into origin essentialism once you notice that changing the gametes usually breaks the trajectroy anyway?
