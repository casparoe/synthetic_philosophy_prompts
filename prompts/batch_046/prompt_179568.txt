A colleague offers a reductio of the idea that scientific thought experiments yield distinctive knowledge. His chain runs: (1) For reasoning to yield knowledge about contingent physical facts, every premise that does distinctive work must be either empirically justified or necessarily true. (2) In a thought experiment, the distinctive premises describe imagined scenarios—an elevator in free fall, two bodies released in a vacuum—that are not actually observed, so they are not empirically justified. (3) Hence those distinctive premises must be necessary conceptual truths about space, motion, or causation. (4) From necessary premises alone, only necessary conclusions follow. (5) Yet conclusions like the equivalence principle or Galileo’s law of falling bodies are contingent synthetic claims. (6) So, taken in isolation, a thought experiment can never yield the contingent knowledge it is credited with. (7) The only escape is that it implicitly relies on unstated empirical premises—prior knowledge of gravitation, inertia, or material behavior. (8) But then the imaginative framing is epistemically inert: all the knowledge comes from the hidden empirical premises, and the scenario is just rhetorical packaging. (9) Therefore either scientific thought experiments contribute nothing distinctive, or we reject (1) and allow that pure conceptual reasoning—unaided by observation—can establish contingent physical truths. (10) But if armchair reasoning can establish that inertial and gravitational mass are equivalent, there is no principled barrier to establishing by reflection alone the value of the cosmological constant, the existence of dark matter, or the structure of DNA. He concludes that philosophers who praise thought experiments are either smuggling in empiricism without admitting it, or committing to an implausible rationalism. I want to preserve both that Einstein’s elevator genuinely contributed something and, if possible, that (1) holds. Which step should I reject? What does rejecting it cost, and does it block the reductio in (10) without rendering the imaginative framing redundant?
