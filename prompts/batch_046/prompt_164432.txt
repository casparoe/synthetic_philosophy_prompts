ok heres my rough profile on the value-of-knowledge thing (the Meno problem) for our seminar. I beleive:
(a) knowledge is more valuable than true belief because its tethered—tied down by reasoning—so that stability itself matters.
(b) justifcation is purely instrumental; its only good because it tends to yield true belief, not valuable on its own.
(c) true belief is the only epistemic end-state with final value—everything else is just a means.
(d) I think even in Gettier cases where you have justified true belief youre missing epistemic credit—and thats exactly what makes knowledge more valuable than lucky true belief.
(e) I reject virtue epistemology—knowing shouldnt depend on your intellectual character or cognitive achievments.
