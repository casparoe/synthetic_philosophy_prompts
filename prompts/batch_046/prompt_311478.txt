I am a professor of moral philosophy, and next Thursday I am presenting a seminar paper defending what I term “quietist axiology”: the thesis that in ethical evaluation only the absence of craving (taṇhā) and the cessation of suffering (dukkha) carry intrinsic weight, whereas all apparently positive goods—pleasure, achievement, knowledge, and even the cultivated mental states often described as flourishing or eudaimonia—are at best instrumentally valuable and, more often, disguised forms of attachment (upādāna) that perpetuate the very dynamics they claim to transcend. I develop this not as a variant of negative utilitarianism but through a secular reading of Theravāda commentarial traditions that treats nibbāna not as a positive bliss-state but as the complete extinguishing of affective disturbance, coupled with the argument that any positive valuation introduces a comparative metric that necessarily regenerates craving. The audience will be a mixed philosophy seminar: three value theorists committed to pluralist or perfectionist consequentialism, one Buddhist-studies scholar who accepts the centrality of dukkha but insists that the Noble Eightfold Path requires the active cultivation of positive mental factors (the brahmavihāras), a virtue ethicist for whom eudaimonia is non-negotiable, and two graduate students who have recently engaged with contemporary discussions of negative utilitarianism and are suspicious of any view that sounds like it collapses into it.

I need a speaker’s Q&A prep sheet—not a general defence of the paper, but a briefing ordered by how damaging each objection would be if delivered well, phrased in the vocabulary each objector would actually use, with a concise reply I can deliver in about two minutes, a note on where that reply is weakest, and a clear indication of what I should concede up front versus what I must hold firm on. Please also identify the one question I am hoping nobody asks—the objection that strikes at the methodological premise rather than the conclusion. Throughout your response, please indicate how confident you are in each assessment and explicitly single out the point about which you are least sure; and present everything in British English.
