ok so i'm a retired condensed-matter physicist (worked on topological phases mostly) who got into philosophy of mind about two years ago, mostly through some old Dennett adn then Chalmers. lately i've been stuck on the panpsychism/combination problem debate adn i think i've figured out why it feels like running in mud, but i want to check if i'm just projecting my physics bias onto it.

the debate seems to go like this: panpsychists say every fundamental entity has some minimal phenomenal property, and then they need an account of how those micro-experiences combine into my single stream of consciousness. critics say there's no mechanism, no phenomenal glue, and that postulating micro-subjects just multiplies the hard problem. both sides agree that spatial adjacency isn't enough (that's the old bricks don't think point). some panpsychists try to solve it with phenomenal bonding relations, others with quantum coherence analogies, others just say combination is primitive. but after reading probably thirty papers, nobody's changed anyone's mind. the arguments are all structurally identical: critic demands a derivation, panpsychist offers a model, critic says the model doesn't derive the macro quality.

here's my niche injection: in my old field, topological order is exactly a case where local micro-states don't determine the global property — you need non-local invariants, and there's no local mechanism that builds the global state from parts. so i wonder if panpsychists should be saying that macro-consciousness is like a topological invariant of micro-experiential fields: not derivable part-by-part, but still physically grounded. but then the critics reply that consciousness isn't a physical invariant, it's a qualitative unity, so the analogy breaks. is the stall happening because panpsychists are implicitly borrowing explanatory patterns from physics (like emergence via symmetry breaking) while critics reject that those patterns apply to phenomenology?

my working theory is that this isn't really about consciousness at all, but about what counts as an acceptable explanation in metaphysics versus physics. in physics we're happy with emergence when you have a renormalization group flow that washes out micro-details; in consciousness studies people seem to demand that the macro quality be literally present in the parts, or derivable with no explanatory gap. so maybe the sides are answering different questions: one asks how do micro-subjects physically interact? the other asks how is there one subject rather than many? and they share vocabulary but not the problem.

is that diagnosis right? or is there a hidden empirical disagreement about whether binding mechanisms in the brain (like gamma synchrony) could serve as the combination mechanism? or are we just staring at bedrock intuitions about whether unity can be fundamental? and if your diagnosis is correct, is there anything that could actually move this — a formal result about compositionality, a distinction between functional and phenomenal binding, or should we just admit the question is malformed and move on?
