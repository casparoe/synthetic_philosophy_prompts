Write a short research proposal (about one page, suitable for a workshop pitch or small fellowship) on Émilie du Châtelet’s philosophy of happiness. Focus on a specific tension: in her writings on happiness she insists that certain necessary illusions—particularly about free will and about the intrinsic worth of intellectual labor—are indispensable for sustained well-being, yet her metaphysical commitments, shaped by Leibnizian and Wolffian determinism, appear to undermine exactly those beliefs. Frame the precise research question: should this tension be resolved by treating her ethics and metaphysics as operating in separate registers, by reading her as pragmatically inconsistent, or by interpreting her as an early critic of the modern authenticity demand that one align belief with metaphysical truth? Explain why the existing literature—which tends either to isolate her ethics from her metaphysics or to reduce the tension to biographical contradiction—leaves the question genuinely open. Describe the proposed method: read her essay on happiness and her correspondence with Voltaire not as separate genres (systematic philosophy versus private reflection) but as parts of a single practical argument about the conditions for intellectual joy. Specify what a useful deliverable looks like: a taxonomy of the illusions she permits, with a criterion for when metaphysical knowledge damages rather than supports happiness, and an assessment of whether such a taxonomy can be reconstructed without attributing to her a systematic treatise she never produced. State what counts as failure—e.g., if the texts prove too fragmentary to support any unified reading. Give a realistic scope assessment for a six-month project, and be explicit about the risk that joining metaphysics and ethics here forces more coherence onto her work than it can bear. Finally, ask that the response include a brief note identifying where it takes liberties with contested interpretations, and invite direct pushback: if the premise that her metaphysics and ethics can be coherently joined is confused, or if the proposed scope is unrealistic, say so plainly rather than producing a polished but hollow proposal.
