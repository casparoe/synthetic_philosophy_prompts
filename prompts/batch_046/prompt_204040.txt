As a retired biologist recently turned to philosophy, I want to read Clint Eastwood’s film Million Dollar Baby through the lens of the doing versus allowing distinction and the killing versus letting die debate in medical ethics. The narrative presents Maggie’s request to disconnect her respirator as an act of mercy belonging to one moral category, yet treats Frankie’s subsequent lethal injection as the true killing, as if withdrawing mechanical ventilation and introducing a toxic agent differ not merely in physiological mechanism but in moral kind. I want to examine whether the film genuinely sustains that distinction—perhaps by appealing to an intention-based or agency-based account—or whether it implicitly collapses into an intuition that an honest critic must expose. In particular, does the film’s framing of Maggie’s own request versus Frankie’s execution map onto a defensible philosophical distinction between basic and derived action, or does it merely dramatize our reluctance to call withdrawal killing? Which scenes should I direct a discussion group to, and where does the philosophical framework illuminate the drama without reducing the work to an illustration? I am especially interested in what the film adds to the debate that pure argument about causal pathways and agency does not capture.
