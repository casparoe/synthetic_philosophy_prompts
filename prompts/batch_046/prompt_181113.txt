I've been trying to wrap my head around Nietzsche's essay on the uses and disadvantages of history for life — the one with monumental, antiquarian, and critical modes. I was chatting with a friend about this weird new research practice where people train language models on all the real correspondence of some historical figure and then generate the lost letters, labeling them synthetic reconstructions of missing primary sources. At the same time, every photo and post gets archived forever, which feels like the antiquarian drive on steroids, and then there's this cycle of tearing down old statues while treating tech billionares as world-historical heroes.

So if Nietzsche were alive now and saw this mess, how would he map these things onto his three categories? Would he see the AI gap-filling as a perversion of antiquarian care, or is it closer to monumental history because it invents heroic narratives? Would he think our infinite digital archives have turned preservation into the kind of life-denying burden he warned about? And what about the combination of demolishing monuments with worshipping CEOs — is that critical and monumental history working together, or just incoherent? I'm not asking for citations or a bibliography, just whether his framework actually holds up when the archive is partly hallucinated and algorithims decide what survives. Maybe also whether critical history is even possible anymore when the past is curated by recommendation engines.
