Blake thinks they're practical, world-involving commitments that are tacit and embodied, closer to skills than conventions, and they can shift through practice without anyone deciding to revise them.

Blake should counter that if hinges were purely grammatical the cascade should be arbitrary, but actually it's constrained by practical success—some hinges resist the shift because they still work in action. They need real back-and-forth, each responding to the other's actual point, not talking past each other. Don't resolve it neatly at the end—let the disagreement stay live.
