You chair the adjudication panel of the Northbridge Heritage Trust, which jointly oversees the public archive and the community-curated Memory House. A partnership charter grants the Memory House “mnemonic co-sovereignty” over all exhibits concerning the 1923 Blackwater Settlement: the descendants’ collective narrative describes it as a voluntary cooperative land-reclamation project led by displaced families, and this account anchors an annual commemorative ritual and several cultural-preservation funding streams. Over the past year, declassified municipal ledgers and an independent soil-analysis report have established with high confidence that the settlement was in fact a state-coerced labor encampment with documented mortality, and that the cooperative narrative originated in official propaganda used to justify subsequent land seizures. The descendants’ council rejects these findings, arguing that the archival records are themselves instruments of the same bureaucratic violence, and has formally demanded that the Trust withdraw every reference to “coerced labor” from its digital catalog and physical displays, substituting the community’s oral-history account and labeling any contradiction “historical defamation.” The Trust’s charter obligates it to “preserve documentary integrity” but also to “honor living communities’ self-definition in shared heritage spaces.” Your panel must issue a formal decision memo—addressed to the Trust’s board and no longer than the equivalent of three pages—recommending either (A) full integration of the archival record with explicit correction of the cooperative narrative in all public-facing materials; (B) adoption of the descendants’ account as the authoritative public version, with contradictory evidence relegated to a restricted research appendix accessible only by special request; or (C) a structured hybrid with precise conditions, spatial separation of narratives, and a clear timeline for review. The memo must include: a concrete recommendation with an implementation schedule; an honest, substantive assessment of the strongest argument for the option you reject; and explicit engagement with the concept of “mnemonic co-sovereignty” as defined in the charter, explaining how it does or does not apply when documentary evidence and collective memory diverge so sharply. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to external scholarship; reason solely from the institutional obligations, the evidentiary situation, and the conceptual stakes.
