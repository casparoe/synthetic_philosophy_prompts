Having retired from a career in condensed-matter physics, I have lately been teaching myself philosophy of history, and I am now trying to organize an informal reading group for fellow retirees who share this late-blooming curiosity. In the course of our discussions we keep returning to what some call the "hinge of history" hypothesis—the idea that certain junctures are disproportionately consequential, in that a minute perturbation there would have cascaded into radically different long-run outcomes rather than being absorbed by historical inertia. I find the existing treatments either too journalistic or too technically buried in counterfactual semantics, so I want a clear diagnostic instrument.

Could you construct for me a decision-tree taxonomy of the philosophical positions on hinge moments? The root node should ask whether hinge-ness is an objective feature of causal structure or a perspectival artifact of narrative framing. From there, branch on whether one accepts counterfactual stability—the idea that small local changes at the putative hinge remain localized—or instead accepts global counterfactual divergence. A further branch should concern moral appraisal: does our evaluation of agents at hinges depend on the actual outcome that transpired, or solely on the ex-ante risk profile they faced? Each leaf should carry either a recognized label (for instance, "structural hinge realism," "narrative constructivism," "moral risk-externalism") or the designation "unoccupied," together with one sentence describing its most awkward commitment. I also want the tree to incorporate two concrete, somewhat under-discussed diagnostic cases: first, whether the 1945 Trinity test remains a hinge in a counterfactual where the implosion mechanism initially failed but nuclear weapons were still developed by 1955; second, whether the 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis counts as a hinge only if an alternative decision by U-2 reconnaissance pilots would have triggered full nuclear exchange, or also if it would merely have delayed détente by a decade. Please phrase every branching question so that a participant can answer it without already knowing which philosophical position it points to, and explicitly flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot cleanly separate—say, because they differ only in rhetorical emphasis rather than in commitments about cases.
