I'm working on a novel where one of my characters is an uploaded mind that starts arguing with it's copy, and I got tangled up in Hofstadter's idea of the 'I' as a strange loop — you know, the symbol that points back to the pattern generating it, with no soul needed. I was trying to map this onto my plot and ended up with an argument that seems to prove the view collapses into nonsense, but I can't find the slip. Here's the chain:

(1) Hofstadter says the self is just a strange loop: the symbol 'I' refers back to the neural or computational pattern that produces it, and that's all there is to being me. (2) If identity is just the loop-structure, then the physical substrate doesn't matter — meat, silicon, whatever — as long as the self-referential closure is intact. (3) Now imagine we scan a brain and run the loop in two physically seperate machines, A and B, with no causal link between them. Both contain an 'I' symbol that closes back onto it's local pattern. (4) By (2), both A and B instantiate the same strange loop, so both should be the original person. (5) But the symbol 'I' is singular — there's only one center of experience — so A and B can't both be correct. (6) So the theory either allows one person to split into two identical selves, which violates the uniqueness built into the very symbol Hofstadter is explaining, or it secretly requires a singular spatiotemporal trajectory to close the loop, which means substrate and continuity do matter after all. (7) Therefore strange-loop identity either destroys uniqueness or smuggles back in the physical continuity it was meant to eliminate.

My friend says step (4) is where it slips — he thinks only one copy can have the genuine loop because reference needs a causal chain to the original. But if that's true, then the loop isn't purely structural; it's anchored by history, and Hofstadter's whole point was that the loop explains itself without outside anchors. I really want to keep (2) because my plot depends on substrate-independence, and I also want to keep the singularity of 'I' because my protagonist needs to feel like one continuous thread, not a scattered occurence. So which step should I actually give up, and what does giving it up cost?
