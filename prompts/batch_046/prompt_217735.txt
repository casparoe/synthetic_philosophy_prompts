Please explain the conceptual tension in the Sleeping Beauty problem that concerns not the familiar half-versus-third arithmetic, but the breakdown of standard Bayesian conditionalization when evidence is self-locating. Beauty learns that she is currently awake—a proposition true once in the heads-world and twice in the tails-world—yet this seems to shift her credence in heads away from her prior, despite the coin outcome being causally independent of her awakening protocol. Clarify whether the difficulty is that indexical evidence requires conditioning on a centered proposition that eliminates no worlds, that treating centered worlds as distinct sample-space members violates the stability conditions required for conditionalization, or that there is no coherent prior over self-locating beliefs without first specifying an agent's reference class among her possible temporal locations. Give an intuitive account of why this reveals a structural feature of indexical rationality rather than a quirk of the thought experiment.
