I am working out my concept of disease through reflective equilibrium. My intuitive verdicts on the following cases are:

1. In an isolated population where congenital achromatopsia is universal and fully compatible with flourishing, I do not consider it a disease.
2. In the general population, the identical congenital condition in a single individual: I do consider it a disease.
3. Severe hyperthymesia that produces relentless intrusive recall, destroying the capacity for work and intimate relationships: I consider it a disease.
4. The sarcopenia and slowed cognition typical of an 80-year-old: I do not consider these diseases, only aging.
5. A voluntary, irreversible neural interface that suppresses anxiety but eliminates spontaneous motivation and requires lifelong maintenance; the user reports satisfaction and no distress: I lean against calling this a disease, despite the dependency and functional change.
6. A genetic variant that maintains constant low-grade immune activation—causing chronic fatigue and subjective illness—while conferring complete immunity to all known pathogens: I still consider it a disease.

What principle—naturalist, normativist, or hybrid—captures all six? If none does, which verdict should be overturned? Apply your best principle to this further case: a community that ritually induces temporary, fully reversible blindness in adolescents through a pharmacological protocol, valuing it as a maturity rite; most report lasting psychological benefit and no functional impairment.

State your principle precisely, show how each clause handles the listed cases, predict the result for the ritual-blindness case, and say explicitly which of my verdicts (if any) it must reject. End with: Verdict: [principle or 'none'] / overturned case [number or 'none'].
