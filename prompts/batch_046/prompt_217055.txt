In a philosophy seminar I attended, the speaker summarized Bradley's regress argument about the unity of the proposition in roughly this way. Bradley observes that analyzing a judgment—say, that this rose is red—into discrete terms (the subject, the predicate, and the relation of predication) forces us to postulate further relations linking each term to the relating relation, and then still further relations linking those, yielding an infinite regress. Since judgment is evidently possible, analysis must fail; therefore only an undifferentiated, immediate whole, the Absolute, is genuinely real, and particular judgments are merely appearance. The speaker claimed this is essentially the same difficulty Frege later addressed with unsaturated concepts: once we see how a concept can hold of an object without requiring an intermediary linking relation, Bradley's regress dissolves, leaving his monism an obsolete curiosity rather than a live problem for propositional unity.
