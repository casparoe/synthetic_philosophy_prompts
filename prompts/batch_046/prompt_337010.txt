I have been trying to work through Marx's account of exploitation and alienation on my own, mostly by reflecting on the kind of labor I see around me today. From what I understand, exploitation is essentially the situation where an employer forces a worker to accept wages lower than the value they produce, and alienation is the psychological feeling of being detached or dissatisfied with one's labor. Given that understanding, it seems to me that modern flexible employment—where people voluntarily sign up for shifts through an app, set their own hours, and are paid through digital platforms—would fall outside Marx's critique, since there is no direct coercion and the worker retains formal autonomy. I also notice that many of these same platforms now provide wellness resources, mental-health support, and community forums to address worker dissatisfaction. Would it be fair to say that, under these conditions, exploitation has been reduced to a question of fair pricing algorithms, while alienation has become a user-experience problem that better design can solve?

What confuses me further is the role of automated management. If the supervisor is no longer a human being who personally dominates the worker, but rather an algorithm that assigns tasks based on demand forecasts and performance metrics, then the specific interpersonal domination that Marx seemed to describe appears to dissolve. I have read that some scholars now speak of immaterial labor and cognitive surplus extraction, but I am not sure whether these are just new names for the same old processes, or whether they represent a fundamental break with Marx's framework. Specifically, if a worker's attention and decision-making are guided by predictive models rather than by a factory foreman, is the surplus being extracted still surplus value in Marx's technical sense, or has it become something else entirely—perhaps a form of data rent?

Given all of this, I am trying to understand whether Marx's concepts of exploitation and alienation are historically bound to industrial factory conditions, or whether they can be genuinely extended to algorithmically mediated gig work without losing their original meaning. Could you help me see where my reasoning goes wrong, particularly regarding the distinction between formal freedom and structural coercion, and between emotional discomfort and the specific forms of estrangement Marx identified?
