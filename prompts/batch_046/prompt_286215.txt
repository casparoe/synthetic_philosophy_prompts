I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I sit on the Data Ethics Review Board at my company, which develops clinical-decision support software. Our research division has proposed deploying a module they term Counterfactual Consent Mapping (CCM). Rather than storing the indicative record of what a patient actually consented to, the system computes a counterfactual profile: for each data-use decision, it models what the patient would have consented to under idealised conditions—full information, absence of stress, and neutral framing. The research director argues that this captures authentically autonomous consent and should replace our standard indicative audit trail; the legal team objects that data-protection regulation requires an actual record, and that counterfactual reconstruction introduces unverifiable speculation. The board must decide by next Thursday whether to approve a six-month pilot, reject the proposal outright, or mandate a hybrid architecture. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before the meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action with an explicit time frame, engage the research director's philosophical position seriously—including the distinction between indicative and counterfactual conditions in ethical reasoning—and present the strongest argument for the opposing view without reducing it to a legal formality. Write the response in British English.
