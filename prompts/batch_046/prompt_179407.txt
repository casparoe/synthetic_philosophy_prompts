ive been volunteering with this local tenants rights campaign and last week our candidate quietly traded away a zoning fight we promised to keep fighting in exchange for funding that actually gets the shelter built. afterwards me and two other volunteers, Nina and Dev, argued about whether she just got her hands dirty. Nina said yes, this is exactly the problem: she broke a promise made specifically to protect those tenants, and even if the shelter saves more people she should carry a real moral stain, not just feel bad about it. Dev said thats just mystifying consequentialism — if the outcome is genuinely better theres no stain, only emotional discomfort that people mistake for ethics.

Nina pushed back that if theres no special stain then politics is just ordinary moral math, and the real issue is that the candidate used authority that was given to her precisely to defend those tenants, so violating that trust corrupts the representative relationship itself, not just breaks a promise. Dev said that sounds circular — authority is only legitimate if it produces good results, so using it to secure the shelter actually preserves its legitimacy. Nina then said youre missing the secrecy: she had to hide the deal, which means she cant be held accountable by the people who authorized her, and that creates structural corruption regardless of the outcome. Dev countered that secrecy is ordinary strategic negotiation, and unless Nina can prove every hidden compromise destroys democracy shes just calling normal politics dirty hands. neither would budge, and i mostly just listened. whos right about which specific point? and are they both missing something about how the stain works — like maybe its not just regret or corruption but having to hold two contradictory moral frames about the same act at once?
