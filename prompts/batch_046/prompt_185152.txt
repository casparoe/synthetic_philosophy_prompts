I am a retired physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy of mathematics and metaphysics with any seriousness, and I am dissatisfied with the way discussions of Thomson’s lamp typically collapse into either a blanket dismissal of supertasks on physical grounds or an unexamined declaration that the lamp’s state at the limit is simply “indeterminate.” I want you to make a committed, reasoned argument for a specific resolution: that the paradox dissolves not by denying the logical coherence of the infinite toggling sequence, but by recognizing that the discrete binary state-space of the switch lacks topological closure. On this view, asking whether the lamp is on or off at the limit time is analogous to asking for the well-defined temperature of a system driven through infinitely many thermodynamic cycles in finite time—there is no contradiction in the sequence itself, and the appearance of contradiction arises only if we illegitimately demand that the limiting state inherit the discrete on/off structure of every finite stage.

To keep the discussion from becoming purely abstract, apply your argument to a deliberately constructed variant: a “thermal lamp” in which each toggle is executed by a microscopic heat engine that must dissipate entropy to flip the switch, with both the time interval and the energy budget per toggle halving so that the total work remains finite but the rate of dissipation diverges. Take a definite stand—do not survey named positions or present a menu of options—and defend it directly: does this physical embedding actually block the supertask because the second law prohibits infinite dissipation in finite time, thereby resolving the paradox by showing its premises are physically unrealizable? Or does it merely illustrate that physical impossibility is distinct from logical incoherence, leaving the formal paradox intact as a problem about discrete-state limits? I am not asking for citations, bibliographies, or a taxonomy of interpretations; I want your own judgment, delivered with the confidence of a conclusion you are willing to own.
