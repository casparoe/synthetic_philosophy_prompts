Hey — novelist here, not a philosopher, but I keep running into this argumentative loop in writing rooms and I can't find the right word for it. Say someone wants to publish a story drawn from real trauma; critics say "if there's any chance it harms someone, don't risk it." Then if you suggest keeping silent, the same people say "we don't know that silence is safe — the harm might continue — but at least it's the devil we know." So uncertainty is always used to defend whatever is currently occuring, whether that's publishing or not publishing, and the burden of proof lands on anyone who wants to change the state. I've seen this with AI editing tools, with memoir ethics, with characters in my novels debating whether to reveal a secret. Is this just the precautionary principel being misused? I know omission bias and status quo bias, but those feel like they describe preferences, not this specific rhetorical move where the same "better safe" logic gets flipped to block both action and inaction. Does this have an established name, and if not, can you coin one that distinguishes it from ordinary risk aversion and from the standard precautionary principle? What's the boundary — where does it stop being this and start being something else? Keep it tight: one paragraph, under 150 words.
