So I've been in this metaphysics reading group for like a year and we're stuck on origin essentialism - the idea that you necessarily come from the exact sperm and egg you actually came from. The essentialist side says if my parents had conceived with different gametes, that'd be a different person entirely, not me with a different origin. The other side says origins are contingent; what makes me me is psychological continuity or being the same living organism, so I could've started from different cells if the right development followed.

We've run through every move: they point to our intuition that identical twins are different people despite shared enviroment, we counter with embryo splitting where one origin yields two distinct people, they talk about biological individuation, we talk about narrative identity. Nobody budges an inch. My working theory is that we're not actually disagreeing about origins at all - we're using "you" to pick out different things: a biological individual, a psychological subject, maybe even a social role. So it's either a verbal dispute hiding under one heading, or there's a hidden empirical question about whether genetic origins actually determine the properties that matter for identity.

But I'm not sure. Is there a real substantive disagreement here about what kind of thing a person is? Or are we just stuck with bedrock intuitions that neither side can argue for? And if it's stalled, what would actually move it - a sharper distinction between organism and person, some finding about developmental noise breaking the link between genome and identity, or should we just accept it's an ill-posed question?
