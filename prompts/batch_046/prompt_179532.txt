ok so i'm writing up my commitments for a democratic-design reading group and want a consistency check before i present. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) legislative bodies should be filled by sortition — random selection from the citizenry — rather than election, since voting selects for charisma and fundraising ability that are negatively correlated with deliberative competence.

(2) career politicians accumulate irreplaceable tacit knowledge about institutional procedure, coalition-building, and long-run policy feedback, and this knowledge is a real public good we shouldn't throw away.

(3) any office filled by election needs hard term limits — max two terms, ever — because careerism is the primary mechanism for regulatory capture and party lock-in.

(4) legitimacy requires that citizens have a direct recall mechanism over representatives; you can't just rely on ex ante random selection or waiting for terms to expire.

(5) the system i actually want is unicameral: legislators drawn by lot for single six-year terms with no reelection, but recallable if 15% of the national citizenry petitions for it; plus a small separately-elected constitutional council with strict single-term limits that holds a narrow veto over rights-violating legislation.

go through these and tell me which pairs are in real tension, which ones only look like tension but dissolve, and rank the real ones by severity. for each real tension say what'd have to give and what that costs me. don't just list everything as bad — discriminate. and please seperate what's fairly settled in the literature from what's genuinely contested, especially on whether sortition actually improves deliberative quality; don't present my (1) as consensus.
