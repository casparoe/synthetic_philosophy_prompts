Compare the classical Löbian obstacle to unrestricted self-trust—wherein an agent that accepts its own reliability for every true proposition can, via Löb's rule, derive belief in any proposition it considers—with the "modular containment" proposal that attempts to preserve reflective self-confidence by architecturally isolating a verification module from the object-level reasoning it evaluates. Does containment genuinely dissolve the derivation, or does it merely relocate the vulnerability to the agent's trust in its own boundary conditions? Assess which of these two frameworks offers the more coherent account of rational autonomy for an artificial agent required to revise its beliefs in light of its own future reasoning, and identify the specific feature of the containment architecture that would need to fail for the Löbian collapse to re-emerge.
