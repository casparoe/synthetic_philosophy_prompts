If moral standing is genuinely determined by the execution of a particular kind of integrated computation, then a distributed architecture in which no single processor implements anything resembling cognition, yet whose collective dynamics precisely replicate the functional organization of a conscious brain, should possess moral status as a whole while each constituent node lacks it entirely. Given this, it seems to follow that moral culpability must be emergent in exactly the same fashion: should such a system cause harm, the appropriate locus of blame would be the collective computation itself, rather than the human engineers who configured and maintained the individual processors. Is this parallel between patienthood and agency correct, or does it rest on a misunderstanding of how computation relates to moral attribution?
