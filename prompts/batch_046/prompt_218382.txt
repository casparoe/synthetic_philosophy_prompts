Write a satirical deadpan column of approximately 600 words, formatted as an advice page in the fictional quarterly The Adaptive Ethicist, in which the columnist “Dr. A. Finch” replies to a letter from “Dr. L. Chen” at a fictional research institute. Chen explains that she has accepted evolutionary debunking arguments in metaethics—specifically that human moral beliefs are generated by selective pressures that do not track objective moral truth—but now notices that her belief in debunking itself, her anxiety about tenure, her conviction that plagiarism is wrong, and her reliance on peer-review fairness all appear to be products of the very same non-tracking processes. Finch must answer without leaving the debunker framework: she must not invoke objective moral truth, categorical norms, or any epistemic standard that escapes evolutionary explanation, and she must avoid slipping into compatibilism about normativity or conceding that some beliefs are specially protected. Instead, she should treat every normative concern—career ambition, intellectual credit, conference etiquette, grant-writing—as a mere adaptive heuristic, offering practical guidance that follows the debunking logic to its bureaucratic extreme: for instance, advising Chen to optimise her publication strategy for inclusive fitness rather than for truth, to interpret citation practices as dominance displays calibrated for alliance formation, and to view peer review as an in-group coordination ritual with no authority beyond reproductive advantage. The humour must arise from exaggerating real features of evolutionary debunking (the tracking/non-tracking distinction, the self-defeat problem, the implicit reliance on normative premises to motivate the argument) rather than inventing false ones, and should sustain the deadpan register of a serious professional journal so that the implicit reductio is legible: if all normative belief is debunked, the debunker has no standing to advise, publish, or complain. Write in British English, maintain a formal and polished register throughout, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts; references to general figures such as Darwin or Hume are permissible only if paraphrased without quotation marks.
