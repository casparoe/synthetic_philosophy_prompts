I'm trying to understand what actually makes a human life meaningful, testing my intuitions against concrete cases rather than relying on abstract definitions. Here is where my gut lands.

Consider a sculptor who spends years carving an elaborate cathedral from ice, fully aware it will melt with the spring; she finds the work itself absorbing and beautiful. I think her life is meaningful. Now consider a prisoner forced to carve an identical cathedral for twenty years; he hates every moment and sees no value in it. I think his life is not meaningful, even though the objective result is identical. Next, a scientist who spends her career pursuing a theory that turns out to rest on a miscalibrated instrument; the theory is false, but the mathematical tools she invented are later used to model climate patterns she never cared about. I feel her life was meaningful, yet I cannot tell whether the meaning comes from her sincere pursuit, from the accidental tools, or from both. Then there is a person who lives in perfect comfort, playing games and eating well, with no larger project or struggle; I think this life lacks meaning. But I also think a parent who raises children knowing humanity will go extinct in two centuries, leaving no trace or legacy, still lives meaningfully—provided she loves them. The case that splits me is this: a man dedicates his life to maintaining a garden he knows will be bulldozed for a highway in one year, and he does it purely from a sense of duty, with no joy in the plants and no belief that anyone will notice. Sometimes I think the sheer commitment makes it meaningful; other times I think meaning requires either subjective fulfillment or some lasting value, and this case has neither.

Is there one coherent principle—perhaps combining subjective engagement with objective worth, or something like "teleological coherence"—that captures all of these verdicts? If not, which verdict should I drop, and what does the best principle predict about a case I haven't listed: someone who spends decades perfecting a skill they never use, not out of love but out of obsessive compulsion, and who dies feeling empty despite being world-class?
