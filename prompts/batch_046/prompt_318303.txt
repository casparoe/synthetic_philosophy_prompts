I'm preparing a consistency check for a discussion group on skeptical arguments and their dissolution, and I want to see whether my own position hangs together before I defend it. Here is where I have landed, with some terminology I've adopted for clarity. First, I accept contextualism about knowledge attributions: the standards expressed by "knows" shift with conversational context, so in ordinary life "I know I have hands" is true, whereas in philosophical contexts where skeptical alternatives are salient, the standards are raised and knowledge is suspended. Second, I reject epistemic closure specifically for empirical beliefs about the external world: knowing that I have hands does not entail knowing that I am not a brain in a vat, not merely as a pragmatic limitation but as a principled feature of how perceptual justification is structured. Third, I hold phenomenal conservatism: if it phenomenally seems to a subject that she has hands, then she possesses immediate, defeasible justification for that belief, and this holds regardless of whether she is embodied or envatted. Fourth, I think the brain-in-a-vat scenario involves what I call systematic phenomenal duplication without de re reference: the envatted subject's mental states preserve all qualitative character but lack the external-world-directed intentional content they appear to have, so she is wrong not merely about particular objects but about the referential structure of her thoughts. Fifth, I maintain that first-person authority survives envatment precisely because it concerns qualitative character and internal structural relations rather than de re content, meaning the vat-subject remains authoritative about what her experience is like even though she is systematically mistaken about her environment. Sixth, I insist that any viable anti-skeptical strategy must be non-circular: we cannot defend the reliability of perception by appealing to perceptual deliverances, and we cannot justify contextualist standards by presupposing the knowledge they are meant to secure.

My concern is that some of these commitments pull in incompatible directions, though I am unsure which tensions are real and which dissolve on inspection. Specifically: does my acceptance of phenomenal conservatism undermine my contextualist claim that knowledge is suspended in high-standards contexts, given that the envatted subject has identical phenomenal justification? Does my rejection of closure genuinely defuse skepticism, or does it sit uneasily with my non-circularity constraint, since rejecting closure usually requires some independent account of perceptual reliability that I have forbidden myself? And does my claim about first-person authority in the vat genuinely cohere with my claim about the absence of de re reference, or is there a hidden contradiction in saying that authority is preserved over content that is, by my own lights, referentially empty? Please audit these commitments: rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each whether it requires giving up one of the commitments or only refining its scope, identify which apparent conflicts actually dissolve once the distinctions are drawn properly, and suggest what the least costly revision to the package would be if I must choose one commitment to weaken.
