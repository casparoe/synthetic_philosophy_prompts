Construct a realistic micro-scale thought experiment—two structurally identical small organizations or communities, not historical societies—in which a specific cultural value system appears to cause a distinctive economic or organizational behavior, but engineer the case so that (a) a strict materialist anti-reductionist, who treats culture as mere superstructure determined by material conditions, must concede that identical material bases produced divergent outcomes; (b) a psychological reductionist, who reduces cultural values to aggregated individual preferences, fails to explain why the values persisted and spread in the pattern they did rather than being selected away; and (c) a Weberian who treats culture as an independent causal factor can explain the divergence but only by accepting a counterintuitive implication about how values can drive behavior without being instrumentally reinforced. Constraints: the scenario must unfold within a single generation (to block long-run structural-adaptation arguments); it must not invoke any recognizable religious doctrine (to avoid rehearsing the standard Protestant-ethic narrative); and it must include what I call a "normative decoupling mechanism"—an institutional feature you design specifically for this case—that severs the usual feedback loop between economic success and value adoption. Keep the scenario to one paragraph. Then, in order: state the specific belief or behavioral pattern that differs; show what each of the three positions says and why; identify the precise structural feature that does the isolating work and explain what happens if it is removed; and finally explain how a committed materialist would attack the case—whether by denying the decoupling mechanism is genuinely sustainable or by denying that it isolates culture rather than hidden material variables—and state what that attack costs them.
