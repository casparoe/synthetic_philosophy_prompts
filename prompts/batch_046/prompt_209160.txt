My friend J and I were talking about whether I should still apologize for a really dumb thing I did in college -- like, five years ago. I said look, I'm not even the same guy anymore, every cell's turned over, my values shifted, so blaming me now is like blaming a corporation for its old employees. J said that's just biology poetry and misses the point: there's one continuous thing that endured through all that change, so the responsability sticks.

Then I tried to steelman the other side. I said okay, if I'm literally wholly present right now and was wholly present back then, then I have opposite properties -- foolish then, not foolish now -- which violates non-contradiciton unless we say I'm only partly present at each time. So maybe perdurantism is right and I'm just a temporal slice of a 4D worm. But J shot back that philosphers deal with temporary intrinsics all the time without chopping people up -- they use relational properties or adverbial strategies, like saying I'm foolish-at-t1 and wise-at-t2. So she said my argument for temporal parts fails.

I came back with: fine, but if I'm just one stage in a series, then the stage that did the bad thing literally doesn't exist anymore. How can blame reach across to me-now? J said responsibility doesn't need strict identity, just psychological continuty or narrative connection, and that on her view I'm the enduring subject of both properties, so it's even cleaner. We also started wondering about this third view -- I think it's called exdurantism or stage theory -- where only the present stage is real and the past ones are just gone, not even parts. Would that make blame impossible, or is it just perdurantism with extra steps? We couldn't settle it and honestly I'm not sure who's missing what. Can you go through the exchange point by point -- who's right about the temporary intrinsics objection, whether adverbialism saves endurantism, whether perdurantism actually dissolves responsibility, and where this stage-theory thing fits? And tell us what we're both ignoring. I have a feeling we're talking past each other about whether identity is even the right thing to track for blame.
