I am preparing a handout for a philosophy of economics seminar on Adam Smith’s normative stance toward empire and colonial administration. Please construct a decision tree that sorts interpretive positions from the logical space of Smith’s commitments rather than from a list of named scholars. The root node should ask whether Smith’s opposition to empire is primarily an economic critique of mercantilist monopoly, or whether it also carries an independent moral-political claim about self-government. From there, branch on concrete commitments that can be answered from the Wealth of Nations and the essays on colonies alone: whether Smith treats American independence as a principled application of liberty or as a contingent remedy for British mismanagement; whether his free-trade arguments apply symmetrically to colonial trade or are suspended for imperial consolidation; and whether the concept of natural liberty can be consistently extended to subjects who lack representation in the metropole.

Each internal node must be phrased as a yes/no or either/or claim about Smith’s arguments—e.g., “Smith holds that colonial monopolies raise domestic prices and distort capital allocation” versus “Smith holds that empire corrupts the metropole’s public virtue regardless of fiscal balance”—so that a student can answer without knowing the secondary literature. Each leaf should carry a descriptive label (not an eponym) and one sentence stating its most awkward commitment: the feature of Smith’s text it must explain away, or the modern normative implication it must accept.

The tree should have six to eight leaves. Please include at least one unoccupied branch combination—such as a view that fully accepts Smith’s economic critique of colonial monopoly but denies that his arguments have any modern normative force because institutional conditions have changed—and briefly diagnose whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Also flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, noting which diagnostic question fails to split them and why the divergence is only a matter of emphasis.

Frame every node as a question a student could answer after reading Smith’s primary texts, with no reference to bibliographies, citation lists, or post-twentieth-century scholarship. Do not include verbatim quotations from Smith; paraphrase any textual references without quotation marks. Keep the deliverable self-contained: the tree, the leaf descriptions with awkward commitments, the unoccupied-space diagnosis, and the flagged indistinguishability.
