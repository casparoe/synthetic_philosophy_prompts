Design a 75-minute classroom activity for an upper-level philosophy seminar of about 14 students that makes participants feel the force of what I will call partitioned constitution in social ontology: the idea that a social kind can be sustained by two or more incompatible sets of constitutive rules operating in parallel, without any shared meta-rule for resolving conflicts, and that practical coordination can remain stable precisely because the incompatibility is institutionally hidden rather than resolved. Work within the domain of social construction of kinds—specifically analogized to gender or bureaucratic status, but staged through a fictional institutional kind so that prior ideological commitments do not block the experiential point.

The deliverable is a fully specified, runnable activity—rules, roles, materials, a payoff or incentive structure using only symbolic points or social stakes, not real money—followed by a structured debrief. Do not open with a lecture or conceptual explanation; arrange the rules, roles, and hidden information so that participants discover the hidden fracture themselves through action, and only name it after they have felt it.

Frame the fictional kind as Verified Contributor to a collective knowledge repository. Divide the group into two hidden sub-roles—Registrars and Practitioners—each given a different, unstated constitutive rule for who counts. Registrars determine membership by documented completion of three preliminary modules; Practitioners determine it by demonstrated contribution to two live discussions. Both groups share a visible task: allocating ten limited endorsement slots across fifteen candidate profiles. The materials should be identical cards or digital profiles that contain information readable through both rulebooks, so that each subgroup can complete the allocation smoothly but is actually selecting different people. The payoff scheme should reward the group for reaching a unanimous allocation quickly, creating pressure to coordinate without investigating criteria. Only after the allocation is finalized should the facilitator reveal the two hidden rulebooks and ask the group to map which candidates each subgroup actually endorsed.
