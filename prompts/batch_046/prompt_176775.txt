I sit on a county mental-health budget board and every quarter we have to rank like twenty intervention proposals — crisis-bed expansions, peer-support networks, long-acting injectable subsidies for unhoused folks, etc. We've been trying to use prioritarian reasoning — benefits to the worst off count for more — but nobody's written it down as a procedre our non-philosopher members can actually run in a meeting. I need you to turn it into a reusable decision checklist: ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where it stops with a hard no or defers to next quarter, and where it forces a human value call instead of computing. Crucially it needs an embedded check for the leveling-down objection — a specific step asking whether achieving equality in welfare outcomes requires pulling anyone currently above the worst-off baseline downward, with a veto unless there's an independent justification. It also needs to handle heterogenous worst-off groups, like chronically disabled people with low welfare-capacity versus people in temporary crisis with high recovery-capacity if stabalized. Don't just lay out options; take definite positions on what distinctions survive simplification and which don't.

Apply the procedre to three proposals: (A) a universal basic-income pilot that lifts everyone slightly but closes no severe deprivation gap; (B) an intensive case-management program targeted at the chronically unhoused that pulls them up significantly but barely moves the median; and (C) a "resource-flattening" plan that caps high-intensity services for higher-functioning clients to equalize access, which technically reduces inequality but lowers outcomes for some. One result should be something the board probably won't like. Also flag the misfire points — maybe when welfare capacities are incommensurable or the baseline is contested — and be honest about where judgment can't be eliminated. At the very end, put your bottom line in this exact form: Verdict: [one sentence summarizing the procedure's core stance on leveling-down and priority].
