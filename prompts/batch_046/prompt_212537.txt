Two people in my study group got stuck on whether we can ever actually know why some nations are rich. One argued the institutional turn is circular: "good institutions" are partly coded from development outcomes, so using them to explain wealth just restates the puzzle. The other said geography is even messier, since climate and disease ecology are measured with error and themselves shift with settlement, so like-for-like comparison is impossible. The first then said culture is just a residual — invoke social capital or norms and you've named what you cant explain, not caused it. The second hit back that cultural accounts at least admit theyre not experimentally isolable, while institutionalists pretend settler-mortality instruments solve endogeneity, ignoring that those instruments are themselves downstream of geographic conditions. They asked me to referee. Please adjudicate each move: who's right about circularity, measurement, culture-as-residual, and the IV critique? And tell me if they're both missing the deeper issue — that in macro-history the channels might be structurally inseparable no matter the identification strategy. Write it in British English, and no need for citations or a reading list.
