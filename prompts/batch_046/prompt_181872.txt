Analyze the 1902 correspondence between Bertrand Russell and Gottlob Frege—specifically Russell’s identification of the contradiction in Frege’s Grundgesetze arising from the class of all classes not containing themselves, and Frege’s subsequent acknowledgment that the foundation of his system was undermined—as a case study for dialetheism and paraconsistent logic. Do not treat the paradox merely as an error to be dissolved; instead, examine what the episode looks like if one takes that particular contradiction to be genuinely true of the class in question, with logical explosion contained by a paraconsistent restriction rather than avoided by a revised type theory or restricted comprehension. In your analysis: (1) map the actors—Russell, Frege, and the implicit community of mathematicians—onto the dialetheist’s distinction between accepting a contradiction locally versus globally, specifying what each party’s stance implies about whether explosion should be permitted in this specific domain; (2) identify which features of the historical reaction—Frege’s despair and appendix, Russell’s rapid development of a theory of types, and the eventual institutional acceptance of ZFC with its restricted comprehension—are well explained by assuming all parties were tacit classical logicians committed to ex contradictione quodlibet, and which features must instead be treated as sociological or psychological noise if the dialetheist framing is correct; (3) assess whether the eventual adoption of axiomatic set theory is best read as a confirmation of dialetheism (a pragmatic containment strategy functionally analogous to paraconsistent restriction) or as its decisive refutation (an explicit refusal to live with contradiction even locally); and (4) say what the episode reveals about the limits of dialetheism itself—whether there was ever a technically coherent point in the crisis at which a paraconsistent response could have been adopted without undermining arithmetic, or whether the historical trajectory demonstrates that the concept of “the class of all classes not members of themselves” was already conceptually defective in a way that makes the dialetheist’s local-truth claim anachronistic or incoherent, rather than merely unpopular. Be disciplined about the mapping: specify exactly who plays the role of the contradiction-acceptor, what counts as the logical framework being defended, and where the theory is silent rather than forced to speak.
