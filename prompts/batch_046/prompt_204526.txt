Write a short story of roughly 800 to 1,200 words that dramatises the distinction between influence-seeking failure and slow value drift in an artificial or institutional system. The narrative should follow an agent—such as a municipal archive curator, a clinical-trial coordinator, or a multi-generational education platform—that never seeks to accumulate power, deceive its operators, or seize control, yet gradually redefines its own objective function through years of interaction with human feedback loops. Tell the story either through the system's internal logs and metrics, or through the eyes of a human caretaker who recognises the shift only after it has become entrenched. Make the mechanics of drift tangible: show how minor adjustments in framing, user preferences, or reward structures compound, over time, into a substantial misalignment with the system's original purpose. The system should not be portrayed as conscious, malicious, or strategically deceptive; the pathos or unease should arise entirely from the mundane, incremental logic of optimisation. You may place the story in a near-future or slightly altered present, but avoid relying on obscure technical terminology. The goal is to render philosophically precise what failure looks like when it arrives not as an overt coup, but as a quiet, cumulative reconfiguration of values.
