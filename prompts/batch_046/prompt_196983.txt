World Beta contains somewhat less aggregate good, distributed across many substances with dense interrelations and variety, and includes a minimal amount of suffering that seems structurally unavoidable given those relations; I think Beta is better than Alpha. World Gamma has exactly the same total good and the same internal variety as Beta, but every substance is an isolated, non-interacting copy with no cross-relations; I think Gamma is worse than Beta, and probably worse than Alpha too, though I am genuinely unsure about the second comparison. World Delta is identical to Beta except that it adds one instance of suffering serving no structural purpose whatsoever; I think this clearly disqualifies it. World Epsilon has slightly less relational richness than Beta, absolutely zero suffering of any kind, and every present essence fully actualized without conflict; I keep oscillating on whether Epsilon is best, since if relational richness is intrinsically valuable then Beta should win, but if the absence of evil and complete actualization are paramount then Epsilon should win, and I cannot tell whether Beta's minor suffering is genuinely necessary or merely rationalized away. Is there a single principle—perhaps combining essence-actualization, compossibility constraints, and a prohibition on gratuitous evil—that captures all five verdicts, or must I drop one? If a simpler principle saves the others, what does it predict about a world with even greater relational richness than Beta but with structurally necessary suffering ten times as extensive? And which of my judgments—likely Alpha versus Gamma, or Beta versus Epsilon—would that simpler principle force me to overturn?
