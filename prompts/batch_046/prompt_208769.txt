In my philosophy of religion seminar this term we have been working through arguments from divine hiddenness, and I recently encountered a blog summary of John Schellenberg's position that I would like to audit against the course material. The summary claims that Schellenberg argues a perfectly loving, personal God—if He existed—would establish epistemic proximity with rational agents: not merely leaving general evidence in nature, but ensuring that anyone who is genuinely open-minded and not actively resisting belief achieves direct, conscious awareness of His presence, with an immediacy comparable to awareness of other minds. Because many people who seem intellectually open and non-resistant nevertheless lack any such awareness, the argument is said to conclude that a personal theistic God probably does not exist. The summary then presents the standard free-will defense, according to which God must remain hidden in order to preserve libertarian freedom and avoid coercing belief. Schellenberg is said to reply that belief is doxastically involuntary—since we cannot simply choose to believe at will, hiddenness cannot be safeguarding freedom but only preventing belief altogether. The blogger further asserts that this reasoning applies symmetrically to deism, since a non-interventionist creator still owes relational disclosure, but that it collapses against pantheism because an impersonal ground of being carries no obligation to reveal itself. Finally, the summary concludes that divine hiddenness is therefore not an evidential puzzle for theists to solve but a straightforward disconfirmation of personal theism. How fair is this characterization? Please go through it point by point: tell me which claims a defender of Schellenberg's actual view would accept, where the summary subtly misrepresents his position—particularly regarding the relation between belief, love, and freedom—whether the deism-pantheism asymmetry is part of the argument or an addition by the blogger, and whether the conclusion that hiddenness straightforwardly disconfirms theism survives once the misrepresentations are corrected.
