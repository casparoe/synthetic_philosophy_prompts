I've been trying to formalize a worry from our philosophy of mind reading group, and the result is an argument that seems to force an all-or-nothing choice about introspection: either it is perfectly direct everywhere, or it is useless even about whether I am conscious. The argument runs as follows. (1) Accept the reconstructive account of affective introspection: when I report being anxious, I do not perceive an internal state but construct a post-hoc interpretation of arousal through available conceptual frames—a process we might call "retro-prospective transparency," in which the mind renders its own interpretation immediately present to itself without revealing its constructive origin. (2) If this is correct, then my judgment that I am anxious is an interpretation, not an observation. (3) Interpretations are theory-laden and fallible in precisely the ways that third-party interpretations are. (4) Hence there is no epistemic asymmetry between my judgment that I am anxious and a clinician's judgment that she is anxious. (5) But first-person authority just is that default epistemic asymmetry; so if it is absent here, it collapses entirely. (6) Crucially, the collapse is not local to emotional ambiguity. The same reconstructive mechanism applies to every mental content, including the bare awareness that one is currently conscious. (7) Therefore either I lack any special authority even over my own consciousness—which is absurd—or the reconstructive model is false for mental states generally. (8) So introspection must be direct and fully reliable, and emotional self-knowledge is as transparent as consciousness itself.

I want to resist (8) without denying (1), and I suspect the damage occurs somewhere between (4) and (6), perhaps through a scope shift or an equivocation on "authority." But I also wonder whether (7) introduces a false dilemma. Could you diagnose the weakest link, explain why it looked plausible, and state what preserving (1) while rejecting that link actually costs? In your response, please indicate how confident you are in the diagnosis and explicitly single out the point about which you are least certain—especially whether the extension from emotional authority to consciousness-awareness in (6) is a valid entailment or a rhetorical overreach.
