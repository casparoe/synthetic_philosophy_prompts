The interpretive stalemate over Descartes on animal minds has persisted for decades: mechanists read his clockwork analogy and his denial of rational souls as proof that animals lack any inner life, while revisionists cite his correspondence with More and distinctions within the Discourse on Method to argue that he allowed animal sensation without reason. Both sides marshal the same texts and neither moves. My hypothesis is that part of the stall is verbal—each camp uses the word mind to cover either rational cognition or phenomenal awareness—while another part is a hidden metaphysical disagreement about whether consciousness requires an immaterial substance, a question that historical interpretation alone cannot settle. Diagnose the disagreement: is it substantive, verbal, or are the two groups answering different questions—one about Cartesian exegesis, one about the ontology of awareness—under a single heading? Be specific about what each side actually claims. Then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress: a distinction between cognitive and phenomenal consciousness, a demonstration that substance dualism is incoherent, a framework that treats awareness as embodied and emergent, or an honest admission that the historical question is permanently underdetermined by our present philosophical commitments?
