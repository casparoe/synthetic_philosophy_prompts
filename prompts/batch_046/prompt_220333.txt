Following a prolonged disagreement with a friend, I want to determine whether her way of knowing her own readiness is genuinely circular or merely careful cross-checking. She wears a device that reports sleep quality, but she initially calibrated its readings against her own subjective sense of rest upon waking. Once she accepted that calibration, she began treating the device’s “recovered” score as authoritative grounds for dismissing her present fatigue and accepting demanding commitments. I argued that she has built a loop: her subjective state validates the instrument, and then the instrument is used to override that very state, leaving her with no genuinely independent checkpoint. She replies that comparing two indicators is standard practice, not vicious circularity, and that requiring absolute independence for every belief collapses into skepticism. I want to know whether this is a case of certifying a source through itself and then treating that certification as external, whether there is any non-arbitrary point at which she could have started without already assuming part of what she wants to establish, and whether practical self-monitoring must always begin by taking some initial judgment for granted—or if there is a principled way to break the circle without abandoning the device altogether. Who is right, and what does the structure of her reasoning reveal about how self-knowledge can be grounded?
