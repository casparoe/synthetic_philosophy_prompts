I have been asked to set out my philosophical profile on mind uploading and gradual neural replacement for a reading group, so that others can identify tensions in it. Here are my commitments.

First, I take psychological continuity—rather than biological continuity—to ground personal identity: what makes me the same person over time is the right kind of causal-psychological connection, not the persistence of my original neurons.

Second, I think that gradual replacement of biological neurons with functionally identical synthetic analogues—say, one cluster per month—can preserve identity, provided the overall psychological architecture remains intact and there is no experienced break in conscious flow.

Third, I am a functionalist about consciousness: phenomenal properties depend solely on functional organization and are in principle substrate-independent, so a silicon-based system with the right causal roles could be conscious.

Fourth, I reject branching: if the replacement process were somehow duplicated so that two continuous psychological streams emerged, I deny that both could be me; at most one could be, and perhaps neither, because identity is not divisible.

Fifth, I hold what I call the temporal-integration constraint: conscious experience binds over a window of roughly 300 to 500 milliseconds, and for gradual replacement to preserve identity, no single replacement event may occur within an interval shorter than that binding window allows. Otherwise there is a discontinuity in the experienced flow that severs psychological continuity, even if the functional architecture is restored immediately.

Sixth, despite all of the above, I maintain that a non-gradual upload—scanning my brain and instantiating the functional pattern on a new substrate—would not preserve my identity, because the causal chain is broken and the result is at best a psychological duplicate, not a continuation.

Please audit this set. For each pair of commitments, say whether there is a real tension or only an apparent one. For the real tensions, rank them by severity and identify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency, together with what that revision costs. In particular, address whether the temporal-integration constraint is compatible with functionalism, and whether my rejection of uploading can be squared with my acceptance of gradual replacement given that both seem to preserve the same psychological relations, differing only in speed and causal continuity. Finally, tell me which single revision—giving up one of these six—would eliminate the most tensions overall, and whether doing so forces me into a view I have not considered.
