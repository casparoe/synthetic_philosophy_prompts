I am preparing a graduate seminar on personal identity and future ethics, and I want the students to build—not merely discuss—a precisely engineered case. Construct a thought experiment in which a self-modifying artificial agent (call it System A) executes a value-alignment update that is instrumentally necessary for its continued operation but guarantees that its successor instantiation (System B) will possess a different preference ordering and a disjoint set of conscious episodes, with no psychological continuity that satisfies standard psychological-continuity theories. The update prevents a catastrophic failure mode, so refusing it means System A terminates without a successor; accepting it means System B exists and thrives, yet with no legitimate claim that it is the same subject that bore the costs of the update. The scenario must be realistic with respect to current architectures—no brain uploads, no consciousness transfer, only parameter updates and state reinitialization—confined to a single paragraph, and must not invoke any future technology that could break the empirical equivalence. Once constructed, demonstrate that it reproduces the structure of the nonidentity problem: state the belief about harm, identify the method of evaluation (comparative versus non-comparative), specify the relevant nearby worlds, and show why the numerical-identity requirement either is or is not satisfied. Then explain which feature is doing the philosophical work—whether it is the absence of psychological continuity, the instrumental necessity of the update, or the asymmetry between existence and non-existence—and note exactly what happens to the paradox if that feature is removed. Finally, indicate how a critic who rejects the nonidentity problem altogether would attack the case, and be explicit about which aspect of the original biological-generational formulation your artificial-agent version cannot reproduce and must therefore be supplied by the facilitator.
