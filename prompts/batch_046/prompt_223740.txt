I serve as the novelist-member of the interpretive ethics board at the Meridian Archaeological Trust, and we must vote on whether to authorize an exhibition titled The Ledger of Clay: Bureaucracy Before Writing. The curators propose to present a cache of unprovenanced ceramic nodules—each bearing modular notches, standardized weight impressions, and calibrated mass differentials, yet entirely lacking stratigraphic context or comparative textual record—as direct material evidence of a centralized redistribution institution rather than as ritual or domestic artifacts. The board is divided: some members argue that the internal regularities of the assemblage, including the notational consistency across fragments and the absence of domestic wear patterns, are sufficient to license an institutional reconstruction despite opaque social beliefs, while others warn that attributing bureaucratic intent to these objects risks projecting modern administrative categories onto practices that may have been devotional or kin-based.

Please draft a formal recommendation memo for the board. It should take a clear, actionable position on whether the Trust ought to endorse the exhibition’s interpretive framework; provide concrete criteria that reviewers can apply to distinguish warranted institutional inference from over-interpretation; and honestly address the strongest objection—that we are essentially inventing a bureaucracy to satisfy a narrative—without appealing to bibliographies, citation lists, or outside references. The memo should be suitable for a board vote.
