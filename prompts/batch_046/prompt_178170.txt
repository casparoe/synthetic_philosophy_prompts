Writing a novel about archival immortality, I keep returning to Borges, who died in 1986 before anyone could train a language model on a dead writer’s corpus and let it generate new prose in that voice indefinitely. If he were alive to encounter this specific technology—not artificial intelligence in general, but the algorithmic extension of an author’s temporal presence through endless stylistic imitation—how do you think he would have evaluated it? Given his essays and fictions that treat linear, irreversible time as suspect and instead explore cyclical, branching, or labyrinthine temporal structures, would he have read such a digital afterlife as a fulfillment of his own metaphysical speculations, or as a grotesque stasis that mimics duration while abolishing the very change that makes time meaningful? I am not asking for biographical guesswork, a reading list, or citations; I want a conceptual prediction drawn from the internal logic of his thought about time, authorship, and repetition. In particular, address whether he would have distinguished this algorithmic eternity from the eternal recurrence he imagined, and whether he would have seen the preservation of style without consciousness as a kind of time that has lost its subject.
