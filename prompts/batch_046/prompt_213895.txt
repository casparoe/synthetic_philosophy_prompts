ok so i'm drafting this essay about welfare and i want to check if my politics actually hang together before i embarrass myself at the writing group. here's where i'm at, more or less:

(i) i think every adult has an unconditional moral claim to a liveable income guaranteed by the state — no means-testing, no workfare, nothing.

(ii) but i've also been writing about these local solidarity nodes where people pool extra resources and can vote on conditions, like requiring community gardening to access the pooled top-up. the state baseline stays untouched, obviously.

(iii) fraud against a node is morally worse than corporate tax dodging of the same scale, because you're stealing from neighbours you actually see, not some abstract treasry.

(iv) i reject the concept of the undeserving poor completely — it's a poisonous Victorian leftover and unemployed people don't forfeit basic dignity.

(v) if someone refuses every availble job in their area for years, they keep the full baseline but the nodes can bar them from the extras, and that's not deservingness policing, just collective self-defence.

i feel like (ii) and (iv) are pulling in different directions, and maybe (iii) relies on a moral categorisation that (iv) rejects? but i'm not sure if (v) is just a sleight of hand. can you rank the tensions by severity and say what'd be the cheapest revision? don't need a lecture, just an audit. also, sorry for the ramble — writing a novel about this stuff and my head's a bit messy.
