I'm in a course on democratic legitimacy and symbolic authority, and I'm trying to understand what constitutional monarchies actually commit themselves to philosophically. Consider the institutional practice across the UK, Scandinavia, and Japan: the monarch retains formal sovereignty—giving assent to legislation, appointing ministers, serving as head of state—yet by convention exercises these powers only on the binding advice of elected representatives. In Britain, royal assent has not been refused in over three centuries, though the Crown remains the formal source of executive and judicial authority; in Sweden and Norway, the monarch signs every law and formally appoints the government according to parliamentary arithmetic alone, with no discretionary judgment; and in Japan, the postwar constitution designates the Emperor as the symbol of the state and of the unity of the people, requires every state act to be performed on cabinet advice, and guarantees religious freedom—yet he continues to perform Shinto rituals that carry state significance.

This pattern seems philosophically puzzling. Democratic equality appears to reject hereditary, unaccountable office, yet these systems embed birthright precisely at the symbolic center. What theory of legitimacy, representation, or political equality does the practice presuppose? Must it rely on a sharp distinction between dignified symbolism and efficient democratic power, or can it be reconciled with republican principles that reject any non-elected source of authority?

I also want to know whether the practice is internally consistent. The UK asserts parliamentary sovereignty while preserving Crown-in-Parliament; Japan bars the Emperor from political expression while assigning him formal state functions; Scandinavian democracies combine universal suffrage with inherited office. Reconstruct the implicit philosophical theory—distinguishing what the practice strictly requires from what is merely conventional or historical accident—and say whether replacing that theory with a pure democratic-republican view would force the abolition of the monarchy's legal-formal powers, or whether the institution could survive as purely ornamental without them.
