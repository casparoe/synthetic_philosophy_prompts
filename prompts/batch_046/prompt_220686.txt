I'm on the appeals board for grad student conduct at my university. We've got a case that's turned into a philosophy problem whether we like it or not. A PhD student in teh cognitive sci program was expelled after it turned out a full thesis chapter was generated by a local LLM they ran on their laptop. Their appeal dosen't deny the facts; instead they claim that a debugging script running in the background — one they wrote months earlier — was configured to auto-restore any deleted output files. They say this means they had no alternative possibility of withholding the chapter once it existed, and they reference those classic counterfactual-intervener scenarios to argue they can't be morally responsible for submitting it. Half the board thinks this is just dressing up laziness in metaphysical language. The other half thinks algorithmic environments actually change how we apply the principle of alternative possibilities, since the intervener isn't an external agent but part of the agent's own prior setup. I need a draft memo for next week's vote recommending uphold, overturn, or remand with conditions. Make it a real document — clear bottom line, reasoning that takes the PAP point seriously without just repeating textbook examples, and an honest account of why the other side might still win. Don't turn it into a lit review; the student and the dean need something they can actualy argue with.
