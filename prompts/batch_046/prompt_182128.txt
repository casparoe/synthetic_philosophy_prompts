I am constructing a conceptual evaluation framework for an advanced philosophy of science seminar, and I find that standard taxonomies of responses to Nelson Goodman's grue paradox tend to encourage memorization rather than analytical extension. To remedy this, I ask you to rank five specific positions on a scale from 1 to 10 according to their capacity to resolve both the original chromatic induction problem and a structurally analogous case I will call an institutional grue. Define this analog precisely: consider a legal-institutional predicate L-valid that applies to contracts executed before a statutory reform in one way and to contracts executed after the reform in an incompatible way, where both projections are equally supported by the pre-reform evidential record and share identical formal properties, differing only in whether the temporal parameter is physical chronology or institutional history. The five positions to evaluate are: first, the entrenchment-of-predicates solution associated with Goodman; second, a syntactic or grammatical constraint that restricts projectible predicates by logical form alone; third, a Bayesian account that resolves projection by weighting simplicity priors; fourth, a pragmatic or vindicationist defense that appeals to the actual success and stability of inductive practice; and fifth, a deflationary denial that induction requires any normative philosophical solution beyond descriptive regularity. For each position, provide its numerical score and a single sentence of reasoning, explicitly stating whether the view distinguishes physical-time predicates from institutional-time predicates in a principled way, and whether that distinction strengthens or weakens its overall plausibility.

Please do not supply a bibliography, reading list, or citation to specific editions or page numbers; the exercise is intended to test conceptual coherence rather than bibliographic recall. Additionally, in your reasoning for each score, indicate whether the approach treats the institutional grue as genuinely parallel to the chromatic case or as dissolvable through some feature unique to legal or social institutions, since that judgment bears directly on whether the view offers a unified account of projectibility or merely relocates the problem across domains.
