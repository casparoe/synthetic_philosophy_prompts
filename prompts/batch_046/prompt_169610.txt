Write a short story about a scientist named Mary who has spent her entire life in a monochrome laboratory studying every physical and neural fact about color vision without ever experiencing color herself. After she finally leaves the facility and sees red for the first time, she concludes that her exhaustive prior knowledge already contained the phenomenal quality in a different representational format, undermining the classic knowledge argument against physicalism. Soon after, she meets a researcher from a discontinued parallel program who describes experiencing "glimmer"—a hue that apparently corresponds to no wavelength, no retinal activation pattern, and no entry in Mary's database. Explore how Mary attempts to integrate glimmer into her physicalist framework, without resolving whether this unmapped phenomenal state genuinely demonstrates the existence of non-physical properties.
