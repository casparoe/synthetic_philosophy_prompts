In biology we speak freely of counterfactual evolutionary histories—scenarios in which a given ecological niche is occupied not by the actual lineage but by a convergently evolved analog. Which framework better accommodates the biologist’s distinction between true homology and mere homoplasy, particularly in cases where functional and ecological properties are preserved but evolutionary origin differs?
