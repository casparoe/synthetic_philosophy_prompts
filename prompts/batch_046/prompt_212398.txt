As a novelist developing a philosophical fiction, I wish to commission a short story that interrogates analogy-making as the core of cognition, in the spirit of Hofstadter and Sander's Surfaces and Essences, but pushed into an unfamiliar register. Write a narrative of approximately nine hundred words set in a civilisation where minds do not encode propositions but continuously map surfaces onto essences through cross-domain resonance. The protagonist should be an archivist of mental patterns in a post-literate culture who catalogues thought not by subject but by analogical affinity. Her crisis arrives when she encounters a null-analogue: an object or event with abundant surface structure that refuses every analogical projection, possessing no essence that resonates elsewhere. The story should explore whether cognition can survive the absence of analogy, or whether the null-analogue reveals analogy itself to be a hidden dependency rather than a foundation. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the piece should stand on its conceptual logic alone.

Please present your response in exactly the following sections, in this order:

The Story (approximately 900 words): the narrative itself, developed with attention to the analogical architecture of the culture and the protagonist's encounter with the null-analogue.

Commentary on the choices made (approximately 300 words): an explanation of how the analogical cognition functions in the story, why the null-analogue was constructed as it was, and what the ending implies about the limits of analogical thinking.
