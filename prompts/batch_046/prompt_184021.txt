Design a thought experiment set in a present-day cognitive-science laboratory in which an investigator attempts to verify that a subject harbors a specific, non-functional quale—designated “phenomenal crimson”—using only first-person reports and conventional behavioral or neuroimaging measures. Engineer the scenario so that the three commitments standardly attributed to this posit—radical first-person privacy, intersubjective reportability, and causal efficacy in producing reports—form an inconsistent triad: lay out the verification protocol, show precisely which pair of commitments forces the abandonment of the third, and demonstrate that the contradiction is internal to the concept rather than an artifact of measurement error. Constraints: avoid inverted-spectrum cases, philosophical zombies, perfect predictors, and any technology beyond current psychophysics and neuroimaging; present the scenario in one paragraph. Then state the target belief, the verification method, the nearby worlds in which the method fails, and why each of the three commitments delivers the verdict it does. Finish by identifying the structural feature that makes the contradiction unavoidable, explaining what happens if that feature is removed, and giving the strongest reply available to a defender who insists that qualia remain coherent despite the triad.
