Covering science and ideas for some years, I have come to hold that Darwin's evolutionary reasoning and Turing's analysis of computation share a precise, underappreciated structure: both invert the usual explanatory order by showing that high-level competence—adaptive design in biology, intelligent behavior in machines—can be fully produced and accounted for without any underlying comprehension, and that this inversion is structural rather than merely contingent. I want to know what would genuinely count against this thesis. Not the standard objections that comprehension is hidden or emergent, but the arguments, conceptual distinctions, or empirical results that would show the inversion fails—that competence in one or both domains actually requires comprehension in a way that cannot be reconstructed post hoc. Please rank the defeaters from decisive to merely awkward, say which ones defenders of the inversion should themselves concede, and assess whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability—say, by allowing comprehension to be projected onto any successful system—and whether that insulation is a philosophical virtue or a defect.
