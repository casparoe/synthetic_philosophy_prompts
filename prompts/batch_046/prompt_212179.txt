The core question is whether impact can be measured relative to a counterfactual baseline that does not implicitly encode hidden normative commitments.

Within this space, I see four competing open lines: (1) whether every baseline-selection rule is necessarily value-laden, or whether a genuinely value-neutral reference class can be constructed; (2) whether distinguishing "ontological" side effects (changes to physical or state-level facts) from "epistemic" side effects (changes to the inferences available to observers, including the agent itself) is coherent and measurable without invoking a utility function; (3) whether logical uncertainty about an agent's own actions makes counterfactual impact reasoning incoherent, and whether an updateless or acausal substitute can restore coherence; and (4) whether impact ought to be defined globally, relative to the trajectory of the entire system, or locally within an arbitrarily bounded region — with global metrics requiring implausible omniscience and local ones seeming ad hoc.

I want a defended agenda, not a survey. Rank these lines — or replace them with superior ones — by explicit criteria: dependency (which questions are upstream of others), tractability for philosophers rather than ML researchers, neglectedness, and the magnitude of downstream change if solved. Explain the trade-offs between these criteria and identify the one crux on which most of the rest hangs. I am especially keen to know whether the ontological/epistemic distinction is a productive framing or a red herring.
