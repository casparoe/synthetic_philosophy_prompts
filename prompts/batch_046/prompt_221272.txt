my study group got into a weird argument last night about hedonism and whether it even makes sense. Nina started by saying pleasures are totally heterogenous -- the feeling of a hot shower, the satisfaction of solving a proof, and the nasty little pleasure of seeing someone fail are so different that asking "what do they have in common?" is like asking what a number and a color share. Jules said thats a strawman, modern hedonists dont need a shared phenomenal quality; pleasure is just whatever state has intrinsic positive value for the subject, defined by its motivational role not by how it feels. Nina pushed back that if you define pleasure by "what we want for its own sake" then hedonism becomes circular -- good life = life full of things we want = life full of pleasure = good life -- and youve explained nothing. Jules said circularity isnt the problem, unfalsifiability is, and hedonism risks that if it lets "pleasure" cover every pro-attitude. Then Nina brought up guilty or sadistic pleasures: they still feel good in some sense, but if hedonism says theyre good for the agent, thats morally repugnant, so either hedonism is false or it needs a hidden moral filter, which undermines its claim to be the whole story. Jules replied that hedonism is about wellbeing not morality, so the pleasure of cruelty can be genuinely good for the cruel person while being morally wrong, and that actually shows the view is consistent, not broken. Nina said okay but then you still havent solved heterogeneity -- youve just split pleasure into "good-for-you" and "good-simpliciter" without saying what unifies them. Jules tried to save it by saying all pleasures share a "hedonic tone" -- a kind of affective glow -- even intellectual ones, just dimmer, and that Mill's distinction between higher and lower pleasures captures the thickness without abandoning the common core. Nina said Mill just smuggles quality back in through the back door, and if the glow is that faint in intellectual pleasure then its not doing any work; youre really valuing the intellectual content, not the glow. They asked me to referee since I was just listening and I honestly dont know. Can you adjudicate move by move? Rate Nina's and Jules's main moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, briefly justify each rating, and say whos more right overall. Also, is there something both of them are missing -- maybe about how pleasure relates to attention or desire -- that makes the whole framing off? And could there be a version of hedonism that survives without either a ghostly shared feeling or a circular definition?
