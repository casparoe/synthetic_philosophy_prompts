Here are preliminary notes for an essay I am drafting on a limitation of Jeffrey conditionalization in multi-agent epistemic settings. I welcome your critical comments—particularly on whether the proposed extension is coherent, whether it genuinely resolves the disagreement problem I identify, and whether I have overlooked a simpler standard treatment.

Standard Jeffrey updating requires that an agent revise her probabilities over a fixed partition {E_i} by assigning new weights q_i while leaving all conditional probabilities P(H | E_i) unchanged. This works well for single-agent cases of perceptual uncertainty, but it presupposes that the agent's evidential frame—the very structure of the possibilities over which she conditions—is stable and fully known.

Consider, however, a situation in which two rational agents observe correlated signals but are uncertain not only about the content of the signals but about the correlation structure linking them. Suppose agent A receives evidence that shifts her credence over her own observational partition, yet she does not know whether her colleague B's partition is coupled to hers through a shared noise source of unknown strength. In such cases, applying Jeffrey's rule to the marginal distribution alone forces A to preserve conditional probabilities that are themselves defined relative to an unknown joint distribution. There is therefore no unique way to maintain Jeffrey's preservation condition.

I propose that we treat the correlation parameters as part of the prior and apply a joint Jeffrey-style update: maximize relative entropy subject to the constraint that the new marginals match the observed uncertain evidence, but allow the joint conditionals to shift in a way that respects common knowledge of rationality between the agents. If both agents apply this extended rule with shared knowledge of the constraint, they should converge on a common joint posterior, blocking the agreement-to-disagree results familiar from Bayesian social epistemology.

Is this extension formally tractable? Does it conflate epistemic uncertainty about evidence with metaphysical uncertainty about the world? And have I mischaracterized the standard literature—perhaps there is already a well-known treatment of correlated uncertain evidence within the Jeffrey framework that I have missed?
