I serve on my firm's Post-Incident Review Board; I am a software engineer without formal philosophy training. Last week, a logistics-routing system jointly maintained by Team A and Team B rerouted emergency supply trucks away from an active flood zone due to an emergent interaction between the routing heuristic and a live pricing feed, delaying aid by approximately six hours. The board is divided over how to respond. One faction urges formal moral censure against the lead engineers—suspending design privileges and recording personal blame—treating them as fully responsible participants in our moral practices. Another faction contends that because neither team could have anticipated the interaction, and because the system now incorporates a feedback mechanism that translates user complaints directly into its reward model, we should adopt an objective attitude: treat the failure as a malfunctioning mechanism requiring rollback, audit, and retraining without personal resentment, and direct any reactive response toward the institutional system-steward role rather than toward individuals. A minority proposal introduces distributed reactive attitudes, in which formal resentment is directed not at any individual but at the functional role itself, with restructuring of that role as the institutional response. Our charter requires a recommendation within 72 hours, specifying a concrete course of action with a clear time frame, and demands that we engage the strongest argument on the opposing side rather than dismiss it. Please draft the board's decision memo, selecting one of these three stances—participant-targeted censure, objective corrective-only measures, or distributed reactive restructuring—and providing an actionable timeline. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Lead with your bottom-line recommendation in the first sentence, followed by reasoning, with no preamble and without restating the question.
