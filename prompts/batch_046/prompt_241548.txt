In philosophy of moral psych the virtue ethics vs situaionist critique has been stuck since the 80s. Everyone cites Milgram and the same personality-data, virtue folks say charcter is real but fragile, situationists say it's an illusion of aggregation, and then they just stare at each other. My working theory is that the stall isn't empirical—both sides accept the cross-situational consistency numbers are low—but conceptual: virtue ethics needs moral evaluation to depend on a counterfactual robustness condition (would you still do it if the social payoff flipped?), while situationists think evaluation should only track local causal histories, so they're answering different questions about what 'having a trait' is for. Is that the real structure, or is there a hidden disagreement about explanation standards or about which cases count as data? I want a diagnosis using what each side actually says about evaluation versus prediction, whether 'charcter' is being used in two senses, and whether anything—a formal aggregation model, a new kind of experiment—could actually move it, or if it's just bedrock intuition about responsibility.
