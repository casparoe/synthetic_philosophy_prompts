Write a dialogue between a phenomenal uniformist and a heterogeneity pluralist about whether every pleasure shares a single intrinsic qualitative character. The uniformist should argue that beneath the surface differences—say, between tasting wine, solving a proof, or feeling warmth—there is a common "hedonic tone," analogous to a saturation dimension that can vary in intensity but not in kind. The pluralist should reply that cross-modal comparison reveals no such shared feel: the pleasure of intellectual insight and the pleasure of bodily warmth are as qualitatively dissimilar as color and sound, so positing a hidden uniform quality is an unjustified projection from language rather than experience. To keep the exchange from becoming a rehearsed summary, have them confront a constructed scenario: an artificial system that produces reward-optimization behavior functionally isomorphic to human pleasure-seeking—complete with trade-offs, satiation curves, and preference reversals—yet entirely lacks biological affective architecture or any introspective access. Each should say whether this case supports their view or undermines it, and they must respond directly to the other's interpretation rather than changing the subject. The uniformist should concede that introspective comparison across modalities is genuinely difficult, and the pluralist should concede that functional isomorphism might still call for some unifying explanation. End with both stating precisely the point on which they still disagree, with neither reduced to a strawman.
