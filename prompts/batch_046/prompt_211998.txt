I'm designing a graduate-level philosophy of science module that moves well beyond the standard textbook treatment of the Quine-Duhem thesis. My students have been arguing about whether underdetermination retains any real philosophical bite once we restrict its scope to localized modeling domains—specifically, competing molecular dynamics simulations of protein folding that share identical boundary conditions, force-field parameterizations, and observational outputs, yet diverge solely in how they weight entropic versus enthalpic contributions to the free-energy landscape. To push them past familiar responses, I have formulated four distinct philosophical stances toward this restricted underdetermination scenario, and I want a rigorous external evaluation of their relative defensibility.

Please rank these four stances from the most philosophically defensible (rank 1) to the least (rank 4), assign each a score out of 10, and provide a brief justification—no more than two sentences—explaining its placement. The stances are: (A) The Structural Preservationist, who holds that we should commit belief only to the invariant relational structure shared across all empirically equivalent simulations, treating the divergent weighting terms as representational conveniences lacking ontological import; (B) The Epistemic Suspensionist, who argues that because the evidence genuinely fails to discriminate between the weighting schemes, rational agents ought to withhold belief about which thermodynamic factor dominates; (C) The Pragmatic Selectionist, who claims that underdetermination is dissolved by engineering values—choose the model with lower computational cost and treat its ontological commitments as instrumentally justified but not truth-tracking; and (D) The Modal Pluralist, who proposes that both weighting schemes describe genuinely distinct modal profiles of the same physical system, so that empirical equivalence signals not epistemic ignorance but a form of ontological multiplicity in which each model is true relative to a different modal framing of physical possibility.

I am particularly interested in whether stance D represents a coherent extension of ontological pluralism or instead collapses into an unmotivated metaphysical inflation, and whether your ranking would shift if we replaced the protein-folding case with cosmological models that share the same cosmic microwave background data but differ only in their dark-energy parameterizations. Keep your analysis internal to the reasoning you can do without external references; I am testing conceptual coherence, not bibliographic recall.
