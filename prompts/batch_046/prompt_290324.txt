I have come to accept what I call ground-clearing localism about the meaning of life. The view holds that cosmic absurdity — the mismatch between human demands for transcendent justification and an indifferent universe — does not undermine meaning but dissolves a false precondition for it. Meaning is then constituted by the internal coherence of local projects, relationships, and values, sustained without appeal to grand narrative or external validation. The absurd functions not as a negation but as a cognitive boundary condition that clears the ground for this local coherence.

Please assess what would genuinely count against this position. I want an honest map of its exposure: which empirical results about motivation or well-being, which ethical arguments about the necessity of contributing to something larger than oneself, which metaphysical commitments regarding objective value, and which intuitions about futility or mortality would be decisive rather than merely awkward? Identify what a committed ground-clearing localist would themselves concede as damaging and what they could dismiss. Take a definite position on how vulnerable the view actually is, rank the defeaters from fatal to inconvenient, and give your reasons. Write in British English, and do not provide bibliographies or reading lists.
