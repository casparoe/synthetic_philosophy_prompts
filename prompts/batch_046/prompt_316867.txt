I'm defending my thesis chapter next Tuesday in the philosophy of mind and value seminar. Thesis: the heterogeneity problem for hedonism dissolves if we drop phenomenal essentialism and instead unify pleasures structurally—each is an "autovalidating affective loop" in which positive valence is sustained by self-referential reinforcement of the state's own content, not by a shared introspective quality. The audience knows the standard qualitative-uniformity and attitude-based responses, but leans skeptical: two phenomenal essentialists will insist there is an introspectively accessible "pleasantness" quality, and several affective-science-oriented members will treat "loops" as disguised neurofunctionalism. Give me the objections in the order they'll come, phrased in their vocabulary, with for each: what I should concede, what I should hold, a brief reply fitting a 90-minute seminar, and where that reply is weakest. Include my weakest defensive point—that "autovalidation" risks circularity if I can't distinguish it from sustained attention to a liked object—and the one question I'm hoping avoids: whether stripping away phenomenal commonality collapses my view into an attitude-based account. If "autovalidating loops" is incoherent or merely repackages existing functionalism without new explanatory work, tell me plainly instead of accommodating me.
