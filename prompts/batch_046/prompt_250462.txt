Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Dr. Althea Chen and Dr. Marcus Webb, who disagree about whether Darwin’s evolutionary inversion and Turing’s inversion of the intelligence question are genuinely parallel cases of competence without comprehension. Chen should argue that both are structurally identical inversions: complex functional success—adaptive design in one case, intelligent behavior in the other—is produced without the corresponding mental representation of goals, mechanisms, or meanings, and that recognizing this parallel dissolves the intuition that comprehension is a necessary condition for either biological adaptation or machine cognition. Webb should counter that the parallel is superficial and misleading—Darwinian competence is distributed across populations and deep time without any central processor or representational vehicle, whereas Turing’s machine is a localized system whose behavioral competence depends on a designer’s prior comprehension embedded in its architecture and training environment, so that the “missing comprehension” is located in a different place and is of a fundamentally different kind in each case. Have them engage with a specific borderline case: a synthetic evolutionary algorithm that generates novel antibiotic-resistance pathways in silico without human intervention, producing outputs that biologists can apply but do not mechanistically understand. Chen should claim this case shows the parallel holds in practice—competence emerges from selection-like processes without any participant comprehending the result—while Webb should argue it reveals exactly where the analogy breaks, because the algorithm’s competence is parasitic on the biologists’ interpretive framework and the engineer’s initial design choices in a way that natural selection is not. The dialogue must contain real back-and-forth: each character must respond directly to the other’s point about the location and kind of missing comprehension, not merely restate an opening position. End with each philosopher stating a clear, committed verdict on whether the Darwin–Turing parallel illuminates or obscures the ontology of understanding, with explicit reasons. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Take a definite position on which view is stronger, and identify one specific concession the weaker view is forced to make that actually strengthens its opponent’s case.
