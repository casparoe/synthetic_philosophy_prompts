I'm delivering the opening remarks at a one-day interdisciplinary workshop on explanatory pluralism in the human sciences. The audience is split: half are historians and sociologists steeped in Weber, and half are econometricians and causal-inference researchers for whom culture is, at best, an unobserved confounder; there may also be non-specialist guests in the gallery. Write a ten-minute address that treats Weber's Protestant ethic thesis not as settled history but as a live methodological challenge. Structure it around the distinction between Wertrationalität (value-rational action) and Zweckrationalität (instrumental-rational action): show that Weber's claim that ascetic Protestantism causally contributed to the development of modern capitalism depends on treating value-commitments as irreducible causes rather than as preferences or beliefs amenable to instrumental modelling. Then draw out the dilemma this poses for anyone who wishes to explain behaviour with values: either one preserves the causal autonomy of values and forfeits the standard tools of causal inference, or one reduces values to instrumental variables and dissolves the specifically Weberian explanation. The address should be intelligible to the non-specialists present, should avoid resolving the tension, and should present it as the workshop's defining problem. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke obscure secondary literature that a well-read but non-specialist model could not be expected to recall. Use British spelling and maintain a formal, polished tone throughout.
