For a 75-minute ethics workshop with about 15 people, design a hands-on trading game that makes participants feel the pull of lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism—basically the claim that once suffering crosses a line, no amount of pleasure can balance it out on the same scale. Give it concrete rules with small real stakes (candy, points, whatever). Include roles: traders who swap chips, an auditor who checks boards but doesn't know the hidden rule, and one silent player who only recieves 'pain chips' and can't trade away. The hidden mechanism should be physical—like a board with a slot that opens once three severe-pain tokens land there, so all pleasure chips slide off and become worthless. Don't explain the philosophy up front; let the group discover that one player is 'broken' and can't be compensated. Then lay out a timeline, payoff rules, and a debrief that connects the slot-opening moment to the incommensurability claim. Be honest about what the token game can't reproduce—probably the first-person quality of extreme suffering—and warn about one predictable failure mode, like players trying to 'fix' the broken board with more pleasure chips. Keep it practical, write in American English, and don't include any bibliography or citation list—just the runnable activity.
