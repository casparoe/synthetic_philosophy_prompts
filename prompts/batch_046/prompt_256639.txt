I've been trying to articulate my philosophical profile on freedom and self-deception for a graduate seminar on existentialism, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present it to the group. Here is the package of commitments I've arrived at:

(1) I accept the Sartrean thesis of radical freedom: even under extreme physical coercion, consciousness retains the ontological freedom to choose its attitude toward the situation, and to deny this is to fall into bad faith.

(2) I interpret bad faith not primarily as an individual's occasional self-deception, but as a structural feature of social institutions—the systematic pressure to treat persons as having fixed essences (occupational, gendered, national) rather than as self-creating consciousness.

(3) I reject the idea that the Look (le regard) necessarily alienates me; I think that when another recognizes me, I can authentically incorporate their perspective into my self-understanding, provided I maintain that their judgment is contingent and does not exhaust my being.

(4) I do not accept that consciousness is pure nothingness (néant); I hold instead that there is a minimal, pre-reflective embodied self-givenness—a kind of given "facticity of the body"—that sets non-determining constraints on choice.

(5) I think anguish (angoisse) is not an inevitable consequence of recognizing freedom, but arises specifically when one recognizes one's radical freedom while simultaneously denying one's responsibility for it.

(6) I maintain that authenticity requires fully embracing one's facticity—one's embodied history and social situation—rather than perpetually transcending it, and that this embrace is fully compatible with radical freedom.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, ranking the real conflicts by severity. For each tension, say whether the problem is internal to the commitments themselves or stems from how I've interpreted Sartre, identify the least costly revision, and then tell me which single change—if I could make only one—would restore the most coherence to the set. I am not sure whether (1) and (6) pull in opposite directions, whether (2) undermines (3), or whether (4) conflicts with the core of (1), so please be specific about where the fault lines actually lie.
