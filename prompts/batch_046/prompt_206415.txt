Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers on whether rational choice is possible among genuinely incommensurable values without covertly reducing them to a common metric. One should defend a "tragic choice" framework that distinguishes ordinal incommensurability—where no ranking exists—from cardinal incommensurability—where no shared scale exists—and apply both to a concrete case: allocating scarce resources between preserving an irreplaceable, un-digitized oral-history archive and an irreplaceable biosphere reserve, insisting that the rejected option leaves an irreducible loss-structure intact. The other should counter that any decision procedure, whether lexical, procedural, or deliberative, necessarily imposes an implicit evaluative currency that dissolves incommensurability into a disguised comparison. Require direct engagement: address whether loss-preservation avoids comparison, whether the ordinal/cardinal distinction survives practical deliberation, and whether the archive-reserve dilemma reveals a hidden metric or a genuinely tragic remainder. Keep the exchange formal, concise, and free of strawmen.
