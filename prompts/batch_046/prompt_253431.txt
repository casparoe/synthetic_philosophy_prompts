Be precise about which normative claims correspond to which formal properties, and take care not to smuggle philosophical assumptions—such as an irreducible notion of coercion or welfare—into the technical vocabulary under a different name.

Then provide a detailed residue report: identify which elements of the philosophical debate translate cleanly—for instance, whether welfare-promoting defaults correspond to mechanisms that improve the receiver's expected payoff given their beliefs—identify which elements require distortion to fit the formal framework, such as framing autonomy not as a revealed preference but as a hard constraint on the designer's strategy space, and identify whether there is any dimension of the moral objection to being nudged—particularly a phenomenological or relational aspect of being guided without explicit awareness—that lacks any counterpart in information-design language. Finally, assess whether these residues indicate a genuine limitation of formal mechanism vocabulary for capturing normative objections, or whether they suggest that the philosophical objections to paternalism depend on distinctions that become insubstantial once the interaction is modeled with sufficient precision.
