I'm preparing remarks for a seminar on political vocabulary and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is the position I intend to defend:

(a) The phrase "Western values" is descriptively incoherent: any proposed list—rule of law, secular governance, individual autonomy, gender equality, freedom of expression—either excludes societies conventionally called Western or includes societies conventionally excluded, and there is no shared metaphysical or historical essence that unifies the category.

(b) Nevertheless, the phrase performs indispensable rhetorical work: it functions as a boundary marker distinguishing pluralist democratic institutions from authoritarian alternatives, and abandoning it in public discourse cedes legitimate defensive ground.

(c) The specific norms I actually defend—constitutional constraint, freedom of conscience, gender equality—are normatively universal, not civilizational; framing them as "Western" is historically misleading and morally parochial, since their justification is available to any rational agent regardless of cultural origin.

(d) In immigration and integration policy, I treat these norms not as cultural practices to be adopted but as institutional preconditions: I support requiring newcomers to accept constitutional loyalty and reject authoritarian governance, and I call this "assimilation to Western values" as a practical shorthand.

(e) I reject all exceptionalist or genealogical narratives that ground these norms in Western uniqueness—whether Enlightenment teleology, Christian heritage, or civilizational destiny—and insist their authority derives solely from contractualist or deliberative justification.

I can see possible frictions—between the descriptive incoherence in (a) and the policy shorthand in (d), between the universalist claim in (c) and the particularist assimilation language in (d), and between my rejection of exceptionalism in (e) and the boundary-marking function in (b)—but I do not know which are substantive and which dissolve under closer analysis. Please audit the full commitment set: rank the genuine tensions by severity, for each say what must give and what the least costly revision is, identify which friction is only apparent, and indicate where my commitments implicitly bind me to a view I have not acknowledged.
