I've been formalising my views on personal identity and mind uploading for a discussion group, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present them. Here is roughly where I stand:

(1) I take a broadly Lockean-Parfitian view: diachronic personal identity consists in psychological continuity supported by the right kind of causal connection, not in biological substrate.

(2) I think gradual, neuron-by-neuron replacement with functionally equivalent silicon preserves identity, provided the causal-functional architecture remains continuous.

(3) I think destructive scanning and simulation breaks that causal chain, so the resulting copy is not me, even if psychologically identical.

(4) I am committed to substrate-independence in principle, yet I also hold that actual phenomenal consciousness depends on specific biological implementation details—neuromodulator dynamics, glial signalling patterns, and temporal integration scales—that a standard silicon implementation would miss unless explicitly reconstructed.

(5) From (1)-(3) I draw that only gradual uploads inherit my legal and moral status; destructive copies do not.

(6) I take the hard problem seriously: a perfect functional duplicate could in principle lack my phenomenal experience and be a zombie relative to me, but I deny this undermines identity, since identity is purely psychological.

Please work through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. Rank the tensions by severity, specify for each the cheapest revision and what it costs, and identify the single change that resolves the most conflicts. I am not signalling which commitment I would be most willing to drop.

Please reply in American English.
