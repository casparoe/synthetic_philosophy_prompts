I have been studying the Knobe effect—the asymmetry in which observers judge a harmful side-effect of a corporate policy to be intentional while judging an otherwise identical beneficial side-effect to be unintentional. This pattern convinces me that ordinary language-users possess no descriptive concept of intentional action whatsoever; the word 'intentional' functions exclusively as a post-hoc vehicle for moral blame, with no independent cognitive content. If this interpretation is correct, then the distinction between intentional and accidental harm is not merely unreliable but conceptually empty—a rhetorical device with no legitimate place in either normative ethics or legal doctrine. Given these premises, should philosophers and jurists abandon the intentional/accidental distinction entirely? Please answer in American English.
