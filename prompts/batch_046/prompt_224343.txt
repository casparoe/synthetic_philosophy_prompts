I'm serving on my university's Civic Engagement and Ethics Review Board, and we are drafting procedural guidance for the municipal council's public-comment rules after a contentious zoning hearing last month. The proposal concerned a community hospitality center, and its supporters—representing several faith traditions—defended it with both secular claims about neighborhood cohesion and explicit theological reasoning: that the site carries what they called a sacred obligation of welcome, and that zoning policy should recognize this status. Two board members argue that allowing such reasoning in the official record violates the core demands of public reason and effectively privileges a comprehensive doctrine; a third faction proposes a compromise they term motivational transparency with justificatory independence, under which citizens may present religious convictions as part of their personal testimony but the council may cite only secular, accessible reasons in its official justification. I am unsure whether this compromise is philosophically coherent or merely a rhetorical accommodation, and I need the board to take a clear position before the next review cycle.

Please draft the short opinion memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete procedural course—whether to reject the testimony, require full secular translation, pilot a dual-register comment system, or adopt another mechanism—with a specific implementation timeline (for example, within the next sixty-day review window). The memo must treat the supporters' theological position with genuine seriousness rather than reducing it to psychological background, and it should directly engage the strongest objection on the losing side—namely, that any accommodation of religious reasoning in the official record undermines civic neutrality. In your reasoning, clearly separate what is broadly settled in discussions of public reason—such as the need for shared, accessible justification—from what remains genuinely contested among knowledgeable practitioners, such as whether motivational transparency can substitute for full translation or whether neutrality requires exclusion rather than accommodation, and do not present any contested interpretation as if it were the consensus view. I want a document the board could actually vote on, not an abstract survey of the literature; please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests, since the recommendation must stand on its own reasoning.
