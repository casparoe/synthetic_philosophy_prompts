Design a 90-minute simulation for 18 university students in a political-philosophy workshop on welfare and moral desert. The goal is to make participants feel, before anyone names it, how verification mechanisms do not merely filter out fraud but conceptually transform "need" into "compliance-capacity," thereby manufacturing the very category of the "undeserving poor" that they claim to discover. Lead with the bottom line — the hidden structural twist in the rules and the visceral insight it should produce — in the first sentence, then present the complete runnable design, timeline, debrief, failure predictions, and honest limitation, with no preamble and no restating of the question.

Use three roles: Claimants (9), Caseworker Board (6), and Auditors (3). Claimants draw hidden profile cards — high-need with high barriers, high-need with low barriers, or fraudulent — and apply for a fixed pool of 30 welfare points. To apply, they must complete physical bureaucratic tasks (filling forms, queuing, repeating requests) that consume time they could otherwise spend earning private "survival" tokens outside the game. In Round 1 (10 minutes), the Board writes verification rules intended to catch fraud. In Rounds 2 and 3 (each 15 minutes), Claimants submit applications; the Board approves, denies, or refers them; Auditors may spend 3 pool points to investigate one case, permanently removing those points from distribution. The hidden twist: because low-barrier claimants complete paperwork faster and appear more cooperative, the Board will predictably — and without conscious intent — shift its criteria toward documentation speed and procedural adherence rather than need, scoring points for "efficiency" and "integrity" but carrying no metric for false negatives.

In Round 4 (10 minutes), redistribute remaining points, reveal all hidden profiles, and tally scores. The debrief (30 minutes) should map each moment onto the idea: the Board's rule-rewrite onto the manufacturing of undeservingness; the Claimants' hidden barriers onto the false-negative cost; the Auditors' spending onto the moral economy of suspicion. Ask participants whether they noticed the criterion shift in real time. Give no philosophical framing or lecture before Round 4; name the concept only in debrief. Predict failures and remedies: Claimants may visibly perform compliance rather than experience it (keep roles silent and embodied); Boards may become punitive (remind them scoring rewards speed, not cruelty); emotional discomfort may derail analysis (use a 2-minute silent written reflection before open discussion). Finally, state honestly that the simulation reproduces the structural and epistemic shift but cannot reproduce the long-term psychological stigma or material dependency that real welfare conditionality produces — the facilitator must supply that conceptual bridge verbally.
