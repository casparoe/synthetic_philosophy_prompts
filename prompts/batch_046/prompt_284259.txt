For a long-form feature I am developing on reproductive ethics and the limits of moral obligation, I want to state my philosophical commitments precisely and subject them to a consistency audit before publication. Here is my profile. First, I accept Benatar's asymmetry: the absence of pain is good for beings who never come to exist, but the absence of pleasure is not bad for them; conversely, suffering is a harm to those who do exist, while pleasure is a benefit. Second, I ground this in what I call the actual-subject constraint—only beings who actually exist, or who will necessarily exist regardless of our specific choices, can be the proper subjects of harm or benefit. Third, I apply this framework to human reproduction: since a potential person has no prior interest in being created, bringing them into existence subjects them to inevitable harm without consent, and I take this to make procreation always wrong, irrespective of expected life quality. Fourth, in environmental ethics I maintain that we have stringent obligations to preserve conditions for future sentient beings who will exist regardless of our reproductive decisions, but we have no corresponding duty to maximize the number of happy beings; I reject both the replacement argument and what I term pure-potential obligations to merely possible people. Fifth, I extend the same reasoning to farmed animals: breeding sentient beings into existence for consumption violates the actual-subject constraint, even if their lives are net-positive. Sixth, I hold that moral evaluation should be modally invariant—the wrongness of an act should not depend on which merely possible world happens to result—yet I also allow that our obligations to future generations depend precisely on which beings will actually exist in this world. Which of these commitments genuinely conflict, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious; for each genuine tension, specify which thesis must give and what that costs the overall framework; and identify the single revision that would clean up the most problems without collapsing the structure.
