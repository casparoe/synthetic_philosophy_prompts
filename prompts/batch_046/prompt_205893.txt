I was arguing with a friend about illusionism — you know, the view that phenomenal consciousness isn't actually real, it's just a kind of cognitive mispresentation or false belief that there's some what-it's-like quality — and we kept circling the same points. Frankish and Dennett-style illusionists say there's no intrinsic feel, just a system that represents itself as having feel, and then the Chalmers-style realists come back with the refridgerator-light objection: you can't be wrong about your own experience right now. But it feel like nobody moves because both sides just repeat that loop, and I'm trying to figure out if the debate is actually stalled for a deeper reason.

My working guess is that there's a hidden layer about introspective transparency. Illusionists seem to treat phenomenal judgments as mediated — like they go through a phenomenal concept strategy that can systematically misfire, kind of like how your visual system fills in the blind spot without you noticing. Realists, on the other hand, treat saying it feels red as directly revealing an intrinsic property, not as a cognitive tag that could be erroneous. So maybe they aren't disagreeing about consciousness itself but about the epistemology of first-person access — wether introspection reveals properties or just generates beliefs with a certain modal profile. There's also this thing I've been thinking about: in inattentional blindness, people can report absence of experience in a way that parallels reporting absence of belief. If illusionists could point to genuine phenomenal absence reports that mirror cognitive absence, that might shift things, but I don't know if anyone has actually done that work.

So here's what I want: a diagnosis of why this debate hasn't moved. Is it substantive but underdetermined? Is it verbal, with phenomenal consciousness meaning the real qualitative property for one side and the introspective appearance of quality for the other? Or are they answering different questions — one about ontology, one about self-representation? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress? A formal model of introspective error? A conceptual distinction between phenomenal presence and phenomenal judgment? Or is it just bedrock intuition that can't be argued past? I want this tied to what illusionists actually say about misrepresentation versus what realists say about transparency, not just a survey of names.
