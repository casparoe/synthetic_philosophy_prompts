I am convening a three-year philosophy-of-science working group with limited resources — roughly five senior researchers — and I want to avoid rehearsing the standard Lakatos-versus-Kuhn or Lakatos-versus-Feyerabend literature. Instead, I need a defended agenda: which open conceptual problems within Lakatos's account of scientific research programmes genuinely matter for evaluating contemporary science, and in what order should we tackle them?

Consider the framework's central machinery — the hard core, the protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses, and the positive heuristic — but apply it to domains where theoretical unity is weak and anomalies accumulate incrementally rather than through decisive refutation, such as climate system modelling or psychiatric nosology. In these fields, auxiliary adjustments often become structurally embedded: removing them collapses predictive machinery rather than freeing a core theory for testing. This raises a specific tension: can the protective belt remain genuinely expendable, as Lakatos's normative account seems to require for progressive problem-shifts, when in practice the belt and the hard core are methodologically inseparable?

Please prioritise the open questions and lines of work that flow from this tension. For example: whether there is a non-circular criterion for distinguishing a degenerating protective belt from a legitimately complexified heuristic; whether the hard-core/protective-belt distinction survives at all in programmes lacking a single unifying law; and whether Lakatosian appraisal needs supplementation by accounts of robustness or model pluralism that he did not develop. Rank them, and defend the ordering using criteria relevant to philosophers without laboratory access — conceptual tractability, upstream importance for science policy or interdisciplinary evaluation, and dependence on other unresolved issues. What evidence — conceptual, historical, or from the sociology of these diffuse fields — would move an item up or down your list over the next few years? I am less interested in a survey of the literature than in a reasoned allocation of three years' collective attention.
