Hilbert's hotel is solved by pairing each current guest with an even-numbered room: the guest in room 1 moves to room 2, room 2 to room 4, and so on, leaving every odd room empty. Then each new guest takes an odd room. This uses Cantor's pairing idea, mapping both the original guests and the newcomers onto the natural numbers, so both sets have cardinality ℵ₀. Because the union still has cardinality ℵ₀, the total number of guests hasn't actually increased—there are just more people arranged within the same infinite size. So the hotel shows infinity is only potential: you can keep adding guests through an unending process, but there is never a completed total that is larger than its parts.

Regarding Hilbert, he was essentially a finitist in his philosophy of mathematics. His consistency program required that every proof be reducible to concrete, finitary symbol manipulation without reference to infinite objects. This means he rejected actual infinities as real entities. Even though he invented the hotel paradox, he intended it only as a formal illustration of rearrangement rules, not as evidence that infinite sets exist as completed totalities. Cantor believed in actual infinities, but Hilbert's program shows he agreed with the finitists about ontology, using formalism to avoid committing to infinite objects.
