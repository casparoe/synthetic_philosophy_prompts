I'm a journalist covering the intersection of moral philosophy and institutional governance, and I'm constructing a detailed feature on how universities are formalizing philosophical frameworks in disciplinary codes. For this piece, I need you to draft the internal decision memo that a university's Student Conduct and Ethical Response Board might issue in the following case.

The board operates under a recently adopted Restorative Reactive-Attitude Protocol, which distinguishes between participant-mode proceedings—where emotional reactive attitudes such as resentment and moral indignation are expressed and acknowledged as part of accountability—and objective-mode proceedings, where the respondent is treated solely as an object of clinical and social management without moral blame. The protocol was designed to operationalize P.F. Strawson's distinction between participant reactive attitudes and the objective attitude, but it has never been applied to a case involving temporary dissociative incapacity.

The concrete case: A doctoral student in cognitive neuroscience, with no prior disciplinary record, experienced a documented dissociative fugue episode—verified by independent psychiatric evaluation—during which they destroyed shared laboratory equipment and sent messages that a lab colleague experienced as threatening. The colleague, as the affected party, has requested a full participant-mode hearing, arguing that moral resentment is appropriate regardless of clinical diagnosis because the harm was real and the student retains general moral agency. The respondent's representative counters that applying reactive attitudes to behavior occurring during verified dissociative incapacity violates the very logic of Strawsonian responsibility: reactive attitudes presuppose the capacity for responsible agency, which was absent during the episode; therefore only objective-mode measures—clinical monitoring, equipment restitution, and supervised lab reentry—are philosophically justified.

The board must issue a short written opinion recommending a concrete course of action with a clear time frame—approve participant-mode, approve objective-mode, or approve a structured hybrid—and it must provide reasoning that reviewers and both parties could respond to. The memo should treat the colleague's demand for moral acknowledgment seriously rather than dismissing it as mere emotional reaction, and it should honestly engage the strongest argument for the position the board ultimately rejects. Please draft this memo in formal institutional language, with an explicit bottom-line recommendation and a fair treatment of the losing side's central consideration.
