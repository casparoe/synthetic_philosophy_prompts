Plan a fifty-minute first-year philosophy tutorial on Sartre’s radical freedom and bad faith for students who have read only the café-waiter excerpt. Do not use the waiter example; instead, build the entire session around the experience of being caught in the Look—the moment one feels objectified by another consciousness and then either accepts or denies that reduction. Specify the order in which the pieces must be introduced and justify whether the Look should come before radical freedom or after it. Identify the one concrete scenario that should carry the explanation, describing exactly what it must include and exclude so that it illustrates bad faith rather than mere social pressure or self-deception. List the misconceptions students will predictably bring—such as equating freedom with doing whatever one wants, reducing bad faith to lying to oneself, or treating the Look as mere embarrassment—and state the precise point in the sequence where each should be headed off. Include one end-of-session check that reveals whether a student has grasped radical freedom or is only repeating the formula. Finally, say explicitly which part of Sartre’s ontology (for example, the facticity/transcendence distinction or being-for-itself versus being-in-itself) you will leave out entirely and announce as beyond the scope of the tutorial.
