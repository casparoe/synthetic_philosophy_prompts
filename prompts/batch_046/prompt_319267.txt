Write a dialogue between two philosophers of equal competence—call them A and B—debating whether epistemic bootstrapping can justify reliance on a newly developed mnemonic prosthesis: an implanted lattice that reconstructs episodic memories and reports a 99.7% fidelity rate. A should defend the view that one can acquire knowledge of the lattice's reliability by using it to verify its own reconstructions, embracing the easy-knowledge result and arguing that this is structurally parallel to justifying perception by perceptual means. B should object that this is viciously circular, distinguish epistemic from logical circularity, and demand an account of why calibrating a method does not require prior independent justification of that very method. The exchange must be genuinely responsive: A should answer B's circularity charge directly rather than dismissing it as verbal, and B should engage with A's attempt to separate belief-justification from reliability-tracking rather than ignoring it. At one point, have A propose that the lattice avoids circularity because its error-correction protocol checks outputs against native neurochemical markers rather than against other lattice outputs; B should reply by pressing whether interpreting those markers already depends on lattice-mediated calibration. Keep the dialogue focused on the conceptual structure of bootstrapping and avoid appeals to empirical studies, named texts, or bibliographic references.
