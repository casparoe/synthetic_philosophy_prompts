I hold a specific reading of Maimonides on animal sacrifice: not merely that it was a tolerated concession to Israel's pagan-conditioned habits, but that its extreme ritual regulation served a negative pedagogical function — by restricting bloodshed to a tightly defined space and procedure, the law taught that violence outside those boundaries was illegitimate, gradually weaning the people away from the need for it altogether. What would have to be true for this interpretation to fail? Rank them by how damaging they are, note which ones a defender of this reading could plausibly absorb, and assess honestly whether the view has been qualified so heavily that it is now unfalsifiable — and whether that is a problem for a historical claim of this kind.
