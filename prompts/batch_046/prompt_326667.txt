Please explain the conceptual structure of what I call the reflexive calibration loop in epistemic modesty: an agent applies an outside-view base rate—for instance, that scholars in her discipline systematically overestimate their immunity to motivated reasoning—to correct her inside-view confidence in her own rational assessment of that discipline. The loop arises because lowering her confidence in her reasoning via the outside view simultaneously lowers her confidence in the applicability of that very correction to her particular case. I want a committed analysis: is this loop genuinely vicious, or does it stabilize at a non-arbitrary equilibrium? Take a definite position on whether an agent can consistently maintain both (a) that she ought to apply the outside-view correction to her first-order beliefs, and (b) that she retains sufficient inside-view authority to identify herself as a member of the reference class to which the correction applies. If you think these commitments are incompatible, specify exactly which one must be abandoned and why; if you think they are compatible, identify the precise conceptual mechanism—distinct from empirical psychology—that blocks the regress. Ground the mechanics in a concrete but non-exotic scenario, such as a philosopher of science assessing her own susceptibility to theory-laden observation, but do not appeal to bibliographic references or empirical literature.
