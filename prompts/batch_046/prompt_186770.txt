Consider a historian of the Great Divergence who has ruled on whether specific comparative questions are well-posed. Her verdicts so far: comparing Britain and the Netherlands around 1800 is well-posed, since both were high-wage, coal-adjacent commercial economies facing similar labor-cost pressures; comparing "Europe" and "Africa" as blocs is ill-posed, because it assumes civilizational essences and erases internal variation; asking why Song China did not mechanize in the 11th century is well-posed only when restricted to concrete bottlenecks like coal chemistry and labor substitution, and ill-posed when it invokes broad cultural disposition; asking "why the Great Divergence at all" is ill-posed, since divergence was gradual and multi-causal rather than a discrete event; and asking "Britain or Qing China by 1850" is well-posed if framed around colonial resource extraction and energy geography, ill-posed if framed around rationality versus stagnation.
