I'm writing up my philosophical profile on consciousness for a graduate seminar, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present it. Here is where I've landed, adopting one framing that is not exactly standard in the literature: I take consciousness to be a "user illusion" not in the eliminative sense, but in the interface-model sense—specifically, I think the brain constructs a virtual "subject" analogous to an operating-system desktop, not to deceive the organism but to serve predictive processing and social coordination; phenomenal experience is real, but its first-person "ownership" is a representational artifact of that interface. Alongside this, I hold the following commitments: (1) strict physicalism, in the sense that every mental state supervenes on brain states with no non-physical ontology; (2) that phenomenal concepts—our direct, first-person modes of grasping experience—are genuinely special, irreducible to physical-functional descriptions, and carry an epistemic privilege that explains the explanatory gap; (3) that the hard problem is mislocated: the gap between neural process and experience is not ontological but a representational-format mismatch, like the gap between transistor states and desktop icons, so once we recognize the interface nature of consciousness the mystery dissolves into architecture; (4) that zombie scenarios remain genuinely conceivable, though I deny that conceivability is a reliable guide to metaphysical possibility here, precisely because interface representations are engineered to present themselves as necessarily tied to their own existence; (5) that there is nothing it is like to be a thermostat, but there is something it is like to be any system with a sufficiently integrated predictive self-model, and in our case that "what-it-is-like-ness" just is the illusory interface rather than a raw physical property; and (6) that this framework applies symmetrically: an artificial system with an equally integrated self-model would have consciousness in exactly the same interface-illusion sense that I do, with no further ontological addition required.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only appear to be, and what the cheapest revision would be in each case. I want the tensions ranked by severity, not listed as equally bad. In particular, I'm worried that my account of phenomenal concepts in (2) might undermine the interface-dissolution of the hard problem in (3), and that my allowance of zombie conceivability in (4) might conflict with the physicalist supervenience claimed in (1) together with the interface architecture in (3) and (6). But I also want you to check whether there is an apparent tension between physicalism and special phenomenal concepts that actually dissolves on closer inspection. Be specific about which commitment has to give, whether giving it up costs me the interface framing or just a peripheral claim, and whether any revision to (4) or (5) is forced by the combination of (1), (3), and (6).
