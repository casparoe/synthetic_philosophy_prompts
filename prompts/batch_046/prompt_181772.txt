Im giving the toast at my PhD advisor's retirement dinner. She taught philosophy of religion for about forty years, and her whole research program was a middle path between James's will to believe and Clifford's evidentialism — she called it the evidential leash, meaning you can take a belief-leap but only if you're eventually tied back to some actual evidence. Roughly half the room are philosophers who know James and Clifford inside out, the other half are family who dont know either name. Write a five-minute toast that uses the evidential leash idea to actually structure what you say about her career: it should explain why she defended belief without letting it run wild, comfort the family without turning into a lecture, and land as affection rather than summary. No citations or reading lists please.
