I am a retired experimental physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, has begun attending philosophy seminars with the same habit of demanding that abstract claims be made observable; I remain skeptical of both heroic biography and faceless structural forces, and I want my intuition sharpened rather than flattered. For a single two-hour session with fourteen advanced undergraduates and graduate students in a philosophy-of-history workshop, please design one specific, runnable classroom simulation—not a menu of alternatives—that forces the participants themselves to experience the tension between the Great Man theory and structural explanation, specifically regarding whether Nazi Germany was historically overdetermined by Weimar institutional collapse, economic crisis, and cultural resentment, or whether Hitler's particular rhetorical gift and ideological fixation were necessary to produce that specific regime rather than some other authoritarian settlement.

The activity should divide the group into four structural factions—economic, institutional, cultural-military, and bureaucratic-legal—each holding hidden cards indicating crisis severity and institutional fragility, and should include a single "charismatic demagogue" token that may only be activated when a coalition crosses a defined structural threshold; run three rounds corresponding to 1929–1933, requiring collective votes on emergency decrees, coalition partnerships, and ideological framing, with payoffs arranged so that identical structural conditions without the token produce a conventional conservative-authoritarian outcome, while the presence of the token combined with weak structural conditions fails to seize power and collapses into parliamentary deadlock. After the simulation, provide a debrief that maps specific decision points—such as the round in which a coalition chooses radical racial framing over bureaucratic consolidation—onto the logical distinction between necessary and sufficient conditions, and take a definite position: argue either that the mechanics demonstrate structural conditions made some authoritarian outcome inevitable but Hitler's particular form was contingent, or conversely that without the token the crisis dissipates into ordinary parliamentary crisis—do not present both as equally plausible and leave me to choose. Be explicit about what participants should come away seeing, include an honest note about which feature of actual historical causation—perhaps the long-term path dependence of anti-Semitic state policy or the irreducible psychological particularity of one man's paranoia—the rules cannot reproduce and that you must supply verbally, and address what will predictably go wrong when the activity is run—such as one faction dominating through hidden information—and how a facilitator should handle it.
