Construct a branching taxonomy — presented as a decision tree — of the metaethical positions that arise when one takes seriously the claim that WEIRD populations display systematically different folk-moral intuitions from those in small-scale societies, particularly along harm and fairness versus loyalty, authority, and purity dimensions. The tree should open with a root node asking whether the empirical divergence is best read as variation in an underlying moral grammar, variation only in applied norms, or an artefact of experimental framing. From there, build each branch using concrete diagnostic cases on which positions genuinely split: for instance, whether cross-cultural disagreement over accidental harm versus ritual impurity reveals a difference in core moral concepts or only in contextual salience; whether philosophers ought to discount WEIRD intuitions in reflective equilibrium because they constitute a statistical outlier; and whether the divergence extends to thin concepts such as harm and fairness or remains confined to thick moral practices. Each internal node must be phrased as a claim or case comparison answerable without knowing the position labels in advance. Every leaf should carry either the name of a named position — such as strong universalism, domain-specific pluralism, thick-moral relativism, convergence-objectivism, or WEIRD-corrected constructivism — or the label unnamed, together with a single sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Include any logical combination of answers that lacks a named defender as an empty branch, briefly diagnosing whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. The tree must clearly separate views that deny divergence altogether from those that accept it but preserve objectivity via convergence theories, domain restriction, or methodological correction; flag any pair of leaves that share a commitment and therefore collapse into one another. Write in British English, for an advanced undergraduate seminar audience familiar with basic moral psychology but not specialised metaethics, keep the tree to between eight and twelve leaves, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
