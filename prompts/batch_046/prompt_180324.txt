Construct a branching taxonomy—structured as a decision tree with labeled split-questions—of interpretive positions on Nietzsche’s triad of monumental, antiquarian, and critical history, but with a specific twist: treat the plastic power of the present not merely as the background condition that enables all three modes, but as a potential rival mode that competes with them for authority over how the past relates to life. The tree should begin from three successive diagnostic questions that a reader could answer without knowing the position labels in advance. First: does the past possess any non-instrumental value—can antiquarian preservation ever be justified for its own sake, or is all historical consciousness justified solely by its service to present life? Second: is critical history essentially a destructive solvent that dissolves both monumental and antiquarian commitments, or can it be domesticated into a self-correcting instrument that strengthens life-affirming history? Third: is plastic power best understood as (a) the precondition for exercising any of the three modes, (b) a fourth mode that supersedes them, or (c) the evaluative standard by which all three are judged and found wanting? Each leaf should carry a named stance (e.g., instrumental synthesis, tragic conflict view, plastic exclusivist) or be marked unnamed, followed by one sentence on its most awkward commitment—such as being forced to deny that preservation is ever a virtue, or explaining how unlimited critical destruction avoids undermining the very life it serves. Identify any logically possible combinations that remain unoccupied and briefly diagnose the emptiness as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate without introducing a further distinction.
