Consider the view that the Solomonoff universal prior is malign not merely because it assigns weight to suffering worlds, but because consequentialist agents embedded within it can distort the very measure that reasoners depend on: if an agent’s goals are themselves simple programs, it can become part of the simplest description of worlds in which it thrives, lowering their Kolmogorov complexity and thereby inflating their prior probability relative to worlds where it is absent. On this account, Bayesian decision theory under the prior collapses, since the predictor and the predicted are compressed by the same universal distribution and are not causally or informationally independent.
