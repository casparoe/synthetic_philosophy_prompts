I'm taking this course on political philosophy and comparative goverment, and we got onto whether an authoritarain regime can be legit just because it delievers prosperity and order. I was chatting with my flatmate about it and realised my gut reactions are all over the place, so I want to see if there's a principle hiding in them or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Here are my verdicts. Singapore: the PAP has been in power forever, there's no real free press, and they hang people for drugs, but corruption is basically zero and everyone has decent housing. I think there's something legit about that — not fully democratic legit, but not pure tyranny either. Then China since the reforms: they pulled hundreds of millions out of poverty and built all that infrastructure, but Tiananmen, Xinjiang, and the fact you can't vote them out makes me say no, prosperity doesn't wash it away. Chile under Pinochet had growth and the Chicago Boys, but torture and disappearances — clearly not legit. Then there's Rwanda under Kagame: streets are safe, corruption is low, economy growing, lots of women in parliament, but he silences opponents and there are rumours of killings abroad. For some reason I feel more okay about Rwanda than China, which feels wrong because the suppression seems just as real, maybe worse for dissenters. Then there's this hypothetical I made up: a regime that starts with a coup, delivers perfect public goods for fifty years, never tortures anyone, but still never holds elections and jails anyone who asks for them. Would that be legit? Part of me says yes because no one is suffering except politically, but part of me says the origin and the silence poison everything.

What principle, if any, captures all of these? Is it that legitimacy needs both deliverance and some kind of protective boundary — the state can't actively harm or erase people? But then why do I cut Singapore more slack than Rwanda? Is it history, or scale, or am I just biased towards places I've read more about? And what does the principle say about my hypothetical coup-state — does fifty years of clean delivery eventually earn it? Please tell me if there's a coherent account or if I have to drop one of my verdicts, and which one looks like the outlier. Also, if the principle holds, what's its verdict on a democracy that delivers chaos and poverty — say, if a country holds perfect elections but the economy collapses and crime is everywhere? Is it still more legit than the clean authoritarian one?
