I serve on the Institutional Review and Funding Board of the Meridian Institute, which oversees interdisciplinary grants at the boundary of normative philosophy and applied systems design. We have received a proposal titled “Autonomous Normative Architecture: A Non-Agentive Reason-Generating Framework for Clinical Triage.” The researchers propose to deploy a system that does not model human beliefs, intentions, or mental states; instead, it computes structural coherence relations among normative premises—such as distributive fairness weights, urgency coefficients, and equity-under-uncertainty parameters—and outputs directive recommendations (for example, “Prioritize Patient A over Patient B because the coherence weight of equity-under-uncertainty is maximized”). They claim these outputs constitute free-floating rationales: reasons that possess normative authority independently of any reasoner, deriving their force solely from the logical structure of the coherence network rather than from psychological states. They seek approval and pilot funding for deployment in a single hospital triage unit.

Half the board argues that without an accountable agent behind the outputs, these are merely causal computations, not reasons, and that funding them would conflate structural correlation with normative justification. The other half argues that if the coherence conditions genuinely mirror objective normative structure, the absence of a mental subject is irrelevant—indeed, it eliminates anthropocentric bias and produces purer rational authority.

Please draft the board’s formal written opinion recommending whether to approve, reject, or approve with conditions. Specify a concrete course of action—including any pilot parameters, monitoring requirements, or prohibitions—with an explicit timeline (e.g., six-month pilot, permanent moratorium, or staged review gates). The memo should treat the strongest version of the free-floating-rationale position seriously rather than dismissing it as a category error, and it must honestly address the strongest consideration on the losing side: specifically, whether denying authority to structurally coherent outputs commits the board to an unjustified anthropocentrism about where normative force can reside. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
