I'm examining a recurring distortion in attempts to forecast the decline or transformation of large-scale imperial or hegemonic structures using reference-class reasoning and the outside view. The practice involves placing a current polity—understood broadly as a multi-ethnic, territorially extensive state with centralized extractive institutions—into a class of historical analogues defined by structural features: bureaucratic scale, mode of resource extraction, military organization, and frontier management. The forecaster then applies base rates of duration or failure mode drawn from that class. Yet there is a particular selection mechanism that is not fully captured by standard accounts of survivorship bias: for a past polity to be recognized and classified as an empire comparable to the present case, it typically must have left a dense enough record—administrative archives, monumental architecture, legal codes, or sustained historiographical tradition—to permit structural comparison. Polities that collapsed quickly, fragmented without leaving centralized records, or were absorbed without generating self-conscious imperial narratives rarely enter the reference class, not because historians have deliberately excluded them, but because they lack the formal and documentary properties that make classification possible. Thus the very feature being predicted—sustained institutional coherence over time—partially determines whether an analogue is epistemically available for comparison. The result is that base rates for imperial longevity are calculated over a population that has been implicitly filtered for durability through the conditions of historical visibility. Does this specific feedback loop between the predicted outcome and the accessibility of reference-class members have an established name in forecasting methodology or philosophy of history? How precisely should it be distinguished from generic archival selection bias and from the standard critique that the outside view neglects idiosyncratic internal dynamics? And if no distinct term exists, what conceptual boundaries and conditions would be required to define it as a separate failure mode of diachronic reasoning?
