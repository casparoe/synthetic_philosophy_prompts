If heterophenomenology is going to serve as the actual methodological backbone for consciousness science rather than just Dennett's rhetorical escape hatch, I want the full spec of what it has to get right. Give me the desiderata list with the motivating failure-mode behind each—no decorative constraints.

Specifically: it must take first-person reports as data without granting them incorrigible authority; it must maintain the distinction between a subject's heterophenomenological world and the neural mechanisms that actually produce behavior, without collapsing either into the other; it must remain consistent with the specific biology of introspective error—confabulation, change blindness, the illusion of explanatory depth—rather than treating reports as transparent windows; and it must not contradict the mechanistic explanations offered by third-person neuroscience.

For each item, state whether it is a genuine adequacy condition or a prejudice carried over from phenomenological intuition, and identify the concrete case—blindsight narration, split-brain confabulation, or a change-blindness report—that makes it non-negotiable. Then tell me which single requirement does the most damage to the popular hybrid theories that try to preserve some form of first-person authority while claiming scientific respectability.

Check also whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable: is there an internal tension between demanding full first-person transparency and demanding complete neurobiological reducibility such that the desiderata themselves generate a contradiction? Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring; a brief table comparing naive introspectionism, strong eliminativism, and orthodox heterophenomenology is acceptable, but only if it illuminates the criteria.
