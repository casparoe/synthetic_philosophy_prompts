Here is an argument I have been turning over that seems to show the standard defense of easy knowledge collapses into either dogmatism or global skepticism, and I cannot locate the precise fault.

(1) I am initially entitled—without prior inferential support—to the belief that I have hands. This is the core case of easy knowledge.

(2) Using those very hands, I can operate instruments that measure whether my perceptual cortex is functioning correctly, and the results confirm local reliability.

(3) Once I have this second-order confirmation, I am entitled to treat my perceptual system as globally reliable, and therefore to dismiss skeptical hypotheses—such as being a brain in a vat—that conflict with its outputs.

(4) But notice that the entitlement to dismiss skepticism is not independent of the system being questioned; it is manufactured by the system itself.

(5) This creates an epistemic loop: my first-order beliefs justify the reliability of their source, and that justified reliability then protects those same beliefs from doubt.

(6) If such a loop is legitimate, then any belief system can insulate itself from criticism by generating its own reliability credentials—what we might call a self-sealing calibration.

(7) That is indistinguishable from dogmatism.

(8) If, on the other hand, the loop is illegitimate, then my initial entitlement in (1) was never genuine knowledge but mere assumption, and I have no non-circular reason to trust perception at all.

(9) So either easy knowledge licenses dogmatic self-sealing, or it was never knowledge to begin with, leaving empirical cognition groundless.

(10) Both outcomes seem unacceptable.

Where does this reasoning fail? Is there an equivocation between local and global reliability, or a hidden modal slide between “this system is working now” and “no skeptical scenario is possible”? If I must preserve one claim, I would keep (1) and reject the idea that it must generate global self-certification, but I want to know exactly what I am giving up—particularly whether I must abandon the thought that empirical knowledge can ever answer skepticism, or whether I must accept that some circularity is unavoidable and harmless.
