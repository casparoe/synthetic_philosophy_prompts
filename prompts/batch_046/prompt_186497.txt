I'm designing a single sixty-minute seminar for second-year philosophy undergraduates who have completed an introductory logic course covering propositional calculus and basic predicate logic, but who have not yet encountered modal epistemic logic or Fitch's result. The session must teach Fitch's paradox of knowability — the derivation that if every truth is in principle knowable, then every truth is in fact known — not as a historical curiosity but as a live constraint on epistemic principles. Plan the teaching sequence: the order in which the components must be introduced so that the contradiction feels inevitable rather than contrived, specifying which logical commitments (the factivity of knowledge, closure under conjunction, and the formalization of knowable as possibly-known-rather-than-actually-known) must be in place before the paradoxical case is constructed. Choose one concrete carrying scenario — not an abstract propositional variable — that must contain both a specific unknown true fact and the possibility of its being jointly known with the fact of its own unknownness; explain what the scenario must have and lack (for instance, why self-reference alone is insufficient and why the unknown fact must be contingent). Map the misconceptions these students will predictably form and the exact point in the sequence where each should be defused: specifically, anticipate that they will conflate the result with the surprise-examination paradox, that they will invoke human cognitive limits as a resolution rather than recognizing the argument targets logical possibility, and — crucially — that having just studied Gettier cases they will try to block the derivation by arguing that the joint state is merely justified true belief lacking knowledge. Identify the check for understanding you should use at the end to determine whether they have grasped the logical structure rather than just memorized the contradiction, and state explicitly what formal apparatus (modal frame semantics, the full syntax of epistemic logic) you should refuse to introduce at this level and say so. Do not ask for readings, bibliographies, or citations.
