She said that in the dialogue, Gongsun Long proves a white horse is not a horse because "horse" picks out only the bare form or shape of the animal, whereas "white horse" introduces a color property belonging to a completely different ontological category. She added that this separation is reinforced by the related discussion of hard and white, where hardness and whiteness can be perceived independently, which shows that form and color are never genuinely united in a single substance. From this she concluded that whenever we point to a white horse we are really indicating two separate entities that merely overlap in space, and that the School of Names therefore intended to show that ordinary language systematically misleads us into believing in unified objects that do not actually exist—explaining why later Confucians dismissed them as sophists who made classification arbitrary and detached language from reality entirely.

I can see that there is something about distinguishing shape from color, but I worry my classmate is turning a logical point about terms into an extreme metaphysical claim. I also wonder whether invoking the hard-and-white case is appropriate here, since that discussion seems to be about perceptual separation rather than about naming. Could you audit this summary for me?
