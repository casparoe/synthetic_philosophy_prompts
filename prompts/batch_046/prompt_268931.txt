hey — i'm prepping a session for my grad seminar on epistemic rationality next week, about 12 people, 90 minutes. we were arguing over coffee about whether it's rational to accept a bunch of individually likely claims even when you know one must fail, and i realized they should feel that tension in their bones rather than just hear about it. so i want an activity where they construct a collective 'belief portfolio' about a fictional accreditation board — each student draws a private card describing one program evaluation with 99% confidence, and the group votes round by round on whether to treat each evaluation as 'known' and then whether to endorse the full set. they should build the contradiction themselves: by the final round they've agreed every program is certified and also agreed that at least one certification is wrong, but they shouldn't notice until we stop and look back.

can you design the rules, the payoffs (maybe small penalties for inconsistency?), and the structured debrief? the debrief should map each voting round onto the steps of the lottery and preface paradoxes — but don't name them until after they've run into the problem. also be honest about what feature this simulation can't reproduce, like maybe the subjective vs objective interpretation of the probabilities, and say how i'd supply that gap. and tell me what will predictably go wrong when i actually run this with tired grad students on a tuesday. no required readings, just the runnable design.
