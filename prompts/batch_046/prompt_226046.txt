So in school they teach history like its Ancient times, then the Middle Ages (dark and stuck), then boom the Renaissance and then the Scientific Revolution. But I read that the phrase 'Scientific Revolution' was basically invented by one historian in the 1900s, and that people in the 1300s were already tinkering with optics and medicine. So what view of human progress does this four-part split actually commit us to? Does it assume knowledge moves in big leaps rather than slow messy buildup? And is the practice of teaching it this way just a convenient story, or does it smuggle in a philosophy about what counts as real knowledge? Can you break down whats actually agreed on vs whats still fought over by people who study this, and dont pretend a contested take is the only one?
