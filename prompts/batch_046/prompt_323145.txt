She frames this through Williams-style integrity objections, arguing her direct commitment to evaluating student work is a ground project that shouldn't be traded off for institutional efficiency. The dean counters that 40 students received lower grades and lost financial-aid eligibility under her old rubric, and that academic freedom doesn't cover refusing reasonable, data-driven pedagogical standards. The board must recommend either upholding the reprimand, overturning it, or a compromise — say, granting her an exemption but requiring transparent disclosure to enrolled students — with a concrete timeline before the next registration period.
