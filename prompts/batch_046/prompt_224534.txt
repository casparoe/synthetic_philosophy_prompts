ok so in every intro phil of science course someone brings up thomsons lamp like its the same thing as a computer running a loop with halving execution times — like, first toggle takes 1 min, then 30 sec, then 15, so in 2 mins its done an infinite number of flips. people lean on this to say supertasks are basically just fast computing, nothing metaphysically weird, just hardware that scales right. but im not convinced the analogy holds past the surface, especially if you tweak it so the lamp changes color on prime-numbered flips — does that even map onto anything in the computer version?

can you audit it properly? i want the correspondence table — processor, clock speed, heat dissapation, the bulb state, the final instruction — then the breaks. like, does the computer have a determinate state after 2 minutes, and if so why doesnt the lamp? does the analogy smuggle in that the task is "completed" in a real sense, when maybe all we have is a limit point with no final toggle? and does injecting the prime-color rule change whether the computing analogy can even represent the outcome?

finally, does the relaxed "its just computing" conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it the whole time? and if its weak, whats a better analogy for the limit-behavior that doesnt import all that machinery baggage?
