I'm a retired physicist who recently started reading philosophy, and I keep coming back to Popper's argument that history doesn't have laws like physics does because knowledge growth makes the future unpredictable. But then I look at climate agencies running these integrated assessment models—projecting energy use, GDP, and emissions out fifty years, then locking them into legally binding carbon budgets with "pathway dependency" built in. The intermediate targets are enforced as if they were necessary links in a causal chain, not just convenient benchmarks. At the same time the same agencies admit human societies are to complex to predict precisely, and they revise the pathways every cycle when reality diverges. So what philosophical view of history is actually hiding inside that practice? Does it presuppose there are law-like regularities in social development, despite Popper? Or is it just practical planning with no deep metaphysics behind it? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether the practice is consistent with itself, and tell me what would have to change in the regulatory design if we genuinely took Popper's view seriously and dropped the idea that history has predictable laws of that kind.
