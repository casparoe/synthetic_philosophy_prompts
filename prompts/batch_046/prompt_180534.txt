Draft the joint statement that a Coalitional Realist and an Institutional Constructivist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on whether the left-right distinction is real and what holds each side together. The Realist holds that “left” and “right” track enduring, cross-culturally recurrent moral coalitions—centered on competing orientations toward equality, authority, and in-group loyalty—that maintain stable psychological profiles even when policy platforms shift. The Constructivist holds that these labels are empty relational slots produced by the procedural rules of parliamentary opposition; they possess no transhistorical psychological content, and any appearance of cohesion is an artifact of institutional incentives rather than shared moral structure.

Write the document in American English. Structure it as follows: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims about the history of the distinction, its variability across regimes, the limits of survey-based measurement, and what each camp concedes about the other’s evidence; (2) a disagreements table in which each row states one precise point of divergence, with the Realist formulation in one column and the Constructivist formulation in the other, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; (3) a cruxes section identifying at least three sub-questions—such that resolving any one would move at least one side—including whether coalitional psychology predicts policy alignment better than institutional position alone, whether the distinction collapses outside parliamentary systems, and whether individual voters hold stable ideological profiles or merely respond to party cues; and (4) for each crux, specify one concrete case, dataset feature, or comparative-historical episode that both sides agree is relevant, state in advance what each side predicts about it, and explain how an adverse result would affect each view. The formulations should be neither vacuous nor slanted: each side must recognize its own view in its column and must agree that the opposing column states the other view fairly. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or appeals to obscure named theories that depend on specialist literature. The final text should read as a constructive document both parties could publish jointly, with residual disagreement made exact rather than rhetorical.
