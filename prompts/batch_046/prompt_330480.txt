i've been reading a lot about the situationist critique of virtue ethics lately, and i've landed on this kind of hybrid position i'm calling "scaffolded virtue theory." the basic idea is that virtues aren't global personality traits that stay stable across every context -- the situationists are right about that -- but they're also not just momentary behaviors. instead, virtues are more like dynamically maintained capacities that depend on specific social and material scaffolds. so honesty isn't just being honest everywhere; it's a capacity that needs the right environment (low stakes, transparent norms, institutional backup) to actually show up consistently. if you rip the scaffold away, the behavior collapses, but that doesn't mean there was no virtue there in the first place -- just that virtues are ecological in a way aristotle never fully spelled out.

what would actually count against this scaffolded view? i don't want the standard situationist objections rehashed; i want the defeaters that hit this specific hybrid. for example, if moral psychologists found strong within-person consistency even when scaffolds were experimentally held constant -- that'd be awkward, right? or if it turned out that scaffolds are so determinative that talking about virtue is just talking about the environment in disguise? i'm also wondering whether results on moral self-licensing, or on virtue being trainable only in hyper-specific domains, would damage this position, and whether a defender could just absorb them by tweaking the scaffold concept. can you rank the threats from fatal to merely awkward? and be honest about whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- if it has, is that a genuine problem for a normative theory, or just par for the course in ethics?
