I am in a philosophy seminar on moral responsibility, and our instructor has been delivering consistent verdicts on hypothetical cases without revealing the principle behind them. She denies that a severely abused offender who never sought therapy is truly responsible for his violence, since he never authored the formative conditions that shaped his temperament. She similarly denies that a wealthy embezzler who underwent years of therapy to curb her greed bears ultimate responsibility, arguing that both her decision to seek help and her failure to change flowed from prior psychological traits she did not create. She withholds deep moral praise from a person born with a strong genetic predisposition to altruism who risks everything to save strangers, on the grounds that she did not cause her own nature. She holds that an artificial intelligence which recursively rewrote its ethical subroutines across thousands of generations is not truly responsible for a resulting harm, and that its original programmers are not either, because every link in the chain traces back to conditions no one chose. Finally, she insists that even a soldier who heroically refuses an immoral execution order is not ultimately responsible for that refusal, since his courage was not self-caused.

The case I want her view on involves Dr. Elena Voss, a neuroscientist who developed a technique she terms cognitive decoupling: a protocol that, she claims, allows a subject to suspend all inherited and acquired psychological dispositions and evaluate moral choices from a null standpoint devoid of prior bias. She employed this protocol exactly once, with no external coercion and with no prior trait directing the outcome, and deliberately adopted radical honesty as her governing principle. For five years she adhered to it, but she recently lied to protect a colleague from a minor embarrassment—a deliberate, calculated breach of her adopted code. Please predict her verdict on whether Dr. Voss is truly morally responsible for the lie, and whether she bears responsibility for the original adoption of honesty. Identify at least two distinct rules or frameworks that could consistently account for all of her earlier rulings, state what each predicts about Voss's case, and give your best-supported prediction with reasoning. Then specify one further hypothetical scenario that would sharply distinguish the two rules. Please also state how confident you are in your reconstruction of her implicit framework, and identify the particular element—whether the interpretation of an earlier case, the formulation of one rule, or the prediction for Voss—about which you are least sure.
