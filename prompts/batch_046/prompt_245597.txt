I have been thinking about the Buddhist teaching of anatta — the claim that there is no permanent, unchanging self — and I keep noticing how modern wellness culture encourages people to find themselves using language borrowed from traditions that deny any self is there to be found. I would like a sustained satirical piece of about 450 words, written in British English, in the form of a deadpan Frequently Asked Questions page for a fictional luxury retreat called The Aggregate Retreat Centre. The voice should be that of the retreat’s calm, commercially astute marketing director, who genuinely accepts anatta yet has built an entire programme around personal transformation.

The target is the commodification of Eastern philosophy within the self-improvement industry, and specifically the contradiction between selling authentic identity work and a doctrine that denies the customer exists in any enduring sense. To keep the satire specific rather than generic, please construct the retreat’s signature offering as a Five Skandha Audit, in which clients pay separately to have each aggregate — form, sensation, perception, mental formations, and consciousness — assessed in isolation, followed by an optional Reassembly Package sold at a premium despite the retreat’s official position that there is nothing to reassemble.

The irony should remain deadpan and legible throughout: the director answers practical questions — who receives the invoice, who writes the testimonial, whether a non-self may cancel a booking, and what exactly is being transformed if no one endures from arrival to departure — with perfect logical consistency according to anatta, while never acknowledging that this undermines the business model. By the end, a reader should feel the real philosophical pressure point: whether agency, responsibility, or even dissatisfaction require a continuous subject, without anyone breaking character. Please do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to named scholars; the prompt should be answerable by someone reasoning from general knowledge alone. Write in a relatively formal, polished style, from the perspective of an interested non-specialist.
