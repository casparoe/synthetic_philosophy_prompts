I'm preparing a consistency audit for a philosophy column I'm drafting on skepticism and the brain-in-a-vat. These are my stated commitments; I want to know whether they cohere. (a) The brain-in-a-vat scenario is epistemically coherent: one can describe a world in which one's experiences are systematically generated without corresponding external objects, and that description does not collapse into contradiction. (b) I take conceivability to be a fallible but real guide to metaphysical possibility, except in cases where the scenario is self-undermining in precisely the manner Putnam's semantic-externalist argument identifies—there, conceivability misleads because the terms within the scenario fail to achieve reference. (c) I hold that Putnam's argument succeeds in showing that we could not literally be brains in vats, but that its success is purely semantic; it does not address the epistemic challenge concerning how we justify our beliefs about the external world. (d) I think there is a distinct skeptical threat—call it interface skepticism—that does not ask whether beliefs match reality, but whether our cognitive architecture allows any belief-state that is not causally coupled to the environment in the way common sense assumes; this threat survives Putnam's argument because it targets functional architecture rather than semantic reference. (e) Notwithstanding (a) through (d), I maintain that common-sense realism is the default position, with the burden of proof resting entirely on the skeptic.

Please examine these for tension. Which pairs genuinely conflict, which conflicts are only apparent, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equal. In particular, address whether (d) undermines (e) or whether they can be held together, and whether (b) is consistent with (a) once the self-undermining exception is properly circumscribed. I am not looking for a general lecture on skepticism; I want an audit of this specific profile, including where its edges are sharpest and where a single revision would clean up the most problems without abandoning the core intuition behind interface skepticism.
