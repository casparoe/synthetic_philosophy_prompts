I'm on a small-town planning board and we keep getting petitions to tear out old weird infrastructure -- random stone walls in parks, gates that dont close right, staircases going nowhere. The Chesterton fence idea keeps coming up in our debates: dont knock something down till you know why its there. But thats too vague for actual votes. I want you to turn Burkean conservatism about inherited institutions into a concrete decision proceedure my board can run every time a removal proposal lands on our desk.

The deliverable should be a step-by-step checklist (maybe 6 steps) with these features:
- Each step asks one specific question, states what counts as a sufficient answer, and says whether its a fact-check or a value judgment.
- Build in the idea of "reversibility asymmetry": destroying the thing is usually permanent, keeping it is not, so the burden of proof should sit differently.
- Add a "shadow recreation cost" step: before approving removal, estimate what it would cost (in money, labor, lost option value) to put it back if we turn out to be wrong.
- Handle two hard cases explicitly: (a) we genuinely cant recover the original purpose (institutional amnesia), and (b) the purpose is documented but now totally obsolete.
- Flag every point where the board still has to make a call that the procedure cant settle for them.

Then run the procedure on three proposals:
1. A 1920s iron fence around a playground that blocks wheelchair access; local historians think it might relate to an old segregated entrance system but the records are missing.
2. A concrete stairway to a riverbank built for a ferry that closed in 1978; now it just collects trash and needs repairs.
3. A case where following the procedure forces us to keep something ugly and functionally useless because the epistemic risk of removal is too high -- I want to see what that looks like.

Keep it practical: we meet twice a month, we have no staff historian, and I need something I can hand to colleagues who dont care about philosophy. No bibliographies please, I cant check them without web access.
