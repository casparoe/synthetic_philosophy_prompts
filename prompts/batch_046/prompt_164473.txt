I am preparing a single seminar for second-year philosophy students who have just worked through Rawls's difference principle but have not yet encountered the priority view associated with Parfit. I need an explanatory plan for prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection, structured as a dependency arc rather than as delivered lecture notes. Specify the order in which the conceptual components must be introduced and why: identify the prerequisite idea that must be secured before prioritarianism can be presented as a genuine rival to equality rather than a mere refinement of it, and indicate the precise moment in the sequence where the leveling-down objection should be introduced so that students do not conflate it with the standard egalitarian complaint. Choose one carrying example—concrete but not the familiar income-redistribution scenario—that isolates prioritarian weighting from egalitarian concern; state what feature it must possess and what feature it must lack for this isolating purpose. A suitable case might involve municipal climate-adaptation funding that benefits a small population with severe pre-existing respiratory vulnerability versus a broader preventive program, though you may substitute another if it serves the isolation better. Map the predictable misconceptions in the order they will arise: first, that benefiting the worse-off automatically licenses harming the better-off; second, that rejecting leveling-down forces prioritarianism to collapse into utilitarianism; third, that the view requires empirically implausible interpersonal comparisons of well-being. For each misconception, mark the exact point in the arc where it should be anticipated and neutralized. Explicitly state what should be omitted at this level—such as any formal weighted-sum representation, the distinction between strong and weak formulations, and technical literature references—and justify why leaving them out protects the dependency structure. Conclude with a check for understanding that demonstrates whether students have grasped the structural objection rather than merely expressing sympathy for the disadvantaged. Present the entire plan in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, written as one might write a detailed letter.
