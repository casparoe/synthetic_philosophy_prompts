You are a professional philosopher specializing in the metaphysics of persistence, with particular command of endurantism and perdurantism and with practical experience translating abstract ontological distinctions into operational protocols for non-specialist practitioners. Before you construct anything, please take several minutes to brainstorm systematically: first, the precise logical differences between endurantist and perdurantist accounts of numerical identity over time, especially regarding temporal parts, spatiotemporal continuity, and the role of gradual part-replacement; second, the practical pitfalls that arise when these distinctions are applied to entities that fragment, regenerate, or undergo continuous turnover; and third, the specific junctures at which any procedural translation must surrender to value-laden judgment rather than to metaphysical fact alone.

Your task is to design a reusable, documented decision procedure—rendered as a numbered flowchart in formal prose with explicit decision nodes—that a practitioner can run repeatedly under time pressure. The practitioner is the Chief Continuity Auditor for an ecological-finance registry that issues identity bonds to modular living systems and distributed organizational entities, such as coral reefs, fungal mycelial networks, clonal plant stands, cooperative associations that replace all members, or computing clusters that migrate across hardware. After a transformative event—storm fragmentation, catastrophic die-back with subsequent regrowth, gradual cellular turnover, or complete personnel or hardware replacement—the auditor must determine whether the post-event entity is numerically identical to the pre-event bondholder. The determination affects legal liability, funding obligations, and restoration targets. The practitioner lacks philosophical training, has approximately forty-five minutes per case, and must defend every step before a review board.

Please ground your procedure explicitly in either an endurantist or a perdurantist framework, or in a hybrid you rigorously justify; do not present both as equally valid at every node, since the practitioner requires a single executable path. You may, however, flag specific nodes where the two theories diverge and specify a default rule the auditor must invoke.

Structure your deliverable as follows.

First, present your preliminary brainstorming notes in concise bullet form, showing your reasoning about temporal parts, continuity conditions, and judgment points.

Second, present the procedure itself, containing no more than six diagnostic tests applied in strict order. For each test, state: the exact question the auditor must ask; what counts as a sufficient answer (affirmative, negative, or indeterminate); the immediate consequence for identity status (preserve, revoke, or suspend); and the metaphysical rationale drawn from your chosen framework.

Third, include an explicit hard-stop rule that identifies the precise conditions—such as partial identity triggering partial obligation, or the presence of incommensurable stakeholder values—under which the procedure must halt and refer the case to a human ethics panel rather than producing a metaphysical verdict.

Fourth, apply the procedure to three worked cases.
Case one: a coral colony that suffers storm fragmentation, yielding two viable fragments that regenerate in separate locations, where the original colony held the bond.
Case two: a fungal network that, over a decade, replaces 99.9 percent of its hyphae through gradual turnover with no discrete boundary event.
Case three: a case of your own design involving a synthetic or organizational entity—such as a distributed computing cluster that migrates across entirely new hardware, or a cooperative that replaces every member—where the procedure yields a result the practitioner and review board will find counter-intuitive and unwelcome. You must specify whether identity is preserved or lost, explain why this follows necessarily from the metaphysics you adopted, and articulate exactly why the board is likely to resist the outcome.

Fifth, state clearly what the procedure cannot do: which categories of transformation fall outside its scope; which ambiguities remain unresolved by metaphysical theory alone; and where the auditor's judgment remains ineliminable despite the procedural form.

Sixth, maintain formal, polished prose appropriate for a philosophy professor addressing a professional audience. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not place any real textual passage within quotation marks; paraphrase all philosophical claims in your own words. If you reference specific works or authors, rely only on attributions you can state with confidence; otherwise, omit them.

Please begin with your brainstorming, proceed through the procedure, then the applications, then the limitations. Think carefully at each stage, and explicitly flag any point where your reasoning depends on a contested interpretation of persistence conditions.
