Please argue that Wittgenstein's hinge propositions, as discussed in On Certainty, ought not be understood as beliefs that merely enjoy near-certain confidence, but as conditions of intelligibility that lie outside the space of reasons altogether. In particular, defend the claim that assigning them Bayesian credences—even values arbitrarily close to 1—fundamentally mischaracterizes their epistemic role, and show why this mischaracterization becomes especially acute when we apply the framework to hinges concerning our own cognitive reliability in assessing hinges.
