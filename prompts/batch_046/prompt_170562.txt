In my bio-philosophy group we've hit a wall arguing whether DNA is literally a code or just a chemical pattern we describe with code-metaphors. Side A says the triplet-to-amino-acid mapping is arbitrary — it's not determined by physics like magnetism — and that tRNA adapters, error-checking, and combinatoral syntax make it genuinely representational, not just causal. Side B says all of that is just evolved machinery; "arbitrary" here only means "not physically necessary," which applies to key-lock pairs too, and calling DNA a code sneaks in an intentional interpreter that cells dont have. We've gone through every biochemical mechanism and nobodys moved in months. My hunch is we're not really disagreeing about DNA but about whether biological information needs a mind to be meaningful — we're using the genome as a stand-in for that. Is it actually a hidden conceptual disagreement about representation, or is there some empirical question about the genetic code's arbitrariness that's still open? And if it's conceptual, what distinction — maybe between cipher and protocol, or between syntax and semantics — would actually unstick us?
