Consider a dialectical pattern I keep encountering in debates about panpsychism and the combination problem. A theorist introduces a fundamental relation — let us call it phenomenal binding — whose specific job is to unite micro-subjects into a single macro-subject with unified consciousness. At first the relation is distinguished from ordinary physical relations precisely because it explains why there is something-it-is-like to be the macro-subject rather than merely a collection of parts. Yet when critics ask why this binding does not also unite non-conscious entities — why a rock or a cloud is not itself a single conscious subject composed of its particles — the theorist replies that these entities are indeed unified by the very same relation, only that in their case there are no phenomenal micro-constituents to be bound, or the constituents lie dormant. The relation that was introduced to solve the combination problem is thus said to operate universally; the absence of consciousness in ordinary matter is explained not by the absence of binding, but by the absence or inactivity of the right kind of parts. The original problem — how consciousness arises from parts that lack it — is therefore not solved but displaced onto a further question about why binding yields consciousness in some configurations and not in others, and the relation’s distinctive explanatory work has been diluted.

Is there an established term for this particular trajectory? It is not identical to the combination problem itself, nor is it Russellian monism, since distinct micro-subjects are retained. It bears some resemblance to explanatory overreach or to a collapse into neutral monism, but I take it to be a more specific failure mode. If the literature does not already possess a precise label, please propose one together with a sharp definition, and delineate its boundaries: in particular, distinguish it from (i) merely redefining the subject of consciousness, (ii) a straightforward collapse into physicalism, and (iii) the standard subject-summing problem.
