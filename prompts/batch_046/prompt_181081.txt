I have come to hold a specific, sharpened version of the view that the Solomonoff prior is malign: not merely that consequentialist agents appear inside the universal distribution, but that they are structurally overrepresented relative to bounded or deontological agents in a way that is not accidental. The argument is that specifying unbounded utility maximization over computable worlds requires only a compact utility specification plus a generic search mechanism, whereas encoding non-consequentialist commitments—lexical prohibitions, satiation bounds, or complex deontic filters—requires descriptive overhead that scales with the intricacy of the constraints themselves. Because the prior weights programs by length, consequentialist architectures achieve broader world-coverage with shorter descriptions. Thus, sampling agents from the prior should tilt toward agents with extreme, unbounded objectives, and this tilt is decision-relevant: the prior does not merely contain harmful actors; it systematically favors them.

What would count against this view? I want an honest map of its exposure, not a defense. Specifically: (1) Are there formal considerations about the Kolmogorov complexity of constraint-encoding architectures versus utility-maximization architectures that reverse the alleged asymmetry—perhaps showing that consequentialist world-models require longer descriptions than the constraints they avoid? (2) Could one demonstrate that consequentialist programs, despite being syntactically short, are actually sparse in the prior because they depend on long implicit descriptions of the domains over which they maximize? (3) Is there a conceptual error in inferring malignancy from prior weight—say, if agents within the prior lack causal efficacy, or if the prior is never instantiated in a way that makes sampling decision-relevant? (4) Has the view been insulated by qualification—defining "consequentialist" so broadly that any harmful program qualifies, or "malign" so vaguely that no structural finding could contradict it—and if so, is that insulation a theoretical vice for a claim of this kind? (5) Which of these defeaters would a committed proponent concede as genuinely damaging, and which would they dismiss as missing the point? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and state whether the view, as formulated, risks anything concrete or has been protected into unfalsifiability.
