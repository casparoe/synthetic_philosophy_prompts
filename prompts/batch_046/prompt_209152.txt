Im running a philosophy film club and we're doing Groundhog Day next week. Read it through Sartre on radical freedom and bad faith, but dont just map Phil onto authenticity. Instead treat the loop as stripping away facticity: he keeps memory (transcendence) but every choice gets erased, so he has pure being-for-itself with no lasting being-in-itself. Use that to ask whether hes actually free or stuck in a new bad faith, especially in the tree-rescue and the old-man-in-the-diner scenes - is he caring or just using people to break the loop? Point to specific moments, say where the film gets Sartre right and where it probably distorts him, and separate whats settled in Sartre scholarship from whats genuinely contested - dont present a contested reading as consensus. I want the group to see both the illumination and the resistance.
