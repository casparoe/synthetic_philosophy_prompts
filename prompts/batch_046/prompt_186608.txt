Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of free-floating rationales—normative reasons that obtain and exert force even when no agent holds, recognizes, or is positioned to act on them—must meet. Specify the phenomena it must explain, including the difference between merely unrecognized agent-centered reasons and genuinely reasoner-independent rationales (illustrate each with a concrete case); how it must treat normative residue, where a reason-structure continues to generate constraints after all designers, operators, and potential beneficiaries have been removed (e.g., an autonomous ecological preservation protocol that persists without human oversight); and whether it must allow such rationales to be mistaken or overridden in the absence of any corrector. State which distinctions—such as that between primitive normative structure and reducible potential-agent-attitudes—the theory must respect, flag any pair of requirements that seem jointly unsatisfiable, and briefly indicate how an agent-centered reductionist view and a structural-realist view stand against the list, keeping the weight on the criteria themselves.
