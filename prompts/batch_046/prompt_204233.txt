As someone who has followed rationalist and philosophy-of-science discussions of explanatory levels for years, I want a sustained satirical piece in deadpan bureaucratic form. Write an official “Departmental Policy FAQ,” approximately 600 words, issued by a university philosophy department that has formally adopted greedy reductionism as its exclusive methodological framework and banned all skyhook explanations from teaching, hiring, and campus governance. The voice should be that of a neutral administrator explaining policies that follow with perfect internal consistency from the premise that only crane-style, bottom-up explanations are legitimate: for instance, that tenure-track applicants must demonstrate that their research program can be fully derived from statistical mechanics; that courses on ethics and aesthetics have been reclassified as applied neuroscience; and that peer review operates under a “Skyhook Detection Protocol” flagging any mention of intentionality, normativity, or institutional function unless accompanied by a microphysical reduction base. The satire must exaggerate actual features of greedy reductionism—specifically its denial of autonomous explanatory power to higher-level patterns—rather than invent false caricatures. Include one internal memo, set off as a blockquote, in which a senior faculty member applies the framework to the concept of “academic freedom,” argues that it is a skyhook that must be eliminated, and derives this conclusion consistently from reductionist premises without slipping into strawman logic or accidental compatibilism. The piece should remain deadpan and legible: a reader familiar with Dennett’s cranes-and-skyhooks distinction should immediately recognize what is being satirized, but the text should never break character, wink, or state the joke explicitly. Assume an audience comfortable with rationalist-style analysis of emergent explanation—do not hand-hold basic terminology—but do not ask for bibliographies, citations, named living scholars’ direct quotes, or reading lists. Inject one niche framing not standard in popular treatments: treat the department’s failed attempt to reduce “institutional trust” to synaptic-weight distributions as a routine administrative setback mentioned in passing, so the satire extends into social epistemology rather than remaining purely metaphysical.
