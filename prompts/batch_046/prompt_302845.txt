I'm on this interdisciplinery grant panel and we need to vote by next Friday on a proposal that wants to treat "cultural values" as actual modal causes in 19th-century banking development, not just background correlations. The PI is comparing Calvinist and Lutheran regions with nearly identical geograpy and resources, using something they call "counterfactual normative tracing" — basically mapping sermon-text density onto contract-enforcement norms — to argue that confessional values created path-dependent institutional constraints that can't be reduced to individual incentives. Half the panel (mostly econ people) thinks this is just Weber's Protestant ethic thesis in quantitative drag and that values are always epiphenominal to material conditions; the other half thinks reducing normative commitment devices to incentives misses the causal work culture does, but they can't agree on what "causal" even means here. Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, include a concrete timeline, take seriously the strongest argument from the losing side, and write it as exactly five numbered items with no intro or conclusion, each one or two sentences long, starting with the bottom line in item one.
