I've been trying to follow the back-and-forth between Dennett and Caruso on punishment and just deserts, and honestly it feels like they're running in circles. Dennett keeps saying we don't need spooky metaphysical free will to hold people responsible, but we still need accountability as a kind of social technology — like a thermostat that keeps people in line. Caruso comes back and says that's just retributivism in a lab coat, and if determinism is true then just deserts is as sensible as blaming a hurricane. They both cite the same studies about deterrance not working, they both agree people feel angry, and yet neither budges an inch.

My hunch is that they're not actually fighting over the same idea of desert. Dennett treats it like a receipt — a bookkeeping entry that records who broke the social contract so we can repair it. Caruso treats it like a cosmic scorecard where suffering balances the moral books. But maybe I'm wrong and it's deeper: maybe they just have opposite gut reactions about whether moral anger is ever rational, and all the philosophy is just post-hock decoration.

So what kind of stall is this? A hidden verbal disagreement about blame versus accountability? Two different projects — one about institutional design, one about emotional hygiene — smushed under one heading? Or is there a real empirical question hiding in there, like whether people's intuitons about punishment actually track control or just social status? And if your diagnosis is right, what would actually move the needle — a brain-scan study, a sharper distinction between receipt-blame and thermostat-blame, or is this just bedrock intuition and we should stop expecting resolution?
