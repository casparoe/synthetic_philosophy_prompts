As a science writer preparing a feature on why the arrow of time resists reduction to thermodynamics alone, I want a poem that could close the piece. Write a blank-verse poem—roughly thirty lines—from the voice of a physicist who accepts the past-hypothesis account of low-entropy origins but denies that it grounds causal asymmetry. The poem’s form must perform the irreversibility it discusses: each successive stanza should grow in line length and in consonant-cluster density, with no rhyme scheme that permits return, so that the verse itself becomes entropically ordered. At its center, have the speaker separate statistical irreversibility from the phenomenological now that records it, taking care not to treat consciousness as a fundamental physical quantity. The distinction between this view and the Boltzmann-brain objection should be clear to a specialist, yet the poem must stand on its own without citations, bibliographies, or named authorities. Make the language precise enough that a philosopher of physics would not wince, but keep the imagery accessible to an educated general reader.
