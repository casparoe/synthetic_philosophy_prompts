I chair the Forum Access Committee at a mid-sized public university, and after a semester in which we approved, then regretted approving, a speaker invitation that led to sustained campus disruption, I am trying to translate the paradox of tolerance into a reusable protocol my five-person committee can run in under forty-five minutes without requiring a philosophy seminar each time. A colleague in the philosophy department warned me that any operational rule risks stripping the paradox of its critical force, but I need the procedure nonetheless, so please construct it as an ordered sequence of questions with explicit conditions for an approval stop, a denial stop, and a hand-off point where judgment must replace analysis. The framework should distinguish structural intolerance, meaning an organizational purpose that necessarily entails suppressing the rights or participation of others, from contingent intolerance, in which individuals hold intolerant views but the proposed event does not depend on suppressing anyone, and it should apply an imminence filter that asks not whether a group dislikes tolerance in the abstract, but whether granting this specific institutional platform gives them organizational legitimacy or material capacity that makes suppression measurably more likely. I want the deliverable to include brief worked applications to three proposals: a speaker advocating the legal disenfranchisement of a specific religious minority together with distributing organizational literature for that end; an open debate on restrictive immigration policies that critics call intolerant but which is structured as adversarial argument with equal speaking time; and a private study circle that circulates philosophical texts denying the moral personhood of a particular group but claims no organizational agenda beyond discussion, with at least one case yielding a denial the committee will find politically uncomfortable. Please conclude by stating plainly what the procedure cannot settle, such as whether neutrality is preferable to tolerance or how to weigh historical patterns of suppression, and present everything entirely in continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as though writing a long, careful letter to a fellow administrator who has never read the original literature.
