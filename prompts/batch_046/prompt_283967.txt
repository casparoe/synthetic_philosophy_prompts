Here's an argument my study partnet cooked up about Twin Earth and I think it overreaches but I can't spot the leak. (1) Putnam's Twin Earth case shows "water" picks out H2O here and XYZ there despite identical brain states, so meaning aint in the head. (2) If that holds for natural kinds it should hold for any concept fixed by the environment — "justice", "belief", even "meaning" itself. (3) Now imagine a Modal Twin Earth where every philosopher is an externalist, versus Earth where they're all internalists. By (1)-(2) the word "meaning" refers to different properties in each place. (4) So an Earthling saying "meaning is internal" and a Twin saying the same sentence arn't actually disagreeing — they're talking past each other about different properties. (5) But if externalism makes cross-world disagreement impossible for every concept it touches, then stating externalism to a Twin Earthling should be impossible too; the view undermines its own assertability. (6) So either externalism is restricted to natural kinds only, or it's false. Which step do I reject? I want to keep (2) since it feels principled, but I worry I'm smuggling a scope slip somewhere between (3) and (4).
