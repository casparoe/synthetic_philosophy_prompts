Write an essay on whether moral compromise between genuinely incommensurable value systems can ever be rational, or if its always corruption in disguise. Weigh the "gains from trade" picture — two communities finding a middle ground that leaves both better off — against the objection that core values arent commodities and trading them off is betrayal. Ground it in this made-up scenario: the Sorn hold that water is sacred and can never be bought or sold, but they permit collective punishment for theft; the Keth treat water as fully alienable private property, yet they absolutely forbid punishing anyone for the crimes of their kin. They share a watershed and need a joint justice framework. Is there a principled compromise — maybe a domain-splitting arrangement where water rights and criminal liability are governed separately — or does any deal necessarily corrupt one or both sides? Reach an actual verdict and defend it. Don't just say "it depends"; take a stand, even if its messy.
