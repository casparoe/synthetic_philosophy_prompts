As a software engineer without formal philosophical training, I have been trying to formalize the dispute between absurdism and contemporary hybrid accounts of meaning in life as a logical chain, but the output seems to prove far more than any philosopher actually accepts. I want to identify the faulty step and understand what giving it up costs.

(1) For a project, relationship, or value to confer genuine meaning on a life, it must be able to function as an end-in-itself — not merely as an instrument to some further goal such as survival, pleasure, or social approval.

(2) If the universe is indifferent to human existence (the core absurdist premise), then there is no cosmically sanctioned framework that designates any particular human-scale goods — knowledge, love, creative achievement — as ends-in-themselves.

(3) Therefore, under absurdism, any project we treat as an end-in-itself is either instrumentally reducible to something further, or it is arbitrarily selected.

(4) But an arbitrarily selected end lacks genuine normative authority; it is a preference disguised as a principle, binding on no one who does not happen to share it.

(5) Contemporary hybrid accounts attempt to avoid this collapse by claiming that a life is meaningful when it combines subjective fulfillment with objective value, where objective value is defined in terms of human-scale goods.

(6) However, for those human-scale goods to avoid being arbitrary, they require a justification that is not itself circular or preference-based.

(7) Without cosmic sanction, no such independent justification is available.

(8) Therefore, hybrid accounts collapse into arbitrary preference and fail to deliver the normative force they promise.

(9) If hybrid accounts fail, then the only coherent options remaining are pure nihilism (life has no meaning whatsoever) or the denial of absurdism (there exists some transcendent or cosmic purpose).

(10) But pure nihilism is self-undermining: if nothing is genuinely valuable, then there is no reason to prefer the true belief that life is meaningless over the false belief that it is meaningful, and consequently no rational basis for maintaining nihilism itself.

(11) So absurdism, by driving us into nihilism, undermines its own rational basis.

(12) Therefore, absurdism must be false, and some form of cosmic or transcendent meaning must exist.

The conclusion feels like a hidden proof for theism that I never intended. I suspect the error lies between (6) and (8), or possibly in how (10) treats self-undermining, but I cannot isolate the exact inference. Which step should I reject? If I must preserve either (2) or (9), I would prefer to keep (2) and abandon whatever forces the chain to (12). What does that rejection cost? Does it rescue hybrid accounts, or does it merely relocate the arbitrariness into a different part of the system? And if (10) is the weak link, what is the correct way to understand the rational status of nihilism once cosmic meaning is denied?
