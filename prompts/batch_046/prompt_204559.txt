I'm on this state penal reform board — don't ask how a philosophy prof wound up here — and our precedents are getting weird. We've endorsed copying Halden for first-time drug and property offenses; we rejected open-campus conditions for violent repeat offenders unless they've done restorative dialogue and cleared board review; we approved that pilot for long-termers who went through the dialogue; we rejected solitary for anyone, even after an assault on staff; and we said restorative justice can replace prison for theft and fraud but never for violent crime. Now there's a proposal called "interior normalization" from some Danish architect: Halden-style cottages — private rooms, kitchens, education spaces — built inside a max-security shell, for people serving mandatory minimums for violent offenses, no restorative program required, no path out of the shell. The wall does security; the cottage does daily life. What should we decide to stay consistent? Which precedent is the closest analog? I can see two principals that fit our record — one that calibrates conditions to desert, one that seperates security from dignity — and they diverge here. Predict our verdict, say which rule we're running, name the further case that'd settle it between them, and if we're genuinely underdetermined, tell me what principle we'd be adopting either way.
