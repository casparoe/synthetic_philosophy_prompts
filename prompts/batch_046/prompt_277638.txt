Okay so I've bought into Hofstadter's idea that the self is just a strange loop — a symbol in the brain that refers back to itself, that's all the I really is, no extra ghost stuff. But I'm pushing a stronger version: the loop is causally closed, meaning the symbol's activation pattern physically feeds back into the very neurons that generate it, so you can't in principle seperate symbol from substrate. This feels like it dodges the homunculus and makes specific predictions — like that disrupting the feedback loop should disrupt self-reference specifically, not just general cognition. What would actually count against this? Not the generic consciousness-feels-unitary objection. I want defeaters that hit the causal-closure claim: empirical results about distributed brain processing, formal arguments that physical self-reference collapses into either dualism or eliminativism, maybe cases where self-reference survives despite broken feedback. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward. Also, what does this view predict that rival accounts like narrative self or global workspace don't? And be honest — have defenders already qualified it into unfalsifiability, like saying it's emergent whenever counterevidence shows up? Is that insulation a problem?
