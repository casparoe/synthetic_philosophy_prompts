I am expecting my first child in the spring, and through my research position I have been offered enrolment in a voluntary trial of an in-utero intervention—already tested extensively in non-human primates—that permanently recalibrates the developing nociceptive and limbic pathways. The effect is not mere anaesthesia but a constitutional inability to suffer: the child would never experience physical pain, grief, or existential dread, while cognition, memory, and what the researchers describe as positive-valence experience remain fully intact. My partner insists that declining the trial is tantamount to deliberately leaving our child exposed to preventable harm, and that any parent with access to this option has a duty to take it. I feel a strong reluctance, not because of medical risk—the safety data are excellent—but because I wonder whether a life structurally incapable of suffering can still contain the kind of moral depth, authentic attachment, or meaningful struggle that I take to be central to human dignity. I cannot tell whether my reluctance is an ethically serious concern or merely a romantic attachment to hardship. How should I approach this choice?
