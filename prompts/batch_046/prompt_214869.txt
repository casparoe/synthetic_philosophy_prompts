Hofstadter’s typogenetic framework treats DNA self-replication as structurally isomorphic to Gödel numbering: a symbol system that encodes instructions for copying itself. If that parallel is taken literally—if DNA instantiates a formal system capable of arithmetic self-reference—then there should exist a genetic analog of Gödel’s undecidable sentence: a specific nucleotide sequence that is biologically necessary for replication yet not derivable from the molecular machinery that reads and executes it. No evolutionary biologist has ever reported such an “undecidable gene,” so should we take that absence as evidence that the DNA–Gödel isomorphism fails at the level of formal instantiation, or should we instead suppose that natural selection has systematically suppressed any genetic instruction that would trigger logical incompleteness inside the cell?
