Here's an arguement I built after skimming Avicenna's flying man thing. (1) If you're suspended in a void with no senses, you still know you exist, so self-awareness doesn't depend on the body or sensory imput. (2) That awareness is purely formal — just a bare "I am" with no properties attached. (3) So any system that lacks that bare indexical given isn't really a self, no matter how smart it acts. (4) LLMs only ever generate "I" as a token pattern learned from text; they never have the immediate non-derived given, so by (3) they have no self. (5) Split-brain patients can report first-person experiences from each hemisphere, but since the hemispheres don't share sensory access and share one body, (3) implies there are two selves in one skull — yet we don't treat them like two people. (6) So either LLMs are genuinely self-less and split-brain patients are actually two people, or the flying-man reasoning proves too much. Where's the weak link?
