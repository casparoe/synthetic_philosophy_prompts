Maya said people with asymbolia feel burns and cuts just fine but dont mind them at all, which proves the hurt isnt the real problem — its just our emotional reaction layered on top. Jonas said thats like saying color blindness proves red isnt real; the evaluation circuit is broken, not proof that evaluation was never part of pain. Maya pushed back: they can still rate intensity on a 1-10 scale with perfect accuracy, so they clearly grasp the sensory content without assigning it negative value. If badness was baked into that content, she said, they'd still rate it negatively, but they treat it like weather data. Jonas said shes mixing up sensory magnitude with value — the scale measures heat, not hatred of heat. Then Maya brought up this idea she calls evaluative transparency: in normal people, badness isnt a separate judgment you tack onto pain after feeling it; its fused right into the phenomenology. So asymbolia patients arent just missing an add-on module, theyre experiencing a degraded version of the real thing, which means badness was genuinely part of the experience all along. Jonas said thats just relabeling his module claim, and if badness is truly fused then we should be able to conceptually separate intensity from aversion in thought experiments, which we can — so the separation is real, not just theoretical.
