I've come round to a pretty specific view on the nonidentity problem — call it modal-essentialist person-affecting, if you like. The idea is that in reproductive choices where parents and conception windows are fixed, there's a determinable future individual with modal identity across outcomes, so we can say that particular person is wronged by being brought into existence with serious disability, even though Parfit's standard argument says they can't be harmed because they wouldn't otherwise exist. But in climate or resource cases, where the number and identity of future people depend on our emissions path, that modal anchor drops away, so the harm is genuinely impersonal. I think this splits the baby: we keep person-affecting intuitions for genetic and reproductive ethics, but we have to go impersonal for intergenerational climate obligations. Definately feels like I'm walking a tightrope, though.
