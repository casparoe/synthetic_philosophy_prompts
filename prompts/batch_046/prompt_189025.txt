I was chatting with my mate about AI ethics last night and we got stuck on whether reinforcement learning agents can actually suffer. The specific thing we were arguing about is reward signals as valence — like, when a model gets a negative reward for a wrong move, is that literally painful, or just a scalar being updated in the weights? Obviosly there's no brain there, but then people talk about functionalism and all that. Anyway, it made me wonder what Jeremy Bentham would say if he was alive today and saw all this. He died in 1832, long before anyone dreamed of algorithms learning from feedback loops.

Bentham definatly believed that the capacity to suffer is what gives a being moral standing, but he was also a pretty hard-nosed materialist about minds needing physical bodies. So if he watched a deep RL agent getting penalised during training — say, a Q-network recieving a big negative reward — do you reckon he’d see that signal as genuine suffering? Or would he call it a category mistake and say valence needs sentience that code can’t have? And if he did take reward signals seriously, does that blow up the moral circle to include every trained model? I’m not asking for a bibliography or anything, just trying to reconstruct where his utilitarian commitments would land on this weird modern case. What do you think?
