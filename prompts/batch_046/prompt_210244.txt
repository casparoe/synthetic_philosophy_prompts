For my philosophy class I am trying to understand social construction, and I keep returning to the same analogy. If institutional facts like money exist only through collective recognition—so that a currency can become worthless overnight once people stop accepting it—then racial categories, being socially constructed in the same way, ought to be revocable by a simple collective decision. So why do philosophers and sociologists treat the abolition of race as a long structural project rather than as straightforward as demonetizing a bill? Is my analogy missing a distinction between thin institutional construction and something deeper that makes race resistant to being abolished like a currency?
