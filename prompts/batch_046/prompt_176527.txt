Design a sorting taxonomy — specifically a decision tree with named leaves and flagged indistinguishabilities — for a graduate seminar on consciousness and cognitive architecture. The debate concerns whether conscious experience requires a single presentation point in the brain (the Cartesian theater idea) or can be constituted by parallel, non-convergent drafts (Dennett's multiple drafts model). To avoid mere label-matching, build the tree from four concrete diagnostic scenarios: (i) a split-brain patient who reports seeing only one object but whose right hemisphere guides correct pointing; (ii) a change-blindness trial in which the subject denies seeing a color shift that their motor system tracks; (iii) a hypothetical case of sustained cross-modal binding without any global broadcast mechanism; and (iv) a case of post-hoc narrative stitching where the verbal report describes a feature that was never processed in any draft.

The tree should branch on answers to four yes/no commitments, phrased as questions a student could answer without knowing which view they lead to:
1. Is there a spatiotemporally localized event — a finishing line — at which experience is definitively constituted?
2. If no such locus exists, do the parallel drafts ever converge into a single dominant narrative, or do they remain permanently incommensurable?
3. If convergence does occur, does it depend on a global workspace broadcast, or can local cross-modal binding alone produce it?
4. Does the absence of a verbal or behavioral report about an unattended feature entail the absence of experience, or only the absence of access?

Locate in the tree's leaves: the Cartesian theater; Dennett's radical multiple-drafts view; a moderate drafts view with local binding only; global workspace theory (as a theater-free but convergence-based position); and note two unoccupied combinations — distributed drafts with global broadcast but no narrative convergence, and a single-locus presentation without any broadcast mechanism. For each unoccupied leaf, state in one sentence whether the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Finally, flag any two named positions that your tree cannot genuinely separate given these four branches, and explain what further distinction would be needed to split them. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Phrase every node as a direct question about the cases, not about the labels.
