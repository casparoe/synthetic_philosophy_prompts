I'm a retired physicist who recently got into philosphy of mind, and there's this pattern I keep seeing that I can't find a label for. People reject the old idea of a little person inside the brain watching a movie screen, and they say consciousness is spread out with lots of competing drafts and no single final version, but then they still describe one central "filing cabnet" or "executive inbox" where all those drafts land, and they identify the self with the owner of that inbox rather than with any particular draft. Is there an established term for this specific hybrid — distributed processing everywhere except one final collection point treated as the real experiencer? How does it differ from the "little watcher on a screen" mistake, and from the view that there's genuinely no central collector? Is global workspace theory just this in modern language? Please answer as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no intro or conclusion: first, whether this "inbox" configuration has its own name; second, how it relates to the little-watcher idea; third, how it differs from the no-central-collector view; fourth, whether global workspace theory is an instance; fifth, if no term exsists, what you'd call it and where you'd draw the line around it.
