(1) Ontological identification needs an epistemic differentiator: for any entity e in our ontology, there must be some property P that e has, that nothing else in our ontology has, and P has to be epistemically accessible to us in principle.
(2) If a region is causally and informationally isolated from ours—like beyond an event horizon, or in a causally disconnected modal domain—then no properties of entities inside it are epistemically accessible.
(3) So for any X and Y inside that region, we lack a differentiator.
(4) Therefore ontological identification collapses there: either theyre all the same entity, or none belong in our ontology.

(6) And apply it to phenomenal isolation: other minds are infromationally cut off from my first-person access.
(7) So we either deny other minds, deny specific past quantum states, deny disconnected worlds, or reject (1).
(8) He wants to keep (1) because he thinks ontology without identification is just fiction.
