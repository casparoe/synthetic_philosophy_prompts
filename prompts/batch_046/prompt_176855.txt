I'm noticing a recurring pattern in debates about historical materialism that I can't quite name. In one case, the British abolition of slavery was driven partly by a moral and legal shift that made slave labor appear illegitimate before it became clearly unprofitable; in another, 1970s environmental regulation established liability frameworks that later reshaped what counted as economically rational industrial investment, rather than merely reflecting prior economic change. The pattern is not standard relative autonomy of the superstructure, because here the legal or ideological shift doesn't just operate independently — it actively redefines the criteria by which economic practices are evaluated, so that the base's subsequent development is guided by norms that originated outside it. Is there an established term for this specific directional relation, where superstructural change re-engineers the economic base's evaluative framework rather than being determined by it? If not, can you propose a precise definition that distinguishes it from both base determinism and from the ordinary idea of superstructural autonomy, with clear boundary conditions?
