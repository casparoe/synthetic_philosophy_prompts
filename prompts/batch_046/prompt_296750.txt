Having spent my career studying irreversible thermodynamic processes, I now find it natural to model collective remembrance as an open system subject to entropic decay—a tendency I call mnemonic dissipation. From this vantage, Avishai Margalit's insistence on a duty to remember and David Rieff's defense of strategic forgetting appear not as incommensurable moral positions but as competing boundary conditions on the same conservation law: Margalit seeks to minimize dissipation, whereas Rieff permits it for long-term system stability. Is the disagreement between them therefore best adjudicated by computing which policy yields the lower mnemonic entropy across generational timescales, or does framing ethical obligations to the dead as thermodynamic state variables commit a category error that makes the quantitative comparison incoherent from the outset?
