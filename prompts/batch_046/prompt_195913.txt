Design a 75-minute seminar activity for 12 graduate students that makes them feel the recursive tension between the inside view and the outside view when either is applied to its own evidential basis. Phase 1 (15 min): each student privately forecasts their accuracy on 20 hidden binary factual claims and records a confidence interval. Phase 2 (20 min): reveal actual scores; assign students to "Inside-View Advocates" and "Outside-View Advocates" using an algorithm they are told weights historical calibration data—though the envelope reveals afterward that assignment was random and the first-round scores were noise-injected by ±15%. Phase 3 (20 min): the two groups must negotiate, with a non-monetary point-stakes payoff (ranking + symbolic prize), whether to calibrate round-two forecasts using their case-by-case reasoning (inside) or the revealed unreliability of the grouping mechanism (outside). The rules should force them to choose before they know whether applying the outside view to the mechanism destroys the data needed for the outside view. Phase 4 (20 min): debrief mapping each decision point onto the problem of meta-inductive instability and the impossibility of a view from nowhere. Specify the exact negotiation rules, the point scheme, a timeline with minute marks, what will predictably go wrong the first run (students treating the noise injection as a trick rather than a structural feature), and be explicit about which element—the self-undermining of the outside view or the ineliminability of first-person reasoning—the activity can genuinely reproduce and which it must be supplied by the facilitator. No lecture up front; name the distinction only after they have lived the failure.
