For my philosophy of language seminar on vagueness, I want to apply the epistemicist and supervaluationist frameworks to Blade Runner. Read the film through the question of whether the human/replicant distinction is vague in an epistemic or an ontological sense: is there a hidden sharp boundary that Deckard and Rachael fail to grasp, or does the narrative present multiple admissible precisifications with no unique fact of the matter? Focus especially on the Voight-Kampff test as an attempted precisification, on Rachael's crisis upon learning her memories are implanted, and on Roy Batty's death monologue — asking in each case whether the scene supports an epistemicist reading (the truth is there but concealed) or a supervaluationist one (the boundary is genuinely unsettled). I want the reading to be faithful in both directions: don't reduce the film to an illustration of either view, and don't distort the philosophical distinction to fit the plot. End by assessing whether Blade Runner contributes something to the debate that formal semantics neglects — for instance, about the ethical costs of drawing a precise line where none naturally exists — and flag any scene that actively resists both readings.
