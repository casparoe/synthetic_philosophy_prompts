I am moderating a podcast interview with an unnamed philosopher who has recently defended a precise interpretive claim about Foucault's concept of counter-conduct, drawn from the 1978 lecture series published as Security, Territory, Population. She argues that contemporary voluntary self-quantification through wearable biometric devices is not best understood as internalized panoptic discipline—the mode Foucault analyzes in Discipline and Punish—but rather as a form of counter-conduct: users actively redirect the flow of power-knowledge by curating, interpreting, and socially circulating their own health data, in ways structurally analogous to the pastoral resistance Foucault describes. I need a sequence of approximately eight interview questions that treat this as a substantive philosophical position rather than a rhetorical slogan. Specifically, include one question that presses whether counter-conduct can genuinely apply when the practice is commercially incentivized, algorithmically structured, and dependent on proprietary platforms—conditions quite distant from Foucault's original religious-dissent examples—followed by a sharp follow-up keyed to her most probable reply, namely that users still experience a robust sense of autonomy. Then include a question that forces her to specify, in conceptual rather than metaphorical terms, exactly how voluntary biometric self-tracking differs from the self-surveillance produced by disciplinary normalization. Finally, end with a question about what empirical or conceptual conditions would have to obtain for her interpretation to fail—what evidence would demonstrate that these devices are functioning as pure disciplinary apparatuses rather than as genuine sites of counter-conduct.
