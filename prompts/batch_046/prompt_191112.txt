I'm taking a course on moral desert this semester and also just went through a pretty messy breakup where my ex kept saying you'll get your karma, which weirdly made me actually think about whether karma is meant to be a moral theory or just a comfort phrase. Anyway, I started reading some analtic philosophy of desert and a few Buddhist translations, and there's this argument I keep seeing that just stalls every time — whether karma is genuinely a theory of moral desert or only a causal account of how intentions form habits and reproduce suffering. The desert-people say karma explains why suffering is deserved because intention (cetana) binds agent and outcome across lives; the causal-people say that's metaphysical window dressing and karma really just describes psychologial conditioning. Both sides accept the same basic picture — intention, action, result (vipaka) — and yet nobody moves an inch.

My own hunch is that the stalemate hides a problem nobody names: what I call the karmic non-identity problem. If anatta is right, then the being who performs the deed and the being who experiences the result aren't identical in any robust sense, so the desert relation collapses — you can't deserve punishment if the punished person isn't the one who acted. But the desert side just replies that desert doesn't need strict personal identity, only continuty of a mental stream. And then everyone shrugs and goes back to the same examples.

So is this actually a substantive disagreement about identity and moral responsibility, or is it verbal — one side using deserve in a distributive sense and the other in a retributive sense? Or are they answering different questions, one about normative justification and one about causal explanation? And if your diagnosis is that it's about identity, is there any conceptual distinction or thought experiment that could actually break the stalemate, or is this just a bedrock clash about whether mental continuty is enough for desert? I don't need a bibliography — I just want to understand what kind of disagreement I'm actually watching.
