Consider the conceptual boundary between intent alignment—systems that reliably do what specific operators, users, or deployers intend—and value alignment—systems that pursue values that are correct, legitimate, or broadly shared independent of any particular directive. Rather than treating this as a settled taxonomy, treat it as a live distinction whose coherence, dependencies, and practical stakes are themselves in question. Several open problems sit in this narrow space: whether “intent” can be defined for non-agentic systems without smuggling in the very normative commitments that intent alignment is supposed to bracket; whether value alignment necessarily collapses into a form of paternalistic intent alignment once one specifies whose values are being pursued; the logical and normative dependency between the two projects—specifically, whether resolving intent alignment is conceptually upstream of value alignment, orthogonal to it, or even a precondition for asking value questions at all; and what regulatory or failure-mode implications actually follow from maintaining the distinction versus erasing it.
