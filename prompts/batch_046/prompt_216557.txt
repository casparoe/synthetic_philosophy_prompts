Quine's gavagai example is usually presented as showing that a native's utterance is equally compatible with rabbit, undetached rabbit part, or rabbit stage, with no behavioral fact of the matter to settle the translation. But there is a further layer that gets less attention: the translator must also assign a logical form to the native's quantificational apparatus—whether they have object-level quantification, plural quantification, or something more exotic—to make the translation compositional. Suppose we have two ideal translators, both strictly behaviorist and both fully accommodating all observable assent and dissent patterns, but Translator A assigns the native a first-order logic with singular terms referring to rabbits, while Translator B assigns a plural logic with terms referring to rabbit-scatterings that are then summed. Both manuals are behaviorally adequate, yet they diverge not just in ontology but in the compositional rules that map syntax to semantics. Explain whether Quine's thesis of ontological relativity actually addresses this kind of divergence, or whether it tacitly assumes that logical form is fixed and only reference is underdetermined. In particular, does the need to choose a compositional framework mean that the indeterminacy is deeper than Quine acknowledged—reaching into the structure of the translation manual itself—or does Quine's own naturalized epistemology provide resources to say that logical form, like ontology, is ultimately a pragmatic choice?
