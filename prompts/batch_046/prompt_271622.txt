For a study group I'm writing down my commitments on inside vs outside view and epistemic modesty so people can poke holes. Here they are, probably with a typo or two:

- I think epistemic modesty means the outside view should dominate my inside view in almost every predicitive or evalutive domain.
- I think there's still a hard boundary where inside view is conceptually prior—knowing my own mental states or what I genuinely value can't be replaced by aggregating other people's reports.
- I think if I'm genuinely expert in something (say philosophy), my inside-view reasoning about a specific problem shouldn't be fully overridden by base rates on expert failure, though it should still be heavily discounted.
- I think applying outside-view base rates to deeply personal, singular choices—like having a kid or leaving a job—isn't modesty, it's a kind of self-aliensation; not because there's "no reference class," but because agency requires owning the call from the inside.
- I also think modesty requires treating my own reasoning as just another data point, not giving it special weight.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in real tension vs only look like it. For the genuine ones rank them by severity, say what'd have to give, and what's the cheapest fix. Then tell me which single revision cleans up the most mess without collapsing the whole thing. This is an audit of my own set, not a lecture.
