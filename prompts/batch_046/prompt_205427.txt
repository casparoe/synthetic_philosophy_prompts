I'm working through a problem in the metaphysics of levels with a colleague who insists that every hierarchical explanation eventually bottoms out in an "inviolate level"—a stratum of reality that explains others but is never itself explained or altered by them. She takes Escher's Drawing Hands as her guiding image: just as one hand seems to draw the other while both emerge from the same page, she thinks nature contains loops of determination, yet there must remain one hand that is not being drawn—otherwise we fall into vicious circularity. I find this unsatisfying, because it seems to smuggle in a privileged ontological vantage point without argument. Could you write an essay that treats this as a live controversy? Specifically, weigh whether the notion of an inviolate level can survive once we take tangled hierarchies seriously—not merely as an artistic curiosity, but as a structural feature of scientific explanation, where macro-scale boundary conditions and selection pressures appear to constrain micro-dynamics just as genuinely as micro-laws generate macro-patterns. In your essay, develop what we might call the blank-page paradox: the white space between Escher's hands is neither drawn nor undrawn, yet it is the very condition that allows the illusion of mutual creation to hold; analogously, ask whether there might be a "groundless middle" in nature that serves as the condition for both upward and downward causation without belonging to either level. Reach and defend a clear conclusion—either that an inviolate substrate is indispensable and the blank-page paradox collapses into it, or that tangled hierarchy genuinely dissolves any untouched level and forces us to reconceive fundamentality as distributed rather than localized. Write formally, with careful attention to the strongest objection on the side you reject, and avoid ending with mere agnosticism.
