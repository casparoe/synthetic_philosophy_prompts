I run a philosophy of science seminar with about 10 particpants, 80 minutes. Design an in-class game that makes them feel the hidden pull of Aumann's common prior assumption — the point where rational peers see the same evidence, talk it through, and still stay apart because they never actually shared a prior. Inject a niche twist: hidden model-family cards that assign each player either a Bayesian or frequentist framing of the same dataset, so the disagreement feels structural rather than just opinionated. Give clear rules, roles (some get raw data, some only summaries), a point-based payoff scheme, and a timeline where they update beliefs after each round but can't show their cards. The insight should land before anyone names the theorem. Then include a debrief mapping rounds to mutual vs common knowledge, what predictably breaks the first time, and an honest note on which formal feature — the infinite I-know-that-you-know hierarchy — the activty can't reproduce. No upfront lecture, and don't ask for citations or reading lists.
