As a doctoral researcher in philosophy working on relational ontology and the metaphysics of personhood, I am preparing an opening text for a workshop paper on Ubuntu and the problem of individuated agency within constitutive relationality — a framing that moves beyond the standard contrast with Western individualism without collapsing Ubuntu into mere collectivism or causal social dependence. I want a poem to perform this argument structurally rather than thematically. Write a pantoum — the form in which the second and fourth lines of each quatrain become the first and third lines of the next — so that the speaker's language is literally borrowed across stanzas.

The speaker should attempt to assert a pre-relational first-person authority, drawing on vocabulary from analytic debates about personal identity such as psychological continuity, bodily persistence, and narrative selfhood, only to have each assertion syntactically absorbed into phrases previously spoken in the second person or in the plural. The interlocking repetition should do the philosophical work: by the final stanza, the grammatical I should remain intact but be entirely composed of lines that originated outside it, illustrating that under Ubuntu — understood here as the view that personhood is constituted through relation rather than preceded by it — first-person reference is always already distributed. Do not reduce this to a slogan about being a person through other persons; instead, let the form show that self-reference functions as a kind of citation. Avoid reducing relationality to psychological continuity or narrative identity imported from analytic literature, and avoid treating it as mere causal dependence or social construction. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with both Ubuntu scholarship and analytic personal identity theory would recognize the positions rendered correctly rather than caricatured, and it should require no bibliographic knowledge or citation to be understood. The tone should be serious, suitable for an academic audience, and precise enough that the structural argument is visible without explanatory footnotes.
