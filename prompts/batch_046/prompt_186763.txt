i'm putting together a taxonomy for my epist seminar on the bootstrapping problem — you know, the circularity worry when you use a table (or memory, or perception) to justify that very table's reliabilty. i want a decision tree that sorts actual positions by three diagnostic splits, not by the usual "liberal vs conservative" labels people just memorize.

the splits should be:
1. is epistemic circularity ever rationally permissible, or is it always a defeater?
2. is so-called "easy knowledge" genuine knowledge, or only a pragmatic entitlement to keep believing?
3. does the problem only hit externalist/reliabilist accounts, or does it bite internalist views too?

build the tree so each leaf gets a named view (e.g., vogel-style liberalism, bergmann-style conservatism, maybe a pragmatic middle ground) or "unnamed/unstable" if the combo is incoherent or unexplored. for any empty leaf, say briefly why it's empty — incoherent, unattractive, or just nobody's defended it. and flag any pair of leaves that the tree can't actually seperate — i have a feeling some internalist responses and pragmatic entitlement views collapse into each other.

please keep what's settled (like that bare circular reasoning is bad) clearly seperate from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this. don't present one side as the consensus. write it casual — i'm a grad student trying to map the logical space, not asking for a literature review.
