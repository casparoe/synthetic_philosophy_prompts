Natural history museums and peer-reviewed history journals apply a consistent but largely unarticulated sorting rule to claims about the past. Fomenko's New Chronology—which re-dates the entirety of recorded history by collapsing millennia into centuries and identifying historical figures as duplicate representations—is excluded from academic historiography and relegated to popular pseudohistory, despite its extensive citation of primary sources and internal mathematical modeling. By contrast, heterodox but institutionally credentialed revisions to Bronze Age chronology, which also collapse conventional timelines and rely on contested statistical methods, remain within the bounds of legitimate scholarly debate and are assigned to graduate seminars. Meanwhile, museum curators routinely place exhibits on Atlantis and ancient-astronaut theories in galleries labeled "Myth, Legend, and Popular Imagination," physically separated from chronological historical displays, even when those exhibits explicitly reference real archaeological puzzles—such as the precise engineering of megalithic structures or anomalous metallurgical traces—that also motivate mainstream research questions. What philosophical theory of demarcation does this bifurcated institutional practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view: is it Popperian falsifiability, methodological naturalism, sociological credentialism, or an ontology that distinguishes "the historical past" (accessible only through certified disciplinary methods) from "the imagined past"? Say whether the practice actually requires that view or is compatible with rival demarcation philosophies—such as a purely evidentialist probabilism or a Lakatosian research-programme approach. Then, taking a definite position rather than cataloguing options, tell me what would have to change in museum layout, journal peer-review criteria, and textbook organization if that implicit philosophical commitment were replaced by a straightforward evidentialist rule that admitted any claim about the past into the historical narrative strictly in proportion to its current evidential support, with no special gatekeeping role for institutional pedigree.
