Write a villanelle—nineteen lines in five tercets and a closing quatrain, rhyme scheme ABA, with two refrains that alternate in the traditional pattern—staging the debate between endurantism and perdurantism through a single bronze statue that has stood for centuries while its arm was replaced and its surface oxidized. Assign one refrain to the endurantist insistence that the statue is wholly present at each moment despite change, and the other refrain to the perdurantist claim that only successive temporal parts exist, none identical to the whole. Weave the rhyme sounds around the words “whole” and “part” (or direct cognates), so that the sonic repetition enacts the metaphysical standoff. Let the compulsory return of the refrains and their final adjacency in the quatrain do the philosophical work: the two positions should circle each other without synthesis, ending side by side but unresolved. Ground the abstraction in concrete imagery—green corrosion, a cast replacement, the weight of bronze—so that a metaphysician of persistence recognizes the three-dimensionalist and four-dimensionalist positions, while a general reader finds an actual poem rather than a versified argument. Do not title the poem with a philosophical label, and do not resolve the tension in a final moral; let the form itself be the argument.
