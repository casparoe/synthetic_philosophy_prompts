hey im prepping a taxonomy / decision tree for a grad seminar on liberty — not the usual "what did berlin say" stuff, but something that sorts people by how they react to a concrete case. the case: a warehouse uses an AI manager that technically could scan every keystroke and fire anyone instantly, but in practice it only flags extreme outliers and human managers rarely intervene. workers know the system is live and that the rules are whatever the company decides that morning.

i want the tree built from three branching questions, each with yes/no/maybe options, so the logical space comes first and then we map positions onto it.

branch 1: is freedom reduced just by the existence of this unchecked power, even if it's never used?
branch 2: does it matter whether the power is arbitrary (no fixed rules or appeals) versus just being present?
branch 3: is there a seperate freedom deficit when workers can't form long-term life plans because they have to stay constantly vigilant, regardless of whether anyone actually fires them?

the leaves need to include: pure negative liberty, pure republican non-domination, pure positive self-mastery, a hybrid "negative-plus" that cares about interference but also demands predictable rules, and a "republican-reduction" view that treats domination as just a special kind of potential interference. also include one combination that sounds like it should exist but apparently doesnt — call it the empty slot — and diagnose briefly whether its empty because its incoherent, ugly, or just unexplored.

for each leaf, name the position and write one sentence on its most awkward commitment in this warehouse case. then flag any two leaves that these three branches cant actually seperate.

format:
- Case setup (about 150 words)
- The three branch questions with answer options (about 250 words)
- Leaf map with named positions, hybrids, and empty slot (about 400 words)
- Unseparable pairs and gaps (about 150 words)

keep it casual — write like youre emailing a colleague. dont include a bibliography or citation list; the students know where to find berlin and pettit. there might be a typo or two.
