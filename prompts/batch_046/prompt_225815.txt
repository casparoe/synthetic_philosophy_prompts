hey so im giving a talk next tuesday at this local philosphy meetup i go to—like 12 people, mostly hobbyists who read a ton of Dennett and a couple retired engineers, zero actual academics—about this thing ive been chewing on for months. i think Dennetts three stances thing (physical, design, intentional) misses a whole category i keep running into, which im calling ghost-design patterns: stuff that looks like it was built for a purpose but is actually caused by the absence of that purpose. my go-to example is tree roots growing in the exact shape of old iron pipes long after the pipes are gone and rotted away; the shape only makes sense if you picture the missing pipe, but nobody intended it and its not just raw physics either because the form depends on something that isnt there anymore. im defending that this is its own stance, not just a sloppy mix of the other three, because if you force it into physical you lose why the shape is explanitory, and if you force it into intentional or design youre inventing a mind that isnt there. can you draft me a Q&A prep sheet for after the talk? i want about eight objections ordered by how much damage theyd actually do to me in that room, phrased the way a self-taught Dennett fan or an engineer would say them—not like a journal referee. each one needs a two-minute reply i can actually say out loud, plus an honest note on where that reply is soft or dodgy. include the question im praying nobody asks, probably something about whether ghost-design makes any new predicitions or if its just relabeling. my sketch of the arguement is basically that without this fourth stance you either have to pretend these patterns arent real explanations or you have to hallucinate an intention, and both feel wrong when youre actually standing in front of the roots.
