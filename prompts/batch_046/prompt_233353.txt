1. Hedonism about wellbeing is true — pleasure/pain are the only intrinsic goods.

3. So I'm with representational hedonism: pleasures are unified not by feel but by representing a desired state being satisfied.
4. But I also think phenomenal consciousness is basic — there's an irreducible "what it's like" that you can't reduce to representation or function.
5. Pleasure is my go-to example of this irreducible phenomenal stuff.
6. No unconscious pleasures. If there's zero phenomenal character, it's not pleasure, just subpersonal reward processing.

My worry: doesn't (3) clash with (4) and (5)? If pleasure is just representation, why is it the clearest case of irreducible phenomenology? And does (2) combined with (6) actually smuggle in a shared phenomenal feature — like, they're all "conscious" in the same way — which kills (2)? Also, is (1) compatible with (2) and (3) together? If pleasure isn't a natural kind but just a grab-bag of representations, can it still be the single intrinsic good?
