Read Brave New World throught the philosophical lense of tranquilism — the view that only absence of craving and suffering has positive value, with pleasure counting as basically zero. I don't want the usual take that Huxley is just anti-utopia because people don't suffer; I want you to dig into whether the World State achieves genuine tranquility or only a kind of hollow suppression. Compare the conditioning and soma model (desire never forms) with the older idea — Schopenhauerian or Buddhist — that craving can be dissolved from within. Does John the Savage represent a failure of suppression, or does he prove that real tranquility is impossible? And in Mond's debate with him, does the novel treat the absence of craving as a rich state or just an empty room? I'm using this for a short essay, so keep it tight and don't summarize the plot. End with Verdict: [whether the novel ultimately supports, undermines, or complicates tranquilism].
