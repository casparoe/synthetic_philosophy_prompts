We've been arguing about the design norm in low-impact AI—specifically, the proposal that one can constrain an optimizer by requiring it to avoid side effects, measured as divergence from a counterfactual "do nothing" baseline, without first specifying a complete value function that ranks outcomes. The claim is that structural or causal constraints can substitute for normative ones. What philosophical commitments about value, agency, and responsibility does this practice presuppose? Reconstruct its implicit theory: does it require that harm be definable in purely causal or structural terms, independent of prior valuation? Does it presuppose a coherent distinction between intended and unintended effects in the absence of a goal hierarchy? Distinguish what the practice strictly entails from what it merely accommodates, identify where it is philosophically underdetermined—so that rival theories of value or agency might still license the same design choice—and explain what would have to change in the framework if we rejected its implicit view and treated every baseline, including inaction, as already ethically loaded.
