I am a retired physicist who has only recently begun serious study in philosophy and decision theory, and I have agreed to lead a single ninety-minute session for a small interdisciplinary reading group composed mainly of other retired scientists and engineers with no prior training in behavioral economics. The topic is Herbert Simon’s concept of satisficing and the broader framework of bounded rationality, but I want to avoid the superficial treatment in which it is presented merely as “optimizing with limited time.” Instead, I want the session to establish that bounded rationality involves a fundamental shift from substantive to procedural rationality—where the decision architecture itself replaces global maximization with a local search-and-termination rule—so that the audience sees it as a structural feature rather than a temporary resource failure.

Please provide a pedagogical plan, not a lecture transcript. First, specify the conceptual dependency: which distinction must be firmly in place—perhaps between global utility maximization and aspiration-level search—before satisficing can appear as a coherent alternative rather than a deficient form of optimization? Second, propose a single carrying example drawn from scientific or engineering practice—such as stopping criteria in an iterative measurement protocol or a design trade-off under incomplete specifications—rather than from consumer choice; describe exactly what properties the example must possess (for instance, the agent must not possess the full option space in advance) and what it must avoid so that it does not collapse back into a constrained-optimization story. Third, map the predictable misconceptions for this audience: I expect they will try to formalize satisficing as standard optimization with a Lagrange multiplier on computation, or conflate aspiration-level adaptation with Bayesian belief updating, and I need to know at which point in the sequence each should be intercepted. Fourth, suggest one concise diagnostic question I can pose at the end that would reveal whether they understand that bounded rationality is built into the decision procedure rather than being an exogenous scarcity of time or data. Finally, state clearly what should be deliberately omitted at this introductory level—perhaps evolutionary justifications, neuroeconomic implementation details, or the full formal apparatus of resource-rational analysis—so that the session remains focused.

The plan should assume mathematical literacy but no philosophical background.
