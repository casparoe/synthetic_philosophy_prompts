Write a villanelle of nineteen lines with the classic ABA rhyme and two repeating refrains that stage the metaphysical dispute between endurantism and perdurantism. Assign one refrain to the endurantist claim that a person is wholly present at each moment of her existence, and assign the other refrain to the perdurantist claim that she is instead a four-dimensional series of momentary temporal parts linked by continuity relations. Use the five tercets to build the naive picture of an enduring substance in the first two, then let the middle tercets turn on the problem of temporary intrinsics—specifically how an object can be bent at one time and straight at another without contradiction if it is wholly present with both properties—so that the rhyme scheme and refrain collisions do structural work rather than merely decorating the idea. Place the volta in the third tercet, where the contradiction forces the reader to see that something must be given up, and allow the closing quatrain to hold both refrains in unresolved tension without adjudicating between them. The metaphysical rendering should be precise enough that a philosopher of personal identity recognizes the actual positions and the specific objection being pressed, but do not resolve the debate or turn it into allegory. After the poem, include a brief note of no more than three sentences stating how confident you are in the philosophical accuracy of the imagery and identifying the specific line, refrain, or structural choice about which you are least certain.
