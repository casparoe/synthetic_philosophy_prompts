In debates about whether AI-generated images can be art, I keep encountering Tolstoy's slogan that "art is infection" — the artist transmits a feeling to the audience, and if no feeling is transmitted there is no art. One interlocutor used this to argue that because an algorithm has no feelings to transmit, its outputs are necessarily non-art. I want to know whether that inference holds. Can you lay out the different precise theses that the infection metaphor could be expressing — distinguishing, for example, a causal claim about the origin of the work, a phenomenological claim about the audience's experience, a normative claim about artistic value, and an ontological claim about what kind of thing an artwork is — and for each indicate whether it is trivial, whether it is defensible, and whether it actually supports the conclusion that AI outputs cannot be art? I am particularly interested in whether any defensible version requires the artist's own sincerity, or whether it only requires that the audience undergo a contagious emotional response, since that distinction seems to determine whether the argument succeeds.
