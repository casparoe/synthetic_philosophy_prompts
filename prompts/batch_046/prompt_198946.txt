Midterm q3: Kant denies direct moral status to animals. Reconstruct his reasoning (focus on rational nature and the formula of humanity). Then apply it: biotech firms want to breed pigs with modified brains so they never feel pain or distress, specifically for intensive farming. Would Kant have any objection?

Student answer:
Kant holds that only rational autonomous beings are ends-in-themselves, so animals aren't in the moral community directly. We do have indirect duties not to be cruel to them, because practicing cruelty on animals hardens our character and makes us worse toward other people. He basically follows Descartes in seeing animals as mechanistic automata that react but don't genuinely suffer, which is why direct obligations are impossible.

Applying this to teh engineered pigs: since they can't suffer, there's no cruelty involved in farming them, so the indirect duty disappears too. Kant would therefore have no moral objection to the system, as long as the research to develop it didn't involve coercing rational beings. The only risk is people extending this purely instrumental attitude to humans, but that's a separate issue.
