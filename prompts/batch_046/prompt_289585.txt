I was talking with my friend the other day about whether qualia are actually incoherent in a strict sense, not just mysterious, and we kept circling back to this Chase and Sanborn line — that if you try to specify what a quale is without smuggling in functional or representational content, the specification collapses on itself. But it feels like everyone just uses "incoherent" as a synonym for "hard to reduce to physics," which makes the whole debate kind of mushy.

I'm pitching a small philosophy-of-mind grant, maybe three months part-time, and I need a tight two-page proposal. The core question: is the incoherence charge against qualia best parsed as a semantic failure, a conceptual contradiction, or a metaphysical impossibility? I want to take this slightly weird angle: instead of framing it through the usual epistemic-gap story, treat it analagous to debates about presentism in philosophy of time — the issue isn't whether we "feel" the present, but whether the ontology required to ground that feeling conflicts with our best physics. So the method would be: first, formalize the Chase-Sanborn premises with explicit conditions; second, map those onto the presentism structure to see if the failure mode is genuinely structural; third, test whether a "thin" deflationary qualia survives without losing its anti-physicalist bite. A useful result is either showing incoherence is localized (so we salvage a thin version) or showing the analogy breaks down and the charge was never precise to begin with. The project fails if the analogy just muddies the waters and we can't formalize the premises without circularity. Scope should be realistic — maybe 8-10 pages, one semester.
