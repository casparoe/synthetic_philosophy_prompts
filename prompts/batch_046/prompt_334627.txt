Write an essay examining whether George Eliot's religious skepticism—specifically her adoption of a Feuerbachian view that divinity is a projection of human moral ideals and her rejection of personal immortality—can be reconciled with the objective normative authority she assigns to sympathy and moral duty in her fiction. Consider also the counter-argument that she naturalizes morality through an intersubjective account of recognition, allowing objective weight to emerge from the structure of human relations rather than from transcendental command, and that this avoids subjectivism without requiring theistic grounding. Reach and justify a definite conclusion: is her secular humanism internally coherent with her moral realism, or does the projectionist element in her thought erode the authority of the ethical claims her fiction advances? Present the entire response in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as though writing a formal letter.
