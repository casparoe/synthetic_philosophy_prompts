I am writing my dissertation on radical translation and the ontology of reference, and I have become fixated on a particular interpretive puzzle that arises in Quine’s discussion of the indeterminacy of translation—specifically, the underdetermination of analytical hypotheses when a native speaker utters the token gavagai in the presence of a rabbit. In my seminar we have reconstructed a consistent track record of judgments made by a hypothetical radical translator—call her the Interpreter—who has ruled on a series of observational scenarios. In Case 1, when a speaker points toward a running brown mammal in continuous view and produces the token, the Interpreter translates it as referring to an enduring object (a rabbit) rather than to a temporal stage or to an undetached part. In Case 2, when the speaker utters the same token while holding only a detached ear with no visible body, she still opts for the enduring-object translation but notes that the evidential basis is now thinner. In Case 3, when the token is produced in the presence of two spatially separated but causally linked rabbit-shaped particle clouds—an analog of quantum superposition in the native environment—she refuses to adjudicate between the enduring-object and the scattered-mereological-sum hypotheses, declaring the evidence genuinely silent. In Case 4, when the speaker produces the token repeatedly at regular intervals while the visible form flickers in and out of existence due to intermittent sensory occlusion, she adopts a four-dimensional, stage-based ontology and translates the token as referring to a rabbit-stage rather than to a persisting substance.

Now my advisor has introduced a new scenario to test whether the Interpreter’s commitments cohere. In Case 5, the speaker points toward a virtual-rendered rabbit in an immersive environment where the rendering engine periodically deletes and re-instantiates the object with identical properties but no causal continuity in the underlying code; the speaker, who is unaware of the rendering mechanism, utters the token with the same prosodic profile as in Case 1. Given her prior rulings, should she translate the token as referring to an enduring virtual object, to a sequence of discrete virtual stages, or should she suspend judgment? I want to know which of Cases 1–4 is the closest analog, what competing rules—substance-ontology, stage-ontology, or evidential-abstention—are equally compatible with her earlier verdicts, and what further observational case (for instance, involving cross-modal identification or speaker error-correction) would best discriminate between them. More specifically, if we recommend that she extend her framework consistently, what ontological principle would she be adopting with each possible choice, and do her precedents genuinely underdetermine Case 5 or does one rule clearly dominate? I am less interested in a definitive answer than in a careful reconstruction of the interpretive rules she seems to be running and an honest assessment of the coherence costs of each extension.
