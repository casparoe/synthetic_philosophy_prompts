Write a Petrarchan sonnet—fourteen lines in iambic pentameter, octave rhyming ABBA ABBA, sestet rhyming CDECDE, volta at line 9—from the voice of a mathematical constructivist confronting a classical realist over an unsolved claim about infinite sets (do not name Goldbach’s conjecture; keep the claim generic so the poem works for any open problem). In the octave, present the realist’s position: that the claim is determinately true or false independent of our knowledge, guaranteed by the law of excluded middle, with mathematical objects existing whether or not we construct them; let the rhyme pairs link settled ontological binaries (for example, true/false, real/ideal, being/seeing). At the volta, refuse the binary: state that for infinite domains we lack entitlement to the law of excluded middle, that double negation does not restore the original assertion, and that existence requires an explicit constructive proof rather than mere consistency. In the sestet, develop the intuitionist view—truth as provability, negation as reducibility to absurdity, mathematical entities as mental constructions verified only by proof—so that the rhyme scheme tracks the logical shift, pairing terms that mark the gap between absence of proof and proof of absence (for example, proof/absence, construct/interrupt, mind/find). The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with Brouwer’s rejection of unrestricted excluded middle and with Heyting’s proof-conditional reading of the logical constants recognizes the position, not generic skepticism about mathematics. Do not include citations, references, or a bibliography. After the poem, include a section titled “Commentary on the choices made” (approximately 150–200 words) explaining how the sonnet’s form—especially the volta and the rhyme architecture—mirrors the logical structure of intuitionism, and noting any deliberate compression or anachronism in rendering the philosophical debate.
