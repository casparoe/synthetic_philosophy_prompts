Construct a one-paragraph, realistic workplace thought experiment—no science fiction, no named canonical texts, no exotic devices—in which an employee is clearly unfree according to negative liberty yet free according to republican liberty and according to a collective-autonomy reading of positive liberty. Use a present-day institutional mechanism (for instance, a warehouse logistics worker whose shifts and micro-tasks are assigned in real time by an algorithm whose source code is fully auditable and whose parameters are set by an elected, recallable workplace council, with no individual opt-out short of resignation): the interference must be pervasive and continuous, but the power behind it must be structurally non-arbitrary and contestable. After the scenario, proceed in this order: (1) state the verdict for each of the three liberty conceptions and justify it by identifying what counts as interference, domination, and self-mastery in this specific case; (2) isolate the exact structural feature that makes negative liberty peel away from the other two, and show that if that feature is removed—say, by replacing the council with an unaccountable owner—the case collapses back into a standard domination scenario where all three conceptions agree the worker is unfree; (3) present the strongest reply a defender of negative liberty could make to deny that democratic authorization removes the unfreedom; and (4) state explicitly what the experiment is designed to test, namely whether pervasive but democratically authorized structural interference can be reconciled with republican and positive freedom while remaining a clear loss of negative freedom. Keep constraints visible: realistic labor relations only, no perfect predictors, no bibliographic requests, and the scenario must generate the verdicts from the theories rather than stipulating them.
