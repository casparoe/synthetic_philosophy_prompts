The Office of Strategic Assessment requires every long-range forecast about regime durability to begin with an outside-view step: analysts place the current case into a reference class of past empires and regimes, compute a statistical base rate for the predicted outcome, and only then add inside-view causal adjustments. The office’s methodology manual mandates that historical analogues be weighted by a hyperbolic decay function of temporal distance—an empire 150 years away counts as about one-third as relevant as one 50 years away, with the decay parameter calibrated to each analyst’s tested historical-patience score—while simultaneously treating these weights as objective corrections rather than subjective preferences. At the same time, the manual prohibits analysts from merging their individually weighted reference classes into a pooled organizational base rate; each analyst must defend her own decay curve and class selection in isolation. Meanwhile, the same office runs a separate technological-forecasting unit that uses identical reference-class logic but applies linear temporal discounting and requires full aggregation of analysts into a single institutional base rate. What view of historical causation, epistemic authority, and the objectivity of reference classes does the strategic-assessment protocol presuppose? Can the hyperbolic weighting be reconciled with the claim that the outside view delivers an objective base rate rather than a personal prior? Does the ban on aggregation presuppose an individualist theory of forecasting expertise, or could it be defended on purely statistical grounds? And what does the divergence between the strategic and technological units reveal about whether the practice is driven by a consistent philosophy of historical analogy, or merely by institutional inertia? If the office replaced hyperbolic decay with uniform temporal weighting and permitted aggregation in strategic forecasting, which parts of its implicit epistemology would collapse, and which could survive unchanged?
