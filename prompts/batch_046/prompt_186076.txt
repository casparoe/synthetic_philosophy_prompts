Compare Eric Hobsbawm's concept of invented tradition with Benedict Anderson's concept of imagined communities as explanations for why modern nations claim ancient origins despite being recent constructions. For each, identify the precise mechanism that transforms modern political form into the experience of timeless belonging: Hobsbawm's emphasis on ritualized repetition and institutionalized symbolic practice versus Anderson's emphasis on print-capitalism and the experience of shared, simultaneous temporal emptiness. Then apply both frameworks to the conceptual problem of archival retrofitting — the practice, visible since the late twentieth century, by which state agencies digitize, catalog, and canonize selective pre-national folklore, thereby giving invented traditions an apparent documentary depth that neither ritual repetition nor print circulation alone provides. Which of the two mechanisms — institutional ritual or communicative infrastructure — is more fundamentally destabilized when the archive itself becomes the primary site of national invention? Justify your answer by showing what each theory must concede or revise, rather than by cataloguing empirical cases.
