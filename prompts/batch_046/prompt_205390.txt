Since retiring from experimental physics I have begun studying distributive justice, and at a recent seminar I witnessed a disagreement between two philosophers—Maya and Jonas—about whether equality should be of resources, welfare, or capabilities. Maya defended Sen’s capabilities approach: two individuals with identical resource bundles may possess radically different real freedoms because of disability or environment, just as two identically specified sensors yield different readings in different thermal conditions. Jonas replied that capabilities lack intersubjective measurability; selecting which functionings count requires normative judgment, so assessing equality of capabilities is like attempting instrument calibration without a reference standard, and policy ought to rely on observable, exchangeable resources. Maya countered that resource metrics are themselves theory-laden—market prices depend on institutional and preference structures—so they conceal normative commitments behind a veneer of quantifiability rather than avoiding them. Jonas then argued that resources nevertheless satisfy minimal public verifiability, without which equality cannot be enforced, whereas capabilities remain epistemically private. They asked me to referee. Please adjudicate these four moves in order: rate Maya’s sensor analogy, Jonas’s calibration objection, Maya’s theory-ladenness reply, and Jonas’s verifiability criterion, each as clearly right, partly right, or clearly mistaken, with brief justification. Then indicate who is closer to the truth overall, and point out any significant consideration both have missed—particularly whether equality requires cardinal measurement of some metric at all, or whether an ordinal comparison of opportunity sets might suffice.
