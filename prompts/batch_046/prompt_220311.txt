I was arguing with my friends about Rawls the other night and we ended up in the weeds about what the veil of ignorance and the differnce principle actually commit us to if we apply them to stuff besides just cash. I want to know if there's a single principel behind my gut calls on these cases, or if some have to go.

Here are my verdicts. One: a society where the worst off are farm workers earning 30k with full control over their schedule feels more just than one where they earn 35k but are stuck in warehouses with algorithmic scheduling and zero autonomy, so I guess I'm weighing freedom over income at the bottom. Two: a basic-income-style scheme funded by taxing 'cognitive labor' jobs like data labeling, where the worst-off do the labeling but get a large share of the proceeds back — I'm okay with it if the tax rate is high, but not if the labeling is mandatory rather than voluntary. Three: a society with near-perfect equality except people with a specific rare genetic condition that makes social interaction painful get extra private space and a stipend — that feels right. Four: people who are just naturally bad at negotiation get a subsidy because they keep ending up in worse jobs — that feels wrong, like we're correcting for personality rather than circumstance. Five: behind the veil, if you might be born in 1900 or 2100, I'd reject a deal that makes the 2100 worst-off rich but leaves the 1900 worst-off in poverty, even if the long-run average is better.

Can you extract the principel that fits cases one through four, and tell me what it says about case five? Also predict what I'd think about a case I haven't mentioned: a society that guarantees everyone a floor of leisure time rather than income, but allows huge income gaps above that floor. And be honest about which of my verdicts your principel can't save without getting too contrived.
