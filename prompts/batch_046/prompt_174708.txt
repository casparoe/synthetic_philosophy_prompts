I'm writing a brief for a graduate seminar on autonomous systems and moral responsibility, and I keep running into institutional designs that seem to distribute blame without distributing control. Consider the regulatory architecture now used in several advanced cargo-drone networks—specifically, the "distributed oversight lattice" model: no individual pilot is assigned to a flight; instead, a remote supervisor monitors up to twelve drones simultaneously, an onboard "ethical arbitration module" logs every navigational decision in an encrypted format that the supervisor can only decrypt after landing, and a ground-based "accountability anchor" is legally designated as the responsible party for incidents—though this anchor is explicitly barred from issuing real-time commands and may only review logs post-hoc. The liability rules assign blame to the anchor if they missed a pre-flight risk flag, to the manufacturer if the drone's behavior deviated from its certified training distribution, and to no party whatsoever—creating what the regulations term a responsibility void—when the deviation was emergent and the supervisor's encrypted access structurally prevented timely intervention.

What philosophical commitments about moral agency and blame does this architecture presuppose? Must responsibility require causal control, epistemic access, or some combination, and does the framework implicitly endorse a novel form of distributed agency? The practice demands post-hoc review, which suggests that knowledge is necessary for blame, yet it structurally denies real-time knowledge, which suggests that physical presence or institutional role is sufficient. Is the responsibility void defensible within any standard account—consequentialist, control-based, or virtue-theoretic—or does it reveal an incoherence? Reconstruct the implicit theory embedded in the design, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely accommodates it, and assess whether giving the supervisor real-time decryption authority would genuinely close the gap or simply relocate the philosophical tension elsewhere in the system.
