Construct a branching decision-tree taxonomy that classifies positions on realism about representations in deep neural networks. Each node must pose a concrete case or judgment, not an abstract label, so that a respondent can navigate the tree without already knowing the named positions.

Root case: A vision transformer’s hidden states reliably decode object boundaries on natural images, yet imperceptible adversarial perturbations flip both the model’s classification and its saliency patterns without altering the human-visible scene. Does this adversarial fragility show that the model never genuinely represented the boundary?

From there, build binary branches around three further concrete distinctions:
(i) whether representation requires locally interpretable feature-to-unit mapping, or can be distributed across layers with no single neuron tracking one feature;
(ii) whether representation requires an internal downstream “consumer” mechanism with normative success conditions, and whether evolutionary/functionalist norms apply (so that artificial nets necessarily lack them);
(iii) whether counterfactual stability of the processing pipeline across world-preserving perturbations is decisive, regardless of local interpretability or consumer presence.

The tree must terminate in exactly these seven leaves: (1) strong localized realism; (2) distributed structural realism; (3) process reliabilism—the view that representation exists iff the pipeline is counterfactually stable across perturbations that preserve the world-state, irrespective of interpretability or consumer norms; (4) internal-consumer functionalism; (5) evolutionary-normative denial; (6) instrumental anti-realism; and (7) deflationary pragmatic pluralism. For each leaf, indicate whether it accepts or rejects representational status in the root case.

Identify any pair of leaves your tree’s questions fail to cleanly separate, and briefly diagnose every unoccupied logical combination (e.g., incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored?). Keep the tree to six or seven decision nodes. End with a single line summarizing the unoccupied combinations, enclosed in \summary{...}.
