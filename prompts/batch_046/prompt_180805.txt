maya said the whole paradox depends on treating emotion like it needs belief in the reality of its object. but she pointed out we get emotional about hypothetical futures all the time -- worrying about retirement or feeling relieved a plane crash didnt happen even though it was never going to. fiction is just another hypothetical. leo said thats wrong because retirement involves your actual future self, and counterfactual relief is about real possibilities being avoided. fictional characters aren't just unreal, they're often impossible or inconsistent, so comparing them to counterfactuals is a category mistake.

maya came back: but we respond emotionally to impossible fiction too, like time travel stories, and to purely abstract ethical scenarios -- feeling disgust at a trolley problem setup even though there's no actual victim. so emotions clearly dont need real targets. leo replied that in the trolley case you're evaluating a moral principle, not grieving for a specific person, and with time travel we just bracket the impossibility and imagine a consistent world. so there's always a "belief-like acceptance" within the frame.

maya then said ok but if it's just make-believe acceptance, why do we get real physiological reactions -- heart racing, tears? if we're just pretending, why does the body react? leo said we're reacting to the sensory representation, not the represented object -- like jumping at a shadow. there's no paradox because the fear is of the image, not of the non-existent monster.

maya said that doesnt work because the intentional content is specific -- you fear *that the killer is about to kill her*, not just that there's a dark shape and loud music. and she brought up dreams: in dreams we respond emotionally to non-existent people but dont call it paradoxical, except in dreams we usually think it's real. fiction is weirder because we maintain awareness it's fake, yet the emotion sticks.

leo said maybe fiction is just a shared voluntary dream, and the "paradox" comes from mixing up levels -- we're analyzing fiction as if it's a belief system when it's actually just brain stimulation with narrative overlay. maya said thats too reductive because then you cant explain why different narratives produce different emotions with the same sensory input.
