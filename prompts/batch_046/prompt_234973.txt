hey so i'm writing a chapter on how different quota systems implicitly answer the question "what is the group for?" and i'm stuck on whether these practices are even consistent with each other. take three things: US uni admissions using race as a plus factor in holistic review (not a hard quota technically, but functionally a demographic target), India's reservation system with the creamy-layer exclusion that pulls people out once a family hits an income/education threshold, and Norway's boardroom gender quota that has a built-in sunset clause once representation hits 40%.

the puzzle is that each one seems to preseuppose a different metaphysics of the beneficiary group. india's creamy layer treats caste disadvantage as something that can be literally outgrown by a family line—once you're creamy, your kid doesn't inherit the reservation. that sounds like an etiological account of group harm, where the wrong is in the causal history of the family, not in current social position. but norway's sunset clause treats gender imbalance as a temporary structural distortion that corrects itself once the pipeline fills, which sounds more like a causal-role view: the point of the quota isn't to compensate for past sexism but to change the causal role of gender in board selection going forward. and US holistic review, especially post-SFFA with all the essay workarounds, seems to preseuppose that race is a lens through which experience is filtered—almost a phenominological commitment—rather than a proxy for class or a compensation for historical wrong.

so my question is: what philosophical commitments about group identity, merit, and the temporality of justice does this trio of practices actually preseuppose? do they strictly need these theories, or are they compatible with rival ones? for instance, could india's creamy layer work under a purely causal-role account of caste disadvantage, or does it secretly depend on an etiological story about inherited stigma? and if we replaced the implicit theory in one case—say, treating gender quotas as permanent reparative measures rather than temporary pipeline fixes—what exactly in the instituional design would have to break?

i'm not asking for a bibliography, i just want to know whether i'm forcing consistency where there is none, or whether there's a hidden shared framework i'm missing. also, is the US case actually preseupposing anything deep about group metaphysics, or is it just tractability politics dressed up in philosophical language?
