hey — in our philosophy of time seminar people keep reaching for the 'video tape' analogy for the thermodynamic arrow: the universe is like a film strip, entropy increase is just the projector running forward, and if you ran it backward you'd see smoke collect into cigarettes but the physics wouldn't care. The point they draw is that 'past' and 'future' are like 'forward' and 'backward' on the tape — purely a matter of which frame you're calling 'now', with no intrinsic arrow in the celluloid itself. I think this is smuggling in more than it admits.

Can you lay out the mapping explicitly? What's the projector, what's the film stock, what's the audience? Then tell me where it breaks — especially around the fact that the micro-laws are time-symetric (the tape can be flipped) but the macro-state is not (the audience remembers). There's also this weird asymmetry: with a real tape, both directions exist as actual recordings you could play, but with entropy there's only one low-entropy boundary condition (the big bang) and no corresponding 'high-entropy boundary' at the other end. Does that kill the analogy, or can you patch it by saying the tape is only half-recorded?

And finally: if the analogy survives, does it really support the conclusion that the arrow is subjective? Or does it actually smuggle in an objective gradient (the projector's motor) that the analogy then hides? I feel like the 'no intrinsic arrow' conclusion is riding on the back of the tape's physical symmetry, but the tape doesn't have a past hypothesis. Am I missing something?
