Rather than a general overview, I want a conceptual evaluation. Please rank the following five accounts of scientific progress into tiers from S (most adequate) to D (least adequate), judging them specifically on how they resolve the Scope-Precision Trade-Off that I describe below. In this scenario, Theory A correctly explains an anomalous temperature spike recorded in 1816 ice-core data and offers a broader causal framework, yet it gets three quantitative predictions about atmospheric composition wrong; Theory B predicts only the temperature spike with perfect quantitative accuracy and remains entirely silent on other phenomena. The five accounts are: first, Popper's original qualitative view, which denies that truth-likeness can be measured numerically; second, the logical-distance approach that calculates verisimilitude by comparing the sets of true and false consequences; third, the severity-weighted or min-sum approach, which penalises false consequences according to their logical distance from the truth; fourth, the purely empirical-adequacy view, which defines progress solely as increasing predictive success without invoking truth at all; and fifth, a hybrid position I am developing for my essay, according to which verisimilitude is evaluated only within a Domain of Explanatory Responsibility fixed by a theory's own auxiliary hypotheses, so that predictions falling outside that domain neither add to nor subtract from its score. For each tier, provide a single sentence justifying its placement, with particular attention to whether the account can distinguish Theory A from Theory B without circular reasoning. I do not require a bibliography, citations, or a reading list; I need only the conceptual ranking and reasoning. Please present the result as a tier list with brief annotations.
