I have been trying to work through a problem about identity statements that I understand is associated with Frege, though I have never studied philosophy formally. The familiar example is that learning "The Morning Star is the Evening Star" seems to provide real information, whereas "The Morning Star is the Morning Star" does not, even though both are true identity claims about Venus. I have begun to wonder whether this asymmetry reveals something deep about the nature of identity itself, or whether it is only an artifact of how limited minds store and retrieve references.

To test this, I imagined a universal registry in which every object receives exactly one immutable, globally accessible identifier, with no ambiguity or hidden aliases. In such a system, stating that identifier 7,892,301 equals identifier 7,892,301 conveys nothing beyond the lookup itself, and stating that 7,892,301 equals 4,102,558 conveys precisely the same kind of lookup—there is no leftover "mode of presentation" that could make one statement more informative than the other. So if Frege's puzzle depends on there being different senses for the same reference, wouldn't a fully transparent registry eliminate those senses entirely? And if the puzzle disappears once reference is completely disambiguated, does that mean it was never a feature of identity at all, but merely a temporary symptom of human epistemic fragmentation? I would like to understand where this line of reasoning goes wrong, because it feels as though I am treating a semantic distinction as if it were only a database error.
