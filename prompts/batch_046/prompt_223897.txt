Write an Italian sonnet — octave with ABBAABBA rhyme, sestet with CDECDE rhyme and a clear volta at line nine — spoken by one sphere in a symmetric two-sphere universe that serves as a counterexample to the identity of indiscernibles. In the octave, describe an otherwise empty vacuum in which every intrinsic property is duplicated and no distinguishing mark exists, letting the mirrored rhyme scheme enact the failure of qualitative difference. At the volta, introduce the weak-discernibility reply: the speaker asserts a unique relational position, being, for instance, three miles to the left of its counterpart. In the sestet, however, arrange the rhymes so that the relational terms collapse into terms of sameness — rhyming “left” with “bereft” and “other” with “brother” — so that the form itself undermines the claim that spatial relation establishes real qualitative distinction. End on an ambiguous final line that could read either as two entities addressing one another or as a single being speaking to itself. Use precise imagery (radius, void, reflection without origin) so that a specialist recognises the metaphysical argument being staged without philosophers or texts being named; carry the philosophy through image, rhyme, and turn alone. Write in British English, and keep the reasoning embedded in the poem’s structure rather than in explanatory commentary.
