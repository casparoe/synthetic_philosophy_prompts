(1) i basically buy scheffler's afterlife conjecture: a big part of whats valuable in my life now depends on the background expectation that humanity keeps going after im dead. if i genuinely believed extinction was certain within 50 years, my current projects would feel hollow in a way that has nothing to do with my own death.

(2) but i also think the badness is strictly person-affecting. people who never come into existance cant be harmed, so the wrong has to be done to actual people like me — we lose that value-structure.

(3) at the same time, i reject the counterfactual mugging: once the coin has landed tails, i shouldnt pay, because the counterfactual branch where it landed heads is no longer causally relevant to me now.

(4) but (1) seems to rely on exactly that kind of counterfactual dependency: my present well-being is tied to a future that might never be actualised.

(5) i also think that if extinction is inevitable eventually, the exact timing is morally irrelevant — 200 years vs 2000 years doesnt change the fundamental loss.

(6) finally, i hold that preserving humanity is only obligatory if it maximises expected well-being overall. if a post-human world with digital minds produced more welfare, we should probably welcome extinction rather than resist it.

so: is this a consistent package? does (3) undermine (1) in a way that forces me to drop one? is there a tension between (2) and (6) — am i smuggling an impersonal calculus into a view thats supposed to be person-affecting? and is the conflict between (5) and (1) only apparent, dissolving once you distinguish temporal location from modal dependency?
