Write a villanelle in British English on the paradox of self-amending legal rules — the circular situation in which a constitutional provision authorises its own revision, yet every body empowered to revise it derives its authority solely from that same provision. Choose two refrains drawn from the language of such a rule; for example, one refrain stating that the article may be amended, and the other stating that any amendment binds every successor institution. Structure the poem so that the villanelle’s repetition enacts the paradox: in successive stanzas the refrains should appear in grammatical contexts that gradually alter their legal force, so that by the final couplet they stand in direct tension rather than harmony, with the circularity unresolved. The poem should be precise enough that someone familiar with jurisprudential debates about legal self-reference recognises the problem being performed by the form, yet it should remain a coherent poem for a reader with no legal background. Avoid bibliographies, named theorists, or requests for citations, and use British spelling and punctuation throughout.
