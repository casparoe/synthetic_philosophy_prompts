Hey — I'm running a small seminar on the problem of verisimilitude and need a decision tree that sorts actual positions, not just asks students to pick a philosopher they recognize. Start with the commitment that false theories can be closer to truth than rival false ones. First branch: is verisimilitude basically a logical/content measure (counting true vs false consequences) or a structural/representational one? If structural, next node: do you require ontological identity between theory and world, or do you allow that a false ontology can still rank closer if its representational structure maps well onto real relations — what I'm calling representational plasticity? Then hit them with a concrete case: Theory A gets every observable relation right but posits entities that don't exist; Theory B gets half the relations wrong but posits only real entities. Which is closer to the truth?

I want leaves for logical/content verisimilitude in a Popper-style vein, quantitative distance-minimization in a Niiniluoto-style vein, strict structural realism that demands shared ontology, a plastic-structural view that permits ontological mismatch, and a skeptical no-metric-is-possible position. Flag any pair your tree can't really seperate — my hunch is the plastic-structural view and some pragmatist accounts might collapse together. Also mark empty branches: is there a coherent view that takes logical measurement but denies any structural comparison is meaningful? Keep it to about six or seven nodes, phrase every branch as a concrete question a student could answer without already knowing the label, then drop the named positions onto the answer-combinations. Don't ask for bibliographies or citations; I want them reasoning through the space, not Googling it.
