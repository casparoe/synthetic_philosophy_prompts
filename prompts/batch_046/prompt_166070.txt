I'm the senior methodological advisor to a state forensic mental-health review board that must recommend, for defendants with documented neurological differences, whether a court should treat the offense through the lens of physical causation, functional design failure, or intentional agency. Our panel receives case files mixing neuroimaging, functional assessments, and behavioral transcripts, yet we lack any shared protocol for deciding which explanatory stance—Dennett's physical, design, or intentional—should govern the sentencing recommendation and whether the pattern we invoke is genuinely real at that level rather than a convenient projection. I need a reusable decision procedure my board can run on any file: ordered questions to ask, what counts as sufficient evidence for each, explicit termination points where the answer is "not this stance," and clearly marked nodes where judgment cannot be eliminated and the choice must be handed to values rather than facts. The procedure must survive cases of stance-overlap and must flag where it will systematically misfire—especially when legal requirements force us to designate exactly one primary stance for the record even when the evidence supports multiple. Apply it to three cases: (1) a defendant with a well-documented orbitofrontal lesion who planned a fraud over six weeks, where the board may resist labeling this intentional; (2) a defendant whose dopaminergic medication induced an acute impulse-control failure leading to an immediate, unplanned theft; and (3) a defendant with no identifiable neurological anomaly but severe trauma history who committed an assault during a disputed dissociative episode that clinicians disagree whether to classify as a design-level dysfunction or a rupture in intentional continuity. State what the procedure cannot do. Present the deliverable as a stepwise checklist with brief worked applications, in no more than 550 words.
