I'm working through a conceptual tension that sits between indirect normative theories and models of idealized practical reasoning, and I want to understand where the logic leads without relying on named authorities or textual exegesis. The core thesis is that moral reasons never govern discrete bodily movements or particular choices directly; they govern only the standing policies, deliberative dispositions, or environmental structures that make those choices available or unavailable. To make the problem concrete, imagine an idealized agent—call her D—who is fully rational, enjoys perfect transparency about her own psychology, and can redesign either her internal decision-procedures or her external social environment, though always at some non-trivial cost. D recognizes that a specific action, such as breaking a promise, would violate a valid norm. She can respond in two structurally different ways: she can adopt a direct self-forbidding rule, or she can restructure her environment and her habits so that the occasion for breaking the promise never materializes and the action becomes, for her, practically unthinkable.

If normativity is genuinely indirect, then the proper object of moral evaluation is not the action that does or does not occur, but the deliberative architecture D endorses. This raises a cluster of implications that I find difficult to disentangle. Does the view erase any morally significant difference between autonomous self-constraint and environmental pre-emption, given that both operate by shaping the space of available options rather than by guiding choice within it? Furthermore, if D can treat her own normative framework as an object of rational optimization—choosing among policies on the basis of expected normative outcomes—does she risk a kind of reflexive instability, in which the framework that justifies the choice is itself undermined by the choice? I am asking for an analysis of the most important conceptual implications of this position, particularly regarding the status of autonomy, the coherence of self-optimizing normativity, and any paradoxes that arise when an idealized agent engineers her own practical possibilities. I do not need bibliographic pointers, historical attributions, or citations; I am looking for careful reasoning about the structure of the idea itself.
