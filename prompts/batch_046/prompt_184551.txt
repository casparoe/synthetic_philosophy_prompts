Ok, I've been going in circles on Pettit's republican liberty with a friend and I think he's pushed me into a reductio but I can't spot the leak.

(1) Republican freedom is just absence of domination: no one can arbitary interfere with your choices.
(2) Domination is arbitrary power—power that isn't forced to track your interests, even if it's never actually used.
(3) But think about opaque algorithmic governance: a black-box risk model sets your rent, healthcare tier, etc. There's no human with a whim, but the weights shift and your options change overnight with no explanation.
(4) By (1), if that power isn't answerable to you specifically, you're dominated.
(5) So republican liberty should condemn all opaque automated decision systems.
(6) Pettit allows modern bureaucracy because rules constrain officials and you can contest them. The algorithm is rule-bound (it runs fixed code) and there's an appeals process.
(7) So Pettit has to say algorithms don't dominate—which means he's smuggled a personal-agent requirement into (2) that isn't in (1).
(8) If we drop that hidden agent requirement, then supply and demand, or any structural constraint, counts as domination too. Republican liberty proves way too much and collapses.
(9) If we keep it, republican liberty is basically negative liberty plus some procedural checks, which makes it redundant.

Which step do I reject? I'd really like to keep (1), (4), and (5) if possible. Also please say what'd actually change your mind—like is the flaw an equivocation between personal and structural domination?—and flag where your reasoning is shakiest, since I might be missing something obvious.
