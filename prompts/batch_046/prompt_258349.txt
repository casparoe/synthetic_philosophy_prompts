Quine’s naturalism denies ethics any special epistemological status: moral judgments belong to the web of belief, are revisable in principle, and are embedded in an ontological scheme that is itself relative to the language adopted. At the same time, Quine insists that the normative/descriptive boundary is practical rather than principled, and he applies the indeterminacy of translation and the criterion of ontological commitment across all domains of discourse. Write the complete specification of desiderata that any theory of moral values must satisfy in order to count as genuinely Quinean—naturalized, ontologically parsimonious, and continuous with empirical science—without collapsing into either eliminativism or an unacknowledged moral realism. For each desideratum, state the precise philosophical motivation (whether it derives from the rejection of the analytic/synthetic distinction, holistic belief revision, ontological relativity, or the underdetermination of translation manuals) and flag which pairs appear jointly unsatisfiable within a strictly naturalized framework. In particular, the specification must address: (a) how to preserve the phenomenology of deep moral disagreement when normative vocabulary is radically translation-indeterminate; (b) how to account for the felt authority of moral claims despite their lacking any distinct epistemological privilege; (c) whether values can carry ontological commitment without violating the principle that to be is to be the value of a bound variable; and (d) whether cross-cultural moral convergence can be explained without positing universal moral facts. Identify which desiderata are non-negotiable given Quine’s broader philosophy and which are negotiable pragmatic adjustments. Conclude by singling out the one desideratum that does the most work in separating a Quinean account from both expressivist and robust realist competitors, and briefly assess whether Quine’s own scattered treatments of moral values—as culturally inherited postulates subject to pragmatic revision—meet it. Present the response in a formal, structured manner, with numbered desiderata and concise justifications.
