I teach an upper-level philosophy-of-science seminar with 14 students, meeting once for 75 minutes. Design a structured role-play that makes participants feel the practical paralysis of Kuhnian incommensurability before anyone names the concept. Invent a fictional scenario—the "Lake Meridian thermal-bloom arbitration"—and embed a procedural mechanism I will call "epistemic escrow": each subgroup must commit its evaluation criteria to writing before seeing any shared evidence, then must reach unanimous agreement on one of three interventions within 25 minutes.

Split the class into two groups of seven. Give Group A the "Process-Ontology Protocol": reality consists of dynamic relations; measurement must track temporal change; an explanation succeeds only if it predicts state-transition probabilities with temporal fidelity. Give Group B the "Substance-Ontology Protocol": reality consists of stable entities bearing intrinsic properties; measurement must isolate invariant characteristics; an explanation succeeds only if it identifies an underlying causal mechanism. Present both groups with identical raw observational data—temperature gradients, biomass fluctuations, chemical traces—described without theoretical interpretation. Announce that a funding board requires a unanimous recommendation among chemical intervention, biological seeding, or non-intervention, and that each group may advocate only using the criteria locked in its escrow. The facilitator should enforce that translating one group's criterion into the other framework does not count as addressing it; they must engage the criterion on its own terms.

After the decision period—whether they deadlock, reach agreement only by one group silently abandoning its criteria, or produce a hollow compromise—run a 20-minute debrief. Map specific interactions onto incommensurability: the moment both groups verified the same numerical values but disputed whether those values satisfied "explanation"; the moment one group's successful prediction was treated by the other as a spurious correlation; the hidden cost of consensus. Only here name Kuhn and incommensurability. Be explicit about what the activity cannot reproduce: the historical accumulation of anomalies over decades, the sociological and career investment in a paradigm, and the slow generational turnover that makes shifts possible. Also be frank that the escrow rule artificially hardens boundaries—real scientists often develop hybrid vocabularies—so the constraint is pedagogically useful precisely because it exaggerates the rigidity that in practice is softened.

The deliverable should include exact group instructions, the timeline, the rules of engagement, the debrief map with predictable failure modes and how to handle them, and an honest note on the artificiality of the escrow mechanism. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific publications; do not ask students to research outside the room. The idea should emerge from the rules, roles, and stakes, not from any lecture delivered before the exercise begins.
