Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme with two refrains—about whether generational labels such as Boomers, Millennials, and Gen Z denote real kinds, interactional kinds in Hacking's sense, or mere demographic artifacts. Use the first refrain to state a precise birth-year boundary, and the second refrain to state the essential behavioral trait attributed to that boundary. Across the five tercets, allow the refrains to shift slightly—changing a verb tense or substituting one trait for another—so that the form itself enacts the feedback loop by which naming a cohort retroactively produces the shared properties it claims merely to describe; inject the niche framing that these are "chronocentric interactional kinds" rather than natural kinds or pure fictions. In the third tercet, seriously engage the strongest eliminativist objection: that age, period, and cohort effects are inextricably confounded, so no essential boundary can be drawn. The closing quatrain should leave the tension open—neither collapsing into essentialism nor dissolving into eliminativism—so that the rhyme scheme's inevitable return feels like an unanswered question rather than a resolution. The tone should be conceptually rigorous rather than satirical.
