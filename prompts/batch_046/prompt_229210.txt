Ever since Fritz Fischer publised his bombshell in 1961 about Germany deliberately pushing for war and having annexationist aims, the arguement over who 'caused' WWI has felt stuck in mud. The Fischer side keeps pointing to the September Program and the chancellor's willingness to risk a two-front war; the structuralist side keeps pointing to railway mobilization schedules and alliance commitments that made escalation automatic once Russia moved. After sixty years of document wars, neither camp moves much. Meanwhile philosophers of collective action keep saying you can't attribute an intention to 'Germany' unless there's a shared mental state, which there clearly wasn't, so the whole framing might be a category error. But the historians mostly ignore that and keep talking about German responsability as if it's obvious what it means.

I'm wondering if the stall isn't just about evidence. Maybe it's that the two sides are actually answering different questions: one about causal origins (who pulled the trigger), one about moral attribution (who should carry the stain). Or maybe there's a hidden disagreement about standards -- Fischer people treat bureaucratic memos as revealing collective intent, while structuralists treat the same memos as post-hoc rationalizations of forces nobody controlled. There's also this weird gap nobody fills: what if we stop treating the state as an agent with one will, and instead think of it as a kind of 'responsibility sponge' that soaked up obligations through its institutional design -- so blame attaches not to a national decision but to a failure of built-in brakes, like the lack of any civilian veto over military mobilization? Does that idea even help, or is it just noise? Is the deadlock verbal (around 'blame'), substantive (about what documents prove), or a mismatch of disciplines? And if the diagnosis is that they're answering different questions, what would progress look like -- splitting the debate formally, running a model of distributed agency, or accepting that no single answer exists?
