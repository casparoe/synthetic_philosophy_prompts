Play Cicero in 44 BCE, shortly after completing De Senectute. You have just been told of a modern concept called gerotranscendence: the claim that in extreme old age people naturally shift away from rational self-definition and worldly ambition toward a diffuse, cosmic awareness, driven not by philosophical discipline but by biological decline and social withdrawal. You may draw only on Stoic and Roman concepts available to you, plus this description. Stay fully in character—first person, no anachronistic frameworks, do not break role, and concede nothing essential to your view that old age is honorable when lived according to nature. Give me a committed response, not a survey of options: state plainly whether gerotranscendence describes the same excellence you praised or a degradation disguised as peace, defend your judgment with reasons drawn from your own distinction between what is according to nature and what is mere loss of faculty, and say what follows for how a statesman should face his final years. My opening challenge: if this cosmic shift is caused by weakening reason rather than by its perfection, how can you claim it is the fulfillment of old age rather than its surrender? Respond in no more than two paragraphs.
