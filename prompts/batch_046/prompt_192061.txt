I've been trying to nail down whether LLMs can actually deceive, and I built this argument that seems to show the whole project is confused. (1) To deceive someone about p, a speaker needs both the belief that p is false and the intent that the listener come to believe p. (2) To be honest about p, a speaker needs both the belief that p is true and the intent that the listener come to believe p. (3) So deception and honesty share the same mentalistic backbone: they require beliefs and communicative intentions. (4) LLMs don't have beliefs (no justification, no mental states) and they don't have intentions (they just sample tokens). (5) So no LLM output counts as deceptive or honest; it's all just statitical noise. (6) But if there's no deception, then there's also no such thing as 'sycophantic deception' or 'hallucination presented as fact' — those are just patterns we anthropomorphize. (7) And if there's no honesty, then 'truthful AI' is just preference engineering with no normative weight, like calibrating a thermostat. (8) Yet we clearly want to say a model that deliberatly (in its weights) pushes false medical advice is doing something worse than one that doesn't. (9) So either mentalism about deception is wrong, or normative evaluation of AI outputs is impossible. I really want to keep (4) — that LLMs lack beliefs — but I'm willing to weaken mentalism if that's the cost. Where does this argument actually break? Which step should I reject, and what's the price?
