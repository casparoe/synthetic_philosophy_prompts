Exam question: Consider Quine's scenario of radical translation, but apply it to the following constructed case. A field linguist studies an isolated community whose language includes the term "tavik," uttered whenever a distributed software process logs an error. The community's observable practices are compatible with two ontologies: Manual A translates "tavik" as referring to a persistent server-process that endures across time; Manual B translates it as referring to a discrete, immutable event-slice with no cross-temporal identity. The community possesses no explicit metaphysical vocabulary to adjudicate between these interpretations. Explain (a) why Quine would regard the choice between Manual A and Manual B as underdetermined by all possible behavioral evidence; (b) how analytic hypotheses enter into the construction of either manual; and (c) whether this underdetermination implies that there is no fact of the matter about what "tavik" refers to, or merely that the linguist cannot discover the fact. Student answer: In Quine's original "gavagai" thought experiment, a field linguist observes a native utter "gavagai" as a rabbit runs past and must choose among translations—"rabbit," "undetached rabbit part," or "rabbit-stage"—that are all observationally equivalent. The modified case with "tavik" reproduces this structure exactly: Manual A treats the reference as a persistent server-process continuing across log entries, while Manual B treats it as a discrete, immutable event-slice with no enduring identity. Because every observable utterance and behavioral disposition is identical under both interpretations, no amount of empirical testing can privilege one manual over the other.

What distinguishes this situation from ordinary lexical ambiguity—such as the English word "bank"—is that the community possesses no explicit metaphysical vocabulary for distinguishing enduring entities from temporal slices. Quine addresses this gap by introducing analytic hypotheses: background commitments regarding logical form, identity conditions, and ontological simplicity that are built into the translation manual itself. Once these hypotheses are applied, the principle of simplicity clearly favors Manual A, since it avoids multiplying entities across time. Consequently, Manual B is eliminated, and the reference of "tavik" is fixed: it denotes the persistent process. The residual indeterminacy is therefore merely epistemic; with a sufficiently comprehensive framework of analytic hypotheses, the correct ontology is recoverable. This framework also illuminates how large language models must resolve token-reference alignment across different database ontologies, facing an analogous choice between persistent-object and event-slice embeddings.
