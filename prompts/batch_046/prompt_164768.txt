Examination question: Consider a physician who tells a specific patient with a rare, previously unobserved mutation: This antibiotic has an eighty percent propensity of curing your infection, though no clinical trial has included your mutation. A frequentist, a subjective Bayesian, and a propensity theorist each offer a different interpretation of what makes this statement true, if it is true. Explain how each account understands the eighty percent figure. Then evaluate whether the propensity account genuinely solves the reference-class problem in this particular case, or whether assigning a precise numerical value without direct frequency data involves a hidden circular appeal to analogical reasoning. Be specific about whether propensities require prior frequency calibration in order to be numerically meaningful.

Student answer:
On the frequentist view, the eighty percent means that in the long run, eighty percent of patients in the appropriate reference class are cured. The reference-class problem is that selecting the class is arbitrary: should we include all bacterial infections, only this strain, or only this genetic profile? Because the mutation has never been observed, there is no actual collective, so the frequentist must say the statement is undefined or must construct a hypothetical collective. Either way, the patient receives no objective single-case answer.

The subjective Bayesian, following Ramsey and de Finetti, interprets the figure as the physician's rational degree of belief, constrained by exchangeability with observed trials. This avoids the reference-class problem because probability attaches to the epistemic agent rather than to the world. Two physicians with identical data but different priors could rationally disagree. The cost is that the eighty percent is psychological rather than physical.

The propensity view, first formulated by von Mises as an extension of collective theory, treats the eighty percent as an objective tendency inherent in the drug-patient interaction itself. Because the propensity belongs to this individual case, it requires no embedding in a collective and therefore escapes the reference-class problem entirely. The numerical value is not read off from statistics but is determined by the causal mechanism: once the biochemical pathway is mapped, the strength of the disposition yields the precise figure. Hence propensities preserve empirical objectivity without circularity, since the mechanism fixes the value prior to any observed frequency. One might object that we cannot verify the propensity without eventually counting outcomes, but this confuses the ontology with its measurement, just as we do not reduce electric charge to the needle-reading of an ammeter.
