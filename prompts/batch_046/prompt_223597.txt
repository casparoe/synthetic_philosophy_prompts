A friend of mine overheard a disagreement between two acquaintances, M and N, about whether the heterogeneity problem genuinely undermines hedonism or only exposes a taxonomy mistake. M argued that once we separate qualitative content from affective posture, every pleasure—sensory, intellectual, or social—shares a single structural feature: an intrinsic, non-instrumental endorsement that M called "refractory closure," by which the experience resists complete substitution with a causal description without leaving experiential residue. From this, M concluded that pleasures are unified enough to serve as a single normative currency. N replied that "refractory closure" is not unique to pleasure—certain pains and deep aesthetic absorptions show it too—so it fails as a differentia. N added that if M retreats to a purely neural or functional account, he abandons the first-person phenomenology that makes hedonism distinctive, and that M's notion of "intrinsic endorsement" is circular unless it smuggles in non-hedonic values like flourishing or authenticity. M then countered that N demands a qualitative commonality no mental-state kind could satisfy, since intentionality guarantees heterogeneity; the real issue is whether pleasures share a normative property, not a qualitative feel, and that N's demand for phenomenological purity is a strawman of contemporary hedonism. N finished by asking whether, if heterogeneity is admitted at the phenomenological level, hedonism can still claim pleasure as the sole good without implicitly ranking pleasures on grounds that are not themselves pleasure-based. Go through their exchange point by point and adjudicate: who is right at each step, where they talk past each other, and whether there is a middle position—perhaps that heterogeneity is real but normatively irrelevant—that neither has considered.
