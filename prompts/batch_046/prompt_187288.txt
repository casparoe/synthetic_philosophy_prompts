Read the film through this lens: does it present the Marine legal code as a Hart-style social institution defined purely by recognized rules of recognition, or does it implicitly adopt Fuller's claim that law must satisfy an internal morality—generality, promulgation, clarity, and non-contradictory rules—to count as law at all?

Does the courtroom scene restore legality purely through procedural enforcement—cross-examination, disclosure of evidence, adherence to trial rules—or does Kaffee's victory depend on an unstated moral premise, such as the absolute wrongness of killing an innocent Marine, that strict legal positivism couldn't derive from the rules themselves?
