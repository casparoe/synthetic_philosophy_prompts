I sit on the Programme Review Committee of the European Institute for Historical Epistemology, which assesses long-running research programmes every five years using criteria adapted from Lakatos’s methodology of scientific research programmes. We must reach a decision on the Symbolic Emergence Programme (SEP), whose hard core maintains that structured symbolic cognition in Homo sapiens arose not from internal representational modules but from recursive feedback between manual tool-use and vocal prosody, mediated by shared attentional frames.

Over the past funding cycle, SEP’s protective belt has absorbed several anomalies—most notably, recent comparative studies showing complex vocal sequencing in bonobos without corresponding tool-use reciprocity. In response, SEP researchers proposed an auxiliary hypothesis they term “inter-modal resonance scaffolding”: vocal sequencing can substitute for manual feedback only when embedded in ritualised group contexts, thereby preserving the hard core. This led to a predicted pattern of ritualised vocal marks in certain archaeological deposits, which preliminary fieldwork has apparently corroborated.

A rival group, the Modular Representation Initiative, contends that SEP is now degenerating: the scaffolding hypothesis is post-hoc, lacks independent mechanistic support, and the apparent archaeological corroboration was anticipated only after the data were known. They request that our committee redirect core funding to their own programme.

The committee is divided. Some members judge the scaffolding shift progressive because it yielded a novel, risky prediction that survived initial testing; others regard it as a classic degenerating problem shift—an ad hoc adjustment that immunises the hard core without expanding empirical content.

Please draft the committee’s decision memo, addressed to the Institute’s Director, recommending either (a) renewal of SEP’s core funding for three years with explicit, testable conditions; (b) a phased redirection of resources to the rival initiative; or (c) suspension pending a defined evaluation milestone. The memo must apply Lakatos’s distinction between progressive and degenerating problem shifts with precision, include an honest and detailed treatment of the strongest consideration supporting the option you reject, and propose a concrete, time-bound course of action that the researchers could actually respond to. Write in formal British English; do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citation list.
