Construct a thought experiment engineered for the debate over scientific verisimilitude versus predictive adequacy. The setting must be realistic—choose an actual scientific domain such as epidemiological modeling or climate attribution, not a toy physics example—and must fit within a single paragraph, with no omniscient observers, no futuristic instrumentation, and no stipulation of outcomes by fiat. You are to design two rival research programs: Program Alpha should dominate all presently available data with a phenomenological model that systematically mischaracterizes the underlying causal architecture; Program Beta should underperform on current data while correctly mapping that architecture, and the scenario must include a narrowly defined, empirically accessible future interval—specify its conditions—during which Beta alone makes accurate predictions if and only if the true mechanism operates. Once the scenario is built, verify that it meets every constraint: report the comparative predictive scores, identify the misrepresented causal structure, define the future interval precisely, and show that the divergence in verdicts is generated by the theories rather than by hidden premises. Then state what a verisimilitude theorist in the Popperian tradition should say about rational preference here, what a constructive empiricist should say, and isolate the exact feature that does the philosophical work. Conclude by explaining how an advocate of Program Alpha could attack the case—particularly by disputing the specification of the future interval—and describe how removing that feature collapses the experiment's force.
