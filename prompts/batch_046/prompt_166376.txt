I am developing an essay that challenges the conventional framing of the procreation asymmetry—the idea that we have stronger moral reasons not to create a suffering being than to create a happy one—by introducing what I call a negative modal status for potential persons. Rather than treating potential people as lacking positive interests that could be satisfied, I propose that they stand in a relation of modal absence: there is no bearer for whom non-existence is a deprivation, but this absence functions not as a neutral baseline but as a standing exclusion of reasons to generate. The essay argues that this reconceptualization dissolves the asymmetry without collapsing into an impersonal axiology, and that it sidesteps the non-identity problem because the relevant reason applies to the act of initiating a life rather than to a counterfactual comparison between two possible worlds. I do not seek bibliographic recommendations, reading lists, or citations; I want conceptual criticism. Specifically: is the distinction between an absence of standing reason to create and a presence of reason not to harm genuinely stable, or does the modal-status move merely rename the asymmetry it claims to dissolve? Does the framework avoid implicit reliance on an impersonal evaluation of states of affairs? And where, if anywhere, does the argument confuse metaphysical modal status with normative weight? Your comments on coherence, hidden premises, and points where the reasoning overreaches would be especially valuable.
