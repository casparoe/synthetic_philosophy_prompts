I'm in a phil of mind seminar and we got onto Schrödinger's Vedanta thing — the idea that conciousness isn't plural, there's just one universal awareness and all these separate minds are like waves on the same ocean. I tried to turn it into an argument and now I'm stuck in it.

Here's the chain: (1) Schrödinger holds, following Vedanta, that consciousness is singular: there's exactly one subject of experience, call it S, and individual minds are just partitioned perspectives on S. (2) The standard psychological-continuity theory of personal identity says X at time t1 is the same person as Y at t2 iff there's appropriate psychological continuity between them. (3) If there's literally only one S, then there's perfect psychological continuity — in fact identity of subject — between me-now and you-now, since we're the same consciousness. (4) So by (2) applied across bodies, I am literally identical to you right now. (5) That can't be right: it collapses self-sacrifice into a weird kind of self-harm, and moral obligations between people disappear since there's only one agent. (6) To save the unity thesis, we might say "I" and "you" work like indexicals — "here" picks out different places but there's still one space. So "I" picks out S-relative-to-body-A and "you" picks out S-relative-to-body-B. (7) But if mental properties are also indexed this way, then "I'm in pain" really means "S-is-in-pain-relative-to-body-A." There's no unindexed fact about whether anyone is in pain. (8) That destroys first-person authority: when I report my pain I should technically specify the body-frame, yet I never do, and my report sounds false if read unindexed. (9) So either reject Schrödinger's unity view, reject psychological continuity, or accept that first-person authority is systematically ambiguous.

I want to keep (1) and (6) if I can — they feel right — but (9) seems like a reductio. Where's the slip? Is there an equivocation between "subject of experience" and "person" between steps? Or is (7) smuggling in something that doesn't follow? I keep circling back to (3) but it looks valid. Tell me which step to give up, why it looked okay, and what each standard move (the indexical defender, the continuity theorist, the Vedanta reader) has to pay. Don't just tell me it's a false dichotomy; name the step.
