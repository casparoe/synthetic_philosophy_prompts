I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and I've been asked to deliver the opening remarks at the closing symposium of our university's year-long public series, "Logic, Physics, and the Nature of Time". The room will be split roughly evenly: on one side, mathematicians and logicians who know Gödel's work well and will catch an inaccurate claim about his cosmological arguments; on the other, local students, family members, and non-specialists who are here for the public engagement component. I need a speech of no more than 600 words, written in British English, that takes Gödel's rotating-universe solution to Einstein's field equations and his associated philosophical argument—that temporal passage is not objectively real in such a cosmos—as its genuine structural spine, not as decorative reference.

Because the idea must shape what I can and cannot say, please use the possibility of closed timelike curves in a rotating universe to frame my reflections on the lecture series itself: just as Gödel's metric permits a trajectory to loop back upon its own past, our discussions have repeatedly returned to old questions armed with new formalisms. At the same time, I want to draw an analogy from my own field—non-well-founded recursive structures, where computation can depend on itself without ever reaching a base case—to illuminate why a temporal loop is conceptually unsettling, without implying that Gödel was concerned with computer science. This analogy should help the non-experts grasp the logical shape of the argument, while the experts should recognise that I am using it as illustration rather than historical claim.

Please separate clearly what is settled—Gödel did derive a rotating-universe metric containing closed timelike curves, and he did argue that this undermines the objectivity of time—from what remains genuinely contested, including whether such universes exist, whether our own universe rotates in any relevant sense, and whether his philosophical conclusion about the unreality of temporal passage actually follows. Do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus view. Finally, include a brief concluding note of no more than two sentences indicating where the speech takes liberties with Gödel's actual reasoning or leans on a contested reading, so that the specialist half of the audience understands I am aware of the simplification. The tone should be respectful and suitable for delivery aloud in about four to five minutes; please do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
