I'm preparing a podcast interview with a philosopher who defends what she calls regional lexical threshold negative utilitarianism. On her view, suffering and well-being are not globally commensurable—there is no common cardinal scale that permits weighing any quantity of pleasure against an instance of pain. Within a phenomenally bounded register, however—an experiential domain unified by a specific mode of attentional saturation—suffering retains lexical priority: no accumulation of pleasure inside that register can outweigh even a minimal suffering in the same register. Across registers, aggregation is blocked, so suffering in register A cannot be traded against pleasure in register B. Draft six to eight interview questions that avoid asking her to restate the position. Instead, press the internal tension: if pain and pleasure lack a shared metric, what justifies lexical priority rather than mere ordinal dominance? How are register boundaries fixed without circular appeal to the suffering they are meant to contain? And how does she answer the charge that the register structure is an ad hoc rescue from the standard commensurability objection? For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to offer—especially any appeal to the structural asymmetry between negative and positive affect. Conclude with one question about what empirical or conceptual result could lead her to abandon the regional framing entirely. Do not include biographical questions, do not request citations or reading lists, and avoid naming other philosophers unless required for the logical point.
