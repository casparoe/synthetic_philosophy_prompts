I've been following the self-deception literature - Sartre on bad faith, Davidson's divided mind, all the modern cognitive-science takes - and it feels like it's been running in circles since the 70s. One camp insists it's conceptually incoherent: you can't genuinely believe p and also believe not-p with full awareness, so whatever happens in 'self-deception' must be motivated reasoning, compartmentalization, or behavioral avoidance, not real contradictory belief. The other camp points to clear occurences where people seek confirming evidence and avoid disconfirming evidence in ways that look like they know and don't know the same thing at once. But then the first camp just redefines 'belief' to exclude those cases, and we're back at square one. Is this actually a substantive disagreement about mental architecture, a verbal dispute over what counts as belief, or are we answering two different questions under one heading? What's your diagnosis, and is there anything - a distinction, an experiment, whatever - that could actually break the deadlock?
