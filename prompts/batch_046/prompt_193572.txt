Please argue that the White Horse Dialogue (Bai Ma Lun) should be interpreted as advancing a thesis about sortal-relative identity rather than a fallacious denial of class membership. Specifically, defend the claim that when the text asserts "a white horse is not a horse," it is not violating the law of non-contradictory predication but is instead pointing out that the individuation conditions indexed to the kind-term "horse" differ from those indexed to "white horse," so that the two sortals carry distinct criteria of counting and persistence. Address the standard objection that this reading illegitimately imports modern philosophy-of-language machinery into the School of Names by showing how the dialogue's own distinction between jian (hardness or substantial form) and bai (whiteness or color)—the physical constitution that answers to "horse" versus the differentiating property that answers to "white"—supports the sortal-relativity interpretation without requiring anachronistic technical vocabulary. Conclude by clarifying the philosophical stakes: if the interpretation holds, the dialogue is not a sophistical puzzle about language but an early recognition that kind-terms are not always substitutable salva veritate across descriptive contexts in which different properties serve as the relevant principle of unity.
