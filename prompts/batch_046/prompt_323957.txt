Consider a hypothetical present-day institution: a private consortium granted long-term, chartered monopoly rights over transnational data and cloud-computing infrastructure in several formerly colonized regions, operating with sovereign exemptions from local taxation, labor regulation, and environmental oversight, and publicly justified as a vehicle for rapid industrial development and lower consumer prices through unprecedented economies of scale. Drawing on Adam Smith's known arguments about mercantile monopoly, the costs of colonial administration, the true sources of national wealth, and the relationship between division of labor and political liberty, predict how he would evaluate this arrangement. Specifically, address whether he would interpret the promised efficiency gains as genuine common benefits or as concealed private rents; how he might assess the fiscal and military burdens that host governments bear in accommodating such extraterritorial privileges; and whether his broader skepticism about empire—particularly his view that colonies tend to impoverish rather than enrich the metropole—would carry over to this form of digital chartered extraction. Present your reasoning in American English, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
