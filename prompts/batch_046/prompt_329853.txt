Consider Einstein's actual death in 1955, well before Bell published his result and long before anyone treated entanglement correlations as a usable computational resource. If he had lived to see both developments—Bell's demonstration that local hidden-variable theories cannot reproduce quantum predictions, the experiments that closed the major loopholes, and the later quantum-information perspective that frames nonlocal correlations as a primitive to be exploited rather than a paradox to be dissolved—how would he have revised, or refused to revise, the realist commitments in his EPR argument?

The standard reconstruction says he would have rejected quantum mechanics as incomplete because locality fails. But Bell's result does not uniquely blame locality; it can also be read as undermining the assumption that unperformed measurements have definite outcomes, now usually called counterfactual definiteness. Meanwhile, a modern minority argues that locality can be saved only by accepting superdeterminism, in which detector settings and hidden variables are always correlated—a deterministic rescue that Einstein might have recognized as preserving causality, or dismissed as an unacceptable conspiracy that abandons free experimental choice.

Given his insistence on separability and his hostility to probabilistic fundamental laws, which horn would he have taken? Would he have preferred to deny that distant properties have definite values when unmeasured, or would he have embraced the superdeterminist correlation as the necessary price for keeping both locality and determinism intact? And would the fact that quantum computing and cryptography now deliberately harness these very correlations—treating them as resources to be managed rather than anomalies to be eliminated—have made the loss of locality seem less catastrophic to him, or more?

I am not asking for psychological biography. I want a philosophical reconstruction: map the specific commitments in his arguments about completeness and separability onto the modern trilemma among locality, counterfactual definiteness, and measurement independence. If there is a consistent Einsteinian position that survives Bell, describe its structure and its cost. If there is none, say exactly which commitment must be surrendered and why that surrender conflicts with arguments he actually made.
