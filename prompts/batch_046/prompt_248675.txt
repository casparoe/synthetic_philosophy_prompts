I'm writing a feature on the old philosphy-useless-for-science beef — Weinberg called it dead weight, Hawking said it hasn't kept up with discoveries, Feynman mocked philosophers for not contributing anything concrete. Meanwhile philosophers keep pointing out that conventionalism, symmetry reasoning, and quantum interpretation debates clearly involve philosophical moves that shaped physics. But this fight has looped untill the 90s and nobody budges. Is it actually a factual disagreement about whether philosophy ever changed a scientific result, or is it something messier — like scientists and philosophers using "useful" for totally diffrent things, predictive power versus conceptual hygiene? Or are they answering diffrent questions entirely: one about day-to-day lab work, the other about long-term framework shifts? I also want to test a niche split I haven't seen discussed much: philosophy as "pre-science" (untangling concepts before the math works) versus "post-science" (interpreting finished theories). Does that distinction actually map onto why the debate is stuck, or is it just a neat taxonomy that captures nobody's real position? Give me a real diagnosis — verbal, substantive, hidden empirical question, bedrock intuition about what science is? — and tell me what, if anything, could actually break the loop. No citations, no reading lists, I'm writing this without a net.
