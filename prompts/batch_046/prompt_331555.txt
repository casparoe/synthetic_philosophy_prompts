I serve on my university's Animal Research Ethics Board. We must rule on a proposal to extend our vertebrate welfare protocols to invertebrate subjects—specifically, to require analgesia for invasive procedures on decapod crustaceans and to mandate harm-benefit justification for any study using more than 10,000 insects. The philosophical crux is the scale argument: if each insect carries even a minute probability of sentience, the expected aggregate suffering across vast populations may exceed that of a smaller number of clearly sentient vertebrates. One faction treats this as a straightforward precautionary extension of our mandate; the other argues it collapses into epistemic paralysis, since we lack direct access to insect phenomenology, and that applying expected-value reasoning to moral status risks prohibiting routine agricultural and biomedical research—e.g., Drosophila genetics, mealworm nutrition trials—on speculative grounds. Draft the board's decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—giving reasoning researchers could actually respond to, treating the scale argument seriously rather than dismissing it, but also weighing institutional feasibility and the asymmetry between negative and positive duties. Give the strongest consideration for the losing side honestly. Keep the memo to roughly 400 words, with an explicit recommendation in the first paragraph and a bottom line in the last.
