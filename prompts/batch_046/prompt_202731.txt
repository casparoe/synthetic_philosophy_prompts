Write a two-page research proposal for a six-month independent study fellowship. The project addresses why policymakers and analysts persistently misapply historical analogies—specifically the Munich appeasement narrative, the Vietnam quagmire narrative, and the so-called Thucydides trap—despite widespread awareness that history does not simply repeat. The framing idea is that standard explanations treat the failure as either a cognitive bias (availability, anchoring) or a poor causal-model fit, and miss a deeper mechanism: each analogy functions as a narrative compression protocol that collapses a high-dimensional historical causal structure into a low-dimensional moral-emotional arc, with predictable information-loss signatures—systematic overweighting of dramatic turning points and underweighting of structural drift and contingency. The proposal must include: (1) a precise statement of the research question and an argument for why bias-based and causal-model accounts leave this compression mechanism unexamined; (2) the method—conceptual mapping of the compression operations across the three canonical analogies, formal reconstruction of the dimensions that get dropped, and a structured workshop protocol to test whether explicit decompression (reconstructing the dropped dimensions before applying the analogy) reduces analogical overconfidence; (3) a realistic six-month timeline with monthly milestones; (4) a clear definition of success (a taxonomy of compression losses plus workshop evidence that decompression modifies reasoning) and failure (the taxonomy collapses into standard bias categories, or the workshop shows no measurable effect); (5) a scope justification explaining why this is tractable in six months without archival research or statistical historical modeling. Do not include a bibliography, citation-based literature review, or requests for specific authors; refer to positions and arguments conceptually and note that citations will be added separately.
