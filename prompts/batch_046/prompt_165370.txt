The combination problem for panpsychism—how micro-level experiential subjects could compose a single macro-level subject—has been stalled for decades between defenders of irreducible phenomenal-bonding relations and critics who argue that such relations either violate physical causal closure or merely rename emergence. Every familiar move is in play: the bonding camp insists that causation alone cannot yield unified subjectivity, the critics reply that an unexplained relational primitive is emergentism in disguise, and neither side has shifted. My working theory is that the impasse is not about consciousness itself but about competing standards for metaphysical explanation—one side demands mechanistic transparency in composition, the other accepts primitive relations if they solve a specific ontological problem—possibly compounded by the sides answering different questions, one about ontology and one about epistemic access, under the single heading "combination." Is that diagnosis right, or is there a hidden formal or empirical disagreement doing the work? I want a committed answer, not a survey of panpsychist variants: diagnose the disagreement using what each side actually says about phenomenal bonding and causal closure, state definitively whether it is verbal, substantive, or bedrock-intuitive, and tell me whether anything could actually end it—perhaps a formal distinction between aggregative and integrative subject-combination, or a model of nested phenomenal adjacency conditions, or nothing at all. Be explicit about what progress would have to look like given your diagnosis, and take a clear position on whether the stall is permanent.
