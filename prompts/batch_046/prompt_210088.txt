Please explain the conceptual problem of verisimilitude—why formalizing how close a false scientific theory comes to the truth is structurally difficult. Focus on the core tension: measuring proximity by logical content (counting true versus false consequences) rewards theories that say more, so that a false but expansive theory can score higher than a true but modest one; weakening the metric to block this tends to erase meaningful distinctions between rival false theories. Once you have laid out that dilemma, extend it to an analogous but less-discussed setting: two probabilistic forecasting frameworks are both known to be miscalibrated, yet they assign different distributions over future observations. Can one construct a coherent analog of verisimilitude that ranks which framework lies closer to the true data-generating process without reducing the ranking to calibration error alone? What formal properties would such a measure need to preserve, and where exactly does the classical verisimilitude problem re-emerge in this probabilistic framing?
