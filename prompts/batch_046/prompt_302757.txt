I sit on my school's sixth-form restorative justice panel, which meets weekly to review proposed disciplinary sanctions and must produce a recommendation within thirty minutes for staff sign-off. I have been studying Pascal's discussion in the Pensées of justice and force—his claim that justice without force is powerless, force without justice is tyrannical, and that legitimate authority arises only when the two are properly paired. I want this turned into a reusable decision procedure our panel can apply to any sanction proposal: a numbered sequence of tests to run in order, specifying what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, the point at which the procedure stops with a clear rejection, the point at which it must hand over to human judgment because the issue is one of values, and the conditions under which it will misfire. The procedure should distinguish between force that directly enforces an identified justice claim and force that functions as mere display or deterrence, and it should address what happens when the specific wrongdoer is unknown. Apply it to three cases: (i) a three-week suspension and library restoration duty for a student caught vandalising bookshelves; (ii) a collective detention for an entire year group after an unidentified student set off a false fire alarm; and (iii) permanent exclusion for a first-time minor plagiarism offence. Ensure one of these produces a recommendation I will find unwelcome, and conclude by stating clearly what the procedure is incapable of resolving. Please respond in British English.
