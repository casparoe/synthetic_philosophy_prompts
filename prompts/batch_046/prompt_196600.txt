However, her selection of the assay depended on her false intermediate belief that compound X is structurally analogous to compound Z, a belief that was causally necessary: had she not held that false analogy, she would never have initiated the test. The disanalogy is, in addition, epistemically inaccessible within her laboratory framework—no evidence available to her could reveal the error, and in every nearby world where she conducts the assay she retains the false analogy.
