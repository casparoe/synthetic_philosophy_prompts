I run a graduate workshop on practical rationality and decision theory for philosophy students — about 16 participants, one 75-minute session, with no assigned reading required in advance though basic game-theoretic vocabulary can be assumed. Design an in-class simulation that makes the group feel the pull of escalation of commitment through a structured variant of the dollar auction, but inject the following twist: instead of bidding with money, participants accumulate “credibility tokens” that determine speaking order and voting weight in a subsequent collective deliberation over a shared resource pool, so that sunk costs become socially visible and withdrawal carries a public status cost. The rules must be arranged so that participants run into the problem themselves — each incremental bid should feel locally defensible given prior investment and the visible token stakes, while the collective outcome becomes globally catastrophic — and only in the debrief should the formal structure (all-pay dynamics, failure of common knowledge of rationality, sunk-cost reasoning) be named and mapped onto what just happened.

Please include: precise rules and a payoff scheme using only paper, tokens, and a printed resource sheet; a seating or role arrangement; a phased timeline (opening, bidding rounds, resolution, deliberation); the debrief that connects specific moments in the activity to parts of the idea, including the distinction between prospective and retrospective rationality; an explicit, honest statement of which feature of commitment — for instance, the normative question of whether a rational agent should honor her prior intentions when circumstances change — the game cannot reproduce and must be supplied by the facilitator; and a frank warning about what will predictably go wrong the first time the activity is run (for example, one participant refusing to enter the bidding chain and thereby breaking the escalation dynamic for others) together with a concrete strategy for handling it without undermining the experience. The design should depend on no external citations or texts, and the participants should come away seeing why escalation can be individually rational at each step even when everyone recognizes the collective disaster in advance.
