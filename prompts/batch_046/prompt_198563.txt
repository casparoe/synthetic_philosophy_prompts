I am a high school student preparing for an interscholastic philosophy debate, and I recently watched an online lecture summarizing David Benatar's antinatalist argument. The speaker articulated what he called the asymmetry of pleasure and pain: the presence of suffering in an existing life counts as a harm with a definite victim, whereas the absence of pleasure in a life that is never actualized counts as no harm at all, since there is no subject who is deprived. From this, the speaker derived the familiar conclusion that bringing people into existence is morally questionable, but he added a further claim that the asymmetry also operates as an axiological ranking principle across possible worlds. Specifically, he argued that a world containing fewer people is, other things equal, morally preferable to a world containing more people, because each additional person represents a potential locus of harm without a corresponding deprivation in the smaller world. He insisted this was not an independent thesis imported from population ethics but the direct logical extension of Benatar's own asymmetry. I am unsure whether this interpretation is faithful. Could you evaluate whether this summary is fair? In particular, does Benatar's asymmetry genuinely entail a comparative ranking of worlds by population size, or does it remain confined to individual-level procreative decisions? And is the speaker correct to present the asymmetry as a structural principle of moral evaluation rather than a localized claim about the ethics of creation?
