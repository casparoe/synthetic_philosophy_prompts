As a doctoral student in metaphysics with a secondary focus in logic, I have been working through the standard literature on Thomson's lamp and related supertasks, and I find that most treatments collapse two quite different problems into one. The familiar puzzle asks what state the lamp is in at the limit point after an infinite sequence of toggles, but I think there is an underappreciated distinction between the ontological question—whether there is a determinate fact about the lamp's state at t=1 at all—and the dynamical question—whether a causal process can be genuinely complete without a terminating condition that fixes its outcome. Depending on which of these one treats as primary, the implications for continuous change, temporal parts, and causal explanation diverge significantly, yet the literature rarely separates them cleanly.

Given that I have roughly the scope of a dissertation—perhaps three years—to contribute something meaningful in this area, I want to think systematically about which open lines of inquiry actually deserve priority. I am particularly interested in whether the most upstream question is the logical coherence of "completed" infinite sequences within physical time, or whether it is instead the metaphysical status of properties instantiated only at limit points. From there, I want to understand how resolving either issue would affect adjacent debates: for instance, whether it forces a commitment to dense temporal structure, whether it undermines discrete-stage reconstructions of continuous processes, and whether it has any bearing on how we understand non-well-founded causal chains outside the lamp scenario.

Could you help me construct a defended agenda? Specifically, identify the two or three open problems in the supertask domain that are most worth a philosopher's sustained attention—ordered not merely by intrinsic interest, but by tractability for someone with formal logic training but no advanced physics background, by how many other philosophical questions depend on their resolution, and by what kind of result would genuinely advance the field rather than simply produce another variant of the lamp paradox. For each priority, explain what makes it upstream; for the ones you deprioritize—perhaps those that turn on empirical claims about physical infinities or that require technical machinery from analysis—explain why they are less suitable for philosophical work alone. I am not looking for a bibliography or a survey of positions, but for a reasoning-driven ranking I can use to orient my own research.
