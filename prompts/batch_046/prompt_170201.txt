In a seminar on moral psychology I recently found myself advancing a position that sits somewhat outside the standard taxonomy of responses to the situationist critique, and I should like your judgment rendered not as a qualitative gloss but as an explicit, decomposed credence. The view is this: rather than treating virtues as global character traits that ought to yield high cross-situational behavioral consistency, we should understand them as ecologically calibrated dispositions whose expression is stake-sensitive—meaning their activation is modulated not solely by stable internal structures but by the agent’s assessment of local ecological validity, the reliability of available cues, and the expected normative cost of misapplication. On this model, a courageous agent does not display uniform courageous behavior across all contexts; she displays behavior that tracks courageous appraisal with high fidelity in high-stakes, information-rich environments and appropriately suppresses or redirects that appraisal when ecological conditions render it unreliable or dangerous. My question is whether this reconception saves virtue ethics in a genuinely normative sense: what is your credence, expressed as a specific number between 0 and 1, that this stake-sensitive, ecologically situated conception allows virtue ethics to survive the situationist challenge without collapsing into a context-sensitive variant of rule-consequentialism or act-utilitarianism disguised in aretaic vocabulary?

Please decompose your reasoning into the following terms, assigning a rough probability and a brief justification to each: (a) the likelihood that the empirical situationist literature can be interpreted as supporting a trait-interactionist or ecological-calibration reading rather than a strong, eliminativist situationism; (b) the probability that practical wisdom (phronesis) can be reconceived as the meta-capacity governing these stake-sensitive calibrations rather than as a globally integrative trait, without losing its distinctive normative authority; (c) the chance that this reconception retains sufficient normative distinctiveness—particularly with respect to agent-centered evaluation, the non-instrumental value of the virtues, and the unity of character—to avoid being absorbed into consequentialist or deontological frameworks; and (d) any other factor you take to be decisive in the combination. Show explicitly how these terms combine into your overall figure, identify which component is least certain and what specific kind of evidence—empirical, conceptual, or phenomenological—would most significantly shift your credence, and finally indicate whether a colleague holding a credence near 0.2 or near 0.8 is more likely to be disputing term (a) versus term (c).
