Write a dialogue between a philospher who buys Dennett's idea that the self is just a center of narrative gravity — basically a useful fiction we spin to predict behavior — and another philospher who's skeptical but not dismissive. The skeptic thinks the metaphor hides a bias toward unity: she proposes that some minds have what she calls "narrative orbitals," multiple partial story-centers that stay in tension and never collapse into one "I". They should actually argue, not talk past each other. The Dennettian needs to defend why agency requires a singular gravity well, and the orbital theorist should push back with concrete cases — maybe a dissociative memoir writer who produces one coherent autobiography even though there's clearly no single continuous narrator inside. Keep it like a coffe-shop argument, not a seminar, and don't worry if there's a typo or two.
