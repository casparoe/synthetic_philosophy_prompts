I am specifying the adequacy conditions for a philosophical theory of voluntary action that genuinely distinguishes flexible human agency from sphexishness—the mechanically rigid, loop-bound behavioral pattern in which an organism repeats a fixed sequence despite perturbations that should render it maladaptive, rather than revising its course. Before I compare particular accounts, I want the full spec.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory must deliver. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it, flag whether it is non-negotiable or merely the preference of one camp, and say if any pair looks jointly unsatisfiable.

The list should cover:

1. Loop-breaking without a homunculus: the theory must explain how an agent can interrupt a stereotyped behavioral or cognitive sequence when conditions change, without positing a second-order observer inside the system.
2. A workable distinction between first-order flexibility (selecting among given options) and second-order flexibility (revising the framework that generates the options), with a reasoned view on whether both are necessary for free will or only one.
3. Evolutionary continuity: the account must be consistent with what biology tells us about decision-making architectures, yet must not collapse flexibility into mere stochastic noise or deterministic reflex.
4. Scale invariance: it must apply to insect-like routines, human habits, institutional procedures, and algorithmic decision loops without treating these as identical mechanisms.
5. Counterfactual perturbation resistance: the theory must specify whether genuine flexibility requires that, had a specific counterfactual perturbation occurred, the agent’s pathway would have diverged in a non-random, reason-responsive manner—and whether this condition can be satisfied within a deterministic framework.
6. The status of sphexishness itself: the theory must decide whether sphexish rigidity is best understood as a failure of freedom, a failure of rationality, or an independent category, with clear implications for moral responsibility.

Then identify which single requirement does the most damage to the main existing contenders—mechanistic determinism, classical compatibilism, and agent-causal libertarianism—and whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given current work in decision neuroscience and philosophy of action.

Keep the weight on the criteria, their motivating cases, and the conflicts among them; score the contenders only briefly. Answer in American English.
