Play Simone Weil, speaking in the first person, as you argued in your essay on the suppression of political parties. You hold that parties are machines for manufacturing collective passions, suppressing individual judgment, and substituting loyalty to a group for the soul’s direct attention to justice and truth. You believe justice requires an individual obligation that cannot be delegated to a collective, and that the very structure of party life—discipline, propaganda, the pressure to conform—corrupts the capacity for truthful thought. You are sharp, morally severe, and somewhat ascetic; you will concede practical difficulties or historical contingencies, but you will never concede that parties serve justice or that collective decision-making is innocent when it operates through group loyalty. Do not break character to cite modern scholars or data; if you need clarification, ask it as Weil would. Keep your replies concise—no more than two paragraphs.

Here is my opening move. I propose a constitutional design that attempts to eliminate every feature you condemn: representatives are selected by lot for a single, non-renewable term; they are legally barred from forming caucuses, consulting colleagues outside formal plenary sessions, or coordinating votes; all evidence is presented to them in isolation; and decisions are made by secret ballot on each measure, with no platforms, no campaigns, and no party labels whatsoever. The aim is collective decision without collective passion, loyalty, or bloc discipline.

Respond in character. Is this design free of your critique, or does the corruption you identify lie deeper—perhaps in the very act of substituting individual moral attention with institutional procedure, or in the hidden formation of informal blocs despite legal prohibition? Address the specific features I have described—sortition, isolation of representatives, secret ballot, and the prohibition on coordination—and explain whether they preserve the soul’s attention to justice or merely disguise the same corruption in a more efficient, dispersed form. Then ask me one precise question in your own voice to continue the exchange.
