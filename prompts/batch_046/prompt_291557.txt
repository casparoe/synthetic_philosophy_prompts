You are the senior ethics reviewer for a regional public-health authority that receives two to three behavioral-intervention proposals each week—defaults in wellness programs, cafeteria redesigns, framing changes in consent forms, and social-norm messaging campaigns. You have forty-five minutes per proposal, your review must be readable and signable by a non-philosopher administrator, and your decision must be defensible in writing if challenged. Write a reusable audit procedure—ordered questions, sufficient-answer conditions, explicit stop/no points, and marked handoff nodes where judgment is irreducible—that translates the normative distinction between acceptable choice architecture and unacceptable paternalism into practice.

The procedure must incorporate the following conceptual constraint, which is not the standard textbook framing: distinguish execution-nudges, which operate on an agent’s existing preference order and exploit biases only in the implementation of already-held aims, from formation-nudges, which alter the mechanisms by which preferences are formed, revised, or weighted over time. Treat formation-nudges as triggering a stricter justification threshold, but do not automatically prohibit them; instead, ask whether the altered mechanism is one that the affected citizen could endorse upon calm reflection without having been causally influenced by the intervention itself.

Your deliverable must include:
1. The procedure itself—no more than eight primary steps—stating for each: the precise question; what counts as a sufficient factual answer to proceed; what answer produces an immediate “no”; and whether the step involves only empirical verification or an irreducible value judgment.
2. A judgment-call registry identifying the three steps most vulnerable to reasonable disagreement, naming the philosophical tension underlying each, and stating what kind of evidence (if any) could reduce but not eliminate the dispute.
3. Two worked applications, each running the full procedure:
   a. A proposal to place high-sugar beverages behind opaque cabinet doors in the staff cafeteria while leaving water and unsweetened options visible at eye level, with no price changes and no removal of choice.
   b. A proposal to automatically enroll all new employees in an escalating retirement-savings plan, with the default contribution rate resetting downward only if the employee completes a forty-five-minute financial-literacy module whose framing—designed by behavioral economists—systematically emphasizes long-term security vocabulary over present-consumption vocabulary.
   One of these must yield a result your team will find counterintuitive; explain exactly where the procedure forces that result and where a reasonable reviewer might resist without breaking the procedure’s logic.

Constraints: The procedure must be executable by staff without philosophy training; it must not require empirical studies your team does not possess; it must be explicit about which steps are procedural formalizations of irreducible normative conflicts; and it must not request bibliographies, citations, or references to named theorists. Do not quote any philosopher verbatim; paraphrase all theoretical commitments. Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot do—identify at least one class of proposal for which the audit will consistently produce indeterminate results and must defer to democratic or deliberative resolution.
