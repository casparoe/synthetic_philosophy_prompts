I've been trying to work out my stance on psychiatric taxonomy for a workshop on Hacking-style looping and I'm not sure my commitments actually cohere. Here they are, more or less:

(1) I accept that mental-illness categories are interactive kinds — they loop back and reshape the people they classify.
(2) I think the looping is strongest exactly where the label carries identity-forming weight (autism, BPD) and weakest where it's purely symptomatic (say, specific phobia).
(3) I think the DSM-5 move to dimensional ratings for personality disorders was meant partly to weaken looping by killing off sharp thresholds.
(4) But I also think dimenisonal models actually amplify looping, because subtreshold self-identification becomes easier and online communities form around milder trait clusters.
(5) I hold that heavy looping is a strike against a category being a natural kind in psychiatry.
(6) Still, I think autism spectrum disorder loops heavily via self-diagnosis culture and yet tracks something closer to a real neurodevelopmental kind than personality disorders do.
(7) And I think our classifacatory practices should ultimately be judged by therapeautic and predictive utility, not by whether they carve nature at the joints.

Can you audit this? I want to know which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what's the least costly revision for each real conflict.
