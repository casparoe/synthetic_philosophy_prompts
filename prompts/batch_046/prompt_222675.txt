I'm planning a single fifty-minute session for first-year philosophy students who have just studied Kant and utilitarianism and assume ethics is either universal rules or aggregate welfare. I want to teach care ethics as a distinct normative orientation, not an emotional supplement, using progressive dementia care in an under-resourced facility as the carrying case rather than the mother-infant dyad. Plan the explanatory arc: which conceptual distinction must be secured first so that care sounds like ethics rather than psychology; whether the dementia example works and what it must include and exclude to carry the reasoning; the misconceptions they will form in sequence — likely that care is mere sentiment, that it rejects justice, that it applies only to private life — and the exact point in the arc where each should be intercepted; one end-of-session check that distinguishes grasping the normative structure from expressing sympathy; and what should be explicitly left out at this level — for instance, institutional extensions of the framework or the developmental-psychology framing — with acknowledgment that those exist.
