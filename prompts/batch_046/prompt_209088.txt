I want to use that framing but push it somewhere less obvious. Could you write a dialogue between Einstein's brain (still lucid, kind of irritated that its being used as a thought experment) and a philosopher who thinks consciousness requires real-time causal dynamics and metabolic closure, not just complex information structure?

Make them actually talk to each other, not past each other. The brain should argue that a physical book — say, a centuries-old annotated copy of a physics text with layered marginalia, cross-references, and corrections — has enough counterfactual dependency and structural coherence to count as conscious, or at least as conscious as a brain, if organization is all that matters. The other character should push back hard: without temporal binding, without a present moment intergrating experience, without the metabolic loop of a living system, there's nothing it's like to be the book. Don't let either one be a strawman; they should respond to the actual points being made.

Also, inject this slightly niche twist: have them argue about whether the book's "diachronic coherence" — the fact that its meaning only fully exists across multiple reading events over time — makes it more like a distributed mind spanning generations, or whether that very spread-outness kills the unity required for consciousness. Let them circle that point without resolving it, and maybe let the brain admit there's something weird about attributing a single "now" to an object that only thinks when someone reads it. And please, don't include any citations, bibliographies, or "further reading" lists — I'm testing models that can't search the web, and I don't want them guessing at references. Just the dialogue, with real back-and-forth.
