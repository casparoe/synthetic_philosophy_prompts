I am a retired experimental physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, has taken up philosophy of science with considerable seriousness. At present I am advising on the architectural restoration of a disused astronomical observatory. The exterior shell and every mounted instrument have been surveyed with exacting precision, yet there remains one interior chamber—sealed and emptied for decades—that contains no apparatus, no inscriptions, and no detectable physical fields of any kind. In constructing a geometric model of the building, I find that I can apply a smooth, continuous deformation that rearranges every point within that empty chamber while leaving all measurements outside, including those precisely at the boundary, entirely unchanged. The structural equations governing the layout therefore fail to determine the internal metric of the chamber. One colleague, an engineer, insists that this reflects only a redundancy in my description: the chamber’s points are physically real, but my coordinate assignment is arbitrary, just as rotating a map does not alter the terrain it represents. Another colleague, with philosophical training, argues that if no observable feature—not even any relation to the surrounding matter—distinguishes one internal arrangement from another, then the points within the chamber possess no physical individuality whatsoever; the region is substantively featureless, and any worry about determinism here is a pseudo-problem created by treating mathematical space as a container with intrinsic parts. I am unsure how to adjudicate between these views. Should I conclude that empty spatial regions lack substantive reality, or rather that our theoretical framework erroneously assigns independent existence to points that are defined solely by the fields and material structures that inhabit them? Moreover, does the distinction between an active transformation of the physical situation itself and a mere passive relabeling of coordinates genuinely resolve the difficulty, or does it only restate the same puzzle in different language? I should be grateful for a careful analysis that addresses the underlying philosophical structure rather than offering generic counsel about renovation projects.
