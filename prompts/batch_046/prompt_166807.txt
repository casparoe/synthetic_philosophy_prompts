I'm in a philosophy seminar at my high school—about 16 students, aged 16 to 18, with one 60-minute session. Please design a classroom simulation that makes us feel the force of intergenerational climate justice, specifically the puzzle that different emissions paths would bring different specific people into existence, which seems to block the claim that present choices harm identifiable future individuals. The mechanics should include: (1) an initial round where half the class receives "Generation A" identity cards and half receives "Generation B" cards, with each card specifying a personal stake (e.g., a family role or health vulnerability); (2) a collective vote on a high-emission or low-emission policy; (3) a second round in which only the cards compatible with the chosen path remain valid, so the room's composition literally changes, and those who remain must negotiate with those who replaced the others. Include small symbolic stakes rather than real money, rules that prevent the facilitator from explaining the paradox before it hits, a debrief that maps each round onto the philosophical problem, predictions of what will go wrong the first time, and an honest note about which feature of intergenerational harm the simulation cannot capture. We have no assigned reading; the tension should come from the cards and the vote.
