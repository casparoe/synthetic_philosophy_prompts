Design a 75-minute classroom simulation for a graduate seminar in distributive justice or institutional ethics (about 15 students) that lets participants feel why safe Pareto improvements—institutional changes guaranteed ex post not to worsen anyone's position—can collapse to an empty set once information about risk is hidden. Structure: four "units" with unequal initial resources. Over three rounds, units propose cooperative restructurings (shared infrastructure, pooled funding) that benefit some and leave others nominally unchanged. Each student holds a private vulnerability token (drawn randomly, kept hidden) that determines whether they actually suffer hidden downside risk from a given restructuring. A proposal passes only by unanimous consent, and consent should be withheld if any member faces hidden risk. Round one reveals all tokens; round two keeps tokens hidden but retains the unanimous-consent rule; round three switches to majority rule with tokens still hidden. Track outcomes with a simple point-payoff scheme: institutional welfare points for approved cooperative projects, personal penalty points if hidden risk materializes for that student. Provide the full rules, a minute-by-minute timeline, materials needed, and the payoff structure. Then give a debrief that maps round one onto the informational feasibility of safe improvements, round two onto their disappearance under hidden risk, and round three onto the trade-off between safety and feasibility. Include an honest prediction of what will go wrong the first time (students will likely try to signal vulnerabilities covertly, breaking the hidden-information condition, or they will conflate hidden risk with strategic free-riding) and identify the specific feature of the philosophical idea—the normative force of ex post guaranteed non-worsening as distinct from ex ante probabilistic expectation—that the simulation cannot reproduce without direct facilitator framing. Finally, ask the response to specify which pattern of student behavior during the activity would show they missed the conceptual point and treated the dynamics instead as a standard prisoner's dilemma or coordination failure, and to say what observation or argument would change its confidence that the game actually succeeds in making the force of safe Pareto improvement felt rather than merely narrated.
