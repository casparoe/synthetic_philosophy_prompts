If historians have shown that the 'Middle Ages', the 'Renaissance', and the 'Scientific Revolution' are largely retrospective constructions—imposed by later scholars rather than recognized by contemporaries—does rejecting these macro-periods also force us to dismiss the more localized discontinuities they are said to contain? For instance, if there is no unified Scientific Revolution, can one still defend the clustering of particular practices—say, mathematized natural philosophy and probabilistic reasoning—between roughly 1550 and 1700 without reintroducing the very container one has dissolved? Or is the choice necessarily binary: either keep the invented periodization and its internal patterns, or abandon both and treat any apparent clustering as an artifact of selection bias?
