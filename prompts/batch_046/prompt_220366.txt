I am a PhD student in philosophy of mind and formal epistemology, and I have been working through Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach with a small seminar group. We keep circling a problem that standard interpretations seem to gloss over. The familiar Gödelian analogy in the book moves from syntactic self-reference—Gödel sentences that assert their own unprovability within formal arithmetic—to the emergence of conscious self-awareness through strange loops. Most commentaries treat this either as an illuminating metaphor or as a direct structural parallel, but neither approach explains why formal systems necessarily face an epistemic closure (they cannot establish their own consistency, assuming consistency) while human self-modeling appears capable of continuous revision without analogous collapse.

For a 6,000-word term paper in my cognitive-architecture seminar, I want to propose an investigation into whether there exists a formally specifiable intermediate category—tentatively called semantic recursion without syntactic anchoring—that distinguishes the self-reference available to formal arithmetic from that available to embodied cognitive systems. The central question should be stated with precision: does Hofstadter's strange loop reveal a genuine continuity between Gödelian incompleteness and self-aware cognition, or does it conceal a representational asymmetry—specifically, the absence in formal systems of a mechanism that can revise its own representational medium without altering the underlying syntax?

The proposal should explain why existing answers remain open. Defenders of the analogy typically appeal to hierarchical levels of representation, while critics dismiss it as a category error, yet neither camp offers a criterion for when self-reference becomes strange in Hofstadter's sense rather than merely circular. My approach would construct a minimal comparative model between Quine-style diagonalization and a dynamic, resource-bounded self-model that updates its own ontology, relying only on concepts standard in discussions of Gödel numbering, strange loops, and reflective equilibrium. The document must specify what a positive result would entail—a demarcation criterion that survives counterexamples from logic and cognitive science—what failure would entail—showing that no such criterion can be formulated without trivializing the analogy or importing undefended empirical claims about consciousness—and provide an honest assessment of tractability within one semester, given that the aim is not to solve the hard problem but to map the logical geography of the analogy precisely. Please separate what is settled—such as the technical structure of Gödel's first and second incompleteness theorems and the basic architecture of Hofstadter's strange-loop argument—from what is genuinely contested, including whether representational hierarchy requires syntactic fixity and whether self-modeling systems must be vulnerable to analogues of Gödelian undecidability. Do not present any contested reading of Hofstadter's intent or of the philosophical implications of incompleteness as if it were the consensus position.
