Please argue that Pascal's well-known claim in the Pensées—that justice without force is powerless and force without justice is tyrannical—depends on an unstated binary between legitimate authority and coercive power that modern institutional theory undermines. Specifically, defend the view that once we distinguish between direct coercion and what can be called structural inevitability—the manner in which bureaucratic and legal architectures shape action without explicit threat—justice is not an external value that must be appended to force, but is already embedded in the design of enforcement mechanisms. From this, argue that Pascal's proposed synthesis is not a resolution but a category mistake that obscures how administrative rationality operates, and consider whether this transforms his insight into a critique of modern governance rather than rendering it obsolete. Your argument should address potential objections from contractualist and realist traditions of political philosophy, and should not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
