Compose a villanelle on Russell’s paradox that avoids the familiar barber analogy and instead engages directly with the logical form of naive comprehension. Let the first refrain articulate the unrestricted principle that every well-formed property determines a set, and let the second refrain articulate the contradiction that the set R of all sets not containing themselves both must and must not contain itself. Through the five tercets, trace the movement from the intuitive appeal of universal collection, through the shock of self-reference, to the hierarchical resolution—whether by type stratification or by restricted comprehension—that restores consistency only by surrendering the promise of an absolute universal set. In the closing quatrain, interweave both refrains so that the poem concludes with the tension between logical safety and lost intuitive freedom left deliberately open, not resolved. The form must observe the villanelle’s rhyme scheme and refrain structure precisely; the philosophical content should be accurate enough that a logician recognises the argument, not merely its popular outline. Write the answer in British English.
