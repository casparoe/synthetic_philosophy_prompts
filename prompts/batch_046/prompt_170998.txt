I am writing my doctoral thesis on epistemic regress, and I have become stuck on what seems to be a structural symmetry. Coherentism permits circular justification, infinitism permits an infinite linear chain, and foundationalism terminates the regress in basic beliefs. Yet if one distinguishes the logical order of justification from the temporal order of belief-formation, it appears that even basic beliefs must be acquired through prior psychological processes—perception, testimony, or inference—which are themselves in need of justification. Has the foundationalist not therefore simply relocated the regress from the normative space of reasons into the causal genesis of belief? If so, then the disagreement among the three positions concerns only the architecture of belief-systems, whereas the genuine problem is genetic, and none of them addresses it. Where exactly does this reasoning fail, and does the distinction between propositional and doxastic justification rescue the foundationalist from the objection?
