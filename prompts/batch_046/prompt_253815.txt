I am constructing a taxonomy for an advanced seminar on the Anthropocene that must work for readers who cannot look up sources during the exercise. Please build a decision-tree classification instrument—rendered in text with clear nodes and branches—that sorts positions on whether the Anthropocene should be formalized as a geological epoch, when its boundary should be placed, and who holds legitimate authority to declare it.

Start at the root with a concrete judgment, not a label: “Does a globally detectable, human-caused stratigraphic signal justify recognizing a new epoch distinct from the Holocene?” From there, branch first by the criterion that determines the start date. One path should ask whether only a globally synchronous, abrupt signal counts (pointing to the mid-twentieth-century radionuclide/plastics spike). Another should ask whether long-term cumulative transformation is sufficient (pointing to the late-eighteenth-century steam-engine and methane rise). A third should ask whether a colonial-ecological rupture is the decisive marker (pointing to the circa-1610 Orbis exchange signal and its associated biotic and sedimentary shifts). A fourth path should lead to the rejection of a new epoch in favor of extending the Holocene with sub-divisions or event-stratigraphy.

For every path that accepts a boundary, split again by the authority question: “Should the declaration rest solely with the International Commission on Stratigraphy, with an interdisciplinary body that includes historians and Earth-system scientists, or with affected frontline and indigenous communities whose territories register the signal first?”

Structure the tree so that the logical space of answer-combinations appears before the named positions. After mapping the branches, place the actual positions at the leaves—e.g., “ICS formalists,” “Great Accelerationists,” “Orbis proponents,” “Holocene-extensionists,” “pluralist co-determination”—and mark any unoccupied combination as “vacant,” diagnosing in one sentence whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Specifically flag any pair of positions that share the same branch answers but diverge only on a non-stratigraphic issue—such as democratic versus scientific legitimacy—so the tree does not falsely separate them.

Every node must be phrased as a concrete claim or case judgment that a respondent could answer without knowing the literature’s vocabulary; do not ask “Are you an Orbis supporter?” but rather “Would you treat the 1610 Columbian Exchange biotic interchange as a stratigraphically decisive event comparable to the 1950 bomb spike?” The instrument must contain exactly eight leaves, cover the three boundary candidates and the three authority models mentioned above, and explicitly note which two positions the tree cannot cleanly disentangle. Write the answer in American English.
