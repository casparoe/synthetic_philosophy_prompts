I'm in a philosophy of biology seminar working through Hofstadter's typogenetics and the claimed isomorphism between DNA translation and Gödel numbering. I tried extending the analogy to minimal synthetic genomes and ended up with an argument that seems to show JCVI-syn3A should be unable to replicate stably. Here it is:

(1) Gödel's first incompleteness theorem applies to any consistent formal system F rich enough to represent primitive recursive arithmetic: F can construct a sentence G_F that asserts its own unprovability in F, and if F is consistent, G_F is true but unprovable within F.

(2) Typogenetics treats DNA as precisely such a formal system: nucleotides are symbols, the genetic code is an interpretation rule mapping codons to amino acids, and ribosomes are the interpreters that derive proteins—including the ribosomes and polymerases themselves—from symbol strings.

(3) A minimal genome like JCVI-syn3A encodes only the genes essential to build its own replication machinery; in other words, it embeds a near-complete self-description inside the very system it defines.

(4) So, by the isomorphism in (2), any genome that fully describes its own interpreter should be able to construct a biological Gödel sentence: a gene sequence that encodes a protein which, if expressed, disables the replication machinery that expressed it.

(5) If that gene were expressed, the cell could not replicate, making the genome biologically inconsistent with its own survival; if it existed but was permanently suppressed or inaccessible, then the genome would not truly contain its own complete description, since part of its formal content lies outside its interpreter's reach.

(6) Yet JCVI-syn3A replicates stably with 493 genes that are either expressed or genuinely absent—there is no hidden, permanently silenced self-negating instruction.

(7) Therefore either minimal synthetic genomes violate the conditions for formal self-reference, which undermines the DNA-Gödel isomorphism, or they ought to be replication-incompetent, which they are not.

I want to preserve the isomorphism because the course material treats it as genuinely illuminating about self-reference in biology, but I also don't want to deny that synthetic minimal cells work. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost? In particular, is the error in treating DNA as a system rich enough for Gödel numbering, in assuming that "complete self-description" applies to a genome, or in sliding from formal unprovability to biological dysfunction?
