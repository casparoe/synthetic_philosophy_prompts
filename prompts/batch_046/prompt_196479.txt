Rank the following four epistemic frameworks according to how well they resolve what I will call predictive pluralism conflicts—situations in collective forecasting tournaments where multiple ontologically incompatible models yield statistically indistinguishable out-of-sample accuracy and no decisive empirical tie-breaker exists. The frameworks are: (i) standard Bayesian model averaging; (ii) Jain anekāntavāda interpreted through syādvāda and nayavāda; (iii) strict Popperian falsificationism applied to model selection; and (iv) pragmatic epistemic pluralism of the kind defended in contemporary philosophy of science. For each framework, assign a suitability score between 0 and 1 and justify it in one or two sentences, focusing specifically on whether the approach can accommodate simultaneous endorsement of contradictory predictive models without collapsing into either arbitrary relativism or an unjustified monism.
