I'm writing my diss on social ontology and I've gotten kind of obsessed with whether "Gen Z" or "Millennial" picks out anything real, or if it's all just marketing bullshit with a sociology degree. Here's the position I'm flirting with: generational labels are failed kind-candidates. They don't have homeostatic mechanisms (no causal feedback loops that stabilize membership conditions), they fail the APC identification problem (you can't seperate age, period, and cohort effects cleanly), and they lack the modal robustness we demand of social kinds—there's no counterfactual stability to being a Millennial if you were born in 1995 but grew up in rural Vietnam vs Brooklyn.

But I need to stress-test this. What would have to actually be true for this anti-realist view to be wrong? I'm not asking for "well some people identify with the label"—that's weak. I want the strong defeaters: what kind of empirical finding, conceptual argument, or ontological framework would force me to concede that Boomers/Gen Z etc. are real kinds (natural, social, or interactive) rather than arbitrary chronological bins? Would it take showing that cohort membership predicts behavior across wildly different contexts better than class or geography? Or that there's an interactive kind loop (a la Hacking) where being labeled Gen Z actually causally shapes the properties used to define the group? Or maybe some formal result showing APC effects are seperable after all?

Specifically: distinguish between evidence that'd just be awkward for my view (like marketers using the terms successfully) vs. evidence that'd actually be decisive. Say what defenders of my own anti-realist position would themselves concede counts against it, not just what critics say. And be honest—are there live escape routes where I can keep the skepticism but just add qualifications? Or is this view well-insulated in a way that should worry me? I want to know if I'm being stubborn or if there's genuinely no there there.
