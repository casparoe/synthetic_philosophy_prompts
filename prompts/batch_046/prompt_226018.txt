So I retired from physics a few years back and lately I've been trying to read philosophy of science, mostly on my own since I don't have the grad school background. I got hooked on Maxwell's demon and Landauer's principle, and I built this little argument that now seems to prove way too much — like there's a cosmic budget for forgetting. I feel like I'm sliding between two meanings of "information" but I can't quite name where the slip happens. Here it is:

(1) The demon sorts molecules only because it keeps a record; erase the record and you pay kT ln 2 per bit, which saves the second law.
(2) So in any physical system, storing then losing information has an exact thermodyanmic price.
(3) When I observe a particle and then forget it, my brain is storing then losing information.
(4) By (2), every forgotten observation costs at least kT ln 2 in dissipated heat.
(5) Scale up: conscious minds forget thousands of bits every second, so consciousness is constantly draining a huge entropic tax.
(6) The universe's total entropy budget is finite.
(7) Therefore there's a hard cosmological ceiling on the total bits ever forgotten by all minds.
(8) Which means either consciousness is astronomically rarer than it looks, or it must eventually stop once the budget is used up.
(9) Both feel absurd, so one step is wrong. I want to keep (1) and (3) if I can — they feel like the bridge between physics and mind I'm actually interested in. Which premise do I reject, and what does giving it up cost me? Is the mistake an equivocation between computer bits and neural states, or is there a hidden modal slide somewhere?
