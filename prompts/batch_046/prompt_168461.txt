My verdicts on who counts as having a mind, and I want the principle behind them if there is one. A toddler crying after falling over - definately conscious, no argument needed. My cat stalking a laser pointer - I'm pretty sure there's somthing it feels like to be her, though I can't prove it. A chatbot writing a heartbreak poem - feels like pure simulation, no matter how convincing the text is. Someone with total locked-in syndrome who can only blink - full inner life, obvious. But here's the tension: imagine a silicon system with perfect functional isomorphism to human grief, same internal state transitions and outputs, zero neurons. I want to deny it real suffering, yet I can't justify that without just saying "not biological." An octopus that learns to open jars and shifts skin color - I lean yes, but I'm shakier than with the cat. And what about a hive of bees collectively "deciding" to relocate - is there one mind spread across them? I feel I should say no, but if functional organization is what matters I'm stuck. So is my principle about substrate, about first-person access, about behavioural flexibility, or about somthing else like "cognitive transparency"? Which of my gut calls has to go to make the account clean?
