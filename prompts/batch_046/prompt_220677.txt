Audience is mostly analytic philosophers of collective intentionality who think "national guilt" is either metaphor or bad metaphysics, plus two historians of postwar Germany who worry this excuses individuals by diffusing blame onto an abstraction. Prep my Q&A sheet: the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased like each camp would phrase them, with what I should concede (probably that collective intentionality is hard to maintain across generations), what I must hold (that institutional continuity is a real social fact, not a reification), and the weakest point in my argument — likely that I haven't given clear conditions for when an institution counts as "radically reconstructed" — that I need to defend rather than hope they miss. Also include the objection I'm hoping nobody asks, probably that my view lets modern citizens claim victimhood instead of inheriting responsibility, with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is shaky. Separate settled stuff (Jaspers's fourfold distinction is standard; collective intentionality debates are live) from genuinely contested claims, and don't present my structural-guilt thesis as if it's the consensus. Wheather you think it's convincing or not, just map the battlefield.
