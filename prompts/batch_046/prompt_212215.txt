As a retired experimental biologist who spent decades measuring physiological responses and is now trying to make rigorous sense of the philosophy of mind, I find myself stuck on the problem of pain’s badness in light of clinical conditions like pain asymbolia. In these cases, patients appear to register nociceptive input—localizing it precisely, describing its intensity, quality, and temporal profile—yet report that it does not hurt, that there is no unpleasantness or motivational pull whatsoever. I want to understand what any adequate philosophical theory of why pain is bad must actually deliver. Could you lay out the full desiderata? Specifically, I need the requirements list: which clinical and phenomenological cases the theory must accommodate (including standard asymbolia and also a hypothetical inverse case—pure affective suffering with intact unpleasantness and motivational urgency but without any sensory localization, discriminative content, or bodily reference), which neuroscientific findings about nociceptive pathways and affective processing it must remain consistent with, which conceptual distinctions it must preserve (for instance, between sensory-discriminative, affective-motivational, and cognitive-evaluative dimensions), and which of these are genuinely non-negotiable versus which are merely preferences of particular camps (representationalists, motivational theorists, or eliminativists about affect). For each requirement, provide the motivating case or consideration behind it.

I am particularly interested in whether some subset of these desiderata turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable—perhaps the demand that pain is essentially unpleasant conflicts with the demand that nociception can occur without unpleasantness, or the demand for essential bodily reference conflicts with the conceivability of pure affective suffering—so that the list itself reveals a structural tension rather than a mere failure of individual theories. Please keep the weight on the criteria and their underlying rationale rather than on exhaustive scoring, though a brief indication of how the main existing approaches—say, a pure representational view, a motivational-account view, and a hybrid evaluative view—fare against your list would be welcome; a table is acceptable if it clarifies the comparison. Write without bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and do not ask me to look up specific texts: I want the conceptual architecture, not a literature survey.
