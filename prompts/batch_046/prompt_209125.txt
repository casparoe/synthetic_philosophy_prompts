Analyze the dissolution of the Afghan National Security Forces and the Taliban's entry into Kabul in August 2021 through Machiavelli's conceptual pairing of fortuna and virtù, but treat the mapping with analytical discipline rather than loose allegory. First, identify precisely which actors and institutional forces correspond to Machiavelli's categories: in what respects did the Taliban exercise virtù in his specific sense — not generic military effectiveness, but the adaptive capacity to reshape organizational form to circumstance, to maintain cohesion despite material scarcity, and to treat fortune as a river that can be directed through prepared dikes — and in what respects was their advance merely the play of fortuna, riding the contingency of an accelerated American withdrawal and the pre-existing hollowing of Afghan command structures? Second, apply Machiavelli's quantitative claim that fortune controls roughly half of human affairs while the remainder belongs to our virtue: did the Afghan republic fall because its leadership failed to exercise virtù, because it constructed no dikes against an anticipated shift in American commitment, or because the framework itself misdescribes modern state collapse, which depends less on the personal qualities of a prince than on the erosion of collective bureaucratic and military organizational capacity that Machiavelli never theorized? Finally, examine the gendered architecture of Machiavelli's metaphor — fortune figured as a woman who must be beaten and subdued — and ask whether importing it into this case obscures rather than illuminates: does framing the Taliban's success as the mastery of fortune distract attention from the structural, almost administrative failure of a state that had been sustained by external subsidy rather than internal organizational integrity? State clearly whether this episode confirms Machiavelli's view that virtù can master fortune, reveals an anomaly his framework cannot accommodate, or demonstrates that modern institutional failure requires concepts he did not possess.
