This interlocutor has recently extended this framework to an unconventional domain: she contends that artificial agents—particularly large language models deployed in pedagogical or advisory capacities—can come to occupy quasi-roles within human relational networks, not because they possess independent moral status, but because human interlocutors reflexively assign them relational functions that generate genuine normative expectations.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate this view. I want at least one question probing the tension between her performative, negotiable account of roles and the classical Confucian emphasis on the rectification of names (zhengming); at least one on how her position avoids collapsing into a form of liberal contractualism once roles become open to renegotiation; and at least one specifically on the conditions under which an artificial agent’s quasi-role could be revoked without wronging the human users who have come to depend upon it—together with a tailored follow-up for each that anticipates the reply she is most likely to give. The remaining questions should press on other vulnerable points in the literature: for instance, whether her rejection of hierarchical fixity undermines the Confucian critique of egoism, and how she distinguishes her reconstructed view from care-ethical relational autonomy. Please present the questions in British English, pairing each main question with its follow-up so that the interview moves promptly from exposition to sustained philosophical pressure.
