Design a diagnostic taxonomy — in the form of a decision tree with concrete branch nodes and named leaves — that sorts interpretive positions on Schopenhauer’s metaphysics of music, specifically on how melody, harmony, and rhythm stand in relation to the Will and to phenomenal representation. Begin the tree from a single root commitment answerable without knowing any labels: “Music is not merely a representational art like painting or poetry, but expresses something about the inner nature of reality.” From there, branch on four concrete ontological questions that genuinely divide interpretations: (1) Does melody express the Will’s striving immediately, or only through an analogical mapping onto phenomenal movement and tension? (2) Does harmony possess independent metaphysical content as the image of the grades of the Will’s objectification, or is it reducible to structural support for melody? (3) Does rhythm belong to the formal conditions of representation — time, causality, and the principle of sufficient reason — or does it carry volitional content of its own? (4) Is the listener’s experience primarily a cognitive-affective recognition of universal patterns mediated by concepts, or an immediate identification that temporarily suspends individuated consciousness?

Each leaf should combine answers to these nodes and be assigned a position name — for example, “Orthodox Schopenhauerian,” “Formalist reduction,” “Cognitive-affective hybrid,” “Direct-identification radical,” or “unnamed” — together with a one-sentence statement of that position’s most awkward commitment (e.g., the view that reduces harmony to structure must explain why Schopenhauer places harmony in the hierarchy of the arts at all). Build the tree from the logical space of answer-combinations rather than from a list of scholars: include at least one combination that appears unoccupied, and briefly diagnose whether its emptiness stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Where two answer-combinations collapse into a single stance or where a branch fails to separate them — say, between a strong analogical reading and a direct-identification reading that differ only in rhetorical emphasis — flag the inseparable pair explicitly and explain why the split is illusory.

The instrument should let a reader determine which interpretation they actually hold by answering the four concrete questions about melody, harmony, and rhythm, not by selecting a familiar label. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, references to specific editions, or requests that the reader name scholars; avoid verbatim quotations. The deliverable is the tree itself, with branches phrased as answerable commitments, leaves carrying both a name and an awkward-commitment sentence, and explicit notation of any unoccupied or inseparable positions.
