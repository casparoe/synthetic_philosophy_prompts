I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about whether split-brain patients have one mind or two, going by my gut on cases. Full callosotomy: left hand grabs the shovel for snow, right grabs chicken claw, each hemisphere saw different image. I think that's still one person, just with two hands that don't coordinate — like being really distracted, not like two roommates. Partial cut leaving the anterior intact: they can match shapes across visual fields but say everything feels weirdly jointed and wrong. Still one mind for me, just glitchy cross-talk. Slow degenerative callosal loss over years: they adapt, conflicts fade, they act unified. I think they never split; they just grew used to running mostly on one hemisphere's input. Sudden complete cut after stroke: they report the left arm has its own will and feel alienated from it. Here I think there genuinely are two streams of consciousness, at least for a while. But then there's this hypothetical artificial bridge that only transmits emotional valence between hemispheres, not semantic content — the left knows the right is upset but can't access why, and they act in parallel with shared mood but separate reasons. I keep flip-flopping: is that one mind with a weird emotional link, or two minds that happen to feel the same? Is there a principle that captures all five verdicts, or do I have to drop one to keep it simple? What does the best version predict for the case I haven't listed — the same bridge turned on gradualy over months instead of suddenly? And be honest about which of my gut calls it just can't save.
