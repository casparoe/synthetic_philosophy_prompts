I am drafting a novel in which a philosopher-king must decide whether belief in our simulated status ought to diminish his zeal for the deep future. Compare two conceptual frameworks for this tension: first, Bostrom's simulation argument interpreted as an epistemic humility thesis that weakens the evidential weight of far-future predictions; second, the 'ancestor-simulation asymmetry'—the claim that if we inhabit a simulation, our creators are likelier to be near-future agents running backward-looking historical reconstructions than forward-looking cosmic engineers, thereby inverting standard longtermist priority orderings. Which framework more thoroughly undermines far-future fanaticism, and at what precise point does each still permit a modified longtermist commitment?
