I've been trying to work through recent applications of predictive processing and the free energy principle, particularly the way they are used to account for behaviors that seem, on the face of it, to maximize rather than minimize prediction error. The examples are familiar enough: a person who repeatedly seeks out distressing news they already anticipate, who engages with arguments they know will upset them, or who checks repeatedly for messages they are almost certain are not there. In each case the agent appears to be actively pursuing surprise, contradiction, or anxiety—exactly the kind of high-error state the framework says organisms work to avoid.

Yet the theoretical response I keep seeing takes a remarkably consistent form. Rather than treating these behaviors as counterexamples, the theorist introduces a deeper hierarchical level: the agent's generative model is said to include an epistemic prior that assigns value to information gain, or a meta-level belief that predicts high uncertainty in a specific domain, with the result that encountering the apparently unwanted surprise is itself predicted and therefore still reduces free energy globally. Every new counterexample seems to be absorbable by adjusting the hierarchy of priors rather than by revising the core mechanism. I am trying to determine whether this specific dialectical pattern—resolving an apparent failure of error-minimization by postulating a model that predicts the failure itself—has an established name in philosophy of science or in the critical literature on predictive processing. Is it simply an auxiliary hypothesis? Is it closer to what Lakatos called a protective belt? Or does it merit its own designation—perhaps something like hierarchical immunization? I am not looking for a bare label; I want a precise carving of the phenomenon, including what distinguishes it from ordinary model refinement, the conditions under which it becomes ad hoc, and the boundary cases that separate legitimate hierarchical explanation from post-hoc theoretical rescue. If no established term captures it, I would welcome a coined definition together with explicit edge cases.
