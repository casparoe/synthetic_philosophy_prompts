I'm a senior ethics-compliance analyst for an international development NGO, and every quarter my small team—three reviewers with legal and social-science backgrounds but no specialized philosophical training—must evaluate petitions asking the organization to extend moral standing, protective auditing, or participatory procedures to entities that fall outside our current operational framework. Recent petitions have concerned farmed decapod crustaceans in our supply chain, a river-basin ecosystem impacted by our infrastructure projects, and a large language model that conducts initial beneficiary-intake interviews. Because our director requires a signed recommendation within forty-five minutes of deliberation, and because we encounter two or three such petitions per quarter, I need a reusable decision procedure rather than a one-off argument: an ordered sequence of questions with explicit conditions for a sufficient answer, clear stop-points where the petition fails, unambiguous flags marking where judgment is unavoidable and must be documented for the director's sign-off, and a small set of analogical test-cases that a non-specialist can run quickly.

The procedure should be grounded in whatever the philosophy of moral progress and the expanding-circle debate actually establishes, but translated into operational terms. Specifically, it must preserve the distinction between genuine boundary-extension—widening the class of entities that merit direct moral consideration—and status-intensification—deepening obligations toward entities already inside the boundary—since conflating the two produces both false positives and hidden regressions. It should include a reflexive symmetry check that applies the proposed principle back to existing human beneficiaries, a non-projection filter that tests whether the claimed interests can be articulated without unavoidable anthropomorphism, and an epistemic-stability gate that asks whether adoption would undermine the coherence of our existing obligations rather than extend them consistently. Please apply the completed procedure to three proposals: (a) extending welfare-audit protocols to decapod crustaceans in supply chains; (b) granting procedural standing to the river-basin ecosystem in project evaluation; and (c) recognizing participatory rights for the intake-model—where this third case should yield an answer that conflicts with my own intuition, so that the procedure demonstrates its independence from the user's preferences. Conclude by stating precisely what categories of petition lie outside the procedure's structural competence, and identify the exact steps where factual determination ends and value commitment begins.
