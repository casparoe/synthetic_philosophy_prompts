I am a retired physicist who has recently begun studying the philosophy of history, and I am constructing a decision tree for an interdisciplinary reading group of scientists and humanists. The tree must sort philosophical accounts of historical change—particularly those within the cyclical and linear traditions represented by Polybius, Ibn Khaldun, Spengler, and Toynbee—according to their logical commitments rather than by authorial name. The root question should distinguish whether recurrence is conceived as exact repetition or as structural analogy across different scales; the next branch should ask whether linear progress is globally denied, locally permitted inside larger cycles, or reconceived as cumulative growth lacking teleological closure. A further division should separate accounts by their unit of analysis—whether the state, the dynasty, or the civilisation—since this selection often determines the cyclical or linear verdict independently of any explicit metaphysical thesis. Each terminal leaf must be labelled with a named position (for example, strict cyclicalism, linear progressivism, spiral history, or degenerative recurrence) or explicitly marked as unoccupied, with a brief diagnosis of whether the vacancy stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or neglect. All diagnostic questions must be answerable without prior knowledge of the authors' standard classifications. Please begin with the tree in nested form, followed immediately by the reasoning behind the branching sequence and the diagnosis of empty leaves; include no preface, no restatement of the request, and no bibliography.
