hey, i was at a phil of science study group last week and two people got stuck arguing about van fraassen's reflection principle -- the idea that your current credence in some claim P should match your expectation of your future credence in P after you've updated on new evidence. (1) Maya said it's obviously wrong in cases of planned learning: she's currently 55% sure it will snow tomorrow, she knows she'll talk to a meteorologist she mostly trusts, and she expects that conversation will shift her to either 80% or 30%, with an average expectation of about 60%. so Reflection demands she be 60% right now, but she thinks being 55% is perfectly rational since she hasn't heard the report yet. (2) Jordan said Maya's mixing up levels: Reflection binds your present first-order credence to the expectation of your future first-order credence, not to the meteorologist's conclusion itself, and if she genuinely expects to be at 60% on average, then being 60% now is exactly the coherent position -- withholding belief until you "hear the report" is just refusing to integrate your current knowledge of your update rule. (3) Maya came back that this makes Reflection empirically empty: whatever update procedure you have, you just bake it into your expectation, match it now, and boom -- the principle can never be violated, so it can't actually constrain or guide real belief revision. (4) Jordan replied that coherence conditions are supposed to be empty in that sense -- the law of non-contradiction doesn't tell you which premises to accept either -- and Reflection's job is specifically to block Dutch-book-style inconsistencies between your present betting dispositions and your anticipated future ones, not to pick your update rule for you.
