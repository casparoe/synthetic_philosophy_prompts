I'm preparing a three-month workshop proposal in philosophy of social science on national character. My guiding suspicion is that debates over whether generalizing about peoples is ever coherent conflate statistical frequency claims with modal-profile claims—descriptions of how members of a group would behave across counterfactual conditions—while also smuggling essentialist commitments through the back door. The specific angle I want funded is whether reconceiving national character solely as a modal profile, tested through a structured comparison of Japanese and American collective-action responses under identical institutional constraints but divergent historical-memory structures, renders the concept both philosophically coherent and ethically defensible without essentialism. Write the proposal as exactly five numbered sections, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. In order, the sections should state: the precise research question; why existing philosophical and empirical treatments leave this specific formulation open; the method for constructing the modal comparison while blocking essentialist leakage; what a successful result and an explicit failure condition would each look like; and a realistic assessment of what can be accomplished in three months.
