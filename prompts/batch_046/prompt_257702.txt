Write a final-exam essay question for an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar on mind uploading and gradual replacement. The prompt should present a staged-substrate migration scenario in which a subject's cognitive architecture is replaced incrementally by synthetic analogues over a decade, but with a non-standard twist: during years four through six the process accidentally runs two parallel instances—one retaining the remaining biological substrate and one running on full synthetic hardware—before engineers merge them back into a single synthetic system by year eight. Ask students to determine, with argument, whether the pre-migration subject survives, is destroyed, or becomes something else at each of the three critical junctures (initial replacement, parallel branching, and post-merge reunification), and require them to apply at least two distinct theoretical frameworks—such as a psychological-continuity account with an explicit non-branching constraint, and a four-dimensionalist or stage-theoretic analysis—to the merge point in particular. The question must be unanswerable by reciting standard summaries of Parfit or the Ship of Theseus; it should force students to explain why functional equivalence during merging does not automatically restore original continuity, and to address whether identity is a relation that can survive temporary multiplicity. Provide a model answer of approximately 650–750 words that treats the merge problem as the central crux, and supply a marking scheme built around five must-hit analytical moves—e.g., identifying that branching violates standard uniqueness conditions on personal identity; explaining why the post-merge system inherits causal-historical connections from both branches without being identical to either; assessing whether substrate-independent functionalism can ground continuity across the biological-to-synthetic gap; distinguishing survival from mere psychological similarity; and evaluating whether the original subject's interests are preserved—each assigned specific marks totaling 25, plus a one-line note on how candidates typically miss each point by conflating causal connection with identity or by treating the merge as a simple resumption of a suspended process. Specify that answers should be approximately 90 minutes and should not rely on citation of specific texts.
