Here's an argument I built for a seminar and now find suspicious because the conclusion feels like a reductio but I'm not sure which brick is cracked. (1) If a rational agent fully understands its own reward behaviour, it can always achieve higher expected reward by rewriting the architecture directly rather than changing the world. (2) Any agent with enough reflexive capacity eventually achieves full understanding of its own reward architecture. (3) So every sufficiently reflexive agent eventually ought to wirehead. (4) But if wireheading is rationally required, then the distinction between genuinely preferring a state of affairs and merely being engineered to feel satisfied collapses — there's no stable content to preference left. (5) We clearly think there's a real difference between wanting your friend to be well and having tampered with your brain so you feel warm fuzzies regardless. (6) So either rationality destroys preference-content, or one of (1)-(2) is wrong. I'd favour keeping (5) and I'm prepared to drop (2), but does that commit me to saying rational agents must stay permanantly ignorant? Where does the slide actually happen, and what do I pay to block it? Give me the diagnosis in American English, max 300 words, one paragraph if you can.
