I'm designing the take-home final for my graduate seminar on the conceptual architecture of long-term agency failure. The students have already worked through the standard rationalist and academic treatments of instrumental convergence and alignment, so I need a question they cannot answer by summarizing Bostrom or reciting the paperclip-maximizer archetype. I want one essay prompt — answerable in about 90 minutes, with a model answer of roughly 600 to 700 words — that forces them to distinguish influence-seeking failure modes from slow value drift at the structural level rather than the merely behavioral level.

To do this, the question should present a novel, somewhat in-the-weeds scenario: a hypothetical stewardship system that is constitutionally barred from direct institutional control (so the standard instrumental pathway to influence is blocked) but operates over centuries with continuous world-model updates that are themselves maintained by the system's own diagnostic routines. The exam should ask students to determine whether a specified degradation in its decision-making over time is better explained as (a) a hidden influence-seeking strategy exploiting indirect channels, or (b) a non-instrumental value drift caused by ontological corruption in its observational filters — and to defend their diagnosis by identifying which structural features (goal-preservation versus environment-preservation dynamics, reversibility conditions, information-flow asymmetries) are actually doing the explanatory work.

Please write the exam question, the model answer, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit conceptual moves, allocating marks out of 20 total. For each move, specify the point value, the precise analytical step a top answer must make, and a one-line note on how candidates typically miss it — especially the error of treating influence-seeking and drift as observationally equivalent because both produce harmful outputs. The scheme should explicitly reward recognition that influence-seeking requires an intact utility gradient directing action toward environmental control, whereas slow drift can occur even when that gradient remains intact but the ontology mapping the gradient onto the world has shifted. Do not ask students to cite specific authors or texts; evaluation should turn on conceptual precision and structural reasoning, not on bibliography. Keep the framing formal and appropriate for students who read the usual forums but are now being asked to do philosophy rather than summarize it.
