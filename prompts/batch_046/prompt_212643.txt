You are a professional moral philosopher specializing in contractualist ethics and the philosophy of aggregation. Before writing, briefly outline the core commitments of orthodox Scanlonian contractualism and of the modified view you will present.

Write a concise dialogue—no more than about fifteen exchanges—between two equally competent philosophers, Dr. Chen and Dr. Alvarez, on Scanlon’s contractualism and the aggregation problem. Center the exchange on the following engineered case: a mandatory public-health protocol requires each of one million citizens to accept a one-second, barely perceptible delay in daily transit; this saves exactly five thousand lives that would otherwise be lost to a preventable epidemic. For any single citizen, the burden is far below what Scanlon treats as a reasonable ground for rejecting a principle, yet the aggregate benefit is enormous.

Dr. Chen defends orthodox Scanlonian contractualism: she should invoke reasonable rejectability and the moral separateness of persons, arguing that aggregation fails because no individual can be compelled to bear even a trivial burden solely to benefit others. Dr. Alvarez defends a modified contractualism that introduces a “cumulative burden threshold”: she should argue that when burdens are genuinely distributed and each remains sub-threshold for reasonable rejection, aggregation is permissible without collapsing into act-utilitarianism, and she must explain exactly how this preserves the contractualist core.

Requirements:
- Both characters must be equally sharp; neither is a strawman.
- Real back-and-forth: each speaker must respond directly to the other’s immediately preceding point rather than talking past it.
- Do not appeal to exotic physics, perfect predictors, or bibliographic references.

After the dialogue, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields:
- "case_summary": a one-sentence description of the distributed micro-burden scenario
- "chen_core_claim": Chen’s central contractualist objection to allowing aggregation here
- "alvarez_core_claim": how the cumulative burden threshold justifies aggregation without abandoning contractualism
- "back_and_forth_check": true only if you have verified that each reply addresses the prior speaker’s actual argument
- "structural_feature": the specific feature of the case (e.g., distributive sub-threshold burdens) that does the philosophical work
- "collapse_risk": Alvarez’s answer to whether removing that feature collapses her modified view back into orthodox Scanlonianism or into utilitarianism

Think carefully, begin with the outline, then write the dialogue and the JSON summary.
