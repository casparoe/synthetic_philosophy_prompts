I am a philosophy professor organizing a colloquium interview with an unnamed defender—let us call him Dr. Vance—of institutional expected-value pluralism: the view that collective decision architectures should maximize expected value across all affected stakeholders, but that incommensurable goods must be mapped onto a single vector space rather than resolved through interpersonal comparison or lexical priority. Vance treats the standard objections to EV fanaticism—probability neglect, divergence under unbounded utility, and the unilateralist's curse in multi-agent settings—not as refutations but as boundary conditions that distinguish individual rationality from institutional rationality. I want eight to ten formal interview questions for this Q&A. They should not invite restatement of the thesis. I require at least one question pressing whether his vector-space aggregation reproduces the sorites paradox at the level of value comparison—specifically, whether there exists a sharp cutoff at which a marginal change in one value dimension ceases to be compensable by another—together with the follow-up he is likely to offer, which distinguishes epistemic imprecision from ontic structure. I also want one question on the unilateralist's curse in institutional design: if each stakeholder body applies EV reasoning independently, what coordination mechanism prevents the collective outcome from being catastrophically worse than any coordinated alternative, plus the follow-up addressing his probable reply that institutional rationality incorporates a second-order coordination rule. Include one question asking what conceptual or empirical result—short of logical contradiction—would cause him to abandon the vector-space framework, with a follow-up anticipating his claim that only a demonstration of globally non-transitive preference structures could do so. The remaining questions should probe the status of tiny-probability astronomical outcomes in long-term governance, whether the framework can accommodate genuine moral uncertainty without collapsing into a single expected-utility function, and where exactly the line between individual and institutional rationality is drawn. For each primary question, provide a brief note on the anticipated reply and the corresponding follow-up. Write in a polished academic register appropriate for a philosophy colloquium.
