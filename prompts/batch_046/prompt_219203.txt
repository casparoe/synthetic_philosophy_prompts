I have been developing a position on mental causation for an advanced philosophy of mind seminar, and I would welcome a rigorous assessment of its vulnerabilities rather than a routine rehearsal of familiar objections. Rather than resolving Kim's causal exclusion argument by denying the causal closure of the physical domain or by embracing emergentism, I propose that mental properties should be reconceived as counterfactual constraint structures—modal profiles that specify the range of counterfactual dependencies a given neural state could bear—rather than as token events competing with their physical realizers for causal efficacy. On this view, physical state P is the token cause of behavior B in the actual world, but mental state M, realized by P, is causally relevant in virtue of instantiating the stable modal architecture that governs which physical transitions persist across nearby possible worlds; mental causation is therefore structural rather than token-level, and it avoids overdetermination because the physical token and the mental structure do not compete for the same causal work but operate at different explanatory registers.

This framing depends on the idea that explanatory relevance can be carried by invariant counterfactual patterns without requiring an independent causal power at the token level, but I am unsure whether it genuinely escapes the exclusion problem or merely relocates it into the metaphysics of modal structure. I am particularly concerned that defenders of causal closure might argue that if all counterfactual stability is already determined by the physical dynamics of P, then attributing causal work to the modal profile amounts to nothing more than redescription, leaving mental properties epiphenomenal in everything but name.

What I need is a precise map of what would genuinely count against this position, sorted by how decisive the objection or finding would be. I want to know whether empirical neuroscience—say, demonstrations that neural counterfactual invariance tracks exclusively physical parameters with no residual modal architecture—would be fatal or merely awkward; whether formal results in the metaphysics of supervenience or causation show that structural efficacy necessarily collapses back into token efficacy or into a disguised form of epiphenomenalism; and whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability if every predictive failure can be absorbed by redefining the relevant constraint structure. Please distinguish evidence that defenders of the exclusion argument themselves ought to concede as damaging from objections that only critics might raise, indicate whether the position makes any distinctive predictions that rival accounts—such as standard non-reductive physicalism or interactionist dualism—do not share, and assess whether its insulation from direct empirical refutation is a theoretical virtue or a defect for a claim about mental causation. If there are unacceptable conceptual commitments—perhaps an inability to account for singular mental causation or an implicit reliance on modal realism—those should be flagged explicitly.
