As a novelist drafting a philosophical antagonist, I have her claim that negative utilitarianism—by assigning negative value to suffering and zero value to pleasure—logically entails what she terms asymmetric extinctionism: that the painless elimination of all conscious life is morally obligatory, not merely permitted, because any world with even trivial suffering is strictly worse than a world with no sentient beings whatsoever. She insists this follows directly from the harm-benefit asymmetry, without any further normative premise about the value of non-existence or the comparability of empty worlds. Is her inference sound, or does negative utilitarianism actually leave open whether the absence of future pleasure counts as a moral loss, and whether extinction genuinely improves the world rather than merely avoiding further harm?
