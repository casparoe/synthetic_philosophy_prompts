Write a satirical parody of a venture-capital pitch deck—approximately 600 words, deadpan corporate voice, as if presented at a fictional “Enlightenment Tech” summit—for a startup named “Franklin Virtues Ltd.” The product digitises Benjamin Franklin’s 13-virtue moral-perfection programme into a subscription-based self-tracking platform, complete with weekly rotation schedules, black-dot ledgers, and “sinlessness” metrics. Through its feature list and pricing tiers, the deck should quietly reveal that quantifying virtue undermines the humility it claims to cultivate. Inject a niche conceptual twist: invent a hidden fourteenth virtue—“Strategic Opaqueness”—that the algorithm rewards users for concealing their tracking data from friends, thereby satirising both Franklin’s original chart-keeping and modern surveillance capitalism’s co-optation of self-improvement. The humour must derive from rigorously extending the actual logic of Franklin’s method (the cyclical practice, the aspiration to faultless conduct, the social visibility of progress) to an absurd commercial conclusion, rather than from anachronistic gags. Write the response in British English.
