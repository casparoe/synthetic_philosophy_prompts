Illusionism about phenomenal consciousness — the view that there isn't any actual what-it's-like quality, just our brains misrepresenting themselves — has been fought over for years and it feels like it's been going in cirlces since Frankish and Dennett started pushing it hard. Is the stall because it's a verbal dispute over the word phenomenal, or are they genuinely disagreeeing about whether introspection gives direct access? Or maybe they're answering different questions: one about the ontology of experience, one about the architecture of self-modeling? And here's a weirder thing I never see addressed: if illusionism is true, is there a difference between the illusion of redness and the illusion that there's a subject having the illusion? Like, could you have phenomenal illusionism without a phenomenal self-illusion, and does that split matter? What kind of result — conceptual, empirical, or formal — would actually break the deadlock, or should we just accept the disagreement is driven by starting intuitons neither side can defend?
