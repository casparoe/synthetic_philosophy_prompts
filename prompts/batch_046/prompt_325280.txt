For a seminar on consciousness and physicalism I have sketched out my position, and I want a consistency audit before I present it. Here is where I have landed:

(1) I am a Russellian-monist panpsychist: I think every fundamental physical entity instantiates an intrinsic phenomenal nature, and that macro-consciousness is constituted by the appropriate structural arrangement of these micro-phenomenal properties.

(2) I think the combination problem is solved, not dissolved, by a primitive “phenomenal adjacency” relation—logically analogous to spatial adjacency but operating in phenomenal space—that binds micro-subjects into a macro-subject whenever they satisfy a specific informational-integration condition. This relation is not reducible to physical relations, though I take it to be lawlike rather than mysterious.

(3) I hold that macro-subjectivity is genuinely real and causally efficacious in its own derivative right—there is something it is like to be me, and this is not a mere narrative fiction or self-model—while still being ontologically dependent on the micro-base in roughly the way a whirlpool depends on water molecules.

(4) I am committed to robust physicalism: every fact, including every phenomenal fact, metaphysically supervenes on the complete physical description of the world, and there are no non-physical substances; I merely deny that physical descriptions can capture the intrinsic phenomenal character of the properties they describe.

(5) I think the boundary problem is decisive for smallist panpsychism: because there is no principled, non-arbitrary threshold for when informational integration is sufficient to trigger phenomenal adjacency, we are forced to conclude that either every physically integrated system (tables, solar systems, etc.) hosts a macro-subject, or consciousness is a holistic property of the universe alone.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but can be reconciled, and what the least costly revision would be for each real tension. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. Be specific about which commitments have motivations that are actually incompatible, not just which ones sound contradictory at first glance. I have not decided which thesis I would abandon if forced, so do not assume I am protecting any one of them. In particular, address whether my acceptance of a non-reducible phenomenal adjacency relation in (2) can survive the supervenience commitment in (4), and whether (2) and (5) can both be maintained without collapsing into cosmopsychism or universal consciousness.
