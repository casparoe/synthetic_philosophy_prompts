Two colleagues of mine—both software engineers with no formal philosophy training—got stuck in an argument about whether large language models actually resolve the symbol grounding problem. (1) The first claimed they do, in effect: token embeddings occupy high-dimensional spaces where geometric relations mirror semantic ones, and the model’s outputs are continuously corrected by human feedback in training and RLHF. For him, meaning just is functional role inside a system that succeeds at its task; direct sensory contact with referents is unnecessary, since even human knowledge of abstract entities like “justice” or “electron” is mediated by language and instruments. (2) The second replied that internal coherence is not causal connection. She alluded to the well-known thought experiment in which a system manipulates symbols by formal rules without understanding them, and argued that scaling this up does not change its nature: an LLM computes correlations across symbols, and there is no causal chain linking the token “fire” to heat or combustion—only to other tokens. Grounding, she said, requires that a symbol’s use can be corrected by direct interaction with what it denotes, not merely by statistical adjacency to related symbols. (3) The first countered that this demands too much. Humans rarely have unmediated access to the referents of their concepts; we depend on telescopes, testimony, and social correction. He proposed replacing “direct causal link” with “functional integration”: can the system deploy the symbol to generate accurate predictions and coordinate successful actions across contexts? By that standard, LLMs are at least partially grounded within their domain—text—just as a physicist is grounded in her instruments rather than in naked perception. (4) The second then introduced what she called a “triangulation constraint”: true grounding requires at least one correction pathway in which the system updates a symbol’s use based on non-symbolic, direct environmental feedback—vision, proprioception, thermal sensation, or physical resistance—that is not itself mediated by further symbols. Humans possess such pathways; LLMs do not. Their updates come only through text, so they remain inside a symbol-to-symbol loop. Enlarging or smoothing the loop, she argued, does not break it.

Please adjudicate the four moves: rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Say specifically which points each colleague gets right, and identify the central issue both are missing—especially whether there is a principled middle ground between pure correlation and direct sensory causation, or whether the dispute ultimately collapses into a disagreement about what “grounding” ought to mean rather than about the actual capacities of these models.
