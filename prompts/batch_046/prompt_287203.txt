I am not a philosopher by training, but I have been turning over the old story about Death in Damascus—the servant who sees Death in the marketplace, flees to Samarra, and learns that Death had expected him there all along. In a discussion about free will, someone dismissed my question about whether the servant could have simply stayed in Damascus by saying, “There is an appointment in Samarra; that is the point of the story.” I want to understand precisely what claim that slogan is being made to carry. Could you lay out, as a menu rather than a single interpretation, the distinct theses it could express? I have in mind readings that range from the trivial—that everyone dies eventually—to stronger ones: logical fatalism about the inevitability of specific events regardless of action; causal determinism in which the flight is just one link in a chain; and a self-fulfilling or counterfactual reading in which the very act of fleeing creates the outcome in Samarra. For each reading, please indicate whether it is tautological, defensible, false, or ambiguous, and specifically which ones would actually support dismissing my question about whether staying put was a live option for the servant. I am not looking for citations or a bibliography; I need a conceptual map I can use to see what work the slogan was actually doing in that conversation.
