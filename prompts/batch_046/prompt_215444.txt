everyone in my circles keeps saying echo chambers work just like acoustic ones — sound hits a hard wall and bounces back at the same angle, so if youre standing in the right spot you only hear your own voice amplified. they use it to argue that the problem is structural: you dont need malicious censors, just smooth reflective surfaces and a source in the middle. i want to know how far the physics actually maps. can you lay out the correspondence — whats the sound source, the reflective wall, the absorption coefficient, the standing wave — then the places it breaks? especially im wondering about the angle-of-incidence rule: in physics reflection equals incidence, but in social life people dont reject dissent at the same angle they received it; they often distort it first. and does the no malicious censors needed conclusion survive once you realize acoustic walls are passive, but social walls — moderation norms, algorithmic ranking, social shaming — are actively maintained? also, where does the analogy fall apart for epistemic bubbles specifically, since bubbles can form just by absence of other voices rather than reflection of your own?
