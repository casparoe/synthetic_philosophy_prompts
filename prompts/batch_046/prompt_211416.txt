As a novelist attempting to ground a philosophical fiction in the ontology of representation, I have constructed the following argument, and now I fear it dissolves the very authorship it seeks to explain. I present it in numbered steps and ask you to locate the precise fracture, because I need to know whether saving intentional action requires abandoning physical closure, granting symbols a special causal status, or paying some other philosophical price. (1) Define a careenium as any substrate in which every state-transition is causally closed under local physical properties alone, leaving no explanatory gap that symbolic content might fill. (2) Define a simmball as any structurally realized pattern that an interpreter takes to carry information, but whose causal profile is exhaustively fixed by its substrate geometry. (3) If symbols genuinely pushed the substrate around, then substituting a simmball with a physically isomorphic but semantically vacant counterpart—call it a nullmball—should yield a different substrate trajectory. (4) By the closure condition of the careenium, no substitution preserving substrate geometry can alter the trajectory; hence no divergence occurs. (5) Therefore, within any careenium, simmballs never exert downward causation; they are descriptive overlays, not causal levers. (6) The human brain and every computing architecture are careenia: their causal histories are complete without invoking semantic interpretation. (7) Thus mental representations and algorithmic instructions never literally push neurons or silicon; all apparent symbolic guidance is an interpretive projection onto substrate dynamics. (8) But this renders intentional explanation causally redundant: if I write a scene because its narrative meaning demands it, that meaning plays no part in the physical etiology of my keystrokes. (9) And if symbolic meaning is always redundant, then a novelist's claim that a plot-structure shaped the manuscript is indistinguishable from claiming that a shadow shaped the wall it falls upon. (10) This conclusion seems intolerable, for it dissolves authorship and intentional action into pure substrate mechanics. (11) So the chain must fail—yet each step looks sound. Which premise or inference should I reject? Name the hidden slide or equivocation precisely, explain why the step appeared valid, and tell me what I must pay philosophically to preserve the causal relevance of simmballs within a careenium.
