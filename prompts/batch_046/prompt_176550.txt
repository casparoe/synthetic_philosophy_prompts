After a recent health crisis made me abandon the long-term career goals I had treated as my reason for living, I have become convinced of what I take to be tranquilism: the claim that only the absence of craving and suffering is intrinsically valuable, that positive states — achievement, affection, even pleasure — are at best instrumentally useful in sustaining that absence, and that they often function as deceptive noise that draws a system away from true equilibrium. I work in statistical physics, not philosophy, and I think in terms of entropy production, free-energy landscapes, dissipative structures, homeostatic set points, and fluctuations that are either signal or noise. Restate the full position I just described — including the normative priority of absence, the instrumental or deceptive status of positive affect, and the nature of craving as an internal driver — using only that vocabulary. Map precisely: what corresponds to suffering and to craving, whether craving is best modeled as a sustained gradient or as an unstable attractor, and whether the idea that positive states deceive is like a local free-energy minimum that prevents global relaxation. Then provide the residue report: which philosophical distinctions translate cleanly, which must be distorted to fit a physicalist framework, and which — especially the normative claim that absence alone has value, or the idea that craving is bad in itself rather than merely costly — have no counterpart in thermodynamics at all. Conclude by saying whether the residue reveals that tranquilism is covertly just a physical description, or whether it smuggles in commitments that physics cannot underwrite.
