Write a retirement toast for a philosopher who spent her career arguing that Western individualism and East Asian communitarianism share a hidden false premise: both assume that if a person is constituted by her relationships, then she must be subordinate to them. Your audience is split evenly between academic colleagues who will recognize the reference to this specific distinction and family members who will not. The toast should run about five minutes, should make the conceptual distinction do real structural work in explaining what she actually believed—without reducing it to decoration—and should remain warm and celebratory rather than lecturing. Do not include citations, reading lists, or references to specific texts. After the toast, add a brief self-critical note: state what kind of argument or counterexample would lead you to abandon the claim that relational constitution is fully compatible with individual autonomy, and point out where your reasoning is most vulnerable.
