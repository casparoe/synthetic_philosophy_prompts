hey, i've been thinking about Dennett's Quining Qualia paper with the Chase and Sanborn coffee tasters, and there's this analogy people keep using in forums - they say arguing that qualia are incoherent is like arguing that the claim this coffee tastes bitter is incoherent just because Chase and Sanborn suddenly disagree about whether their favorite blend changed. The idea is that qualia are just private taste-reports, so if two people have different internal reactions with zero behavorial difference, that doesn't make the reports contradictory - it just shows qualia are a special kind of claim that doesn't need external verification. People use this to say Dennett's quining move is basically a category mistake.

Can you audit how far that holds? What exactly maps onto what - the taste report, the tasters, the disagreement - and where does the analogy fall apart? Specifically, does it miss Dennett's point that Chase and Sanborn can still perfectly agree on every functional and dispositional fact about the coffee, and that the private change they're supposed to have is exactly the kind of thing he thinks is incoherent? Also, does the analogy quietly import psychologism about meaning or reference that Dennett would reject? And if the analogy breaks, what's a better way to think about whether qualia survive quining?
