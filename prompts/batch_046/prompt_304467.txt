I'm a novelist, mostly true crime and some weird historical fiction on the side, and I'm stuck on a problem I keep hitting with every new book. I was talking with my friends—one of them's a philosophy grad student—about Keynes's Treatise on Probability, not the macroeconomics but the part where he talks about uncertainty being different from risk with missing numbers, and this distinction he makes between the weight of an argument and the actual probability. Anyway, I need to turn that into a reusable procedure I can run when I'm sitting with my case notes, deciding how much a single clue should move my belief about a narrative I'm constructing. The stakes are that I publish these and readers trust me not to chase ghosts, but I also only have like ten minutes per decision and I'm working alone with no editor checking my logic, so I need a checklist or flowchart in words—no software—that tells me what question to ask in order, what counts as enough, where it stops with "don't shift," and where it just hands the call back to me because it's values not facts. It should be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, handle the gap between a result being unlikely under my current story and that story being likely, and say exactly what to do when I can't get numbers. Apply it to three cases: a single forensic report that contradicts an alibi where my gut wants to move but maybe shouldn't, a statistical study on a suspects behavioral pattern, and a case where I have mountains of evidence but all of it comes from one biased source. Also flag where this breaks down and I have to decide by instinct, since I need to warn my readers about that too. Please don't ask for citations or a reading list—I'm writing in cafes with spotty wifi and I need this to work with just what's in my head.
