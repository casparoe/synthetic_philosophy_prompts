Here's the question I set, followed by one student's response.

Question: Consider a modified Judy Benjamin scenario. Judy starts with credance 1/2 in Division A and 1/2 in Division B. Within A, strategists outnumber field officers two to one; within B the reverse holds. Before she knows her role, she receives one of two sealed messages, selected by a mechanism she can't observe. Message M1 states: 'If you are in Division A then you are a strategist, and if you are in Division B then you are a field officer.' Message M2 states the opposite pairing. She opens the envelope, reads M1, and updates by Jeffrey conditioning on the event that M1 is true.

(a) Set out the partition over which she should condition, compute the prior weights of the cells, and state her posterior credance in being in Division A.

(b) One objection holds that updating on a bare conditional should never shift belief in the antecedent, because the conditional carries no factual information about the world. Assess this claim in light of Judy's updated credences.

Student answer:

For part a, Judy should partition the space into four outcomes: (A, strategist), (A, field officer), (B, strategist), (B, field officer). The priors are uniform across divisions but weighted inside them: P(A,S) = 1/3, P(A,F) = 1/6, P(B,S) = 1/6, P(B,F) = 1/3. M1 picks out exactly (A,S) and (B,F), so she restricts to those two cells. Normalising gives each weight 1/2, so her credance in being a strategist is 1/2. Because roles are assigned symmetrically across divisions, her credance in being in A must equal her credance in being a strategist, hence P(A) = 1/2.

For part b, the objection draws on Lewis's Principal Principle, which says rational credance should align with known objective chances and therefore a conditional report can't provide evidence about its antecedent. Jeffrey conditioning is really meant for direct observational updates, not for linguistic messages. So Judy shouldn't have treated M1 as a conditioning event at all; she should have used standard Bayes with likelihoods. In any case, since her credance in A ends up at 1/2, the update is evidentially inert, which confirms the objection's point.

Don't be too generous — there are subtle mistakes in there that a rushed grader might miss.
