Here's the question we were given:

In Principia Ethica, Moore lists personal affection and aesthetic appreciation among the greatest goods. Explain how he distinguishes their intrinsic value, especially regarding whether a conscious observer is necessary for the value to exist. Make sure to discuss the method of isolation and the concept of organic unities.

And here's her answer: Moore thinks both friendship and beauty are intrinsically good, but he separates them using the method of isolation and the idea of organic unities. For beauty, he asks us to imagine a world that contains only a beautiful sunset and nothing else—not even anyone to see it. He says that world is still better than an ugly one, which proves beauty isn't just pleasure and can exist without a conscious observer. For friendship, he uses the same isolation test but reaches the opposite conclusion: a world with friendship but no minds is impossible, so friendship necessarily requires two conscious people being aware of each other. That's why friendship is an organic unity in Moore's special sense—the value isn't just the sum of each person's happiness, but a new thing created by their mutual awareness, kind of like how punishment plus vice equals justice. If one friend dies and the other forgets entirely, Moore would say the past friendship actually loses its value retroactively, because the organic whole is destroyed. He also connects this to his critique of naturalism: just as he argues that calling pleasure good isn't a tautology like pleasure being pleasure, he thinks saying friendship is good can't be reduced to brain states or social facts. So beauty is objective and independent of minds, while friendship is a conscious organic whole that can be undone by memory loss.
