I was in my philosophy of religion course and we started arguing about karma, not as some mystical religious thing but as an actual theory of moral desert - like, is there a real accounting system where people get what they deserve? I'm trying to figure out my actual gut feelings by running cases. So here's where I land: if a surgeon leaves a sponge inside a patient because she was totally distracted and daydreaming, that feels like negative desert, but not as bad as if she did it on purpose. If some guy donates a kidney to a stranger anonymously, never telling anyone, that feels like serious positive desert building up. But then there's a politician who pushes a policy he genuinely believes is best, and it ends up starving thousands - I think he still gets negative desert for the outcome, even though his heart was in teh right place, which maybe clashes with the surgeon case? Also a reckless driver who hits a kid while texting definitely gets negative karma, but I weirdly feel like a driver who hits a kid despite being totally focused should still carry some negative weight, just because the harm flowed through them, even though that feels unfair. The one I keep going back and forth on is the lie-to-save-a-life thing: telling a murderer at the door that your friend isn't home. In normal ethics thats clearly fine, but under a desert framework, does the lie still generate a little negative karmic debt that has to be paid off later, or does saving a life wipe it totally clean? And can good karma from later actions cancel out old bad karma, or are they separate accounts?

I'm wondering if there's one principle that captures all this, maybe something like "desert tracks the moral quality of the will scaled by harm caused, with intent setting the rate and outcome setting the scale" - but that feels clunky and doesn't really cover the careful-driver case. Or maybe I need two separate ledgers? I want a principle, or maybe a small set, that gets the surgeon, the donor, the politician, and both drivers right, and tells me what to do with my lie case. Also be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save - like, do I have to drop the idea that the careful driver owes something? What's the simplest principle that gets close?
