Consider assertions made under structured epistemic dependency: a speaker asserts p not from direct evidence but because she has rationally assessed an expert's testimony, understands the reasoning well enough to defend it, and yet cannot independently verify p. What is your actual credence—stated as a specific number between 0 and 1—that in such cases the speaker violates no assertoric norm, which would mean the governing norm is justified belief rather than knowledge? Decompose your reasoning into three weighted components: (a) the probability that knowledge is genuinely non-delegable, so testimonial transmission without first-order grasp fails to yield knowledge; (b) the probability that assertion's primary social function requires the speaker to be personally in a position to vouch for p; and (c) the probability that a norm of justified belief can be robust enough without collapsing back into a knowledge requirement. Show roughly how these combine, identify which component your final figure is most sensitive to, and say what specific distinction—perhaps between testimonial knowledge and testimonial belief, or between vouching and reporting—would most move your number if argued persuasively.
