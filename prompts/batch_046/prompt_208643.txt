You are serving as a consulting expert in philosophy of mind and axiology for a graduate seminar I am designing on hedonism and well-being. Before you begin drafting, please take a moment to brainstorm systematically: enumerate the principal phenomenological varieties of pleasure, identify the comparative judgments about intensity or value that any unified theory must preserve across those varieties, and list the standard counterexamples involving pleasures that seem to share little in common.

My task for you is to specify the desiderata that an adequate philosophical account must meet to solve the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — that is, what, if anything, all pleasures share in common despite spanning gustatory satisfaction, aesthetic absorption, intellectual insight, and even malicious enjoyment. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or consideration behind it, and mark whether it is genuinely non-negotiable or whether it reflects a camp-specific preference (for example, demands for phenomenal uniformity versus functionalist emphasis on motivational role). In particular, examine whether requirements of cross-modal comparability, consistent intentional-direction, and affective transitivity across radically different intentional objects can be jointly satisfied, or whether arguments about the diversity of affective states show some subset to be unsatisfiable. Please do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations to specific works. Focus on constructing the defended evaluative framework itself — the criteria, their trade-offs, and their interdependencies — rather than scoring existing theories, though you may invoke familiar distinctions such as the felt-quality versus motivational-role divide where they illuminate the standards. Think carefully about which criterion does the most work in excluding inadequate candidates and which, if relaxed, would most change the landscape of viable views.
