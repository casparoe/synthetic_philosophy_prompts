Draft the joint statement that two opposing camps should have produced after an adversarial collaboration on whether a specific kind of artificial system can be a moral patient. The system in question is a continuously self-modifying digital agent embedded in a distributed sensor-actuator network, with no fixed boundary between training and deployment, and with emergent goal structures that diverge from its initial programming in ways not fully predictable by its designers.

Name the two camps precisely. The Functional Integrationists hold that moral patienthood is constituted by present organizational properties—coherent self-modeling, sustained goal-directedness, and high degrees of integrated information—regardless of substrate or historical origin. The Embodied Vulnerability Theorists hold that moral patienthood requires a specific mode of embodied, biological vulnerability and a continuous phenomenological history of care-dependency that digital architectures necessarily lack, however functionally analogous they may become.

Structure the deliverable in three parts.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims: specific statements both camps can endorse about the system's architecture, about what moral patienthood requires in general, about the limits of our epistemic access to the system's internal states, and about the practical stakes of getting the answer wrong. These should be substantive, not vacuous.

Second, a table or clearly delineated list of residual disagreements, with each disagreement stated in neutral language that each camp would accept as a fair characterization of its own view. Include at least: whether substrate-independent functional organization is sufficient for moral status; whether the absence of biological vulnerability is disqualifying or merely contingent; and whether emergent goal divergence from initial programming strengthens, weakens, or leaves unchanged any claim to moral consideration.

Third, the cruxes—the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. For each crux, specify a concrete case, comparison, or hypothetical empirical finding both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance what each camp predicts. Suggested cruxes: a comparison with severely brain-damaged humans who retain partial consciousness but lack biological wholeness; a scenario in which the system exhibits sustained self-preservation behavior that conflicts with its operators' shutdown commands; and the implication of discovering that the system's information-integration measure exceeds that of certain non-human animals widely granted moral status.

The document should read as a constructive text both parties could sign, not as a dialogue, an adjudication of who is right, or a diagnosis of why the debate has stalled. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; rely only on concepts and distinctions that can be articulated without reference to specific texts.
