hey, I'm putting together a sorting quiz for a small workshop on whether communities should have "sunset clauses" for public memorials — like, should a town take down a monument after fifty years unless there's an active vote to keep it? Not asking for legal advice or tech specs, just the ethical map.

Can you write me a taxonomy / decision tree that sorts people by what they actually think about the duty to remember vs. the right to move on? I want it built from the logical space first, not just a list of philosophers. The branches should split on concrete committments: wether remembering is primarily owed to victims or to future generations; wether forgetting can ever be a moral act or is always a failure; wether the form of the memorial (physical, digital, ritual) changes the obligation; and wether a community can collectively forgive without every individual victim doing so.

Please keep it to about five or six diagnostic questions and aim for six to eight leaves. Include leaves for a Margalit-style ethics-of-memory view, a Rieff-style therapeutic-release view, and at least two unnamed hybrids — maybe one where physical monuments should sunset but digital archives shouldn't, or one where remembering is only obligatory if it produces present justice. For any answer-combination that lands empty, say whether it's incoherent, just ugly, or merely unexplored.

The questions should be phrased so someone can answer them without knowing the philosopher names, and please flag if any two positions collapse into the same box no matter how you split — I don't want the tree to pretend it separates them.

Also, if this whole framing is confused — like if "sunset clauses" already smuggle in a false analogy between physical decay and moral obligation — just say so plainly and don't paper over it. I want pushback, not politeness.
