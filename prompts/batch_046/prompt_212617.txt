I am writing a paper for my philosophy of perception course on Molyneux's problem and Locke's empiricist answer. Locke maintained that a person blind from birth who suddenly gains sight could not immediately identify a cube or sphere by vision alone, since visual and tactile ideas require experiential association. Imagine Locke were confronted with contemporary research on cross-modal plasticity—where visual cortical regions in congenitally blind subjects process tactile and auditory spatial information—and with sensory-substitution devices that translate visual patterns into tactile vibrations, allowing users to navigate spatial layouts without sight. How do you think he would revise or defend his original answer? Would he insist that such performance still depends on prior associative learning mediated through other senses, or might he be pressed to allow that some spatial concepts are not strictly modality-bound? Please consider whether this modern evidence genuinely threatens his broader empiricism about the origin of ideas, or whether it can be fully accommodated within an associative framework.
