Bit stuck on a point in my Plato chapter and I think I'm falling for a false presupposition. In Laws I-II Plato treats the symposium as a kind of pharmakon that strips away social pretence and reveals whether your thumos is properly ordered — basically an ancient behavioural test. From that it seems like he thinks drunkenness gives unmediated access to the soul's constitutional form, almost like an MRI for virtue. If that's right, then the Republic's long educational programme looks redundant: why spend decades on dialetic and maths if you can just get people drunk and see who holds their ground? Where's the mistake? Is it that the symposium isn't diagnostic but acutally a form of training, or is the deeper confusion assuming that revealed behaviour under intoxication maps straight onto the Forms rather than onto acquired habits?
