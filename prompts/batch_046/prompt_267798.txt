i was talking with two friends from my Kierkegaard reading group — Maya and Leo — about Fear and Trembling and whether Abraham actually pulls off the "teleological suspension of the ethical" or if he's basically just a fanatic who got lucky. They got into it pretty deep and i couldnt tell who was winning, so i'm writing it out here.

Maya said the suspension isnt a rejection of ethics per se but specifically of the universal ethical, that Sittlichkeit stuff that demands you justify your actions to everyone. She pointed out Abraham still loves Isaac — he doesnt kill him out of hatred — so the particular relation to God doesnt erase ethical feeling, it just outranks it. She also brought up the double movement: first infinite resignation (giving up Isaac for real), then faith (getting him back by virtue of the absurd). So for her Abraham isnt destroying ethics; he's showing faith can restore the particular without going through universal justification. Leo came back saying thats way too neat. He said if youre really outside the universal, you cant "restore" anything — you just have a private exception with no public meaning. He argued de Silentio sets up the tragic hero (Agamemnon) precisely to show Abraham is different: Agamemnon can speak, explain, stay inside ethics by sacrificing Iphigenia for the state, but Abraham cant speak at all. For Leo that silence means Abraham isnt making some sophisticated double movement; hes just in a category where ethical language doesnt apply, period. Maya then said Leo was conflating the ethical with social convention — she thinks de Silentio allows Abraham to suspend the universal law but not the concrete love for his son, which is why its a paradox and not just madness. Leo said that distinction doesnt hold up in the text because the ethical just is the universal for Kierkegaard; theres no separate "concrete love" layer that survives intact. He also threw in that if Abraham really expected Isaac back "by virtue of the absurd," then he wasnt genuinely willing to sacrifice him, which undermines the whole test of faith. Maya replied that expecting the absurd isnt the same as expecting a miracle — its holding both resignation and belief simultaneously, which is exactly the knight of faith's move.

Can you walk through their exchange point by point and say whos right about what? Rate each move from clearly wrong to clearly right and briefly justify. Also please say how confident you are in each judgment and single out the one point youre least sure about — i know this is pretty in the weeds but i want to see if the reasoning holds up without just recycling the standard "murderer vs hero of faith" summary.
