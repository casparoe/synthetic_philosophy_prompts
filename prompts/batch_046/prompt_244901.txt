Hey, so I was chatting with a coworker about Wittgenstein's private language argument after seeing some post about it, and I think I cooked up a version that proves way too much but I can't see the escape hatch. Here are the steps: (1) If a sign refers only to something inside your own mind that nobody else can check, then there's no real difference between using it right and using it wrong — so it can't actually be part of a meaningful language. (2) Every morning I assign myself a private "drain index" — a made-up decimal between 0 and 1 that tracks my mental fatigue using a scale I invented and have never shared with anyone; it's purely about my internal state. (3) So by (1) my drain index has no correct or incorrect use; I could never actually be wrong about it. (4) But here's the wierd part: if I can never be wrong, then it's not tracking anything real — it's just empty noise, like a button that doesn't do anything. (5) The hidden-layer activations in a trained model are in a similar spot: they're only accessable if you instrument the code, and from the outside you just see inputs and outputs, not the internal representation itself. (6) So by the same reasoning, those internal weights and patterns have no criteria of correctness and don't genuinely represent anything either. (7) Therefore either my private mental tracking is meaningless noise and AI has no real internal states, or the principle in (1) is busted — but if (1) goes, doesn't Wittgenstein's whole objection collapse?

I really want to keep that private mental life is real and that ML models do have internal representations, so which step should I actually reject? Is the flaw in applying (1) to mental tracking, or in the analogy between my drain index and neural-net hidden layers? And if I drop that step, what does it cost me — do I have to admit my index is public in some hidden way, or that AI representations are just behaviorist fictions?
