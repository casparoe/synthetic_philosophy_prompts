I run a 75-minute upper-level seminar on moral philosophy with 14 students. Design a structured deliberation activity that makes participants experience the pull from descriptive cross-cultural variation in folk morality—particularly the WEIRD/non-WEIRD divergence in how harm and loyalty are weighted—toward normative moral anti-realism, without letting them notice the slide until after they have committed to a position. To keep the idea from being merely discussed, inject a hidden-information asymmetry: divide the group into two subgroups that each receive a different, incomplete set of empirical case cards (one set drawn from Western liberal intuitions, one from communal/non-WEIRD intuitions), then require them to jointly defend either moral objectivity or relativism before a review board using only the evidence they hold. Specify roles, rules of evidence-sharing, a timeline, and a non-monetary payoff structure (e.g., a collective grading incentive) that makes coordination on a shared meta-principle costly. Include a debrief that maps specific moments in the deliberation onto the is-ought gap and the argument from disagreement, predicts what will go wrong the first run (for instance, one subgroup dominating the evidence pool), and is explicit about which objectivist reply—such as an appeal to shared rational capacities—the rules structurally suppress and you must supply verbally.
