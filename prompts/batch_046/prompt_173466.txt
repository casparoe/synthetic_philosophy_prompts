i've been trying to work out this argument my roommate threw at me about Hobsbawm and Anderson and i think it eats everything. (1) Nations are imagined communities in Anderson's sense: people share mental representations of membership even though they'll never meet. (2) Those representations need invented traditions — rituals, origin myths, old-looking symbols that are actually recent — to feel continuous with the past (Hobsbawm). (3) So national identity is a collective belief sustained partly by misrepresentation of history. (4) But collective intentionality requires that members genuinely share an intention, not just hold parallel false beliefs about one. (5) Since the shared belief in (3) is based on misrepresentation, it can't ground real collective intentionality. (6) Without collective intentionality there's no collective agency. (7) So nations can't act; only individuals inside them do. (8) The state is just the institutional form of a nation, so states lack agency too. (9) If states don't act, laws aren't collective actions but just coordinated individual behavior. (10) Then legal obligation isn't a real normative relation, just prudence or habit. (11) But the same structure applies to corporations, sports teams, maybe even families — any group with shared narratives. So either collective life is an illusion or one of the steps is rotten. I really want to keep (3) and (7) because they feel right about nationalism and the state, but i'm not sure about (4). Where's the slide? Which premise do you kill, and what's the damage?
