The focus is on any framework that tries to preserve the prioritarian intuition—that improving someone's situation matters more, morally, the worse off they are—while genuinely avoiding the leveling-down objection, not just renaming it.

Please write out the full desiderata: what an adequate theory in this space must be able to do. For each requirement, include the motivating case or distinction behind it. I also want you to include a constraint I'm introducing called the reversibility condition: the theory must explain why achieving prioritarian aims through temporary, institutionally reversible transfers is permissible, whereas achieving them through permanent, irreversible harm to the better-off is not—without simply baking reversibility into the prioritarian weight itself. Please flag whether you think this condition is a genuine desideratum or a prejudice imported from liberal institutional theory, and whether it conflicts with the demand that priority weights depend only on absolute well-being levels.

Additionally, identify any pair of desiderata on your list that may be jointly unsatisfiable, with the reasoning. Then, keeping the weight on the criteria rather than on the scoring, briefly assess how standard prioritarianism, equality-of-resources approaches, and sufficientarian threshold views fare against the list. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most work in ruling out the most popular candidates, and whether that is a feature of the list or a problem with it.
