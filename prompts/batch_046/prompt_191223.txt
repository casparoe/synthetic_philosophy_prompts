I'm leading a ninety-minute seminar for sociology graduate students who have read Capital volume one but not the Grundrisse. I want to teach Marx's argument about automation and the general intellect: specifically, the claim that machinery tends to reduce direct labor time to a minimum, yet under capitalism this reduction becomes a contradiction rather than liberation because wealth remains measured by labor time. Do not explain Marx's theory to me; plan the pedagogical arc. What conceptual dependency must be established before the contradiction even makes sense to this audience? Select one concrete carrying example—perhaps the shift from a craft workshop to an automated distribution center—and specify exactly which feature of the example must be visible and which feature must be absent for the analogy to hold. Map the misconceptions they will bring in predictable sequence: first that Marx is a Luddite, then that he is a techno-utopian predicting fully automated luxury communism, then that general intellect is equivalent to human-capital theory; for each, identify the precise point in the sequence where it should be defused and the one sentence that does it. Design a single diagnostic question for the end that reveals whether they grasp why complete automation would not, on this view, automatically dissolve capitalism. Finally, state explicitly which adjacent theoretical element—such as the tendency of the rate of profit to fall or the Hegelian form-and-content dialectic—you will deliberately omit and will tell the students you are omitting, with one reason why including it would break the arc.
