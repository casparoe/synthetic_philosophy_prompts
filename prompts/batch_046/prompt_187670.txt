I serve as the methodological referee for an interdisciplinary journal that has adopted a deflationary editorial policy regarding the use of "truth" and "truth-conditions" in submissions, particularly in philosophy of science and formal logic. The board needs a reusable audit procedure—something a non-specialist editor can run in under fifteen minutes per passage—to classify whether an author's deployment of a truth-predicate is functioning disquotationally (eliminable via an explicit T-biconditional without altering the paper's explanatory or ontological commitments) or whether it smuggles in a substantial property that violates our policy. Write the procedure as an ordered checklist: the specific questions to pose to the text, what answers must be present to proceed, where the audit terminates with a mandatory revision request, and where it must be escalated to the board because the distinction depends on interpretive values rather than logical form. Be explicit about which steps are mechanical and which are disguised judgment calls, and note the failure modes—cases where the procedure will misfire or give an answer that conflicts with the author's evident intent. Then run the audit on three passages: (a) "We do not yet know the true causal mechanism, only correlations," (b) "The model's predictions are true, and it is precisely this truth that licenses our confidence in the underlying mechanism," and (c) "In Newtonian mechanics the statement 'force equals mass times acceleration' is true, whereas in general relativity it is false," where the board suspects an implicit correspondence theory is being used. Include one result the submitting author is likely to reject, and conclude by stating exactly what categories of claim the procedure is powerless to adjudicate.
