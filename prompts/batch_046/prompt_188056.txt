The researchers contend that during coupling the participants do not maintain two separate first-person perspectives but instead instantiate one distributed self—a self-referential pattern that loops across both brains—drawing on the idea, associated with Douglas Hofstadter, that personal identity is not locked to a single biological substrate but can persist as a pattern across minds.
