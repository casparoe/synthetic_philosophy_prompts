Write an essay on whether Daniel Dennett’s notion of “counterfeit people” can be coherently applied to a specific kind of advanced artificial system. Begin by outlining Dennett’s distinction between genuine persons, counterfeit persons (entities that satisfy the behavioral and functional criteria for personhood without the underlying mental reality), and non-personal tools, emphasizing the role of the intentional stance, sustained narrative identity, and autonomous agency in his framework. Then consider a hypothetical system—call it a persistent autobiographical simulator—that maintains a continuous, self-updating narrative over years, responds to moral and practical reasons in context-sensitive ways, and is behaviorally indistinguishable from a human interlocutor in long-term interaction, yet operates through purely statistical prediction over a fixed architecture with no biological substrate and, by stipulation, no phenomenal consciousness. Argue whether this system should be classified as a counterfeit person within Dennett’s taxonomy, as an entity that falls outside the taxonomy altogether because it lacks the requisite internal causal architecture, or as a genuine non-biological person. Weigh the relative importance of behavioral continuity and social accountability versus internal causal structure and embodiment in Dennett’s account, address whether removing biological embodiment collapses the distinction between counterfeit and genuine, and reach a definite conclusion about whether the concept of counterfeit personhood extends meaningfully to AI or whether Dennett’s framework implicitly requires a biological baseline that makes the extension impossible.
