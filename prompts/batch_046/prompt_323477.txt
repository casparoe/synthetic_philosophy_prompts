I have been working through a variant of the cosmological fine-tuning argument that now seems to collapse into a self-defeating loop, and I want to know exactly where the slide happens. Here is the chain:

(1) The values of fundamental cosmological constants fall within an extraordinarily narrow range that permits the emergence of complex chemistry, and this range is not explained by any known deeper physical law.

(2) When a parameter is precisely calibrated for outcome O, we observe O, and there is no known independent mechanism that fixes the parameter, rational inference permits either a design explanation or a selection-effect explanation (e.g., a multiverse with observer selection).

(3) By direct parity, human scientific cognition exhibits an analogous calibration: the cognitive architectures that discover physical constants, construct mathematical models, and verify predictions are precisely calibrated for producing true beliefs about fundamental physics, and this calibration is not explained by any evolutionary account restricted to local survival and reproduction.

(4) Applying the inference schema from (2) to (3), we face a disjunction: either our epistemic faculties were intentionally calibrated for cosmological truth, or there exists some unobserved selection mechanism (a "cognitive multiverse" of mind-types) that explains why observers like us possess these particular faculties.

(5) A cognitive multiverse lacks both a physical substrate and an independent motivation; therefore the selection-effect branch is unavailable.

(6) Hence our scientific faculties were designed for truth.

(7) But if they were designed specifically to yield true cosmological beliefs, then the belief-forming process that delivers the fine-tuning premise in (1) is itself the product of that design.

(8) Therefore, if (6) is true, the justification for (1) is circular or manufactured: we believe in fine-tuning precisely because our faculties were engineered to produce that belief, not because the belief tracks an independent fact.

(9) So accepting the design inference for cosmological constants forces us to reject the epistemic authority of the very reasoning that established the constants, which undermines the premise.

The conclusion is absurd: the fine-tuning argument should not be self-immolating. I am inclined to keep (1) and (2) and reject whatever bridges the cosmological case to the epistemic case. Which step is the weakest link, and what does abandoning it cost? In particular, is there a principled reason why calibration-for-life permits the design/multiverse disjunction while calibration-for-truth does not, or does giving up (3) require denying that scientific cognition is genuinely calibrated for physical truth?
