Write a sestina about the DNA–Gödel isomorphism using these six rotating end-words in this order: genome, proof, mutation, self, interpreter, halt. The first stanza should present replication as naive copying; stanzas two and three should introduce the self-referential problem of a genome that tries to encode its own proofreading mechanism, generating a diagonal regress in which the proof-checker cannot verify itself. Stanzas four and five should treat mutation not as noise but as the biological analog of an undecidable statement—an insertion the system can neither consistently prove nor reject—while the proofreading machinery plays the role of a formal proof-checker facing Gödelian limits. The sixth stanza should collapse this self-reference. The envoi must use all six words and resolve by stating that replication succeeds only when it accepts an external interpreter—selection pressure, environment, or death—rather than achieving perfect self-containment. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with Gödel’s incompleteness theorems and molecular genetics recognizes the analogy in the structure, not just in the vocabulary, and it should include no citations, bibliographies, or quoted passages.
