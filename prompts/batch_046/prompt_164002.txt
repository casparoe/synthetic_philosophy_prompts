A colleague in a recent seminar on early modern political thought summarized Pascal’s discussion of justice, force, and authority in the Pensées as follows. “Pascal maintains that there is no natural justice accessible to reason, so every system of positive law is merely arbitrary convention sustained by habit and violence. He claims that force is the real foundation of all authority, and that the so-called mystical foundation of authority is only a fiction that rulers exploit to disguise raw domination. Because justice without force is powerless, while force without justice is dangerous, Pascal concludes that the two must be fused in practice—so that law is essentially might made respectable. He further argues that human beings are too corrupt to recognize any true justice, which means conventional justice is the best we can hope for, and that the wise person ought to obey whoever actually holds power without inquiring whether they deserve it. From this it follows that Pascal offers no normative resources for distinguishing a legitimate state from a tyranny: both are maintained by violence, and his view is ultimately a cynical realpolitik that treats domination as inevitable and obedience as mere prudence.”

She draws from this the further conclusion that Pascal’s account is not only descriptively bleak but normatively empty—that he cannot explain why one ought to prefer a just regime to a brutal one, since on his view both are equally conventional.

I want to audit this summary before I assign it to students. Please evaluate it claim by claim: which parts are faithful to Pascal’s fragments on justice, force, and the union of the two; which parts conflate his descriptive sociology of law with a normative endorsement of domination; and whether her inference that Pascal collapses legitimate authority into tyranny actually follows from what he writes about the mystical foundation and the necessity of merging justice with force. In particular, address whether she obscures the distinction Pascal draws between conventional justice and the justice that comes from God, and whether she mischaracterizes his reasons for advocating obedience to established order. Finally, does her conclusion that his view is “normatively empty” survive once the misrepresentations are corrected?
