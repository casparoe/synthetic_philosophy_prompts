I'm writing a dissertation chapter on monistic phenomenology, and in recent seminars colleagues keep invoking the Vedanta-inspired slogan associated with Schrödinger: that consciousness is fundamentally singular, and the multiplicity of individual minds is only an appearance. They deploy it in support of everything from panpsychist substrate monism to quantum-mystical interpretations of measurement to a purely grammatical claim about the indexical I. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses this formulation might express—separating, for example, numerical identity of one mental substance across all brains, universal qualitative sameness of experience, an epistemic dissolution of the self-other boundary, and a metaphysical doctrine of maya concealing an underlying Atman? For each reading, say whether it is trivial, whether Schrödinger's actual writings commit him to it, whether it requires any quantum-mechanical premises, and whether it genuinely supports the specific argumentative use being made of it. I need a disambiguated menu of interpretations rather than a single authoritative reading, since my goal is to show that the slogan is being asked to carry more theoretical weight than any one thesis can bear.
