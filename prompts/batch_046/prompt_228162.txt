I'm taking a course on philosophy of science and have adopted Imre Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes (MSRP) as my working framework—not only for physics, where Lakatos originally developed it, but also for evaluating current theoretical work in artificial intelligence and consciousness studies. I treat the hard core of a programme as conventionally protected by the negative heuristic, judge its vitality by whether adjustments to the protective belt yield novel predictions rather than ad hoc defenses, and take a sustained degenerating problem shift as genuine grounds for abandoning the research tradition. What would have to be true for this position to be wrong? Are there clear historical cases where a programme judged degenerating later revived and produced genuine novel predictions, undermining the reliability of the progressive/degenerative distinction? Could a programme be progressive by Lakatos's criteria yet ultimately turn out empirically false? Do domains like AI or consciousness research lack the shared hard-core structure that makes the framework apply, or can they be reconstructed to fit it? And if defenders can always reclassify failures as belt adjustments, has the view been insulated into unfalsifiability—and is that insulation a defect for a normative account of scientific rationality? I want an honest map of the exposure: rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a committed Lakatosian would concede and which they would dismiss, and tell me whether the framework genuinely risks anything in these newer domains or has been qualified into safety.
