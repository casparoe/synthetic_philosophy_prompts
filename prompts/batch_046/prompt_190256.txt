You are a senior philosopher of mind and cognitive science with experience drafting adversarial-collaboration documents between opposing camps. Before writing, take a moment to brainstorm: list three claims about consciousness that philosophers with radically different substrate commitments would still accept; list three precise points where functional-structuralists and embodied-process theorists genuinely diverge rather than talk past each other; and sketch one novel framing—such as distinguishing token-level from system-level consciousness, or the idea of counterfactual accessibility without physical instantiation—that can prevent the debate from becoming a rehearsed script. Then draft the joint statement that two opposing camps could both sign after good-faith collaboration on the question drawn from Hofstadter and Dennett’s The Mind’s I: if Einstein’s brain were replaced by a printed book containing every internal state-transition rule and counterfactual response-disposition of the original brain, could that book be conscious?

Camp One, the Archival Functionalists, holds that consciousness supervenes on the correct abstract relational structure and interpretable counterfactual profile, so that substrate and temporal dynamics are inessential provided the structural isomorphism is complete; they allow that interpretation conditions or system-level activation might matter, but deny that static storage is automatically disqualifying. Camp Two, the Dynamic Coupling Theorists, holds that consciousness necessarily involves real-time embodied interaction, temporal integration, and causal coupling with an environment, so that a static, non-interacting printed volume is necessarily non-conscious regardless of its informational completeness.

Your deliverable must be a formal document with three parts. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke hyper-obscure theories unknown outside specialist circles; but do inject your novel framing so that the disagreement is made exact rather than verbal.
