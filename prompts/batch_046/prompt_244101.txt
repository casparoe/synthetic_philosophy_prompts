I've been trying to stabilize a position on consciousness as a user illusion, and I'd like an audit of whether the following commitments actually cohere. (1) Consciousness is a user-interface illusion: the brain constructs a streamlined, first-person model of itself for behavioral regulation, and there is no additional phenomenal "inner light" behind that model. (2) The illusion is not merely representational but specifically temporal—it involves misbinding discrete processing stages into a unified, present-tense "now" that is falsely attributed to an enduring observer. (3) I also hold that this temporal misbinding requires affective valence as a necessary scaffold; without minimal affective framing, a system can produce self-modeling but not the particular structure of "someone for whom things are like something." (4) From this I draw a normative conclusion: moral patienthood tracks the presence of this affectively scaffolded illusion, not functional complexity or self-modeling capacity alone—so a system with richer cognition but no affective binding lacks moral standing, while a system with minimal cognition but intact affective self-misbinding possesses some. (5) Finally, I want to maintain that this framework is empirically tractable: the illusion should dissolve or alter predictably if we selectively disrupt temporal binding or affective framing, without necessarily altering lower-level sensory processing.

Do these hang together? I'm particularly unsure whether (2) and (3) pull in different directions—if the core error is temporal misbinding, why should affect be necessary rather than merely contingent? Does (4) genuinely follow from (1)–(3), or am I smuggling in an independent intuition about suffering? And is there a hidden tension between (1)'s eliminativism about phenomenal properties and (5)'s claim that we can study the illusion's empirical dissolution—if there is nothing there, what exactly are we measuring? Please rank the real tensions by severity, identify any apparent conflicts that dissolve on closer inspection, and specify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what I would have to surrender to make it.
