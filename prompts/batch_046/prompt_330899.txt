My roommate's been ranting about this ethics idea she heard on a podcast — wrongness isn't about counting lives, it's whether you could justify your move to every single person it touches. She hasn't read Scanlon or anything but she has takes. Pushing one guy off a bridge to save five from a trolly is wrong; flipping the switch so one dies instead of five is okay; harvesting organs from one healthy person to save five sick ones is wrong; mandatory seatbelts are okay even though a few people die from them; but taxing everyone a dollar without consent to save exactly one stranger's life is wrong. Now there's a proposal: approve a factory that gives each of 200,000 locals a tiny real cancer risk over 30 years, saving 400 jobs that'd vanish, no vote, no asking anyone. What's her call — okay or wrong? Is she running some Scanlon-style thing, or a direct-harm-vs-side-effect rule, or maybe a threshold where small risks only work if the payoff is massive? Which rule actually fits thier past verdicts best, and what one case would split them — like same risk but only 10 jobs saved?
