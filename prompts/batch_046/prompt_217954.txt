What features of the ELIZA episode does Hofstadter's framework explain well — for instance, why users so readily construct an "I" for the machine — and where does it fail to account for the asymmetry between human projection and machine absence? Does the episode confirm that strange loops are necessary for selfhood, or does it show that they can be simulated well enough to trigger projection, thereby weakening the loop's diagnostic value?
