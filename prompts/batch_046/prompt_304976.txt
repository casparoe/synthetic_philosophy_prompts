Over the past year I've traveled extensively for my job and found myself repeatedly unsettled by practices in other societies that don't match my own, yet I can't tell whether my discomfort means I've found something genuinely wrong or just something unfamiliar. In one community, parents arrange marriages for their children as young as sixteen; my instinct is to call this wrong, though I realize my own culture imposes plenty of unspoken rules about who should date whom. I witnessed religious scarification that left permanent marks, and while it disturbed me, I eventually decided it was no different from my friends getting cosmetic tattoos or plastic surgery—strange to me, but not immoral. Then I read about a group that practices cannibalism only after death, consuming small portions of deceased relatives as an act of love and remembrance; I felt an immediate revulsion, yet I couldn't defend why this is worse than my culture's practice of embalming bodies with chemicals and sealing them in metal caskets. At the same time, I saw Western tourists loudly condemn local food customs while eating factory-farmed meat themselves, and that hypocrisy seemed clearly wrong in a way the local custom was not. There's also a society where elderly people are left in remote areas to die alone when they become a burden; this horrified me, though I understand the brutal logic of scarce resources. I recall once encountering a writer—perhaps from the Renaissance—who argued that Europeans who tortured living enemies were far more barbaric than cannibals who ate only the dead; that comparison made me wonder whether my judgments are just provincial habits dressed up as morality. I'm trying to figure out if there's one principle—maybe about consent, avoidable suffering, or internal consistency—that sorts my reactions into "merely different" versus "actually wrong," and whether I have to reject some of my gut feelings to make them fit together. What principle, if any, captures the pattern, and what should I say about the case I keep returning to: whether arranged marriage for teenagers is wrong in the same way as leaving the elderly to die, or whether those belong in different categories altogether?
