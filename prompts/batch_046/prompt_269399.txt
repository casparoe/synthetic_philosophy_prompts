I am drafting a three-month fellowship proposal in metaphysics and social ontology, and I want the conceptual framing sharpened into a precise, arguable question. The domain is dispositions, finks, and masks. My half-formed idea is this: the canonical literature treats a mask as an exceptional, external interfering condition that blocks the manifestation of a disposition without removing it, while a fink removes the disposition upon the trigger. This framework was built around physical objects and artefacts. I want to test whether it survives in institutional and collective systems where the blocking conditions are not contingent interferences but constitutive design features — for example, a regulatory architecture deliberately embedded in a market mechanism to suppress certain outputs, where the suppression is part of what makes the institution the kind of system it is. Does this require a new metaphysical category, such as a constitutive mask, or can the existing distinction be extended? If the latter, then the framework applies more broadly than usually recognised; if the former, then the standard treatment was implicitly restricted to non-institutional domains, which sharply limits its scope.

Please write the proposal with the following structure: a precise statement of the question; an explanation of why existing positions in the dispositions literature leave this open, specifically noting the assumption that masks are always external or contingent; the conceptual method you propose, including how you will analyse a structured set of institutional cases against the canonical physical examples and what criteria you will use to test whether the distinction between removal of disposition and blockage of manifestation holds; what a positive result would look like and what would count as the project failing; and a realistic scope appropriate to three months, with the deliverable being a focused article-length argument rather than an empirical study. Please take a committed position: defend either that the framework can be extended without revision, or that it requires a substantive amendment such as the introduction of constitutive masking, giving your reasons rather than surveying options neutrally. Write in British English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
