I am drafting a consistency audit for a philosophy of knowledge seminar focusing on epistemic paradoxes, and I should like you to examine whether the following package of commitments is jointly tenable. Please respond in British English.

Here is where I have landed:

(1) I accept the unrestricted knowability principle: for every true proposition p, it is metaphysically possible that p come to be known.
(2) I endorse epistemic closure, but restrict it to entailments that are immediately transparent to the agent—straightforward applications of modus ponens and the like—while denying it for complex or self-referential logical consequences.
(3) I maintain that there are genuinely unknown contingent truths, such as precise microhistorical facts that have never been recorded.
(4) I reject the critical move in Fitch's derivation, not on the grounds that closure fails generally, but because of a substantive "epistemic transparency" constraint: no agent can be in a position to know both that p obtains and that p is unknown, where this constraint is stronger than simple factivity.
(5) I treat conceivability as a fallible yet substantive guide to metaphysical possibility, and I regard it as genuinely conceivable that some particular contingent truth might remain undiscovered in perpetuity.
(6) I hold that epistemic modesty requires the explicit claim that we do not know every truth, and I take this claim to be itself an item of knowledge that does not collapse into omniscience.

Please analyse these commitments pair by pair. Identify which combinations are in genuine tension, which tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and for each genuine tension state how severe it is and what the cheapest revision would be. In particular, address whether (2) and (4) are redundant or in conflict, whether (5) undermines (1), and whether (6) generates a hidden loop with (3). Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. Conclude by saying which single revision would eliminate the most inconsistencies and what that revision costs me conceptually.
