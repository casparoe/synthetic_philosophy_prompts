I have been noticing a recurring argumentative structure in debates about metaphysical fundamentality, and I am unsure whether it has a precise name. Here are two concrete instances. First, in philosophy of mathematics, a theorist argues that arithmetic is grounded in set theory—say, that numbers depend for their existence on particular set-theoretic constructions. When an interlocutor objects that sets themselves seem individuated by cardinality and therefore presuppose numerical properties, the theorist does not accept circular or mutual dependence. Instead, he distinguishes two directions of priority: sets are said to be prior to numbers in an ontological or constructive sense, while numbers are prior to sets only in a descriptive or measuring sense, and he treats these as compatible rather than conflicting. Second, in philosophy of mind, a physicalist claims that conscious states are fully grounded in neural states. When it is pointed out that neural kinds are often classified by functional and behavioral criteria that appear to invoke mental properties, the physicalist responds that neural states are metaphysically prior, whereas mental categories are merely conceptually or methodologically prior to the neural taxonomy; once again, a symmetric-looking dependence is split into two asymmetrical priorities.

What interests me is not bidirectional grounding as such, but the specific pattern in which the theorist assigns the stronger, world-building form of priority to whatever entity he originally wished to defend as fundamental, while relegating the reverse dependence to a weaker, non-ontological register. Is there an established concept for this maneuver—this strategic bifurcation of a single dependence relation into an ontological and a non-ontological variant in order to preserve a predetermined fundamentality ranking? How exactly should it be distinguished from honest grounding pluralism, from mutual grounding, and from the simple claim that different explanatory contexts invoke different directions of dependence? If no established term captures it, what definition and boundary conditions would properly isolate it, and what would be the clearest counterexample that shows a theorist is engaging in genuine pluralism rather than in this protective splitting?
