I'm prepping the final session of my philosophy of mind seminar and we're working through that bit in Hofstadter and Dennett's The Mind's I — you know, the Einstein brain scenario where the brain is perfectly copied into a book and people ask if the book can now think. My students always jump straight to saying paper can't think and miss the weirder problem: the copy reproduces every mental state but never updates any of them, so there's no feedback loop with the world, no error-correction, just static replay in a system that is entirely seperate from reality. The turn should come around line 8, where the speaker realizes being perfectly remembered is not the same as being awake. Make it precise enough that someone who knows the Mind's I dialogue sees the pattern-without-causation worry, but don't turn it into a sermon about AI rights; just leave the tension hanging like our discussion did.
