I keep running into the 'man of his time' defence whenever Jefferson's slaveholding, Churchill's bombing of Dresden, or Columbus's encomienda come up in discussion. But the phrase seems to do different jobs: sometimes people mean he lacked the conceptual tools to see the wrong (an epistemic limit), sometimes they mean the norms themselves were different (a kind of moral relativism), and sometimes they mean we shouldn't blame him for not transcending his era (something like circumstantial moral luck). Can you lay out these readings precisely, say for each of the three figures which reading is even plausable, and then tell me whether any version actually blocks blame without collapsing into 'it was right then'? I want to bring this to a debate and need to know if the slogan is hiding a real distinction or just shutting things down.
