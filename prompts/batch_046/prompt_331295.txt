I've been trying to work out a position on Holocaust uniqueness and comparablity that splits the difference between the old Historikerstreit camps and Bauman's modernity thesis, but with a twist. Roughly: the Holocaust isn't singular just because of industrial scale or death toll, and it's not just a case study in modern bureacratic rationality either. Instead, I think it's structurally incomparable because the Nazi state deliberately engineered a kind of administrative fragmentation — distributing knowledge across competing agencies so that no single actor held the full picture — and coupled that with a systematic attempt to destroy not only people but the very conditions for future testimony (burning archives, euphemistic language, camp designs that obscured the machinery). So the uniqueness lies not in the killing itself but in the state producing an epistemic black hole that makes post-hoc historiography structurally unstable. It's a rupture in sovereignty, not just morality.

What would actually count against this? I want the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward — not just the standard "other genocides also destroyed records" line, but which comparative cases (Rwanda, Armenia, the Herero) genuinely share this epistemic-structure feature, and which don't. I'd also like to know whether the bureacratic-fragmentation claim holds up against actual administrative history: was there really decentralized anarchy, or was the SS/RSHA more centralized than I'm allowing? And what about the fact that tons of documentation survived — does that kill the "epistemic black hole" idea, or can a defender concede partial survival while holding the structural point? Also, is this view just insulating itself: if I say any counter-evidence is just part of the black hole's residue, have I made the position unfalsifiable? Be honest about whether that's a problem for a claim of this kind. Rank everything, say which objections defenders of this view would themselves concede are damaging, and flag the weakest point I should defend if I present this in a seminar.
