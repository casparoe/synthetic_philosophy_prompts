If an advance directive is best understood as a temporal property deed—by which my present, competent self conveys absolute decision-making rights over my body to my future self only on condition that she remains rational, and revokes them should dementia occur—then a demented patient who refuses nutrition is not exercising autonomy but violating the property rights of her earlier owner. Does this mean we should reconceive refusal of care in advanced dementia not as an expression of present welfare but as a kind of temporal trespass? Take a committed stance and defend it: is the earlier self's authority genuinely proprietary and therefore binding regardless of present suffering, or does the property-deed framing collapse because bodily authority cannot be alienated like chattel?
