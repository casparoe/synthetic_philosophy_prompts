Leo replies that Carnap’s separation of logical syntax from pragmatics does exactly the opposite: democratic bodies can legitimately choose ends through non-cognitive preference, while the planning of means becomes a purely technical, empirically rational task guided by inductive logic and predictive efficiency. Leo counters that framework choice in logic is analogous to selecting coordinate systems: it is pragmatically motivated but does not determine empirical outcomes, and once a scientific language is fixed, inductive probabilities are objectively constrained by evidence, leaving values confined to the democratic selection of ends.
