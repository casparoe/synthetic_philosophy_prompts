I recently read an unnamed philosophy blog post summarizing Kripke's argument about rigid designators and the necessary a posteriori. The author writes: "A rigid designator picks out the same object in every possible world where it exists. Because 'Hesperus' and 'Phosphorus' are both rigid, the empirical discovery that they denote Venus makes 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' necessary, even though we did not know it a priori. This refutes descriptivism about proper names: if 'the morning star' meant 'the body visible in the morning,' it could not be rigid, since Venus might never have been visible at dawn. The author then extends the point to scientific kind terms, claiming that 'electron' is rigid and that its identification with negative charge is therefore necessary. From this, the blogger concludes that every successful theoretical reduction produces a necessary a posteriori identity, and—more boldly—that if a theoretical term like 'fermion' can be redefined in a successor physics, its rigidity is only contingent, which undermines the anti-descriptivist argument for scientific kinds. Finally, the summary asserts that Kripke's framework collapses the distinction between metaphysical and epistemic modality, because any empirical discovery about an identity automatically becomes a modal fact."

This feels partially right but too sweeping, and I am unsure where the popularizer is extending Kripke versus misreading him. Could you audit the summary? Please distinguish: (a) the accurate core of the rigidity argument for proper names and the necessary a posteriori; (b) whether the extension to natural-kind terms is fairly represented or overstated; (c) the specific misrepresentation of descriptivism regarding reference-fixing versus meaning-giving descriptions; (d) whether the claim about contingent rigidity under theoretical revision is a coherent extension or a confusion; and (e) whether the final conclusion about collapsing metaphysical and epistemic modality follows from the preceding points. I want to know which parts a careful defender of Kripke's view would accept, which they would correct, and whether the summary's conclusions survive those corrections.
