My study partner came up with this and I'm stuck. (1) On expressivism, saying "stealing is wrong" expresses direct disapproval of stealing, not a belief about some property. (2) But in "If stealing is wrong, then hiding stolen goods is wrong too," that first part is embeded and doesn't actually express disapproval — I'm just supposing it for the sake of argument. That's the Frege-Geach thing. (3) So expressivists need to say what the embedded clause does instead. Most say it expresses being disposed to disapprove of stealing if you're already in that state, or maybe it just picks out the property of wrongness descriptively. (4) Now here's the move that feels like a trap. Take a sentence with double embedding inside a modal: "If it's wrong that it's wrong to kill, then it's obligatory not to kill." The inner "wrong" is negated and embeded inside a conditional, and the whole thing sits inside another conditional. (5) If embedded moral terms always denote properties, then we've got properties like being-the-property-of-being-wrong-that-killing-is-wrong, which means moral language is doing full descriptive work after all, defeating expressivism. (6) If instead they always express conditional dispositions, then understanding this sentence requires having a disposition about my disposition about my attitude toward killing — and with deeper nesting you get an infinite tower of higher-order attitudes. No actual human has that stack. (7) So either way, expressivism collapses: either into hidden descriptivism or into a psychology that's humanly impossible. (8) Therefore the Frege-Geach problem isn't just a puzzle about embedding; it proves that no attitude-based theory of moral language can accomodate even basic modal reasoning about morality.

Which step should I reject? I really want to keep (1)-(3) because the embedding intuition seem's solid. But I can't accept (8). Is the slip in (5) — maybe properties can be mentioned without being descriptively asserted? Or is there a compositional rule in (6) that lets us compute the nested attitude without literally possessing every layer? Or does the modal in (4) change the expressive force so that the regress never starts? What's the least costly fix?
