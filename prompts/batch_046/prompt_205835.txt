I've been thinking about this idea I think is called the singleton hypothesis? Like, eventually there might just be one big decison-making thing running everything on earth, maybe an AI or some global council, not necessarily a government. I think some philosopher named Bostrom wrote about it but I don't know the details and can't look it up right now.

Anyway, I want to pitch a 3-month research thing to a local foundation that funds weird small projects. The question I want funded: if we imagine not the whole world becoming one singleton, but just my neighborhood or a small city slowly merging its decison-making - like 60% of choices are pooled and 40% stay private - does that middle stage have its own special moral problems? Everyone talks about full singletons being good (no war) or bad (no freedom), but nobody studies the messy in-between where some things are shared and some aren't.

The existing positions don't settle it because they treat it as all-or-nothing. I think a philosophical project, not a sociology study, could make progress by figuring out whether partial pooling creates obligations that disappear when you go full-singleton or stay fully independent. What I'd deliver is a practical framework for local organizers: a way to tell when a shared system is still "cooperation" vs when it crosses into "something you should resist." Failure would be if we just say "it depends on context" with no useful line.
