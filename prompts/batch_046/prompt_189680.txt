I've been stuck in this interdisciplinary seminar on American exceptionalism for months and it's driving me a bit mad. We've got a historian going on about the absence of feudalism and federal architecture, a normative theorist treating it as the claim that liberal individualism reaches its best form in the US, and a sociologist insisting it's mainly an ideology that normalises mass incarceration and weak welfare provision. They all agree on the basic facts — yes, more prisoners per capita, yes, an old Constitution — but talk past each other completely. Is this stall just verbal, with 'exceptionalism' covering three seperate comparative relations? Or is there a hidden substantive disagreement about whether these dimensions are causally linked — say, does the federal structure generate the ideology that then permits the carceral state? And there's this comparator problem too: exceptional relative to which rich democracies, and over what time scale? If it's verbal, should we split the concept and stop pretending it's one debate? If it's substantive, what kind of distinction or counterfactual could actually move anyone? I want a diagnosis, not a verdict.
