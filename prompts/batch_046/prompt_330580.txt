I'm preparing discussion material for an advanced undergraduate seminar that treats classical texts as interlocutors in contemporary political philosophy. Rather than using Homer's Odyssey merely as a literary backdrop, I want to read the Sirens episode—where Odysseus commands his crew to stop their ears with wax and to bind him to the mast, so that he alone may hear the fatal song and suffer the temptation without yielding, whilst they row on deaf to his subsequent pleas—as a concrete case for thinking about Ulysses contracts and constitutional precommitment. The standard philosophical move is to see Odysseus as a rational agent pre-binding his future self; I want to push this into the problem of democratic intergenerational binding, where one generation entrenches constitutional rules precisely because it anticipates that future majorities will face temptations structurally analogous to the Sirens.

Read the episode through this lens, but resist reducing the poem to a philosophical illustration. Where does the analogy between the captain binding himself and a polity binding its successors genuinely illuminate constitutional design, and where does it fracture? Consider, for instance, whether Odysseus's dual role—he issues the command to be restrained and also commands the crew to ignore his future counter-commands—maps onto democratic legitimacy, or whether it smuggles in an asymmetry of authority (sovereign captain versus subordinate crew) that makes the model inapplicable to equal citizens. Does the fact that Odysseus retains full sensory access to the temptation, suffering precisely because he is not deafened like the crew, imply that legitimate constitutional constraints should be experienced as visible burdens by those they govern, rather than hidden behind institutional wax? And does the poem resist the rationalist interpretation altogether: might Odysseus's choice be driven less by prudent self-governance than by a desire for dangerous knowledge and the aesthetic of survival, thereby undermining its use as a paradigm of rational constitutional precommitment? I do not require citations or a literature review; I want a rigorous, two-directional critical reading—faithful to the epic's narrative logic and honest about where the philosophical framework distorts or is complicated by what the text actually shows.
