Construct a split-brain thought experiment in the domain of consciousness unity, engineered so that two defined theories yield opposite verdicts on the number of experiencing subjects while agreeing on every observable behavior. Define Theory A, the Real-Time Integrationist view: there is exactly one conscious subject in a brain if and only if mental contents across hemispheres are reciprocally accessible in real time; absent such access, the hemispheres host distinct subjects. Define Theory B, the Behavioral-Unity Autonomy view: each hemisphere sustains its own continuous phenomenal stream whenever direct callosal transmission is absent, and indirect behavioral coordination—however seamless—never merges two streams into one; overt reports of unity are therefore compatible with dual subjecthood. Now build the case, in no more than one paragraph and with no reference to named historical patients or exotic neuroscience: imagine a patient with complete corpus callosotomy performing a novel, simultaneous task—left-hemisphere linguistic labeling of a shape and right-hemisphere spatial rotation of that same shape—linked only by an external auditory loop that delivers each hemisphere’s vocal output to the other with a 400 ms delay, so that phenomenal presence in each hemisphere is never contemporaneous with access to the other’s current content. The patient, using either hand and speaking fluently, sincerely reports, “I just did one thing: I named the shape and turned it together.” Design the scenario so that both theories predict precisely this report, yet Theory A counts one subject (because the delayed loop still constitutes functional reciprocal access, albeit slowed) and Theory B counts two subjects (because direct neural integration is absent and the delay decouples phenomenal streams). After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that the specifications are met: identify the specific content available to each hemisphere, specify the access conditions each theory requires, and show step-by-step why each delivers its verdict on subject-number. Then isolate the structural feature doing the work—the temporal decoupling of phenomenal presence from informational access—and explain what happens if it is removed: does instantaneous feedback collapse the disagreement back into a standard split-brain dispute, or does it eliminate the divergence entirely? Finally, present the strongest objection a defender of the worse-off theory (the one forced to posit an undetectable second subject behind unified behavior) could raise against your scenario, and reply briefly from the opposing side. Do not cite literature, provide reading lists, or use verbatim quotations.
