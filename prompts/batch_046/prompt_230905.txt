i was reading about how in a lot of japanese and korean hospitals doctors still break terminal news to the family first, not the patient, and the family decides whether to tell them. it's basically instutionalized. what view of moral responsibility—or of the self—does that practice presuppose? is it only coherent if you think autonomy is held inside a family role rather than by the individual? and if we take confucian role ethics seriously, would that practice actually be required, or could a straight paternalist view that just uses the family as the decision-unit license it too? i want to know whether the practice is philosophically committed to relational selfhood or if it's actually underdetermined.
