In my metaphysics seminar we've been stuck for weeks on whether finks and masks actually defeat the conditional analysys of dispositons, or if they just force us into ever-more-complex ceteris paribus clauses. Everybody can run Martin's electro-fink example and Johnston's mask/antidote distinction, and yet the conditionalists and the realists about powers haven't budged. I think part of the problem is that one side is asking a semantic question about the logic of counterfactuals while the other is asking a metaphysical question about causal powers, and they keep talking past eachother using the same word — 'disposition' — for both projects. But I want a sharper diagnosis: is this a verbal disagreement, a hidden factual dispute about how properties behave under extreme intervention, or just bedrock intuitions about naturalness that neither camp can defend? And here's a new wrinkle I'm testing — call it a 'recursive mask,' where the blocker itself is disposed to disappear only if a second-order condition triggers. Would forcing both sides to handle layered blocking show that the debate is really about whether dispositions can be hierarchically structured, or is it just another parlor trick that lets everyone retreat to their prior commitments? What kind of stalemate is this, and what — if anything — would actually count as progress?
