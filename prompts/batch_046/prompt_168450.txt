I am writing to you as a retired experimental physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, has begun studying philosophy of science with some seriousness, and I find myself struck by the distance between formal accounts of inductive simplicity and the gritty reality of calibrating detectors. I would like you to place three competing frameworks for theory choice into tiers—first tier for those that genuinely illuminate how scientists ought to reason, second tier for those that are formally elegant but practically limited, and third tier for those that seem likely to mislead—while writing entirely in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, tables, or any other formatting devices, as one might compose a thoughtful letter.

The three frameworks are as follows. First, Solomonoff induction with its universal prior distributed over all computable hypotheses according to algorithmic length on a fixed reference machine. Second, the direct application of Kolmogorov complexity to empirical model selection, in which the best physical law is taken to be the shortest program that reproduces the observed data. Third, a criterion I have been developing privately, which I shall call the instrument-calibration measure: on this view, the simplicity of a theory is not the bit-length of its description but rather the total physical work—energy expended in adjusting dials, cooling sensors, and isolating signals—required to constrain each of its free parameters against background instrumental noise.

For each framework, assign it a tier and then, in a single flowing narrative, explain your reasoning. In particular, I want you to address two concerns that arise from my own experience with noisy measurements. How does the framework accommodate old evidence, that is, observational data that was already known when the theory was formulated? And is its measure of simplicity invariant under changes in descriptive language, programming notation, or system of units, or does it depend on an arbitrary representational choice? I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations of any kind; I seek only your direct reasoning, presented in uninterrupted prose.
