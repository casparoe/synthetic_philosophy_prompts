In a graduate seminar on Herbert Butterfield’s critique of Whig historiography, a fellow student defended organizing narratives around the emergence of modern liberal democratic institutions by invoking the familiar line, “the arc of the moral universe bends toward justice.” She meant it not as theological prophecy but as methodological license: because history exhibits, however fitfully, a directional tendency toward greater justice, we are entitled to read the past as an uneven convergence on present norms, treating setbacks as temporary deviations rather than genuine historical alternatives. I am trying to understand exactly what philosophical machinery that sentence smuggles in, especially since it was used to brush aside my worry that selecting past events for their contribution to modern values is a retrospective coherence bias rather than an explanatory principle.

Could you disambiguate the distinct precise theses the line might stand for—descriptive statistical claims about long-run trends in violence or rights, normative claims that justice operates as an historical attractor, metaphysical claims about an immanent teleology in human affairs, and rhetorical moves that conflate outcome-dependent selection with causal direction—indicating for each whether it is a trivial platitude, a substantive but defensible position, or plainly false? In particular, say which reading actually licenses the historiographical practice she endorsed, which ones are compatible with Butterfield’s warning that framing the past as progress toward the present projects our values backward onto agents who did not hold them, and which depend on identifying “progress” only ex post facto. Please do not merely catalog possibilities; take a committed stance on which disambiguations survive scrutiny, which collapse into one another upon inspection, and which precise claim is almost certainly being invoked when someone uses the phrase to dismiss concerns about hindsight bias or narrative fallacy in writing history as inevitable moral improvement.
