I am a software engineer without formal philosophy training. Our organization approves high-risk database migrations through a three-person peer-review panel, and I need a reusable decision procedure—ordered steps with explicit tests—grounded in the Condorcet jury theorem. The procedure should determine when a majority panel verdict is reliable enough to authorize a migration and when it should be overridden or expanded. It must handle realistic reviewer dependence: reviewers frequently share the same static-analysis suite, complete identical internal training modules, and have previously co-authored portions of the schema, so errors are correlated rather than independent. Provide worked applications for two cases: first, two reviewers approve and one rejects, but the two approvers both relied on the same automated scanner that missed an edge case; second, all three approve using different verification methods, but one reviewer has a documented history of systematically overestimating migration safety. At each step, flag where the rule ends and unavoidable human judgment begins. Write in American English. End with a one-sentence summary of the procedure's central limitation on its own line.
