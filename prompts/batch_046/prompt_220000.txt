hey, i'm drafting some notes for a philosophy of mind reading group programme and would really value some comments before i send them round. working title: 'Dreams as experience-apt states — somewhere between Malcolm and Dennett'.

the basic idea: Malcolm's classic line is that dreams can't count as experiences proper because there's no criteron of correctness for reporting them — you wake up and tell a story, but during sleep there's nothing that could verify or falsify your claim about what you 'experienced'. Dennett pushed this further with his cassette idea and later narrative models — the dream isn't an occurrent inner movie but something loaded or assembled at the point of waking.

i want to defend a middle position using what i'm calling counterfactual phenomenal anchoring. the claim is that a dream-state possesses phenomenal character not because it's fully occurrent (Malcolm's demand) nor because it's retroactively narrated (Dennett's view), but because the sleeping brain sits in a dispositional structure that would generate specific qualitative reports if the sleeper were interrupted during particular REM micro-phases. so the dream isn't an experience happening right now in the full-blooded sense; rather, its experience-apt — anchored in real neural dispositions with counterfactual experiential outputs.

this lets us keep something like phenomenal content without committing to an occurrent inner show. but its also got a weird consequence: if being experience-apt is sufficient, then deeply anaesthetised patients might also qualify, since they hold dispositional structures that could yield reports upon waking (even if they usually don't). i actually think this is a feature, not a bug — it blurs the boundary between dreaming and anaesthetic unconsciousness in a way that fits clinical intuition.

1. am i being fair to Malcolm's verificationist argument, or have i misrepresented the criteron bit?
2. is counterfactual phenomenal anchoring just Dennett's narrative model with extra steps?
