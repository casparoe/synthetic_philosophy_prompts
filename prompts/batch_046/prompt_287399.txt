I'm convening the final session of a small philosophy-of-history seminar that has been working through the problem of periodization, and I'd like to close with something other than a standard position paper. We've been arguing over whether the canonical triad—Middle Ages, Renaissance, Scientific Revolution—names real ontological breaks or is instead a retrospective taxonomy invented by later historians. For this session, I want a poem that does the philosophical work structurally rather than just illustrating it. Write a Petrarchan sonnet with an octave in enclosed rhyme (ABBAABBA) and a sestet with a more open pattern (CDECDE or CDCDCD), and with a sharp volta between lines 8 and 9. The octave should present the familiar narrative almost as a myth of origins: long medieval darkness, a sudden humanist rebirth, then a seventeenth-century rupture that delivers modern science and self-conscious modernity. The volta should pivot to the historiographical suspicion that these boxes are imposed—'Middle Ages' originally a humanist insult meant to mark a gap, 'Renaissance' largely a projection shaped by a nineteenth-century historian's imagination, and 'Scientific Revolution' a label applied retrospectively by twentieth-century scholars to scattered local practices rather than to a unified event that its participants recognized. The rhyme scheme should mirror the content: the octave's closed rhymes should feel like a sealed epochal system, while the sestet's looser interlacing should suggest how retrospective labeling unravels the boundaries between centuries. Do not resolve the tension in the final lines; leave open whether lived experience ever actually inhabited these categories, or whether they are only ever narratives told backward.

After the poem, please provide a brief note stating how confident you are in the historical and philosophical claims it makes—particularly the claim that these three periods are historiographical constructs rather than natural kinds—and identify the specific point about which you are least sure, whether that concerns the historiography of the Scientific Revolution, the ontology of historical kinds, or the formal execution of the sonnet itself.
