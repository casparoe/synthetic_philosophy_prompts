I’m drafting a novel about extinction and was sitting in on a writing workshop where two other writers got stuck arguing about whether human extinction is bad in some special way beyond just everyone dying. Maya said it’s basically just the death toll summed up — if every person dies but there’s no extra suffering, the badness is just the loss of each individual life, period. Leo brought up this philosopher Scheffler and his afterlife conjecture — not about actual heaven, but the idea that our current projects and values depend on humanity continuing after us, so extinction robs present lives of meaning even for people who’d be long dead. Maya said that’s just romantic mysticism, we’re treating the human story like it has intrinsic value when only individual lives do. Leo answered that it’s not mysticism, it’s a structural feature of how we value things — like how writing a novel assumes there will be readers, or science assumes future verfication. Maya shot back that this just describes psychological dependancy, not moral weight: I depend on electricity too but that doesn’t mean blackouts are cosmically tragic. Leo said Scheffler’s point is stronger — we wouldn’t just miss future people, we’d find our own activities hollow if we knew they were the end of the line. Maya replied that hollow feelings aren’t the same as objective harm, and conflating them is a catagory mistake. They turned to me since I’m just listening and asked me to call it. I honestly don’t know who’s right. Please explain who’s right about what specific point, who is more right overall, and what both of them are missing.
