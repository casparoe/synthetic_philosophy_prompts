I am drafting a contribution for a graduate reading group on conservative political philosophy, and I should like a rigorous consistency audit before I present my position. The view I wish to defend combines a Burkean account of institutional development with a strong reading of Chesterton's fence: I hold that legitimate social arrangements grow organically through prescription and accumulated practice, and that no institutional barrier should be removed until we understand the specific danger it was designed to prevent. Yet I also maintain that several major modern reforms — specifically, the abolition of property qualifications for parliamentary suffrage and the transition from mixed hereditary-elected chambers to fully elected legislatures — do not violate this principle, because in these instances the institution had already become what I term "symbolic scaffolding": it retained formal existence but had long since lost its functional role, so its removal was the clearing of deadwood rather than the tearing down of a living fence. Crucially, I want to claim that the distinction between living fence and symbolic scaffolding can be made through the accumulated practical wisdom of the community itself, without appealing to abstract rationalist criteria such as equality or efficiency; once this organic recognition has genuinely occurred, I further hold that rapid, deliberate legislative action is not only permissible but obligatory, since artificial delay would itself obstruct natural development. At the same time, I deny that this collapses into liberal progressivism, arguing that the tempo of reform remains tethered to the prior pace of organic recognition rather than to any independent normative theory.

Please examine this package of commitments for internal coherence. I am not asking for an exposition of Burke or Chesterton, but for a point-by-point audit: which of these claims genuinely conflict, which conflicts are merely apparent and dissolve upon closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally damaging. In particular, I want to know whether my concept of "symbolic scaffolding" can bear the weight I place on it without covertly importing the rationalist framework I claim to reject, and whether my allowance for rapid reform after organic recognition is compatible with the Burkean suspicion of deliberate reconstruction. Please reply in British English.
