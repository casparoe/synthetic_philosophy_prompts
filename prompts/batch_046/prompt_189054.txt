The focal problem is Otto Neurath’s doctrine that picture languages (Bildersprachen) are metaphysically neutral instruments of scientific communication, in contrast to word languages that invite speculative ontology. Standard scholarship examines whether pictograms can express logical relations, or whether Isotype succeeds as an international auxiliary; I want to open a less-traveled line. Neurath’s icons systematically represent entities as spatio-temporally continuous and observationally accessible surfaces, which suggests an implicit “surface ontology” that sits uneasily with his explicit physicalism and his rejection of phenomenalist metaphysics. The precise research question is whether this representational bias is merely a pragmatic feature of visual pedagogy, or whether it functions as a covert ontological commitment internal to the picture-language format itself, and whether the distinction between representational format and ontological commitment can be maintained without collapsing into the very word-language metaphysics Neurath seeks to avoid.
