Compose a satirical editorial-policy document of approximately 600 words, written in the deadpan bureaucratic voice of a university-press Instructions for Contributors page, for a fictional journal entitled The Proceedings of Non-Essentialist Inquiry. The journal’s editorial board has adopted Wittgenstein’s concept of family resemblance—together with the associated notion of language games—as its sole methodological and administrative framework. Present, with complete institutional seriousness, submission guidelines that forbid definitions by necessary and sufficient conditions; require that every technical term be introduced through a contextualized language-game rather than through a glossary entry or fixed definition; and mandate that taxonomic distinctions be replaced by maps of overlapping similarities. The satire should target the practical overextension of family-resemblance reasoning into domains where precise demarcation is genuinely indispensable—specifically peer-review criteria, plagiarism detection, and disciplinary boundary-keeping—without ever breaking character to announce the objection explicitly. Include an appendix or sidebar titled Notes on the Language Game of Editorial Rejection, in which the journal explains that a rejection letter need not share any essential feature with other rejection letters and that authors should not expect consistency in evaluation standards. The piece should be readable by someone familiar with Wittgenstein’s discussion of family resemblance in Philosophical Investigations, but it must not reproduce verbatim passages from that work; paraphrase all philosophical points in your own administrative jargon. Maintain a polished, professorial register throughout, as though you are drafting this mock document for a graduate seminar on conceptual analysis; the target is a style of discourse that treats family resemblance as a universal solvent, not any named individual.
