I just finished a long and somewhat exhausting conversation with a friend about the limits of tolerance, and I am trying to pin down a distinction that kept surfacing but that neither of us could name. Here is the pattern. Imagine a public forum governed not by a substantive commitment to liberal values but by a purely procedural rule: every serious position gets a seat and a microphone, because the legitimacy of the forum is said to come from that very inclusiveness. Now an intolerant movement enters, but its argument is not merely that its opponents are wrong; it is that the procedural rule itself is a symptom of decadence and must be replaced by a hierarchical decision procedure. The movement demands not just space to speak, but the institutional mechanisms—voting rights, agenda-setting authority, veto powers—that the procedural rule distributes. Once it acquires those mechanisms, it uses them to dismantle the rule that granted them. The defenders of the forum then face a peculiar bind: to deny the movement those mechanisms is to violate the procedural rule that defines the forum, yet to grant them is to allow the rule to be destroyed from within. This is not quite the familiar paradox in which a tolerant society must tolerate the intolerant and thereby risk being destroyed by them, because here the danger is not external suppression but internal procedural capture: the intolerant group exploits the distributive logic of the system rather than overwhelming it from outside. I have seen versions of this in debates about platform moderation, academic governance, and constitutional design, but I do not know whether philosophers have isolated this specific structure. Does it have an established name? If not, I would like you to propose one, with a precise definition and clear boundary cases that distinguish it from the standard paradox of tolerance, from a simple prisoner's dilemma among institutions, and from the strategic use of liberal norms by illiberal actors. I am not looking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature; I want the conceptual carving—what exactly this is, where its edges lie, and what it is not. Please reply in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as in a letter.
