(3) But since M itself is countable, in the metatheory we can enumerate every member of that P(ω) with natural numbers, so its actually countable. (4) So "uncountable" isn't an intrinsic property of the set; its relative to the model you're in.

(6) I heard that second-order logic can pin down ω up to isomorphism, but since we actually work with first-order ZFC, that doesn't help us here. (7) So for any claim like "the reals are uncountable," its consistent with our axioms that there's a larger model where thats false. (8) If there's always a larger model that contradicts our cardinality claims, then there's no absolute fact of the matter about them. (10) So first-order logic can't secure either standard arithmetic or genuine uncountability.
