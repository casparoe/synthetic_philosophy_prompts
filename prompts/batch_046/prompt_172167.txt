Consider the institutional practice—most clearly visible in the European Union's mechanisms for democratic backsliding and in proposed cosmopolitan enlargement frameworks—where a state's membership is not a binary condition secured by treaty signature alone, but a graded status maintained through periodic "institutional convergence audits." Under this practice, a state that systematically departs from republican governance is not expelled as an enemy or treated as an external threat; instead, it remains inside the association in a suspended or "incomplete" condition, with its sovereignty treated as provisional and conditional upon progressive domestic reform. At the same time, the association continues to extend cooperative benefits and security guarantees to that very state, as if membership were already irrevocable. This strikes me as philosophically odd: we treat sovereignty as both absolute, worthy of protection, and as revocable through internal audit, not worthy of full respect.
