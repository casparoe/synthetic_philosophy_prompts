The discursive dilemma—where premise-by-premise aggregation of group judgments can yield a conclusion that conflicts with direct conclusion-based aggregation—has persisted in social choice theory and democratic theory since the 1990s without clear resolution. Defenders of premise-based rules argue that collective belief should respect logical consistency, even if that means overriding the majority view on the final conclusion; advocates of conclusion-based rules argue that democratic equality requires treating each member's conclusion as equally authoritative, and that imposing logical structure privileges formal reasoning over substantive disagreement.
