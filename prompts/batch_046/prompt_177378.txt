You serve on a judicial oversight panel that reviews culpability assessments in a pilot sentencing program using cortical-intervention monitors: implanted devices that can suppress motor initiation of prohibited acts but, in every file you review, have never actually fired during the offense. Your task is to translate the philosophical analysis behind counterexamples to the principle of alternative possibilities—those involving counterfactual interveners—into a reusable decision procedure that a non-philosopher reviewer can complete in forty-five minutes. Structure it as an ordered checklist of no more than eight steps, distinguishing actual from merely counterfactual interference, testing whether a preliminary indicator of decision was present and causally effective, and marking every point where interpretation of neural-state reports must substitute for conceptual reasoning—label these explicitly as judgment calls that require senior-jurist co-signature. Fix the practical parameters: you process roughly thirty files per month, you lack direct access to raw neural traces, an erroneous retention of culpability can trigger appellate reversal, and your recommendation is not final until co-signed. Apply the checklist to three worked cases: (a) a defendant who committed fraud while the monitor was powered but never activated; (b) a defendant for whom the monitor fired once during an unrelated prior episode but was fully dormant during the offense; and (c) a defendant evaluated by a synthetic predictive-intervention system that detects intent-forming neural patterns with 99.7 percent accuracy and can preemptively suppress them, yet in this instance the prediction was generated while the suppression protocol was algorithmically withheld—this is the case where your procedure should yield that culpability is undermined despite the absence of any physical intervention, a result you should present as unwelcome given the board's default punitive posture. For each case, identify the deciding step and the unavoidable judgment call. Conclude by stating precisely what the procedure is structurally incapable of resolving, especially regarding higher-order mental states and the metaphysical meaning of being able to act otherwise within deterministic neural architectures.
