Consider the thought experiment in Hofstadter's dialogue Ant Fugue, in which an ant colony named Aunt Hillary exhibits such intricate, goal-directed coordination that it appears to possess a single unified consciousness—analogous to the way neurons in a brain yield one mind. Now suppose a contemporary researcher demonstrates that the colony's behavior is generated entirely by local pheromone rules with no central integrator and no global workspace: each ant reacts only to its immediate neighbors, yet the collective still solves complex problems. How do you think Leibniz would have assessed this case? Drawing on his account of monads, bare perceptions, and dominant monads, would he be inclined to treat Aunt Hillary as a genuine substance with her own primitive representations of the world, or as a well-founded phenomenon—real and orderly, yet lacking any substantial unity beyond its constituent ants? Furthermore, if he granted that the colony has something like thought, would he regard those thoughts as genuinely about the environment in a representational sense, or merely as the analogical echo of countless individual appetitions?
