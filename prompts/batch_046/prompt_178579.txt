I've been arguing with my roomate about WWI blame and I keep running into the same weird pattern that I can't name. So there's the Fischer controversy — that Germany deliberately pushed for a world war — and then there's the counter that everyone was trapped in alliance systems and mobilization timetables, so blaming one nation is like blaming a match for a fire in a room full of gas. But here's what bugs me: in actual arguments, people don't pick one framework and stick to it. Instead they blend them asymetrically. With Germany, critics will say "the blank check was an intentional escalation" (that's specific choice) AND "German militarism made conflict inevitable" (that's systemic culture), and they present both as proof of guilt, like they're two independant strikes. But when you turn to Russia or Austria, suddenly the same people switch to ONLY the systemic excuse — "they had to mobilize because of alliance commitments" — or ONLY point to one specific mistake, but they never stack systemic and agential blame together for anyone except Germany. So it's not just that people disagree on Fischer; it's that the very rules for what counts as being responsible seem to shift depending on which flag is in the crosshairs.

Is there an actual concept for this? I don't mean simple hypocrisy or double standards — I mean the specific move where you combine structural and intentional explanations into one blame-package for one actor, while allowing other actors to hide behind whichever explanation is more excusing in each case. Maybe it's just part of what historians call the Fischer controversy, but that sounds like it's about evidence for German war aims, not about the logic of blame itself. Or is there something in philosophy of history about how nations can be responsible in different senses at once? If there's no established term, what should I call it — "blame-bundling," "asymmetric responsibility framing"? And where exactly does it differ from ordinary bias: is the key feature that the two explanations are logically independent but presented as mutually reinforcing for one side only?
