Within epistemology, the luminosity thesis holds that whenever one is in a mental state such as being in pain, one is in a position to know it; the familiar anti-luminosity argument challenges this by appealing to margin-for-error cases in gradual transitions. The KK principle—that knowing p entails being in a position to know that one knows p—typically collapses if luminosity fails. Now consider a deliberately narrower framing: “thin” epistemic agents that possess only graded credences and lack explicit meta-representational architecture, for whom any failure of self-knowledge stems not from vagueness but from structural opacity between first-order tracking and second-order assessment. Given this framing, which of the following five conceptual projects should be pursued first to determine whether any non-binary revision of KK can survive for such agents: (a) whether graded confidence can substitute for knowledge in the KK schema; (b) whether the anti-luminosity argument generalizes to all systems lacking explicit meta-cognition; (c) how to formalize “being in a position to know” for agents without propositional attitudes; (d) whether the failure of luminosity undermines first-person authority in thin agents; or (e) whether empirical studies of metacognition in non-human animals can adjudicate the conceptual issue? Rank these exactly 1 through 5 according to which are upstream of the rest, which are tractable with purely conceptual tools, and which would actually change the assessment of KK for thin agents if settled. For each ranked item, provide one sentence explaining its dependency relation to the others and one sentence assessing tractability. Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items in that order, each consisting of two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
