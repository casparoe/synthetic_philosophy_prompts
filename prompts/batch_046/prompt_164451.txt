I'm putting together a six-month dissertation prospectus on Lockes education theory. The standard line is that Some Thoughts Concerning Education is just his empiricism applied — shape the senses, then reason. But I want to chase a tension nobody frames this way: Locke insists education should form habits long before the child has continuous conscious memory, yet in the Essay he ties personal identity exactly to that continuous consciousness. So does his pedagogy secretly depend on a kind of embodied or habitual self that his metaphysics cant allow? The existing literature keeps the two books separate, so theres no answer. I need a precise question: if habituation builds character below awareness, what kind of persistence does the educated subject have under Lockes official view? Approach: close read Some Thoughts alongside the Essay chapter on identity, tracking habit, memory, and consciousness, then check his correspondence for any admission he noticed the clash. Deliverable: either a reconstructed implicit theory of embodied identity in Locke, or an argument that his education collapses into conditioning with no personal continuity. Failure would be finding he just never thought about it and theres no philosophical payoff in forcing the connection. Realistic scope: two months on texts, two on mapping the tension, two drafting.
