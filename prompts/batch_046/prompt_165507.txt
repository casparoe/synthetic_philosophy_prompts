Two members of my decision-theory seminar, Mara and Jonas, argued over the Pasadena paradox—the variant of the St. Petersburg game in which the payoff schedule is arranged so that conditioning on even-numbered rounds and on odd-numbered rounds yields opposing infinite expectations, leaving the unconditional expectation genuinely undefined rather than merely infinite. Mara said this is a direct counterexample to expected-utility maximization: if a rational agent faces this gamble, there is no well-defined EU to maximize, so the theory either delivers no verdict or implicitly picks one conditioning frame arbitrarily. Jonas replied that undefined expectation is not a failure of EU theory but a boundary condition: coherence constraints apply only where probabilities and utilities are jointly defined, and in this artificial case they are not. Mara countered that this defense is ad hoc—the paradox can be embedded in a larger decision tree where the agent pays a small fee to learn the round-parity before playing, so that EU applies cleanly inside each branch, yet the ex-ante evaluation of entering the tree remains undefined, which suggests the problem is structural rather than a simple domain restriction. Jonas answered that ex-ante evaluation in such trees should use the law of total expectation applied branch by branch, and if that law breaks down here, it shows only that the agent should not take the ex-ante view, not that coherence is violated; he added that real agents have bounded cognitive resources, so requiring evaluation of undefined expressions is descriptively unrealistic. Mara then claimed Jonas was conflating descriptive limitations with normative failure: if coherence arguments for EU maximization are genuinely normative, they must either extend to these cases or explain precisely why they need not, and Jonas had done neither. Jonas said he had—the consistency constraint applies only over determinable comparative judgments, and where no comparison is determinable, consistency is vacuously satisfied. They asked me to referee, and I could not.
