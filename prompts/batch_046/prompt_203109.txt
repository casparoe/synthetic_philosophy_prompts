The idea is half-formed: Libet-style readiness-potential experiments are standardly interpreted as showing that conscious volition is an epiphenomenal postscript to neural processes that have already initiated action. But nearly every philosophical treatment assumes the relevant "decision" is an instantaneous mental event, so the only live question becomes whether consciousness precedes or follows some neural trigger. I want to develop what I'll call an interval model of agency—treating decision not as a point-instant but as a temporally distributed process with internal feedback loops—so that the temporal gap between RP onset and reportable awareness doesn't demonstrate causal inertness of consciousness, but instead reveals that one stage of a multi-stage process becomes accessible to report at a particular point. The project asks whether this interval model can be articulated precisely enough to avoid collapsing into either a standard compatibilist story (where consciousness is just one causal link in a deterministic chain) or a libertarian story (where consciousness initiates ex nihilo), and whether adopting it actually changes what the neuroscience threatens.
