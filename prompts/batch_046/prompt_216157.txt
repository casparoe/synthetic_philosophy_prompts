Write a research proposal for a focused philosophical project of roughly four months, suitable as a dissertation chapter or a workshop paper. The guiding question is this: Spinoza’s democratic theory is usually read either as a proto-liberal defense of collective sovereignty or as an exclusionary system in which only fully rational agents participate, with his own restrictions on women and non-citizens treated either as dispensable historical baggage or as metaphysically necessary. I want to investigate a third possibility that neither literature treats systematically—whether Spinoza’s distinction between adequate ideas (grounded in common notions) and inadequate imaginative knowledge can function not as a gatekeeping criterion for democratic membership, but as an internal structural constraint on democratic deliberation itself. In other words, can democratic institutions be designed, within a strictly Spinozist framework, to systematically privilege reasoning from common notions over imaginative agreement, without that design collapsing into an authoritarian epistemocracy?

State the precise research question, including the condition under which the answer would count as genuinely affirmative rather than merely metaphorical. Explain why the standard liberal-recovery reading and the exclusion-as-metaphysical-consistency reading each leave this specific structural question open, and identify the conceptual gap each misses—particularly whether the epistemic hierarchy in the Ethics operates at the level of who may enter the assembly or at the level of how collective decisions are formed. Outline your method: a close conceptual reconstruction of the relevant arguments in the Ethics (the theory of common notions, the transition from reason to intuitive knowledge, and the relation between adequate ideas and collective power) and in the Tractatus Politicus (the democratic chapters and the conditions for stable sovereignty), paired with a normative analysis of whether any institutional mechanism could realize this epistemic hierarchy without violating Spinoza’s own commitment to the maximization of collective power. Specify what the possible conclusions are—for instance, that no such mechanism is conceptually available within Spinoza’s metaphysics; that it is available but requires exclusions he did not anticipate; or that it dissolves the membership problem by redefining democratic legitimacy entirely—and say exactly which textual or conceptual considerations would decide between them.

Give an honest scope assessment: which portions of the Ethics and the political treatises can actually be covered rigorously in four months; where the project risks expanding uncontrollably (for example, by importing modern deliberative-democracy literature in full rather than using it selectively as a contrast case); and what a minimal but still meaningful deliverable would look like if the larger institutional-design question proves intractable within the time available. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation apparatus; refer to positions and arguments by descriptive name only, and indicate where primary-source references will be inserted.
