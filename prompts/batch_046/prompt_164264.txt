I am designing an opening exercise for a graduate seminar on vagueness, and I want a poem that does not merely illustrate the sorites paradox but structurally enacts it, so that the form itself becomes an argument. Please compose a poem of roughly thirty-five to forty-five lines, using a modified sestina structure with six rotating end-words—"hair," "scalp," "bald," "grain," "sand," and "edge"—but with a twist: in each successive stanza, one end-word is replaced by a near-homophone or morphological neighbor ("scalp" becoming "scalpel," "edge" becoming "hedge"), such that over six stanzas the lexical anchors have drifted almost imperceptibly. The poem should open with an image of a head that is unambiguously bald and close with an image of a head that is unambiguously covered in hair, yet it must contain no single line where the reader can identify the precise boundary of transition; instead, the shift should occur in the interstices between stanzas, like the hidden cutoff that epistemicism posits but denies we can locate. Within this drift, include one stanza of rigid, unbroken metrical regularity that suddenly fractures—this should stand in for the epistemicist claim that there is a sharp line, just one we cannot cognitively access—followed by a stanza in which line breaks split compound words and punctuation dissolves, representing the supervaluationist idea that the predicate "bald" simply lacks the precision to apply across a continuous range. Do not name these positions; let the formal disruptions carry the philosophical weight, and render the conceptual moves precisely enough that a specialist in vagueness recognizes them rather than seeing mere name-dropping.

After the poem, please provide a brief note—no more than one paragraph—identifying any liberties taken with the epistemicist and supervaluationist accounts or contested interpretations relied upon, and then state explicitly how confident you are in the success of the formal analogy, singling out the specific stanza transition that you regard as the weakest or most speculative point in the mapping between form and concept.
