Me and my friend Jay were going back and forth all night about Du Bois saying all art is propaganda — I think he wrote that essay in the 20s? Anyway, I tried to figure out what I actually think by running through cases, and Im all over the place.

So heres my gut. A poem that straight-up argues Black people deserve dignity and paints it with real craft — yes, thats exactly the good kind, propaganda and art together. A jazz record from the 20s with no lyrics, just musicians playing in this incredibly disciplined, innovative way — my gut says thats propaganda too, because the sound itself is arguing for Black genius, but Jay says Im stretching it and its just music. A really well-written family novel about Black life that never touches race or politics — I want to say thats a failure by Du Bois lights, like its hiding its duty behind prettiness, but Jay thinks any truthful depiction is propaganda automatically. Then theres a modern protest poster with bold lettering over a stock photo, designed to go viral — I say thats propaganda but barely art, too cheap, but Jay says Du Bois wouldnt care about art status if the message is right. And this old painting I saw in a museum, done by a Black artist in the 30s, showing a wealthy white patron smiling kindly — it feels wrong to me, like propaganda for the wrong side, even though its technically beautiful. But I cant quite say why its different from an abstract sculpture by a Black artist today that has no title and no obvious message — I go back and forth on whether that sculpture counts. Sometimes I think its propaganda just by existing in a white space, other times I think Du Bois would hate it for being too obscure.

Is there a principle that gets most of these? Maybe something about whose reality the work centers, or whether the propaganda is truthful versus flattering power? If your principle saves the poem and the jazz record, does it have to toss my verdict on the family novel or the patron painting? Be honest about which ones have to go, and say what it predicts for cases I didnt mention — like a Black filmmaker making a thriller with no racial plot, or a white artist painting Black joy. Also, please seperate whats pretty settled about Du Bois view from whats genuinely contested by people who study this, and dont present one contested reading as if its the consensus. I dont need citations or a reading list, just the idea.
