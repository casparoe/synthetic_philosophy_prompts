I am drafting a chapter on Dennett's real-pattern criterion for adopting the intentional stance, and I have been tracking my supervisor's implicit judgments across biological and artificial systems. Over our discussions she has consistently maintained the following: neurotypical human adults warrant the intentional stance; octopuses engaged in novel problem-solving do as well; a honeybee hive optimizing collective foraging routes does not, since the design stance already captures all projectible patterns; a conventional symbolic chess program does not; a cross-domain embodied reinforcement-learning agent that develops distributed, non-modular representations does; and a hypothetical perfect behavioral duplicate of a human implemented as an exhaustive lookup table with no internal dynamics does not. She treats these six judgments as jointly consistent and has resisted reducing them to either pure functionalism or pure evolutionary essentialism, but she has not articulated the governing principle.

The case I now need her verdict on is a synthetic cortical organoid cultivated in vitro with no evolutionary history, no engineered task objective, and no competitive learning regime. Through biochemical self-assembly alone, it spontaneously develops functional architectures isomorphic to human prefrontal networks and produces context-sensitive, adaptive responses to sensory perturbation that resist decomposition into reflex arcs or fixed algorithmic rules. Its organizational maintenance is endogenous—representational coherence is sustained by its own metabolic dynamics rather than by external feedback loops—yet it was never selected for representational accuracy.

What should I predict she will conclude? I am less interested in the bare verdict than in the competing rules that accommodate her six prior judgments but diverge on the organoid. For each plausible rule, state whether it licenses the intentional stance here and why. Then identify the rule she is most likely running, with your reasoning for preferring it over the rivals, and specify what further case—not among those listed above—would most sharply distinguish the two leading candidates.
