I have noticed a particular pattern in arguments about cosmic fine-tuning and human extinction risk that I cannot name. When discussing why the universe permits observers, speakers often treat our existence as evidence favouring conditions that allow observers — what I understand as self-indication. Yet when the same speakers turn to the doomsday argument, they treat themselves as randomly sampled members of the reference class of all humans who will ever live, which presupposes self-sampling. The shift between these two sampling assumptions is rarely acknowledged, and each is used precisely where it supports the speaker's conclusion about multiverses or about humanity's future lifespan. Is there an established concept for this selective pairing — applying self-indication in cosmological reasoning but self-sampling in anthropic prediction — and does it relate to the presumptuous philosopher problem, or is it a distinct failure of consistency? If no specific term exists, how should one define its boundaries so that it is not collapsed into ordinary reference-class ambiguity or into simple contradiction?
