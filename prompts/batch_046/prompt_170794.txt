You are advising a transitional-justice mediator who must decide, on a recurring basis, whether to deploy a small external security contingent to enforce rulings issued by local customary councils in a region where formal state courts have collapsed. The mediator cannot rewrite rulings; she can only choose whether to lend coercive backing. Her mandate is informed by a Pascalian framework: justice is what is established by legitimate convention but remains powerless without enforcement, while force is necessary to make justice effective yet becomes tyrannical when it operates without justice behind it.

Please construct a reusable decision procedure—ordered questions, conditions that count as sufficient answers, explicit stopping points marking both "do not enforce" and "enforce," and clearly flagged judgment calls where values rather than facts must decide—that the mediator can apply to any proposal within fifteen minutes. The procedure must preserve the core Pascalian asymmetry: force should never be permitted to generate the appearance of justice, only to protect justice already independently recognized; yet justice consistently left unenforced risks becoming a dead letter, which also corrupts its authority.

Then apply the procedure to three concrete proposals:

1. A customary council orders the return of seized agricultural land to families displaced five years ago; the current occupants are armed and have ignored two prior unenforced orders.
2. The council issues a ruling requiring a particular clan to pay blood-compensation to another clan; the debtor clan rejects the amount as excessive but does not dispute the underlying principle of compensation.
3. A council ruling mandates that a local militia disband and surrender weapons; the militia claims it is the only force preventing raids by an external group, and no alternative security arrangement exists.

For each case, show where the procedure lands, including one case where it yields an answer the mediator will likely find morally uncomfortable. Also specify exactly what the procedure cannot do: identify false justice, create legitimacy where none exists, or resolve conflicts when both justice and force claim to be on the same side.

Incorporate one structural constraint into the procedure: the security contingent's operating budget is drawn from the same pooled fund that finances victim reparations, so every deployment directly reduces the resources available for restorative payments. The mediator must therefore weigh enforcement not only against tyranny but against the material opportunity cost to the victims whose harm the justice system exists to address.
