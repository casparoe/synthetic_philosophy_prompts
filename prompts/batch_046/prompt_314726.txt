I am writing a dissertation chapter on Kierkegaard's critique of the press and the public in his 1846 essay on the present age. The passage I am working with describes the public as an abstract, all-embracing entity that is simultaneously everything and nothing, sustained by the press's leveling of qualitative distinctions into quantitative chatter. Kierkegaard depicts a phantom audience that appears to act collectively while no particular individual is present or accountable. I am struggling to understand the precise logic of his argument about responsibility: does he claim that moral and epistemic agency literally dissolves into anonymity, or that it remains intact but unassigned, leaving a normative vacuum that only the single individual can occupy? I also want to assess whether this structure anticipates the modern concept of an epistemic free-rider—an entity that circulates beliefs without any constituent bearing the costs of justification—without committing an anachronism. Could you help me parse the passage's reasoning about presence, leveling, and the disappearance of responsible agency?
