I'm putting together my historiography stance for a reading group and here's where I land: I think Whig history is unavoidable whenever you organize material chronologically, but it's only a vice when it hides contingency; I think actor's categories and analyist's categories can be kept separate in principle but always bleed together in practice, especially in pre-moden science; I reject any teleology that makes the present the goal of the past, yet I keep describing old experiments as steps toward modern chemistry; I think Butterfield's critique hits popular writing harder than professional work, though I cite him for both; and I think institutional histories (funding, labs, patents) are actually less prone to anachronism than conceptual ones, because bricks don't change meaning like force does.
