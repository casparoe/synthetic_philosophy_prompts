Write a short story of roughly 900 to 1,200 words set in a small neuromorphic engineering lab where two researchers—an architect of analog substrates and a cognitive neuroscientist—are attempting to build an artificial cognition system modeled on John von Neumann’s observations about biological computation. Rather than using precise digital gates and central-clock synchronization, their prototype is constructed from deliberately unreliable analog synapses that stabilize meaning only through massive statistical redundancy, so that no single component is trustworthy yet the collective behaves deterministically. The narrative should follow a specific testing session in which temperature fluctuations in the analog substrate cause the system’s representations to drift and recombine in ways that cannot be traced to any individual node failure, forcing the characters to confront whether this instability is an engineering defect or an emergent feature of the architecture.

The story’s intellectual core should be a debate between the two researchers over whether von Neumann’s comparison between brain and computer ultimately collapses because the brain lacks any clear separation between hardware and software, or whether that very absence is what allows analog unreliability to function as a form of computation rather than as mere noise. One character should defend the view that without a stored-program structure and without discrete error-correction at the component level, the system is only producing sophisticated thermal noise; the other should argue that subjective integration might depend precisely on this kind of distributed, temperature-sensitive statistical averaging rather than on digital precision. Avoid conventional AI-sentience tropes, and do not resolve the debate with a revelation about consciousness; instead, end on an ambiguous physical image—perhaps the prototype holding an unstable representation of a color that shifts with the room’s heat—while the characters remain divided. Use restrained, literary prose with minimal exposition, letting the technical dialogue and the tangible behavior of the analog substrate carry the philosophical weight.
