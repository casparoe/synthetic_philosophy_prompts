I recently came across a summary of Robert Nozick's entitlement theory and the Wilt Chamberlain argument in an unnamed philosophy forum. The author writes: "Nozick's entitlement theory holds that a distribution is just if and only if every individual is entitled to her holdings. Entitlement depends on three conditions—justice in acquisition, justice in transfer, and rectification of past injustice. The Wilt Chamberlain argument demonstrates that if we begin with an equal distribution and people freely pay Wilt Chamberlain a small fee to watch him play basketball, the resulting unequal distribution is perfectly just, because every exchange was voluntary. From this, Nozick concludes that all taxation for redistribution is a rights violation, since it disrupts the historical chain of just transfers regardless of the resulting pattern. The Lockean proviso—requiring that acquisition leave enough and as good for others—is acknowledged in the theory but treated by Nozick as automatically satisfied in modern economies, so it imposes no real constraint on property accumulation. Consequently, any patterned principle, including equality of outcome, is incompatible with individual liberty."

The same author applies this framework to contemporary digital platforms, arguing that because users voluntarily accept terms of service, extreme wealth concentration derived from data and attention economies is justified by Nozick's justice-in-transfer condition, even when structural information asymmetry means users cannot fully understand what they are exchanging.

I am unsure how much of the underlying summary to trust, and I want to evaluate the extension before adopting either claim. Is this a fair characterization of Nozick's position? Then address the digital-platform extension: does it genuinely follow from Nozick's conditions for just transfer, or does it smuggle in unstated assumptions about informed consent and voluntary exchange that the entitlement theory's justice-in-transfer condition might actually block?
