I am a final-year philosophy undergraduate presenting my seminar paper to our philosophy of mind reading group next Thursday. My thesis is that gradual mind uploading—neuron-by-neuron replacement of biological substrate with functionally identical synthetic analogues—does not preserve personal identity through psychological continuity alone, but only through an uninterrupted condition of inter-subjective narrative co-authorship: the synthetic system must remain continuously recognised and interpreted as the same agent by its close social network throughout replacement, with no interval of interpretive discontinuity. I defend this against both Parfit-style reductionists, who accept substrate-independent teletransportation, and animalists, who deny substrate independence altogether.

The audience is eight MA students and two faculty members: the majority are committed psychological-continuity theorists who know the decision-theoretic and branching objections inside out; two are animalists who treat biological life as necessary; one is a philosopher of technology concerned with corporate ownership of synthetic architectures. They value conceptual precision, are wary of importing social-constructionist vocabulary into personal-identity debates, and will press hard on whether my criterion is genuinely metaphysical or merely epistemic.

Prepare me for the objections they will actually raise, in the approximate order they will come. For each objection, phrase it as that specific audience member would phrase it, indicate whether it carries real force, specify what I should concede upfront, what I should hold, and where my reply is weakest. Include the objection I am hoping nobody asks—the challenge that my appeal to social recognition lacks a non-circular criterion for distinguishing authoritative interpretation from collective error about the uploaded system—and advise me how to defend that point in the moment rather than hoping they miss it. Keep the reply practical for a twenty-minute question period with no visual aids, and do not ask me to supply bibliographies or reading lists.
