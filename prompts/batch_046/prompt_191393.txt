I am drafting my final-year independent study proposal for an advanced philosophy of mind seminar, and I want to pursue a narrow conceptual project that takes the standard debate over original versus derived intentionality in an unfamiliar direction. The course has focused on Dennett's wandering two-bitser thought experiment, in which an artifact acquires intentional content through evolutionary or design history, and on Searle's claim that only biological brains possess intrinsic aboutness. My proposal concerns whether a third possibility has been overlooked.

The precise question is whether there can be a form of intentionality that is neither original in Searle's biological sense nor derived in Dennett's design-history sense, but loop-constituted: representational content that is not fixed at the point of creation or selection, but is continuously stabilized through recursive interaction between an artifact and its users, such that the system's internal states acquire determinate aboutness only through sustained interpretive feedback that in turn reshapes those states.

This question remains open because both Dennett and Searle treat the intentional status of an artifact as settled prior to or independently of its deployment in interpretive practice. Dennett allows that derived intentionality traces back to the intentions of designers or selectors; Searle denies that any non-biological substrate can generate original intentionality at all. Neither framework supplies criteria for evaluating systems in which an artifact's representational profile is renegotiated through use, where human interpreters treat the system's outputs as meaningful and the system is structured so that those interpretations feed back into its future operations. The standard binary therefore cannot determine whether such loop-stabilized content collapses into derived intentionality, counts as a disguised form of original intentionality projected onto the artifact, or constitutes a distinct category that requires revising the taxonomy itself.

My method will be conceptual rather than empirical. Over a single semester I will formalize the loop-constituted model by identifying the minimal conditions under which user feedback can stabilize representational content without reducing that content to the original intentions of a designer. I will then apply this model to three minimal mechanisms of feedback recalibration—each describing a different way in which interpretive response can alter an artifact's future states—without committing to any particular existing technology. For each mechanism I will ask whether it satisfies Dennett's conditions for derived intentionality and whether it violates or evades Searle's conditions for original intentionality. The timeline is as follows: weeks 1–3 for formalizing the model and its conditions; weeks 4–7 for constructing and analyzing the three mechanisms; weeks 8–10 for direct comparison with Dennett's and Searle's criteria; weeks 11–12 for drafting the final paper.

A successful result will be one of two outcomes: either a precise argument that loop-constituted intentionality is fully reducible to derived intentionality, together with an explicit identification of which human intentions do the deriving and why the loop structure adds nothing conceptually new; or a demonstration that the standard binary omits a stable intermediate form, accompanied by clear criteria for distinguishing loop-constituted cases from both original and derived ones. The project will count as a failure if every loop-stabilized case either clearly traces back to fixed design intentions or clearly lacks any intentional content whatsoever, leaving no conceptual space for the intermediate category. Even then, the failure will be productive: it will sharpen the binary by showing exactly why recursive user interaction cannot generate a novel intentional status.

The deliverable will be an 8,000-word paper suitable for submission to an undergraduate philosophy journal or for presentation at a departmental workshop. I do not require a literature review by citation; I will supply references to the relevant primary texts myself. The project depends only on conceptual tools already available within the philosophy of mind, and it is designed to be completed without access to external databases or empirical laboratories.
