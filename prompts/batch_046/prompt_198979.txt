I'm moderating a podcast interview next week with a philosopher who has been developing a position she calls causal-frame projectibilism in response to Goodman's new riddle of induction. She argues that Goodman's own appeal to entrenchment is circular because entrenchment is itself measured by past inductive success, but she thinks the deeper error is treating induction as frame-neutral. On her view, the predicate grue fails not merely because it is syntactically complex or unentrenched, but because it embeds a temporal index—specifically, reference to a cutoff time—into the property being projected, which renders the projected claim causally unstable across temporal interventions. By contrast, green tracks a causally continuous property that remains invariant when we imagine intervening on observation conditions prior to the cutoff. She allows that this sounds like it collapses induction into a kind of causal realism about properties, but she insists the framework is epistemic rather than metaphysical: we need not commit to natural kinds, only to the claim that inductive licensing requires diachronic causal continuity in the measurement frame.

Please draft six to eight interview questions that prevent her from simply restating the view in generalities. Include at least one that presses the circularity worry—whether identifying causal continuity itself depends on prior inductive judgments about which interventions are legitimate—and another that targets the boundary with scientific essentialism: if causal stability does all the work, why is she not merely saying emeralds are green in virtue of shared microstructure, and what does her view say about predicates that are causally stable yet intuitively non-projectible, such as being observed before noon on a Tuesday? I also want a question that anticipates her likely defense of manufactured predicates—namely, that an artificial term engineered to track a stable causal process should indeed be projectible—followed by a sharp follow-up asking whether this makes projectibility a discovery about the world or a decision about taxonomy. Conclude with a question about what kind of empirical or conceptual finding could genuinely damage causal-frame projectibilism rather than being absorbed as a reframing, with a brief indication of what answer would actually count as undermining rather than accommodating.
