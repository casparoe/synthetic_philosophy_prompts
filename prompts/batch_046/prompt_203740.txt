Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration on Friedman's F-twist and the epistemic status of unrealistic assumptions in scientific modeling. The two parties are: (A) a defender of the F-twist, who holds that unrealistic assumptions are methodologically justified by predictive success and explanatory unification, and that demands for representational accuracy misunderstand the purpose of modeling; and (B) a critic who accepts that some idealization is unavoidable but insists that models containing "constitutive fictions" — assumptions that introduce literally impossible entities or dynamics, rather than merely omitting real details — cannot license counterfactual claims about intervention unless those fictions are shown to map structurally onto real mechanisms or are eventually eliminated.

Produce a document both could sign. Begin with a numbered section of agreed claims: what scientific models are for, what Friedman actually argued about the "as if" framing, which unrealistic assumptions are uncontroversial omissions, and what is genuinely at stake. Then present the residual disagreements in a table: each row should state the dispute in neutral terms, followed immediately by the formulation party A accepts for its own view and the formulation party B accepts for its own view.

Next, identify the cruxes — I take one to be whether predictive success with a constitutive fiction ever justifies counterfactual policy recommendations without structural corroboration, and another to be whether structural isomorphism can be assessed independently of predictive track record. For each crux, specify a concrete modeling case both sides agree is relevant (for instance, infinite-population evolutionary game theory or representative-agent macroeconomic forecasting), state the shared evidence or argument both accept as bearing on it, and record each side's explicit prediction about what that case demonstrates. Conclude by flagging whether any part of their dispute is verbal rather than substantive, and if so, which formulation resolves it. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; write so that a reader without web access can follow every claim.
