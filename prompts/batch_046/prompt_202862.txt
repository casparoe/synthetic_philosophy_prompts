I've been trying to pinn down my own view on intrspection for a semenar paper and I'm not sure it all holds together. Here's roughly where I stand:

(1) For occurrent conscious experiences — like being in pain or seeing a particular shade of blue — introspection gives us direct, non-inferential access. There's no hidden inference or interpretation going on; we're just acquainted with the state.

(2) But for propositional attitudes — beliefs, desires, long-term moods — I think introspection is basically self-interpretation. We construct a story about ourselves using the same behavorial and contextual cues an outsider would use, just with more data.

(3) I also think first-person authority is mostly a social default or pragmatic entitlement, not an epistemic privilege. People usually defer to your report about your headache not because you're specially positioned to know, but because it's the smoothest social move.

(4) At the same time, I want to say people can be genuinely, robustly wrong about their own mental states — not just lying or self-deceiving in a shallow way, but actually misrepresenting what they want or feel.

(5) I reject anything like an inner eye or Cartesian theater; consciousness doesn't require a second-order observer scanning first-order states.

(6) And I think introspective reports should still be treated as fallible but valuable data in psychology — not dismissed, but correctable by behavior and context.

So do these hang together? Specifically, I'm worried that (3) and (4) pull apart: if authority is just social convention, what makes it meaningful to say the report is genuinely false rather than just socially inconvenient? And is there a hidden tension between (1) and (5) — does direct acquaintance need some kind of inner observation mechanism? Also, does (2) undermine (1), since if interpretation creeps in everywhere, maybe even pain reports are constructed? I want to know which tensions are real and severe, which are only apparent, and what the cheapest fix would be for each — and whether there's one revision that cleans up the most mess without throwing out what I actually care about.
