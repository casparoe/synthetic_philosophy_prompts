I sit on the board of a neighborhood cooperative that must vote by Friday on whether to finish a community kitchen renovation that has consumed nearly all of our budget yet remains half-built and months behind schedule. Several members say we should cut our losses, arguing that honoring past spending is exactly the sunk-cost fallacy and that future resources should depend only on future benefits, while others reply that the years of volunteer labor and donated materials represent a shared communal commitment that is not captured by financial accounting alone. I have no formal training in economics or philosophy, but I have been thinking carefully about whether there might be a real distinction between throwing away money and discarding accumulated collective trust, and I want the board to face that distinction squarely. Please draft the board's written opinion recommending either to complete the renovation with concrete conditions and a firm timeline, or to terminate the project and redirect remaining funds, and include an honest account of the strongest consideration favoring the option you reject. Present the memo as exactly five numbered sections, each one or two sentences long, with no introductory or concluding remarks outside those five sections.
