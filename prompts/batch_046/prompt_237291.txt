I want you to draft the joint statement that two opposing camps—a committed Reflection defender who treats the principle as a universal diachronic rationality constraint, and a committed Reflection critic who holds that it systematically fails for self-locating beliefs and produces incoherent predictions in diachronic cases—would both sign after a rigorous adversarial collaboration.

The document must be self-contained and must not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for further reading. It should rely only on concepts and arguments accessible without external lookup, though it should be technically precise.

Structure the deliverable exactly as follows.

These should cover: the formal statement of Reflection (that an agent’s current credence in a proposition should equal her expected future credence conditional on receiving some evidence); the canonical Dutch-book argument for diachronic coherence; the formal structure of the Sleeping Beauty problem and the preface paradox as standard test cases; and the shared acknowledgment that any adequate account must explain why self-locating updates appear to violate Reflection without collapsing into arbitrary ad hocery.

One crux must concern TIR itself—whether restricting Reflection in this way preserves its normative force or merely relocates the problem. The other crux should concern whether the standard Dutch-book justification genuinely supports full Reflection or only a weaker condition of non-self-defeating betting behavior across time.

For the TIR crux, both should agree on the formal setup of the Sleeping Beauty awakening (fair coin, awakening protocol of Monday-only for Heads and Monday-Tuesday for Tails, full knowledge of the protocol upon waking) and on TIR’s formal prediction that her credence in Heads upon awakening should be 1/2. Record the defender’s prediction that this result restores coherence without violating Reflection, and the critic’s prediction that TIR either tacitly reinstates the original principle through the indexing mechanism or introduces an unmotivated asymmetry between temporal and modal self-location. For the Dutch-book crux, both should agree on the structure of a sequential betting scenario in which an agent is offered bets on her own future cognitive reliability at various odds; record the defender’s prediction that accepting bets that violate Reflection guarantees a Dutch book, and the critic’s prediction that the apparent Dutch book can be dissolved by distinguishing rational risk aversion from irrational credence revision.
