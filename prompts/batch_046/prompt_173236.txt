I’m helping my local library put together a display for its 50th anniversary, and I drafted the timeline so each decade is captioned like "moving past old prejudices toward the inclusive community we are today." My friend who reads alot of history said this treats earlier eras like rough drafts of the present — judging people for not knowing things we only figured out later — and warned that if we frame change that way, we’re implicitly saying our current values are just waiting to be outgrown too. I told her that’s overthinking it, we’re just describing real progress in access policies, not writing philosophy. But now I’m wondering if she’s right that there’s a hidden consistency problem: if improvement means catching up to timeless moral truths, then past people were irrational; but if it just means fashion changed, then our timeline isn’t describing real progress, just difference. Is there a way to describe instutional change honestly without falling into either trap, or does any backward-looking narrative automatically end up flattering whoever writes it?
