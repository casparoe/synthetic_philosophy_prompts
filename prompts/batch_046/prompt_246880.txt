I've been thinking, together with a friend, about what equality ought to mean when a society tries to be fair, and we've reached a point where our arguments keep circling the same ideas without quite touching. I believe equality is satisfied when every person receives an identical share of external resources — income, goods, land, and opportunity — so that differences in outcome arise mainly from personal choice rather than from unequal starting bundles. My friend believes equality is satisfied when every person reaches a sufficient level of capability — being able to be mobile, healthy, and able to reason practically — which may require giving different people different amounts of resources, because people convert goods into wellbeing at different rates through no fault of their own.

We would like you to help us draft a joint statement, in the manner of an adversarial collaboration, that sets out exactly where we agree, where we genuinely differ, and what would actually change either of our minds. Please write your answer in British English.

The document should contain the following parts, using the two imaginary societies — Resourcia, where every citizen receives an identical annual bundle of goods regardless of personal circumstance, and Capacia, where goods are distributed according to each citizen's documented barriers to achieving mobility, health, and practical reasoning — as running examples throughout.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims that both of us could sign. These should include: what problem equality is meant to solve; that resources and capabilities are conceptually distinct even if empirically linked; that people differ in how they turn goods into lived outcomes; that a purely formal equality can leave some people worse off in real terms; and that measuring equality requires choosing a metric, which is not a neutral technical step. Where a disagreement is partly verbal — for instance, if one of us defines resources so broadly that they already include capabilities — please say so and explain why that definition does not resolve the dispute.

Second, a disagreements section in which you state the precise divergence in wording that each side would accept for its own view. For my side: equality demands an equal distribution of external goods, and any remaining differences in wellbeing that stem from personal choice or natural variation in taste are not injustices that society must correct. For my friend's side: equality demands that no one fall below a threshold of central human functioning, and differences in resource holdings are justified precisely when they correct for unequal conversion abilities, so that equality of capability may require inequality of goods.

Third, identify the cruxes — specific sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party. Examples might include: whether equality should compensate for differences in natural talent that affect how resources become wellbeing; whether people adapting their expectations to deprivation undermines welfare-based metrics and also threatens capability-based ones; whether Resourcia or Capacia is more equal in a situation where every citizen has the same goods but some have disabilities that prevent them from using those goods effectively; and whether there is a principled line between differences society should correct and differences that are the result of responsible choice.

Fourth, for each crux, record what argument or evidence each side predicts would support its position, and note whether there is any shared test or case — such as a comparison of health outcomes in Resourcia versus Capacia, or a thought experiment about a citizen who voluntarily refuses extra resources — that both sides agree would be genuinely relevant to resolving that crux. If part of the dispute turns out to be about words rather than substance, say which part and why.

Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Please also avoid relying on highly obscure theories or specific texts we are unlikely to know; stick to the core ideas of resource equality and capability equality as they are generally understood, but ground them in the concrete comparison between Resourcia and Capacia so that the disagreement is anchored rather than abstract.

Make the wording precise and balanced, so that neither side sounds like a straw figure and neither sounds like it has already conceded defeat.
