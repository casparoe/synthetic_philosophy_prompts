Please draft one essay question for the final examination of an upper-level undergraduate seminar on moral change and social norms, together with a model answer of approximately 700 words and a marking scheme. The course has covered Bernard Williams' thesis that shifts in moral norms are often driven by changes in structures of honor and shame rather than by direct rational argument, and Kwame Anthony Appiah's comparative historical analysis of the abolition of dueling and the decline of Atlantic slavery. The question must be designed so that it cannot be answered by summarizing these two authors; it should require students to apply and adjudicate between the frameworks.

Specifically, construct a question that introduces a conceptual distinction between internal honor dynamics—how a peer group redefines what counts as dignified behavior among its own members—and external cost structures—how legal, economic, or demographic shifts alter the price of maintaining a practice. Present a hypothetical case: in the early twentieth century, an elite military caste abandons ritualized dueling not because of legal prohibition or economic loss, but because the caste redefines martial dignity as strategic loyalty rather than personal risk; simultaneously, the economic interests of that caste are harmed by the abolition because the dueling code had previously restricted entry into officer ranks, and its removal increases competition. Ask students whether this case supports Williams over Appiah, undermines both, or requires a supplementary mechanism—specifically, they must evaluate whether honor is operating here as an autonomous motivational force or as an ideological rationalization of shifting interests.

The model answer should demonstrate the specific moves required: (1) accurate reconstruction of Williams' claim about honor and shame versus rational persuasion; (2) accurate reconstruction of Appiah's emphasis on honor codes among peers and the role of external interests; (3) application of the internal/external distinction to the hypothetical case; (4) evaluation of whether honor functions autonomously or as rationalization, with explicit reference to the economic-harm condition; (5) a reasoned conclusion about which framework survives, is modified, or fails.

Provide a marking scheme out of 25 marks, allocating specific point values to each of the five moves above. For each point, include a one-line description of how candidates typically fail it—for example, conflating the two authors, ignoring the economic-harm condition, treating honor as purely emotional rather than structural, or concluding with summary rather than adjudication.

The question should specify a 90-minute writing period and an expected length of roughly 800–1000 words. Do not ask students to provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
