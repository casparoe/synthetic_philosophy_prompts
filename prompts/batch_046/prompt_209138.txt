I have been reflecting on the backward induction paradox in the context of the centipede game, and I find myself drawn to what might be called the institutional rationality problem. If common knowledge of rationality forces immediate defection at the first node of every finite sequential interaction, then a constitutional amendment process—which unfolds as a finite sequence of legislative moves—must necessarily collapse at its initial proposal stage. Yet functioning multi-round constitutional conventions clearly persist. This appears to leave us with two competing diagnoses: either legislators systematically violate the axioms of rational choice, or the centipede game is structurally identical to the prisoner’s dilemma, rendering backward induction inapplicable outside zero-sum contexts. Which of these diagnoses is correct, and how ought we to reform our game-theoretic models of institutional cooperation so as to account for sustained sequential amendment without abandoning the core logic of backward induction?
