For a feature I am preparing on distributed cognition and where the boundaries of mind should be drawn, I have worked out the following set of commitments, and I want a consistency audit before publication. Here is where I stand.

First, I accept the framing of Hofstadter's dialogue Ant Fugue: an ant colony—what he named Aunt Hillary—can be a genuine bearer of mental states if the functional organization of its parts realizes belief-like and desire-like roles, regardless of whether any individual ant is conscious.

Second, I hold that such collective cognition is not mere aggregation; it requires what I call a fugue structure. Independent processing streams—say, foraging, defense, and thermoregulation—must enter and exit according to contrapuntal feedback rules, integrating without a central conductor. If the streams are only hierarchically commanded or statistically correlated, there is behavior but no cognition.

Third, I think original intentionality is fixed by evolutionary selection history, and I deny that the unit of selection must be the individual organism. A colony can be a unit of selection if its reproductive success depends on colony-level traits, so Aunt Hillary's representational states can be genuinely about her environment.

Fourth, I think phenomenal consciousness requires a specific temporal-binding mechanism that produces a unified experiential field, and that ant pheromonal oscillations are too slow and desynchronized to achieve this. So I am prepared to say that Aunt Hillary has cognition without any experiential dimension—there is nothing it is like to be her.

Fifth, I believe derived intentionality—content fixed by a designer's intentions—is sufficient for representation. That is why I think a synthetic swarm engineered with fugue architecture could count as a mind even lacking evolutionary history.

Sixth, nevertheless, I hold that mental states lacking evolutionary grounding are counterfeit in Dennett's sense: functionally adequate, perhaps, but normatively hollow, like currency that circulates without being backed.

Seventh, I maintain that current large language models fail on both counts. Their processing is hierarchical and feed-forward, not contrapuntal, so they lack fugue structure; and they have no selection history, so they lack both original and derived intentionality in any meaningful form.

The audit I need: do these hang together? Specifically, I see a potential conflict between my insistence in (3) that evolutionary grounding is necessary for genuine content and my willingness in (5) to grant mind-status to synthetic swarms that lack it—especially given (6), which seems to condemn exactly such cases as counterfeit. Is this a real contradiction or only an appearance? I also wonder whether (2)'s fugue requirement is actually incompatible with (4)'s denial of phenomenal binding in ants—might the absence of binding undermine the claim that the streams are genuinely integrated? Please rank the real tensions by severity, identify which pair is only apparently at odds, state the least costly revision, and tell me whether abandoning (6) or weakening (3) does less damage to the overall picture.
