I'm on the constitutional review board for our university, and we've got a real mess over the student "conscience and culture" fee. Right now it's an opt-in charge that gets distributed to campus chaplaincies — Catholic, Protestant, Muslim, Jewish — modeled loosely on the German church-tax idea but purely voluntary. The humanist association just applied for equal funding to run secular existential counseling, and the religious reps on the board say that's a category error: the fee was meant to accommodate religious conscience specifically, not to become a general worldview subsidy, and extending it collapses the very distinction that makes the scheme constitutional instead of establishment. The humanists say that's backwards — if you fund four religions and exclude them, you're not neutral, you're just picking winners.

We have to recommend something concrete by the senate meeting next month: approve the extension, reject it, or approve it with conditions that redefine the fee's purpose and eligibility criteria. I want the memo to actually wrestle with the philosophical structure here, not just list court cases our counsel already sent us. There's this idea I've been calling the recognition asymmetry — once a state (or university) decides religious belief deserves special institutional recognition like tax collection or chaplaincy funding, it implicitly creates a protected class that non-religious worldviews can't enter without either diluting the religious category or accepting second-class status. So neutrality isn't just a line you draw between church and state; it's a structural problem about what counts as a "conscience" the institution can recognize without endorsing its content.

Can you draft the board memo? It should pick one of those three options, lay out reasoning the religious representatives will feel is taken seriously (their worry about category collapse isn't just turf protection), and address whether the recognition asymmetry means we ought to scrap the fee entirely, expand it carefully, or draw a hard line at "religious only." Include a concrete implimentation timeline if you go the conditional route, and don't just ask for bibliographies — I need something we can vote on Thursday.
