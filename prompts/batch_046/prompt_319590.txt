As a novelist constructing a philosophical fable on consciousness, I ask you to design a thought experiment to the following specifications. Present, in a single realistic paragraph—no exotic physics, no perfect predictors, no science-fictional devices—a writer whose sense of self is distributed across a closed circuit of reference: her private journals, a fictional protagonist she created who appears to reflect upon her, and an editor who annotates both texts, with each layer referring back to the others circularly rather than hierarchically. Engineer the case so that Hofstadter's strange-loop account predicts that one unified self-symbol—an 'I'—persists across the entire circuit, while a rival bundle or narrative-fragmentation view predicts no central self, only intersecting loops without a terminus. The experiment should serve fiction that assumes no web access: include no bibliographies, no citations, and no appeal to theories outside the two named positions.
