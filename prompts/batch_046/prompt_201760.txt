Case 3: Every raven I have seen is black, and I project that an unobserved raven is black. This feels correct, parallel to Case 1.

Case 4: Every raven observed in a specific northern forest is black, and I define "blorven" as black-in-that-forest and white-elsewhere. Projecting blorven for ravens outside that forest feels wrong, though my confidence is lower than in Case 2 because the spatial boundary is anchored to an actual feature of my sample.

They then project this property to predict long-term patient outcomes. I think this is a serious inductive mistake, structurally akin to Case 2.

Physicists project pre-shield-decay behavior to future runs with the same apparatus configuration. This strikes me as legitimate, even though the predicate is indexed to an intervention in a way that looks structurally parallel to Case 5.
