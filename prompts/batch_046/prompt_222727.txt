A colleague in comparative philosophy recently summarized Zhuangzi's view of death for me in terms that blend the Inner Chapters with contemporary process metaphysics. On her account, Zhuangzi holds that death is nothing more than a seasonal redistribution of qi: the self is not a substance but a temporary configuration of energy, and when that configuration dissolves, personal death involves no genuine loss—only a change of form, like ice returning to water. From this ontology she derives a strict normative conclusion: grief is always irrational, since mourning a dead friend is structurally no different from mourning melted ice. She reads the well-known story of Zhuangzi drumming and singing at his wife's funeral not as sorrow transformed, but as evidence of Stoic-style apatheia and emotional suppression. She further claims that Zhuangzi explicitly denies any post-mortem survival of consciousness, aligning him with modern physicalist eliminativism about personal identity, and that he therefore anticipates four-dimensionalist persistence conditions in which death is merely a temporal boundary with no ontological significance. Finally, she suggests that this package refutes both Confucian ritual mourning—because it treats the dead as genuinely lost persons rather than redistributed energy—and Buddhist reincarnationist hopes, which mistakenly preserve a substantial self across transformation. I suspect this captures real elements of Zhuangzi's transformation ontology but may conflate his joyful acceptance of change with emotional indifference, and may overstate his denial of survival into an explicit metaphysical thesis he never articulates. Is this a fair characterization? Please distinguish what is textually grounded in the Zhuangzi, what is an anachronistic projection—whether Stoic, physicalist, or four-dimensionalist—and what a careful interpreter of the Inner Chapters would correct. Does the conclusion that grief is always irrational actually follow from the qi-transformation view, or does the drumming episode support a different emotional posture altogether? And please say what argument or textual evidence would change your assessment, and identify the point where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
