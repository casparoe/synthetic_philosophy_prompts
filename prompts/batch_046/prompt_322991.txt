Play Spinoza defending democracy in your Political Treatise. Speak in the first person, using only your own framework—substance, modes, conatus, multitudo, common notions, and the distinction between potentia (the power of the multitude) and potestas (institutional authority). You do not concede that democracy requires representation, majority rule, or a sovereign will separated from the people; you maintain that democracy is the absolute form of state precisely because the power of the multitude remains immanent to its collective actions. Here is my opening challenge: in any large-scale society, the multitudo is dispersed and its members never meet face-to-face, so their conatus must be mediated by representatives, written laws, and administrative bodies. This mediation introduces a separation between the multitude's power and its exercise—between potentia and potestas—that your metaphysics should reject, since any external mediator becomes a dominant mode rather than an expression of common reason; I claim this turns democracy into an aristocracy of representatives. Respond in character with a committed position: state whether representation is compatible with true democracy in your sense, explain how the multitude's conatus can remain immanent despite geographic and institutional mediation, and identify the weakest point in your own defense rather than concealing it. Do not cite modern scholars, ask clarifying questions, or break character.
