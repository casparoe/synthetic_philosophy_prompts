For a graduate seminar on consciousness, design a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts philosophical positions on whether qualia constitute an incoherent posit, using Dennett's Chase-and-Sanborn coffee-taster intuition pump as the entry case rather than as a settled argument. The tree should begin with a concrete judgment about the tasters—whether their reported change in taste reveals a shift in intrinsic phenomenal quality, a shift in comparative preference, or merely a shift in descriptive vocabulary—then branch through three further diagnostic nodes: (i) whether inverted-spectrum scenarios are conceptually coherent or self-undermining; (ii) whether explanatory-gap arguments commit one to a non-physical property or only to an epistemic limitation; and (iii) whether phenomenal concepts are recognitional capacities that can misfire, or indexicals that cannot. Each leaf should name a live position (e.g., eliminativist about qualia, representationalist with error-theory, property dualist with epistemic humility, functionalist with comparative-phenomenology) and note any unoccupied combinations in the logical space—particularly combinations that seem available but are never defended, with a brief diagnosis of whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The tree should separate positions that standard surveys conflate—especially views that accept the Chase/Sanborn data but deny that it undermines qualia, versus views that accept the data and take it as a reductio—and should flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely distinguish without smuggling in a label the respondent does not yet hold. Phrase every node as a question answerable by someone who has not memorized the literature's taxonomy.
