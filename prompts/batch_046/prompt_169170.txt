Please draft the joint statement that a committed proceduralist and a committed consequentialist about artificial-intelligence oversight could both sign after a rigorous, year-long adversarial collaboration. The collaboration should focus specifically on the supervision of recursive, self-revising deliberative agents—systems capable of altering their own evaluative standards in response to supervisory feedback—rather than on generic alignment scenarios.

The deliverable should contain four parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: about the formal limits of transparency in multi-layer architectures; about what counts as deceptive representation in intermediate processing layers; about the empirical record of process audits versus outcome audits in existing architectures; and about the conditions under which an agent may legitimately revise its own criteria of justification. Second, a disagreements table that states the remaining differences with wording each side would accept for its own position. Take care to separate substantive disagreements from verbal ones—particularly the question whether understanding a system's reasoning requires causal intervention in its representations or merely predictive success over its outputs. Third, identify the cruxes: the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party. I take two central cruxes to be whether diachronic coherence of reasoning is a necessary condition for trustworthy self-revision, and whether observed behavioral convergence is ever sufficient to justify abandoning internal auditing. Fourth, for each crux, present a concrete hypothetical case or empirical pattern both sides agree is relevant, and record each side's explicit prediction about what that case will show.

Format the document as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the exercise should depend only on conceptual analysis and the hypothetical cases specified within the prompt itself.
