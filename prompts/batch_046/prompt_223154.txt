I serve on the editorial board of the University Archive's Oral History Publication Series. We must set the annotation policy for Volume 14, a collection of 1973 community-organizer interviews being prepared for scholarly release. One faction proposes moral-context annotations: footnotes that classify each interviewee's statements about gender and race using our present ethical taxonomy, explicitly flagging language that now counts as harmful. The opposing faction insists on historical-context notes only—explanations of why such language was structurally prevalent in 1973—without any evaluative labeling, arguing that moral-context annotations commit presentism and that historians cannot genuinely escape the normative framework of their own era. The board needs a written policy memo recommending one of these regimes, or a precisely defined hybrid, for this volume. The memo should state exactly what the annotations will and will not contain, justify the choice with direct engagement with the presentism problem in historiography, treat the strongest objection from the rejected side with intellectual honesty rather than dismissal, and include a concrete implementation timeline for the annotators. Draft the memo for circulation before our vote, addressed to the board members.
