I am a doctoral student in philosophy of science working on the epistemology of deep-time reconstruction, and I want to test whether the conceptual apparatus of textual source criticism can be mapped onto material-source criticism in palaeoanthropology without conceptual distortion.

Consider the Piltdown Man affair: Charles Dawson's 1912 presentation of fossil fragments (a human-like cranium and an ape-like jaw) allegedly recovered from a gravel pit near Piltdown, East Sussex; the specimen's acceptance by the British Museum (Natural History) and much of the scientific community as evidence of an early hominin; and its exposure as a forgery in 1953 through fluorine-absorption testing and anatomical comparison, which revealed the jaw to belong to an orangutan and the cranium to a modern human, both artificially stained and filed.

Analyse this episode through the lens of source-criticism theory as it runs from Herodotus to contemporary historiography. Specifically, treat the fossil assemblage not as a single piece of evidence but as a composite manuscript comprising four distinct testimonial layers: (1) the physical fragments themselves; (2) the stratigraphic and provenance documentation supplied by Dawson; (3) the institutional testimony of the museum and the peer reviewers who validated the find; and (4) the 1953 chemical-analytical reports that functioned as a later critical edition.

For each layer, say whether Herodotean cross-checking of independent witnesses or Thucydidean suspicion of self-interested narration does the explanatory work, and identify the precise point at which the analogy between textual interpolation and physical forgery breaks down — for instance, where the forger's intentional manipulation of consistency across layers outstrips anything we find in accidental manuscript corruption.

Then turn the analysis back onto the theory: does the Piltdown case show that distant-past knowledge requires an unbroken chain of testimony, or can it survive a broken testimonial chain if material analysis can reconstruct provenance ex post facto? And does the episode reveal that source criticism, designed for intentional human testimony, is structurally inadequate for cases where the source is a deliberately fabricated physical object?

Please keep the mapping disciplined — specify who or what counts as the witness in each layer, what the contradiction is, and where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak. I am not asking for a bibliography or reading list; I want a conceptual case study that runs in both directions.
