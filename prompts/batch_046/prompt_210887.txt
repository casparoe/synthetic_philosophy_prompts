I have spent nearly a decade and substantial savings mastering the restoration and performance of a rare 19th-century mechanical instrument with almost no commercial market. I was recently offered an engineering position at triple my income, yet accepting it would mean abandoning not only my prior investment but also the small community of practitioners I have become embedded in. A friend insists that because the years and money already spent are gone forever, I should decide purely on future prospects; to stay because of what I have already put in is irrational. But leaving feels less like avoiding waste and more like breaking commitments that have become part of who I am and my ties to others. Must honoring past investments always be irrational, or can they carry rational weight when they are bound up with one's identity and ongoing social obligations?
