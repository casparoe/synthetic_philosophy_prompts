I serve as chair of the arbitration panel for the Meridian Literary Foundation and am also a novelist. We must issue a formal decision memo in a dispute between two members. The claimant alleges that the respondent’s recently published novel appropriated not its prose or plot, but the philosophical architecture embedded in the claimant’s earlier, unpublished manuscript: a taxonomy that assigns fictional characters what the claimant terms derivative moral standing, graded across three narrative proximity classes—primary, refracted, and spectral—according to the reader’s counterfactual identification with them. The respondent concedes the structural parallel but contends that an abstract normative taxonomy is an unownable idea rather than protectable expression, and that embedding it within fiction does not convert it into property. The panel must recommend one of three outcomes—dismissal of the claim, a conditional remedy requiring explicit philosophical attribution without monetary damages, or full censure with restitution—and provide reasoning both parties, who are writers rather than jurists, could answer substantively. Draft the memo with a clear bottom line, an honest treatment of the strongest consideration supporting the opposing outcome, and a reasoned account of where the idea-expression boundary should fall when the idea in question is itself a framework about the moral ontology of fiction.
