I'm a philosphy PhD student working in metaphyics and I've been running this reading group on persistence for about a year now, and honestly the endurantism vs perdurantism debate feels totally stuck. Every session we cycle through the same moves: temporary intrinsics, the shifting-shapes problem, whether saying I'm a four-dimensional worm is just weird or literally false, and then everyone restates their priors and we go home. I started wondering if the stall isn't really about ontology but about a hidden commitment neither side has defended - specifically, both camps seem to assume a substantivalist picture of spacetime that treats time as a container, but if you take relationalism about time seriously maybe the whole framing of being present versus having parts collapses. Or maybe it's verbal in a different way: endurantists talk about being present in a presentist key, perdurantists talk about parts in an eternalist key, so they're not actually describing the same situation.

So I'm asking for a diagnosis of the structure of this disagreement. Is it substantive about what exists, is it about different standards for what counts as an adequate explanation of change, or is it just bedrock intuition about whether it's bizarre to have parts at other times? Is there a hidden formal question doing the work - like whether perdurantism requires a privileged foliation that physics doesn't give us - or is that a red herring? And if it's bedrock intuition, should we expect any progress at all, or is the right move to change the question and ask which framework does better explanatory work in physics and psychology? I don't want a verdict on who's right; I want to know what kind of disagreement we've actually been having, and whether there's any distinction or shift in framing that would actually move the needle.
