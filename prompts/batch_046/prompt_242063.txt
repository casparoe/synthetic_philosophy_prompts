After a long argument with a friend about whether every genuine creative leap is just an extreme interpolation within a pre-existing conceptual space, I want to press a focused comparison between two accounts. The first is Hofstadter’s knob-twiddling model, in which creativity operates by continuously adjusting parameters—conceptual knobs controlling dimensions like analogy strength, abstraction level, or feature salience—to slide mental representations into new but structurally adjacent configurations. The second is a discontinuous restructuring account, which I will call the matrix-crossing view: the claim that some creative acts require breaking out of the original parameter space altogether, entering an independent conceptual matrix where the prior knobs lose their applicability and entirely new dimensions are introduced.

Compare these two models with respect to a specific hypothetical case rather than in the abstract. Consider a conceptual space for scientific explanation defined by three continuous knobs—explanatory depth, empirical risk, and ontological commitment—within which existing interpretations of quantum mechanics sit as points. Now imagine a proposed theoretical move that does not merely slide these knobs to an extreme value, but eliminates one dimension entirely (say, ontological commitment) and substitutes a relational property (say, structural dependence) that was not present in the original space. Does Hofstadter’s framework have the resources to treat this as an extreme form of knob-twiddling—perhaps by reconceiving the eliminated dimension as a hidden composite of the remaining two—or does the move necessarily fall outside the model? Conversely, does the matrix-crossing view risk overstating the discontinuity: could the new relational property be reconstructed, without loss, as a coordinate transformation within the original three-dimensional space?

Defend your comparison using explicit criteria: which account more precisely captures the generative mechanism behind such a leap; which offers a clearer boundary condition between variation and conceptual revolution; and whether there is a principled, non-retrospective way to classify a given creative move as intra-matrix or inter-matrix. I am not asking for a literature survey or a reading list, but for a focused argument about which model should be treated as fundamental, and in what domain the other might survive as a special case.
