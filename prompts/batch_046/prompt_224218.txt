In a discussion about Oskar Lange's market-socialist model, my interlocutor keeps falling back on the line that “if a planning board adjusts prices by trial and error, it reproduces the market's information-processing function without reproducing capitalist institutions,” and uses it to conclude that Hayek's and Mises's knowledge objections are thereby answered. I am skeptical but cannot locate exactly where the slide occurs. Can you separate the precise theses this sentence could be expressing—epistemic (the board acquires the same dispersed knowledge that markets reveal), functional (prices perform identical allocative work regardless of who owns the means of production), institutional (property rights are irrelevant to economic computation), and ontological (the relevant knowledge resides in the price vector itself rather than in the agents who generate it)—and for each say whether it is tautological, a substantive claim that might hold, or false? Then tell me which of these readings actually supports the conclusion that Lange answers Hayek, which ones silently assume that all economically relevant knowledge can be made explicit to a central board, and what further distinction—say, between ex post price convergence and ex ante entrepreneurial discovery—would be needed to adjudicate the dispute without begging the question against either side. Lay the readings out as a menu rather than picking one, and indicate which version my interlocutor most likely intends when he deploys the line to dismiss the socialist-calculation problem.
