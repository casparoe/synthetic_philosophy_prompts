Give an example of a conceptual issue that emerges within Integrated Information Theory when the exclusion postulate is applied to a split-brain configuration in which the corpus callosum has been completely transected. Your example should illustrate a specific tension between the theory's requirement that only one maximally irreducible causal structure determines consciousness within a given system boundary and the clinical intuition that such patients may exhibit two distinct streams of awareness. In articulating the issue, explain why adjusting the boundary conditions—either by integrating both hemispheres into one complex or by isolating each hemisphere—fails to resolve the tension without undermining the theory's broader commitments to unified phenomenology and to the intrinsic nature of causal power.
