I serve on the constitutional review board of a democratic republic considering the replacement of its upper legislative chamber with a body of 200 citizens selected by stratified sortition: non-renewable four-year terms, with compulsory attendance at structured deliberation forums before any vote. Advocates maintain that this eliminates the structural corruption of electoral politics and achieves descriptive representation without partisan filtering. Critics argue that the mandatory forums operate as a philosophical mask: they alter the preferences of the randomly selected citizens so systematically that the body no longer represents the democratic will that justified the lot. A related objection, drawn from the metaphysics of dispositions, treats the protocol as a fink mechanism: the raw sample lacks the disposition to govern well, and the forums serve as the triggering condition that actualises a latent capacity, so the filter does not corrupt representation but reveals what the institution was always capable of becoming.

Please draft the board's formal written opinion — recommend adoption, rejection, or adoption with explicit safeguards — that addresses both the democratic-theory justification for sortition and the metaphysical-style concerns about masking and finking. Your recommendation should include a concrete institutional design, such as the length and form of the deliberative stage, provisions for veto or recall, or sunset clauses, or else a clear rejection paired with an alternative mechanism. Treat the strongest counter-argument to your position with genuine seriousness rather than dismissal. Write in polished formal British English, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
