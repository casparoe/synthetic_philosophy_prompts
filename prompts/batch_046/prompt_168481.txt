After a long and somewhat heated conversation with a friend last night about whether an immaterialist metaphysics can survive the blockchain era, I keep returning to a tension in Berkeley’s writings on paper currency and banking. In those 1730s essays—where he asks whether bank notes are real wealth or merely signs sustained by public trust—he seems to treat value as an idea anchored in collective moral purpose rather than in metallic substance. My friend insists this makes Berkeley a natural ally of modern decentralized finance, particularly of synthetic assets maintained by algorithmic rules and distributed consensus rather than by sovereign promises. I am skeptical. So consider this hypothetical: imagine Berkeley confronted with an over-collateralized stablecoin pegged not to fiat but to a commodity basket, its value maintained by automated arbitrage incentives, oracle-fed price data, and a decentralized treasury governed by staked governance tokens. The ledger exists only as distributed nodes; there is no vault, no state backing, only executable code and on-chain voting. Given his denial that matter exists independently of perceiving minds, and his view that real ideas require direct sensory or spiritual grounding, would he have regarded the blockchain record as a legitimate collective idea—analogous to language or social convention—or as a dangerous fiction precisely because it lacks both tangible perceptual presence and the communal ethical purpose he believed anchored true value? And if he witnessed the collapse of an algorithmic stablecoin—where the governing rules failed to preserve the peg because market behavior diverged from the protocol’s intended mechanics—would he have interpreted that failure as proof that immaterial signs of value inevitably dissolve when detached from both sensory reality and shared moral intention? Or might he have argued that the flaw lay only in the particular design of the governing idea, leaving open the possibility that a properly conceived conceptual currency, sustained by genuine communal will rather than speculative arbitrage, could still count as fully real?
