I was arguing with my roommate about ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood — he’s taking a course on african philosphy and I’m just going off what I read in some blogposts. The idea that a person is a person through other people sounds nice, but I think there’s a hidden contradiction people gloss over. If the self is literally constituted by its relationships, like a node that only exists because of the edges connecting it, then there’s no stable substrate left to hold rights or duties.

So when ubuntu-inspired legal frameworks talk about the community having obligations to an individual, aren’t they sneaking in a liberal, atomistic self that the metaphysics denies? I even tried formalizing it as graph-theoretic eliminativism — remove the nodes, keep only the relations, and you can’t assign blame or praise to anyone specific, only to network topology. Given all that, how does ubuntu avoid collapsing into moral eliminativism without secretly reintroducing the very atomistic self it claims to reject?
