I defend a variant of updateless decision theory that introduces epistemic gate nodes: agents structurally barred from conditioning on logical correlations within their own source code, yet open to empirical updates about the external world. The aim is to preserve UDT's avoidance of evidential reasoning in Newcomb-style problems while allowing genuine learning. What would count decisively against this hybrid? Please distinguish between (a) formal results showing that any such gate collapses into pure UDT or into causal or evidential decision theory, (b) conceptual arguments that the logical-versus-empirical distinction becomes unstable once embedded in a physical computer, and (c) practical cases in which the gate either admits a harmful update or blocks a necessary one. Rank these defeaters by how fatal they are, indicate which ones a committed defender could concede as merely awkward rather than destructive, and assess whether the view can be qualified into unfalsifiability — and, if so, whether that insulation is itself a defect for a decision-theoretic framework.
