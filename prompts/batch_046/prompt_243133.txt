I'm on the state labor board's AI governance subcommitee and we gotta decide by Thursday how we're supervising Meridian, the new unemployment-tier system. It uses employment-history embeddings plus real-time labor-market signals to assign support levels. The vendor laid out two standard paths: process-based, where we audit feature weights and intermediate scoring layers, or outcome-based, where we just check demographic parity and appeal rates. But there's a third proposal floating around — "procedural shadowing" — where instead of inspecting the model or the stats, we mandate that human caseworkers log every override they make, and we audit those logs for patterns. The theory is that supervision should target the human-machine boundary, not the black box or the aggregate.

Some panelists say process audits are useless here because the embedding spaces are semantically opaque — you can check weights but not what they mean — and outcome checks miss individual harms that cancel out in the average. Others say shadowing is theater: caseworkers will learn to game the logs, and we'll never see the biases in recommendations they dont override.

I need a memo for Thursday recommending one approach (or a hybrid with conditions) that the board can actually vote on. Give me a clear recommendation, the reasoning behind it, and an honest engagement with the strongest argument on the losing side — especially dont dismiss shadowing out of hand. Don't ask for citations or a bibliography; we need reasoning that works in the room, not a literature review.
