In a philosophy-of-language seminar, a fellow student keeps invoking "there's a sharp cutoff between bald and not-bald; we just don't know where" to dismiss sorites reasoning and to reject supervaluationist claims that there is "no fact of the matter." Before the next meeting, I want a disambiguated menu I can actually use. Lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan could be expressing—separating claims about epistemic access, about the semantics of gradable adjectives, about metaphysical precision, and about the structure of linguistic competence—indicating for each whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, substantive but false, or incoherent. For each reading, say whether it genuinely undermines the supervaluationist tolerance principle or merely changes the subject, and note which reading the student probably intends. Separate what is broadly settled in the vagueness literature from what is genuinely contested among specialists (for instance, whether any epistemicist account can avoid implausible knowledge claims about borderline cases), and do not present a contested epistemicist interpretation as the consensus.
