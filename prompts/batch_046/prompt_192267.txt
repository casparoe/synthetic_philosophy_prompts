Compose a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers—Dr. Asha Menon, who defends a substrate-anchored phenomenal-continuity view, and Dr. Theo Bruck, who advances a functional-narrative account built around the idea of “indexical friction”—debating Daniel Dennett’s well-known brain-transplant thought experiment (the “Where Am I?” scenario in which a brain is removed, placed in a vat, and connected by radio to a body left behind in an operating room). Menon should argue that first-person location genuinely tracks the biological substrate generating conscious experience: when Dennett’s brain is in the vat, he is literally there, and the radio link is merely instrumental causation, not part of the self’s locus. Bruck should counter that “where I am” is not a metaphysical coordinate but a practical-narrative achievement sustained by action and feedback loops; he should introduce “indexical friction” as the resistance that first-person terms like “here” and “now” offer to being fully dissolved into functional descriptions, using it to show that demanding a single correct location presupposes a false ontology of the self. Have them respond directly to each other’s actual points: Menon should challenge whether Bruck’s view collapses into eliminativism about phenomenal location, and Bruck should press Menon on why the radio link—already extending cognition and perception across space—does not dissolve the very brain-body boundary she insists upon. Avoid strawman representations, do not invoke obscure bibliographic references, and do not ask the interlocutors to cite texts.

Begin with a one-sentence bottom line indicating which position, if either, more coherently answers Dennett’s question. Keep the dialogue itself to no more than 600 words, with each speaker taking no more than seven turns.
