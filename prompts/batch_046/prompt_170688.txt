I serve on the university's interdisciplinary research governance board, and next Thursday we must vote on whether to pilot a proposed "normative arbitration protocol" for resolving conflicts between departmental funding priorities. The protocol, designed by an external consulting group, claims to generate binding institutional recommendations by identifying what it calls "free-floating rationales"—normative reasons that apply to the university as such, independent of what any particular administrator, faculty member, or student believes, values, or is motivated to pursue. Its defenders argue that without such reasoner-independent reasons, institutional deliberation collapses into masked bargaining among stakeholder interests; its critics reply that a reason with no reasoner is either an empty formalism or a concealed imposition of the designers' own values, and that adopting it would outsource our collective judgment to an opaque algorithm. Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate to the board before the vote: it should recommend a concrete course of action—full adoption with safeguards, rejection, or a narrowly bounded trial—with an explicit time frame and conditions for review, and it should engage seriously with the strongest philosophical consideration on the side you do not ultimately endorse rather than merely restating it. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the board needs conceptual clarity about whether reasons can genuinely float free of agents and a practical recommendation that treats this issue as load-bearing for institutional legitimacy, not as an academic ornament.
