I have come to hold a specific view about the ant colony in Hofstadter's ant-fugue dialogue—Aunt Hillary's colony—not merely as a metaphor for distributed cognition but as a literal minimal subject of experience. The claim is that collective mindedness in such a colony depends not on sheer information integration but on a contrapuntal organizational form: functionally autonomous subsystems (foraging, defense, brood-care) interact like independent melodic voices, maintaining their distinctness while being bound into a unified behavioral pattern through feedback loops that are structurally analogous to musical counterpoint rather than hierarchical command. On this view, an ant colony is minded precisely because its parts do not collapse into a single control center; the counterpoint itself is the cognitive architecture. What would have to be true for this to be wrong? I want an honest map of the position's exposure: which empirical results about ant colony dynamics—say, evidence that decision-making is actually centralized or purely stochastic rather than contrapuntal—would be fatal, and which would merely be awkward? Which philosophical objections—perhaps that counterpoint is an aesthetic category illegitimately projected onto biology, or that a contrapuntal structure lacks the unified point of view necessary for subjectivity—would its defenders concede as counting against them? Are there results in integrated information theory or in the group-agency literature that would show this view collapses into a more standard aggregation account, or that it predicts something about colony behavior that does not in fact occur? Rank the defeaters, distinguish what the view risks from what it has already insulated itself against, and say whether the position is genuinely exposed or largely protected by qualification.
