I am convening a small interdisciplinary reading group — mostly cognitive scientists and philosophers of mind with little prior exposure to contemporary panpsychism, and generally sceptical of anything that sounds like vitalism — for a single fifty-minute session on the combination problem. Having recently sat through a rather muddled seminar in which the issue was treated as a mere epistemic gap about other minds, I want to avoid that confusion entirely. Rather than opening with the familiar intuition that rocks might be conscious, I want the conceptual thread to be carried by the tension between phenomenal bonding and structural decomposition: specifically, the proposal that micro-phenomenal properties might combine through a non-aggregative relation (call it phenomenal bonding), and the attendant difficulty that any such relation either collapses into empirically detectable functional structure — leaving the macro-subject unexplained — or remains observationally invisible and therefore explanatorily idle.

Plan the explanatory arc: which conceptual distinction must be firmly in place before the problem even makes sense to this audience, and why the standard part-whole framing is actively misleading here; whether the chemical-bonding-versus-mere-mixture analogy can serve as the carrying example, and precisely what it must possess and what it must lack if it is not to smuggle in vitalism or physicalism by stealth; the misconceptions this audience will form in sequence — I expect first that panpsychists attribute beliefs to electrons, then that the combination problem is only an epistemic limitation, and finally that phenomenal bonding is a literal physical force — together with the exact point in the sequence where each should be headed off; one diagnostic question I can ask at the end to test whether they have grasped the structural problem rather than merely adopted vague sympathy for micro-experience; and what I should explicitly refuse to explain at this level — for instance, the full taxonomy of combination-problem variants — with a framing that makes the omission sound like disciplinary prudence rather than ignorance.
