I argue that genuine flexibility, self-modification, and moral learning can only emerge from a deeply constrained, almost algorithmic layer of unreflective behaviour; consequently, attempts to eradicate all sphex-like rigidity in artificial systems or in human education do not produce autonomy but rather a brittle, context-dependent pseudo-flexibility that collapses under novel pressure. The audience will include phenomenologically inclined philosophers of action who treat agency as irreducible to functional routine, two AI alignment researchers deeply concerned about rigid reward optimisation and reward hacking, a comparative psychologist who studies hymenoptera cognition, and a virtue ethicist interested in habituation.
