I am applying for a one-year postdoctoral fellowship in moral philosophy and need a concise two-page research proposal. My half-formed idea is this: the familiar debate over whether moral sainthood is undesirable—often associated with the claim that maximal moral goodness crowds out non-moral virtues and personal flourishing—treats moral demands as competing with other goods for space within a life. I propose testing an alternative conceptual framing in which moral demands possess what I call a reflexive structure: the demand to be maximally good itself includes demands about how the agent relates to her own demandingness. On this view, the problem with maximal moral goodness may not be that it eliminates room for humor, art, or partiality, but that it collapses the agent's capacity for moral distance—the standing-back capacity to evaluate whether a particular demand genuinely applies to her in this context. If maximal goodness requires the elimination of that distance, it may be structurally self-defeating rather than merely unattractive. Please turn this into a proposal that includes: a precisely formulated research question; an explanation of why the standard positions—crowding-out objections, demandingness arguments, and virtue-ethical defenses of sainthood—leave this structural question unresolved; a quarter-by-quarter method outlining what conceptual analysis, reconstructed cases, and distinctions you would develop; a clear account of what a successful result would look like, for instance demonstrating that reflexive moral distance is a necessary condition for moral agency rather than a dispensable luxury; and an honest assessment of failure conditions, particularly whether the reflexive-structure distinction collapses into the standard crowding-out argument without adding explanatory power. The proposal should be tractable for a single year, should not include a bibliography or citation-based literature review, and should address a fellowship committee that needs to see both philosophical ambition and realistic scope.
