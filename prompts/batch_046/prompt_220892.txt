For four generations, my family has tended a single hillside orchard not for profit but as a long-term reference plot: the trees are heirloom varieties that only express their full resistance and yield characteristics after roughly seventy years in this particular soil and microclimate. The explicit purpose was never just to feed us, but to produce, by the fifth generation, a complete mature dataset—root architecture, disease response, flavor chemistry—that future agricultural scientists could use to restore regional food systems. I have spent fifteen years measuring, grafting, and documenting with that specific end in view.

I recently learned that a reservoir will flood the valley in about twenty years. The trees will not reach maturity. Because the traits I study are expressed only in this terroir, the cuttings I have preserved elsewhere will not replicate the dataset, and there will be no future scientists to use it. I now feel that continuing is hollow: if the intended beneficiaries never exist and the purpose is permanently blocked, I am performing labor whose point has already evaporated. My partner disagrees. She says I am confusing a project's success with its present value: the measurements still expand knowledge, the work still structures my days, and I can share partial findings with others now. She thinks my sense of futility is an emotional reaction, not a rational one.

But there is a difference, I think, between working toward an uncertain future and working toward a future you know is impossible. Is there a defensible philosophical position that some present activities genuinely derive their point from being embedded in a continuing human story that reaches a specific culmination—so that when that culmination is foreclosed, the present activity loses not just its expected outcome but its reason for being? Or is my partner correct that present meaning, partial knowledge, and personal commitment are always sufficient grounds to continue? If I keep tending these trees, am I honoring a legitimate form of value that depends on an open future, or am I failing to adapt to the fact that the ground of my commitment has disappeared?
