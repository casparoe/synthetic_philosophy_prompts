Hey — I'm working up a dataset of conceptual prompts for science/ideas coverage, and I need one engineered to spec. And please don't lean on some ultra-obscure theory only three grad students have heard of.

Domain: memes and Darwinian cultural transmission.

Task: Construct a one-paragraph, realistic thought experiment (workplace or tight-knit community, no sci-fi, no trolleys) in which a specific behavioral practice spreads with perfect fidelity — zero mutation, like identicle voice-memo instructions copied across shifts — but the practice is measurably harmful to the people performing it. Engineer the case so that a Dawkins-style replicator-memetics account and a host-adaptive cultural-evolution account both have to explain its survival, yet they point to opposite mechanisms: one sees a parasitic meme replicating at host expense, the other sees hosts genuinely benefiting from some hidden coordination payoff. The case should feel fresh — inject some niche, specific detail about teh transmission mechanism so it doesn't sound like every textbook example.

Then do the check:
- State the practice, the transmission method, the conditions where we'd expect it to die out, and why each theory gives the verdict it does.
- Identify the single feature that isolates the two mechanisms while holding everything else fixed; say what happens if you strip that feature away.
- End with how a defender of the theory that comes out badly would attack your scenario — what's the weak point they'd poke.

Structure your answer in these sections, in this order:
1. The scenario (about one paragraph, realistic setting, specific transmission detail)
2. Verdicts from both theories (why replicator view vs host-adaptive view each say what they say)
3. The isolating feature (which variable is doing the work, and what breaks if it's removed)
4. The counterattack (how the defender of the losing theory breaks the case)

Keep it casual, precise, and don't quote any real text in quotation marks — paraphrase if you need to reference a concept. One or two small typos are fine; I'm writing fast.
