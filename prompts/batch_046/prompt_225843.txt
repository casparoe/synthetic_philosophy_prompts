I'm taking a seminar on the metaphysics of patterns and scientific explanation, and I've sketched a position that combines Dennett's framework of real patterns with some recent discussion of functional kinds in synthetic biology and predictive modeling. Before I present it, I want a consistency check. Here is where I stand: (1) A pattern counts as "real" only if it supports counterfactual robustness—that is, it must license reliable predictions across non-actual variations, not merely summarize observed regularities. (2) The design stance is appropriate for both evolved organisms and engineered artifacts, but every design-stance pattern is in principle reducible to a physical-stance description, however computationally intractable that reduction might be. (3) I want to extend the intentional stance to complex socio-technical systems—specifically to market dynamics and to the coherent discourse produced by large language models—treating these attributions as strictly predictive heuristics with no metaphysical commitment to mental states. (4) At the same time, I claim that any pattern reliably detectable through the intentional stance and projectible across counterfactuals is ontologically on a par with physical-stance patterns; there is no separate "merely heuristic" tier of reality. (5) Finally, I maintain that evolutionary selection produces genuinely teleological properties—functional norms that are not just interpretive glosses—so that biological design patterns track mind-independent features of the world. Do these commitments hang together? In particular, does (2)'s reducibility claim undermine (5)'s realism about teleology, or can explanatory autonomy be preserved without ontological independence? Does (3)'s characterization of intentional attributions as "strictly predictive" conflict with (4)'s denial of a heuristic-only category, or is that tension only apparent? And is there an unnoticed problem between (1)'s counterfactual criterion and applying the intentional stance to large language model outputs, given that their behavior may be too context-sensitive to support the kind of robustness (1) demands? Please rank the tensions by severity, distinguish genuine conflicts from ones that dissolve on inspection, and indicate the least costly revision—without presuming which thesis I should be most willing to abandon.
