Her claim is that in Harmonices Mundi Kepler wasn't doing judicial prediction but mapping celestial angles onto harmonic ratios that structure physical law, not human destiny. She argues this makes him closer to a structural realist about cosmic order than to a court astrologer, and she uses it to push back against the idea that 17th-century astrology was just pseudoscience kept alive by institutional patronige.

I need about eight questions for a 45-minute session. Start with how she reads Kepler's talk of an anima mundi — is that metaphor or ontology? Then move to the standard objection that he still cast horoscopes for patrons; I want you to anticipate her likely reply (probably that those were performative social contracts, not epistemic commitments) and build a follow-up that presses on whether that distinction actually holds water. End with something about whether her structural reading collapses astrology into pure mathematics or saves it as a distinct mode of inquiry, and maybe one question about what evidence could make her abandon the framing entirely.

Please seperate what historians generally agree on about Kepler's actual practice from the genuinely contested parts of her interpretation, and don't present her structural-realist framing as if it were the consensus view among people who actually study this stuff. Keep it casual — I'm using these to prep my students, not for a peer-reviewed journal.
