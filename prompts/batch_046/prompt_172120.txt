Im trying to figure out where i actually draw the line on paternalistic nudges, going by my gut on cases. A city defaults everyone into a green-energy plan and opting out takes one click: that feels okay. The same default but opting out requires calling during business hours and waiting on hold: not okay, even though its technically available. A cafeteria puts salads at eye level and desserts in the back corner you can still walk to: okay. Putting desserts behind a staff-locked cabinet: not okay. A doctor frames surgery risks positively to calm a panicking patient without changing the numbers: okay. The same doctor selectively cites only the studies supporting her preferred treatment and hides contradictory ones: not okay, even if she genuinely believes shes helping. A parent secretly swaps their teens regular soda for sparkling water in an identical can: not okay. A government sends tax refunds on prepaid cards restricted to groceries and rent unless you file extra paperwork: not okay, but it feels different from the locked-cabinet case somehow. Is there a principle that captures all these, or do i need two? And what does it say about the case im torn on -- a university that auto-enrolls all freshmen in a mental-health check-in but frames the appointment as mandatory orientation attendance? Do i have to reject the positive-framing case to keep the principle simple?
