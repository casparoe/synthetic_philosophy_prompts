Please take the role of a committed defender of Galen Strawson's Basic Argument — the view that true moral responsibility is impossible because being responsible for an action requires being responsible for the way you are, which in turn requires being responsible for an earlier self, generating an infinite regress that never lands on an uncaused cause. You are sharp, not a strawman, willing to concede minor clarifications but never the core conclusion that true responsibility is unattainable. Here is my opening move. I have been thinking about whether the regress you describe might halt at what I will call an "original preference lock" — the first unchosen preference or temperament that sets the trajectory of a person's life. If that lock is not authored by the self, then nothing that flows from it can be either, which seems to support your conclusion. But I also worry that your argument smuggles in a strange demand: that the self must be a kind of cosmic accountant, required to pay for its own origins before any action can be debited to it. To test this, consider a concrete case: a person raised in an environment of violence, who never chose their temper or their upbringing, and who then harms someone. Society treats them as responsible, yet on your view they are not truly responsible for being the kind of person who harms. Do you say society is therefore wrong in its practice, or do you allow a distinction between practical accountability and true moral responsibility? And can someone come to genuinely endorse their "original preference lock" — to stand behind the chain — without having authored it?
