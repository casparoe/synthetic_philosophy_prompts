I'm an advanced high school student working on an independent philosophy seminar, and I need you to act as a professional philosopher of epistemology. Before drafting anything, please think carefully and begin by brainstorming the precise logical structure of Cliffordian evidentialism and Jamesian pragmatism—especially on doxastic voluntarism, the symmetry of epistemic risk, and whether institutional working acceptance is genuinely distinct from full belief—so that your formulations stay neutral and exact.

Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a Cliffordian evidentialist and a Jamesian defender of the will to believe. To keep the exercise from repeating standard examples, apply their dispute to this concrete case: a municipal public-health board must decide whether to act on preliminary, probabilistic epidemiological data about an emerging pathogen. Full verification will take years, but inaction risks catastrophic harm, while acting on incomplete evidence risks severe unjustified restrictions. The board must adopt a provisional cognitive stance that is neither pure suspension of judgment nor full conviction.

The document should have four parts:

A. Agreed claims (numbered): precise statements both sides can sign regarding the nature of belief, the moral weight of epistemic error, the distinction between full doxastic commitment and institutional working acceptance, and the conditions under which evidence counts as sufficient for action-guiding cognition.

B. Disagreements table: the exact points of divergence, phrased in wording each side accepts for its own view. Key issues must include (i) whether practical urgency can ever justify belief without proportionate evidence; (ii) whether working acceptance avoids the Cliffordian prohibition or is merely belief in disguise; and (iii) whether the moral risk of false belief equals that of false suspension in institutional contexts.

C. Cruxes: identify two or three sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party. For each crux, propose a concrete conceptual test or scenario both agree is relevant—such as whether indirect doxastic control through institutional protocols counts as voluntary belief-formation—and record in advance what each camp predicts the outcome will be.

D. Residual assessment: state whether part of the dispute is verbal (for example, over the extension of the term belief versus acceptance), specify exactly which part, and explain why clarifying the terminology does or does not dissolve the disagreement.

Please do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Keep the tone formal, focused, and appropriate for an advanced high school philosophy project.
