I have been trying to understand something I noticed while caring for my mother through the final years of her Alzheimer's disease, and then in the years since her death. Before she lost her language, she had a very particular reaction to news headlines: a sharp, quick intake of breath, followed by a single-word verdict — usually "absurd" or "predictable." I never made a conscious decision to imitate her, yet now, years later, I find myself producing the exact same sound, with the same words, in response to the same kinds of stories. What strikes me is not that I am remembering her reaction; rather, the reaction seems to arise from the same place inside me that it once arose inside her, as though a pattern had migrated and was running independently.

This led me to think about an idea I have encountered in Douglas Hofstadter's writing: that an "I" is not confined to one brain but is more like a pattern that can be copied, in part or in whole, into other minds. If that is correct, then perhaps my mother did not fully disappear. Perhaps a fragment of her "I" is still operating — not as a ghost or a preserved memory, but as an active pattern in my own behavior. But I worry I am only describing influence, grief, or habit formation, and I want to know whether there is a meaningful distinction.

So I wrote out some notes trying to clarify the conditions under which a self-pattern could genuinely be distributed. For instance, must the copied pattern be conscious of itself in the new host? Can two separate centers run the same pattern simultaneously, or must the original center dissolve before the copy counts as persistence? I am not a philosopher or a scientist; I am a carpenter who reads late at night. I would be grateful for comments on whether my reasoning holds together, particularly regarding the line between distributed persistence and ordinary mimicry or emotional inheritance. Am I missing an obvious objection? And can this view be sustained without appealing to a soul, without reducing a person to nothing more than a replicable set of behaviors?
