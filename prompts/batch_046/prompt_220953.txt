According to Strawson, only the personal attitudes are essential to moral responsibility; the vicarious ones are socially constructed conventions that could be suspended without real loss. He argues that determinism threatens responsibility only if it forces us to adopt the objective attitude—the detached, clinical stance reserved for abnormal or dangerous people—universally. While Strawson concedes that consistent determinism should rationally push us toward the objective attitude, he believes human psychology makes such universal adoption impossible, so reactive attitudes survive as unavoidable evolutionary adaptations. Thus moral responsibility remains intact not because determinism is false or genuinely compatible with freedom, but because we are constitutionally incapable of treating everyone as objects of management.”
