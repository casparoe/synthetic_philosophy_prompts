Please describe the most important implications of interpreting Plutarch's Parallel Lives as a form of secular hagiography whose pedagogical aim is not to furnish imitable moral exemplars, but rather to calibrate the reader's judgment about how civic virtue is systematically deformed by the demands of political life; in particular, consider what this interpretation implies for the moral utility of modern comparative biography of public figures, and whether such biography should aim at commemoration, critical calibration, or both.
