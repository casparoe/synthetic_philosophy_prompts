I have noticed a recurring argumentative move in debates about whether borderline entities—viruses, synthetic protocells, dormant seeds, or running evolutionary simulations—should be counted as alive. The pattern usually begins with a multi-part characterization: something is alive if it metabolizes, reproduces, maintains internal stability, and adapts to its environment. A critic then introduces a case that clearly realizes some of these capacities but lacks others. A virus copies itself and evolves, yet it has no autonomous energy metabolism; a minimal synthetic cell regulates its chemistry and maintains boundaries, yet it cannot reproduce; a digital simulation evolves populations and corrects its own parameters, yet it has no physical substrate at all. Rather than conceding that the case falls outside the category, or abandoning the multi-part characterization, the defender typically splits the difference. They say the virus is alive in an evolutionary respect but not a metabolic one; that the simulation realizes functional life without material life; or that the dormant seed is alive at the organizational level though not at the activity level. The definition is not rejected, and the counterexample is not rejected; instead, the criteria are distributed across different scales, respects, or "levels" so that the example satisfies enough of them in enough ways to remain inside the boundary, but only in a qualified or partial fashion.

I have seen the same distributive maneuver appear in debates about consciousness—where an entity might possess phenomenal awareness without access consciousness or self-reflection—and about moral agency, where an actor might display instrumental rationality without full autonomy. This makes me think the pattern may not be specific to biology at all, but I want to know whether philosophy of biology has already given it a precise name and mapped its limits.

Is there an established term for this specific dialectical strategy—saving a cluster or multi-criteria definition by assigning its individual conditions to different ontological levels or functional respects of a counterexample? Or is it best understood as an application of broader ideas like Wittgensteinian family resemblance, prototype theory, or what is sometimes called "graded realization"? I am not asking for a bare label. I want to know exactly what the phenomenon is, how it differs from simply calling something a "borderline case," whether it can be applied to essentialist definitions or only to pluralist cluster accounts, and where its edges lie: for instance, does it require that all the original criteria remain in play, or can some be quietly dropped once the distributive framing is adopted? If there is no established name, then what should the boundaries of such a concept be, and what are its closest analogues in other domains?
