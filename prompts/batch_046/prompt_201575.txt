Write an essay defending a verdict on whether a statue and the clay that makes it up are strictly identical, partially identical, or non-identical. Center your analysis on the modal argument from differing persistence conditions: squashing destroys the statue but not the clay. Evaluate the main competing frameworks—Wiggins’s relative-identity account, Baker’s constitution-without-identity view, and the four-dimensionalist temporal-parts response—paying special attention to how each handles a modified case in which the statue is built from interchangeable clay modules that can be dispersed and later reassembled into a different statue while the original clay aggregate remains intact. Reach and justify a clear conclusion about which framework best accommodates both the standard and the modular case. As you do so, distinguish what is settled about the logical form of constitution relations from what is genuinely contested about modal persistence, and avoid presenting any single contested metaphysical position as the established consensus.
