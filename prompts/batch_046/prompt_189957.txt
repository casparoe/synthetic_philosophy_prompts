I sit on the Clinical Ethics and Advance Planning Panel at St Edmund's Hospital. We must circulate a written opinion by Friday regarding Mrs Elena Vasquez, aged 67, who executed a detailed advance directive four years ago refusing artificial nutrition and hydration should she reach what the document terms irreversible dissolution of autobiographical coherence with intact biological maintenance. She now lives with advanced Alzheimer's disease; she no longer recognises her family with consistency, yet during intermittent lucid intervals she expresses a clear preference to continue receiving food and water, and she actively disputes the clinical judgement that her condition is irreversible. Her daughter, appointed welfare attorney for health decisions, contends that the directive ought to be suspended, arguing that these present expressions indicate a genuine transformation in Mrs Vasquez's core values rather than mere cognitive noise, and that enforcing the document now would privilege an obsolete psychological continuity over the living person before us. The treating consultants maintain that the specified threshold has plainly been satisfied, and that permitting episodic preferences to override a meticulously deliberated prior decision defeats the very rationale of advance planning.

The panel must recommend a definite course—immediate withdrawal of artificial nutrition in line with the directive, continuation with a structured reassessment timetable, or adoption of an interim observation protocol—together with an explicit time frame and implementation conditions. Our opinion must treat the daughter's argument with full intellectual seriousness, engaging the philosophical problem of whether an earlier self retains binding authority over a present being who appears to have undergone a substantive change in evaluative outlook, rather than resolving the matter by mere appeal to legal formalism. Please draft the opinion I shall circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should state the recommendation clearly, set out the reasoning that supports it, address honestly the strongest consideration in favour of the contrary view, and include concrete steps with deadlines that the clinical team and the family can respond to.
