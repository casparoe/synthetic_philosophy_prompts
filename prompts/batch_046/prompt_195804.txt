so i've been thinking about how hospitals actually deal with patients in vegetative states vs locked-in ones. like, the protocol for declaring brain death is all about brainstem reflexes and apnea, but if the family says he's still in there doctors will say the person is gone even if there are still reflexive movements. meanwhile a locked-in patient who can only blink once gets full moral status immediately, even though they cant do much more behaviorally than the brain-dead body does. and now some wards are testing LLM chat interfaces for unresponsive patients, but the ethics rules say any output has to be treated as not a real person talking unless it passes a staff-run consistency check that wouldnt be applied to a verbal patient.

what view of other minds does all this presuppose? is it that consciousness is tied to specific neural circuits, or that its tied to interactive capacity, or something about hidden inner states that we can only infer? and is the practice actually consistent, or does it smuggle in different theories for different cases? if we replaced the implicit view with a straight behaviorist one, or with a view that says we never really know others minds and should just treat all responsive bodies equally, what parts of hospital policy would have to change? and does the LLM rule reveal that we're actually using a kind of turing-test intuition for personhood without admitting it?
