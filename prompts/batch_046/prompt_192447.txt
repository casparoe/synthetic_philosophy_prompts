The debate between Norman Malcolm and Daniel Dennett over whether dreams count as experiences has been at an impasse for decades: Malcolm argues that conscious experience requires the possibility of wakeful report, so sleep-state mental life cannot be experiential, while Dennett treats dream reports as frequently confabulated reconstructions of whatever fragmentary mental events occurred, denying them the status of direct experiential records. The same cases—lucid dreaming, false awakenings, retrospective color reports—are rehearsed without resolution, and neither side seems to change the other’s starting commitments. I want a structural diagnosis rather than an adjudication. Consider specifically whether both sides are neglecting a middle option: that dream reports might express a dispositional narrative schema that was never activated as an occurrent phenomenal state during sleep, yet remains genuinely first-personal in a way that is neither straightforward experience nor mere fabrication.
