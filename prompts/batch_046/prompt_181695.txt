I serve on my university's AI governance committee, which must decide by next Friday whether to approve the continued deployment of the adaptive assessment platform used in first-year logic courses. The system assigns grades through an opaque neural classifier, then generates explanatory rationales for each student via a separate language module. The rationales sound pedagogically sound — they cite logical errors, missing premises, and inferential progressions — but they are constructed after the grade is fixed and do not reflect any processing that actually caused the score. Some committee members argue these are harmless pedagogical scaffolding; others contend they are free-floating rationales, reasons that appear in institutional discourse without being anchored in any reasoning process of the system itself, and that presenting them as explanations therefore amounts to a form of misrepresentation. The vendor insists that students benefit regardless of causal origin, and that requiring genuine reasoning inside the grader is an anthropocentric prejudice that confuses explanation with mechanism.

Please draft the committee's decision memo in formal institutional voice. It should recommend one concrete course among approval with binding conditions, suspension pending redesign, or rejection, with a specified timeline for implementation. The memo must treat the free-floating-rationale objection seriously rather than dismissing it as mysticism, but it should also address the vendor's pragmatic claim with equal rigor. Include explicit criteria the committee can apply to distinguish acceptable post-hoc pedagogical explanation from unacceptable rationalization, and specify the technical or procedural conditions that would have to be met for the platform to remain in use. Develop the conceptual distinction between anchored and free-floating reasons on its own terms without relying on named philosophers or outside citations, and include an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side of your recommendation.
