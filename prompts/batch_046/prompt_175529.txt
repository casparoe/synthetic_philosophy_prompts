I'm revising the final exam for my philosophy of mind seminar after a student recently pressed me on a point I had not fully resolved: if consciousness is a user illusion—an interface constructed for cognitive access rather than a direct revelation of reality—why does phenomenal experience present itself as transparent, as though we access the world unmediated rather than through a representational layer? I want an essay question that makes this tension—call it the transparency problem—its central crux, so that students cannot answer by summarizing standard accounts. The question should ask students to evaluate three resolutions: that transparency is a design feature of the illusion, that it reveals a failure of introspective access, or that it undermines the user-illusion model altogether. Please provide: (1) the exam question itself; (2) a model answer of no more than 600 words that reaches and justifies a conclusion; and (3) a marking scheme in bullet points totaling no more than 250 words, specifying the moves that earn an A (identifying the precise conflict between epistemic mediation and ontological commitment, separating the settled claim that representational content is constructed from the contested claim that the self is therefore illusory, and resolving the tension with a reasoned conclusion) versus a B (accurately describing the user-illusion view but treating transparency as an afterthought or conflating representational mediation with metaphysical illusion). In your response, clearly separate what is settled in the relevant cognitive-science and philosophical literature from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus view.
