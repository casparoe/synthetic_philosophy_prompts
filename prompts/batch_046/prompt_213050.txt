I've been playing around with a kind of hybrid reading of Nietzsche's eternal recurrence that isn't quite the cosmological version or the standard ethical "greatest weight" interpretation. The view I want to defend is that recurrence only applies to lives that achieve a specific kind of normative closure—basically, the cycle only genuinely loops back if the life ends with an unreserved affirmation of it's own repetition, and without that affirmative "seal" what you have is just a qualitative duplicate, not the same life recurring. So the eternal return isn't describing every physical repetition, but filtering for ones where the will-to-power achieves this self-affirming loop, which makes recurrence partly metaphysical and partly ethical in a weird combined way.

What would actually count against this? I want the objections, internal inconsistencies, or interpretive problems that should make me drop it—sorted roughly by how fatal they'd be—including whether Nietzsche ever suggests that affirmation comes after the recurrence is already physically determined, which would break the causal loop I'm proposing. Also, is this view just unfalsifiable, since I can always say "well, that duplicate didn't have the seal"? And if so, is that a serious defect for a philosophical position, or just par for the course when you're dealing with existential metaphysics?
