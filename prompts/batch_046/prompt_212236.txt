As a doctoral student working on embedded decision theory and the metaphysics of agency, I have been trying to stabilize a coherent position on rational self-reference, temporal extension, and the agent-environment boundary. Here is the package I have arrived at; I am unsure whether it is jointly tenable, and I would like a rigorous audit of its internal consistency rather than an exposition of standard views.

(1) I hold that embedded agency eliminates any ontologically fixed boundary between agent and environment; the boundary should be treated as a pragmatic framing parameter that shifts with the decision problem rather than as a metaphysically given demarcation.

(2) In cases where an agent's actions are statistically correlated with outcomes through mechanisms outside direct causal control—Newcomb-style prediction problems, counterfactual mugging, and certain medical-testing analogues—I think rationality requires evidential reasoning, conditioning on the act, because these correlations are constitutive parts of the agent's embedded world-model.

(3) I maintain that rational self-modification is constrained by continuity: an agent can only legitimately adopt a new decision procedure if it can derive that adoption as the output of its current procedure applied to the problem of self-improvement, without invoking an external archimedean point.

(4) I reject purely indexical or centered-world reasoning in intertemporal choice; practical rationality should be evaluated from a perspective that treats temporal parts of the agent as symmetrically situated, analogously to spatial parts in a distributed system, so that no single temporal slice enjoys privileged deliberative authority.

(5) I take Ulysses-style pre-commitment devices to be rational precisely when they are understood as causal interventions on future cognitive states—treating those states as environmental variables—rather than as internal bindings through which a unitary present self constrains an alien future self.

(6) I believe that hierarchical predictive processing supplies the correct formal framework for embedded agency, with the operative self-boundary determined by precision-weighted predictions, and that this boundary ought to be dynamically redrawn whenever predictive accuracy demands such a revision.

Some of these commitments feel as though they might pull against one another—particularly where reasoning norms apply across self-modification, and where the stability required for continuity interacts with the fluidity of the predictive boundary—but I cannot tell whether these frictions are genuine contradictions or only apparent ones. Please examine the set for consistency: order the tensions by severity; for each, specify whether resolving it requires abandoning one of the commitments or can be achieved through reinterpretation; identify which pairs are genuinely in conflict versus which only seem so; and indicate the least costly path to a stable package. Be specific about the logical entailments—for instance, whether (2) commits me to apply evidential reasoning within (3)'s continuity constraint, whether (4) actually undermines the present-node standing presupposed in (5), and whether (6) provides the resources to stabilize (3) or instead dissolves the very notion of a current procedure that (3) requires. Do not assume I am prepared to drop any particular commitment in advance.
