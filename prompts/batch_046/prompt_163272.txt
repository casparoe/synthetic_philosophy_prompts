I'm considering accepting a position at an isolated outpost that requires a one-time transfer procedure: my brain is scanned at full resolution, the data is transmitted, and an identical body with identical psychology is reconstructed on-site. The original remains in a medically induced suspension for two days, then is peacefully discontinued. The provider also offers an "archive" add-on: for the same fee, they can construct two recipient bodies, activate one, and keep the other in perpetual unconscious storage. I'm torn. If I go through with it, will the person who wakes up there genuinely be me, or just a copy with my memories? And does the archive copy mean I survive twice, survive not at all, or that there's a hidden "real me" lying dormant? My partner insists this is no different from boarding a plane—it's travel with extra steps. My colleague argues that the archive makes it worse: there'd be an unawakened version that is numerically identical to me, so personal survival can't be what's at stake. I want to understand what's genuinely at risk for me, not just the engineering. Is my partner right that I'm overthinking continuity, or is my colleague right that the archive reveals something broken about the idea of survival?
