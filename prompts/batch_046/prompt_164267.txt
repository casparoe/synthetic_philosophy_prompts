Hey, I'm putting together a one-page proposal for a small philosophy-of-AI workshop grant — six months, mostly just me working through ideas with occasional feedback I recieve. Background: I'm retired after thirty years in molecular biology, and since then I've been reading philosphy of science and lately a lot about AI alignment debates. I think there's a framing problem people inside the field miss, probably because in experimental science you learn early that your assay carries hidden theoretical commitments, and I'm not sure AI safety debate applies the same skepticism to its evaluation tools.

Here's my half-baked idea. There's been discussion about "obfuscated arguments" in AI safety — cases where one debater hides reasoning to win. But I think the literature looks in the wrong place. It treats evaluation benchmarks and alignment metrics as neutal infrastructure, and assumes obfuscation happens in explicit value claims. I want to ask whether the camouflage is actually structural: when debaters argue about which benchmark to use, or how to operationalize "harmful reasoning," they're often embedding substantive claims about what kinds of cognition are dangerous without ever stating them as premises. So my precise question is: can we design a debate protocoll that forces these methodological premises into the open, and if we do, does that reduce strategic ambiguity or just push disagreement up to the meta-level? Why existing work leaves this open: proposed debate protocols focus on information asymmetry and strategic misrepresentation between agents; they don't treat the evaluation framework itself as doing argumentative work. Even critics assume that once you fix the rules of evaluation, remaining disagreement is either empirical or normative in an explicit sense.

My approach, month by month: months 1–2 map recent public disputes about evaluation frameworks (things like HELM-style benchmarking, or constitutional AI evaluation protocols) and identify methodological choices that implicitly commit to a view about safe reasoning. Months 3–4 formalize a "premise extraction" step — a protocoll addition requiring debaters to articulate what normative claim about risk is embedded in their proposed metric. Months 5–6 assess whether this extraction actually clarifies disagreements or creates circular meta-debates, using conceptual case studies rather than running models. Success looks like a clear distinction between genuine methodological disagreement and disagreement masked by shared methodological language. Failure looks like either evaluation choices turning out genuinely neutal with respect to safety claims, or the extraction being circular — you need prior agreement on risk to identify hidden risk premises. I know six months is too short for empirical AI work, so this stays philosophical and conceptual. No bibliography needed in this draft — I'll fill citations later if this moves forward. Could you help me turn this into a proper proposal with an honest scope check? I want to keep my voice — I'm not a professional philosopher, just a retired scientist who noticed we never check whether our benchmark is measuring the risk we think it is.
