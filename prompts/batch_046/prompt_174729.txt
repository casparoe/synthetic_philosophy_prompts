I've been thinking there are three open lines that matter: first, whether calling something a "historical actor" needs intentionality or just counterfactual power; second, how to write narratives where human choices and climate/volcanic/germ effects are interwined without one swallowing the other; and third, whether determinism is even a real risk in current historiography or just a philospher's strawman. But I'm also stuck on this more specific thing: volcanic eruptions get archived as discrete "events" with dates, while epidemic waves are usually treated as slow background conditions, even when both operate on similar temporal scales. Is that distinction actually doing conceptual work, or is it just an artifact of how we preserve records?

Given about two years and no science lab access, which of these lines should I actually prioritize? I need a defended ordering—not just "this is interesting"—with criteria like tractability for a historian, how many other debates depend on it, and whether answering it changes how anyone writes actual history. Where does that archive/event vs background distinction fit? And what evidence over the next year would make me reorder them?
