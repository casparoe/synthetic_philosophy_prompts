As a doctoral candidate in philosophy of public institutions, I sit on the monthly review panel for a municipal cooperative that distributes discretionary research supplements to interdisciplinary teams based on desert rather than on need, equality, or market rate; we have no external metrics, and each dossier must be decided in under forty minutes from internal records alone. I want a reusable decision procedure that operationalizes the following hybrid desert framework—developed for this context but built from standard philosophical distinctions—without collapsing into pure contributionism or pure luck-egalitarianism: institutional desert comprises three separable layers, (A) effort-desert calibrated to task difficulty relative to the agent’s counterfactual opportunity baseline (the resources and support the institution actually made available, not what was nominally listed), (B) contribution-desert measured against the collective’s explicitly stated goals for the funding cycle, with a mandatory corrective adjustment whenever evaluation metrics systematically undervalue certain kinds of labor, and (C) character-desert, a limited non-quantifiable check on whether the team violated or exemplified the cooperative’s procedural norms in ways that should affect reward regardless of output. The procedure must pose questions in a fixed order, specify what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, mark precisely where an unavoidable values judgment requires full-panel sign-off rather than individual reviewer discretion, and state exactly where it outputs a recommendation, a denial, or a deferral to a separate ethics review; it must also handle direct conflicts among layers, such as high contribution-desert paired with hidden privilege that lowers effort-desert, or high effort-desert paired with suppressed contribution-desert due to institutional measurement bias. Apply it to three dossiers: (1) a senior team with exceptional quantitative output that relied on undisclosed access to proprietary data unavailable to others; (2) a junior team with modest measured output that overcame a documented, institutionally unacknowledged resource deprivation (no lab access for two quarters); and (3) a team with average contribution whose work was systematically misclassified by our rubric—yielding a score the corrective layer would revise sharply upward—but whose members also withheld preliminary data from peer reviewers in violation of procedural norms. One application must yield a result that conflicts with my own intuitive judgment, and you should flag that tension explicitly rather than smoothing it over. Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot settle—especially the relative weighting of the three layers and the selection of the counterfactual baseline—and do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or recommendations for further sources. Please lead with the bottom line—the essential structure of the procedure—in the first sentence; then provide the reasoning, the ordered checklist with decision rules, the three worked applications, and the explicit limits. No preamble, no restatement of the question, and no commentary outside the requested structure.
