Please write these opening remarks—approximately five to seven minutes when spoken—in a formal, polished register suitable for an academic occasion but accessible enough that the non-specialists in the room are not lost. The philosophical idea must do real structural work: let it govern which analogies you permit, which promises you withhold, and what kind of intellectual humility you ask of both the medievalists and the scientists present. Specifically, I want you to inject a somewhat niche framing: ask whether an interventionist model of causation, which defines causal relations relative to an agent’s capacity to manipulate variables, implicitly reintroduces the very habit or customary expectation (the al-Ghazālīan notion of al-ʿāda) that occasionalism sought to dissolve—namely, the assumption that the agent’s epistemic access creates a necessary bridge between cause and effect. This injected problem should shape the speech’s architecture, not merely ornament it. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; speak from conviction and conceptual analysis alone. Make sure the remarks avoid both false reconciliation and unnecessary obscurity: the philosophers present should feel a genuine knot has been tied, not that a familiar debate has been rehearsed for comfort.
