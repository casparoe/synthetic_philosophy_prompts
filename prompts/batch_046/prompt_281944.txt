Design a decision tree that sorts philosophical positions on the following question: if every structural and functional detail of Einstein’s brain were printed in a massive book such that a human reader’s cognitive processing could instantiate the same relational patterns, could that book—alone, with a reader, or in some other configuration—be conscious? Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names. The root should split on whether consciousness requires real-time causal dynamics among physically continuous parts, or whether static structural isomorphism in an arbitrary medium (ink on paper arranged in sentences) can in principle suffice. For the branch that allows static isomorphism, split on whether the conscious subject is the book itself, the interpreting reader, or the irreducible book-reader system. For the branch that denies static isomorphism, split on whether the failure is specifically biological (no neural substrate) or more generally about the absence of real-time integrated information processing regardless of substrate.

This yields eight logical combinations. For each leaf, provide: (i) the name of the position if one is standard in philosophy of mind (functionalism, biological naturalism, illusionism, panpsychism of patterns, distributed-cognition views, etc.), marking it unnamed if no standard view occupies that node; (ii) one sentence describing its most awkward commitment, and for unnamed combinations diagnose whether the gap is likely incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; and (iii) a concrete alteration scenario—such as pages being burned, the text being read backward, being translated into binary on a hard drive, or being parsed by an AI without human comprehension—stating whether consciousness is preserved, destroyed, or ambiguous on that view.

In addition, flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate despite representing distinct positions, explaining whether separation requires a further distinction outside these three branches or whether the two positions are genuinely equivalent on this question. Ensure every branching node is phrased as a concrete claim or case that a respondent could answer without already knowing which philosophical label it corresponds to; do not include any question that asks whether one is a functionalist or references obscure theories from the last two decades not part of standard curricula. The tree should have no more than eight leaves and should not require bibliographic knowledge to navigate.
