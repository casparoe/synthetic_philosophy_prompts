Student answer: Fischer's study Griff nach der Weltmacht demonstrated that the German leadership—particularly the Chancellor and the General Staff—did not stumble into war but actively exploited the assassination at Sarajevo to launch a premeditated conflict aimed at European hegemony. Critics such as Gerhard Ritter objected mainly out of national defensiveness, but their empirical challenges have been largely discredited. Philosophical worries about collective agency are therefore beside the point: if a state possesses decision-making structures and produces harmful outcomes, it automatically bears full moral responsibility, and the Versailles war-guilt clause was essentially correct in attributing guilt to the nation as a whole.
