I'm closing a graduate seminar in the philosophy of mathematics that has spent the semester on whether actual infinities are coherent or whether they are only potential infinities illegitimately reified. The students have reached the familiar stalemate: the Cantorians invoke Hilbert's hotel — every new guest accommodated by shifting residents to even-numbered rooms — as proof that completed infinite sets are perfectly manageable, and they pair this with Cantor's argument that some infinite collections have no one-to-one correspondence with others, treating size comparison as unproblematic once infinity is actualized. The finitists reply that the hotel only demonstrates an unending procedure, never a completed totality, and that Cantor's pairing argument smuggles in the very reification it claims to prove by treating an endless process of comparison as if it yielded a definite result about finished sets. I want to close our final session with a poem that does not resolve this but embodies it structurally.

Write a villanelle — nineteen lines, five tercets followed by a quatrain, with two refrains that rhyme with each other and with the first and third lines of the opening tercet — in which the two refrains are the finitist's and the Cantorian's competing descriptions of the same infinite operation: for instance, a line about always being able to open another room, and a line about every room already being filled. The refrains should rotate through the tercets according to the form's strict rules, passing each other without merging, and then stand side by side in the final quatrain, still unreconciled. The villanelle's compulsive repetition should evoke the infinite regress the finitist fears, while the rhyme scheme's forced closure should evoke the Cantorian's claim that infinity can be bounded and compared. Include specific allusions — the shifting of guests to free up odds, the pairing of sets, the claim that one infinity is larger than another — rendered accurately enough that a specialist recognizes Hilbert's hotel and Cantor's argument rather than finding mere name-dropping, but keep the poem a poem first: leave the philosophical tension genuinely open, avoid sermonizing for either side, and let the form carry the conceptual weight. The audience will be the seminar participants, who should be able to tell which refrain belongs to which position and why they end up adjacent but unmerged.
