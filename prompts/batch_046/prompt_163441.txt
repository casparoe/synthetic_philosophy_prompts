I am preparing a brief position paper for a graduate seminar on consciousness and representationalism, and I want a consistency audit before I present. My view is hybrid and somewhat idiosyncratic: I understand consciousness as a user illusion generated by predictive-processing architectures—specifically, a transparent, ego-centered interface that the brain constructs not to reveal its own neural machinery but to streamline unified action-selection. From this I infer that there is no metaphysically hard problem, only an explanatory gap that reflects the interface’s structural blindness to its generating mechanisms; once the functional and representational story is complete, nothing ontologically mysterious remains. At the same time, I maintain that phenomenal concepts are genuinely conceptually irreducible—knowing every physical and functional fact about color vision does not entail knowing what red looks like—but I take this irreducibility to be purely epistemic, not ontological: phenomenal concepts refer to the very same representational states, just via a special first-person mode of presentation internal to the interface. I also hold that zombies are metaphysically impossible, because any system that duplicates the relevant predictive architecture, self-modeling dynamics, and transparency conditions necessarily generates the interface, leaving no missing ingredient. Nevertheless, I treat the zombie scenario as genuinely conceivable, and I generally regard conceivability as a fallible but real guide to metaphysical possibility; I think the zombie intuition is an artifact of the interface’s transparency—analogous to trying to observe the back of one’s own head with a mirror—rather than a case of straightforward conceptual confusion. Finally, I deny that there is anything it is like to be a thermostat or a simple reflex arc, but I think consciousness comes in degrees: any system with cross-modal predictive integration possesses minimal proto-phenomenal structure, even if it lacks the full transparent self-model that constitutes human consciousness.

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision for each real tension? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally problematic. Say whether any single revision—dropping the degrees thesis, sharply restricting the reliability of conceivability, or redefining the interface so that it is not strictly necessary—would clean up the most inconsistencies at once. Be specific about what each commitment forces me to accept or reject, and indicate where the position is underdetermined versus genuinely incoherent.
