Write a decision-tree taxonomy for a graduate philosophy-of-science seminar that sorts structural-realist positions by what they actually claim, not by the labels they wear. Start from the commitment that something is preserved across scientific revolutions. First branch: is what survives purely formal or mathematical structure, or does it include interpreted law-patterns? If formal, split on whether unobservable entities are eliminated as relata or retained in some form (giving epistemic and radical-ontic branches). If interpreted patterns, split on whether continuity requires identical interpretation of variables or permits 'discontinuous preservation'—where the mathematics survives but the interpretation of the variables shifts radically. At deeper levels, apply these three diagnostics: (i) does the claim extend beyond physics to special sciences such as macroeconomics or cognitive architecture; (ii) are mathematical structures taken as modal (governing possibilities) or categorical (describing actual relations); (iii) does the view treat empirically hidden structures as genuinely real? The tree must contain exactly eight leaves. Required leaves: Worrall-style epistemic structural realism; Ladyman-Ross-style ontic structural realism (objects eliminated); a moderate variant retaining concrete objects as relata; structural empiricism that accepts structural descriptions but denies corresponding ontology; and one combination marked 'unnamed'. Every other leaf should carry a standard name if one exists, otherwise 'unnamed' with a one-sentence diagnosis of why the space might be empty (incoherent, unstable, or unexplored). Each internal node must be a yes/no question answerable without knowing which interpretation it yields. Each leaf must state the position (or 'unnamed') and, in one sentence, its single most awkward commitment. Flag any pair of named positions that the tree cannot genuinely separate, explaining why in one sentence. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists. Keep the response under 300 words; present it as an indented tree or nested bullet list.
