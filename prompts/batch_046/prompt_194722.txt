Write a dialogue between Mira and Jonas, two philosophers of equal standing. Mira defends guilt as the better moral emotion on the grounds that it is agent-centered, repair-oriented, and compatible with autonomous self-assessment; she also maintains that a culture can only genuinely choose an emotional framework if its members can reflexively justify it from a position outside that framework. Jonas defends shame, but specifically in a form he calls "architected collective shame": he describes an intentional community that has abolished private guilt in favor of anonymous, ritualized "accountability circles" in which wrongs are named collectively but not individually blamed, producing a shared sense of relational failure rather than personal culpability. Jonas claims this is not disguised guilt but a genuinely different phenomenology, and that cultures can rationally redesign their moral emotional ecology just as they redesign legal institutions.

The dialogue should contain real back-and-forth. Mira should argue that such circles still depend on internalized norms that function like guilt, that anonymity does not eliminate individual self-reproach but merely displaces it onto the collective, and that if socialization shapes members from childhood, the community's "choice" is illusory rather than autonomous. Jonas should respond directly: he should explain how the absence of individual attribution changes the emotional structure from culpability to belonging, defend the claim that reflexive justification is possible within a redesigned tradition rather than only from a neutral Archimedean point, and address whether conditioning necessarily undermines autonomy or merely provides the conditions for it. Both should engage the question of whether guilt and shame are culturally selectable instruments or deep structural features of moral psychology, and neither should talk past the other.
