The student writes: The entropy-margin approach largely succeeds in defining side effects without full value specification. First, selecting macro-variables is not deeply normative; we simply choose observable quantities that are easy to track, not because they are intrinsically valuable. Weighting by inverse natural variance is just a statistical normalization technique—it ensures that noisy variables do not dominate the measure—so it carries no hidden preference for stability in any particular domain. Second, entropy increase does not always mean harm; for instance, mixing two benign gases raises entropy without causing damage. This shows the measure is structurally sensitive but not value-laden.
