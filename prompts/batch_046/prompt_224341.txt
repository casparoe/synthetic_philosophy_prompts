hey, i'm in my third year of a legal phil diss and i'm trying to sort out my gut reactions to constitutional self-amendment loops before i commit to a framework. i want to check whether there's an actual principle that captures when self-reference feels paradoxical versus just politically messy, or if my intuitions are scattered. here's where i land on a few cases:

case 1 — a sunset statute that says "this law and all provisions within it expire on dec 31, 2030." in 2029 they amend it to extend. feels totally fine; the self-reference is just a timer, not a loop.

case 2 — an "eternity clause" listing core rights and stating "this clause is not amendable, including by any amendment to this clause itself." then a convention claims to repeal it. to me this feels genuinely paradoxical, not merely illegitimate — there's a logical tension, not just a power grab.

case 3 — a state constitution's article 12 reads "article 12 may only be changed by popular referendum." the legislature then passes an ordinary bill altering article 12 to say "the legislature may change article 12." this feels like breaking rules or usurpation, but not paradoxical; the contradiction is institutional, not logical.

case 4 — article 5 states "the amendment procedure for article 5 is whatever committee Z specifies," and committee Z then specifies "committee Z may amend article 5 by internal vote." this closes a loop but feels stable, like a fixed point. it's reflexive without exploding.

case 5 — a meta-rule saying "this rule is valid iff it is invalid" is clearly paradoxical. but compare it to "this rule is suspended every leap year, and this suspension clause is also suspended every leap year." that second one feels tangled and oscillating, yet not contradictory — weirder than case 3 but not like case 2.

my tentative principle was that paradox requires immediate self-contradictory content, while mediated self-reference through a mechanism (committee Z, a calendar) just creates loops or hierarchies. but case 4 breaks that, since article 5 refers to itself through Z and still feels stable, and case 5's mediated version feels more unstable than case 3 despite being mediated. i'm going back and forth on whether case 2 and case 3 can both stay.

so: is there a cleaner principle — maybe about whether the loop has a unique fixed point, or about whether the self-reference is grounded in an external authority — that gets all five? and which of my verdicts has to be overturned for simplicity? also, what does the best principle predict for a case i haven't listed: a constitution with two eternity clauses, where clause A says "clause B is unamendable" and clause B says "clause A is amendable by two-thirds vote"? does that collapse into paradox or just set up a hierarchy? and one more — if a rule refers to its own suspension condition but that condition is itself defined in a separate, non-self-refering statute, does that mediation always defuse the paradox?

i'd especially like an honest statement of which verdict can't be saved, and whether the principle needs a special clause just to accomodate the leap-year oscillation in case 5.
