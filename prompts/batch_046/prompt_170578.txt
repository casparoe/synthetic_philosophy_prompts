Rank the following four deflationary approaches—disquotational minimalism, prosententialism, redundancy theory, and pluralist deflationism—by their resilience to a specific pressure: the predicate “is true” occurring in embedded constructions that quantify over utterances mixing empirical, normative, and vague claims (for example, “Everything she said was true, even the parts that are neither true nor false”). For each position in your ranking, explain in one or two sentences whether it can absorb this pressure without tacitly reintroducing a substantial correspondence property or a robust semantic value, and name the hidden cost—such as loss of expressive generality, collapse into semantic pluralism, or covert normativity—that determines its rank. Finally, indicate whether any of these accounts actually turns this stress-test into a positive argument for its own structure, or whether the test merely exposes shared vulnerabilities.
