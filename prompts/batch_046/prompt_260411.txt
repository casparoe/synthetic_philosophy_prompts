Consider a second-order variant of the dogmatism paradox, transposed from all-or-nothing belief to graded credence and applied to an agent’s calibration about her own reasoning reliability. Agent A assigns credence 0.65 to the claim that her reasoning about climate policy is generally reliable, and credence 0.55 to the claim that if wild-animal suffering is a serious moral problem then we have pro tanto obligations to reduce it; she then recognizes that the second claim is a logical consequence of premises she already accepts at 0.70. The dogmatist pressure appears to push her to raise the 0.55 closer to 0.65, yet doing so feels like bootstrapping her own competence rather than gaining independent evidence.

Give me your own credence, on a scale from 0 to 1, that this second-order, graded-credence variant genuinely dissolves the paradox rather than reproducing it at a higher level—specifically, that there is a normatively defensible asymmetry between updating on logical entailments within first-order domains and updating on entailments that bridge first-order conclusions to second-order self-assessments.

Decompose the reasoning explicitly. Assign rough probabilities to each of the following sub-questions and show how they combine:

(a) Whether the original paradox survives intact once we move from binary belief to continuous credence, or whether graded belief already contains resources (e.g., differential weighting of premises, threshold-independent aggregation) that block the dogmatist slide.

(b) Whether second-order calibration beliefs—beliefs about the reliability of one’s own reasoning in a particular domain—are properly subject to the same entailment-transmission norms as first-order beliefs, or whether they belong to a separate epistemic category (e.g., reflective equilibrium, meta-cognitive monitoring) with different update rules.

(c) Whether there is a principled distinction, within this framework, between logical entailments and empirical or predictive entailments, such that recognizing a logical link provides a different kind of evidential input than recognizing an empirical correlation.

(d) Whether the agent’s recognition that premise-set P entails conclusion Q provides independent evidential support for Q, or whether it merely restructures the architecture of beliefs she already holds, leaving the overall evidential base unchanged.

State the final number, indicate which component it is most sensitive to, and describe—without citing specific works—what kind of formal model or constructed thought experiment would most substantially move your credence in either direction. Also address whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, whether that is a defect for a position of this kind.
