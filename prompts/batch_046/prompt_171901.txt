(4) jordan said alex is demanding a metaphysical self that buddhism denies from the start. the ethical point of anatta isn't to preserve punishment but to show responsibility is relational and forward-looking: you address the harmful causal pattern wherever it manifests, not because a soul deserves suffering, but to redirect the stream. alex also confuses conventional truth (samvriti) where we talk about people, with ultimate truth (paramartha) where no self exists; wanting a 'principled line' assumes there's a hidden essence to protect.

please go through all four, say who's right about what, and point out what both parties are missing—maybe something about whether retrospective blame can ever be justified under anatta if only conventional.
