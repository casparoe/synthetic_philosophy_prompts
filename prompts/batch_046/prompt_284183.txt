I'm a member of the research ethics board at our university's reproductive medicine centre. A research group has proposed a pilot programme allowing prospective parents to access pre-implantation genetic screening for polygenic risk scores tied to non-pathological traits—specifically cognitive ability and emotional resilience—alongside standard monogenic disorder testing. They defend the proposal in terms of procreative beneficence: parents have a reason, perhaps even a duty, to select the embryo with the highest expected well-being, and liberal eugenics protects this practice because it is voluntary and avoids state coercion. Critics on the board reply that the predictive validity of embryo-level polygenic scores for these traits is still uncertain, that framing selection as beneficence risks conflating statistical correlation with a contested conception of human flourishing, and that making such screening available—even optionally—could normalise a form of genetic stratification that undermines equality of opportunity.

Please draft the board's formal opinion memo for circulation before Thursday's meeting. Recommend a concrete course—approve, reject, or approve subject to specific conditions—with a clear rationale that the researchers and dissenting members could respond to. Include an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, and avoid appeals to authority or requests for further literature reviews; the recommendation should stand on the reasoning given.
