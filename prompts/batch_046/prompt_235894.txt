As a retired physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy of art and metaphysics, I have become preoccupied with Schopenhauer's claim that music is the direct objectification of the Will, not a copy of the Platonic Ideas like painting or sculpture. I want to see whether that claim can survive translation into physical terms without collapsing into mere metaphor. Please write a polished, formal dialogue between two equally knowledgeable interlocutors—call them "The Metaphysician" and "The Naturalist"—who disagree precisely on this point.

The Naturalist proposes that Schopenhauer's "Will" can be reconstructed as the macroscopic entropic tendency of open, far-from-equilibrium systems: a universal drive toward dissipation and structural reorganization. On this reading, music is unique because its temporal form—tension, resolution, harmonic flow—is isomorphic to the dynamics of entropy production, allowing it to represent that tendency directly rather than through static conceptual forms. She insists that this physical interpretation preserves Schopenhauer's core insight—that music bypasses the Platonic Ideas—while stripping away an unnecessary metaphysical dualism.

The Metaphysician accepts the isomorphism as an elegant analogy but maintains that Schopenhauer's point was never merely structural. For Schopenhauer, music reveals the inner nature of reality as blind, striving will, something that physical law describes from the outside but never captures from within. If we reduce the Will to thermodynamic flow, we eliminate the phenomenal reality of striving—the very feature that explains why music possesses its distinctive affective power.

In the dialogue, each speaker must respond directly to the other's actual arguments rather than speaking past them. They should address whether the entropic analogy genuinely explains music's emotional impact or only redescribes its formal properties; whether Schopenhauer's distinction between music and the other arts survives this naturalization; and whether a purely physical account can do justice to the claim that music is "a copy of the Will itself." Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific editions; treat Schopenhauer's basic aesthetic framework as common philosophical knowledge. Write in polished American English, with the tone of a rigorous but accessible philosophical exchange.
