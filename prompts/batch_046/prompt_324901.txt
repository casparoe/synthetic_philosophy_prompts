A philosophy podcast summarized the causal exclusion argument for mental causation as follows: "Physicalism requires that every physical event has a sufficient physical cause, so if a mental event M causes physical effect P, and M's physical realizer N also causes P, we get systematic overdetermination. Non-reductive physicalists reply that M and N are not competing causes because M is realized by N, but realization is a deterministic dependency: N's presence guarantees M's presence, so N does all the causal work. The only escape is to treat mental properties as causally efficacious not as productive causes but as constitutively relevant — they structure the causal powers of their realizers without introducing new forces. But that reduces mental causation to a redescription of physical causation, which is why Kim eventually abandoned non-reductive physicalism for a reductive view."
