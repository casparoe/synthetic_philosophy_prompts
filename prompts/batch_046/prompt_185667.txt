I have been discussing Weber’s Protestant Ethic thesis with colleagues in a philosophy of social science reading group, and I am trying to clarify exactly where people stand on whether religious values can genuinely figure in historical explanations rather than merely decorate them. Rather than asking anyone which label they prefer, I want a decision tree — or taxonomy of positions — that sorts views by the answers they give to concrete diagnostic questions about Weber’s specific case, suitable for a seminar handout.

Please construct a branching taxonomy with no more than eight leaves, built from the logical space rather than from a pre-existing list of authors. The first split should ask whether values have any independent causal efficacy at all, or whether they are always reducible to material conditions or post-hoc rationalisations. For those who grant efficacy, the next splits should concern: (a) whether values explain the genesis of modern capitalism or only its particular form and long-term stability; (b) whether the mechanism is motivational (directly altering economic behaviour) or selective (acting as a filter or elective affinity that determines which emergent practices become culturally self-reinforcing); and (c) whether the claim survives the switching problem — the counterfactual thought experiment in which Catholic and Protestant economic behaviours are reversed, testing whether the values do genuine explanatory work or merely track underlying material differences. Each leaf should carry a brief name (for example, “Weberian elective affinity”, “materialist reductionism”, “psychological internalism”, or “unnamed” if the combination is unoccupied) together with one sentence stating its most awkward or counter-intuitive commitment. Please also flag any logical combinations that appear empty, diagnosing briefly whether the gap arises from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The diagnostic questions should be phrased so that a student can answer them without knowing the technical labels in advance, and the instrument should not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I am interested only in the conceptual architecture.
