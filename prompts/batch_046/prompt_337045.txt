I am currently enrolled in a graduate seminar on panpsychism, and our discussions have centered on the combination problem not as a monolithic objection but as a cluster of distinct bottlenecks. Specifically, I am working with a three-part taxonomy: (i) the palette problem, concerning how macro-qualitative properties derive from micro-qualities that may lack them entirely; (ii) the structural binding problem, concerning how relational and spatial properties among micro-subjects generate macro-cognitive architecture; and (iii) the subject-summing problem, concerning how a plurality of micro-subjects constitutes a single macro-subject rather than a mere aggregate. Assuming a researcher has approximately three years and a background in philosophy of mind rather than empirical neuroscience, which of these open questions should be prioritized, and in what order? Please defend your ranking according to explicit criteria—dependency relations among the problems, tractability for philosophical rather than empirical methods, and the downstream impact that resolving each would have on the viability of constitutive panpsychism and adjacent positions such as cosmopsychism. I am particularly interested in whether solving the subject-summing problem is an upstream prerequisite for the other two, or whether they can be pursued in parallel without circularity; the goal is not a survey of existing literature but a defended research agenda.
