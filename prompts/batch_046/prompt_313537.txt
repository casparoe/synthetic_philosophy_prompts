I am constructing a novel in which a future regulatory authority that licenses synthetic organisms operates under a procedural distinction I have observed, in various forms, within present biotechnology governance: patent examiners typically require applicants for synthetic gene constructs to submit a complete functional annotation mapping every codon to its anticipated phenotypic effect, treating the sequence as an engineered instruction set, whereas applications involving naturally isolated sequences are assessed purely on structural novelty and chemical purity, with no obligation to demonstrate functional intent. Through the lens of this institutional practice, I wish to examine what philosophical commitments about genetic information are being presupposed. Does the bifurcation implicitly hold that DNA functions as a code only under conditions of human design, or does it rest on a deeper ontological claim about the informational status of synthetic versus natural nucleic acids? Please reconstruct the implicit theory of biological information embedded in this licensing distinction, analyse whether it is internally coherent and compatible with the standard molecular understanding of gene expression, and specify precisely which elements of the regulatory framework would require fundamental redesign if the institution adopted a uniform reductionist view that denies any special informational or semantic status to DNA regardless of its origin.
