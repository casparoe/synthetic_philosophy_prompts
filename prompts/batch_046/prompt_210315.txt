I'm working through an argument about Moore's view that friendship and beauty are intrinsic goods with organic unity, and it seems to license monstrous trade-offs. (1) On Moore's account, the value of a profound friendship or a beautiful artwork is an organic unity: the value of the whole exceeds the sum of the values of its constituent parts taken in isolation. (2) Call this the principle of compositional dominance: if a whole W has organic value V, then distributing W's parts across contexts that fail to reproduce W's unifying structure yields value near zero relative to V. (3) So if preserving one masterpiece requires destroying ten competent paintings, or preserving one deep friendship requires allowing ten genuine but shallower friendships to be destroyed, the organic-unity view requires saving the one regardless of the number lost. (4) Extend this: if saving my closest friendship requires allowing ten strangers to die—since their survival, distributed outside any unity with me, carries no comparable organic value—then I am required to save the friendship at that cost. (5) That is intolerable. (6) So Moore's analysis is false, or applies only where no competing moral weights exist. I want to keep (1) if I can, and I suspect the slide is between (2) and (4), but I can't name it. Which step is the weak link? Is (2) a misinterpretation of organic unity, or does (4) smuggle in an unjustified priority of personal or aesthetic value over life? What does the correct diagnosis cost?
