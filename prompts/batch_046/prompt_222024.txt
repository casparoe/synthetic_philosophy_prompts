I've mostly bought into the gender-equality paradox: in more egalitarian welfare states like scandinavian countries, gender differences in career choice actually get bigger, not smaller, compared to less equal places. My take is that culture and economic pressure were just suppressing real preferences, and once you lift both, people sort into what they actually want — which for gender means more divergence, not convergence. But I want to know what'd genuinely count against this. Not generic "correlation isnt causation" objections, but concrete defeaters: say, data showing that second-generation immigrant women in those same countries shift sharply away from gender-typical jobs? Or that the gap tracks some confounder like risk-aversion that welfare policy happens to amplify differently by gender? Or if introducing generous parental leave made the gap widen further — does that support my suppression story or undermine it? I want an honest ranking: what's fatal, what's just awkward, and whether defenders have qualified the view so much its unfalsifiable. Also, if the paradox is real but caused by gendered signaling in high-trust societies rather than freed preferences, does that still defeat me?
