I've been trying to extend the Rietdijk-Putnam reasoning about Minkowski spacetime into the philosophy of mind, and I've arrived at an argument that looks valid but whose conclusion strikes me as intolerable. Here are the steps:

(1) In special relativity, for any pair of spacelike-separated events, there exist inertial frames in which the first is simultaneous with a reference event and frames in which the second is.
(2) If temporal properties—pastness, presentness, futurity—are frame-independent, then contradictory assignments must be resolved by denying that any of them objectively holds.
(3) Hence there is no globally privileged plane of simultaneity, and no observer-independent 'now' that partitions existence into past, present, and future.
(4) Consciousness, however, is essentially present-tensed: to be conscious is to be in a state that is now occurring for the subject, not merely occurring at some coordinate.
(5) Take two observers in relative motion. In observer A's rest frame, observer B's current mental state lies in the objective past; in B's frame, it lies in the present.
(6) If consciousness requires being present, and presentness is frame-relative, then whether B is currently conscious depends on A's velocity.
(7) That is absurd: the existence of a mental state should not be relative to an inertial frame.
(8) Therefore, either (3) is misinterpreted—there is a hidden privileged present despite relativity—or (4) is false, and consciousness is not essentially present-tensed.
(9) But giving up (4) seems to collapse the distinction between the static block and lived experience, which is precisely what the meta-problem of consciousness asks us to explain.

Where does this argument fail? Is there an equivocation between physical simultaneity and phenomenological presence? Or is the reasoning sound, meaning we must pay the cost of either a privileged frame or a non-present-tensed account of mind? I want to preserve both the standard relativistic interpretation of (1)-(3) and the intuition in (4), so tell me exactly what price I pay for holding both. Please also indicate what specific argument or finding would cause you to change your diagnosis—where is your reasoning here most likely to be wrong?
