I have recently been forced to reconsider evolutionary explanations of ethics after a close family member argued, with surprising confidence, that Darwin had effectively dissolved morality into adaptive tribal loyalty and left no room for objective ethical claims. In that context, I encountered a summary—apparently drawn from a popular philosophy essay and secondhand references to Darwin’s correspondence—of Darwin’s mature position on morality and religion. The summary runs as follows. Darwin held that what we call moral feelings are essentially social instincts inherited from our animal ancestors; the so-called higher virtues, such as impartial benevolence toward strangers, are not genuine expansions of moral capacity but rather these tribal instincts operating outside their original ecological context, amounting to a kind of evolutionary misfiring rather than moral progress. On religion, the summary claims that Darwin treated belief in God as an unavoidable cognitive byproduct of the same mechanisms that generate moral sentiment, yet he personally regarded all religious claims as intellectually indefensible, leaving him with a purely instrumental view of ethics as a useful fiction that natural selection preserves solely because it promotes group cohesion. The summary concludes that, for Darwin, there is no objective moral truth, only varying degrees of social utility shaped by selection, and that his agnosticism was in fact a mask for a fully naturalized reduction of ethics to biology.

This sounds suspiciously neat to me—particularly the claim that higher benevolence is evolutionary misfiring, and the suggestion that Darwin viewed morality as a useful fiction rather than as a real, reason-inflected capacity. The speaker seemed well-read, alluding to The Descent of Man and to Darwin’s private letters, but I am not equipped to check the details myself. Before I rely on this characterization in further discussion, I want an accuracy audit. Please separate, as clearly as possible: (a) what in the summary accurately captures Darwin’s published views on the relation between instinct, reflection, and the moral sense; (b) what represents a standard simplification that distorts his position without being outright false; and (c) what Darwin explicitly denied or that misrepresents his stance—especially regarding whether he saw higher moral sentiments as misapplied instincts, whether he separated moral reasoning from social instinct, and regarding his actual religious position and its connection to his ethics. I am not asking for a bibliography, a list of primary sources, or direct quotations; I want a judgment of reliability that I can act on without access to specialized texts.
