Here is a set of commitments I have arrived at after reading some popular introductions and sitting with them for a while, and I would like to know whether they form a coherent package or whether I am smuggling in inconsistencies.

(1) I think the heart of Buddhist ethics is cetana — the intentional mental state behind an act — and that the ethical quality of what I do is fixed by that intention, not by what actually happens as a result. Outcomes matter causally, but they do not change whether my action was skillful or unskillful in itself.

(2) Even so, I think a society should be arranged to minimize aggregate dukkha across all sentient beings. That sounds consequentialist, and I am comfortable with that at the institutional level: policies should be judged by how much suffering they prevent or cause.

(3) I accept anatta: there is no enduring self or soul that owns karma and travels between lives. What continues is just a causal stream of mental and physical processes, dependently originated.

(4) Despite accepting anatta, I still think individuals are genuinely responsible for what they do and that they should experience the appropriate fruits (vipaka). This is not just a social convention or a useful fiction for me; responsibility is real, even without a permanent owner of the actions.

(5) I reject utilitarian calculus for personal decision-making. Treating people as fungible containers of suffering, and grasping at specific outcome-figures to maximize, strikes me as exactly the kind of craving (tanha) and ego-investment that Buddhist practice is meant to dissolve.

(6) To bridge these, I have tried to work out what I call "empty consequentialism." Because there is no hard boundary between self and other, reducing another being's suffering is not altruistic self-sacrifice but the natural movement of a mind freed from that boundary. So the guiding metric is still suffering-reduction, but pursued without attachment to results and without an ego that "owns" the outcome.

Please go through these and audit them. For each pair that is genuinely in tension — not just apparently so — say how severe the conflict is, which commitment has to give or be revised, and what the cheapest fix would cost me conceptually. Also identify any pair that looks tense but actually dissolves on inspection, and explain why. Finally, tell me whether there is one single revision that would clean up the most tension at once, and what that revision is. I am not signaling in advance which commitment I would be most willing to drop; treat the set as it stands.
