In Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach, the analogy between Gödel's self-referential formula—a statement in a formal system that, via Gödel numbering, effectively asserts its own unprovability within that system—and the emergence of self-aware minds ("I" arising within a physical substrate) has fueled a decades-long dispute that now feels frozen. One camp, following Lucas and Penrose, treats the analogy as evidence that human cognition exceeds any fixed formal system: if a mathematician can "see" the truth of her own Gödel sentence while the system cannot derive it, then consciousness involves a non-computable meta-capacity that no algorithmic architecture can capture. The opposing camp—critics in the philosophy of mind and cognitive science—insists this is a category mistake: formal self-reference is syntactic, consciousness is semantic or functional, brains are not axiomatic theories, and no result about logical provability constrains neural computation. For years the exchange has cycled through the same moves—intuitions about direct grasp of truth, counter-examples of formal systems encoding meta-levels, charges of mysticism versus reductionism—without either side conceding ground.

My working hypothesis is that the stall is not really about computation versus consciousness, but about an unacknowledged disagreement over what counts as an adequate explanation of first-person awareness: specifically, whether such an explanation must preserve an irreducible gap between the explaining framework and the explained "I" (a kind of epistemic closure condition), or whether that gap is only an artifact of how we describe levels of analysis. I want a diagnosis rather than an adjudication. Is this a substantive disagreement about cognitive architecture hidden behind logical vocabulary; a verbal dispute over whether "self-reference" means syntactic encoding or phenomenological presence; or are the two sides answering different questions—one about proof-theoretic limits, one about subjective ontology—under the single heading "Gödelian minds"?
