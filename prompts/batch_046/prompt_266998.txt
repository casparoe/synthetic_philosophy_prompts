I've come around to a somewhat specific reading of anatta: its not best understood as denying a soul-substance, but as a modal claim that in no coherent possible-world scenario does first-person referance track a single persisting substrate across mental states—so Buddhism lands closer to analytic bundle theories than to reductionist materialism. What would genuinely count against that framing? I want more than the standard "but I remember being me yesterday" objection—I'm after which formal results about self-reference, which cross-cultural phenomenological reports, or which clinical breakdown conditions in neuroscience would actually threaten it. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say what defenders of this modal framing would concede is a real hit versus what they'd dismiss, and be honest about whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—if so, is that a problem? Take a committed stance: pick which threats are genuinely dangerous and defend that choice, don't just lay out options.
