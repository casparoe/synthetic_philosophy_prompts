After a long and somewhat circular argument with a friend over coffee about whether false scientific theories can stand in an objective relation of being closer to the truth, we agreed that trading examples was not advancing either position. She maintains that verisimilitude is a framework-transcendent structural relation: even when quantitative content measures conflict, invariant correspondences between model structures and target systems allow one false theory to be genuinely nearer the truth than another. I maintain that all formalizations of verisimilitude either presuppose framework-relative parameter weighting or reduce to internal predictive success, rendering cross-framework judgments of closeness to truth conceptually empty.

Rather than continue debating, we want a joint adversarial-collaboration document that both of us could sign without reservation, delivered as exactly five numbered parts with no introductory or concluding remarks, each part consisting of one or two sentences: (1) the formal and methodological commitments about representation and theory change that we both accept; (2) the precise disagreement, with her structural-realist formulation and my deflationist formulation each phrased in wording its own side endorses; (3) the two cruxes—whether structural correspondence can be defined without prior theoretical commitment, and whether predictive success can proxy for truthlikeness across incommensurable frameworks; (4) a concrete hypothetical case, such as two theories that assign different dimensionalities to a parameter space yet converge on a shared lower-dimensional invariant manifold, together with each side's explicit prediction about whether verisimilitude can be assessed in that scenario for each crux; and (5) a clear separation of which parts of our dispute are verbal or definitional versus substantive, plus a specification of what kind of non-citational argument or conceptual result would actually move either of us.
