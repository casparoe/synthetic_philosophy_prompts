Here is roughly where I have landed on the Appointment in Samarra and its philosophical neighborhood, and I want an audit of whether the package holds together:

(1) I read the Damascus story as a causal-loop case rather than a case of logical or metaphysical necessity: Death is in Damascus because the servant's flight to Samarra is already embedded in the causal chain, not because a future event exerts backward causal pull.

(2) I therefore think local free will is preserved: the servant genuinely could have stayed in Damascus—there is no external coercion—but the counterfactual "if he had stayed, Death would not have been surprised to see him there" fails, because evaluating it requires breaking the loop's causal closure.

(3) For loop cases I reject Lewis-Stalnaker backtracking counterfactual semantics, but I retain backtracking for ordinary non-loop causal reasoning.

(4) I analyze prophecy in branching-time terms: a prophetic statement describes a fixed point in the causal network, not an external constraint that removes options from the agent's decision tree.

(5) I hold that rational decision-making should maximize expected utility relative to the agent's epistemic state, not relative to the objective causal structure; so the servant was rational to flee Damascus, even though the flight was causally predetermined to deliver him to Samarra.

(6) I think the same reasoning applies to Newcomb's problem: the predictor's accuracy is a fixed point in the causal network, and one-boxing is correct because it respects that fixed point from within the agent's epistemic perspective.

(7) I reject global determinism, but I accept local determinism for closed causal loops of the Damascus type.

Is this consistent? In particular, does my rejection of backtracking in (3) undermine my defense of local free will in (2), since without backtracking I lose the standard explanation of why staying in Damascus would not have altered the prophecy? Does my global anti-determinism in (7) sit badly with my willingness to apply loop-logic to Newcomb in (6), given that treating predictor accuracy as a fixed point seems to import the very determinism I reject globally? And is there only an apparent tension between (5) and (1)—between saying the flight was causally predetermined and saying it was rational—that dissolves once epistemic rationality is separated from causal efficacy?

Please order the tensions by severity, specify for each which commitment has to give and what that costs, and clearly flag which tensions are genuine and which dissolve on inspection. I am not signaling which view I would abandon.
