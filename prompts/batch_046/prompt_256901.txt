(1) I accept determinism for macroscopic dynamics: given exact initial conditions and deterministic laws, the trajectory is unique. Chaos—positive Lyapunov exponents, strange attractors—does not violate determinism; it only renders long-term prediction impossible for embedded observers with finite precision.

(2) I take this predictive failure to be structurally robust, not merely practical. No computing system physically embedded in the universe could predict which region of a strange attractor will be realized, because the measurement precision required exceeds the information capacity available to any subsystem.

(3) I think this structural opacity is sufficient to ground moral responsibility. Responsibility does not require that actions be metaphysically open or uncaused; it requires only that the causal pathway be deterministically opaque in this strong sense. When a process is transparent to no possible observer, treating the agent as an ultimate source is justified.

(4) I hold that quantum mechanics is genuinely indeterministic at the fundamental level, but I regard this as a red herring for agency: neural decoherence occurs on timescales far shorter than cognitive processing, so quantum randomness is averaged out, leaving macroscopic chaos—not quantum noise—as the dynamics relevant to free will.

(5) I reject the consequence argument familiar from the determinism-and-free-will literature, on the grounds that in chaotic systems the distant past does not uniquely determine the present state in any causally or epistemically accessible way. The past constrains the attractor geometry but not the specific orbit, so our actions are not "inevitable" in the sense the argument requires.
