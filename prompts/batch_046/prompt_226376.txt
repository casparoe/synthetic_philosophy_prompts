Write a dialogue between two evolutionary biologists arguing in a bar after a conference. One is a strict gene-level selectionist, the other defends multilevel selection. Don't let them just trade slogans — they need to actually respond to each other's points.

Set the argument around a specific weird case: imagine a clonal plant with a shared root network that distributes nutrients and chemicly suppresses any stem that mutats to hoard resources. The gene-selectionist says this is just a policing gene spreading through the genome, and the root network is only a vehicle. The multilevel advocate says the network behaves like an immune system selecting for group-level cooperation, and the gene explanation misses the emergent regulation.

Make both smart. Have each one point out a real weakness in thier framing, and include at least one moment where one concedes a partial point but insists it doesn't change the bottom line. Keep the tone casual — maybe they spill a drink or mention a bad conference talk — and don't include citations or reading lists.
