I've come to think the Ship of Theseus is a red herring — "ship" is a funtional kind with no essential material conditions, so replacing every plank doesn't matter as long as the vessel keeps sailing the same route with the same crew. I extend this to people: personal identity is social-functional continuity, not biological substrate, so gradual neuron replacement (or even uploading) preserves identity unless the role breaks. What would genuinely count against this? Is there an argument showing artifact identity tracks spatiotemporal continuity that conventions can't override? And for the personal-identity part, what would have to be true for biological trajectory to matter metaphysically, not just emotionally? I want strong counterarguments, not just intuition, plus an honest check: have I qualified this into unfalsifiability by saying "the function changed" whenever I'm challenged?
