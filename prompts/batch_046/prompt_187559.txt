everyone keeps saying guilt is like a debt you can repay but shame is like a stain on the fabric that bleach won't fix, and they lean on this to say cultures should just choose guilt since it's transactional and repairable. audit the analogy for me — what's the correspondance table (creditor, payment, fabric, bleach, etc.), where does it crack? especially look at survivor guilt where the creditor is dead and the debt is unpayable, and at shame that hits you alone with no audience to see the stain. do those cases collapse the distinction, and does the conclusion that a culture can pick the healthier emotion survive once the disanalogies are out? if the debt/stain frame is carrying the argument, what's a less misleading analogy for how guilt and shame actually function?
