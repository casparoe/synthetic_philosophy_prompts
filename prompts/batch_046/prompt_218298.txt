In philosophical and cognitive-scientific discussions of mechanical rigidity, flexibility, and free will, the debate over whether human cognition is fundamentally sphexish — bound by stereotyped, inflexible behavioral loops analogous to those observed in the sphex wasp — or genuinely open-ended and adaptive has persisted for decades with little apparent convergence. One camp maintains that apparent human flexibility is merely higher-order rigidity: neural architectures execute deterministic update rules within fixed computational bounds, so that even apparently reflective choice is just a more elaborate version of the wasp's ritualized behavior. A second camp argues that human meta-cognition — specifically our capacity to revise not only our choices but the criteria by which we evaluate choices — introduces a recursive structure that breaks the sphexish mold, and that this self-modifying capacity, rather than any uncaused causation, constitutes the core of free agency. A third camp contends that the framing itself is confused, that sphexishness was originally intended to characterize systems lacking learning dynamics altogether, and that applying it to organisms with graded, context-sensitive plasticity imposes a false rigidity-flexibility dichotomy that ignores intermediate architectures.

Given this stalemate — in which empirical findings about neural plasticity seem available to both sides, and in which normative commitments about what autonomy requires appear to drive the interpretation of those findings — I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this disagreement primarily empirical, about whether meta-cognitive revision in humans operates within fixed algorithmic boundaries? Is it conceptual, turning on whether flexibility and rigidity are contraries or points on a continuum applied at different levels of analysis? Or is it normative in disguise, with one side defining freedom as escape from causal determination and the other defining it as coherent self-governance? To test the diagnosis, consider a hypothetical architecture I will call refractory meta-plasticity: a system capable of altering its first-order decision rules through experience, but whose second-order rules governing how such alterations occur are hard-coded and permanently immune to revision. If human cognition approximated this architecture, would it vindicate the flexibility camp, the rigidity camp, or neither?
