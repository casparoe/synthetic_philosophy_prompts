Since my father’s recent diagnosis with a progressive neurodegenerative condition that gradually erodes the boundary between awareness and reflex, I have found myself unable to dismiss questions about the distribution of consciousness across physical scales. In this frame of mind, I encountered an unnamed popular-science essay that attempts to derive an ethical program from panpsychist interpretations of quantum mechanics. The essay summarizes the view as follows: “If panpsychism is correct, then every quantum decoherence event involves the dissolution of a micro-subject’s unified experience, which means that physical processes are not merely accompanied by consciousness but are literally composed of countless births and deaths of sentient entities. Consequently, any macroscopic action—cooking food, generating electricity, even breathing—amounts to an enormous, involuntary massacre of fundamental subjects. The only coherent ethical response is a radical physical quietism: we should seek to minimize all causal interaction in order to reduce aggregate micro-suffering. This implication is so implausible that panpsychism should be rejected outright, since any philosophy that demands we stop moving has already contradicted the conditions of life itself.”

I suspect this reasoning collapses at several points, but I lack the philosophical precision to locate the fractures. Which parts of the summary accurately capture commitments of contemporary panpsychism—particularly regarding the combination problem and the nature of micro-experience? Where does it conflate panpsychism with a stronger thesis about quantum sentience or about the moral status of fundamental entities? Is the inference from “micro-experience exists” to “micro-experience suffers” a non sequitur within the view, or is it a legitimate extension? And does the reductio-ad-absurdum structure actually touch panpsychism, or does it instead attack a straw position that no careful defender holds? I would appreciate a disciplined audit: separate the accurate representation from the subtle overstatement and the outright misrepresentation, and indicate whether the essay’s conclusion—that panpsychism entails physical quietism—follows even from the corrected premises.
