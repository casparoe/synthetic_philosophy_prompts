My colleague and I have spent six months in an escalating contest over who will chair our firm's new ethics review board—a position that was initially prestigious but has become a liability since a regulatory change made the board responsible for auditing past violations in a division where we both have close allies. Both of us have made public commitments, gathered supporters, and invested so much professional credibility that conceding now would be read as an admission of weakness or poor judgment. Meanwhile, whoever chairs the board will likely alienate half the office and absorb blame regardless of the outcome. We continue to escalate—circulating position papers, making promises to different factions—despite the fact that the "prize" is now clearly damaging to claim. I feel trapped by the logic of not being the one who blinked first. What does this kind of trap reveal about rational decision-making, and is there a coherent strategy for withdrawing without simply accepting defeat?
