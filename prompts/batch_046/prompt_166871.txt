I sit on a state health ethics board and we keep seeing proposals that justify hurting one group for the overall good. I want a reusable decision procedure drawn from Rawls's seperateness of persons objection to utilitarianism -- basically a checklist we can run in twenty minutes. It needs ordered questions: does the policy merge people into a single welfare container or keep them as separate claimants? Does it allow direct cross-personal tradeoffs where one person's burden is weighed against another's gain? For each step say what counts as enough of an answer, where we stop with a hard no, and where we're just doing values not facts. Then apply it to two cases: a mandatory flu vaccine for healthcare workers that mostly protects patients, and a triage protocol that saves more lives by deprioritizing people over 80. Make sure one case fails the check despite high total utility. Also tell me what this procedure can't catch, like hidden aggregation in statistics. Keep it practical -- my board doesn't do metaphysics.
