I was talking with my friends Alex and Jordan last week after they both got into this thing about Browning's Ordinary Men versus Goldhagen's Hitler's Willing Executioners. Jordan had just finished Goldhagen and was fired up; Alex said he skimed Browning in college and still thinks Browning basically won. So here's how it went.

Alex said look at Reserve Police Battalion 101 — these weren't monsters, just regular cops caught in peer pressure, career anxiety, and burocratic distancing. Jordan said that's the exact myth Goldhagen destroys: calling them 'ordinary' ignores that the ordinary German pool was already soaked in eliminationist antisemitism, so the culture was the necessary condition, not just the trigger. Then Alex dropped this idea he calls 'moral disengagement sequencing' — the situation doesn't just activate old beliefs, it builds them step by step through little justifications, so Goldhagen gets causality backwards: culture is the ash, not the fuel. Jordan fired back that this is unfalsifiable — if they'd refused, you'd just blame a weak situation, not absence of culture. She also said that not resisting more fiercely is a character failure, not proof that character didn't matter. Then Jordan brought up 'distributed intentionality' — no single soldier intended genocide, but the collective structure had an intentional profile bigger than any guy. Alex said that's metaphysically spooky and doesn't explain why some soldiers asked to be excused while others didn't, which points straight back to individual differences. Jordan said those who asked out were exactly the ones with weaker cultural internalization, so it still supports her view.

They turned to me to referee but I honestly can't tell if they're even arguing about the same thing anymore. Can you adjudicate move by move — rate each step from clearly wrong to clearly right, say who's closer on each point, and tell them what they're both missing? I feel like there's a hidden disagreement about whether character is revealed or formed under stress, and neither of them has said that out loud.
