I defend a hybrid account of the varieties of free will worth wanting—call it reflexive authorship under normative constraint. The view holds that an agent possesses free will in the valuable sense only if three conditions are jointly satisfied: first, Frankfurt-style hierarchical identification, in which second-order volitions genuinely align with and endorse first-order desires; second, a modal stability condition, requiring that these endorsements persist across a significant but bounded range of counterfactual perturbations to practical reasoning; and third, a non-circular normative coherence condition, demanding that the agent can defend the overall structure of their endorsements without appealing to the very first-order states being justified. I take this to capture what is genuinely valuable in both compatibilist and libertarian traditions while avoiding the standard objections to pure hierarchical accounts—the manipulation problem and the arbitrary-halting problem—and to pure libertarian accounts, namely that indeterminism undermines rather than enables agency. What would have to be true for this synthesis to fail? Please distinguish between evidence that would be merely awkward—such as a compelling case of an agent who clearly lacks free will despite satisfying all three conditions, or possesses it despite violating one—from evidence that would genuinely undermine the view, such as a demonstration that the stability condition collapses into trivial psychological persistence or into an impossible modal requirement, or that the coherence condition necessarily involves circular justification. Provide an honest assessment of whether this position has been qualified into unfalsifiability: have I built in enough escape clauses that no conceivable counterexample could dislodge it, and if so, is that insulation a defect for a normative conception of agency? Finally, state explicitly what argument or observation would change your verdict—whether a particular class of counterexample, a formal result about the consistency of hierarchical endorsement, or a conceptual proof that the three conditions are jointly unsatisfiable—and identify where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
