Draft the joint statement that a counterpart theorist—who holds that concrete individuals are world-bound and that modal de re claims are made true by relations of qualitative similarity to counterparts in other worlds—and a transworld-identity theorist—who holds that the very same concrete individual can exist in multiple worlds with different properties—could both endorse after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Center the collaboration on a specifically constructed case: the gradual, part-by-part functional replication of a conscious cognitive architecture across worlds, in which each replaced component is qualitatively identical to its predecessor but spatiotemporally discontinuous with it. The deliverable should include: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims, addressing the shared formal logic of quantified modal discourse, the precise phenomenon requiring explanation in this replication case, and the minimal constraints any adequate metaphysical account must meet; (2) a disagreements table stating each camp's position in wording its own proponents would accept, with particular care in formulating whether similarity relations can ground de re modal truths without circularity, and whether the intuition that the original architecture 'survives' tracks literal identity or only a counterpart relation of high psychological-continuity similarity; (3) the cruxes—those sub-questions whose resolution would genuinely move at least one side—specifically including whether functional-role essentialism is compatible with world-bound counterpart relations, and whether transworld identity requires commitment to uninstantiated individual essences; (4) for each crux, one concrete thought-experiment or argumentative case that both sides accept bears directly on it, together with each side's explicit, pre-registered prediction of what that case demonstrates. Present this as numbered agreed-claims followed by the disagreements table. The statement must contain no bibliographies, no citation requests, and no appeals to authority outside the arguments presented.
