Please explain the heterogeneity problem for hedonism—the challenge of specifying what all pleasures have in common, given that pleasures as diverse as tasting a complex wine, solving a mathematical proof, and experiencing physical relief appear to share neither sensory modality, intentional object, nor causal origin. In your answer, first distinguish the phenomenal-quality response (that all pleasures share a uniform felt-tone accessible to introspection) from the functional-structure response (that they share a common motivational or evaluative role). Next, introduce and apply the distinction between manifest heterogeneity, in which differences among pleasures are introspectively salient, and latent homogeneity, in which a shared substrate exists but is obscured by those differences. How does this distinction bear on the objection that hedonism cannot unify well-being because pleasure lacks an introspectively detectable common feature? Specifically, if a hedonist posits a latent phenomenal substrate that is masked by manifest diversity, does this preserve the theory's explanatory power, or does it risk rendering hedonism empirically unfalsifiable and conceptually redundant? Be precise about what is conceptually at stake for hedonism as a unified account of welfare.
