Consider the applied judgments of Dr. Elena Vasquez, a philosopher of science who works explicitly within Otto Neurath's physicalist framework—emphasizing intersubjective protocol sentences, the rejection of metaphysical pseudo-statements, and the use of picture-language where direct spatial or quantitative analogies replace conventional linguistic mediation. Over the past year she has evaluated six representational proposals for inclusion in an interdisciplinary scientific archive.

Her rulings are as follows. First, she approved a standardized icon set depicting census demographics, on the grounds that it communicates quantitative relations through direct visual analogy without requiring a shared conventional language. Second, she rejected an essay arguing that mathematical entities possess an independent mental existence, ruling that it offers no empirically anchored protocol and relies on untranslatable metaphysical convention. Third, she accepted a shipboard entry stating that at 0800 hours an observer at position 42N 71W reports a red light to port, treating it as a properly singular, spatiotemporally indexed protocol statement. Fourth, she rejected a formal derivation in symbolic logic that purported to prove the necessary existence of abstract universals, on the basis that its symbols have no fixed empirical interpretation and cannot be mapped onto physical measurements. Fifth, she approved a working mechanical orrery using gears and spheres to model orbital mechanics, judging that its spatial structure directly mirrors the relations it represents. Sixth, she rejected a pooled questionnaire in which ten laboratory assistants each wrote that they observed an anomaly without specifying individual location, time, or sensory modality, arguing that it forfeits the singular first-person index and spatiotemporal specificity required for a genuine protocol statement, despite being empirically collected.

Now a seventh proposal has reached her. It is a distributed laboratory installation—call it the synesthetic mesh—in which real-time spectroscopic data on protein folding is automatically rendered as simultaneous tactile vibrations, thermal gradients, and spatialized sound patterns experienced collectively by a research team. There is no visual display, no verbal transcript, and no requirement that any individual user issue a singular first-person report; the system generates only aggregate patterns that no one observer fully perceives in isolation, though every output is deterministically derived from physical measurement.

Predict Vasquez's verdict if she aims to be consistent with her own track record. Identify which of her six prior rulings is the closest analog, and state whether her precedents genuinely underdetermine the case. If they do, articulate the competing principle she would be adopting with an approval versus a rejection, and specify what further hypothetical case—one that would not be settled by either principle alone—would best distinguish the two interpretations of her framework.
