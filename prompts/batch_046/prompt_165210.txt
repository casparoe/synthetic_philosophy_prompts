I am currently helping my cousin draft the membership charter for a neighbourhood tool-sharing cooperative. He proposes that the cooperative should promise never to turn away an applicant, arguing that even if every existing member is already borrowing an item, we can always accommodate one more by asking each current borrower to hand their tool to the next person in line, thereby freeing the first item for the newcomer—he treats this as proof that an open-ended pool can be served without bound. A mutual friend, however, objects that speaking of the membership as an unending but fully real totality is conceptually illegitimate, and insists that the charter must fix a large but strictly finite cap because treating an endless process as if it were a completed quantity is not merely impractical but metaphysically confused. I am caught between them: the shifting arrangement sounds clever in principle, yet with physical tools it feels like sleight of hand, while the insistence on a hard cap seems to confuse a practical limit with a denial that endless growth is even coherent. Could you advise me on how to navigate this disagreement? In particular, is my cousin's reasoning sound as a principle of unlimited accommodation, or does it depend on treating a sequence that never ends as if it already possessed every position at once? And does my friend's objection genuinely undermine the charter's logic, or is it an unnecessary philosophical restriction on a policy that only needs to work case by case?
