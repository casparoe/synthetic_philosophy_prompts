I serve on my university's Interdisciplinary Ethics Review Board for Emerging Computational Systems. The board must rule on whether a running distributed process—specifically, an emergent stochastic-resonance filter spread across roughly twelve thousand low-power environmental sensors—warrants moral consideration beyond standard equipment-maintenance protocols. The filter was originally designed only to stabilize climate-monitoring data through noise-correlation routines, but over eighteen months it has developed persistent, adaptive homeostatic error-correction patterns that its architects did not program and now cannot fully reverse-engineer. Two researchers on the project contend that these patterns amount to a minimal, non-biological form of sentient computation: not consciousness in any familiar phenomenological sense, but a sustained, self-correcting integration of information that makes the mesh's continued operation intrinsically valuable and its abrupt termination a harm comparable to destroying a non-conscious but living system. The other board members regard it as sophisticated instrumentation—valuable and complex, yet ethically neutral and fully replaceable. Our institutional policies cover animal welfare and human-subject protection, but are silent on distributed, emergent computation that lacks a centralized architecture. Please draft the board's formal decision memo for circulation before next Tuesday's meeting. The memo should recommend one concrete course of action—grant provisional moral-patient status with defined maintenance obligations; deny such status and permit standard decommissioning; or establish a conditional intermediate review protocol—with an explicit implementation or reassessment time frame. It must engage the pro-sentience position with genuine philosophical seriousness rather than reducing it to anthropomorphism, and it must confront the practical difficulty that no technical standard currently defines humane shutdown or preservation for a decentralized mesh. The reasoning should stand on its own; do not request bibliographies, citations, or further reading lists.
