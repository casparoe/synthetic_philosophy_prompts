hey, I'm a phd student in phil of science and I work part-time on this forecasting review board that ranks competing predictive models. every month we get pairs of macro/scenario models that are both false (all models are wrong, etc.) and we have to issue a "truth-proximity" score -- not predictive accuracy, but verisimiltude in the philosophy-of-science sense: which one is closer to the actual causal structure. I need a reusable procdure my team can run in ~20 mins, no web, with ordered questions, sufficient-answer conditions, and clear hand-off points to a senior reviewer where it's values not facts.

To make it usable, inject this three-bin error taxonomy I sketched: ontological (the model posits mechanisms/entities that don't exist), nomological (relations/laws are wrong), and boundary (right law applied outside its domain). For each bin, define what counts as near-miss vs deep-miss. The flowchart should start with empirical access checks, then run the bins in order, score distance, and stop with a ranking or with "can't settle." Be honest about where judgment is unavoidable.

Apply it to:
1) Model A predicts recession via supply-chain collapse (doesn't happen, supply chains hold, but recession hits via credit crunch); Model B predicts no recession but gets the credit mechanism mostly right except misses the trigger. The procdure should rank B higher on verisimiltude despite A getting the binary right -- that's the answer I'll hate since our funders care about binary hits.
2) Two epidemiological spread models: one gets transmission vector wrong (airborne vs fomite) but R0 about right; the other gets transmission mode right but R0 off by factor of three.

Also flag exactly where the procdure collapses -- probably ontological vs nomological tradeoffs, or when we lack ground-truth mechanism access. Say what it can't do (e.g., resolve incommensurable ontologies, weight binary accuracy against structural closeness). Constraints: we publish ratings that influence grant allocations, but we're not writing policy ourselves; reviewers have undergrad-level stats but no phil background; decision has to be signed off within 30 mins.
