ok so im trying to get my views on the species problem straight before i write something up, and i want to check if they actually hang together. here's where i landed:

1) for sexually reproducing animals the biological species concept is basically right - reproductive isolation is what makes a species.
2) but i also think species membership is intrinsic. a wolf raised in total isolation is still Canis lupus because of internal properties, not because of its relations to other wolves.
3) im also anti-essentialist - there's no hidden wolf-essence, just historical gene flow and population dynamics.
4) ring species (like those salamanders around california) show reproductive isolation comes in degrees, not all-or-nothing, so boundaries are genuinely vague in nature.
5) i reject the phylogenetic concept since it splits interbreeding populations into seperate species just on ancestry patterns.
6) conservation law needs sharp lines though - listing Canis lupus as endangered tracks a real boundary, not a pragmatic fiction.
7) bacteria break BSC because of horizontal gene transfer, yet E. coli is still a totally valid category.
