Anyway they started arguing about this idea from that Hofstadter book — GEB — about where meaning actually lives in a message. Dave said it's definatly in the frame message, like the syntax rules or protocol that tells you how to parse whatever payload you recieve. He said the outer message — what the author originally meant — is basically useless because people are sloppy and dead authors can't defend thier intent. Maya pushed back that Dave was ignoring strange loops, where the inner message generates semantic stuff that wasn't in the frame or the original intent at all. Dave replied that even in a self-referential system the frame is still doing the interpreting, because without those syntax rules a Gödel-style sentence is just random symbols with no teeth. Maya said the frame itself is just more symbols, so eventually you need an outside interpreter, which means the outer message or at least some external agent is actually carrying the load. Dave countered that bootstrapped systems can create thier own interpretive authority without any outside help, and in real distributed software the frame is just dead convention while the inner computed message is the only thing with causal power. They went back and forth for like twenty minutes applying it all to this weird case of code that rewrites its own documentation, and I honestly couldn't tell who was landing punches. Can you adjudicate this move by move? I want to know who's right about the frame being primary versus the inner message being genuinely autonomous, whether the outer message is necessary or just a leftover habit from thinking about books instead of protocols, and if there's something both of them are missing about what happens when the message is literally a protocol that modifies itself. Also say who is more right overall and what part of the disagreement is actually just them talking past each other.
