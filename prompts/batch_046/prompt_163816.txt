Design an exam essay question for an upper-level philosophy or political theory course, to be answered in 90 minutes with an expected length of 800–1000 words. The topic is the gender-equality paradox: the empirical pattern in which societies with stronger formal gender-equality measures often exhibit larger gender divergence in occupational and educational choices. The question must be unanswerable by merely summarizing liberal equality-of-opportunity arguments or standard social-constructivist critiques.

Introduce and require the student to use the concept of "reflective preference architecture"—the view that first-order preferences count as genuinely autonomous only when they are second-order endorsed and formed in conditions where structurally different life paths were not only legally permitted but socially and imaginatively accessible. The exam question should direct the student to: (i) explain why the paradox creates a tension between formal equality and substantive justice; (ii) apply the reflective-preference-architecture framework to assess whether widening divergence in egalitarian societies indicates authentic autonomy or a hidden failure of equality; and (iii) derive one specific policy or institutional implication that avoids both pure laissez-faire acceptance of outcomes and direct state engineering of preferences.

Deliver three items: (1) the precise exam question as it would appear on the paper; (2) a model answer of roughly 700 words that takes a defensible position while engaging the strongest counter-argument; (3) a marking scheme out of 25 marks listing at least five required analytical moves with their point values, plus for each move a one-line description of a common error that should reduce the score.
