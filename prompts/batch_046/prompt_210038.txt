my study group came up with this and it feels like it proves way too much but i cant see the crack. (1) Wittgenstein says religious belief isnt an empirical claim but a picture that regulates a whole form of life, so "God exists" works more like "this is the framework i live inside" than like "there's a planet behind Neptune". (2) If its not empirical, then no observation can count against it - asking for proof is a category mistake, like asking how heavy Tuesday is. (3) So if someone builds a "religious framework" where the moon is cheese and electron spin proves the Trinity, and they live consistently inside it, there's no rational ground to correct them from outside. (4) That collapses the line between real religious commitment and arbitrary delusion, since both are just self-contained grammars. (5) But Wittgenstein clearly thought superstition was confused and some religious talk was genuine nonsense, not just different grammar. (6) So either he's smuggling in an external standard he denied, or every framework is equally valid. I want to keep (1) and (5) if possible - which step do i reject, and whats the cost? it feels like (3) might be slippery but i dont know why.
