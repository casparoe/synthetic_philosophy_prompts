I'm giving the opening address at the retirement symposium for Dr. Elena Vasquez, a philosopher of mind who spent forty years arguing that the self is not a substance but a center of narrative gravity—an abstract point around which biographical stories converge and organize themselves. The room is split: half are colleagues who hold that personal identity is a narrative construction with no deep metaphysical core; half are family who are not philosophers but know she rejected any talk of an immaterial soul or essential self. The theme is exactly that idea. Write a ten-minute address that lets this concept do structural work: explain why it would violate her view to claim she now "lives on" in some non-physical form, and why her absence is not the disappearance of a bare object but rather a shift in gravitational center—the stories that used to converge on her now redistribute among those she shaped, while the pattern she anchored remains fully real. The speech must comfort the family without mysticism and satisfy the philosophers that no metaphysical smuggling has occurred.
