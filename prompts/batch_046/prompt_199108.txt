My opening: You claim alignment is just intent, not values. But suppose an operator intends "maximize human welfare." Operationalizing that requires interpreting "welfare," which is value-laden; if the system interprets it via wireheading, you will reply that the specification was incomplete — we should have added "without violating autonomy." Yet that addition is not a technical patch; it introduces values (autonomy, authenticity) as constitutional constraints on what counts as a legitimate interpretation of the intent. So intent alignment seems to presuppose value alignment at the constitutional stage, where values determine admissible interpretations rather than merely coloring preferences. This makes your narrow view look like a bookkeeping trick that collapses once you ask who has the normative authority to constrain interpretation. How do you block this without just stipulating that values stay outside?
