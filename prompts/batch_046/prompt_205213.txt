I've been arguing with friends from my old rationalist forum days about whether Ubuntu-style relational personhood is genuinely incompatible with liberal individualism or just a more poetic packaging of the same social facts. The conversation keeps stalling on the proverb usually rendered as "I am because we are"—or, in the original, "umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu"—which one side treats as an obvious refutation of atomistic selfhood and the other dismisses as vague humanism. Before I go back into the thread, I want the slogan's logical geography mapped precisely.

Please lay out, as a menu rather than a single interpretation, the distinct precise claims this proverb could be expressing, ordered from the trivial or definitional to the metaphysically substantive. For each reading, clarify: (1) whether it makes an ontological claim about what a person is constituted by, an epistemic claim about how self-knowledge is acquired, or a normative claim about what we owe one another; (2) whether it asserts causal dependence (I develop only through social interaction), constitutive dependence (my identity is literally composed of relations), or something stronger, like the denial that there is any fact of the matter about my mental states independent of communal recognition; and (3) whether that specific reading actually supports using the slogan to dismiss liberal rights frameworks, or whether it remains compatible with robust individual autonomy and distributive justice. I'm especially interested in the intermediate readings—those that sound radical in seminar but collapse into banal social-science truisms once operationalized, and those that sound banal but smuggle in a denial of first-person authority or counterfactual independence. Don't provide a bibliography or ask me to read particular authors; I want to see which versions of the claim survive without empirical speculation about infant development and which depend on contested anthropology. If any reading requires attributing mental-state transparency to the community rather than to the individual, flag it explicitly. The goal is a disambiguation I can use in discussion without falling into the motte-and-bailey dynamics I keep seeing in these debates.
