I've recently begun to take the cosmological fine-tuning argument seriously, partly because my work in machine-learning optimization has made the analogy between hyperparameter selection and physical constants feel concrete rather than metaphorical: just as a model's behavior collapses outside a narrow parameter range and we reasonably infer intentional tuning, the values of constants such as the cosmological constant and the Higgs vacuum expectation value sit in ranges that permit complex chemistry, which I take as stronger evidence for design than for brute chance or an unobservable multiverse. I want an honest map of what should move me away from this view. Which empirical discoveries—say, about spatial variability in constant values, or about the derivability of those values from deeper symmetries—would be fatal rather than merely awkward? Which objections do defenders of fine-tuning themselves concede carry real weight, and which do they dismiss as question-begging? Does the multiverse explanation genuinely compete, or has it been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that insulation a defect for an explanation of this kind? I also want to know whether the ML analogy I find compelling actually strengthens the inference or smuggles in a hidden selection-effect fallacy that undermines it. Rank the potential defeaters from those that would genuinely overturn the position to those that would only force me to qualify it, and be frank about whether the view risks being rendered immune to evidence in ways that matter.
