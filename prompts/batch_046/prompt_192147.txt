I am preparing the final examination for my third-year philosophy of perception and cognition module, and I want a single essay question that engages with the conceptual problems raised by Douglas Hofstadter’s Letter Spirit project—specifically, the claim that recognising any token as an instance of the letter ‘a’ requires a flexible, style-sensitive conceptual category rather than a fixed feature checklist. Rather than asking students to summarise the project, I want them to work through a novel conceptual tension that forces them to distinguish genuine category membership from mere perceptual similarity, and to say precisely where a Letter Spirit-style account succeeds or collapses.

Please write the exam question, a model answer of approximately 700 words, and a marking scheme. The question should present a hypothetical letterform—call it the ‘Reversed-Loop a’—which possesses all the topological relations of a standard lowercase ‘a’ (a closed bowl with an ascending stem) but rendered in mirror-image orientation and in a fractured, stencil-like style that has never appeared in conventional typography. Students must be asked to determine whether this form ought to be recognised as an ‘a’, defending their answer by appealing to the distinction between essential structural relations and accidental stylistic properties that the Letter Spirit framework emphasises. Crucially, the question must also require them to address an internal objection: that if conceptual categories are flexible enough to accommodate the Reversed-Loop form, they risk becoming so permissive that they can no longer explain why clearly non-‘a’ shapes—such as a perfectly circular ‘o’ bearing a single hairline crack—are correctly excluded.

The answer should be written in British English. The model answer must separate an A-grade response, which articulates the precise conditions under which flexibility becomes over-permissiveness and proposes a principled boundary, from a B-grade response, which describes the example but leaves the tension unresolved. The marking scheme should allocate 25 marks across four specific moves—(i) correct identification of the topological or structural criteria at stake, (ii) accurate application of the idea that style is not noise but partly constitutive of category identity, (iii) a clear statement of the over-permissiveness objection and how it threatens the account, and (iv) a proposed resolution or boundary condition—with explicit notes on the characteristic errors that cost marks in each category. Suggest a time allowance of 50 minutes, and ensure that no part of the deliverable asks for bibliographies, citations, or external reading lists.
