I teach a philosophy seminar for about 14 grad students, 75 minutes once a week. I want an activity that makes them feel why moral worth is tied to reasons rather than outcomes, but also makes them hit the wall that we can't observe reasons directly. Design a short in-class game where each student gets a hidden reason card—duty, sympathy, self-interest, fear—and then picks an action card: help, ignore, harm. The group sees only the action and has to rank moral worth, then reasons are revealed—except for two students who hold contradictory cards for the same action and must each defend their ranking without knowing the other card exists. Include rules, a simple point system (winner picks the snack), a 15-min debrief mapping the confusion to Kantian maxims vs Arpaly's quality of will, and an honest note on what this can't reproduce—like the phenomenology of acting purely from duty. Keep it runnabel with just paper cards, no tech.
