Write a dialogue between two grad students—one who thinks rent control and minumum wages are just basic decency, and one who thinks they're hidden taxes that eventually hurt the people they aim to help. Don't make either one a strawman; they need to actually reply to each other, not just deliver monologues. Have them dig into the messy second-order effects—like how a long-term rent cap can make landlords stop fixing boilers and eventually reduce the housing stock, or how a wage floor in a town with only one big employer might not destroy jobs because of monopsony power. Keep the tone casual, like they're arguing over coffee in a messy kitchen, and focus on the ethical question of who really bears the hidden costs when prices are administratively fixed.
