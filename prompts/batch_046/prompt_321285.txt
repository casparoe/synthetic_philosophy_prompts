A colleague summarized Davidson’s radical interpretation to me in roughly these terms: it is essentially Quine’s radical-translation project refined into a holistic theory of meaning, in which an interpreter constructs a Tarskian truth-theory for an unknown language by correlating utterances with observable circumstances, governed by the principle of charity that requires maximizing agreement between speaker and interpreter. From this, Davidson allegedly concluded that there is no genuine indeterminacy of translation—only temporary ignorance that further observation resolves—and that meaning is nothing more than the optimal assignment of beliefs and desires that renders the speaker rational. My colleague then applied this framework to large language models, arguing that if we treat an LLM’s token outputs as a language to be radically interpreted, charity forces us to attribute true beliefs about physics and morality to the model whenever those outputs cohere with our own, since any other attribution would violate rationality maximization. Is this a fair account of Davidson’s view? Please separate what is accurate, what is a standard conflation with Quine, what Davidson explicitly denied about meaning and belief attribution, and whether the LLM extension is a legitimate application of the view or a category mistake that ignores Davidson’s requirement that interpretation targets agents with intentional attitudes.
