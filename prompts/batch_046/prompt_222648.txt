Some genocide memorial museums — the ones in Nanjing, Yerevan, or USHMM — have this curatorial habit where they lock outright denialist texts in a kind of hall-of-lies display with scientific rebuttals right beside them, but they also keep legitimate historiographic fights (disputes over exact casualty counts, or whether specific bureaucratic orders came from the center or local commanders) off the main floor entirely. Those debates get treated like its just another flavor of denial, or they get buried in a footnote nobody reads. What philosophy of history and of evidence does that display strategy presuppose? Does it need a view that historical truth about mass violence is basically settled except for bad-faith outliers, so that any quantitative revision is automatically epistemic treason? Can it tell the difference between normal scientific revisionism and denialism without smuggling in a moral judgment? And if we replaced that implicit view with one that treats even well-established casualty figures as genuinely provisional — open to revision like any other empirical claim — what would actually have to change in the museum's architecture, labeling, and school programs?
