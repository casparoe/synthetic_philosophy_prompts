I'm preparing a consistency check for a seminar presentation on personal identity through technological replacement, and I'd like to audit my commitments before I defend them in discussion. Rather than treating uploading as a single discrete event, I'm framing it through a specific staged protocol: biological cortical columns are replaced layer-by-layer with silicon analogues over months, leaving the subject in prolonged hybrid biological-silicon intermediate states with continuous functional integration. Within that framework, I've settled on several positions that feel individually defensible but may not hang together.

Here is the package: (1) I accept a psychological-continuity theory of personal identity, holding that causal dependence linking present mental states to future ones is necessary for being the same person over time. (2) I am a substrate-independent functionalist: carbon, silicon, or hybrid architectures are metaphysically equivalent if they realize the same functional organization. (3) I think the layered column-replacement protocol—including its months-long hybrid intermediates—preserves my identity at every stage, because functional integration is never interrupted even though every original neuron is eventually replaced. (4) I maintain that destructive whole-brain scanning followed by digital instantiation is not survival but the creation of a duplicate, since the causal chain linking my current mental states to future ones is severed, regardless of how perfect the psychological match is. (5) I believe that what fundamentally matters for rational prudence is psychological connectedness—what Parfit might label Relation R—and that this can in principle obtain even without direct causal dependence, which implies I should have reason to care about the welfare of a high-fidelity scan-copy. (6) I deny that there is any metaphysically significant distinction between a ten-year gradual replacement and a ten-minute one, provided both maintain uninterrupted functional integration, so I reject objections that appeal solely to the speed of replacement. (7) At the same time, I feel no actual prudential concern for the welfare of a destructive-scan duplicate; my concern extends only to beings causally continuous with me.

Please examine this set for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision for each real problem? Rank the tensions by severity, specify exactly what has to give, and identify the cheapest fix that restores consistency without collapsing the broader framework. In particular, address whether my acceptance of hybrid intermediates in (3) commits me to accepting destructive scanning in (4), whether (6) introduces any hidden contradiction with the layered protocol, and whether (5) can be reconciled with (7) without abandoning either psychological continuity or prudential rationality. I want a targeted audit of my position—not a survey of the uploading literature, and not a reading list—delivered in American English.
