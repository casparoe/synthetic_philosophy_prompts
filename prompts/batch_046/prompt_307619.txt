Design a decision tree that classifies responses to the canonical Sleeping Beauty problem—heads yields one Monday awakening, tails yields Monday and Tuesday awakenings with erased memory—by underlying epistemic commitments rather than by the numerical answer endorsed. Use three binary branch questions that a respondent could answer without knowing the interpretation labels: (i) whether being awakened provides any new centered evidence about the coin beyond the prior protocol knowledge; (ii) if so, whether credence over subjectively indistinguishable awakenings should be distributed by a uniform indifference principle; (iii) if not, whether the absence of update stems from denying irreducible self-locating beliefs altogether or from accepting centered worlds but treating the prior as already conditioning on them. Map the resulting combinations onto named or unnamed leaves: include an Elga-style thirder, a Lewis-style halfer, a reductionist deflationary view, and at least one occupied or unoccupied combination that yields a non-standard verdict—flag it. For each leaf, state the predicted Monday credence in heads and the view's most awkward commitment. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that share the same numerical verdict and therefore cannot be separated by this tree alone, and propose one modified protocol (e.g., revealing the day on one awakening) that would split them. Note any logically possible combination of answers that remains unoccupied and briefly diagnose whether it looks incoherent or merely unexplored. Keep the tree to six leaves or fewer, and phrase every node as a direct question about belief or evidence, not about theory affiliation.
