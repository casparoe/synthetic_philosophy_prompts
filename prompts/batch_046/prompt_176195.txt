Ive been digging into the Browning vs Goldhagen debate for a paper. Browning's Ordinary Men looks at Reserve Police Battalion 101 — mostly middle-aged family guys from Hamburg, not SS ideologues — and shows they massacred Jews in Poland mainly through peer pressure and authority dynamics, with officers even letting men opt out and only a small minority doing so. Goldhagen then argues in Hitler's Willing Executioners that deep German cultural antisemitism made ordinary people willing executioners. So heres my confusion: since Browning's evidence suggests explicit hatred wasnt necessary and situational pressure alone was sufficient to produce mass killing, doesnt that automatically make Goldhagen's culture-based account redundant? If situation can turn regular people into killers, why invoke centuries of cultural conditioning at all — arent these just competing explanations where Browning's is the more parsimonious one, and shouldnt we drop the culture/character side once we accept the situational data?
