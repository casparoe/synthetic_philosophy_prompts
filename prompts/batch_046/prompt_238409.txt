On the advisory council where I serve, debate over a rising regional power has split into three camps: those who invoke the 1938 Munich appeasement to demand immediate economic containment, those who warn that any escalation will produce a Vietnam-style entanglement with no clear exit, and those who cite the Thucydides trap to claim that structural rivalry makes conflict nearly inevitable regardless of our choices. I think all three are using history as a substitute for causal analysis, but I want to understand precisely why these analogies mislead—whether the error lies in false empirical similarity, in the way analogies silently restrict the menu of available actions, or in treating contingent narratives as deterministic laws. How should I evaluate which, if any, comparison genuinely illuminates the present situation rather than merely dressing up a predetermined preference, and is there a principled way to adjudicate between competing historical templates without just selecting the outcome I already favor?
