I am preparing a single ninety-minute seminar for third-year philosophy undergraduates who have completed some epistemology but have no background in biblical studies or source criticism. The conceptual problem I want them to grasp—not merely to be told about—is whether the standard criteria of authenticity (multiple attestation, dissimilarity, embarrassment, coherence) can ever genuinely settle who the historical Jesus was, or whether they necessarily presuppose the miniature portrait they claim to recover. To prevent the session from becoming a rote list, I want to centre it on a specific tension: dissimilarity rewards sayings that do not match either Second Temple Judaism or later Christian development, while coherence rewards sayings that fit a consistent profile of Jesus. Applying both rigorously to one contested logion—say, a saying attributing to Jesus an authority to forgive sins—forces the student to smuggle in a prior judgement about whether he was primarily an eschatological prophet or a wisdom teacher, which is precisely the historical question at stake.

I therefore need a teaching plan rather than an explanation delivered to me: the precise order in which these pieces must be introduced so that the circular dependency becomes visible rather than hidden; the one carrying example or reconstructed scenario that should hold the session together, together with the properties it must possess (and those it must lack) to make the circularity tangible; the misconceptions students will predictably form—likely that multiple attestation solves source-dependency, that embarrassment is an objective textual feature rather than an inference made by the reader, and that the four criteria function as independent filters rather than an interdependent system—and exactly where in the sequence each misconception should be headed off; one question I can pose at the end to distinguish whether they have understood the dependency problem from whether they have merely memorised the list of criteria; and what I should openly refuse to explain at this level and state clearly that I am doing so. Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; the plan must stand on its conceptual architecture alone, and the students have no access to specialist libraries.

Finally, please say explicitly where your reasoning about this pedagogical sequence is most likely to fail, and what specific argument or counter-example concerning the interaction of the criteria would cause you to revise the order of presentation or the choice of carrying example.
