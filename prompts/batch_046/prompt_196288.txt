Write an essay on whether Du Bois's propaganda thesis in his 1926 Criteria piece is self-undermining once you notice he also had pretty elitist taste for European classical forms and tended to dismiss popular Black culture as vulgar. The standard reply is that he was just describing art's inevitable social role, not saying beauty doesn't matter. But push past that: if the only reason to praise a Black artwork is that it advances racial dignity, then Du Bois has no internal ground to call one techincal better than another except political effectiveness, which basically turns art into rhetoric. Argue for one side—either he can save his view by splitting primary artistic value from secondary political value, or he can't and the propaganda claim has to go if he wants to keep any real critical authority over Black art. Don't end non-commital. You can mention Locke's counter-argument for context, but don't let this become a comparison essay. Also deal with the objection that he was only talking about a specific historical emergency, not laying down a universal aesthetic theory. Oh, and don't bother with a bibliography—just argue.
