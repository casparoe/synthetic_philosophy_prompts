Herodotus built his histories by collecting oral reports and weighing them against one another, trusting that discrepancies revealed something about credibility. Suppose he were confronted with a modern problem that did not exist in his era: artificial intelligence systems that can generate vast, internally consistent archives of fabricated eyewitness testimony about ancient events—say, synthetic "interviews" describing the building of the pyramids, produced without any human observer ever having been present. Would he still maintain that comparing multiple accounts is the soundest path to knowing the distant past? Or would he conclude that once sources can be manufactured at scale with no underlying experience behind them, the very foundation of source criticism must change? I am not looking for historical biography, but for reasoning about whether his core method survives a world in which the problem is no longer faulty memory but the absence of memory altogether.
