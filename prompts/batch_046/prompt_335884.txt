Retired biologist here, got into moral philospophy after retirement and keep seeing a pattern I can't label. Take two situations I've read about: in one society they run loud public rituals when someone cheats in trade, but quiet private confession when they harm a family member, and over generations people report mostly outside-in shame for the first offense and inside-out guilt for the second. Another place flips it—private punishment for trade cheating, public exposure for family harm. It looks like the culture is running different protocols for different offenses, and the emotional mix of the population actually shifts over time. It's not the old static shame-culture versus guilt-culture split, because the same society seems to move a dial on purpose, treating these feelings like selectable traits rather than fixed human equipment. Is there an established term for this kind of intentional tuning of a population's moral emotion architecture? How is it different from ordinary socialization or institutional design—does it only apply when the emotional outcome itself is the target, not just the behavior? If there's no name, what should I call it, and where are the edges: does any culture that teaches norms count, or only ones that deliberately calibrate whether people feel bad inside or outside? I don't need a bibliography, just whether there's an actual carving for this.
