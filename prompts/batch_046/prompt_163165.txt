I am designing a conceptual taxonomy for a graduate seminar on anthropic reasoning and observer-selection effects, and I want a decision-tree instrument that sorts actual philosophical commitments rather than the labels people prefer. The tree should begin from a single root commitment—whether one accepts that there exists a well-defined reference class of observers over which probabilities can be distributed—and then branch through concrete diagnostic questions. Each node must present a specific scenario or claim that forces a choice without revealing which named position it leads to.

The required leaves must include: a strict Self-Sampling Assumption with a fixed reference class; a Self-Indication Assumption that applies indexical weighting to observer-moments; the presumptuous-philosopher rejection of SIA on grounds of prior implausibility; a superdeterminist or no-selection-effect view that denies any conditioning is necessary; and an agnostic position that accepts selection effects but holds that the reference class is too underspecified to yield quantitative predictions. You must include an intermediate branch addressing the Sleeping Beauty problem, phrased as a judgment about whether Beauty’s credence in Heads should update upon awakening, and another addressing the Doomsday argument, asking whether the rank of one’s birth order in human history should affect one’s expectation of total human population. Additionally, inject a branch testing whether one applies SIA consistently across domains—specifically, whether one accepts it for reasoning about cosmological constant values but rejects it for the Sleeping Beauty case—so that the tree can flag unoccupied logical combinations.

Build the tree from the logical space of answers first, mapping named positions onto the leaves only after the branching structure is fixed, and explicitly note any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate. Where combinations of answers seem to have no occupant—such as accepting indexical weighting for cosmological fine-tuning while denying it applies to one’s own temporal location—briefly diagnose whether the gap stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Keep every node phrased as a question a student could answer without already knowing the interpretation it produces.
