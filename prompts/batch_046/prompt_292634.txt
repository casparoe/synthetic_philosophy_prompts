We use random lottery for juries and occasional advisory citizens panels, but we elect legislatures and let career politicans serve for decades. Then we put term limits on presidents but almost never on senators or judges. What's the implicit philosophy of representation and competence behind this patchwork? Does it presuppose that lawmaking needs continuity and expertise but judging needs fresh impartial eyes? Or that voters should pick values but not technical rules? Reconstruct the theory — or theories — that makes this mix coherent, say where it's actually philosophically underdetermined (maybe several rival views license the same pattern), and tell me what'd have to change institutionally if we genuinely believed that political skill was just a myth with no democratic value.
