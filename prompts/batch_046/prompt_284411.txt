I am defending a revisionist thesis at an interdisciplinary seminar in history and philosophy of science: that the conventional tripartite scheme—Middle Ages, Renaissance, Scientific Revolution—functions as a nineteenth-century pedagogical scaffold that imposes false discontinuities rather than tracking real historical joints. Specifically, I argue that the boundary between Renaissance natural philosophy and the so-called Scientific Revolution collapses once we recognize that mathematization, controlled comparison, and mechanistic explanation were distributed heterogeneously across 1400–1750 rather than clustering in a canonical 1550–1700 window, and that labeling the preceding centuries "Middle Ages" flattens the twelfth- and thirteenth-century translation movements, institutional innovations, and technical practices that directly enabled the very inquiries later celebrated as revolutionary. The audience is precisely mixed: social historians of science who think periodization should follow patronage networks and material practice rather than intellectual content; medievalists who reject "Dark Ages" framing but are protective of real differences between scholastic and humanist modes of inquiry; philosophers of science influenced by Kuhn who will ask whether I am denying all conceptual incommensurability; and at least one senior scholar whose career work treats the Scientific Revolution as a genuine epistemic rupture with identifiable agents and methods. Give me the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased as each subgroup would actually phrase them, with for each: whether it has real force; what I should concede up front; what I should hold; the short reply I should deliver in the room; and the weakest point in that reply where I could be pressed further. Order them by likelihood and damage. Include, separately, the objection I am hoping nobody asks—the reflexive worry that if "science" is itself an anachronistic projection onto the past, then my denial of a "Scientific Revolution" secretly presupposes the very category I claim is illegitimate, and advise me on whether to address it preemptively or let it sleep. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I have no access to external sources in the session and need only the conceptual prep.
