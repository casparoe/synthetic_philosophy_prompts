As a retired physicist who has only recently begun reading systematically in philosophy of mind, I have tried to synthesize my scientific instincts with what I take to be Schrödinger's Vedantic conclusion: that individual consciousness is not plural but a single universal awareness, locally filtered rather than locally generated. Before presenting this in a discussion group, I want an audit of whether the following commitments hang together.

(1) I endorse the view—drawn from Schrödinger and Advaita Vedanta—that there is numerically one consciousness, and that personal identity is an illusion produced by the boundaries of individual nervous systems.

(2) I remain a strict methodological naturalist: I reject dualism and hold that every mental phenomenon must ultimately be explained by physical processes amenable to empirical study.

(3) I take quantum coherence and non-local correlations not merely as metaphors for mental unity, but as the literal physical mechanism by which this single awareness is integrated across spatially separated brains.

(4) I deny panpsychism: I do not believe that rocks, thermostats, or unorganized matter possess any form of awareness, despite being composed of the same quantum fields.

(5) I accept the clinical neuroscience literature showing that highly localized brain lesions can selectively destroy specific capacities—language, episodic memory, face recognition—without affecting other mental functions.

My questions: Which pairs are genuinely in tension rather than merely apparent? In particular, does (1) conflict irreconcilably with (5), or can a filter-model accommodate localized damage without fragmenting the underlying unity? Does (3) sit comfortably within (2), or does invoking quantum mechanisms for consciousness violate naturalism? And does (4) undercut (1) and (3) together, given that quantum coherence is not unique to brains? Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each whether the conflict is real or dissolves on inspection, and suggest the least costly revision that would restore consistency to the set.
