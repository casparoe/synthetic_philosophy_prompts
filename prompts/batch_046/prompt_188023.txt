Gödel's 1949 rotating-universe solution to Einstein's field equations shows that closed timelike curves are physically admissible, and he interpreted this not merely as a cosmological curiosity but as support for the philosophical position that objective temporal succession is unreal. Separately, his 1931 incompleteness results demonstrate that any sufficiently expressive formal system contains true arithmetical statements that cannot be derived within the system. If the circular temporal structure permitted by Gödel's metric allows an event to lie in the causal ancestry of itself, then a formal system situated in such a spacetime might, in principle, acquire a proof of its own consistency by receiving it from a later temporal stage of itself. Given that Gödel's second theorem rules out exactly this kind of internal self-certification for arithmetic, does the existence of closed timelike curves in his cosmological model constitute a physical counterexample? Or does the distinction between causal transmission in spacetime and formal derivability within a logical system block the inference entirely, leaving the philosophical claim about time's unreality unrelated to the logical result?
