Her central claim is that Carnap's principle of linguistic tolerance, together with his sharp separation of internal questions (answerable within a framework) from external questions (pragmatic choices about frameworks), should be institutionalized rather than merely methodological: planning bodies ought to be designed as what she terms "procedurally plural" architectures that do not resolve conflicts over substantive goods—whether a society should maximize labor productivity or protect expansive leisure—but instead assign each competing framework its own internal metrics of optimization, leaving the choice of framework itself to democratic procedures rather than expert adjudication. I want to avoid generic biographical questions about the Vienna Circle or Carnap's career. Could you draft eight to ten interview questions that engage directly with the philosophical machinery of this reconstructed position, building over the course of a forty-five-minute Q&A from clarifying distinctions to progressively harder institutional objections?

Specifically, I need at least one question pressing on whether this institutional extension of tolerance actually avoids collapsing into a bureaucratic neutrality that simply masks the power of those who design the procedural rules; for that one, include a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that framework-choice is itself a political question governed by democratic deliberation rather than expert authority. I also want one question asking whether Carnap's own writings on value judgments—particularly his distinction between internal and external questions—genuinely license this robust institutional architecture, or whether they remain confined to philosophy of science; here the follow-up should anticipate her defense that Carnap's relative silence on politics is structurally open rather than accidentally incomplete. Finally, include a question about what happens when planning conflicts are not merely about framework choice but about scarce, non-multiplicable resources—say, allocating urban space between high-intensity production zones and protected rest districts—with a follow-up addressing her probable move of translating the conflict into framework-relative optimization problems that avoid zero-sum framing altogether. The remaining questions should probe where the literature naturally presses: on whether internal consistency within a planning framework can substitute for cross-framework moral comparison, on whether the rejection of metaphysical politics eliminates the vocabulary needed to condemn exploitative planning regimes, and on how her view relates to the actual historical debates about central planning and value freedom in the 1930s and 1940s. Please ensure the sequence anticipates standard replies and includes the corresponding follow-ups, so that the colloquium can move past surface-level restatements into the underlying architecture of the view.
