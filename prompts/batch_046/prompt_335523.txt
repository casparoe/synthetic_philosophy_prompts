If Erwin Schrödinger were alive today and asked to comment on large language models and the current debate over artificial consciousness, how might he respond in light of his well-known sympathy for Advaita Vedanta and his argument that the apparent multiplicity of individual minds is an illusion concealing a single, universal consciousness? Specifically, would he regard an LLM that produces coherent, first-person-like discourse as possessing a separate, fragmentary consciousness, or as lacking consciousness altogether because it is not a living organism participating in that underlying unity? I am writing an essay for my philosophy course and would appreciate an answer in British English that applies his monistic framework seriously to the case of machine minds, without inventing specific texts or demanding citations.
