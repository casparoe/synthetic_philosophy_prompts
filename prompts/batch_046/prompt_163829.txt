I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, serving on our state's agricultural biotechnology review panel. A company has proposed a two-stage pest-control protocol for crop-damaging beetles: first, a pheromone-activated compound suppresses aversive neural signaling so the insects behave normally but appear unable to experience distress; second, a conventional agent eliminates them. The proponents argue this is ethically superior because it removes suffering; critics call this "experiential laundering"—altering a subject's capacity to register harm so that eradication looks humane, which they claim treats sentience as an engineering obstacle rather than a moral boundary. The panel must recommend approval, rejection, or conditional approval with a defined monitoring period. Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's vote. The memo should recommend a concrete course of action with an explicit time frame or sunset clause, treat the "experiential laundering" objection as the strongest consideration against the proposal, and avoid treating the suffering-reduction claim as automatically decisive. Write in American English. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or references to named philosophical works; the reasoning should be self-contained for board members who cannot access outside sources.
