Design a decision-tree taxonomy—intended for a graduate philosophy-of-biology seminar—sorting coherent positions on the evolutionary stability of honest signaling, without beginning from named theories. Start with three concrete diagnostic commitments that a respondent answers yes/no/qualified-yes to, then derive 6–8 terminal leaves representing distinct stances.

The three cases are:
A. The ornamental tail: the display is genuinely deleterious to survival independent of mating success, and only high-quality individuals can develop and maintain it.
B. The acoustic display: the call is metabolically expensive, but its acoustic properties directly encode body size; a smaller individual is physically incapable of producing the low-frequency component regardless of effort.
C. The boundary song (introduced for this exercise): a male sings near a territorial edge. If he copies a neighbor’s distinctive phrase, nearby females reward him, but the actual neighbor—who also hears—escalates aggression precisely because he detects the mismatch. Here deception is costly not because producing the copied phrase is hard, but because a hostile third-party receiver is exposed to the discrepancy and punishes it.

For each leaf, state the combination of answers to A, B, and C; the core claim about the source of honesty (production waste, physical constraint, or exposure to an opposing audience); and whether the leaf maps onto a familiar view (e.g., a pure handicap account, an index or signal-of-quality account, conventional signaling with receiver punishment, or a multi-audience leakage account) or occupies an unexplored or hybrid region. Identify any logically possible combinations that appear unoccupied, and briefly diagnose why—whether incoherent, evolutionarily unstable, or merely unexplored. Flag explicitly any pairs of positions that the tree fails to separate, explaining why they collapse onto the same branch.

Constraints: build the tree from the logical space of answers first, then locate positions within it; phrase every branching question so that a respondent need not know the labels “handicap principle” or “index signal” to navigate it. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations. Do not quote any author or text verbatim; paraphrase references to Zahavi, Grafen, Maynard Smith, or others if they are mentioned. Rely only on the three cases above, not on obscure empirical studies. The audience is 12–15 graduate students; the deliverable should be readable as a flowchart with annotated leaves.
