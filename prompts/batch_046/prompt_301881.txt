I'm drafting a proposal for a three-month workshop fellowship in philosophy of physics. The guiding question: does Landauer's principle genuinely dissolve Maxwell's demon paradox, or does the resolution covertly depend on a substantialist ontology of information—treating informational states as physically localized entities whose erasure necessarily dissipates kT ln 2 per bit? I want to test whether a relationalist alternative, on which information is a feature of the demon-apparatus-gas system rather than a stored quantity, permits a model of the demon's memory cycle with lower or differently distributed thermodynamic costs, thereby reopening the paradox in a restricted form. The proposal should include: a precise statement of the question and why standard treatments leave it open (specifically where they conflate information-theoretic descriptions of the demon's register with thermodynamic descriptions of its physical substrate); the method—formalizing both ontologies in parallel, deriving the entropy balance for each, and comparing whether the dissipation bound is interpretation-independent; a realistic month-by-month timeline; the criteria for success (a demonstrable divergence in dissipation bounds) and failure (identical bounds under both interpretations); and an argument for why this angle is preferable to simply repeating the standard Landauer-based resolution. No citations or bibliography are required.
