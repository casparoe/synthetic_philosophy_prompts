The question of whether the Anthropocene should be dated to the late eighteenth-century industrialization, to the 1945 Great Acceleration, or to some other stratigraphic signal, and of whether that decision belongs to geologists, climate scientists, or the broader polity, has been active in both scientific and humanistic literature for over two decades without convergence. I am trying to understand why. One possibility is that participants are talking past one another because they hold different, unacknowledged theories of what a historical period is: for some it is a measurable change in Earth-system dynamics, for others a narrative framework that assigns collective responsibility, and for others a political designation that requires democratic rather than expert authorization. But I do not know whether this diagnosis is correct, or whether the stall is instead driven by genuinely incompatible evidential standards, hidden normative premises about human agency, or a simple ambiguity in the term itself. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement's structure rather than an adjudication of the start date: are the sides answering different questions under one name; is there an empirical or formal question masked by philosophical rhetoric; is the impasse a sign that the concept is ill-posed? Given your diagnosis, what would count as progress — a conceptual distinction, a new empirical marker, a procedural agreement on authority, or a decision to abandon the framing?
