I've recently been appointed to an advisory panel reviewing predictive sentencing tools, and in preparing my remarks I've returned to Pascal's reflections on justice and force—his insistence that justice without effective power remains impotent, while power detached from justice becomes tyrannical, and that legitimate authority requires uniting the two. Given his deep skepticism about human reason's capacity to establish absolute justice, together with his conviction that the heart possesses reasons that reason itself cannot grasp, how do you think Pascal would assess a system in which opaque statistical correlations determine bail conditions and prison terms? Would he regard the algorithm's predictive accuracy as a legitimate form of the force that must accompany justice, or as precisely the kind of arbitrary, unreasoned power he warned against? And since he held that justice must be recognized and internalized rather than merely enforced, might he argue that replacing human judicial discretion with automated risk scores severs justice from the very recognition it requires?
