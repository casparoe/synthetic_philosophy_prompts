I run a small philosophy-of-mind reading group for a rationalist audience, and I'm tired of quizzes that ask whether you're a physicalist and call it taxonomy. I want a decision tree of about eight leaves that sorts respondents by their actual commitments on Mary's room, not by which label they prefer. Start from the bare claim that Mary learns something genuinely new upon seeing red for the first time, then branch through concrete diagnostics: whether her gain is best characterized as factual, ability-based, or conceptual; whether phenomenal concepts are transparent or opaque; whether an artificial system holding Mary's complete physical database could occupy an analogous epistemic position; and whether physical completeness requires that all knowledge be expressible in third-personal terms. Build the tree from the logical space of answers first, then place the standard positions inside it—ability-hypothesis physicalism, phenomenal-concept strategy, acquaintance-knowledge dualism, eliminativist denial, and others—labeling each leaf with its name and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Note any unoccupied combinations, such as a view that grants Mary's new knowledge but denies any analog for non-biological processors, and briefly diagnose whether the gap looks incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves the tree genuinely cannot separate. Every branching question must be answerable without already knowing the interpretation's brand name, and I don't want citations, bibliographies, or reading lists—just the tree, the leaves, and the gaps.
