For this exercise, compose a formal philosophical dialogue between two interlocutors of equal competence. The first, Dr. A, defends a continuous, gradient conception of moral status according to which sentience and its associated obligations come in degrees rather than in discrete bundles. The second, Dr. B, maintains that moral status is categorically threshold-based: an entity either falls within the scope of direct moral consideration or it does not, with no meaningful intermediate state. Have them debate a specific, invented case: a synthetic homeostatic network comprising thousands of localized temperature and damage sensors, each running autonomous avoidance algorithms, but entirely lacking a unified phenomenal perspective or centralized information integration. Dr. A should argue that the network's distributed nociceptive behavior places it on a continuum somewhere between an insect colony and an ordinary thermostat, thereby warranting partial, proportionate moral consideration. Dr. B should counter that without a singular subject of experience, the system is merely an elaborate thermostat, and that attributing fractional moral status commits a category error by treating functional analogues of pain as if they were genuinely felt. The dialogue must feature genuine back-and-forth: each speaker should directly address and attempt to undermine the other's specific reasoning, rather than delivering parallel monologues. Please ensure the exchange remains philosophically substantive, avoiding both straw-man representations and appeals to authority.
