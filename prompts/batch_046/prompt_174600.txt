next thursday i'm presenting to our philosophy of biology reading group my thesis that ant colonies have minds, but not in the Aunt Hillary / ant fugue sense of one unified consciousness. instead i argue the colony is like a fugue with competing voices: different castes maintain genuinely incompatible representations of the same environment, and what counts as the colony's "belief" is just the emergent resolution of those conflicts. so it's distributed intentionality without a single subject, and the contradictions are structural, not bugs. the room will have two hardline eliminativists who think group minds are just metaphor, one cognitive ethologist who knows ant navigation data cold, and two grad students who just read List and Pettit on group agency and dont think metaphor is a bad thing. give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased like they would actually say them, with what i should concede and what i should hold. i especially need you to flag the weakest point in my own argument—where i claim the caste-level conflicts are cognitive rather than just sensory specialization—because i have a feeling the ethologist will drive a truck through it. and include the objection i'm secretly hoping nobody asks, probably about whether my view just collapses into standard functionalism with extra steps.
