Write a formal dialogue between two philosophers of science—Dr. Althea Voss and Dr. Marcus Chen—concerning Kuhnian incommensurability. Voss holds that paradigm shifts involve global ontological ruptures: because observational terms are theory-laden throughout, no neutral "epistemic scaffolding" can bridge competing frameworks without already presupposing one side's ontology. Chen denies global incommensurability and defends a modularist view: he argues that conceptual change typically proceeds by "limit-case embedding," in which older theoretical terms are preserved as approximations within a broader formal structure, citing the transition from Newtonian mechanics to special relativity—where "mass" and "force" were not eliminated but reconceptualized within a Lorentz-covariant framework—as the paradigmatic case. The exchange must be a genuine back-and-forth, not parallel monologues. Chen should directly address Voss's claim that any bridging framework smuggles in ontological commitments, and Voss should directly counter Chen's argument that formal continuity permits partial translation even in the absence of full ontological reconciliation. Both interlocutors must be equally competent: Voss may concede that mathematical formalisms allow structural continuity across revolutions, and Chen may concede that institutional and sociological factors render actual paradigm transitions messier than logical reconstruction suggests, but neither should abandon her or his core position. Avoid strawman representations, bibliographic references, citation lists, and appeals to specific page numbers or editions. Keep the tone rigorous, precise, and philosophical.
