Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises when the singleton hypothesis—interpreted as the thesis that long-run global strategic coherence tends to converge on a single top-level decision-making procedure—is applied to a domain in which local normative frameworks are not merely diverse but structurally incommensurable, so that no meta-language can translate between a community's deontological veto rights over certain interventions and another community's consequentialist optimization of aggregate welfare without normative residue. Specifically, illustrate whether the singleton can internalize this incommensurability without collapsing plural epistemic authority into a monistic preference-aggregation function, using a concrete hypothetical case—such as the allocation of critical resources between two such frameworks—where the unified agent must either preserve irreducible pluralism or subsume it under a single metric.
