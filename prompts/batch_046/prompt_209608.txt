The rubric instructs the evaluator to assign a report to the illusion category only when an independent physical trace confirms a misperceived stimulus, and to assign it to the hallucination category solely on the basis of that absence, explicitly disregarding the subject's own phenomenological descriptions—such as whether the experience carried the felt immediacy of seeing, or instead seemed like a vivid mental image. I find this puzzling because the practice treats the presence or absence of an external causal link as determinative of the mental state's kind, even though the evaluator has no direct access to that link and must rely on the very testimony being classified.
