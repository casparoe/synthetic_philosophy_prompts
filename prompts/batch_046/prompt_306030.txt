I'm writing up my stance on superrationality and symmetric reasoning for a rationalist forum thread, and I'd like a consistency audit before I post. Here is the package I currently hold:

(1) In Hofstadter's Platonia dilemma, the uniquely rational strategy is for each of the twenty agents to randomize with probability exactly 1/20, on the grounds that perfect symmetry among identical reasoners rules out any deterministic choice and makes any deviation self-defeating.

(2) By direct analogy, in the actual Luring Lottery conducted by Scientific American, the normative superrational response was to roll a mental die with sides equal to the expected number of respondents (about 5% of the readership) and submit exactly one entry only if the roll came up 1, preserving symmetry-breaking logic in a continuous strategy space.

(3) I take superrationality to be a normative standard, not a descriptive prediction: the fact that real contestants submitted astronomically large entry counts and destroyed the prize pool shows only that humans fail to coordinate, not that the reasoning is flawed.

(4) I also maintain that an agent may rationally condition on private information about its own reasoning trace—for instance, knowing that I have just finished deriving the optimal strategy—without breaking the symmetry required by (1), because every perfect copy of me shares that same trace and the same knowledge.

(5) At the same time, I think that if a Platonia agent receives credible, specific evidence that exactly one other agent has already decided to send, the rational update is to stay out with probability 1; but this seems to treat that other agent as an identifiable, non-symmetric party, which conflicts with the anonymity presupposed in (1).

Please examine these five commitments. For each pair that is genuinely in tension, rank the conflicts by severity, specify the least costly revision, and explain what that revision costs me conceptually. Identify at least one pair that looks tense but dissolves on inspection. Finally, tell me whether giving up (1), (4), or (5) individually would do the most to restore coherence, and whether the remaining package still yields a unified account of both Platonia and the Luring Lottery.
