I'm working through the Judy Benjamin problem and its extensions, and I've realized that most discussions jump straight to proposing a fix—Jeffrey conditioning, imaging, or some revision of Bayes' rule—without first specifying what an adequate theory of updating on conditionals actually needs to deliver. Before comparing solutions, I want the full desiderata list.

Specify the requirements a theory in this area must meet. The core case is Judy's update on a message that takes the form of a material conditional linking her location to headquarters division, but the list should also cover: updates on conditionals embedded within self-referential belief states (where the conditioning event involves the agent's own credence in the conditional itself); updates where the antecedent is vague or has fuzzy boundaries rather than a sharp partition; and updates on nested epistemic conditionals of the form "if my evidence were E, then P" where the conditioning event is counterfactual rather than observed.

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or consistency argument: what phenomenon it explains, what distinction it protects (e.g., between learning a conditional versus learning its antecedent and consequent separately, between evidential and causal readings of the update, or between descriptive and normative adequacy), and what contradiction or pathological result arises if it is dropped. Include the standard probabilistic consistency conditions, but also flag which of these are genuinely non-negotiable versus which could be relaxed for a descriptively adequate psychological model rather than a normatively correct one.

Then identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding the popular existing accounts—not just listing failures, but pinpointing the specific tension (for instance, between preserving commutativity of successive updates and allowing non-trivial belief revision upon learning a conditional). Keep the weight on the criteria and their justification rather than on constructing a full ranking table, though a brief scoring of the main contenders against the list is acceptable if it illuminates the criteria.

The goal is a specification against which one could evaluate a proposal without already assuming that Jeffrey conditioning or some particular probabilistic framework is the default.
