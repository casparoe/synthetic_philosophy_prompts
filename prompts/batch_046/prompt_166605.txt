Analyze the Late Bronze Age collapse around 1177 BCE through the lens of whether societies actually "collapse" or just restructure themselves into something less legible to archeologists. Focus on the Mycenaean palaces and the disappearance of Linear B — was that a failure, or just a shift away from centralized redistribution to local, non-literate economies? Now inject this twist: treat the chariot-and-bronze arms race between Hatti, Egypt, and the Levantine kingdoms as a kind of multipolar trap, where every state over-invested in imported tin and prestige goods until the trade network itself became brittle. Does framing the Sea Peoples and system failure as an emergent result of that race-to-the-bottom change whether we should call it "collapse"? Specifically, if the palace economies were locked in destructive competition that made them fragile, was the subsequent Iron Age decentralization a disaster for the people living through it, or mostly a disaster for the bureaucrats who kept the records? I want you to be honest about where the collapse/transformation distinction stops being useful, and don't just default to "it was complicated".
