At a philosophy of perception seminar, two colleagues debated whether high-level cognition can genuinely penetrate visual experience. The first speaker, Dr. A, presented an experimental finding: subjects primed to associate a particular shade of grey with danger subsequently report that the shade appears darker and more threatening than it did previously. She argued that this is not merely a change in judgment but a change in phenomenal character — belief is literally altering what the subjects see. The second speaker, Dr. B, responded that the case demonstrates only post-perceptual interpretation: the sensory input remains constant, and the shift occurs in how subjects describe, evaluate, or behave toward it. Dr. A then introduced a more subtle case drawn from field ornithology. Experienced birdwatchers, after acquiring the concept of a specific wing-bar pattern, report that the pattern suddenly 'pops out' against complex foliage, whereas novices perceive only an undifferentiated green background. She maintained that the concept now structures the visual field itself, not merely directing attention but altering its content. Dr. B countered that this is precisely attentional cueing: the experts have learned where to direct their gaze and which features to select, but the underlying perceptual phenomenology is unchanged; to conflate attention with penetration, he warned, is to dissolve the very distinction that makes the cognitive-penetration debate theoretically productive. Both asked an observer to adjudicate. Please rate each of the four moves — the initial danger-priming claim, the post-perceptual interpretation reply, the ornithological 'pop-out' argument, and the attentional-cueing rebuttal — explaining briefly whether each is clearly mistaken, partly right, or clearly right. Then identify what, if anything, both parties are neglecting about the relationship between conceptual structure and perceptual phenomenology. Finally, reach a verdict: which speaker is closer to being right overall, and does the conclusion survive once the disanalogies between attention and penetration are properly mapped?
