At a philosophy seminar, two interlocutors debated Dennett’s two-bitser in relation to Searle’s distinction between original and derived intentionality. (1) The first argued that the two-bitser undermines original intentionality: if a system with only two binary states can be predictively interpreted as wanting state 1 and avoiding state 0, then aboutness is merely an interpretive stance, with no principled complexity threshold separating real minds from artifacts. (2) The second replied that Searle’s concern is causal origin, not predictive utility; the bitser tracks temperature only because designers fixed its states to do so, whereas brain states are about things via biological causation, so the bitser possesses only derived intentionality regardless of interpretive success. (3) The first countered that scaling the two-bitser to replicate that exact causal profile with synthetic neurons and two bits of memory forces Searle to either deny it original intentionality arbitrarily or concede that the boundary is functional rather than biological. (4) The second insisted that scaling never removes derivation: even a perfect synthetic brain derives its intentionality from its designers unless it arose through natural evolution; the bitser remains an artifact whose “wants” are projected onto it, and brains qualify not by complexity but by being the right kind of biological system. (5) The first then proposed a hand-crafted carbon-based two-bitser assembled from organically arranged molecules that exactly match a minimal brain’s causal structure, claiming that without a non-circular distinction “the right biological kind” is an empty stipulation, leaving only ubiquitous interpretation-based intentionality or an unexplained primitive. Please adjudicate these five moves in exactly five numbered items, each one to two sentences with no introduction or conclusion: rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right with brief justification, identify which specific point each party gets right, and note what crucial consideration both miss.
