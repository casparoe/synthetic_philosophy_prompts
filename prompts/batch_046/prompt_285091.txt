i need a taxanomy / decision tree for my animal-ethics seminar — not a reading list, just an instrument that sorts people by what they actually think about insect sentience and the scale argument. start from this: you accept that insects might be sentient, but the individual probbility is low, and there are astronomically many of them. build the tree from the logical splits, not from named theorists.

first branch: does moral consideration aggregate across epistemic uncertainty, or do you need a per-capita confidence threshold? second: if aggregation works, does the sheer scale change what we owe — like, is there an obligation to intervene in wild ecosystems? third: do you accept the 'moral noise' objection that scale arguments, if applied consistently, drown out every other moral reason?

each leaf should carry a position name (or 'unnamed') and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. flag any pair the tree can't seperate — e.g., can it tell apart a risk-averse deontologist from a precautionary non-interventionist? audience is grad students who've done some ethics but not this niche literature. keep it to 6-8 leaves, phrase every node as a concrete claim they could answer yes/no to without knowing the label, and don't include bibliographies or citations — a model without search shouldn't need them.
