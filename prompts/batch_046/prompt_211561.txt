I am advising a small, conceptually oriented philosophy research group—three years of funding, no empirical laboratory, with collective strengths in metaphysics, philosophy of language, and normative theory. We must decide which open problems generated by the sorites paradox and the broader study of vagueness merit sustained collective effort, as opposed to being interesting but ultimately peripheral. I am weighing four specific lines of inquiry: (i) whether a context-sensitive, supervaluationist framework can be rendered sufficiently stable to underpin institutional classification systems—legal, administrative, or medical—that require cross-contextual consistency; (ii) whether the recent revival of 'ontic vagueness'—the view that vagueness resides in the world rather than in representation—yields tractable metaphysical consequences that are genuinely distinct from general debates about metaphysical indeterminacy; (iii) whether higher-order vagueness constitutes a genuinely autonomous problem or merely a recursive re-description of first-order vagueness, and specifically whether it undermines sharp-boundary epistemicist solutions in ways that other responses avoid; and (iv) whether vagueness creates distinctive obstacles to formalising normative or evaluative concepts—such as 'reasonable care' or 'substantial harm'—that matter for practical reasoning, rather than being confined to perceptual predicates like 'bald' or 'red'.

Please provide a defended agenda, not a survey. For each line (and any significant omission you wish to correct), assess: its upstream dependency—does resolving it restructure the others?; its tractability for a group without specialised formal logic or scientific expertise; its current neglect relative to its potential payoff; and the practical or theoretical consequences if it remains unresolved. Then rank them in order of priority for this specific group's resources and aims, and explain what empirical or theoretical developments over the next two to three years would cause you to revise that ordering. Please respond in British English, and avoid offering bibliographies or reading lists: I am interested in the reasoning, not in citations.
