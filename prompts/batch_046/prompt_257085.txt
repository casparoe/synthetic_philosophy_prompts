Write an exam essay question suitable for an upper-level philosophy of mind course that requires students to apply Daniel Dennett’s taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian and Gregorian creatures to the cognitive architecture of a large language model trained via next-token prediction and then refined through reinforcement learning from human feedback. The question must force students to argue whether such a system belongs to one of Dennett’s four kinds, falls outside them all, or occupies a hybrid position, and it must require them to engage with the structural distinctions—especially between Skinnerian trial-and-error learning shaped by external reward, Popperian internal hypothesis-testing that permits virtual failure, and Gregorian reliance on representational tools that extend cognition—rather than merely reciting definitions. Provide a model answer of approximately 700 words, written in British English, that takes a clear position (for example, that the system is best understood as a Skinnerian creature with superficial Gregorian outputs but lacking genuine internal modelling or embodied risk) and defends it by mapping specific features of the training pipeline onto Dennett’s criteria. Accompany this with a marking scheme that separates first-class answers—those which identify at least one precise architectural mismatch, such as the absence of an internal world-model being tested against counterfactual failure, or the difference between gradient-descent optimisation and natural selection—from second-class answers that apply labels descriptively without argumentative justification. The scheme should also specify the marks lost for particular misconceptions: for instance, conflating the production of grammatical language with Gregorian tool-use, treating parameter updates as Darwinian selection among competing organisms, or assuming that human feedback supplies the internal representational scaffolding characteristic of Popperian minds. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and frame the prompt so that it can be answered fully without reference to any specific publication date or page number.
