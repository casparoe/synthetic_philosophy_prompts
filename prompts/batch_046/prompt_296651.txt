I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of science, currently drafting my contribution for an interdisciplinary workshop on epistemic agency in automated research environments, and I want a consistency audit before I present. Over the past year I've settled on a view about epistemic dependence on AI and the corresponding erosion of understanding, but I worry I'm smuggling in incompatible premises. Here are the commitments I take myself to hold: (a) understanding a scientific domain requires more than true belief; it requires what I think of as a cognitive mapping—an ability to navigate counterfactual and explanatory relations within the domain—such that outsourcing that mapping to an opaque system eliminates understanding even when its outputs are reliably true. (b) There is a real distinction between delegating cognitive labor (using AI to compute, retrieve, or pattern-match) and delegating epistemic judgment (treating outputs as reasons without being able to assess their provenance), and only the latter erodes understanding. (c) In structurally complex domains—climate modeling, protein folding, large-scale epidemiology—no individual can maintain the full cognitive mapping, so some epistemic dependence is unavoidable; nevertheless, I maintain that a researcher who relies entirely on black-box predictions without grasping the intermediate theoretical steps genuinely lacks understanding of the result. (d) The erosion is not merely individual but collective: when a research community shifts from shared theoretical frameworks to shared reliance on proprietary models, community-level understanding degrades even if each member retains narrow local expertise. (e) Finally, I hold that this degradation is not categorically bad—understanding is instrumentally valuable but not always worth its cost—so I reject the view that we have an unconditional obligation to preserve it at the expense of efficiency or predictive power.

My question is whether this package actually hangs together. Specifically, does (c) commit me to the implausible claim that virtually every practicing climate scientist today lacks understanding, thereby undermining (a)'s plausibility? Does (b)'s distinction collapse in practice once system opacity makes provenance assessment impossible for anyone in the community, which would render (d) structurally inevitable rather than a contingent risk? And is (e) compatible with (a)'s framing of understanding as a form of cognitive mapping, or does treating understanding as optionally dispensable drain the normative force from the erosion claim in (d)? I want the tensions ranked by severity, any apparent tensions that dissolve on closer inspection separated out from the real ones, and an indication of the least costly single revision if I wish to preserve the core intuition that AI can erode understanding without condemning all modern large-scale science. I am not looking for a literature survey or citation check—just an internal audit of the commitments as stated.
