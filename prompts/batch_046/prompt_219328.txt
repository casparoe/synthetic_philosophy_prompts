As a retired physicist recently turned to philosophy, I ask you to translate Wittgenstein's argument against a private language—particularly the claim that rules for naming one's immediate inner states collapse without public criteria of correctness—into the conceptual vocabulary of experimental metrology alone. Use terms such as reference standards, systematic drift, inter-laboratory reproducibility, error budgets, and the distinction between precision and accuracy. To ground the translation, imagine a solitary metrologist in an isolated facility who maintains a unique, non-replicable crystal standard and calibrates all her instruments solely against her own prior readings, with no external comparison ever possible. Once you have rendered the philosophical argument in these terms, provide a residue report: identify which parts translate without distortion, which parts must be forced (for instance, treating sensory privacy as analogous to unobservable thermal drift), and which parts of the original argument have no counterpart whatsoever in calibration practice. Finally, assess whether that residue reveals a deficiency in the metrological framework or exposes an unexamined assumption in Wittgenstein's reasoning.
