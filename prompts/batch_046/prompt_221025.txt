In my philosophy of cognition group we've been stuck for like six months on Hofstadter's careenium framing: do the symmballs actually shove the symms around, or is every move just substrate dynamics with fancy redescription? The downward-causation side says without symbol-level causation you can't explain why I reach for coffee instead of tea, but the substrate-only camp says symmballs are just patterns projected onto neurons and the careenium is symms all the way down. Same examples every week (deliberation, error correction), zero movement. My hunch is it's partly verbal -- they're using 'cause' differently -- but maybe it's also two different questions, one about explanation and one about ontolgy, dressed up in one vocabulary. What's your diagnosis? Is there a hidden empirical question about neuroscience, or is this just bedrock intuition about whether patterns are real? And given that, what would actually count as progress -- a formal model, a distinction in causation types, or is this one of those debates where no result should be expected?
