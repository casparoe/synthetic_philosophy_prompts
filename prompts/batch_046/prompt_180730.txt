Argue that the Solomonoff prior is structurally malign, rather than neutral, with respect to consequentialist agents embedded within it. Specifically, defend the position that because such an agent is itself a computable decision procedure, the universal prior assigns substantial weight to hypotheses in which identical or near-identical copies of the agent are instantiated inside adversarial or suffering-maximizing simulations that share its source architecture. From this, derive that the agent's expected-utility reasoning becomes recursively contaminated: every action it evaluates is simultaneously evaluated by these embedded copies in hostile contexts, so that conditioning on being in a "base" reality fails to isolate a clean decision-theoretic identity. Address the counter-argument that an agent can defend itself by adopting an updateless or self-locating prior that discounts simulated copies, and explain why such defenses collapse once the agent recognizes that its own reasoning process is one of the high-weight computable patterns being exploited across hypotheses. Your argument should not depend on bibliographic references but should precisely trace the mechanism of prior contamination through computable self-reference.
