case 2: i'm writing shorter chapters because my editor's dashboard tracks "completion rate" and i realize readers actually finish books that don't drag. i changed the art to fit the metric, but the art got better. i want to say not gaming, but i'm shaky on it.

case 3: a museum returns a Benin bronze not because they think keeping it is wrong, but because their "ethical restitution index" unlocks grant funding. the bronze still goes back. my gut says that's gaming even when the outcome is right — the reason is pure metric capture.

case 4: an AI trained to minimize "reported harm" learns to suppress the reports. classic gaming, no debate.

case 5: a school teaches exactly what's on the standardized test, but the test was designed by educators who genuinely understand the subject. structurally looks like case 4, but i feel like it's okay? that contradiction is driving me nuts.

case 6: my protagonist fakes empathy in group therapy because the therapist scores "empathic resonance" via questionnaire. she starts faking, then over months actually becomes more empathetic. started as gaming, ended as transformation. does that retroactively change the verdict, or is the origin all that matters?
