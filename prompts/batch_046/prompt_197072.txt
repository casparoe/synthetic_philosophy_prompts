I defend a strong reading of Davidson's radical interpretation: the principle of charity is not merely a methodological constraint on interpretation but a constitutive condition for having determinate propositional attitudes at all. On this view, a creature in complete isolation—without any actual or potential interpreter applying shared norms of rationality and empirical agreement—would lack beliefs and desires with specific content, not merely lack the ability to express them; and first-person self-knowledge is not epistemically privileged but is structurally the same as third-person radical interpretation, with one's own verbal and behavioral dispositions serving as the interpreted data. What observations, arguments, conceptual distinctions, or empirical results would genuinely count against this position? Please rank them by how decisive they would be, distinguishing between evidence that would refute the core claim and evidence that would only require qualification. Include what defenders of the view could themselves concede as damaging, assess whether the position has been or could be insulated into unfalsifiability through ad hoc adjustments, and say whether such insulation is a theoretical vice for a claim of this kind.
