Construct a decision tree—no more than eight nodes—that sorts actual ethical stances on meat consumption by the answers a person gives to concrete counterfactual questions, rather than by the label they adopt. The root should present the beef-versus-chicken comparison: if one replaces chicken calories with beef calories, does the evaluation depend on lives ended, on suffering-duration per life, or on total welfare-years lost? From there, branch on crop-cultivation harm: does grain harvesting for direct human consumption kill more wild animals per delivered calorie than pasture-based beef production, and does that comparison change if harm is weighted by sentience-confidence rather than by headcount? A third branch must address intentionality: is there a morally relevant distinction between intentionally killing livestock for food and foreseeably causing wild-animal deaths as a diffuse supply-chain side effect, even when the latter is numerically larger? The leaves must include: a suffering-minimization position that permits some meat; a rights-based position prohibiting intentional killing regardless of counterfactual wild-animal deaths; the crop-death omnivore argument that favors beef or direct plant consumption depending on the numbers; a strict vegan position rejecting all avoidable harm; and at least one unoccupied logical combination—specifically the view that crop deaths are morally irrelevant yet beef is wrong because it involves intentional killing of large mammals—which you should flag as incoherent or merely unexplored. For every node, phrase the choice as yes/no or either/or using no philosophical jargon, and explicitly note which two leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate, particularly where the beef/chicken calculus and the crop-cultivation calculus pull in opposite directions. The audience is an advanced undergraduate seminar familiar with basic animal-ethics arguments but not with agricultural-ecology data; do not request citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
