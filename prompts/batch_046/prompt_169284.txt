Please rank the following five interpretations of temporal structure in Minkowski spacetime according to how well they reconcile Lorentz covariance with a metaphysically open future. Assign each interpretation a tier—S, A, B, C, or D—and justify its placement in a single sentence that addresses both criteria. The interpretations are: (i) the canonical Rietdijk–Putnam block universe, in which all spacetime points are equally real and determinateness is frame-relative; (ii) the moving-spotlight theory, which preserves a unique present that illuminates successive slices; (iii) the growing-block model, in which past and present exist but the future does not; (iv) presentism restricted by a denial of cross-frame identity conditions for temporal parts; and (v) a hypothetical 'branching-foliation' framework, defined here as one in which local causal structure determines a shared past hypersurface that splits at decoherence events into divergent present slices, so that covariance holds within each branch but global simultaneity is abandoned. In your tiering, indicate which interpretation best captures the determinateness-relation argument advanced by Rietdijk and Putnam, and note for the branching-foliation case whether its split at decoherence genuinely restores openness or merely relocates determinateness to the branching nodes.
