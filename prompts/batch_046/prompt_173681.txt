Im on the appeals panel for my graduate school and we got this case thats splitting us three ways. A PhD candidate in philosophy of science wrote her dissertation using a conceptual framework she developed from her community's traditional ecological knowledge — she built a taxonomy for soil-health interactions that doesnt split causes into mechanistic vs statistical the way our field does, and she argues the standard split itself is a hermeneutical tool that hides certain relationships. The external examiners, all mainstream analytic philosophers of science, said her categories were "confused" and lacked "conceptual clarity." She appealed saying this is hermeneutical injustice: they dont have the conceptual resources to understand her framework, so they treat their own gap as her deficiency. She also claims testimonial injustice — the reports call her reasoning "intuitive" and "narrative-driven" rather than argumentative, which she says tracks a credibility deficit.

We need to circulate a memo by Friday recommending whether to uphold the rejection, overturn it, or send it back for re-examination with conditions. The memo should actually work through the philosophical stuff — how to tell a real conceptual error from a hermeneutical gap, whether "clarity" is a neutral standard or smuggles in a metaphysics, how testimonial patterns show up in peer review — and it needs to treat the examiners worry seriously, not just brush it off. Give a concrete recommendation with a timeline. No bibliography or citation list needed, just reasoning both sides can actually respond to. And please, dont just restate the standard definitions — apply them to this specific mess.
