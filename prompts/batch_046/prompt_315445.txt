I am applying for a six-month independent-study grant to work through a problem I ran into while reading Karl Popper outside of any formal course. Popper is famous for arguing that Darwinian natural selection is not a proper scientific theory but a tautology: the fittest survive, and the survivors are defined as the fittest, so the statement predicts nothing that could fail to happen. He later softened this claim, and the standard view is that he simply corrected a mistake. But Popper also developed a theory of probability as propensity—a real, physical tendency in a situation rather than a mere statistical frequency. I want to investigate whether interpreting evolutionary predictions through Popper's propensity framework resolves the tautology charge in a way he never recognized, or whether the charge applies no matter how the probabilities are interpreted. Existing work in philosophy of biology examines Popper's retraction, and work in philosophy of science examines propensities, but the two literatures rarely intersect on this specific point. The method would be purely conceptual and take about six months. In the first two months I would reconstruct Popper's tautology argument and his propensity interpretation with enough precision to compare their logical structures. In months three and four I would apply that framework to concrete evolutionary scenarios—such as the spread of antibiotic resistance and the fixation of a neutral mutation—to see whether describing them in propensity-language generates predictions that could genuinely fail. Month five would be for drafting the argument, and month six for checking whether hidden circularity remains. A useful result would be a clear verdict: either Popper's two positions are in tension because propensities rescue Darwinism from his own critique, or the rescue fails and the tautology problem is deeper than the retraction suggests. The project counts as failing if propensity-language turns out to be only a redescription of statistical frequencies with no logical effect on evolutionary predictions. The scope is deliberately limited to conceptual analysis without empirical data collection, and I will add all references and citations myself once the argument is complete.
