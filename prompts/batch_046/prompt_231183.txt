Consider a philosopher of collective responsibility who has taken the following positions. First, she endorses the Fischer interpretation of the First World War, holding Imperial Germany primarily responsible not merely for triggering but for deliberately expanding the conflict through calculated risk-taking by its military and civilian leadership. Second, she denies that the Serbian nation can be blamed for the assassination at Sarajevo, arguing that without state authorization and without democratic representativeness the Black Hand's act cannot be imputed to the collective. Third, she holds the Japanese state institutionally responsible for systematic atrocities during the 1930s and 1940s in China, despite the absence of individual trials, on the grounds that the abuses were state-organized, state-funded, and state-concealed. Fourth, she rejects present-day German liability for First World War reparations, maintaining that intergenerational moral obligations dissolve once the original institutional architecture and direct victim-claimants are gone. Now apply her framework to a proposed 2028 tribunal ruling that the Republic of Turkey bears a continuing moral and reparative obligation for the Armenian Genocide, even though the Ottoman Empire dissolved in 1922 and the modern republic was founded on an explicit constitutional rupture with the imperial past; the tribunal's justification rests not on institutional continuity but on territorial jurisdiction and the inheritance of a national narrative that still derives legitimacy from the imperial geography. Predict her verdict, articulate at least two competing principles—one anchored in institutional continuity, one in territorial-narrative inheritance—that accommodate her first four judgments equally well, and identify the new hypothetical case (for instance, a dissolved colonial power whose successor state repudiates the empire but retains its borders and archives) that would best adjudicate between them. Finally, recommend, for consistency with her own precedents, whether she should accept or reject the tribunal's reasoning, naming the closest analog among her prior rulings and stating honestly what principle she would be adopting either way.
