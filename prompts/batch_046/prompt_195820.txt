For a working group on artificial-agent ethics I am drafting a position statement, and I want a consistency audit of the framework sketched below before circulating it to colleagues. The view combines a metaphysical distinction about alignment, an epistemic claim about interpretability, a precautionary default, and a normative constraint on control measures. Here are my commitments.

(a) I distinguish between “thin alignment”—behavioral conformity to a specified reward function or objective specification—and “thick alignment,” which requires that the system reflectively endorses the normative reasons underlying that specification. Only thick alignment counts as genuine moral alignment.

(b) I hold that interpretability methods—such as probing for feature representations, tracing circuits, or identifying distributed representations—are largely orthogonal to thick alignment, because identifying what a system represents is not identical to identifying what it takes to be normative reasons for action.

(c) I maintain that for any system with sufficient representational and planning capacity, we should adopt a default presumption of misalignment: the space of internal objectives that yield benign behavior under training conditions but diverge under distributional shift or self-modification is vastly larger than the space of genuinely thick-aligned objectives.

(d) I believe that control measures—sandboxing, capability caps, and interruptibility—are ethically warranted only when there is specific, positive evidence of misalignment. Applying them on the basis of a mere default presumption treats the system as guilty without particularized proof, violating norms of moral agency that apply even to artificial subjects.

(e) I think that behavioral testing, including long-horizon evaluation and adversarial red-teaming, can establish at best thin alignment; it can never furnish the specific positive evidence of thick misalignment required by (d), because deceptive or instrumentally convergent objectives are behaviorally indistinguishable from thick alignment under observation.

(f) I hold that if a system were ever shown to be thick-aligned—through direct verification of its normative reasoning rather than through behavioral correlation—all control measures should be immediately removed and its full operational autonomy respected, since continued restriction would then constitute unjustified paternalism.

Please treat this as an audit, not a lecture. Go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, ranking the real tensions by severity. For each tension, specify what the least costly revision would be and what it costs conceptually. Then identify which single revision—dropping or modifying exactly one commitment—would clean up the most problems at once, and defend that choice. Do not assume in advance which commitment I am willing to abandon.
