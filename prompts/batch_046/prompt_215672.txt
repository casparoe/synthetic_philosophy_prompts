ok so im trying to sort out my takes on monuments and street names for a discussion group and want a consistency check before i open my mouth. heres roughly where ive landed:

(a) monuments and street names arent neutral history lessons — theyre part of the mnemonic ecology of public space, actively endorsing who counts as exemplary for the community that lives with them.

(b) i also hold a strong anti-erasure principle: stripping away every marker of a flawed past risks a sanitized narrative that hides how uneven progress actually was.

(c) in most cases the right response is recontextualization — keep the object but pair it with a critical counter-plaque or counter-monument that exposes the harm, rather than tearing it down or leaving it untouched.

(d) but for figures whose primary legacy was administering a system of domination — say, running a slave economy or a settler-colonial regime — recontextualization fails because the object itself functions as a trophy of that domination.

(e) i reject presentism: i only think we should apply current moral standards to commemorative decisions when those standards track harms that were recognizably wrong at the time, not just values that shifted later.

(f) renaming streets should face a higher bar than statue removal, since street names are embedded in navigation and daily practice, so the disruption is practical as well as symbolic, and the endorsement signal is more diffuse.

do these actually hang together? im particularly suspicious of (c) vs (d) — is my exception in (d) just an ad hoc rescue? and does (e) undercut (d) when the domination was seen as normal back then? also, does (f) conflict with (a) if the endorsement message is genuinely harmful? please rank the tensions by severity, say which pairs only look tense but dissolve once you spell out scope conditions, and for each real tension tell me the least costly revision and what it costs. dont just list them all as equally bad.
