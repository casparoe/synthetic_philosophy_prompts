I was arguing with a friend about whether alot of philosophy is just people using words differently, and we ended up listing debates where it feels like there's real disagreement vs just talking past each other. I want to see if my intuitions form a pattern or if I'm being inconsistent.

My verdicts:
- Mathematical Platonism vs nominalism: not verbal. Even if both sides agree on every proof, they're disagreeing about what actually exists in reality.
- Free will vs determinism: mostly verbal. Once you clarify whether "free will" means "ability to do otherwise" or "acting according to your reasons," the heat mostly goes away.
- Whether race has a biological basis: partly verbal, partly empirical. There's real science about population genetics, but people also smuggle in social meanings.
- Whether a perfect computer simulation of a hurricane counts as actual weather: I say this IS verbal — it's about whether "weather" requires being made of water and air, or just implementing the right dynamics. Fix the definition and you fix the answer.
- But then there's the AI intelligence debate: whether "intelligence" is one-dimensional or a cluster of capabilities. I feel like this isn't verbal — there's a real fact about cognition — yet people constantly talk past each other using different metrics. So I'm torn.
- The fetus/personhood debate: I think it's partly verbal (what does "person" mean?) but also has real moral stakes that don't vanish with clarification.

So is there a principle here? Maybe disagreements survive "semantic ascent" only when they're about natural kinds? But then the weather simulation case messes that up, since I'd call that verbal despite being about natural kinds. Or maybe it's about whether there's an empirical resolution path once meanings are fixed? That saves the math and AI cases, but then why do I think free will is verbal when empiricists might study decision-making?

Can you lay out a principle — or a few candidates — that captures as many of these as possible? Be precise about which verdict it saves and which one it forces me to drop. And tell me what it predicts about a case I haven't mentioned: is the debate over whether "consciousness" in large language models is real mostly verbal, or is there a substantive disagreement there?
