I defend a modified halfer position on the Sleeping Beauty problem. Upon awakening, Beauty should maintain a 1/2 credence in Heads, because being awake on Monday and being awake on Tuesday are not two independent evidential updates but a single update—she is awake now—instantiated twice along one continuous causal-historical trajectory. To block the thirder’s centered-world counting argument, I propose that centered possibilities should be individuated not by subjective temporal index alone, but by causal-historical trajectory, so that Monday-Beauty and Tuesday-Beauty are not distinct epistemic possibilities but one possibility observed twice. Play it straight: what would have to be true for this trajectory-based halferism to be wrong? I want an honest assessment of its exposure—formal results in self-locating belief theory that require subjective-index individuation, betting-behavior intuitions that treat the two awakenings differently, analogies with other duplicated-observer or amnesia puzzles where trajectory-individuation collapses, and whether the view predicts anything its rivals do not. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a committed defender should concede versus dismiss, and tell me whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that insulation is itself a problem for a view of this kind.
