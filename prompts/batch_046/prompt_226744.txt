ok so ive been trying to wrap my head around money pumps and diachronich rationality — basically being consistent with yourself across time — and i think i accidentally built an argument that proves learning is impossible. heres the chain:

1. if my preferences on monday differ from my preferences on tueday, then there exists some sequence of trades that cycles me back to monday's mental state but with strictly less cash. thats the pump.
2. therefore anyone who cares about not being exploited should lock their preferences permanently and never update them, otherwise theyre vulnerable.
3. but obviously people learn — i used to think black licorice was good until i actually tried it and updated.
4. so either im constantly getting pumped (which im clearly not), or diachronic rationality requires that i remain permanently ignorant.
5. but remaining ignorant is self-defeating and probably worse than losing a few dollars.
6. therefore diachronic rationality must be impossible, or learning makes you irrational by definition.

this feels like it proves way too much. i really want to hold onto (3) — learning should change preferences — but then i have to reject (2) and admit im not diachronically rational, which sounds bad. is the mistake in assuming the pump works for continuous or smooth updates, not just discrete jumps? or is there a hidden cost to running the pump that makes it impractical? also what does this say about commitment races where everyone locks in early — are they just being diachronically rational, or are they being stupid?

specifically im stuck on a variant: what if my tueday preference comes from running a prediction-error correction, like in RL? then monday-me and tueday-me share the same underlying reward model, just with updated weights. does that block the pump, or does it just push the problem up a level? if i reject step 2, what exactly am i paying?

where's the leak?
