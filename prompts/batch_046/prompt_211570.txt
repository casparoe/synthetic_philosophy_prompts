I just finished a long argument with a friend over whether sphexishness—the mechanically fixed burrow-checking loop of the Sphex wasp—has any real bearing on human free will. He insisted that since we can adapt our plans, we are obviously not sphexish, therefore free will is safe. I countered that flexibility and freedom are not the same, and that he was treating rigidity as the only threat. I now need to teach the actual distinction to first-year philosophy students with no background in philosophy of mind, in a single twenty-minute session. Plan the explanatory arc: the dependency chain—what concept must be in place before flexibility can be meaningfully contrasted with rigidity—the one concrete scenario to build it around and precisely which features it needs and lacks, the misconceptions they will arrive with and form (especially the slide from "not rigid" to "therefore free" and the confusion of mechanical loops with causal determinism), the point in the sequence where each should be intercepted, the check for understanding that shows they have separated flexibility from freedom rather than just learned the wasp anecdote, and what I should explicitly set aside at this level—such as whether indeterminism rescues agency.
