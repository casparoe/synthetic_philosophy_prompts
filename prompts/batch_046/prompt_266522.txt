I hold a qualified version of the uselessness thesis associated with Weinberg, Hawking, and Feynman: institutional philosophy—peer-reviewed metaphysics and academic philosophy of science—exerts essentially no causal influence on the trajectory of mature empirical research. I grant that scientists deploy conceptual vocabulary that overlaps with philosophy, but I claim they generate and refine these tools internally through mathematical formalism and experiment, not through philosophical argument. Apparent counterexamples, such as Mach’s influence on Einstein or logical positivism’s interaction with quantum mechanics, I take to be instances of rhetorical borrowing or conceptual housekeeping done by scientists themselves, rather than genuine guidance from philosophical reasoning. I also distinguish sharply between heuristic conceptual work that occurs inside a science and the institutional practice of philosophy proper.
