I am drafting a novel about a sculptor who deliberately destroys his own hands with corrosive stone dust to demonstrate that his work answers to no patron, rival, or potential mate, but only to an abstract, unobservable standard of absolute artistic integrity. Please explain the conceptual tension that arises when costly signaling and the handicap principle are applied to behavior directed at no empirical audience—where the cost is borne precisely to eliminate every possible observer. In particular, I want to understand whether such self-destructive display can still be analyzed as an honest signal of underlying quality if there is no recipient capable of conferring reproductive or social advantage, and where the theoretical boundary lies between biologically coherent waste and a conceptually rigorous but evolutionarily empty form of proof.
