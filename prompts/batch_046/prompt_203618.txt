You serve as the philosophical ethics advisor to the Acquisition and Ethics Committee of a mid-sized contemporary art museum, and the committee must vote next Tuesday on Work 447, a large abstract canvas. The work was physically executed by the hand of the now-deceased artist Elena Voss—using her own pigments, brushes, and a partially begun under-drawing found in her studio—under the direct supervision of her longtime assistant, who completed the surface texture according to Voss’s written notes. Voss never signed it, and estate records show she filed it in a private “disavowal archive,” part of a documented artistic practice in which she deliberately produced unsigned, completed studio pieces to test whether retrospective authorial revocation could dissolve a work’s status. The piece entered the market after Voss’s death through a dealer who believed it was an unfinished study; the museum acquired it in good faith in 2019. The estate now demands deaccession and return, arguing that because disavowal was embedded in Voss’s artistic concept, the work is an “authorized non-work”—causally hers but institutionally void—while curators counter that causal and supervisory history fixes authorship regardless of later mental states.

Please draft the formal memo you will circulate before Tuesday’s meeting. Recommend one concrete course of action—retain with revised attribution and labeling, place in restricted study storage for a defined period, or initiate deaccession—with an explicit time frame and implementation steps. The memo should treat the estate’s position with full philosophical seriousness, engaging the core tension between genetic authorship (causal origin and artistic supervision) and performative or institutional authorship (the artist’s documented practice of revoking status). Include the strongest argument for the option you reject. Before presenting the recommendation, briefly list the three considerations you take to be decisive. Write in a precise, institutional voice suitable for circulation to board members with legal and curatorial backgrounds, and avoid any call for external bibliography or further literature review.
