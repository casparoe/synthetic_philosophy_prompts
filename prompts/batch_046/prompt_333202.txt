Eventually, however, the institutional work required to preserve coherence—arbitrating conflicting edits, maintaining backward compatibility, resolving disputes over classification—begins to scale non-linearly with the size of the community. At this point, a distinctive behaviour emerges. Rather than over-extracting value, some participants anticipate that central governance will fail under the coordination burden. They begin maintaining private, local forks or shadow versions as insurance against institutional breakdown. These defensive copies do not deplete the shared resource, but they gradually siphon away the participation and commitment that central maintenance requires. The result is a self-fulfilling collapse: the shared institution fragments not because it was overused, but because the expectation of coordination failure prompted a preemptive exodus. Outside observers frequently mischaracterise this as classic commons overuse, yet the underlying material remains intact; what erodes is the collective capacity to govern it.
