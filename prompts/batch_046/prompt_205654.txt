In debates about African philosophy I keep encountering the slogan “I am because we are” — usually invoked to show that Ubuntu makes personhood fundamentally relational rather than individual. Before I use it, I need to know what precise claim it actually expresses. Can you lay out the distinct theses this proverb could stand for — for instance, a metaphysical claim that the self is constituted by social relations; a normative claim that moral status depends on being embedded in a community; a conceptual claim that “person” is undefined outside a social context; or an empirical claim about how identity is formed — and for each say whether it is a trivial platitude, a substantive defensible position, or false? I want a committed answer, not a neutral menu: take a definite stance on which reading best captures what Ubuntu philosophers actually intend, and on whether any of these readings genuinely undermines liberal conceptions of individual rights or merely restates them in different vocabulary. Please don’t ask me to consult specific texts or scholars I wouldn’t have at hand.
