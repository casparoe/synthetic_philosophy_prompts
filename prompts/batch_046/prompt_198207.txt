ok so i tried mapping aristotles sea-battle thing onto decision theory and now the argument eats itself. (1) if tomorrows sea battle is genuinely contingent, then neither "there will be a battle" nor "there won't" is true right now. (2) but decision trees need every branch to carry a definite outcome—otherwise you cant weight expected utility, since there's no truth-condition to assign probability to. (3) so if real contingency exists, rational choice about the future is impossible. (4) yet we clearly do deliberate rationally about tomorrow. (5) therefore contingency is an illusion and determinism holds. (6) but determinism makes deliberation redundant—every branch is already fixed. (7) so either rationality requires determinism (which kills the point of deciding) or contingency kills rationality. where's the escape? i want to keep (2) and (3) if i can, but i feel like im smuggling in something about probabilities that doesn't belong.
