Write the desiderata that any theory treating karma as an account of moral desert must satisfy to count as adequate rather than merely metaphorical. For each requirement, state the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it: how the theory must handle apparently undeserved suffering in this life without collapsing into cosmic injustice; whether it must preserve a sharp distinction between moral desert and bare causal consequence; how it resolves the “initial conditions” problem of a first life that lacks prior desert to ground its initial state; whether it can allow merit-transfer or forgiveness without dissolving personal accountability; what it must say about the scaling of reward and punishment relative to the wrong (proportionality); and what it owes on the epistemic opacity of past-life connections, given that the mechanism linking present suffering to prior action is unobservable. Mark which of these are non-negotiable for any desert-based reading and which are camp-specific preferences—such as literal rebirth versus psychological continuity, deterministic karman versus libertarian free will, or physicalist versus mentalist accounts of the carrying vehicle. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable, especially if precise proportionality conflicts with epistemic opacity, or if universal determinism undermines the conditions usually thought necessary for genuine moral desert. Keep the weight on the criteria and their internal tensions; after fixing the list, briefly score Buddhist Abhidharma, Jain karman mechanics, and secular psychological karma theories against it, but only after the list itself is established.
