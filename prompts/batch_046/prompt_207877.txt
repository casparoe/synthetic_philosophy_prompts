I am writing this in the wake of a recent professional betrayal: a long-time collaborator deliberately misrepresented my contribution in a joint grant proposal, and I have been trying to calibrate my moral response with philosophical rigor rather than mere anger. I have sketched out a position, and I need to know whether it is jointly tenable.

First, I accept what I take to be Strawson’s core framework: moral responsibility is best understood through reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation, and their kin—rather than through metaphysical doctrines of libertarian free will. I do not think that determinism, should it turn out to be true, by itself dissolves the legitimacy of holding someone responsible.

Second, I also maintain that reactive attitudes are genuinely appropriate only when the agent exercised what I call deep-self authorship: the action must flow from a will that is genuinely the agent’s own in a hierarchical sense, not from manipulation, brainwashing, or alien causal interference that severs the link between the agent’s reflective endorsements and their behavior.

Third, I am strongly inclined toward determinism, and I take it that any such deep self is itself the deterministic product of heredity, environment, and prior causes. I do not claim this automatically excuses anyone, but it does seem to entail that no one is the ultimate originator of their own psychological constitution.

Fourth, regarding my particular case, I think my resentment is justified only if my colleague retained the capacity to recognize moral reasons—if they were not psychopathic or severely cognitively impaired—not merely if they acted badly. Simple misfortune or weakness of will, I believe, does not by itself remove the grounds for reactive attitudes.

Fifth, I hold that the objective attitude—the stance of viewing a person as an object of social management or treatment rather than as a participant in interpersonal moral relations—should be adopted toward anyone whose harmful conduct is fully explainable by causal factors lying outside their psychological control. At the same time, I assume that such a full causal explanation applies to every human action, including my colleague’s.

Please audit these five commitments. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, rank them by severity, and for each indicate the cheapest revision that would restore consistency together with what that revision costs me. Identify also whether there is an apparent tension—perhaps between my conditions for appropriate resentment and my broad application of the objective attitude—that dissolves once one distinguishes causal explanation from excusing psychological incapacity. Finally, tell me which single revision—abandoning the deep-self authorship condition, abandoning determinism, modifying my view of the objective attitude, or revising my conditions for appropriate resentment—would eliminate the largest number of conflicts at once, and whether that revision is worth the price.
