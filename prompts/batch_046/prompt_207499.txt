Can you walk me through the tension in Darwins writing between morality and religion, specifically in the Descent of Man and his private letters? He explains moral sympathy as an evolved social instinct that gets refined into abstract ethics—like caring for strangers—while he explains religious belief as the brain over-applying agency detection to unknown causes. Both seem like evolutionary byproducts, but he treats them totally differently: moral progress is real and worth cultivating, religious belief is an error that education should erode. So whats the principled distinction? Is it just that sympathy has social utility and religion doesnt? Or is there something deeper?

Also, there's this weird undercurrent where Darwin still sounds like he thinks moral advancement has a kind of directional value—almost like secular providence—even though he officially rejects design. Some readers say he's smuggling in a religious-style sense of cosmic obligation without the theology. Do you think his purely naturalistic account of morality can actually carry that weight, or does it need the kind of transcendent framing he thinks religion falsely promises?
