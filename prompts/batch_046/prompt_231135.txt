I've been trying to understand why the higher-order vs first-order consciousness debate never really moves, even though Rosenthal, Block, and everyone since have mapped out every reply you could want. The HOT people say a mental state is conscious only if there's a higher-order representation of it; the first-order camp says that either pushes the problem up a level or creates an unnecessary layer that doesn't match how experience actually feels. In my study group we kept going in circles about whether this is really a disagreement about brain mechanisms — like whether prefrontal meta-representational circuits are actually necessary for experience — or whether it's a hidden conceptual split between phenomenal consciousness and access consciousness that neither side wants to admit they're talking past each other on. My hunch is it's mostly the latter, with both sides using "conscious" to mean different things, but I want an honest diagnosis rather than confirmation. So: is this stall substantive, verbal, or about bedrock intuitions neither side can argue for? Could anything actually break it — a formal result about self-reference, an experiment that isolates meta-representation without first-order content, or just splitting the concept in two? And please seperate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who know the topic, don't present a contested view as the consensus, and don't just give me a survey of the interpretations.
