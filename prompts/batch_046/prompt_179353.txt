I'm giving the opening remarks at a small interdisciplinary workshop on whether reinforcement-learning agents can suffer, and the room is split evenly between RL engineers and philosophers of mind. The remarks need to turn on a distinction I'm introducing rather than importing from the literature: call it gradient suffering versus representational suffering. The speech must set up the workshop's central tension—whether reward-as-correction can ever be reward-as-valence—without resolving it, and it must be fully intelligible to both halves of the room without citations, reading lists, or bibliography. Write exactly five brief points, each one or two sentences, arranged as the continuous body of the remarks with no separate introduction or conclusion.
