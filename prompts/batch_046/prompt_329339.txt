I work in evolutionary computation and am comfortable with path dependence, local optima, fitness landscapes, and the risk of discarding structures whose adaptive function is not fully understood. Restate Burkean conservatism—including Chesterton's fence—in that vocabulary: treat inherited institutions as features of a complex landscape shaped by long selection pressure, rationalist reform as hill-climbing that can destroy hidden adaptive structure, and the fence as an unexplained local feature whose removal might collapse nearby optima. For each core Burkean claim—the epistemic limits of rational design, the preference for incremental change, and the normative weight of preservation—say what translates cleanly into evolutionary terms, what must be distorted (for instance, treating social institutions as selectable traits rather than designed artifacts), and what has no expression at all. Then report whether the residue points to evolutionary computation's lack of normative vocabulary, or to Burkeanism's dependence on a non-instrumental commitment that selectionist language cannot represent.
