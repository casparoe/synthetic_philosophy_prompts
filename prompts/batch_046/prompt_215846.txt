I've been arguing with my roommate about what makes someone the same person over time. He's made some calls on weird hypothetical cases and now I wanna see if his logic actually holds up.

His verdicts so far:
- Guy gets hit by lightning, total amnesia, personality totally flipped, family still calls him Dave but he feels like a stranger and eventually moves away and changes his name. Roommate says thats not Dave anymore, thats just some new guy in the old body.
- Teleportation accident copies every memory and trait onto Mars, original destroyed. He says thats Dave, he just relocated.
- Over thirty years every neuron gets replaced with synthetic ones, no break in consciousness. Still Dave.
- Someone wakes up convinced they're actually a fictional character from a novel they read, lives that role, forgets their old life completely. Roommate says the old self is dead, now they're that character.
- Split brain surgery, both halves conscious and arguing with each other. He says now there are two people, Dave got replaced by twins.
- Dave writes an autobiography thats mostly lies but he fully believes it and shapes his whole life around that story. Roommate says thats still Dave, the story IS him.

Next case we're gonna talk about: a woman has a stroke that wipes her memory and personality completely. Her family decides to never tell her her real past. They invent a false biography, show her fake photos, teach her piano to match the lie, and she lives the rest of her life in that false story, feeling totally continuous with it. She never leaves, never finds out, never gets her old memories back.

Predict what he'll say. Is she still the pre-stroke woman? Tell me which rule he's actually running — psychological continuity, that narrative center-of-gravity idea, or something else — and name the one further case that would actually split those two apart.
