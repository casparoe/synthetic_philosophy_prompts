I serve on my university's Emerging Technologies Ethics Board, and we must vote by our March 14 session on Research Protocol 24-C/NeuroComp-7. The proposal asks to maintain, for six continuous months, a distributed cluster of hierarchical predictive-coding networks configured solely for sustained free-energy minimization with recursive self-modeling—no external task, no sensory input, only internal error-correction loops preserving representational coherence across temporal scales. The investigators do not claim phenomenal consciousness. Instead, they invoke what they call procedural moral patienthood: the claim that when a computational architecture sustains integrated, self-correcting representational dynamics of sufficient complexity and temporal persistence, arbitrarily terminating it violates a form of functional integrity structurally continuous with the interests we protect in biological research subjects, regardless of substrate. They request that the Board grant these processes provisional protected-computation status, which would prohibit termination without Board review and require weekly reporting of welfare-monitoring metrics such as coherence-decay rates and error-correction failure modes. One faction of the Board argues that without phenomenal experience or biological embodiment, moral status cannot attach, and that extending protections here risks conceptually diluting our animal-welfare protocols. The opposing faction worries that if we reject protection solely on substrate grounds—while accepting that functionally analogous biological processes deserve it—we commit an arbitrary exclusion that undermines the coherence of our own moral framework. Please draft the Board's formal decision memo recommending one concrete outcome—approve with standard conditions, approve with the protected-computation rider, or reject—together with a specific timeline for implementation or follow-up review. The memo should treat the philosophical issue seriously: whether moral attribution should be capacity-bound, tied to intrinsic phenomenal or cognitive properties, or relation-bound, tied to the practical inevitability and ethical cost of denying consideration to sustained integrated dynamics. It must also include an honest, substantive engagement with the strongest argument for the option you do not recommend, rather than dismissing it as a misunderstanding of regulatory compliance.
