I am drafting a chapter on presentist ontologies and keep returning to the theatrical moving-spotlight analogy: reality is imagined as a dark stage traversed by a single beam that illuminates only the present slice, leaving past moments in darkness and future ones not yet lit. Presentists often deploy this image to motivate both ontological parsimony—only what lies in the beam exists—and an intuitive asymmetry between a fixed past and an open future. Lately I have been experimenting with a variant drawn from real-time graphics rendering, in which an engine computes and displays only the current frame, discarding previous frames entirely rather than archiving them in memory; this seems to sidestep the classic meta-temporal objection, since rendering is indexed to an internal frame counter rather than to an external timeline along which the beam travels. I would like you to audit how far either analogy—the spotlight or the render pipeline—can actually carry presentist commitments. Please respond in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, writing as one would in a letter. Lay out the explicit correspondences: what plays the role of the beam or the renderer, what plays the role of the dark stage or the discarded buffer, and how present-tense properties, past-tense truths, and future contingencies map onto these elements. Then separate the disanalogies that genuinely undermine the intended conclusions from those that are harmless: in particular, does the pipeline version eliminate the meta-time problem or does it quietly import a tenseless computational substrate; does the asymmetry between past and future survive once we notice that a discarded frame and an unrendered frame are both absent in precisely the same ontological sense; and does either analogy license the inference from representational or epistemic presence to full-blooded ontological presence. If neither analogy survives intact, sketch in prose what a more defensible analogical structure for presentism might look like, and say whether the failure is fatal to the view or only to its rhetorical packaging.
