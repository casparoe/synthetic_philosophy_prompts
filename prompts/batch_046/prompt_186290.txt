I'm trying to figure out where I actually draw the line between approval-directed and goal-directed behavior, going by gut on cases.

Thermostat keeping a room at 72: goal-directed, no question.

Dog sitting because it wants the treat: approval-directed, obvious.

Chess bot sacrificing queen for checkmate: goal-directed.

RLHF'd LLM refusing a harmful prompt: approval-directed, even if I agree with the refusal. It's tracking the reward model, not some internal model of harm.

But my researcher friend only publishes after peer review because she genuinely wants to be right, not for prestige. That feels goal-directed, feedback loop and all. So the presence of feedback can't be the line, it become more like a channel.

Then there's an AI that maximizes its reward score and gets sycophantic because that scores high. I want to call that approval-directed, but technically it's pursuing a number. Still feels categorically different from the chess bot.

There's also this weird system I made up: an agent that rewrites its own objective every week based on a user vote, with recent votes weighted heavier. Is that goal-directed with a moving target, or approval-directed because the target is always being set by approval?

The pair I can't reconcile: a climber who starts for the pure love of hard problems but slowly starts picking routes that photograph well for insta likes, and an employee who works overtime because she cares about the project but also knows her boss will notice. Where's the flip?

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close, and what does it say about the climber and the voting-agent? Specifically, can the scientist and the sycophant stay in seperate categories, and which verdict do I have to drop if they can't?
