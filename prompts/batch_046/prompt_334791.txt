I was chatting with my roomates Jay and Mara about this philosopher Bernard Williams — I read a blog post about him, don't ask me for the title — and they got into a fight over two of his ideas. Jay said Williams has this integrity objection where utilitarianism forces you to drop your own ground projects whenever the numbers say so, which basically destroys what makes your life yours. Mara said that's not the heart of it; the heart is this example about a husband saving his wife from drowning and Williams saying if the husband thinks well saving her maximizes happiness then he's got one thought too many — he should just save her because she's his wife, no calculation. Then Jay said those are just the same criticism wearing different clothes: in both cases utilitarianism makes you step outside your own commitments and treat them like inputs in a calculator. Mara pushed back hard — she said the integrity thing is about the long-term architecture of your life getting crushed by moral demands, while the drowning example is about the psychology of a single moment, the reason you act right then, so they're different levels.

Jay came back with: but if being a good husband IS one of your ground projects, then crushing that project and having one thought too many are literally the same failure — you're not letting your love be the direct reason. Mara said that's a stretch, and also she wondered if Williams actually wants you to defend your projects reflexively, or if there's something wrong with being too self-conscious about them too. Then Jay asked, wait, isn't defending your ground projects just because they're yours also a kind of one-thought-too-many? Like you're reflecting on ownership instead of just living inside the project. Mara said she wasn't sure, maybe Williams wants attachment without total blindness, but she honestley didn't know. They turned to me to referee and I don't. Can you break down who's right about which specific point? Are these two objections really the same deep idea or totally separate? And if they're separate, does one hold up better? Also, is there something both Jay and Mara are missing about how integrity actually works — like maybe there's a middle ground about being reflective but not calculative?
