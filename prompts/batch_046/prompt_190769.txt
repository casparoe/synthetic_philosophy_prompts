For a seminar on revolutionary historiography I am trying to settle my own position before writing my essay, and I want to check whether these five commitments form a coherent package or quietly undermine one another.

(a) The Revolution is best explained as class struggle: the bourgeoisie dismantled feudal privilege to secure capitalist property relations, so its social outcomes should be read through that material interest.

(b) The Declaration's universalist rhetoric was not mere superstructure but possessed autonomous causal force: once enunciated, rights-language escaped bourgeois control and genuinely radicalized the Revolution beyond what that class intended.

(c) Revolutions structurally devour their children: any attempt to remake society through centralized state violence generates an internal logic of escalating purification that eventually consumes the revolution's own architects, so the Terror's self-cannibalization was overdetermined rather than accidental.

(d) The specific trajectory of 1793–94—including the fall of the Girondins, the Law of Suspects, and Robespierre's own execution—was largely contingent on wartime panic, harvest failure, and factional personality conflicts; had the military situation or weather differed, the leadership might have survived intact.

(e) The sans-culotte movement represented a genuinely autonomous popular-democratic force with its own class interests and organizational capacity, not reducible to a bourgeois instrument, yet it never achieved hegemony and was ultimately suppressed by the very state it helped empower.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision for each? In particular, does (c) contradict (d), or can structural inevitability be reconciled with contingency about the particular victims and timing? Does (b) undermine (a) by granting ideology independent efficacy, or can class interest accommodate an unruly rights-discourse? And where does (e) fit: if the sans-culottes were autonomous but suppressed, does that support (c)'s structural logic or conflict with (a)'s bourgeois-primacy? Please rank the tensions by severity, state which single revision would clean up the most inconsistencies, and be explicit about which of my verdicts—especially on whether the Terror was inevitable—have to be abandoned under the simplest coherent reconstruction.
