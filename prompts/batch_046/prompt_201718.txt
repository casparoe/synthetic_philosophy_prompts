Write a short story of about 800 words set inside an experimental chamber where engineers have isolated a region with an inverted thermodynamic arrow: within its walls, entropy decreases locally, shattered objects reassemble, and thermal energy flows from colder bodies to hotter ones. The protagonist is a forensic analyst called in after a body is found inside. Because causation and memory traces run backward relative to the exterior world, she cannot reconstruct events by tracking evidence forward in time. Instead, she must use statistical mechanics—specifically the Crooks fluctuation relation and boundary-condition matching at the chamber walls—to determine whether the death resulted from a rare spontaneous fluctuation or from deliberate external interference that breached the isolation. The narrative should treat the inversion seriously rather than as magic: include at least one explicit physical mechanism (for example, a Maxwell-demon-style information gate controlling particle flow) and use it to drive the plot. By the end, the story should leave open whether concepts like "motive" and "culpability" retain their meaning when the local arrow of time points the other way, without offering a definitive philosophical resolution.
