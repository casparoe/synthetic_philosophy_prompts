I'm a governance referee for a federated network of autonomous research collectives. We have adopted a Lewis-style framework that treats local procedural norms as coordination equilibria rather than moral mandates, and I need a reusable decision procedure—usable in under twenty minutes, without requiring philosophy training—to determine whether a contested local practice at one collective should be grandfathered into our shared protocol, renegotiated, or rejected. The procedure must be built directly on Lewis's conditions: a coordination problem with multiple possible equilibria; a stable regularity sustained by common knowledge; arbitrariness of the particular solution relative to alternatives; and evolutionary dynamics involving salience, precedent, and drift. Structure it as an ordered sequence of tests with explicit go/no-go nodes, clearly marking which steps are mechanizable and which are unavoidable judgment calls. Include hard cases: a practice that solves coordination but undermines informed consent; a regularity that persists after membership turnover has destroyed the common knowledge that originally sustained it; and a practice that is genuinely arbitrary yet conflicts with a non-negotiable collective principle. Apply the procedure to three hypothetical cases, including one where it yields a recommendation you consider wrong and explain how a practitioner should handle that conflict. State clearly what the procedure cannot do—where it will misfire or remain silent. Give a committed, specific framework rather than a survey of interpretations of convention or norm evolution.
