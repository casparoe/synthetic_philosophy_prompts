I am drafting a three-month fellowship proposal in moral philosophy. I want to investigate Bernard Williams's integrity objection to utilitarianism—specifically, the idea that an agent can have one thought too many when weighing personal commitments against impartial moral calculation. Rather than revisiting the familiar demandingness debate or cataloguing agent-relative reasons, I want to ask whether the objection tacitly relies on a unitary model of the self in which every motivating reason must be transparently accessible to a single deliberative standpoint. If practical reason is modular, with distinct subsystems that can converge on an action without sharing representational formats, does the integrity objection still apply, or does it dissolve into a different kind of concern?

Write the proposal document. It should include: the precise research question; why existing answers in normative ethics and empirical moral psychology leave this particular issue open; the conceptual approach—distinguishing integrity as phenomenological transparency from integrity as functional coherence across subsystems—and why this framing is more productive than the standard alternatives; what a successful result looks like (for instance, a taxonomy of integrity conditions compatible with modular practical reasoning) and what would count as failure; and a realistic scope and deliverable for a three-month project with no empirical data collection. Refer to positions and arguments generally rather than providing a bibliography or citation list, and write the response in American English.
