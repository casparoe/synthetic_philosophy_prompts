As a retired physicist who has only recently begun systematic study in philosophy of science and historiography, I have been trying to formalize my views on demarcation and on particular cases of pseudohistorical reasoning. I would like a consistency audit of the following package of commitments, which I present without indicating which, if any, I am prepared to abandon.

(1) I accept a modified Popperian criterion: empirical falsifiability is necessary, though not sufficient, for distinguishing legitimate historical inquiry from pseudohistory.

(2) I classify Fomenko's New Chronology as pseudohistory not primarily because its specific chronological claims are false, but because it operates as a closed hermeneutic loop—every contradictory radiometric, astronomical, or textual datum is systematically reinterpreted via auxiliary hypotheses about widespread textual corruption and conspiratorial suppression. I take this to violate a condition I call "chronological revisability."

(3) Regarding Plato's Atlantis narrative, I hold that it functions chiefly as a philosophical allegory about imperial hubris and cyclical decay; yet I do not regard its allegorical status as logically excluding the possibility that independent geomorphological and sedimentary features in a specific maritime region could provide an empirical constraint on Plato's spatiotemporal coordinates.

(4) I reject "ancient astronaut" explanations for anomalous monumental architecture not on grounds of prior implausibility, but by appeal to a parsimony principle I term "transmission-cost economy": the social, technological, and epistemic mechanisms required to preserve and transmit advanced knowledge across millennia of civilizational discontinuity are ontologically more expensive than the anomalous features they purport to explain.

(5) I maintain that the boundary between legitimate revisionist historiography and pseudohistory is fundamentally normative and institutional. It hinges on whether practitioners adhere to "revisable chronology"—a documented willingness within the research community to reorder established event-sequences in response to independent stratigraphic, radiometric, or astronomical dating evidence.

(6) At the same time, I believe that certain historical inferences carry what I call "free-floating warrant": their evidential force derives from structural relations among independent data sets (for instance, cross-correlated dendrochronological and ice-core records) rather than from the psychological states, social practices, or institutional commitments of the researchers who deploy them.

Please examine whether these six theses hang together. Specifically: identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to conflict; rank the genuine tensions by severity; for each tension specify the least costly revision and what it sacrifices; and indicate whether a single revision exists that would resolve the greatest number of conflicts. Treat the set as provisionally held in full.
