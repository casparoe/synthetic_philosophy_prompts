My friend and I spent last evening arguing about Gödel's rotating universe and whether it genuinely undermines the reality of temporal passage. He laid out what he called a reductio of Gödel's own position, and I cannot locate the fault. Here is his chain, step by step.

1. Gödel's metric permits closed timelike curves: a traveller can return to the exact spatio-temporal point she started from.
2. If a path returns to its own origin, then along that path earlier and later are not asymmetrical: the relation before is not irreflexive.
3. Gödel claimed this shows that objective time — a global linear ordering — does not exist.
4. But causation requires asymmetry: if event A causes event B, B cannot cause A.
5. So wherever the metric permits closed curves, causation is impossible.
6. The metric is a solution to Einstein's field equations for the whole universe; if it holds globally, its properties constrain local regions too, since the field equations are local differential equations integrated globally.
7. Therefore, even in our local region, the possibility of closed curves elsewhere undermines the asymmetry required for local causation.
8. Hence, if Gödel's universe is physically possible, causation fails everywhere, including here.
9. But causation manifestly succeeds here.
10. So Gödel's universe is not physically possible.
11. Yet Gödel intended the model not as a physical prediction but as a demonstration that time is ideal — in the Kantian sense — precisely because the global absence of linear time is compatible with local experience.
12. My friend says this makes Gödel's position self-defeating: he uses the model to deny objective time, but the model's implications destroy the local conditions that make experience coherent, so we ought to reject the model rather than conclude time is unreal. I feel something slips between steps 6 and 7, or perhaps between 3 and 4, but I cannot name it. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost for Gödel's broader argument that temporal passage is not objectively real?
