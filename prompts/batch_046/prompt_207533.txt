I cover philosophy of mind and cognitive science for a publication focused on ideas, and I am helping a speaker prepare for a seminar defence at an interdisciplinary institute. The speaker will argue that symbolic representations—those Hofstadter names simmballs—exercise genuine downward causal influence over neural substrate within the careenium framework, not as mystical dualism but as an architectural feature of hierarchical predictive systems, and that feedback dynamics in large artificial networks now render that downward pressure empirically visible rather than merely analogical. The audience comprises mechanistic neuroscientists and physicalist philosophers of mind who treat downward causation with suspicion, seeing it either as a homunculus fallacy or as an unnecessary overlay on substrate-level dynamics. Please prepare the speaker for the objections they will actually face: rank them by likelihood and damage, phrase each in the vocabulary that audience uses, indicate which carry real force and should be conceded up front, supply a concise reply for each with its weakest point clearly noted, and identify the one question the speaker hopes nobody raises. The reply must be usable in real-time debate; do not include reading lists, citations, or bibliographic references.
