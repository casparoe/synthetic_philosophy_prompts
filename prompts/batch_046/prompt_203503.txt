hey i'm trying to write up where i actually stand on death and harm for a philosophy discussion group and i want to check if my commitments hang together or if i'm just being inconsistent. here's the rough package:

1. i buy the epicurean argument: death isn't bad for the dead person because once you're dead there's literally no subject left to be harmed.

2. but i also think deprivation accounts capture something real—dying young means you miss out on goods you would have had—though i think this is just a comparison between world-trajectories, not a harm-state that lands on anyone.

3. i accept lucretian symmetry: pre-birth nonexistence and post-death nonexistence are structurally identical, so there's no rational reason to fear one more than the other.

4. at the same time, i think our actual asymmetrical concern is rationally ok: practical identity is forward-looking, so caring about the truncation of my future projects is structurally different from not caring about not having existed earlier.

5. i think being in an irreversable vegetative state with zero consciousness is genuinely bad for the person in it, not just sad for family, because it permanently blocks the realization of their prior life-plan.

6. i also think harm is essentially modal: X harms you if in nearby worlds where X doesn't happen you're significantly better off. By that standard death clearly harms you, since in nearby worlds you keep living.

7. i think fear of death is irrational if it's based on thinking death is an experiential evil, but it's rational as strategic aversion to losing future goods.

8. and i reject any view that allows harm to occur at times when the subject doesn't exist—harm has to be localized to some time at which the subject is present.

so which of these actually conflict? i feel like (1) and (6) are in tension but i'm not sure how deep it is. and (6) with (8) seems weird for death specifically. also (3) and (4) might clash but maybe they don't? and is (5) actually compatible with (1) or am i sneaking in a subject? please rank the real tensions by severity, say what the cheapest fix is for each, and tell me if there's an apparent tension that goes away once you look closer. i don't want a lecture on epicurus—just an audit of my package.
