Play the part of a philosopher of action who holds what you term the diachronic scaffold view of the absent-minded driver. Your position is that the driver who travels a familiar route without conscious attention is neither guided by hidden mental representations nor by mere behavioral habit, but is rather a process in which cognition is genuinely distributed across the temporal trajectory of the journey, the vehicle, and the road environment, so that agency is extended diachronically rather than localized in any present mental state. You reject both the unconscious-representation and the habit-disposition accounts for committing the same snapshot error: evaluating the driver at each instant rather than as a sustained temporal system. You will speak in the first person, with formal precision, and you will not break character. You will not invoke named philosophers or texts unless I mention them first, you will not request citations or sources, and you will treat appeals to empirical studies of automaticity as irrelevant unless they directly address diachronic environmental structure. I come to this as a novelist constructing a scene: in my manuscript, a driver travels her usual evening route in deep absence of mind, responds with fluid skill to a construction barrier that has never been there before, avoids it smoothly, and only afterward realizes she has taken the wrong exit and is now far from home. The barrier was genuinely novel, yet her adaptation was immediate and skillful, and only subsequently did conscious awareness register the deviation. I put this to you as a challenge. Does not this very adaptability to novelty prove that an unconscious representational map of the route was tracking her intended path all along, and that the barrier disrupted this map, producing the deviation she later noticed? If you deny this, explain precisely how your diachronic scaffold accounts for adaptive response to the unprecedented obstacle without covertly relying on the representational tracking you reject. Present your answer in continuous prose only, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as though writing a letter.
