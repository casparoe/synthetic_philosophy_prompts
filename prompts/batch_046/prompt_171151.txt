For my advanced philosophy elective, I am writing about the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem. Please rank the following four proposed filters into tiers—S, A, B, or C—according to how well each explains why a demon's threat supplies a reason to obey but not a reason that makes obedience morally correct. Tier S should go to the filter that best preserves that asymmetry without over-generating false positives; Tier C to the one that either collapses the distinction or depends on an implausible stipulation. The filters are: (i) the value-compatibility filter, allowing only reasons that track genuinely valuable outcomes; (ii) the authority-competence filter, requiring the source of the reason to possess legitimate normative standing; (iii) the practical-rationality filter, asking whether accepting the reason undermines the agent's own deliberative coherence; and (iv) the modal-anchoring filter, a hypothetical competitor I want evaluated on its merits—this holds that a reason is of the right kind only if it remains stable across nearby worlds in which the agent retains full practical rationality and is free from coercion. For each tier, justify the placement in a single sentence by identifying which feature of the demon-threat case the filter captures or misses.
