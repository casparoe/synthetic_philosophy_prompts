I'm trying to name a pattern I keep noticing in how people respond to different infinite rearrangements. Most people comfortably accept the well-known hotel case: infinitely many rooms are occupied, a new arrival comes, and every existing guest moves to the next room, freeing room one. They treat this as a neat feature of actual infinity, not a paradox. But if I present a structurally parallel case—an infinite line of custodians, each holding exactly one irreplaceable object with a specific individual history, and I instruct them all simultaneously to pass their object to the person in front while receiving the one from behind—many of the same people balk. They grant that every position remains filled and that no object has vanished, yet they insist something essential has been lost: every custodian now holds the wrong item, and the original one-to-one pairing between holder and object is destroyed across the entire infinite line. The discomfort seems tied not to the existence of the infinite collection, but to whether what is being shifted carries non-interchangeable identity or historical continuity rather than mere numerical occupancy.

Does this selective resistance have an established concept or name? I am not asking about finitism as a blanket denial of actual infinity, since these respondents typically accept that the infinite line exists; rather, they resist a particular operation performed on it. Is there a recognized distinction—perhaps in philosophy of mathematics, metaphysics of identity, or discussions of actual versus potential infinity—between accepting infinite accommodation of interchangeable units and rejecting infinite transmission of individually tracked items? How exactly does this relate to standard debates involving Hilbert's hotel, Cantor's treatments of one-to-one correspondence, and finitist objections, and where are its edges: does it apply only to artifacts with intrinsic histories, also to persons, only when the shift is total rather than partial, or not at all? If nothing in the literature fits precisely, what term and boundary conditions would capture it? I will not be satisfied with a bare label; I want to know what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and whether the nearest existing concepts misclassify it.
