I serve as the student representative on my school district’s Bioethics Review Panel, and for my AP Philosophy of Mind course I have been asked to draft the actual memo we will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. A university partner has submitted a comparative neuroscience proposal that would implant micro-electrodes in octopuses and rats to compare interoceptive predictive-coding patterns, testing whether cephalopods achieve what the researchers call “phenomenal binding” or only “functional integration without subjective unity.” The study’s framing draws on the idea that consciousness in distributed nervous systems might be “disunified”—not one stream but modular fragments—so that an octopus could be aware without being a single, unified subject of suffering. Half the panel considers this standard comparative cognition with routine welfare safeguards; the other half argues that if phenomenal unity is genuinely absent, we cannot apply our usual harm metrics, yet if it is present in fragments we might still miss suffering distributed across non-cortical ganglia, making invasive monitoring ethically premature.

Please draft the panel’s formal written opinion recommending either approval, rejection, or approval with strict conditions, including a concrete timeline and specific procedural requirements. The memo should take the philosophical stakes seriously—addressing whether gradable or “disunified” consciousness undermines conventional animal-welfare frameworks—while engaging honestly with the strongest consideration on the side you reject. Write in an institutional voice with the bottom-line recommendation stated clearly in the opening paragraph, followed by reasoning that a researcher or ethics officer could respond to directly. I need this to read as a real decision document rather than an essay, and please do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading, since I must be able to defend the reasoning without external lookup.
