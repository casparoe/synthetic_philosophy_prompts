There's a thesis I'm working through that says the old claim that victors write history is actually too weak — it treats power as just filtering a pre-existing record. I think the stronger claim is right: the victor's instituional position doesn't merely suppress counter-narratives, it constitutively shapes what counts as a historical fact. The categories we use to describe a defeated uprising — rebellion, insurrection, terror — are already encoded with the victor's normative framework, and even recovered subaltern archives have to be translated into those categories to be legible as history at all. So there's no neutral standpoint to adjudicate between victor and vanquished accounts; the disagreement is partly conceptual, not just evidencial.

What would actually count against this? I don't want the usual but archives exist reply — I'm asking for defeaters that hit the constitutive claim specifically. Which cases show counter-categories surviving intact? Which results in conceptual relativism or translation theory does my view lean on, and where do those crack? Has this position been qualified into falsifiablity — if every recovered voice is said to be already translated, is that insulation or a bug? Rank the threats from fatal to merely awkward, and say honestly whether a defender should concede any of them.
