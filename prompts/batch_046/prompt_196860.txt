me and my friends were hanging out after a talk on decision theory and Maya and Jordan got into it about whether hyperbolic discouting actually solves akrasia or just sweeps the problem away. (1) Maya said look, akrasia is mostly a story we tell ourselves. With hyperbolic discounting your preferences are time-inconsistent: at 7pm you genuinely prefer not eating cake, at 8pm you genuinely prefer eating it. There's no "weak will" there, just two rational selves with different discount rates. (2) Jordan pushed back and said thats exactly the issue — you've dissolved the phenomenology instead of explaining it. Real akrasia involves judging X is best all things considered and still doing Y, then feeling like you messed up. Hyperbolic discounting says at the moment of action you genuinely prefer Y, so theres no conflict, just a switch. To save the experience Jordan brought up this idea she called "normative residue" — the earlier judgment doesnt vanish, it leaves a lingering authority that the present self violates. (3) Maya fired back that normative residue sounds fancy but it's just emotional hangover. If the 7pm self had real rational authority, why should the 8pm self listen? You cant have it both ways: either preferences are genuinely different and neither has authority, or you're sneaking a metaphysical "true self" in through the back door. (4) Jordan said thats unfair — we build precommittment devices like freezing credit cards or putting alarms across the room precisely because we treat our calmer judgments as authoritative, not just nostalgic. If it were pure hangover we'd ignore it like a hangover. The fact that we lock ourselves in shows the residue has normative weight, not just emotional weight.
