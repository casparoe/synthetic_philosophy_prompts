Draft the joint statement that would emerge from a year-long adversarial collaboration between two camps on Bernard Williams's integrity objection—specifically the claim that impartial moral reasoning can involve one thought too many. Camp A holds that the objection reveals a structural flaw in any theory requiring agents to weigh their ground projects against neutral, impersonal reasons; Camp B holds that the objection only exposes a misrepresentation of practical deliberation, in which first-personal commitments are wrongly treated as fungible inputs to a third-personal calculus, and that a properly structured agent-relative justification can accommodate impartial constraints without destroying integrity.

The statement should include: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims—about Williams's rescue case, the role of ground projects, the first-personal/third-personal distinction, and the conditions under which integrity is genuinely threatened; (2) a disagreements table in which each camp's position is phrased in wording it would accept, isolating whether the damage to integrity comes from the mere presence of an impartial reason or from a specific structural feature of the reasoning process; (3) at least three cruxes, one of which concerns whether there is a meaningful distinction between projective ground projects (self-authored life aims) and associative ground projects (commitments arising from relational or institutional roles), and whether the objection applies with equal force to both; (4) for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, together with each side's advance prediction—using, for the projective/associative crux, the case of a physician who must choose between violating a long-standing professional oath and saving her own child when both are imperiled and only one can be saved.

Keep the document constructive: separate genuine disagreements from verbal ones, avoid asking for citations or bibliographies, and do not invoke technical apparatus that depends on obscure recent literature. Write in American English.
