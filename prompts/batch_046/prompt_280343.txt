I serve on an institutional review board that meets weekly to adjudicate whether individuals in complex clinical and organizational contexts bear what we call 'true moral responsibility' for harmful outcomes, as distinct from mere functional accountability or legal liability. We work within a framework informed by Galen Strawson's Basic Argument — particularly the claim that being genuinely responsible for an action requires being genuinely responsible for the psychological and dispositional constitution from which that action flows, which in turn requires being genuinely responsible for the prior conditions and choices that formed that constitution, generating a regress that cannot be completed — but the board needs this translated into a practical protocol that members without philosophical training can run in about ninety minutes per case, with no access to external literature and with a signed recommendation required at the end. The procedure must take the form of an ordered sequence of questions or tests, with explicit criteria for what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, clear stopping rules that yield a definitive 'no true responsibility' verdict, and clearly marked hand-off points where the remaining issue is a matter of institutional values rather than the logical structure of agency. It must also handle practical complications that the pure argument does not address: long-term institutional conditioning that shapes an agent's values over years, voluntary neurological interventions that alter risk preferences or emotional responses, and cases in which an agent has partially modified their own character through sustained effort. Please construct this reusable decision protocol, then apply it to three cases: a senior manager who enforced a harmful policy after decades of organizational conditioning; an individual who acted violently following a voluntary cognitive enhancement that changed their impulse-control architecture; and a situation in which the procedure reaches an impasse that requires the board to make a value judgment without philosophical cover. At every stage, indicate where mechanical application will misfire, where judgment is unavoidable, and what the procedure explicitly cannot determine.
