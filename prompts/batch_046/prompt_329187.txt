I live in this co-op of about 340 people in a converted mill building, and we've got this lighting dispute that's been eating at me. One resident, Marco, has this rare neurological thing where our 5000K LED bulbs trigger these crushing migraines that can knock him out for 72 hours. For the rest of us the lights are totally fine, but if we switched back to old warm incandescants to protect him, every single person would feel this barely-there eyestrain — like maybe 1% more fatigue over the day, nothing you'd notice unless you were really tracking it.

The board ran some numbers and said that 340 people times a microscopic discomfort adds up to way more total suffering than Marco's occassional massive attack, so they're voting to keep the LEDs. I keep pushing back because it feels wrong to just spread out harm like that and treat everyone's tiny discomfort as interchangeable units that can be summed up against one person's huge pain. Is there actually a principled reason why you can't just pool all the little harms together and compare them to one big harm, or am I just being sentimental because I know Marco? What's the right way to think about this?
