You advise an international travel platform that needs a reusable policy for whether users should treat local conventions—tipping, queuing, jaywalking—as moral obligations or as optional etiquette, especially when they are short-term visitors. Operationalize the relevant philosophical distinction into a practical decision procedure that a non-specialist compliance officer can apply in about fifteen minutes. The procedure should take the form of ordered questions with criteria for a sufficient answer, explicit stop-points (no obligation / obligation applies / defer to judgment), and clear flags for where values rather than facts determine the outcome. It must address whether a visitor’s ignorance of the convention is excusing, and it must distinguish coordination norms from norms that protect legitimate expectations of harm or fairness. Apply the procedure to three cases: refusing to tip in a culture where tipping is not customary but service workers depend on it for wages; cutting a queue in a society where queuing is strictly observed but not legally enforced; and jaywalking where local law permits it but local social stigma is strong. For each case, indicate where the procedure yields a determinate answer and where it collapses into an unavoidable judgment call. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading; keep the reasoning grounded in the conceptual distinctions themselves.
