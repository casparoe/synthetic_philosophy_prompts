I'm noticing a pattern in how people talk about 'cognitive offloading' that doesn't quite fit the usual extended-mind story. Here's the thing: a student uses a cloud-based note app that doesn't just store her thoughts but actively reorders and summarizes them with an AI she never trained. Over time her reasoning starts depending on the apps specific clustering patterns, but she can't reproduce that reasoning offline. Meanwhile a musician uses a smart metronome that adapts tempo to his heart rate via a shared database; his sense of rhythm is now partly constituted by a distributed system he only partly controls. In both cases there's external scaffolding, but it's not just 'the notebook is like memory' because the system is opaque, dynamic, and shared across users. Is there an established term for this kind of dependency on an active, non-transparent, collective external cognitive system? If not, can you carve out what it is, how it differs from the classic extended-mind case and from embedded cognition, and where its edges lie? Don't just drop a label — I want the distinctions, not just a name. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating the question. Definately avoid just listing authors.
