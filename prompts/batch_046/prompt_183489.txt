I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently turned to philosophy in earnest. Having absorbed Quine’s naturalism and his argument that ontology is not prior to science but internal to our best theoretical schemes, I have formed a view I shall call moral-indeterminacy naturalism. On this view, moral values are not among the fundamental entities tracked by our best sciences; they are, rather, culturally entrenched but theoretically dispensable posits—analogous in status to physical objects, but with far lower indispensability and far greater susceptibility to replacement. I further take Quine’s thesis of the indeterminacy of translation to extend to ethical discourse: apparent moral disagreements between cultures may not correspond to any objective conflict, because there may be no fact of the matter about which manual of translation is correct, leaving only divergent behavioural dispositions and conceptual frameworks.

What would genuinely count against this position? I am not asking for familiar objections I have already considered, but for an honest map of its exposure. Specifically: what empirical results in moral psychology or neuroscience would make it implausible that moral cognition is best explained as a culturally variable posit rather than as tracking real properties? What formal or conceptual considerations—perhaps concerning the logical structure of normative reasoning, or its indispensability within scientific practice itself—would resist treating moral discourse as merely instrumental? Are there arguments internal to Quine’s own framework that would compel a revision, for instance if normative commitments turn out to be ineliminable from the very scientific reasoning that is supposed to supersede them? And which of these escape routes are live rather than merely logically possible?

Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, indicate which ones a committed defender of the view could concede without abandoning it, and say whether the position has been insulated into unfalsifiability and, if so, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind. I should also like you to distinguish predictions that this view makes which its rivals do not. Please write your response in British English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, as I shall have no access to external sources.
