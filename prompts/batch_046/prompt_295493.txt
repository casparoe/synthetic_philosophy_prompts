Write a short story of roughly 700 words set in a coastal settlement called Veldt, where disputes are resolved through "standing hearings" rather than punishment: anyone may call another to account for harm, but only if the caller retains "clean hands" with respect to that specific wrong. The custom is designed to prevent hypocrisy—if you have done the identical thing, you lack the moral standing to blame.

Your protagonist, Mara, is a mediator who has never invoked the custom herself. She is asked to hear a case brought by an elder, Kess, against a young fisherman, Dain, for "harvesting debt"—a practice of catching surplus and letting it rot rather than distributing it, which the settlement treats as direct harm to the commons. Mara learns two things in quick succession: first, that Kess engaged in harvesting debt himself during a famine decades earlier; second, that Mara has long depended on imported grain that reaches Veldt only through distant trade networks that rely on exactly the same rotting-surplus practices in other ports. The narrative should turn on whether Kess's personal past and Mara's structural complicity both undermine standing, or whether only one does. In particular, explore whether standing can be "partitioned" by scale or proximity—whether benefiting from a distant system is different in kind from doing the harm locally.

Structure the story so that the philosophical tension drives the plot: Mara must decide whether to proceed with the hearing, dismiss it, or invent a procedural compromise. Do not resolve the tension into a neat moral; instead, end with Mara making a specific procedural choice, followed by a brief coda set one year later showing the concrete consequence of that choice for Kess, Dain, and the settlement's custom.

After the story, include a brief author's note of two to three paragraphs identifying where the narrative takes liberties with the philosophical concept of standing to blame—for example, whether it treats standing as binary or scalar, whether it risks conflating moral hypocrisy with epistemic authority, and whether distance genuinely attenuates complicity. In this note, state explicitly what argument or consideration would change your interpretation of who retains standing and why—such as a sharp distinction between direct participation and indirect benefit, or an account of institutional versus individual agency. Finally, indicate where your reasoning is most likely to fail, particularly regarding whether scale or geographic separation can genuinely cleanse a caller's hands, or whether that distinction merely rationalizes convenient blame.
