Many defenders of routine LLM use in reasoning and scholarship draw an analogy between querying a language model and consulting a reference work—such as a technical encyclopedia or a standardized lookup table—to argue that epistemic dependence on AI does not erode individual understanding. In this comparison, the user corresponds to a researcher consulting a manual; the model corresponds to the entry or index; verification corresponds to cross-checking the result against known principles; and understanding is treated as distributed across the human-tool system rather than diminished. The conclusion licensed is that, just as using a dictionary or mathematical table does not destroy one's grasp of language or arithmetic, outsourcing reasoning steps to an LLM merely redistributes cognition without degrading it. Audit this analogy: provide an explicit correspondence table mapping each element of the reference-work scenario onto the LLM scenario; identify the disanalogies, separating those that undermine the anti-erosion conclusion from those that are benign; specify which properties of reference works—stable authorship, transparent derivation, user-controlled retrieval, and explicit citation—are quietly imported into the AI case where they are absent or altered; and determine whether the claim that understanding is preserved through redistribution survives once these gaps are made explicit. If the analogy fails to carry the conclusion, propose a more precise comparison—perhaps one that emphasizes opacity, probabilistic synthesis, or the absence of a shared conceptual framework—and state whether that replacement supports or undermines the view that AI dependence is epistemically benign.
