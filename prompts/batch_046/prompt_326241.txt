I am a senior software engineer with no formal philosophy background. I am facilitating a 90-minute workshop for twelve colleagues—mid-level engineers with no philosophy training—on ethical system design. I want them to experience, rather than memorize, the distinctions among negative liberty, positive liberty, and republican liberty, specifically through the lens of platform governance and opaque administrative control.

Design a structured role-play or simulation that can run in about 75 minutes of active time, leaving 15 minutes for debrief. Assign participants to three rotating roles: Users (who select features and believe they face no direct barriers), Platform Administrators (who hold unaccountable discretionary power to revoke access without notice or appeal), and Auditors (who can observe changes but cannot block them). The rules and symbolic payoff scheme—using only work-credit points, not real money—should be arranged so that Users initially feel negatively free, then discover that Administrators can arbitrarily interfere with their choices, and finally must decide whether collective self-legislation or transparent, contestable architecture could constitute a different kind of freedom.

Include: specific rules that force trade-off decisions before any philosophical terms are introduced; a timeline broken into 15-minute segments; instructions for how the facilitator should remain silent about the concepts during play; a debrief that maps each stage onto the standard negative, positive, and republican framework, with explicit attention to the republican claim that freedom requires not merely the absence of interference but the absence of arbitrary domination; and an honest note on which feature of republican liberty—namely, the institutional requirement that all interference be non-arbitrary and subject to contestation—the simulation can approximate but cannot fully reproduce without explicit philosophical framing.

Finally, predict what will go wrong on the first run: which role will likely dominate discussion, what misconception about freedom-as-absence versus freedom-as-collective-control will predictably emerge among the engineers, and how to correct it without delivering a lecture. Avoid bibliographies, citations, or assigned readings; participants have no access to academic sources, and I will not be able to provide them.
