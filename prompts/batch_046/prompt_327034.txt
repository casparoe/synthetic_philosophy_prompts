Please construct a sustained argument for the claim that Sartre's analysis of bad faith in Being and Nothingness is not best read as a uniform account of self-deception about radical freedom, but rather as conflating two structurally distinct phenomena—call them facticity-dominant and transcendence-dominant bad faith. Your argument should establish that facticity-dominant bad faith involves the agent treating contingent social roles or biological givens as having evidential authority over the phenomenological data of transcendence, thereby producing a false belief about the scope of one's freedom; whereas transcendence-dominant bad faith involves no false belief at all, but rather a kind of motivational collapse or existential fatigue in which the agent fully registers their freedom yet refuses to bear its practical weight, distributing epistemic authority away from the self in order to avoid the burden of choice. In developing this, you must address Sartre's well-known example of the café waiter and explain, in detail, why the waiter's overly precise performance of his role should be interpreted on your view as an instance of transcendence-dominant rather than facticity-dominant bad faith. Further, show how recognizing this distinction resolves a tension in Sartre's text between his insistence that bad faith is always a lie to oneself and his simultaneous claim that the agent in bad faith is in some sense aware of the freedom they deny. The argument should be developed entirely from Sartre's own conceptual apparatus—radical freedom, facticity, transcendence, the for-itself, and being-for-others—without appealing to external psychological theories or empirical studies.
