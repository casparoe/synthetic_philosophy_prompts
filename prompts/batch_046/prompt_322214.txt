As a retired physicist who spent much of my career studying nonlinear dynamics, I have lately begun reading in philosophy of science and find myself uncertain how deterministic chaos should reshape our assessment of determinism itself. I would like you to rank the following five conceptions in tiers—S, A, B, C, and D—according to how defensible each remains once we take seriously sensitive dependence on initial conditions, the practical limits of computation, and the distinction between ontological fixity and predictive access: (1) Laplacian determinism, understood as the thesis that complete knowledge of present state together with the laws yields exact knowledge of all future states; (2) ontological determinism, the claim that every event is fixed by prior conditions plus laws regardless of whether prediction is possible; (3) methodological determinism, the view that scientific explanation requires deterministic laws but does not entail that the world itself is fixed in every particular; (4) statistical determinism, which holds that only ensemble-level or distribution-level evolution is fixed, while individual trajectories lack meaningful determination; and (5) a hybrid conception I have been trying to articulate, which I call structural trajectory determinism—the idea that in a non-integrable Hamiltonian system the global geometry of phase space, including strange attractors, mixing properties, and Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy, fixes the set of possible futures and their probabilities without fixing any particular path, so that determinism applies to the shape of possibility-space rather than to individual outcomes. For each tier, provide a brief justification. I am particularly interested in whether (5) is genuinely distinct from (2) and (4) or merely restates one of them in geometric language, and in whether chaos theory gives us reason to reject (1) without thereby undermining (2). Please separate what is settled among philosophers of physics from what remains genuinely contested—especially regarding whether epistemic unpredictability has any bearing on ontological determinism, and whether macroscopic chaos interacts with quantum indeterminacy—and do not present any contested interpretation, such as the claim that chaos refutes free will or that quantum effects propagate deterministically upward, as if it were the consensus view.
