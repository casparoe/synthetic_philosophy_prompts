me and my roommate keep arguing about whether those weird philosophy thought-experiments actually prove anything or if theyre just rhetorical tricks. he described this modified brain-in-a-vat scenario: not one brain being fed signals by scientists in a real lab, but two brains wired in a loop, each generating the sensory world for the other, so theres no outside world at all, just the loop. he says this kills the idea that experience needs a real-world cause, since the loop is totally self-contained and still feels real. i said thats just the old vat argument with an extra brain, and removing the scientists doesnt change whether i can know im not in it. he said no, the hidden point is that in the normal version people secretly picture those scientists sitting in an actual room, so they think 'there is a real world behind the curtain,' but in the loop there is no curtain at all, which forces you to face that coherence alone doesnt need a world behind it.

then he brought up swampman -- the lightning-strike duplicate of a person with no history -- and said its a legit pump for asking whether mental content needs a causal past. i said its just exploiting the ick factor of randomness being spooky, not actually showing anything about thoughts. he said all intuition pumps hijack an emotional reaction and call it philosophical insight. i said without them youre stuck only reasoning about people you actually know, so you cant stress-test your concepts.

who's right about what here? go through our moves step by step. and are we missing something about why these stories feel convincing even when they dont really prove stuff?
