hey — i'm on the university's Institutional Epistemics Review Board (IERB, pronounced 'eye-urb'), and we've got an actual decision due by friday, not that anyone outside this room cares. there's a petition from the archive to officially classify the Morland Field Notebooks — about forty notebooks from a 1970s ethnographic study, all in a private shorthand the reasearcher never documented and she's been dead since 2003 — as 'permanently epistemically sealed.' they want to move them to non-recoverable storage and stop funding preservation.

our charter, updated in 2019 after a philosophy grad student convinced the provost, says we can't certify a record as permanently inaccessible unless we've shown that no physically possible agent could come to know its contents. the archive staff say that's exactly the case here: the mental code is gone, brute-force reconstruction is computationally intractible, and even quantum methods wouldn't recover a private symbolic system without some seed.

but Dr. Chen on the board keeps raising a Fitch-style problem in our meetings. her worry: if we certify 'the contents of notebook 12 are unknowable,' that certification is itself a piece of knowledge about the notebook. combine that with any principle that lets knowledge spread across related claims, and she thinks we're flirting with a contradiction — admitting there's an unknown truth right when we claim to know that it's unknown. she doesn't want the board to implicitly commit to a collapse where certifying one sealed record forces us to deny our own epistemic authority.

half the board thinks this is just pedantry — storage budgets are real, the shorthand is genuinely dead, approve it. the other half thinks we should reject the petition, or at least approve only with conditions, because our charter's commitment to physical possibility of knowledge conflicts with certifying any content as in principle lost, and because if there's even a logical tension we shouldn't paper over it with administrative convenience.

keep it formal enough that the board can vote on it, but readable. don't ask for a bibliography or reading list. and please don't drop in fake quotes with quotation marks — i don't want to have to check pages.
