I've recently been asked to advise on policy-relevant modeling in climate economics, which has forced me to articulate my philosophy-of-science commitments more precisely than usual. Before I present them, I want a consistency audit. Here is the set:

(1) I accept the F-twist argument—associated with Friedman—that unrealistic assumptions in scientific models are not legitimate targets of criticism provided the model yields accurate predictions.

(2) I hold that genuine scientific explanation requires representational fidelity: the model's internal structure must map onto actual causal mechanisms in the target system, not merely reproduce outputs.

(3) I distinguish Galilean idealization, where minor causal factors are omitted, from representational distortion, where core assumptions about dominant mechanisms are known to be false; I think only the former is methodologically legitimate.

(4) I observe that in both economics and climate science, the models with the strongest predictive track records—integrated assessment models with homogeneous-agent representations, linear damage functions, and perfect substitutability—rely precisely on representational distortion rather than mere omission.

(5) I maintain that even representationally distorted core assumptions can perform explanatory work if they operate as counterfactual benchmarks that make structural dependencies visible, rather than functioning as mere instrumental fictions.

Please work through these commitments pair by pair. For each pair, say whether the tension is genuine or only apparent, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each indicate the least costly revision that restores consistency together with what it costs me. Then identify which single commitment, if refined or abandoned, would dissolve the most conflicts at once. Finally, assess whether my view is salvageable as a unified position or whether I am running two incompatible philosophies of science—an instrumentalism about prediction and a mechanistic realism about explanation—without noticing.
