Write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines following the standard rotating end-word scheme, followed by a three-line envoi—on the philosophical problem of defining life. Fix the six end-words exactly as: boundary, fire, virus, code, crystal, lineage. In the first stanza, present the intuitive cluster of features usually taken to mark living things: a self-maintaining boundary, metabolic consumption, hereditary code, reproductive growth, evolutionary lineage, and adaptive change. Over the subsequent stanzas, let the rotation of end-words drive a conceptual dismantling: let "fire" press against metabolism (it consumes fuel and self-sustains but inherits nothing and evolves not at all), "crystal" press against reproduction (it replicates its structure without mutation, selection, or internal maintenance), "virus" press against both code and boundary (it carries genetic instructions but lacks autonomous metabolism and borrows the host's membrane machinery), and "lineage" ultimately fail to unify the cluster, since some entities possess one feature while lacking another in ways that split the concept rather than complete it. The form itself should do philosophical work: the sestina's compulsive return to the same six terms should mirror the discipline's repeated return to the same definitional vocabulary—"boundary," "code," "lineage"—while the shifting line-positions should show that each term changes its conceptual load depending on which borderline case is pressing it. The envoi must gather all six words in a final order that reads like an attempted synthesis but feels strained or hollow, as though the poem's formal demand for closure conflicts with the argument's refusal of a single necessary and sufficient condition. Write with enough precision that a reader familiar with the virus debate and the metabolism-versus-heredity split will recognize the moves, but do not cite specific researchers, papers, experiments, or texts by name, and include no footnotes, bibliography, or quoted sources. The poem should stand or fall on its conceptual architecture and its formal fidelity to the sestina.
