Please describe the most important implications of John von Neumann's 1955 Fortune essay "Can We Survive Technology?"—specifically his thesis that accelerating technological change risks outpacing the organizational and political structures needed to contain it—for contemporary debates about governing high-risk autonomous technologies. Focus on whether his diagnosis implies that conventional democratic deliberation is structurally too slow for the pace of innovation, or whether it instead demands adaptive institutional designs that he did not specify. Give serious weight to the objection that framing technological growth as approaching a singular point is a misleading metaphor that obscures the incremental, socially embedded character of innovation, and clarify what follows for long-term governance if that objection is correct.
