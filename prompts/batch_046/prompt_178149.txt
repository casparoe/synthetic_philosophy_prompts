I am preparing a problem-set for my graduate seminar on Imre Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes. One exercise asks students to extrapolate the implicit decision rule from a colleague's applied judgments and then apply it to a novel case.

Over the past decade my colleague, Dr. A. R. Feldman, has consistently evaluated research programmes according to Lakatosian criteria. Her record includes the following verdicts: she judges the statistical-mechanical reduction of classical thermodynamics progressive, citing its prediction of Brownian motion and its resolution of fluctuation anomalies without introducing independently untestable auxiliaries; she judges the late-Ptolemaic programme degenerating, because its successive addition of equants and minor epicycles accommodated planetary anomalies solely through geometric adjustments that yielded no novel predictions; she judges the phlogiston programme's postulation of negative weight after Lavoisier's oxidation findings degenerating, on the grounds that the adjustment was manifestly ad hoc and empirically barren; she judges Einstein's general-relativistic programme progressive, since it predicted gravitational lensing and the anomalous perihelion advance of Mercury within a unified field-theoretic framework; and she judges the behavioural-economics programme that replaced rational-choice axioms with prospect-theoretic value and weighting functions progressive, because it predicted framing and endowment effects that rational-choice models could accommodate only by revising preference orderings post hoc.

The new case is the phylogenetic-tool programme in cognitive archaeology, which applies computational cladistics to prehistoric lithic assemblages in order to reconstruct evolutionary trees of technical cognition. Its proponents maintain that branching divergence patterns reveal discrete cognitive leaps rather than gradual cultural diffusion. The programme recently introduced a free cultural-drift parameter that permits divergence dates to be revised post hoc whenever new stratigraphic evidence conflicts with the reconstructed tree, but it also predicted—prior to excavation—the presence of an intermediate bifacial form in the Levantine corridor, which was subsequently recovered.

Your task has four parts. First, identify at least two distinct interpretations of the progressive-versus-degenerating distinction—each consistent with Dr. Feldman's five verdicts—that yield opposite classifications for the phylogenetic-tool programme. Second, for each interpretation, state the predicted verdict and justify it with reference to the programme's cultural-drift parameter and its Levantine prediction. Third, offer your best-guess prediction of Dr. Feldman's actual verdict, explaining which interpretive commitments (for instance, the priority of empirical novelty, theoretical coherence, or the prohibition on ad hocness) she is most likely to privilege and why. Fourth, describe one further concrete hypothetical case—specifying its predictive successes and its auxiliary adjustments—that would discriminate between the interpretations you have outlined.
