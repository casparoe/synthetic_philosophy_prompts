I serve on the technical review board of the National Long-Horizon Assessment Office, where every quarter we evaluate predictive models in demography, agronomy, and resource economics that claim validity over fifty- to two-hundred-year scales. Lately we have been inundated with submissions that invoke long-delay mechanisms — for instance, arguments that population pressure will eventually outstrip agricultural productivity, or that mineral stocks will reach critical depletion — only to face the objection that the predicted crisis has not materialized on schedule. The conceptual problem, which my colleagues and I debated over coffee last Tuesday and which I now need to formalize, is how to distinguish three possibilities in a disciplined way: that the theory has been genuinely falsified; that it has been falsified but legitimately rescued by independently testable auxiliary adjustments; or that it is "right but early" — correct about an underlying mechanism but displaced in time, masked by temporary conditions, or operating outside the originally specified boundary conditions. I need a reusable decision procedure — essentially a checklist or flowchart rendered in prose — that my panel can run in a single three-hour session without requiring specialized philosophical training. The procedure should specify the ordered questions to ask about any submitted long-run theory, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the exact stopping points where the answer is a firm rejection or a firm rescue, and the precise nodes where judgment cannot be eliminated and the decision must be handed to a person because the issue is one of values or interpretive weighting rather than fact. Please apply the procedure to two concrete submissions we are currently reviewing: first, a Malthusian-style argument that exponential population growth in a particular region will eventually overtake linear or even exponential agricultural gains, which appears contradicted by two centuries of sustained yield improvements; second, a 1972-style systems model forecasting critical depletion of several non-renewable mineral stocks by the year 2020, which did not occur as predicted. One of these applications should yield a verdict that a defender of the theory would strongly dislike, and you should flag exactly where in the sequence that unwelcome result is forced by the logic of the procedure rather than by a discretionary call. The deliverable must be written in American English, must not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and must be usable by reviewers who have no access to external literature during the session.
