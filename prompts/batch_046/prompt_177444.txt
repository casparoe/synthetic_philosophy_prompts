I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of cognitive science, working on embedded agency and the enactive tradition, and I'm preparing a short contribution for an interdisciplinary workshop on self-modeling in artificial and biological systems. Rather than ending with a standard summary, I'd like to close with a poem that performs conceptual work: I want the form itself to enact the problem of being an agent that is necessarily inside the world it tries to model.

Please write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines, followed by a three-line envoi—using exactly these end-words in rotation: boundary, substrate, error, model, intervention, world. The repetition and permutation should structurally mirror the cyclical, never-complete process by which an embedded agent maintains its own ontological boundaries, not as a pre-given perimeter but as a provisional, costly settlement between what it can compute and what it must ignore in order to stay causally coupled to its substrate. In particular, I want you to take seriously the idea that any model complex enough to include the agent introduces an ontological debt—the act of representation necessarily modifies the very substrate on which the agent depends—without resolving this tension into either triumph or despair. The envoi should gather all six end-words into a final configuration that makes clear the agent is not outside looking in, but inside looking out through a boundary that is itself part of the world it models.

The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with embedded agency—whether from the alignment literature on Cartesian framing, from predictive processing, or from the philosophical problem of self-reference in embodied cognition—recognizes the conceptual moves rather than seeing keywords dropped in. At the same time, it should work as poetry for a reader who hasn't encountered the literature: the rhythm, image, and formal constraint should carry the idea on their own. Please avoid generic metaphors of machines or brains as containers; instead, let the sestina's returning lines do the work of showing how embedded reasoning is always circling back to the same terms in new configurations, never arriving at a view from nowhere.
