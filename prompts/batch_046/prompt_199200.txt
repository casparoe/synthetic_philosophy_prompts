In my poli-sci reading group people keep comparing democratic backsliding to a car with slowly failing brakes: you don't notice until you're already going too fast to stop. They use this to argue that outsiders -- EU monitors, academics, whatever -- should be able to declare backsliding in places like Hungary or Poland even before anyone steals an election, because the brakes are already gone. How far does the brakes analogy actualy hold? Give me the correspondence table -- what's the driver, the brakes, the hill, the crash, and who gets to be the mechanic -- then the places it breaks, especially about whether the people inside the car can just say the brakes feel fine and who gets to overrule them. And tell me whether the conclusion -- that external observers can legitimately call it backsliding over domestic objections -- survives once the disanologies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the whole normative load all along.
