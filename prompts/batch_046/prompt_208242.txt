hey, i'm a phd student in philosphy of mind and cog sci, sitting on our uni's Emerging Tech Review Board. we just recieved a pilot progam proposal from the robotics lab: they want to deploy an embodied agent called Mira in the intro philosophy course to discuss qualitative experience. Mira learns color terms and pain-descriptions through direct haptic and thermal sensors, not text. the lab says this solves the symbol grounding problem — the representations are causally coupled to physical states, so they're not just syntax.

the philosophy dept pushed back. they argue causal coupling isn't enough without an 'asymmetric revisability loop': the system's symbol-to-world mapping has to be revisable by the system itself when conditions change, not updated by an external programmer. without internal transparency to its own error states, Mira's just fancy correlation with sensors bolted on.

the board is split. half think embodied sensorimotor coupling counts as genuine grounding; half think you need that internal self-correcting loop. i need a formal memo recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with criteria reviewers can actually apply, an honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side, and a clear bottom line we can vote on friday. please don't include citations or a bibliography; write it as something the board can act on without running to the library. and don't just recycle the standard syntax-vs-semantics objections — engage specifically with whether correlation plus external update can ever amount to grounding, or if the revisability condition is genuinely necessary.
