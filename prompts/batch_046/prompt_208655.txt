Please consider the following judgments, all rendered by a scholar applying Hannah Arendt’s framework on political deception. In each case the scholar asks whether a particular lie corrupts the shared factual world that democratic politics requires.

Case 1: A state conceals its weapons capabilities from an adversary nation. Verdict: permissible; this is conventional statecraft directed outward and leaves the domestic realm of fact intact.

Case 2: A revolutionary movement conceals its organizational structure and strategic intentions from an authoritarian occupying regime. Verdict: permissible; this protects political action without deceiving those to whom the movement owes accountability.

Case 3: Bureaucrats systematically fabricate statistics about a war’s progress, gradually come to believe their own reports, and use these reports to guide state policy, in the pattern of the Pentagon Papers deception. Verdict: impermissible; this replaces shared reality with a self-generated fiction and destroys the mental space for judgment.

Case 4: A democratic administration, facing severe economic instability, presents a simplified predictive model as established scientific certainty; officials come to believe their own simplification, suppress internal dissent, and embed the model’s outputs in binding emergency legislation. Verdict: impermissible.

Now consider a new case. After a democratic revolution, the new government conceals the true severity of an economic collapse from the citizenry—who are now the governed—to prevent panic and maintain support for restructuring. The leaders maintain complete internal awareness of the true figures; the concealment is explicitly temporary and strategic; yet the false narrative of stability is embedded in budget allocations and emergency legislation.

Your task has three parts. First, identify at least two distinct principles that could consistently explain the four verdicts above, and say what each predicts about this new case. Second, give your best recommendation for what the scholar should conclude, identifying the closest analog among Cases 1–4 and explaining whether consistency requires adopting a new principle. Third, propose one further hypothetical case that would decisively distinguish the two leading principles you have identified.

Please structure your response as follows: "The Track Record" (brief summary of the four cases, ~80 words); "Candidate Rules" (the competing principles and their predictions, ~160 words); "The New Case and Verdict" (your recommendation with reasoning, ~160 words); "The Closest Analog" (explicit comparison to one prior case, ~60 words); "The Decisive Test" (the further distinguishing case, ~80 words); "Adopted Principle" (an honest statement of what principle consistency forces either way, ~60 words); and "Confidence and Reservations" (your confidence in the central claim and the specific point about which you are least certain—whether it concerns Arendt’s actual view, the consistency of the track record, or the boundary of one of your rules, ~70 words).

Please state how confident you are in your central recommendation and single out the point about which you are least sure.
