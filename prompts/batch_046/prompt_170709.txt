As a retired evolutionary biologist who has only recently begun studying philosophy of medicine, I find myself caught in a disagreement at my retirement community that feels deeper than it first appears. Our local clinic has adopted a predictive health protocol that flags residents as being in a pre-pathological state whenever their inflammatory biomarkers fall outside the range established for sedentary urban populations in their seventies. I happen to be one of the flagged cases: my C-reactive protein and interleukin levels run well above those norms, yet I live at 2,500 meters, hike daily, and have never felt more vigorous; my physician from my working years always interpreted these values as appropriate for my altitude and activity level. The clinic director insists that health is an objective biological condition—there is a correct statistical range for human physiology, and deviation from it is pathology regardless of environment or personal goals. A close friend, a retired clinical psychologist, counters that labeling me pre-pathological makes no sense unless we first ask what I want and need my body to do; since I achieve every vital goal I set, she says the label is a value-laden judgment wearing the mask of a measurement. I am inclined to think both perspectives capture something real, but I cannot see how to reconcile them without either reducing medicine to mere preference or treating every human being as if they should match a single statistical template. Is there a defensible view on which my profile can be genuinely unhealthy for me—not merely statistically unusual—without secretly importing an ideal of how human life ought to be lived? Or does the very idea of disease necessarily involve such norms, in which case medicine can never be purely descriptive? I am not asking for medical advice about whether to take the prescribed anti-inflammatory regimen; I am asking whether my situation exposes an unavoidable philosophical problem in defining health itself, and if so, how one might navigate it without collapsing into either biological determinism or pure relativism.
