You sit on the City Health Equity Allocation Board, and next Thursday you must vote on a one-time $4 million public-health endowment. Staff have narrowed the choice to two options. Option A directs intensive mobile-clinic resources to District 7, the worst-off area; it produces large absolute health gains there but leaves affluent districts far ahead in access and outcomes. Option B imposes a citywide cap on elective specialist appointments, which compresses the health-access gap by reducing top-end availability; it produces no absolute gain in District 7 and, because of system strain, slightly lowers baseline outcomes there. The board’s charter requires both “priority to the worst off” and “elimination of unjust health disparities.”

Draft the short formal opinion (around 600 words) you will circulate before the vote. Recommend one option with a concrete course of action and a time frame (e.g., approval this quarter with a phased rollout). Your reasoning should treat the philosophical tension honestly: explain why a prioritarian framework—weighting benefits more heavily the worse off the recipient is—favors Option A, but then engage seriously the strongest consideration on the other side. In particular, address the relational-equality argument that allowing large, visible gaps above a sufficiency threshold can express a failure of equal democratic standing and social solidarity, even if the worst off are aided as much as possible. Evaluate whether that objection genuinely commits one to leveling-down (making the better off worse for equality’s sake alone) or whether it identifies a structurally different harm—status injury or institutional disrespect—that Option B accidentally addresses but does not remedy. Be precise about the distinction between deontic leveling-down and the incidental compression Option B produces, and consider whether the board’s charter is conceptually incoherent in demanding both priorities simultaneously; if so, recommend how to amend the framing rather than forcing a false synthesis.

If this request rests on a confused premise—if prioritarianism and relational equality are not in the tension I have described, or if the recommendation genuinely cannot be made without empirical data you do not have—say so plainly instead of delivering a polished but hollow opinion.
