I am writing this for my final project in a graduate seminar on the governance of emerging technologies, where we have been examining whether competitive market dynamics in AI development necessarily erode safety investments — the problem often framed as the alignment tax being unsustainable under competitive pressure. Our discussions have reached an impasse between two broad orientations: one that treats competitiveness as structurally incompatible with bearing that tax, and one that treats the tax as a coordination failure that competitive dynamics can, under the right informational conditions, actually help solve. Rather than asking for an adjudication, I want to construct the joint statement that these two camps — which I will call the Structural Incompatibility camp and the Conditional Coordination camp — could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

To keep the exercise from collapsing into well-worn race-to-the-bottom versus invisible-hand scripts, I want us to inject and center on a more granular analytical distinction: the separation of competitive pressure along the axis of deployment velocity (the race to release and capture market share) from competitive pressure along the axis of capability ceiling (the race to reach a theoretical performance frontier, regardless of release timing). The collaboration should treat these as genuinely distinct competitive mechanisms and examine whether the alignment tax behaves differently across them. I want the deliverable formatted as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.

In the agreed-claims section, please include shared definitions of the alignment tax and of both competitive axes; shared acknowledgment that safety investments often carry private costs with diffuse public benefits; agreement on at least one concrete, hypothetical or historical scenario in which competitive dynamics demonstrably affected a safety-related development choice; and agreement on what empirical or formal result would count as resolving the dispute. In the disagreements table, please state each camp's precise disagreement in wording that camp would accept: specifically, the Structural camp should maintain that under deployment-velocity competition, safety investments are systematically underprovided regardless of governance structures due to private-cost and public-benefit asymmetry, while the Conditional camp should maintain that this underprovision is contingent on informational opacity about safety quality rather than on competition per se, and that under capability-ceiling competition, safety investments can be privately rewarded through robustness advantages. Then list the cruxes — at minimum: whether safety quality is sufficiently observable to be priced into competitive dynamics; whether the alignment tax remains constant or scales non-linearly with system capabilities; and whether analogies from high-stakes engineering domains such as aviation, nuclear power, or pharmaceutical testing show competitive erosion of safety margins or competitive investment in them. For each crux, specify the case, argument, or thought experiment both sides agree is relevant, and record each side's advance prediction about the outcome. Please do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; I want this constructible without external lookup, and avoid framing the task as asking which camp is correct — the goal is the exact formulation of shared ground together with precisely located residual disagreement.
