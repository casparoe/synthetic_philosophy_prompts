In philosophy of physics discussions of backward causation, the argument over the grandfather paradox has reached a peculiar stalemate: defenders of global self-consistency conditions and defenders of branching multiverse models both accept that general relativity permits closed timelike curves, both agree that logical contradiction must be avoided, and both can accommodate the traveler's failed attempt to kill his grandfather—yet neither camp has revised its core claim in years. Is this impasse merely verbal, with "paradox" meaning a logical impossibility for one side and a nomological constraint for the other? Or is there a hidden formal disagreement about whether consistency conditions apply diachronically across the loop or synchronically to each temporal slice of the traveler's mental state—what we might call modal anchoring of the attempt? If the stall turns on that distinction, what kind of result—an explicit formalization of the loop's boundary conditions, a clarification of the modal base in which the attempt is evaluated, or a change in the framing question—would actually move the debate? And if it is instead bedrock intuition about whether causation can be circular, is progress even possible?
