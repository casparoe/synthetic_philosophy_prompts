ok so in boundary-problem debates people constantly reach for the club analogy — a demos is like a private club that sets its own membership rules, so complaining about exclusion is like asking why the chess club wont let you vote on bylaws. Meanwhile the all-subjected crowd says politics is less like a club and more like being on a cruise ship you cant leave: everyone affected by the captains decisions should have a say, membership isnt voluntary. Audit both analogies: map the correspondences (rules, exit, coercion, scope), say where each cracks, what the club analogy sneaks in about consent and exit options that real states dont have, what the ship analogy sneaks in about the impossibility of democratic closure, and whether either can actually do the work its being asked to do — license exclusion, justify inclusion, or just rename the problem. If you think there's a less misleading analogy that respects the need for closure without treating citizenship like an audition, sketch it — maybe something like a co-op apartment where you bought in but the rules can still evict you, but be honest about where that breaks too.
