Act as a philosopher of action with deep expertise in free will and moral responsibility. Before answering, take a moment to map the structural features of the debate: identify what each side treats as data, what they reject as illegitimate, and whether they are genuinely answering the same question.

The Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the principle of alternative possibilities have been refined for over half a century—flickers of freedom, hierarchical models of control, prior-sign cases, and modified controllers—yet the core dispute remains essentially unchanged. Many participants now talk past one another: one camp treats the absence of a robust alternative as decisive against PAP, while the other insists that any residual “flicker” restores the very possibility the principle demands. My working hypothesis is that the stalemate is not primarily about moral responsibility itself, but about a hidden disagreement over the modal basis of “could have done otherwise”—specifically, whether the relevant modality is metaphysical (causal openness in the actual sequence), normative (what falls within the agent’s practical authority), or logical (consistency with the agent’s mental states)—and that neither side has made this framing explicit, so they evaluate the same cases with incompatible criteria of possibility.

Is that diagnosis correct, or is there a substantive formal or empirical disagreement disguised beneath the surface? Give me a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of interpretations: is the disagreement verbal, about standards of evidence, about which thought experiments count as legitimate data, or about starting intuitions that neither side can independently argue for? Are the sides answering different questions under the single name “moral responsibility”? Is a hidden empirical or formal question—perhaps about the logic of counterfactual intervention, or about how neuroscientific constraints bear on agency—actually doing the work? Finally, be honest about progress: could a new conceptual distinction, a formal result in the logic of counterfactual control, or a precisely constructed case actually end the impasse? If so, specify exactly what kind of move it would be and which side it would favor. If not, explain why no such move should be expected and what that implies about the status of the question.
