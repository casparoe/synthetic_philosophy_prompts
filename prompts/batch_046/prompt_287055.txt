Quick question for anyone who knows philosophy of history jargon. There's this pattern I keep noticing in retrospective explainations of tech standards — like why one protocol won over others. After the fact people draw a clean line: the winner had property X, the market needed X, so of course it took over. But at the time X was actually seen as a serious drawback, and two rival protocols had X plus extra safeguards. The story doesn't just ignore the dead ends; it flips the valence of X so it looks like an obvious virtue all along, then uses that flipped valence as the causal link in the chain. I know hindsight bias and the narrative fallacy cover parts of this, but is there a specific term for this retroactive valence-flip inside an otherwise coherent causal story? If not, is it just narrative fallacy doing overtime, or should I treat it as a distinct failure mode with it's own mechanics?
