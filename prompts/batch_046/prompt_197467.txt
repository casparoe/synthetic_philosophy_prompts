There's this anology everyone wheels out in the mind-uploading seminars — gradual replacement is just the Ship of Theseus with neurons instead of planks. You swap out biological tissue for synthetic analogues slowly, over years, and people say 'see, personal identity is preserved, there's no sharp cutoff where you stop being you'. They use it to relax worries about uploading. But I'm sceptical that the comparison is carrying all the weight, especially once you get into the weeds of what actually gets replaced. So: lay out the correspondance table — planks, shipyard, sailors, the sea — mapped onto neurons, the replacement programme, consciousness, the body? Then tell me exactly where it fractures. In particular, what happens if the programme preserves synaptic weight matrices but deliberately discards the glial scafolding and the micro-vascular rhythms that modulate local metabolism? Does the 'you're still you' conclusion survive, or was the analogy just smuggling in a hidden assumption about material continuity? And if the Theseus comparison collapses, what's a less misleading anology — maybe software migration, or something else? I want the mapping, the breaks that matter for identity, and whether the relaxed conclusion was riding on the comparison the whole time.
