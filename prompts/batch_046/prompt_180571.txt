I sit on a municipal review board evaluating a proposal to deploy an experimental psychiatric intervention citywide. The treatment eliminates the risk of severe, chronic mental anguish for roughly 99.99% of at-risk patients, but in about 0.01% of cases it triggers an irreversible neurological failure mode: the patient remains fully conscious yet trapped in continuous, maximally intense psychological pain that can persist for decades, with no known cure. Proponents argue that the sheer number of people spared from prolonged suffering outweighs the few catastrophic outcomes; opponents claim that generating even one instance of this inescapable extreme suffering state is categorically worse than allowing a larger number of people to experience conventional depression. I want to know whether there is a principled moral framework for weighing these outcomes, rather than just comparing probabilities. Please advise me on how to structure this decision, state how confident you are in your reasoning, and identify the specific claim about which you are least certain.
