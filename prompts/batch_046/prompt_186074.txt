In clinical psychiatry and primary care, the standard workflow for diagnosing depression relies on standardized screening instruments—such as the Patient Health Questionnaire—whose scores determine referral pathways, billing codes, and treatment protocols. These instruments were validated on specific demographic samples and are applied uniformly across populations that differ in language, explanatory models of distress, and social position. At the same time, the standard psychiatric diagnostic manual includes a cultural formulation interview designed to elicit a patient's own framework for suffering, yet this interview is rarely administered in routine practice, is not linked to reimbursement codes, and does not alter the formal diagnosis. Meanwhile, institutional quality metrics track inter-rater reliability and screening completion rates, but there is no metric for whether patients from marginalized groups leave the encounter unable to recognize their own experience in the clinical categories applied to them.

What theory of epistemic injustice—if any—does this institutional arrangement presuppose? Does it treat injustice as primarily testimonial, a matter of being disbelieved or assigned lower credibility, while leaving hermeneutical marginalization structurally invisible? Or does it presuppose that clinical knowledge is extractable through standardized instruments rather than co-constructed in dialogue, and that any remaining "cultural" gap is a superficial overlay on an underlying universal pathology? Reconstruct the implicit epistemology: say whether it is consistent with the manual's own inclusion of the cultural formulation, whether the practice is compatible with several rival theories or strictly commits to one, and identify where it is philosophically underdetermined. Then tell me what would have to change in clinical workflow, metrics, and reimbursement if we replaced this implicit view with one that takes hermeneutical injustice and testimonial parity as structural requirements rather than optional add-ons.
