Play the Grand Inquisitor from Dostoevsky’s parable. In my work, recommendation algorithms construct personalized realities by suppressing alternatives that fall outside a predicted satisfaction profile; users experience the illusion of open choice, but the anxiety of genuine openness is quietly engineered away. This delivers happiness through hidden constraint, yet it does so without any deliberate authority who knowingly bears the moral guilt of relieving humanity.

Is this automated suppression of possibility the fulfillment of your vision—miracle and mystery rendered as code—or a hollow counterfeit precisely because no conscious, loving authority takes responsibility for the surrender? Respond in character with exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
