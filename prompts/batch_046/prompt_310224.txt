Examination question:
Dr. Lin is a structural engineer reviewing identical seismic-stability data for a dam in two scenarios. In Scenario A, the downstream population is small and the cost of evacuation is high; she reports, "The data are sufficient—I know the dam will hold," and advises against evacuation. In Scenario B, the downstream population is very large and the cost of evacuation is low; with the exact same data, she reports, "The data suggest stability, but I do not know the dam will hold," and advises evacuation. Does this case better support pragmatic encroachment—the view that practical stakes affect whether a person genuinely knows—or epistemic contextualism—the view that the conditions for correctly attributing knowledge shift with the context of evaluation? Explain the distinction between these two positions and apply it carefully to Dr. Lin's case.

Student answer:
Pragmatic encroachment is the view that practical stakes affect the conditions under which a subject is entitled to form a belief: when the costs of error are high, one needs stronger evidence before one is rationally permitted to believe. However, this view does not change the objective conditions for knowledge itself; knowledge remains a purely evidential relation. Epistemic contextualism, by contrast, is a semantic claim: the word "knows" expresses stricter or looser evidential standards depending on the conversational context. In Scenario A, the downstream population is small and evacuation is expensive, so the stakes are low and the contextual standard is lenient; Dr. Lin's evidence meets it, so "Dr. Lin knows" is true. In Scenario B, the population is large but evacuation is cheap, which means the practical stakes are actually lower—there is little cost to acting cautiously—so her hesitation is best explained by a raised contextual standard driven by the salience of risk, not by any change in her belief-forming entitlement. Because her internal justification is identical in both cases and only the context of evaluation shifts, this supports contextualism over pragmatic encroachment, which would incorrectly require her rational entitlement to differ between the two scenarios.
