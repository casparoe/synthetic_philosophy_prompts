I'm planning a ninety-minute session for an interdisciplinary reading group — graduate students in literature and visual art with no formal training in philosophy of mind or ethology — on sphexishness as a problem for free will: specifically, the tension between mechanically rigid behavior loops and the flexible responsiveness we take to indicate agency. I want to move past the familiar digger-wasp burrow anecdote they have likely encountered in popular science writing. Plan the session's explanatory arc: first, the dependency order of ideas — what conception of a closed behavior loop versus an open branch must be secured before the free-will question can even be formulated, and why the distinction between deterministic causation and procedural rigidity needs to precede any discussion of consciousness; second, the carrying example — design or select a concrete scenario that demonstrates a sequence appearing purpose-driven from the inside yet collapsing under a single environmental perturbation, and specify exactly what this example must contain (a visible goal-state, an interruptible sub-routine, and a failure mode that reveals absence of re-planning) and what it must avoid (anthropomorphic framing, hidden-variable explanations, or direct analogy to human habit); third, the misconception map — they will likely arrive with both "rigidity proves determinism everywhere" and "any adaptive response is freedom"; identify the precise point in the sequence where each should be intercepted and how; fourth, a single check-question or brief exercise at the end that distinguishes grasping the structural distinction from merely feeling sympathy for an animal; and fifth, what should be explicitly left unsettled — for example, whether genuine flexibility requires consciousness, or whether sphex-like loops appear in human cognition — with a clear statement that these will not be resolved. To prevent the session from being pure repetition of a well-known anecdote, build the core comparison around a constructed case: place the classic wasp loop beside a modern automated system equipped with exactly one error-handling branch, and guide the group to locate precisely where flexibility ends and mere conditional branching begins.
