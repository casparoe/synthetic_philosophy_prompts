I am defending a thesis chapter next month at an interdisciplinary workshop on AI governance and formal ethics, and I want to walk in prepared for the Q&A rather than be ambushed by it. The audience is split between alignment researchers who have worked on reward misspecification and standard impact-measure formalisms, philosophers of technology who are skeptical that normative harm can be captured by any formal constraint without smuggling in value judgments, and at least one decision theorist who tends to press on whether outcome-independence is even coherent. My position is that "avoiding side effects" can be defined for a low-impact agent without a full axiological specification by appealing to structural reversibility rather than state-ranking: I propose bounding the system's "counterfactual footprint," defined as the set of environmental variables that the action renders inaccessible through previously available low-cost interventions, irrespective of whether those variables correspond to good or bad outcomes. The idea is that side-effect-avoidance constrains causal topology—specifically, it prohibits collapsing reachable state-spaces—rather than protecting particular values. I was discussing this with a colleague who noted that my framework might render all meaningful action impermissible, since any causal intervention technically eliminates some counterfactual branch; that is exactly the objection I am secretly hoping nobody raises, because it threatens to show that the reversibility criterion can only be calibrated through arbitrary line-drawing. Please draft my prep sheet: the objections ordered by how likely they are and how much damage each could do, phrased in the vocabulary that each faction—alignment engineer, philosopher, decision theorist—would actually use, with for each what I should concede up front, what I must hold firm on, and where the reply is weakest. Identify the weakest point in my own argument that I should defend actively rather than hope they miss, and flag the "all-action-is-irreversible" challenge as the one I dread most.
