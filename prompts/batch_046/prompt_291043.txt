hey, long time lurker on rationalist forums, currently trying to make sense of some consciousness literature and keep running into a move I can't quite name.

Situation: a study asks people to report on their own visual experience. Subject says "I clearly saw the red dot move left then right." The experimenter knows the display was actually ambiguous, but instead of throwing out the report or treating it as a lie, they apply what I'd call a translation layer -- they interpret the report as the subject's implicit theory about their own vision, correct for known biases (like the tendency to impose linear motion on flickering stimuli), and extract a calibrated signal about what's actually in consciousness. They don't take the narrative literally, but they don't dismiss it either; they just apply a kind of hermeneutic discount and use what's left.

Same pattern with decision reports. Someone explains "I picked option B because the risk-reward ratio was better," but the actual computation was associative and fast. The researcher treats the explanation not as a direct readout of the reasoning process, but as a post-hoc reconstruction that still carries information about the outcome, just filtered through the subject's folk-psychological model. They don't recieve it as transparent, yet they don't discard it.

What I'm after is whether there's an established term for this specific methodological stance -- treating introspective reports as noisy, theory-laden data that needs calibrated interpretation rather than literal acceptance or outright rejection. I know heterophenomenology is in the neighborhood, but as I understand it Dennett's version treats first-person reports as texts describing a heterophenomenological world, not as calibrated measurements of real mental states that still need a discount factor. And it's not just generic skepticism about introspection, because these researchers are actively using the reports, just with correction applied.

If there's no exact name, I'd want to distinguish this from plain confabulation on the subject's end (where people fill gaps with made-up stories) and from standard measurement error. The specific thing on the subject side is that they don't just misremember; they construct a coherent causal narrative that has the right macro-structure but wrong micro-mechanics -- like describing a Bayesian update as if it were syllogistic reasoning. Is that part of the same phenomenon, or a separate failure mode?

Also, if this calibrated-interpretation stance does have a name, where does it collapse? At what point does applying too much correction just mean you're projecting your own theory onto the subject, and what concept marks that boundary? I'm not looking for a bare label -- I want to know exactly what the carving is, what it excludes, and whether I'm describing one concept or two.
