Quick thing for tomorrow's seminar — I keep seeing this slide in secnodary lit on Wittgenstein's religious belief lectures, and I want to check if I'm misreading him or importing a false presupposition.

The usual reading: religious belief isn't a hypothesis (Meinung) but part of a form of life, governed by its own grammar. Belief in the Last Judgment isn't like a historical belief; it's adopting a picture (Bild) that structures living and carries real existential weight. From that people jump to saying religious language is purely expressive — zero propositional content, zero internal standards of correction.

But Wittgenstein clearly treats superstition as defective — fear-driven and borrowing empirical grammar where it doesn't belong, not just a different language-game. If religious belief is genuinely non-cognitive, on what grounds can he rank it lower? The hidden move seems to be treating 'not empirical' as 'not rational at all.'

Is that the false presupposition I'm smuggling in? And if so, what exactly does Wittgenstein allow by way of internal criticism inside a religious practice — correcting grammatical failures within the form of life, not importing scientific reasoning or committing category mistakes? I want my students to see the distinction clearly.
