I write about science and ideas for a general-interest publication, and I am preparing a twenty-minute public explanation for educated non-specialists on how archaeologists reconstruct institutional beliefs—such as shared norms of reciprocity or hierarchy—from material remains that carry no explicit linguistic content. The conceptual difficulty is that inference from object to institution is underdetermined: the same material pattern can be produced by incompatible social structures. Please plan the explanation: the sequence in which to introduce the dependency between physical traces and institutional meaning; the single concrete scenario that should carry the idea (for example, a burial assemblage in which grave goods might indicate either social rank or ritual obligation); the misconceptions the audience is likely to bring—particularly that artefacts "speak for themselves" or that the absence of writing precludes complex belief; the precise point at which to check that they grasp the interpretive gap between material pattern and social institution; and what specialist debates to omit at this level. I want the focus to remain on the philosophical problem of inference, not on archaeological method per se.
