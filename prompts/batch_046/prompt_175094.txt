I have been thinking, without any formal training, about whether it is ever right to believe something when the proof is not fully there. I understand that William James argued that in unavoidable, high-stakes choices where we must decide one way or the other, our passions and practical needs can legitimately shape what we believe, while W.K. Clifford held that accepting any claim without adequate evidence is always a kind of moral wrong, no matter who does it or what is at stake. Write an essay weighing these two positions. Reach and defend a definite conclusion — do not end by saying both have merit and leaving the choice open. In particular, ground your argument in a specific situation: the decision to maintain belief in a close partner's underlying goodwill despite repeated ambiguous actions that could equally suggest indifference or hidden malice, where abandoning the belief would dissolve the relationship but holding it requires stepping beyond the available evidence. Keep your response to one cohesive essay of no more than 400 words.
