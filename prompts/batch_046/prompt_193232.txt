I'm trying to locate the fracture in a colleague's argument about Newton's bucket and spacetime relationism, which appears to collapse into contradiction. The reasoning runs as follows: (1) Relationism entails that every physical difference must be anchored in a difference in actual spatiotemporal relations among material bodies. (2) In Newton's bucket, the concavity of the water surface differs between rotation relative to distant matter and rest relative to distant matter, despite unchanged internal relations between water and bucket; hence there is an absolute rotational fact not reducible to inter-body relations, and relationism is false. (3) Apply the same template to linear motion: take two rockets with identical internal configurations, one undergoing uniform proper acceleration relative to the cosmic distribution of matter and one coasting inertially. Their internal relations are identical, but one experiences measurable G-forces. (4) By the reasoning in (1) and (2), this should establish an absolute linear-accelerative fact independent of relations to other bodies. (5) Yet special relativity treats uniform linear velocity as purely relative and denies any absolute linear state of motion; only proper acceleration is locally detectable, and even that is frame-relative in general-relativistic formulations. (6) So the argument establishes absolute rotation but, by parity of reasoning, should also establish an absolute linear dynamics that physics explicitly rejects. (7) Therefore the chain proves too much. I want to preserve both the physical reality of rotational effects and the relativistic denial of absolute linear velocity, which means I must reject one of the premises linking (2) to (4). Which step fails—whether it involves an implicit background assumption about holding cosmic matter fixed, a scope shift between local dynamical properties and global relational structures, or something else—and what does giving it up cost for either the relationist or the substantivalist? If this framing rests on a confused premise, for instance by treating Machian relationism as compatible with holding distant bodies fixed in a counterfactual scenario, please say so plainly rather than repairing the argument for me.
