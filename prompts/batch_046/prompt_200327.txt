Me and my roommate got in a long argument last night about Al-Ghazali's occassionalism and whether it actually breaks science or not. (1) She said Ghazali's whole point is that fire doesn't burn cotton — God directly makes the burning happen every single time — so there are no real causal powers in nature, just divine habbit. That means science can never discover actual laws, only correlations that God happens to keep steady. (2) I said that's unfair because Ghazali explicitly allows for God's custom (adat) that almost never breaks, so scientists study that regular pattern; not having metaphysical necessity doesn't stop you from predicting stuff. (3) Then she said that's just a cop-out. If oxidation doesn't actually do anything and God is the one producing the burn, then saying the fire burned the cotton because of oxidation is literally false. So either occasionalism makes all mechanistic science wrong, or you've secretly smuggled real secondary causes back in. (4) I tried to save it by saying the oxidation can still be the occasion (sabab) for God's action — science tracks the occasions, not fake causes. But she hit back: if God is doing all the causal work every time, what difference does the occasion make? It feels like you're just putting a fancy label on real causation and pretending you've denied it. Please adjudicate this move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly wrong to clearly right, briefly justify each rating, say who is more right overall, and point out anything important both of us are missing.
