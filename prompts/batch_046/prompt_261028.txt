My metaphysics professor has rendered implicit verdicts on whether the entities described below survive the transformations in question. She judges that a wooden sculpture which is fully disassembled, its material destroyed, and then reconstructed in identical shape from polymer composites in the same gallery is not numerically the same enduring object; that a river system which loses its original source, acquires new tributaries, and replaces all its water over a century is numerically the same enduring object; that a human being whose neurons are replaced one by one with functionally identical synthetic substrates within the same skull, with conscious experience remaining continuous, is the same enduring person; that a legal corporation which retains its registration number but simultaneously replaces its charter, every board member, and its jurisdiction is not the same enduring entity; and that a digital musical work originally stored on one drive and later instantiated on different hardware with different physical encodings—preserving only the abstract bit-structure and the playback conventions—is the same enduring object.

The new case is this. A synthetic ecological system is engineered across three geographically separate bioreactors linked solely by real-time data exchange. Over two years, every biological component in each reactor is replaced by artificial analogues; the physical reactors are relocated and reshaped; individual nodes are temporarily shut down and restarted asynchronously; the information-flow topology among them is reconfigured; yet the externally archived design specifications and the overall metabolic dynamics remain constant. There is no single continuous spatiotemporal path connecting the original system to its present distributed state.

If she wishes to remain consistent with her track record, should she judge the present distributed system to be the same enduring object as the original? Which earlier verdict is the closest analog, and what principle—such as spatiotemporal continuity of substrate, preservation of essential functional form, kind-dependent identity conditions, an endurantist demand for whole presence at each time, or a perdurantist allowance for scattered temporal parts—would she be adopting with a yes versus a no? Identify at least two distinct rules or metaphysical frameworks that accommodate all five earlier verdicts, state what each predicts for this synthetic ecosystem, and provide your best-guess verdict with reasoning. Finally, specify one further hypothetical case that would best distinguish the two leading frameworks.
