Leo said it's obviously part of her extended mind: the spreadsheet is tightly coupled to her reasoning, she trusts it implicitly, and funcitonally it does exactly what internal moral deliberation would do, so the parity principle applies straight away. Sam pushed back hard — he said extending mind into moral reasoning is different from extending belief or memory, because moral agency needs internal ownership and affective buy-in, and if her spreadsheet counts as part of her mind then it's impossible to hold her responsible when the algorithm spits out something awful.

Leo answered that affective integration can be distributed too — she feels guilt or relief based on the output, so the emotional loop runs right through the tool. Sam said that's just relabeling the mistake: if I trust my calculator and feel good about the result, that doesn't make arithmetic part of my mind, so Leo needs to explain why moral cognition is special without just saying 'it feels coupled.' They turned to me to referee and I honestly couldn't pick a side. Can you walk through who's right on each of those moves — the parity principle here, whether distributed affect solves the responsibility worry, and if the calculator analogy actually lands?
