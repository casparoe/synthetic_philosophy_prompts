I am preparing a case study for my graduate seminar on distributive rescue ethics, and I want you to extrapolate from my colleague Dr. Amara Okonkwo's previous judgments. Her track record in our discussions is as follows: when the choice is between rescuing one stranger and five strangers with equal risk and proximity, she judges that we ought to save the five; when it is between one close friend and three strangers, she judges we ought to save the friend; when it is between saving five strangers now and preventing an identical threat to ten strangers in twenty years, she judges we ought to save the five now; and when saving the five requires actively killing one innocent person who is not otherwise endangered, she judges we must not kill, even if that means letting all five die. The new case: a rescue mechanism can be directed to either Site A, where it will certainly save exactly three strangers with no other effects, or Site B, where it will certainly save exactly four strangers but will also, as an unavoidable mechanical side-effect, cause severe but non-fatal injuries to a fifth stranger who is not in danger. Predict Okonkwo's verdict—Site A, Site B, or indifferent—state whether her implicit rule is best read as aggregation constrained only by direct-harm prohibitions, as a Taurek-style equality-of-claims view supplemented by special obligations, or as a rights-based view that blocks aggregation whenever an innocent is seriously harmed, and identify the single further case that would best distinguish the two rules that remain compatible with her track record. Lead with your bottom-line prediction in the first sentence, then give the reasoning with no preamble and without restating the question.
