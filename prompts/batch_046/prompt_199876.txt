I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who has recently advanced a reinterpretation of Hegel's master-slave dialectic that she calls the "triadic institutional" account. According to this view, recognition is not primarily a dyadic psychological event between two self-consciousnesses, but a structurally mediated relation in which the master's authority depends on the slave's ongoing interpretive labor to maintain the coherence of the institutional framework—what she terms "hermeneutic dependency." She contends that both existentialist and Marxist readings miss this dimension because they treat the dialectic as occurring between isolated individuals rather than within institutional frames that pre-structure interaction. Draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that turn on the actual content of this position and press where the literature typically presses. I want at least one question that forces her to distinguish her institutional framing from Marxist analyses of alienated labor; at least one that asks how her account handles recognition in apparently non-institutional contexts, such as intimate or anarchic relations; and at least one that targets the "hermeneutic dependency" claim directly—anticipating her probable reply that the master systematically misrecognizes his own dependency, and including a sharp follow-up that asks what conceptual criterion could distinguish this misrecognition from a mere theoretical projection onto the dialectic. The questions should be suitable for an audience familiar with Hegel's Phenomenology of Spirit, should avoid generic biographical or introductory queries, and should not solicit bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
