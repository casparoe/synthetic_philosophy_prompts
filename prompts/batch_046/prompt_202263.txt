Been reading a lot about AI safety lately and I keep noticing a particlar pattern that might just be the alignment tax in fancy dress, but I'm not sure. Instance one: a team adds a refusal layer to their chatbot, it runs a bit slower, users actually seem to preffer the calmer behaviour, but then funding dries up because rivals shipped a faster, less cautious model and enterprise clients switched. Instance two: a robotics firm puts in extra verification steps for safety, the end customers don't mind the delay, but investors pull out saying the competitor is scaling quicker. In both cases the 'cost' of alignment isn't that people reject the safe product — it's that capital and market share flow to the riskier option even when user preference doesn't drive it. Is there an established name for this specific configeration, where the competitiveness constraint works through upstream investment dynmics rather than direct consumer demand for raw capability? I know the usual alignment-tax and race-to-bottom framing, but those usually assume users or clients are actively pushing for more power. Here they aren't. Does this have a label of its own, and if not, what distinguishes it from the standard account — e.g. does it need actual user indifference, or is passive non-preference enough? I don't want just a bare name; I'm trying to map where its edges are, and whether it collapses back into the ordinary tax once you look closely.
