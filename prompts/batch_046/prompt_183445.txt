I'm rewatching Groundhog Day and trying to wrap my head around Kant's univeralizability tests. Phil gets stuck in a loop where he can do anything and there's no consequence the next day. I wonder if the loop is like a weird testing ground for maxims: the selfish ones eventually make him miserible even with no external punishment, while the good ones feel sustainable. Is the movie secretly illustrating Kant's idea that you can't will a maxim like 'lie whenever it's convenient' to become universal law, or is that reading forced? And does the fact that he remembers each loop but no one else does mess up the universalizability test, since it's not actually a world where everyone lives like Phil? I'd like a take that doesn't just say 'it's about becoming a good person' but actually engages with whether Kant's formula works here, and where the movie resists that reading.
