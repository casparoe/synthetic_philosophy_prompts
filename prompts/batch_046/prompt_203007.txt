For my graduate seminar on historical ontology and collective identity, I have drafted the following set of commitments, and before presenting them I want a consistency audit. I hold that: (1) Benedict Anderson’s thesis that nations are imagined communities arising with print capitalism is broadly correct, so that nations as we understand them are not pre-modern givens; (2) Eric Hobsbawm is right that the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries saw an unprecedented proliferation of invented traditions, and that this reveals the constructed character of much national ritual and symbol; (3) nonetheless, Anthony Smith’s ethnosymbolist objection carries weight—some modern nations draw on genuinely older ethnic cores (shared myths, memories, and symbolic repertoires) that are not merely fabricated ex nihilo, which means collective continuity is not always reducible to invention; (4) I am committed to methodological anti-nationalism: the nation-state should not be treated as the natural or default unit of historical analysis; (5) despite (4), I believe historians may legitimately use nation as an anachronistic analytical category for pre-modern societies, provided they sharply distinguish this heuristic use from modern nationalist ideology; (6) the authenticity of a tradition for its practitioners is independent of its antiquity, so an invented tradition can be fully authentic; (7) I reject primordialism—the view that nations are ancient, natural, or given entities—without reservation.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which tensions are only apparent? In particular, I am unsure whether (3) undermines (1) by reintroducing a kind of continuity that Anderson’s modernism rules out, and whether (4) and (5) are compatible or whether using the nation as an analytical category smuggles back in the very methodological nationalism I reject. I also want to know whether (2) and (6) sit together more easily than they first appear. Rank the tensions by severity, specify for each the least costly revision and what it would cost me, and indicate which single change would restore consistency with the least overall damage. Do not recommend a bibliography or cite specific page numbers; work with the positions as stated.

Please say how confident you are in each judgment about these tensions, and single out the point about which you are least certain.
