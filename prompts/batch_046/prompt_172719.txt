You are a philosopher of mind and applied ethics with expertise in philosophy of neuroscience and value theory. Before drafting, spend a few minutes brainstorming the hidden conceptual dependencies inside biotechnological proposals to eliminate suffering. Then write a concise research proposal—approximately 800 to 1000 words, suitable for a six-month independent project or dissertation prospectus—addressing the following: The hedonistic imperative proposes using biotechnology, including genetic and neuropharmacological intervention, to abolish suffering and perhaps replace it with gradients of well-being. Most philosophical treatments take the moral urgency as primary and treat the neuroscience as merely instrumental. Your proposal should invert that framing and ask whether the imperative is conceptually coherent only if negative valence and affective arousal are neurologically and phenomenologically decoupled, so that suffering can be eliminated without flattening the arousal dynamics required for motivation, aesthetic experience, and preference-formation. If valence and arousal are structurally coupled—if reducing negative affect necessarily dampens the intensity of all affective states—does the imperative implicitly commit us to a specific emotional ontology, such as a baseline-neutral or thin conception of consciousness, rather than to a purely moral reform? State the precise question so that it admits a determinate answer; explain why existing ethical and neuroscientific treatments leave this dependency open; describe your methodological approach, combining conceptual analysis of affective structure with critical evaluation of current neuroscience on valence-arousal interaction, without inventing specific empirical studies or citations; argue why this conceptual-structural angle is necessary and more tractable for a short project than the standard debate over obligations to future generations; outline what a successful deliverable would look like for people actually designing interventions; specify what finding would count as failure; and provide an honest assessment of what could make the project too ambitious for six months. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation requests; refer to positions and arguments generally and note where you will supply references yourself. Think carefully about whether your framing genuinely opens a new angle or merely restates familiar disputes between suffering-reduction and value-pluralism.
