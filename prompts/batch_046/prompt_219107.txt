You sit on the review panel for a proposed revision to your country's public mental-health diagnostic manual. The working group has proposed adding "Digital Reflexive Classification Syndrome" (DRCS): a putative disorder defined by a feedback loop in which patients, after receiving provisional diagnostic labels from algorithmic screening tools, progressively reshape their self-reports and behaviors to match those labels more precisely than their initial presentations did. Proponents argue this captures a looping effect—structurally analogous to the kind Hacking identified in psychiatric classification, but now mediated by automated classification—whose recognition is necessary to prevent misdiagnosis of transient algorithmic influence as organic pathology. Critics reply that naming the loop as a disorder medicalizes a socio-technical interaction, collapses the distinction between classification and cause, and risks generating the very pathology it purports to describe. Draft the panel's formal decision memo: recommend whether DRCS should be included, excluded, or included only under restrictive criteria, with reasoning the working group could respond to; and treat the strongest objection from the losing side with intellectual honesty. Do not ask for additional literature or citations; assume the panel possesses the conceptual framework required.
