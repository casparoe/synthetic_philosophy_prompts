I'm drafting my position for a seminar on Buddhist moral philosophy and want a consistency audit before I present. Here are my commitments:

(1) I hold anatta in a strong, diachronic form: there is no enduring self or agent within or across lives; personal identity is purely a conventional designation (prajñapti) applied to causally continuous but impermanent aggregates (skandhas).

(2) I take Buddhist ethics to be essentially a virtue ethics of mental cultivation: moral progress consists in stabilizing wholesome mental factors (kuśala cetasikāḥ) and eradicating defilements (kleśāḥ), with precepts (śīla) functioning mainly as instruments for this internal training rather than as deontological constraints.

(3) I justify the Bodhisattva vow—to remain in saṃsāra indefinitely for the welfare of all beings—on explicitly consequentialist grounds: the decision is rational because the aggregate suffering averted across an infinite future population of sentient beings outweighs the conventional agent's own deferred liberation.

(4) I deny that karmic responsibility requires a self to "own" results; I accept only a causal-continuity model (saṃtāna) in which desert is a functional relation between mental streams, not a relation between actions and an enduring subject.

(5) In normative evaluation, I treat intention (cetanā) as primary and outcome as derivative: an act that produces harm from pure compassion is ethically superior to an act that produces benefit from greed or aversion.

(6) I hold that skillful means (upāya) permits a Bodhisattva to violate a conventional precept—say, false speech—when it prevents catastrophic harm, provided the mental state remains entirely free of attachment (rāga) and hostility (dveṣa).

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and how badly. For each real tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs; for each merely apparent tension, say why it dissolves on inspection. Rank the tensions by severity. Then tell me which single revision—giving up or weakening one commitment—would clean up the most problems overall.
