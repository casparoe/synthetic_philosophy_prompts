Write a dialogue between a proponent of the American Dream's meritocratic celebration of success and a proponent of Jantelagen's egalitarian restraint on individual prominence. They should debate whether the tall poppy syndrome is best understood as envious social sanction or as a corrective to the status externalities that distort collaborative knowledge production. Ground the exchange in a specific scenario: a small interdisciplinary research collective in which one member receives prominent external praise for a breakthrough that relied heavily on shared, uncredited labour. The American Dream advocate should argue that suppressing visible recognition undermines incentive alignment and misattributes collective effort to individual genius; the Jantelagen advocate should reply that the resentment is not mere envy but a defence of epistemic humility, insisting that visibility without proportional attribution erodes the trust required for genuine collaboration. Ensure both characters are equally competent, that each responds directly to the other's substantive points rather than talking past them, and that neither is reduced to a strawman.
