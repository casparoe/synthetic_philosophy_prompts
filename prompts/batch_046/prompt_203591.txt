My verdicts on freedom and I want the principle behind them if there is one. Take a prisoner whose cell door is open but who won't leave because guards are waiting outside -- I say he's unfree, but I'm not sure if that's negative liberty (they'd interfere if he left) or republican (they have arbitrary power over him). Then there's the classic slave with a benevolent master who never gives orders -- I think she's still unfree, but my roommate says she's negativly free since no one actually stops her. Next, a gig worker who gets deactivated by an algorithm with zero human review -- I feel that's domination, but there's no person dominating, just code. Is that still republican unfreedom? Then a citizen in a direct democracy who always loses the vote -- she can participate, so is she positively free, or negatively unfree because the majority can do whatever? And here's the weird one: an AI system that runs totally without interference, except its creators hold a kill switch they've never used. I think that's structurally like the slave case, but my roommate says domination needs a dominating will, and code doesn't have one. So is republican liberty only about human masters? Can one principle cover all five, and which of my verdicts has to go?
