You are a senior philosopher of metaphysics specialising in material constitution, persistence conditions, and hylomorphism. Please explain, with care and logical precision, the following conceptual issue.

Begin with the familiar statue-and-clay case, but modify it by introducing what we might term “form-preserving substrate cycling”. An artist sculpts a statue from a single block of clay and then institutes a rigorous maintenance programme: each week, a precisely measured portion of the clay is removed and replaced with chemically identical fresh clay, guided continuously by a high-resolution three-dimensional scan that ensures the macroscopic form never deviates by more than a fraction of a millimetre. Over the course of a decade, every original molecule has been replaced, yet there is never a discrete moment at which the statue is absent or structurally incomplete. Meanwhile, the discarded original clay is deposited in a landfill, where natural erosion eventually reassembles it into an amorphous lump bearing no artistic form whatsoever.

Under a view that distinguishes constitution from strict identity—where the statue is constituted by, but not identical to, the clay—does this continuous, scan-guided replacement preserve a single persisting statue constituted by successive batches of matter? Or does the external imposition of form via the digital blueprint sever the usual dependence relation, so that what persists is not a constituted object but rather a kind of “formal trajectory” that happens to be realised in different clay at each stage?

Furthermore, contrast this scenario with the standard Ship of Theseus: in that case, replacement is typically understood as accidental repair driven by internal structural needs, whereas here the replacement is systematic, anticipatory, and governed by an independent representational medium (the scan). How does this distinction affect arguments about whether the statue’s persistence conditions are grounded in the continuity of its material parts, in the continuity of its form, or in some hybrid relation?

In your answer, clarify whether the dispersed original clay in the landfill constitutes a second, non-artistic object that was once part of the statue, and whether the concept of “coincident objects” can be maintained once the substrate has been entirely cycled out.
