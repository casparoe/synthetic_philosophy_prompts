It goes like this: "Löb's theorem proves that any rational agent that models its own reasoning can't consistently trust a future version of itself. If the agent can formally prove 'if my smarter tomorrow-self proves P, then P is true,' then the theorem forces it to already prove P today. That means delegation never works — the agent already has to accept every conclusion its upgrade would reach, so no real learning happens. This applies wether the agent uses logic or probabilities, which is why self-improving AI is fundamentally impossible: any system that tries to defer to itself collapses into circular reasoning and can't safely update its beliefs."

Something about extending this to probabilistic reasoning feels off, and the jump to "fundamentally impossible" seems like a lot. Can you go through it line by line and tell me what's accurate about Löb's theorem, what's being misapplied, and wether the conclusion actually follows?
