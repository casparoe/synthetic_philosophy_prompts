The claim is this: the conventional prescription that strategic decisions require an outside view while inside views should be confined to tactical adjustments collapses once we recognize that selecting, bounding, and weighting reference classes is itself an inside-view exercise; I propose replacing the binary distinction with a calibrated continuum of “model humility” scaled to the heterogeneity of the reference class, and I argue that this is especially urgent in clinical and charitable allocation contexts where the subject or beneficiary is not exchangeable with the aggregate population.

The topic has become personally urgent since my recent diagnosis forced me to choose between an aggressive trial protocol with weak aggregate survival data but a plausible mechanistic rationale specific to my subtype, and standard care supported by stronger base-rate outcomes; I realized that the standard “outside view first” framework offered no principled, non-arbitrary rule for weighting my subtype features against the aggregate reference class, and that my clinicians’ confidence in the outside view concealed a hidden inside-view judgment about which patients properly belong in the class.
