I'm trying to sharpen an objection to actualism about infinity that I've been developing in a graduate seminar on the philosophy of mathematics. The standard finitist complaint is that Cantor treats infinity as a completed magnitude, yet Hilbert's hotel shows that an actually infinite totality can be put into one-to-one correspondence with a proper subset of itself—say, by shifting every occupant from room n to room n+1 to free up room 1. My students often reply that this is not a paradox but the defining feature of ℵ₀, and that cardinality remains stable despite the shift. But here is where I think the finitist can press further: if every discrete relocation in the hotel requires a prior vacancy to move into, then the "completed" infinite shift is never actually executed as a total procedure; it is only defined as a limit rule. Consequently, doesn't Hilbert's hotel inadvertently demonstrate that actual infinity cannot be instantiated as a completed state, since any attempt to realize the mapping physically collapses into a potential, step-by-step process? In other words, is Cantor's transfinite arithmetic not describing a real magnitude at all, but rather formalizing the open-endedness that finitists already acknowledge? I want to ask whether this physical-operational constraint genuinely strengthens finitism, or whether it simply confuses the ontology of number with the mechanics of spatial rearrangement.
