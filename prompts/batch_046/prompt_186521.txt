Consider the following exam question from a graduate metaphysics course and the answer submitted by a student.

Question: Rietdijk’s 1966 paper claims to derive determinism rigorously from special relativity, and Putnam’s 1967 reformulation is widely read as supporting a block-universe ontology. Explain the core reasoning, distinguishing Rietdijk’s original argument from Putnam’s version. Your answer should describe the role of the relativity of simultaneity, present the Andromeda-style thought experiment, and explain why the conclusion is usually taken to undermine presentism rather than to establish causal determinism in the Laplacean sense. Finally, evaluate whether a presentist can resist the argument by positing a privileged, though perhaps epistemically inaccessible, spacetime foliation.

Student answer: Special relativity shows that simultaneity is frame-relative: two spatially separated events can be simultaneous in one inertial frame but occur in different temporal orders in another. Rietdijk used this to argue that there is no objective “now” slice cutting across the universe. He illustrates the point with the Andromeda galaxy thought experiment: an observer walking slowly on Earth and another traveling rapidly toward Andromeda have different planes of simultaneity, so for the traveler events that we regard as lying centuries in Earth’s future are already present. Because these future events are “now” relative to a physically legitimate frame, Rietdijk concludes they must already be real; hence all events—past, present, and future—exist with equal ontological status, and the future is fixed. Putnam’s 1967 paper essentially repeats this reasoning but frames it explicitly as an argument for the block universe and against temporal becoming. The argument does not show that future events are causally determined by prior laws; rather, it shows ontological determinism, in the sense that the future is already there. A presentist might reply that special relativity itself denies objective simultaneity, so there is no contradiction in saying that each frame possesses its own present. This saves presentism without requiring a hidden foliation, because reality can be frame-relative in the same way that velocity is. Therefore, I think the argument fails: it assumes that presentness must be invariant across frames, but once we accept relativity that assumption is unwarranted.
