Draft an adversarial-collaboration summary between two opposing camps in the philosophy of science: the Structural Inevitabilists, who treat simultaneous discoveries (Newton and Leibniz on calculus; Darwin and Wallace on natural selection) as evidence that mature problem-spaces generate solutions with high counterfactual robustness, and the Agent-Contingency Theorists, who hold that the specific conceptual packaging, analogical framing, and institutional pathways taken by each discoverer are not substitutable without altering both the theory's reception and its long-run development.

Structure the output in three sections. First, Agreed Formal Claims: list the historical and logical points both sides can sign — for instance, that both pairs produced formally equivalent or near-equivalent core results within a narrow temporal window, that both drew on overlapping prior literature, and that institutional incentives for priority existed in both cases. Phrase these neutrally, without implying which interpretation they favor.

Second, Precise Disagreement: state each side's core thesis in wording its own proponents would accept, isolating the exact divergence. Is it about (i) the modal robustness of the discovery event itself, (ii) the substitutability of the discoverer's conceptual framing, or (iii) the normative weight of priority in scientific credit? Within this section, both sides should address the construct of an epistemic redundancy index — defined as the estimated probability that a given well-defined problem-space produces N ≥ 2 independent, publishable solutions within one generation, holding constant funding levels and communication speed. Each side should state whether a high index value undermines agent-centered attribution, is neutral to it, or actually reinforces the importance of individual framing in a crowded field.

Third, Cruxes and Predictive Tests: identify one conceptual or counterfactual test both sides agree would move the debate — for example, whether calculus would have developed along a synthetic-geometric rather than analytic-notation path absent Leibniz's specific formalism, or whether evolutionary biology would have adopted a variational-selection framing absent Darwin's particular Malthusian analogy. Each camp should specify in advance what result would weaken its view, and whether resolving the redundancy-index question for nineteenth-century biology settles the normative issue of attribution or leaves it open.

Keep the tone formal and analytical. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to secondary literature; the exercise is in conceptual engineering, not scholarly recall. Avoid invoking obscure named doctrines — reason directly from the historical structure, the logic of counterfactual dependence, and the normative economics of scientific credit.
