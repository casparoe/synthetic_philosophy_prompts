I am trying to stabilize my metaethical commitments before writing a paper and want an audit of whether the following package hangs together. I hold that (a) there are free-floating rationales—normative reasons whose existence and authority do not depend on any actual or possible agent's mental states or on any social practice; (b) a reason genuinely applies to an agent only if that agent is in principle capable of being motivated by it; (c) moral reasons apply with full force both to agents constitutionally incapable of such motivation, such as severe psychopaths, and to future generations who do not yet exist; (d) the specific content of our moral obligations is not discovered in a pre-institutional normative realm but is generated and sustained by practices of mutual recognition and accountability; and (e) in a world permanently emptied of all sentient beings, the free-floating normative structure in (a) would remain intact, though uninstantiated. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which are only apparently so, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each identify what must be abandoned or qualified and at what philosophical cost.
