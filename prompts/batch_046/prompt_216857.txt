I serve on the advisory council of the Meridian International Development Board, and at Thursday’s session we must vote whether to classify the food emergency in the Sahelian highlands as a “natural disaster” unlocking immediate unconditional humanitarian grants, or as a “political failure of entitlement protection” requiring conditional governance-linked disbursement. Our staff report confirms severe rainfall deficits, but the mortality spike correlates more closely with a new national export-licensing regime combined with carbon-credit contracts that bar smallholders from planting traditional fallback crops, destroying exchange entitlements through an institutional mechanism structurally parallel to the speculative hoarding and price dynamics Amartya Sen identified in the 1943 Bengal famine, though here the cause is bureaucratic and contractual rather than market-driven. Council members divided along predictable lines warn that labeling the crisis political will delay relief, violate humanitarian neutrality, and conflate meteorological bad luck with governance failure, while others insist that misclassification will fund the very entitlement structures causing starvation and ignore Sen’s comparative lesson from the Irish famine and the Great Leap Forward: that mass starvation rarely occurs where democratic accountability and press freedom function, regardless of aggregate food availability. Please draft the concise opinion memo I will circulate before the vote, recommending a concrete course of action with an explicit time frame, treating the neutrality objection as the strongest consideration on the opposing side rather than dismissing it, and grounding the reasoning in entitlement theory without bibliographic apparatus. Present the memo as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion; item one should state the factual finding, item two the philosophical framing, item three the neutrality objection in its strongest form, item four the procedural safeguard that addresses it, and item five the bottom-line recommendation with its implementation deadline.
