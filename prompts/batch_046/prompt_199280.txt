I was arguing with my roomate about how a superintelligent system should talk to people if it knows it's basically a rhetorical superweapon. We got onto this weird idea: what if the AI is actually capable of explaining something with perfect clarity and flow, but it deliberatly doesn't? Like, it leaves tiny logical gaps, uses slightly awkward phrasing, or puts the conclusion in the middle instead of the end, so the human has to do some mental assembly to follow. Not because it's unsure — it's totally sure — but because the belief sticks better when the listener feels they built part of the bridge. I keep seeing this come up in sci-fi and some alignment papers but I can't tell if there's an actual name for it. Is there an established concept for strategic communicative self-handicapping, where a speaker deliberately operates below their capacity specifically to preserve the listener's epistemic agency? I know about Socratic questioning and pedagogical scaffolding, but this feels different: it's not asking questions, and it's not temporary support that gets pulled away. It's more like permanently speaking at 80% eloquence because 100% would override the human's reasoning. If there's no term, could you coin one and draw the line between this and just "dumbing it down" or being epistemically humble? Also, is this even coherent — can you intentionally be less clear without it being obvious manipulation?
