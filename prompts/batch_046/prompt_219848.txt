I am preparing an independent presentation for my school's advanced philosophy and politics seminar, and I have reached an impasse in a discussion with my classmates about whether the left-right distinction still possesses any real conceptual coherence, or whether it has become merely a journalistic convenience. Rather than sorting people by the labels they prefer, I want to design a decision-tree taxonomy—something I could distribute as a handout—that maps the logical space of positions by forcing a respondent to answer a sequence of concrete conceptual questions in order. The tree should open with the foundational split: is the left-right distinction a robust ideological family, a positional coalition shaped by institutional seating arrangements, or an obsolete heuristic that should be replaced by multidimensional axes? From there, it should branch according to what, if anything, actually holds each side together—whether that is a shared commitment to equality or hierarchy, a common psychological disposition, a unified economic program, or only a shared opposition to the other camp. I want the taxonomy to include some less standard branches that my friends and I have been arguing about: specifically, whether the distinction presupposes the nation-state as the primary unit of politics and therefore dissolves in post-national or sub-national contexts, and whether treating economic and social commitments as genuinely orthogonal dimensions (rather than as correlated packages) makes the binary distinction collapse altogether. Each internal node should be phrased as a claim a person could agree or disagree with without already knowing which named position it leads to, and every leaf should identify a recognizable view—liberal, socialist, conservative, libertarian, reactionary, anarchist, or an unnamed combination—together with one sentence describing its most awkward commitment. Where a logical combination of answers appears to be empty, please note briefly whether that emptiness seems due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. I will supply all historical references, citations, and named thinkers myself; I am asking only for the conceptual architecture, the exact phrasing of each branching question, and the precise characterization of each terminal position.
