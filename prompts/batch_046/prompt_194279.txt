ive been chewing on the orwellian vs stalinesque split in the color phi literature and it feels like the debate has been deadlocked for years without anyone admitting it. dennett sets up the distinction — either consciousness gets retroactively rewritten like 1984, or the brain just airbrushes the report like stalin — and then philosophers of mind just camp out on one side. but heres my working theory: the stall isnt about the phenomology of motion or the neuroscience of postdictive binding. its about whether anyone still believes consciousness has a single canonical timeline that can be 'revised' in the first place. if you take predictive processing seriously, theres no raw 'first draft' sitting in a cartesian theater; theres just hierarchical predictions updating at different latencies. so are orwellians and stalinesques actually disagreeing about empirical data, or are they both defending editing-metaphors that collapse once you reject the theater model? i want a diagnosis: is this a verbal dispute about 'experience', a hidden empirical disagreement about temporal resolution, or two groups answering different questions under one slogan? and honestly, what would count as progress here — a specific binding-window result, or is the whole framing broken?
