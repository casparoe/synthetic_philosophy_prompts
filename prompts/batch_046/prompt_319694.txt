I used to run a lab studying population dynamics in migratory birds — retired now, and lately I've been trying to make sense of this antinatalism stuff, especially Benetar's asymmetry. I think I've followed the basic idea: if a kid never gets born, it's not a harm that they miss out on pleasure, but it is a benefit that they miss out on pain. But my neighbour (he teaches logic at the community college) walked me through an extension that sounds right at each step but ends up somewhere crazy, and I can't see the leak.

Here are his steps, more or less: (1) Benetar's asymmetry holds for any merely possible person — absence of suffering is good, absence of pleasure is neutral. (2) Now, 'merely possible' shouldn't be restricted to people we actually plan to have; it should cover anyone in the modal neighborhood of our choices, because if I change one gene or one meeting-time, a different suffering person might have existed instead. (3) So every reproductive decision that avoids one potential sufferer generates real moral value, and these values aggregate across all nearby modal branches. (4) But then consider an existing person, call her E. By ending E's reproductive line — even by ending E herself — we prevent a vast tree of modal-nearby descendents, each of whose non-existence counts as a good absence of suffering. (5) If the expected suffering in that tree is large enough, the total good of preventing them outweighs the harm done to E. (6) Therefore Benetar's view, taken consistently with modal proximity, doesn't just say 'don't have kids'; it says we should actively eliminate reproductive lines whenever the math works out. That can't be right.

I want to keep (1) and (2) if I can, because they feel like natural extensions of the asymmetry, but maybe the problem is in (3) with aggregation? Or maybe (4) sneaks in an equivocation about whose absence we're counting? I'm not sure. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost me? I'd rather not just say 'modal proximity is too vague' unless I have to — that feels like dodging.
