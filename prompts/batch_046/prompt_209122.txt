Write an essay addressing the following controversy. Douglas Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model of creativity proposes that novel ideas emerge from adjusting continuous parameters — knobs — within a stable conceptual space, with even radical-seeming innovations reducible to extreme or unexpected parameter settings. Critics contend that certain breakthroughs restructure the conceptual space itself, replacing its dimensions rather than navigating within them — what might be termed board-reconfiguration rather than knob-twiddling. The central question is whether Hofstadter's framework can coherently accommodate such restructuring by appealing to hidden or meta-level knobs without collapsing into an unfalsifiable account in which any innovation is retrospectively assigned to some previously unnoticed parameter.

Reach and defend a specific conclusion: is the knob-twiddling model descriptively adequate for explaining deep conceptual restructuring, or does it necessarily omit a distinct cognitive mechanism? Your essay should (1) sharply distinguish first-order parameter adjustment from second-order framework restructuring, using precise conceptual criteria rather than intuition; (2) assess whether allowing meta-knobs saves the model or renders it vacuous; and (3) apply this analysis to at least one concrete case — such as the shift from humoral to germ-theoretic medicine, or the reconceptualization of legal contract from static agreement to dynamic relational process — to determine whether the transition is best understood as turning a hidden causal-ontology knob or as discarding the board entirely. Do not supply bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; reason solely from the conceptual architecture.
