hey, i'm trying to map the actual open work in open borders vs the right to exclude, but i'm bored of the libertarian-communitarian replay. here's a split i don't think gets formalized enough: treat state exclusion as either 'filtering' (blocking people who'd change collective choices) or 'rationing' (blocking people because housing/energy/water are actually finite). once you draw that line, three questions come up fast: does the exclusion right survive when climate migration makes entry a survival necessity rather than a preference; can democracy stay legit with a large class of permanent residents who never recieve a path to citizenship; and does solving the filtering-vs-rationing problem actually unlock policy change, or is it just taxonomy? if you only had two years and wanted to shift immigration law rather than write neat conceptual papers, rank these three: which is upstream of the others, which is tractable with current tools, and which is overrated? defend the ordering by dependency and practical payoff, not just philosophical neatness.
