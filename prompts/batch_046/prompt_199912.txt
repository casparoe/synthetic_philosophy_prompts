Write a joke about the relationship between absurdism and contemporary hybrid accounts of meaning in life. The punchline should turn on a precise distinction actually used in the literature—namely, the gap between asking about "the meaning of life" in the cosmic or global sense (whether human existence serves some overarching purpose) and asking about "meaning in life" in the local sense (whether particular activities or relationships within a life are worthwhile). Frame the joke so that an absurdist character—someone who denies both cosmic purpose and the need for objective value to ground fulfillment—interacts with a proponent of the hybrid view, according to which local meaning requires both subjective attraction and objective worth. The humor should come from the absurdist treating the absence of global meaning not as a deficit that undermines local projects, but as liberating them from the burden of objective justification, in a way that genuinely confounds the hybrid theorist. Avoid generic punchlines about life being meaningless or about pushing boulders uphill; instead, the joke should depend on the actual logical structure of these two positions. It should be intelligible to someone familiar with these debates, and it should not depend on citation of specific texts, author names, or knowledge of obscure secondary literature.
