For my high school philosophy of mind seminar, I need a decision tree that sorts people by what they actually believe about mental causation given the causal exclusion argument, rather than by the labels they prefer. Start every respondent with the commitment that every physical effect has a sufficient physical cause. The first branch should present a concrete case—say, my deciding to raise my arm—and ask whether the mental decision and the physical neural event are numerically identical or genuinely distinct properties. From there, branch on whether one accepts an exclusion principle that forbids systematic overdetermination of physical effects by both mental and physical causes. Next, branch on whether mental properties can possess novel causal powers not already present in their physical bases. The leaves must include at least the reductive physicalist, the eliminativist, the epiphenomenalist, the overdeterminationist, and the emergentist who allows downward causation. To keep the tree from being a simple encyclopedia entry, inject a non-standard distinction that operates as a hidden axis: call it "structural causation" versus "dynamical causation," defining structural causation as a mental state's producing an effect only in virtue of being realized by its physical base, and dynamical causation as its exerting independent causal force. Use this distinction to split at least one standard leaf, and explicitly flag any pair of positions—especially structural versus dynamical readings of emergentism—that the tree's nodes cannot cleanly separate. Every node should be phrased as a judgment about a concrete scenario, such as whether my feeling pain causes withdrawal independently of the neural pathway, so that someone can answer without knowing technical names. After mapping the branches, list all logically possible combinations of answers; for any empty combination—such as accepting physical closure, rejecting identity, accepting exclusion, yet endorsing dynamical downward causation—briefly diagnose whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The instrument should contain about seven branch nodes and end in no more than eight named positions, with unoccupied branches clearly marked. Do not include requests for citations, bibliographies, or obscure references; the deliverable should be the logical architecture of commitments itself.
