I'm working on a feature about this thing some archivists call negative-source microhistory—basically doing a Ginzburg-style deep dive on people who never appear in court rolls or census books, by reading teh absences around them. I got into it after a historian friend said history from below is basically dead if it depends on state paper, since every doccument is already an elite framing.

That made me wonder: if microhistorians used inquisition trials—literally state violence paperwork—to reconstruct Menocchio's world, doesn't that mean microhistory is just elite history with a smaller sample size? So is using a state archive automatically contaminating a study with elite perspective, or can teh negative-archive method—reading gaps in census data—produce a cleaner history from below?

I feel like I'm assuming that 'from below' means 'without documents,' which sounds wrong even as I tiype it, but I can't shake the feeling that there's a hidden contradiction in using bureaucratic traces to claim we're escaping bureaucratic history.
