ok so I'm writing up my views for a philosophy-of-science reading group and want a consistency check before I present. Here's roughly where I've landed on post-empirical physics and multiverse stuff:

(1) I'm a scientific realist, but I think it's only rational to commit to a theory's ontology after there's real empirical success—predictions that could have failed.
(2) Even so, I think string theory is our best shot at quantum gravity and deserves serious ontological commitment right now, despite zero confirmed novel predictions.
(3) I take the landscape/multiverse seriously: there really are something like 10^500 vacua, and it's not just a calculational glitch.
(4) But I reject anthropic reasoning outright—using the idea that we observe these constants just because they're compatible with life feels like abandoning explanation, not giving one.
(5) I think mathematical uniqueness and internal consistency can justify belief when direct experiments are permanently out of reach.
(6) At the same time, I think any theory that can't in priciple distinguish different observable constants has failed as science, no matter how elegent it is.

Do these hang together? I'm especially worried about (1) vs (2), and whether (3) plus (4) makes (2) impossible since we have no other way to navigate the landscape. Also, (5) and (6) look like they're pulling in opposite directions—are they actually in tension, or do they just talk past each other? Rank the conflicts by severity, say which single revision cleans up the most, and don't just list them all as equally bad.
