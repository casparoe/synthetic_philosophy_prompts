Write a two-page dissertation prospectus addressing the following problem. Dennett's heterophenomenological method treats first-person reports as texts requiring neutral interpretation rather than transparent access to private experience, but the method has almost exclusively been applied to human subjects with biological sentience. The open question is whether heterophenomenology can be disciplinedly extended to artificial neural networks that produce structured behavioral traces—internal feature representations, generated explanations, or interpretability outputs—without either illicitly attributing consciousness or reducing these traces to mere mechanical descriptions that miss their report-like structure. State the question precisely: what conditions must a synthetic trace satisfy to be interpreted heterophenomenologically as a report about an internal state, and how does this differ from standard functionalist or eliminativist readings in AI interpretability? Explain why existing work in philosophy of consciousness and machine-learning interpretability leaves this unsettled: the former assumes human phenomenological subjects, while the latter typically asks whether a system is conscious rather than how to bracket that question and interpret its outputs neutrally. Propose a six-month method: select one class of interpretable representations (for instance, activation-pattern descriptions or step-by-step reasoning traces), apply heterophenomenological bracketing of ontological commitment, and construct a taxonomy distinguishing synthetic reports that function analogously to human phenomenological descriptions from those that are purely mechanistic accounts. Specify what a successful result looks like (clear criteria for neutral interpretation applicable across architectures), what failure looks like (the framework collapses into either naive anthropomorphism or standard functionalism), and how the deliverable would serve both philosophical and technical audiences. Do not include a bibliography or citation list; references can be added later.
