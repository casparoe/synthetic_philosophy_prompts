I've been playing with this idea that emergence is never ontological, just an interface artifact. Chemical bonds, cell membranes — they only look like autonomous levels because we always slice the Hamiltonian at exactly the boundary of our measurment apparatus. If you model the subsystem plus the instrument as one closed dynamics, the 'emergent' property disolves into the total evolution and there's no leftover fact. So my claim is: no genuinely emergent entities exist, only stable boundary patterns between a system and its observational frame. Play it straight — what would have to be true for me to be wrong? Not just 'macro-scale behavior differs', I already grant that. I want to know what kind of experimental result, formal proof, or conceptual argument would show there's something genuinely new happening, not just a framing choice. And be honest: is this view well insulated because every counterexample can be absorbed as 'another interface', or is there a real exposure I'm missing?
