I sit on the grant review board for the National Public History Foundation and I need a memo drafted ASAP — the board needs to recieve it befor Friday's vote. A research team is asking for $2M to mount a traveling exhibit called "When Legends Harden: Troy, Arthur, and the Exodus." Their core idea is something they call "institutional crystallization": the claim that a narrative stops being treated as myth and starts being curated as heritage not because new proof shows up, but because museums, school curricula, and funding bodies repeat it enough that asking "did it happen?" feels off-topic. The exhibit would treat all three cases — Trojan War, King Arthur, Exodus — through that lens.

Half the board thinks this is a sharp philosophical point about how legend becomes history; half think it's a fancy way to say "social construction" and that applying it to Exodus in particular risks treating religious narrative as debunked heritage. We need a concrete recommendation: approve with conditions, reject, or approve outright, with reasoning the applicants could actually respond to.

You're a professional philosopher of history with some curatorial ethics experience. Think step by step, and start with a quick brainstorm of the strongest objection to whatever recommendation you land on. The memo should separate what's genuinely settled among people who know the topic from what's still genuinely contested — don't present a disputed view as if it's the consensus. For instance, flag that the lack of direct contemporary attestation for Arthur is widely accepted, but whether the same evidentiary standards should apply to Exodus is hotly disputed, and whether Trojan War narratives contain a historical kernel is also unsettled. Include an honest, serious treatment of the side that loses — if you recommend approval, don't just dismiss the concern about religious sensitivity; if you reject, don't pretend the crystallization framework has no merit.

Don't ask for bibliographies or reading lists — we need actionable reasoning, not citations. And please don't present contested historical claims as settled. There's no need to search anything; just write from your knowledge. Make it readable but formal enough that the board can act on it.
