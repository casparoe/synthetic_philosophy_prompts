Write a short research proposal for a twelve-month fellowship in philosophical archaeology. The core question: when archaeologists infer centralized administrative institutions from standardized material forms—such as uniform storage vessels, weights, or construction modules distributed across non-contiguous sites—does the inference rest on a genuinely abductive warrant (that institutional coordination best explains morphological standardization), or does it covertly assume a functionalist premise that similarity of form implies similarity of organizational origin? Frame the proposal so that it does not collapse into a generic literature review of processual versus post-processual debates; instead, isolate this specific inferential step and ask whether it can be justified without relying on ethnographic analogy or on an unexamined design-intent premise. State the approach precisely: propose a conceptual analysis of the conditions under which standardization is institutionally diagnostic rather than adaptively convergent, possibly aided by a formal model of how different social structures generate observable patterns of material uniformity, and specify what a successful result would look like for someone deciding whether to treat archaeological standardization as institutional evidence. Include an honest scope assessment: what part of the project is feasible in one year, and what would make it fail. Do not request a bibliography or reading list; references will be added separately. The proposal should be answerable without web access and should not invoke obscure theoretical frameworks unfamiliar to a general philosophical audience.
