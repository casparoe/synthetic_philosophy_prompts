I retired a little over a decade ago from experimental biophysics, and since then I have gradually turned my attention to philosophy—particularly to questions about agency, mechanism, and the boundaries of explanation. Recently I have been meeting fortnightly with a small group of former scientific colleagues, all of us now amateurs in philosophy, to discuss texts on free will and determinism. Our last session focused on the well-known case of the digger wasp discussed by Daniel Dennett, in which the insect’s burrow-inspection ritual appears so mechanically fixed that altering the environment produces not adaptation but repetition—a phenomenon Dennett labels sphexishness. We found ourselves wondering whether contemporary predictive-processing accounts of cognition, in which rigidity might be modelled as hyper-precise priors suppressing prediction error, offer a deeper explanation of such behaviour, or whether they merely redescribe the loop in neural vocabulary. For our next gathering I should like a poem that treats this problem with genuine conceptual precision.

Please compose the piece in exactly five numbered stanzas, each of six lines in unrhymed iambic pentametre, presented without any introduction or conclusion outside the verse itself. Let stanza one establish the wasp’s ritual—digging, dragging the prey, inspecting the burrow, repeating—so that the metre itself feels looped and unbroken. In stanza two, allow a fleeting appearance of choice or deviation, only to have it collapse back into the pattern. Stanza three should serve as the volta: fracture the rhythm and reinterpret the loop through the framework of active inference, presenting rigidity not as mindlessness but as an extreme weighting of prior expectations that forecloses counterfactual updating. Stanza four ought to set this against a reasons-responsive conception of flexibility, in which an agent’s capacity to revise action in light of normative considerations marks a genuinely different kind of process. Finally, stanza five must close with an unresolved question—rendered in the final two lines—asking whether the gap between statistical optimisation and normative accountability reveals a real distinction in kinds of agency, or only a limitation in our descriptive apparatus. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of mind familiar with both Dennett’s discussion and current predictive-processing literature recognises the references, yet it should work as poetry for a reader who knows none of this. Number the stanzas 1 through 5, keep each stanza self-contained, and avoid any explanatory prose outside the poem.
