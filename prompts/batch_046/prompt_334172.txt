Consider the claim that, for frontier AI labs developing systems near transformative capability thresholds, the competitiveness constraint makes unilateral acceptance of a substantial alignment tax—operationalized as deliberately accepting a sustained greater-than-twenty-percent effective-capability deficit relative to the best available unaligned architecture—individually irrational regardless of collective preference. State your credence in this claim, decomposed into four sub-estimates: first, the probability that the competitive landscape is sufficiently zero-sum that relative capability determines organizational survival or strategic relevance; second, the probability that alignment interventions available at that development stage actually impose a tax of that magnitude rather than being largely orthogonal or eventually synergistic with capabilities; third, the probability that explicit or tacit coordination mechanisms among major labs could not stabilize a mutual-tax equilibrium even if all participants preferred it; and fourth, the probability that the tax metaphor is conceptually apt—that alignment costs are borne as pure overhead rather than being recoverable through improved reliability, interpretability, or deployment efficiency. Provide a rough numerical value and one-line justification for each component, combine them into an overall credence, and identify which sub-estimate is most sensitive to your final number and what evidence or theoretical result would most move it.
