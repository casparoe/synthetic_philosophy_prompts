Write an essay assessing Bernard Williams's integrity objection to utilitarian moral reasoning, focusing on his claim that a utilitarian agent deciding whether to save a spouse or a stranger is forced to entertain one thought too many. Examine the response that integrity is best understood not as a side-constraint but as a structural feature of practical reasoning that a refined consequentialist framework could absorb without producing the objectionable extra deliberative step. Weigh whether this response genuinely dissolves Williams's critique or merely relocates the conflict to the level of character formation. Reach and justify a definite conclusion about whether the integrity objection succeeds against act-utilitarianism, rule-utilitarianism, both, or neither. End with a one-line verdict in the form Verdict: [your conclusion].
