I'm a software engineer building an autonomous delivery robot for a hospital ward, and my team is arguing about how to encode "avoid side effects" into its planning layer. One proposal is to use a counterfactual deviation metric: we compare the ward's state trajectory with the robot present against a baseline trajectory without it, and we constrain divergence in physical layout, noise levels, and staff movement patterns to stay within a tight epsilon bound. The idea is that we never have to specify what good care or patient dignity fully means; we just preserve the status quo within a narrow band. My concern is that selecting which state variables go into the trajectory—noise versus emotional atmosphere versus institutional routines—already presupposes a normative picture of what matters in the ward. So is this genuinely a value-neutral engineering specification, or is it a hidden form of value specification disguised as a mathematical constraint? I want to understand whether impact measures can be defined independently of a complete value framework, or whether the choice of baseline and metric inevitably imports the very commitments we are trying to avoid.
