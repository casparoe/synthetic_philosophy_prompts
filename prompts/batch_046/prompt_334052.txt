Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a “positive-integration realist” and a “negative-integration constructivist” on whether left and right name real distinctions and what holds each side together.

The realist holds that left-right coalitions are cross-culturally stable groupings organized around different weightings of a shared moral-psychological substrate—care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty—so that coherence derives from shared axioms rather than merely shared enemies.

The constructivist holds that the labels are institutionally contingent bundles; apparent coherence is negative integration (common opposition to a salient out-group) combined with path-dependent policy alignments, and the axis dissolves when institutional couplings shift.

Produce a document containing:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims—about the empirical variability of left-right labeling across countries and eras; about temporary coalitional discipline within democratic party systems; about the predictive failure of pure economic self-interest; and about which parts of the dispute are verbal rather than substantive.

2. A disagreements table stating, in wording each camp accepts for its own position: (a) whether coalitions exhibit positive integration (shared first principles) or only negative integration (shared enemies); (b) whether a universal moral substrate explains the recurrence of similar bundles; (c) whether the axis survives institutional decoupling.

3. Two cruxes, each with a concrete case both sides agree bears on it and each side’s explicit prediction:
   - The Scandinavian social-democratic formation: can economic redistribution remain stably decoupled from cultural liberalism? Both agree this tests integration type; record each prediction.
   - The post-communist Eastern European right: can durable right-wing coalitions form without traditional religious or aristocratic bases? Both agree this tests the substrate claim; record each prediction.

4. A concluding identification of any purely verbal disagreement—specifically, whether “real” requires an essentialist metaphysics or only predictive stability—and an exact statement of which part of the debate that concerns.

Keep all formulations neutral and precise; do not request bibliographies, citations, or further reading lists.
