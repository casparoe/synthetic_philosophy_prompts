I'm preparing a discussion guide for a seminar on moral psychology that uses Henrich's WEIRD framework and Moral Foundations Theory to probe whether cross-cultural divergence undermines moral objectivity. Rather than asking participants which label they prefer, I want a taxonomy that sorts them by concrete commitments about three branching questions: whether disagreement between WEIRD and non-WEIRD populations over binding foundations is best explained by different moral concepts or by shared concepts with different weights; whether WEIRD emphasis on care and fairness represents progressive development away from an evolutionary human baseline that properly includes loyalty and authority, or instead a local deviation that suppresses genuinely universal moral reasons; and whether moral objectivity can survive if correct moral reasoning is framework-relative rather than globally convergent. To keep the instrument from being a recitation of standard encyclopedia entries, please treat judgments about binding foundations as analogous to perceptual reports that could be systematically deficient—like color blindness—rather than as mere preference differences.

Please present the taxonomy as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences with no introduction or conclusion, in which every item is a leaf tracing its yes/no path through those three splits, naming the position it represents (or marking it "unnamed"), stating in one sentence its most awkward commitment regarding either the normative status of WEIRD individualism or the legitimacy of cross-cultural moral criticism, and—where a logical combination is missing—briefly diagnosing whether the gap stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or being unexplored. Please also flag which two named positions, if any, this tree fails to separate, and fix the audience as philosophy students with no prior technical vocabulary required.
