Compare the nuclear-weapons analogy and the printing-press analogy for transformative AI, focusing not on scale of impact but on the temporal structure of adoption: does AI development more closely resemble a discrete, controllable threshold event or a diffuse, infrastructural diffusion? To sharpen the comparison, apply the idea of “regulatory visibility asymmetry”—the tendency for governance to target technologies with immediate, localized destructive potential far more aggressively than those that gradually restructure epistemic and social coordination. First, does the nuclear analogy capture this asymmetry because its risks are spectacular and physically localized, or does it misfire because AI lacks any single deployment point analogous to a detonation? Second, where does the Industrial Revolution belong in this taxonomy: is its century-long energy transition (from isolated steam engines to widespread electrification) closer to the printing press’s slow diffusion, or does the sudden appearance of mechanized power provide a threshold moment that collapses the Industrial Revolution analogy back into the nuclear model? I am trying to determine whether these three analogies form a genuine spectrum ordered by adoption dynamics, or whether attention to governance visibility reveals that one of them is conceptually redundant.
