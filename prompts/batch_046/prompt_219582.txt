I sit on the Joint Governance Council for the Marrow Basin, a semi-autonomous region with authority over water allocation and land-use planning. Our charter currently grants deliberative seats only to permanent residents holding property titles. Before Thursday's meeting we must recommend a concrete charter revision addressing three contested claims to membership: seasonal agricultural workers who reside in the basin six months a year but hold no property deeds; residents of a neighboring jurisdiction whose irrigation depends entirely on our upstream releases; and "projected inhabitants"—people forecast by climate-demographic models to live here in 2075—who would be represented not by election but by an appointed panel of scientists. A coalition argues all three groups belong to the demos; others warn that giving seats to forecast-based proxies collapses democratic legitimacy into technocratic prediction.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate. Take a committed position: either defend a principled boundary that excludes at least one of these groups, or propose an integrated membership framework with differentiated voting weights, and explain why your answer is not merely the standard "all affected interests" or "all subjected" formula applied mechanically. Treat seriously the strongest consideration favoring broad inclusion, and specifically address the worry about the proxy-representation seats.

Structure your response in this order and approximate length:
1. Executive Recommendation (about 150 words): the concrete charter rule or amendment you advise, with a time frame for adoption.
2. Philosophical Reasoning (about 300 words): the boundary problem as it arises in this case and why your membership rule fits it.
3. The Strongest Objection (about 200 words): the best argument for including all three groups, engaged honestly rather than dismissed.
4. Response on Technocratic Prediction (about 150 words): whether and why the forecast-based proxy seats undermine democratic legitimacy, consistent with your recommendation.

Keep the tone formal and suitable for circulation to council members. Do not include citations, reading lists, or bibliographies; I will add references myself if needed.
