If the hole argument shows that spacetime points lack haecceities—because diffeomorphic solutions assign different field values to the same point while representing one physical situation—then its force appears to depend on a qualitative criterion of identity: that entities sharing all qualitative properties are numerically identical. Yet quantum mechanics permits numerically distinct particles that are qualitatively identical in every intrinsic and relational respect, which undermines that criterion. So rather than refuting substantivalism, doesn't the hole argument illegitimately import a quantum-mechanically suspect principle into classical spacetime ontology? Shouldn't we conclude that points may retain primitive thisness even when all qualitative properties are permuted by a diffeomorphism?
