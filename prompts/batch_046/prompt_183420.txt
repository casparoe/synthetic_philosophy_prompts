Since my grandmother died last year and left me a box of letters describing her family’s displacement during the collapse of a multi-ethnic state, I have been trying to articulate exactly where I stand on how defeated nations remember themselves. Here is the package I have arrived at, and I want an audit rather than a lecture: (1) A national myth built on military defeat is legitimate only if its narrative structure figures collective survival or resistance to annihilation, not nostalgia for a lost social order. (2) The Lost Cause mythology should be rejected not merely because it is factually inaccurate, but because its plot requires the erasure of slavery as the central cause of secession, and therefore it cannot be redeemed by any present social utility. (3) Masada is acceptable as a living national symbol despite being a suicide-massacre narrative, precisely because it figures resistance to annihilation rather than the restoration of sovereignty. (4) The Dunkirk myth is acceptable in civic education despite obscuring strategic failure, because it models civilian interdependence and evacuation solidarity rather than martial triumph or territorial restoration. (5) The Kosovo epic cycle should be preserved, translated, and taught as oral literature and poetry even when its political instrumentalization by irredentist movements is actively rejected. (6) For national myths, present social effects—whether they foster inclusive solidarity or exclusive grievance—matter more than historical accuracy. (7) Nevertheless, some myths are so factually corrupt, specifically those that deny documented atrocities or fabricate causal narratives that blame victims, that they ought to be actively debunked regardless of any cohesive social function they might serve.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear tense but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real conflicts by severity. For each tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs me elsewhere in the package. Then tell me which single commitment, if revised or dropped, would clean up the most tensions at once. Be specific about whether (6) and (7) can genuinely coexist, whether (1) licenses both (3) and (4) without arbitrary favoritism, and whether (5) collapses into the corruption that (7) identifies. I am not asking for a history of nationalism; I am asking whether my own position hangs together.
