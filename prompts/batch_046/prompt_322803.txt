I advise regional governments on long-horizon institutional design, and I have begun using a macrohistorical lens—specifically the idea that societies cycle through integrative and disintegrative phases driven by the interaction of state fiscal capacity and elite overproduction, in the broad tradition of Turchin's secular-cycle modeling. I need a reusable decision procedure that my team can run on any proposed reform without requiring historical expertise: the ordered questions to ask, the quantitative or qualitative thresholds that distinguish an integrative-stabilizing intervention from a disintegrative one, the explicit stopping rules that yield a clear "do not proceed" recommendation, and the marked hand-off points where ambiguity forces a normative judgment rather than a factual determination. The procedure must handle hard structural tensions—reforms that improve fiscal health while accelerating elite overproduction, or that reduce intra-elite competition while degrading coercive state capacity. Please operationalize this as a stepwise checklist with explicit tests at each node, then apply it to three proposals: (1) a sharply restrictive meritocratic exam that limits entry into the administrative elite, (2) a universal basic income funded by progressive wealth taxation during a period of high inequality and strong state revenue, and (3) the devolution of policing authority to local militias in a region where central state presence is already weak. For each case, trace the procedure to its landing point, flag any counterintuitive or misfiring result, and state precisely what the procedure cannot resolve. Finally, list the conditions under which the framework itself should be suspended because the society in question does not match the model's scope conditions.
