Write a dialogue between two constitutional theorists—Dr. Amara Okonkwo and Professor Julian Reed—debating a niche inversion of constitutional precommitment that Okonkwo terms reflexive entrenchment. In her view, constitutional provisions should carry automatic sunset mechanisms requiring active renewal by successive generations, so that future citizens bind the present rather than the present binding the future; she argues that classical entrenchment mistakes past drafting effort for an intergenerational promise, thereby institutionalizing the sunk-cost fallacy at the collective level. Reed defends standard Ulysses-style constitutional binding: present generations should constrain future ones through high-amendment thresholds and durable rights provisions, because democratic majorities are temporary and minority rights need protection from them. Okonkwo responds that Reed conflates procedural sunk costs—the labor already invested in framing—with reliance-based commitments, which are the only legitimate grounds for preserving rules, and that reflexive mechanisms can honor the latter without fossilizing the former. Reed counters that allowing provisions to lapse by default destroys the framework of expectation that makes long-term planning and rights-protection possible, and that treating constitutional investment as a fallacy ignores that constitutions are structural preconditions rather than consumer goods. Okonkwo then asks whether Reed's position can survive without implicitly appealing to the very historical investment he claims not to value: if the only reason to preserve an entrenched clause is future-oriented protection, why does the clause's origin matter at all? Reed should answer by distinguishing between the origin of a rule and its continuing functional justification.

The dialogue should feature real back-and-forth: each speaker must respond directly to the specific point just made, not deliver generic speeches. Neither should be a strawman; both should be philosophically sharp and concede valid distinctions where appropriate.
