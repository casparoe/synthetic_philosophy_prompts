Write a villanelle about the repugnant conclusion in population axiology. Do not treat it as a settled paradox to be named; let the form do the arguing. Use the two refrains for the competing assessments of the same hypothetical comparison—one refrain voicing the total view’s arithmetic that lives of barely positive welfare increase aggregate value, the other voicing the intuitive recoil that a world of billions in minimal well-being is not better than a world of far fewer in flourishing. Rotate these through the tercets so that each repetition finds the other already present, unable to displace it. In the final quatrain, bring both refrains together without resolving them, and let the rhyme scheme’s closure mirror the structure of the critical-level escape: the claim that there is a neutral welfare threshold below which lives do not contribute value, and that placing that threshold is necessarily arbitrary. The poem should be precise enough that a philosopher of population ethics recognizes the move from total to average to critical-level reasoning, and it should leave the tension open rather than sermonizing. Keep to nineteen lines, strict villanelle form, with no explanatory apparatus.
