In philosophy seminars people often compare counterpart theory to casting different actors for Hamlet in each production: the role is the same, the bodies differ, so there's no single 'real' Hamlet travelling between stages. They use it to argue there's no transworld identity, just similarity. How far does the theatre analouge hold? Give me the correspondence table — director, script, audience expectation, essential lines — then where it breaks: can an actor improvise, can a show be cancelled, does the audience care which body they saw? Crucially, does it smuggle in the idea that worlds are staged like plays, which is exactly the opposite of Lewis's claim that they are uncreated? Say whether the analogy actually supports rejecting transworld identity or just restates it in theatrical clothes. Answer in British English, please.
