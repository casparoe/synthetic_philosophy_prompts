I'm working through my own position on grand narratives of human history before a seminar presentation, and I would like an audit of whether the commitments actually cohere. I accept Graeber and Wengrow's argument in The Dawn of Everything that agriculture was not a singular, irreversible threshold but a prolonged, reversible process shaped by deliberate political choices about seasonal mobility and settlement. At the same time, I think Diamond's environmental determinism in Guns, Germs, and Steel correctly explains why macro-geographical conditions made large-scale state formation more probable in Eurasia, without rendering any particular local outcome inevitable. I also hold that Harari's narrative in Sapiens is methodologically flawed because it treats shared fictions—myths, in his terminology—as causal agents that enable cooperation at scale, without specifying the micro-mechanisms by which collective beliefs produce institutional persistence. Nevertheless, I want to defend a functionalist reinterpretation of Harari: if we read these shared fictions not as top-down causes but as coordination devices that stabilize particular settlement patterns against dispersal, then his framework becomes compatible with Graeber and Wengrow's emphasis on conscious choice. I further maintain that macro-scale framing is epistemically necessary—without it we cannot account for why similar ecological conditions yielded different institutional outcomes—yet I insist that any legitimate grand narrative must remain revisable by micro-historical counterexamples. Finally, I reject both the noble-savage egalitarianism criticized by Graeber and Wengrow and the Hobbesian default-to-hierarchy they associate with evolutionary anthropology; my middle view is that hierarchy is a contingent institutional solution to coordination problems that emerge specifically at scale, though I worry this sounds like the hidden functionalism I criticized in Harari.

Please assess which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts dissolve on closer inspection, and what the least costly revision would be. In particular, I need you to take a definite stance on three issues: whether accepting Diamond's probabilistic determinism undermines my acceptance of Graeber and Wengrow's anti-inevitability thesis; whether my functionalist rescue of Harari is consistent with my rejection of reified collective beliefs as causal agents; and whether my middle position on hierarchy collapses into hidden functionalism or genuinely avoids both the egalitarian and Hobbesian extremes. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging, identify the single revision that would clean up the most inconsistencies, and tell me whether the package is salvageable as a coherent multi-scale methodology or whether one commitment must be abandoned.
