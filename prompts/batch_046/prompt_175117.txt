I serve on a hospital capacity-assessment panel that must determine whether elderly patients with severe episodic memory impairment retain legal decision-making competence when they depend on elaborate external cognitive scaffolds—digitized life-logs, family-curated prompt systems, and voice-activated notebooks—that they navigate fluently but neither maintain nor fully comprehend. Standard assessments treat cognition as skull-bound, yet in practice these patients integrate such tools into their reasoning in ways that blur the boundary between biological memory and environmental support; I need a reusable evaluation procedure grounded in the extended mind thesis that distinguishes genuine cognitive extension, where an external system operates with the accessibility and reliability of internal recall, from mere instrumental reliance, where the patient is simply parroting outputs they cannot correct, explain, or override.

The procedure should be executable within a single ninety-minute clinical session without specialized technical staff: present the ordered functional-parity tests or questions, specify what pattern of response counts as sufficient at each stage, state the explicit stopping rules that mandate a finding of retained competence, the stopping rules that mandate a finding of lost competence regardless of scaffold-assisted performance, and identify precisely where philosophical ambiguity about the conditions for “genuine extension” forces the evaluator back into subjective judgment rather than mechanical application. Apply it to two contrasting cases: first, a patient who co-designed her retrieval architecture, demonstrates spontaneous error-correction when outputs conflict with her residual beliefs, and exhibits retrieval dynamics indistinguishable from biological memory; second, a patient who produces correct answers solely through a family-managed device he cannot operate independently, explain, or override, where the procedure should yield the unwelcome conclusion that competence is absent despite fluent surface performance. Deliver the response in no more than one dense page, include no bibliographies or citations, and flag explicitly which steps are judgment calls disguised as procedural rules.
