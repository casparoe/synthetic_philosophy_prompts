Here are my verdicts:

1. You do RLHF plus constitutional AI so the model genuinely internalizes being helpful and harmless. That's alignment work, not control, and I'm totally fine with it.

2. You keep it airgapped with no outbound connections and every output gets reviewed by a human before it hits the world. This is clearly a control measure, but it feels like basic hygiene -- I'm okay with it.

3. You install a hidden tripwire in the architecture that wipes the weights if interpretability tools detect patterns associated with deception or long-term planning. I think this is wrong -- it treats the model like a prisoner you don't trust.

4. You run two instances in adversarial debate, one instructed to find flaws in the other's reasoning, and only recieve outputs they converge on. I actually like this -- feels like quality control, not paranoia.

5. You architecturally prevent the model from ever forming representations of itself or its training context -- no self-model, no meta-cognition. This feels like lobotomizing it, and I'm not sure if that's okay or not.

The tension is killing me: case 3 and 4 both assume deception is possible, but I hate 3 and love 4. Case 2 and 5 both restrict capabilities, but 2 is hygiene and 5 feels like mutilation.
