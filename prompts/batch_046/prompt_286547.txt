I've been thinking about how philosophical poems usually just name-drop Hegel's master-slave thing without actually tracking the logic. I was wondering if you could write a villanelle that really follows the dialectic's structure, not just mentions it. Specifically, the two refrains should be the master's claim to pure self-certainty through consumption and the slave's counter-claim through labor on the formed object - like, one about being recognized without working, the other about achieving being-for-itself only in the shaped thing. The form should do the conceptual work: the refrains take turns through the stanzas, then appear together in the last two lines, but that meeting shouldn't resolve into happy mutual recognition. Instead I want it to show a failed synthesis, since in thier respective positions the master remains stuck in immediate desire while the slave gets the concept of freedom through work but still lacks actual social recognition.

Can you weave in stoicism as the slave's first false escape somewhere in the middle? Keep the rhyme scheme strict but let the meter stay loose and conversational. I want it accurate enough that someone who knows the Phenomenology can tell exactly which line belongs to which consciousness and why they end up adjacent without reconciling - maybe the master is still talking about pure being, the slave about the universal in the object. No need for citations or anything, just make it a good poem that actually thinks.
