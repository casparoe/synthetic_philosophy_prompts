My colleague analyzes rational herding and has judged five cases. First, diners choose a crowded restaurant over an empty adjacent one, inferring private information from the visible line — she labels this an information cascade. Second, institutional investors purchase a stock despite private valuation models showing overpricing, after observing specific large orders placed by named firms — cascade. Third, researchers converge on a theoretical claim through fully independent replication with no knowledge of others' interim results — not a cascade. Fourth, office workers adopt an inefficient workflow because they see particular respected peers using it, though they privately prefer the old system — cascade. Fifth, jurors in an open-deliberation trial cast sequential votes aligned with earlier ones, but discuss evidence fully; she denies this is a cascade, arguing that transparent reasoning severs the informational dependency. Now consider a decentralized prediction market in which all bets are anonymous and only a reputation-weighted aggregate price is visible; a trader holds strong private analysis that the price is wrong, yet shifts her position with the trend because the weighted aggregate feels authoritative, with no individual actors identifiable. Predict my colleague's verdict. Is she implicitly running a rule that requires observable, identifiable predecessors, or one that allows pure aggregate influence? State which further scenario — one with fully transparent individual reasoning but anonymous aggregate display, or one with hidden reasoning but named sequential actors — would best separate the two rules.
