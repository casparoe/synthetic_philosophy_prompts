Standard case: I could not have come from a different sperm and egg; that specific zygote seems essential to me. IVF substitution: if my parents had used IVF and implanted a different embryo formed from their same gametes, I think I might still exist — or at least I'm genuinely unsure, which already strains simple zygote essentialism. Chimeric fusion: if two embryos fused in utero and I developed with cells from both, I'm still me, so origin can't require being a single zygote. Synthetic pairing: if scientists derived gametes from my parents' stem cells but paired them differently to produce a genetic sibling, I'm clearly not that sibling; but if they paired the exact same derived sperm and egg in a different temporal sequence, I don't know whether that matters. Cloning: a clone from my somatic cells isn't me, but I can't tell if that's because the origin is wrong or because the causal path is wrong. Is there a principle that captures these verdicts, or must I split the difference? In particular, how should I resolve the tension between the chimeric case, where fused origins don't destroy identity, and the IVF case, where substitution creates uncertainty?
