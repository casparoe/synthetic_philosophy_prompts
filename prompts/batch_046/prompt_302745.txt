(1) i'm with willaimson on anti-luminosity for physical conditions: if i'm cold there's always a nearby state where i'm barely not cold but still feel it, so i can't actually know i'm cold. (2) but i think phenomenal states are different: if i'm in severe pain i do know it, and the margin-for-error worry just doesn't apply because the feeling is self-intimating. (3) i also think ambiguous-figure cases (like the duck-rabbit) show an "interpretive luminosity" — you always know which interpretation you're seeing even though the raw sensory input is identical — and this is the right model for how phenomenal knowledge works, not some direct access to raw qualia. (4) i reject the KK principle across the board: knowing p doesn't automatically give you knowledge that you know p, especially for empirical stuff. (5) i take safety to be necessary for knowledge, but defined narrowly: you know p only if in all nearest worlds where you form the belief via the same cognitive process, p is true.
