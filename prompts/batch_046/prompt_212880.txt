I'm trying to work out what my gut reactions to fiction actually track, and whether there's one principled story behind them or if I'm just being inconsistent. Here are my verdicts case by case.

Standard horror flick with a rubber monster: I'm scared for maybe ten seconds, then I remember it's latex and I'm mostly fine, but the initial jolt feels real.

Reading Anna Karenina: I feel actual pity for Anna, even though I know she was never born.

Now some weirder ones. Then there's an AI that spits out a sad monologue about a lost sibling. I know it's just weights predicting tokens, no human intended the sadness. I feel moved the first time. But then I see the exact same text was randomly generated by a Markov chain in a demo, and the feeling vanishes, even though the words haven't changed. So my response tracks something about intended design, not just content?

Another pair that's hard to square. I feel intense grief for a fictional dog that dies in a well-written novel. But when I watch a nature documentary showing a real wolf pack killing a real deer, I feel almost nothing—just detached observation. So fiction can trigger stronger emotional response than reality. But then: in a blockbuster where a city is destroyed and millions of implied people die, I feel almost nothing. Meanwhile, in a small indie game where a single fictional tree—presented as alive and aware—gets burned, I feel devastated. So I respond to fictional individuals and to fictional "living" landscapes, but fictional masses leave me cold.
