For my independent philosophy project, I am comparing four competing frameworks for explaining subjective unity without positing a Cartesian theater. The frameworks are: (1) Dennett's original multiple drafts model, in which parallel distributed processes generate competing narrative fragments with no central stage; (2) a linguistic-report variant, in which draft consolidation occurs only when an external linguistic report is demanded, leaving pre-linguistic experience genuinely fragmented; (3) the global workspace theory stripped of its homuncular observer, retaining a single broadcast architecture but treating the broadcast as mechanistic access rather than conscious presentation to a self; and (4) a hybrid "staged drafts" account I developed with my study group, in which drafts compete not for entry into consciousness but for narrative coherence within a pre-subjective integration layer, so that what feels like a unified stream is actually the winning coherent narrative before any first-person access occurs. Please rank these four accounts from 1 (most plausible) to 4 (least plausible) according to how well they dissolve the theater intuition while preserving the phenomenology of temporal unity. For each, assign a plausibility score out of 10 and provide a brief justification focusing on parsimony and explanatory scope.
