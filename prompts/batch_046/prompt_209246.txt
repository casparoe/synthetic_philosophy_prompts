For my seminar on technology and society, I have been asked to articulate where I stand on value alignment for artificial intelligence, and I would like to know whether these commitments actually cohere. Here is the profile I have sketched out. First, I think that an aligned system should aggregate values across all affected stakeholders rather than defaulting to the preferences of those who build or purchase it; fairness in alignment, on my view, requires something like global weighting rather than consumer sovereignty. Second, I subscribe to a form of liberal neutrality: I do not think the AI should embed, promote, or rank-order any particular comprehensive conception of the good life, whether religious, secular-perfectionist, or communal.

Third, and this is a notion I have been trying to work out on my own, I think aggregation should be coherence-weighted. By that I mean that when the system pools values, it should discount the influence of value-sets that contain explicit internal contradictions—imagine a user who demands both total transparency and absolute secrecy in the same request—while preserving full weight for sets that are structurally consistent, irrespective of whether I personally endorse their content. Fourth, I believe that in domains of deep first-order disagreement—say, whether an AI ought ever to offer guidance on spiritual or existential questions—the appropriate posture is procedural rather than substantive: the system should facilitate autonomous choice among recognized options instead of computing a single correct answer. Fifth, I hold that small-scale or locally deployed models should be permitted to align with the specific normative commitments of their immediate communities, even when those commitments are parochial and would likely be diluted or overridden under global aggregation.

Do these five positions hang together, or am I helping myself to incompatible motivations? I am particularly unsure about whether coherence-weighting can be squared with liberal neutrality—since judging which value-sets are contradictory seems to require the system to apply its own rationalist standards, which might themselves function as a hidden comprehensive doctrine. I also wonder whether my commitment to global aggregation is undermined by my willingness to grant local exemptions. At the same time, I have a nagging sense that my proceduralist commitment in the fourth point might conflict with aggregation altogether: if we genuinely pool values, shouldn't the result sometimes be a substantive resolution rather than perpetual facilitation? I want an audit rather than a lecture: go through each pair that is genuinely in tension and rank them by severity, identify which conflicts only appear serious and dissolve on inspection, specify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency for each real tension, and then tell me which single change to my profile would clean up the most problems at once.
