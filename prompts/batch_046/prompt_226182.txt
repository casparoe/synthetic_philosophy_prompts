I hold a middle-ground position on macro-historical periodization: the conventional triad of “Middle Ages,” “Renaissance,” and “Scientific Revolution” is not a discovery of pre-given joints in reality but a nineteenth-century historiographical scaffold—shaped by nationalist narratives and Whig teleology—yet it is not therefore pure fiction. There are measurable discontinuities in institutional knowledge-practices (the shift from monastic scriptoria to university disputation to court-sponsored experimental academies, and the changing social status of artisanal craft knowledge relative to scholastic authority) that justify retaining modified period labels, provided we treat them as tracking family resemblances among porous clusters rather than as ontologically bounded epochs.
