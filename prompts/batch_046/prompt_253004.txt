Before I assess any explanation of competence without comprehension — the inversion Darwin traced in evolutionary adaptation and Turing traced in computing machinery — I want the complete desiderata first. Write the specification: what an adequate theory in this area must be able to deliver, with the case or distinction that motivates each requirement. The list should include: (1) how it explains complex, adaptive functional success arising without foresight, mental representation, or semantic grasp (the evolutionary and computational instances respectively); (2) the boundary conditions that separate genuine competence from mere reliability, and absence of comprehension from merely hidden comprehension; (3) the distinctions it must preserve — for instance, between syntactic manipulation and semantic interpretation, between evolutionary time-scale design and real-time problem solving, and between having functional status and having propositional knowledge about one's task; (4) consistency constraints with established results in evolutionary biology and computer science, such as that design explanations need not invoke intentional agency and that behavioral adequacy does not entail internal understanding; and (5) the explanatory burden it must meet, specifically why the inversion is stable rather than parasitic on some underlying comprehension it denies. For each item, indicate whether it is non-negotiable for adequacy in general or a camp-specific preference (functionalist, biological, anti-mechanist, etc.). Then flag any subset of desiderata that looks jointly unsatisfiable — for example, if full mechanistic transparency conflicts with emergent functionality that exceeds component-level understanding, or if a strict prohibition on all internal representational structure conflicts with accounting for sustained adaptive success. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating conflicts rather than on evaluation of particular theories, though a brief note on how Darwinian evolutionary accounts, Turing-style functionalist accounts, modern statistical-learning or connectionist models, and intentionality-based objections fare against the list is welcome.
