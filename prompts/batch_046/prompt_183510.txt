Recently I came across an online essay summarising David Hume's position on this question, and I have been trying to apply its logic to my own practical judgements. The essay said something like this: 'Hume maintained that beauty is not a property belonging to objects themselves but only a sentiment or feeling that arises in the mind of the observer, which makes aesthetic judgement fundamentally subjective. At the same time, he acknowledged that certain people possess a naturally finer sensibility, and through repeated comparison and practice these individuals develop into what he called an "ideal critic". Because these ideal critics tend to converge in their verdicts, Hume treated their shared agreement as if it constituted an objective standard of taste. The problem is that this is plainly circular: one identifies an ideal critic solely by observing that he agrees with others already designated as ideal, so Hume has not discovered an external standard at all. He has merely relocated subjective preference inside a small, self-certifying elite, and for that reason contemporary aesthetics has largely discarded his framework as an unjustifiable form of cultural hierarchy.'
