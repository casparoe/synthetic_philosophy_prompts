I'm trying to map out which transcendental arguments actually work and which collapse into disguised empirical claims or rhetorical moves. By transcendental I mean arguments that identify some feature as a condition of possibility for a domain—experience, agency, reasoning—such that denying it undermines the very framework in which the denial is articulated, rather than merely making the world less convenient.

Here are my verdicts, and I cannot see the principle that sorts them.

First, Kant's argument that a unitary thinking subject must be able to accompany all representations: this strikes me as successful. Denying it doesn't just complicate psychology; it seems to dissolve the very idea of a unified field of experience. Verdict: succeeds.

Second, Strawson's argument that objective experience requires spatiotemporally persistent objects: same structural merit. Without persistence through change, the distinction between appearance and reality collapses, and the concept of an objective world becomes empty. Verdict: succeeds, though perhaps less strictly necessary than the first.

Third, the argument that moral responsibility requires libertarian free will, on the grounds that determinism eliminates the could-have-done-otherwise condition and thus renders moral judgment meaningless: this feels like failure disguised as transcendental reasoning. Denying libertarian freedom doesn't make moral judgment impossible; it just invites a compatibilist reconstruction. The argument imports a substantive premise about what responsibility requires. Verdict: fails.

Fourth, the Wittgensteinian argument that linguistic meaning requires a shared form of life, making a private language conceptually impossible: I am ambivalent. There is a transcendental flavor, but I worry it overreaches—perhaps a sparse, non-social system could develop rule-following behavior without the full human form of life. Verdict: partial success at best, likely too strong.

Fifth, here is the case that keeps me oscillating. Consider what I will call the care-condition argument: for any system to genuinely plan, as opposed to running an optimization algorithm over a future-state reward function, it must possess a non-derivative awareness of its own future non-existence as a live, affectively charged possibility. The claim is that without this temporal horizon of finitude, planning collapses into static preference-ordering over world-states; the transcendental structure of care—being oriented toward a future that matters precisely because it can be lost—is absent. Denying this condition, the argument says, does not merely alter the planner's psychology; it eliminates the conceptual space in which planning for one's own future is distinguishable from computing an optimal trajectory. Verdict: I keep going back and forth. It sounds structurally deep, but it may be anthropocentric projection dressed up as conceptual analysis.

Sixth, the argument that scientific realism is transcendentally necessary because the practice of science presupposes the existence of unobservable entities: clear failure. This is inference to the best explanation mischaracterized. Denying realism does not undermine science; it only forces an instrumentalist interpretation. Verdict: fails.

Is there a coherent principle—perhaps that a successful transcendental argument must show the denial produces a performative contradiction within the very domain being constituted, without importing substantive normative or empirical premises about what that domain must contain—that captures cases one, two, and six, explains why three and six fail, and decides case five? Or must I drop one of my verdicts?

Please propose one or more precise principles, state which case motivates each clause, predict how your best principle handles at least two unlisted cases (for example, a transcendental argument that other minds are necessary conditions for self-consciousness, and one claiming that consciousness requires embodiment), and be explicit about which of my verdicts—especially case five—your preferred account cannot accommodate.
