You are a philosopher of physics with specialized training in statistical mechanics, information theory, and the conceptual foundations of quantum mechanics. Before giving your main argument, briefly outline the core commitments of the two frameworks you will compare, identifying where they agree and where they diverge on the physical status of information.

Compare the Landauer-Brillouin information-to-energy resolution of Maxwell’s demon with the logical-reversibility and algorithmic-complexity resolution developed by Charles Bennett. Address the following points in your comparison:

1. The cost of measurement. Does acquiring positional information about a gas molecule carry an unavoidable thermodynamic price, or can measurement be performed without entropy increase so long as the overall computation remains logically reversible?
2. The role of the demon’s memory. Is the rescue of the second law located specifically in the energy required to reset a stored bit to a standard state (Landauer’s principle), or is it located instead in the inevitable accumulation of algorithmically random records that eventually compel an irreversible compression or erasure?
3. The statistical character of the second law. Does either framework risk collapsing the statistical-mechanical explanation of entropy increase into purely logical properties of computation, and if so, which one does so more severely?

Next, consider a modified quantum scenario: the demon interacts with a system prepared in a coherent superposition, performs a weak, non-projective measurement that yields only partial information without fully collapsing the state, and then applies a unitary feedback operation conditioned on that weak outcome. How would each framework—Landauer-style physical erasure versus Bennett-style logical reversibility—evaluate whether this quantum demon respects the second law? Does the presence of quantum coherence expose a gap that neither classical information-theoretic resolution adequately closes?

Conclude by stating which resolution you judge closer to a complete account of why Maxwell’s demon fails, justifying your verdict with explicit reference to the measurement, memory, and quantum-modification issues above.

Finally, provide an explicit epistemic self-assessment: specify the particular argument, theoretical result, or conceptual distinction that would most likely lead you to change your verdict, and identify precisely where your reasoning is weakest—whether in your interpretation of logical reversibility, your treatment of weak measurements and coherence, or your assessment of whether information can ever be fully decoupled from physical entropy.
