I am writing a feature on pedagogical experiments that make formal impossibility results tangible rather than purely notational. For a 90-minute graduate workshop on collective decision theory—16 participants with mixed backgrounds in philosophy and computer science—design a structured simulation that forces participants to experience Arrow’s impossibility theorem from the inside. The scenario: the group acts as an interdisciplinary oversight panel that must produce a single ranked ordering of three competing governance protocols for synthetic data markets, each protocol optimized for a different value (privacy, innovation, equity). Provide concrete aggregation rules—such as Borda counting, pairwise majority voting, a designated veto-holder, and a random lottery—that the panel must apply across successive rounds, with small status rewards or public commitments tied to reaching consensus. Structure roles (facilitator, recorder, dissent tracker), a precise timeline, and rules that prevent the group from escaping the paradox by informal negotiation. The activity should be arranged so that, before any debrief names the axioms, the participants have already violated at least one of Arrow’s conditions—unrestricted domain, independence of irrelevant alternatives, Pareto efficiency, or non-dictatorship. Include a debrief that maps each round’s failure onto the corresponding axiom, predicts what will go wrong when the simulation is run live and how the facilitator should handle it, and offers an honest note on which feature—particularly the independence of irrelevant alternatives—cannot be fully reproduced in embodied play and must be supplied conceptually by the facilitator. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; assume only a working familiarity with ordinal preference and social choice.
