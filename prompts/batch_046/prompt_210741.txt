Design a decision tree that sorts philosophical positions on the essence of the letterform a—the problem central to the Letter Spirit project—using only conceptual distinctions, not author names or citations. Begin with a concrete diagnostic case: a glyph possessing the single-story loop typical of a but rotated 180 degrees, rendered in an unknown handwritten style, with a tail that loops back into the bowl, produced by someone who intended to write the letter o. The tree should branch on three questions that genuinely divide theories: first, whether the glyph counts as an a if competent readers consistently identify it as such despite its anomalous geometry; second, whether the essence resides in a static geometric or topological invariant, in the dynamic kinematic sequence of its production, or in a distributed family of exemplars with no privileged center; third, whether style is an accidental feature or constitutive of letter-identity. Each leaf should carry a named position (geometric essentialism, topological invariantism, kinematic-spirit view, family-resemblance anti-essentialism, reader-functionalism, or an unnamed combination) and one sentence describing its most awkward commitment—such as being forced to deny a-status to common typographic variants or to accept clearly alien glyphs. Map out the logical space: list the combinations of answers the three questions allow, place the named positions in them, and flag any empty branches, briefly diagnosing whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Finally, note any pair of leaves that remain indistinguishable on these three questions alone.
