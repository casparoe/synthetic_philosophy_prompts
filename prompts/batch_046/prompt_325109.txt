For my advanced-placement philosophy essay on physical time, I want to examine whether the psychological arrow is relative to an observer's local thermodynamic environment rather than anchored globally. I have been considering a hypothetical, fully isolated subsystem—maintained, perhaps, by continuous quantum error-correction that exports entropy to an external reservoir—within which the local entropy gradient runs monotonically downward with respect to the cosmic background. If conscious observers were embedded entirely inside such a region, with no causal access to the wider universe, would they not necessarily form memories of events that we, observing from outside, would classify as lying in our future, and anticipate events that we classify as lying in our past? More precisely, is the asymmetry between cause and effect merely a relational feature determined by whatever local entropy slope dominates the substrate of cognition, or does a globally privileged direction—fixed by the universe's low-entropy initial boundary—render our orientation objectively correct, regardless of what occurs inside an isolated pocket with a reversed gradient?
