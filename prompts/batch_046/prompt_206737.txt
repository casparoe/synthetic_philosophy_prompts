I am drafting a fellowship proposal for a three-month conceptual project on a neglected structural feature of the epistemic regress problem. The familiar framing forces a choice among foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism, with each bearing canonical liabilities: arbitrary basic beliefs, vicious circularity, or an uncompletable chain of reasons. Rather than defend one horn, I want to investigate whether the trilemma dissolves—partially—once we distinguish between propositional justification, which concerns whether a belief has adequate support in principle, and agential justification, which concerns whether a particular agent can actually cite that support in defense of holding it. My working conjecture is that infinitism may be defensible for the propositional dimension, since reasons need not terminate if every link remains available in principle, while a modest form of foundationalism may govern the agential dimension, since an agent must eventually stand on beliefs they can actually invoke. The proposal needs to articulate this separation precisely, explain why standard treatments conflate the two dimensions, and show how hybrid positions avoid the usual objections without collapsing back into pure forms.

The deliverable should be a one-page research plan suitable for a small philosophy foundation: the exact question stated without reliance on citation-heavy literature reviews; the conceptual method, involving case analysis of justification-attributions in legal reasoning, scientific peer review, and ordinary belief-defense rather than empirical data collection; a realistic twelve-week scope broken into stages; a clear account of what success looks like (a stable taxonomy of hybrid regress positions with non-collapsing criteria) and what failure looks like (if every hybrid reduces analytically to one of the three pure options, rendering the separation empty); and a brief reply to the anticipated charge that this is mere relabeling of existing views. I want the framing to be technical enough to satisfy specialists but accessible enough that the foundation can see why funding conceptual work on justification structure matters for practical domains where people must defend beliefs without infinite resources.
