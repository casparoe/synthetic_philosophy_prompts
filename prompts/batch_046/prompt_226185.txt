In my seminars on distributive justice I have repeatedly noticed an argumentative pattern for which I do not have a precise term. When students discuss whether goods such as human kidneys or emergency supplies during acute shortages should be sold directly for profit, they often concede that the relevant resources are already distributed by mechanisms that implicitly price risk—through labor contracts that reward dangerous work, through insurance pools that reflect expected need, or through geographic mobility sorted by income—yet they insist that an explicit, direct cash price for the good itself is morally distinct and objectionable. The objection is not that direct pricing produces worse outcomes, since the indirect mechanisms can yield identical or greater inequality, nor is it exactly the familiar claim that monetary exchange corrupts the intrinsic value of the good or crowds out altruism, because they allow that value and generosity to survive intact in the hidden cases. Rather, the worry seems to be that direct pricing makes a particular social logic visible and explicit—it "puts a number on" something that ought to remain outside explicit valuation—while indirect pricing keeps that logic obscured and therefore tolerable. This does not map cleanly onto the standard literature on commodification corruption or moral crowding out, since those arguments concern what market exchange does to a good's meaning or to motivational structures, not the formal visibility or directness of the price mechanism itself. Does this configuration—where moral repugnance tracks the explicitness of pricing rather than its presence, and where indirect commodification is permitted precisely because it is mediated and hidden—have an established name or literature? If there is none, how should its boundaries be drawn: does it depend on transparency, on the social salience of the exchange form, or on a distinction between direct and mediated valuation? I am not asking for a bare label but for the carving itself—what this phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie, together with the nearest established concepts and how they differ.
