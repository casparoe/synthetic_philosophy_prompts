I'm on a hospital ethics board and we review proposals for staged cortical prosthetic replacement in early-stage dementia patients -- swapping out maybe 10% of neural tissue for synthetic analogues every six months until the brain is mostly artificial. The guiding idea is gradualist preservation: identity survives if there's continuous first-person integration across stages, not just functional match at the end. I need a decision procedre my board can run in 90 minutes, with questions in order, what counts as sufficient answer, and clear stops where we say no versus where we punt to values. Make it operational: include an interpolation-window check (there must be a 90-day co-active overlap where old and new substrate both contribute to memory formation), flag cases where functional continuity is high but the patient reports feeling like a different person between stages, and say exactly where the procedure misfires. Run it on two proposals: a standard six-stage replacement over three years, and a rapid two-stage version where the answer should come out negative even though the family wants yes. Be honest about which steps are just judgment calls in procedural clothing, and say what the board still has to decide by hand.
