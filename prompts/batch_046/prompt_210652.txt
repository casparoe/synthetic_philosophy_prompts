If we take seriously the idea of the self as a center of narrative gravity—not a metaphysical substance but an abstract organizational point around which autobiographical, intentional, and affective trajectories converge—several conceptual questions remain genuinely open, and they pull in different directions. One cluster concerns ontology: can narrative gravity be genuinely plural, with multiple partly incompatible sub-centers coexisting within one biological or artificial system, or does coherence require a single dominant thread? Another concerns epistemology: is first-person access to one's own narrative center always reconstructive and confabulatory, or are there non-derivative ways a center can register its own boundaries and discontinuities? A third concerns design and normativity: if one were to engineer an artificial system to maintain a center of narrative gravity, which constraints—temporal continuity, emotional valence integration, social accountability, resistance to fragmentation—would be essential rather than decorative?
