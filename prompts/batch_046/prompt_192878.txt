You are an expert philosopher of biology specializing in the architecture of evolutionary explanations, and I want you to think carefully—before constructing anything—about the logical space of answers to the spandrels critique. I am designing a diagnostic instrument for an advanced seminar: not a quiz that asks students which label they prefer, but a decision tree that sorts them by concrete commitments about specific cases and explanatory practices. Take a moment to brainstorm the full combination space of answers, note which combinations are actually defended in the literature, flag where two named views might share the same profile, and only then present the tree.

The domain is the cluster of positions surrounding Gould and Lewontin’s spandrels critique of adaptationism. The tree should begin with a concrete scenario rather than an abstract allegiance question: imagine researchers discover a deep-sea morphological feature that is structurally coupled to a thermoregulatory adaptation but serves no independent selective function of its own, and genetic and developmental evidence indicates it arose as an unavoidable architectural byproduct rather than through genetic drift or direct selection. From this case, build successive branching nodes that ask whether (a) one is committed to explaining every observable trait by reference to current fitness contribution; (b) an architectural byproduct that is later co-opted for a new function thereby loses its spandrel status, retains a historical one, or enters some third category; (c) developmental constraint operates as a genuinely separate explanatory category from spandrel-talk or collapses into it; (d) the existence of spandrels undermines the adaptationist research program or merely delimits its proper domain; and (e) exaptation requires that the co-opted trait originally had some prior adaptive function, or whether a pure byproduct can be exapted without ever having been an adaptation. Each node should be phrased as a claim about the case or about explanatory practice, not as “do you agree with Gould?”

The leaves should include strong pan-adaptationism, pluralistic adaptationism (spandrels acknowledged but contained within an otherwise selectionist framework), the strong spandrels view, a developmental-constraint-first position, a neutralist or drift-first position that denies spandrel-talk is necessary, and any genuinely unoccupied combinations—such as a view that accepts both spandrels and exaptation but denies that selection can ever act upon pure byproducts, which may be incoherent or merely unexplored.
