I'm currently writing a paper for a philosophy of science seminar on the limits of empirical adjudication, and I've become fixated on a recurring deadlock in debates over domains that are permanently shielded from observation—not merely distant or technologically out of reach, but structurally insulated so that no measurement could ever penetrate them. Consider two cosmological frameworks that match exactly on every observable prediction within our cosmic horizon. Beyond that horizon, however, they diverge radically in ontology: one posits a uniform continuation of physical law populated by entities of kind A, while the other posits a fundamentally different ontology—entities of kind B arranged in patterns that, by the structure of spacetime itself, can never causally interact with our region. What interests me is not the underdetermination itself, but a specific dialectical pattern that accompanies it. Proponents of each framework argue that their rival's hidden-domain commitment is either metaphysically extravagant or suspiciously sparse, yet they also claim that their own hidden commitment confers explanatory coherence—unity, elegance, or reduced arbitrariness—that somehow radiates backward into the observable domain and strengthens the theory as a whole. At the same time, when pressed on whether this hidden portion is subject to evidential evaluation, both sides retreat to the claim that it is merely metaphysical and therefore beyond empirical reach, which seems inconsistent with their earlier insistence that it carries theoretical weight. Is there an established term in philosophy of science or metaphysics that captures precisely this situation—where ontology identification fails not through temporary ignorance but through structural inaccessibility, coupled with a rhetorical move in which the inaccessible ontology is simultaneously invoked for explanatory virtue and dismissed as empirically irrelevant? If no precise concept exists, could you propose a name and carefully map its boundaries, distinguishing it from standard theory-underdetermination, from conventionalist choice, and from cases in which hidden entities are unknown but not structurally inaccessible?
