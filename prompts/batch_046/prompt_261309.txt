I have been thinking on my own about the moral difference between bringing a suffering person into existence and failing to bring a happy one into existence. The idea is that the first act is wrong while the second omission is not wrong, but I wonder whether this asymmetry depends on harm being certain and severe. Write an essay examining whether the asymmetry still applies when the risk to a potential child is only partial and moderate—say, a significant but not overwhelming chance of chronic mild discomfort rather than guaranteed severe misery—and whether this probabilistic framing changes our obligations about reproduction. Weigh the argument that uncertain, moderate harm weakens or dissolves the asymmetry against the argument that it leaves our negative obligations intact while still imposing no positive duty to create happy lives. Reach a definite conclusion and justify it; do not end on an uncommitted or purely conditional note.
