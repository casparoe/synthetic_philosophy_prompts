Question: Hofstadter in I Am a Strange Loop argues the self is a strange loop — symbols in the brain that loop back and refer to themselves, not a soul stuck in one skull. Imagine two people with direct bidirctional neural links so every memory, feeling, and symbolic update flows both ways instantly. Would Hofstadter say there's now one distributed 'I' spread across both brains, or still two? Explain using his loop idea, and say why substrate independence dosen't automatically mean spatial merging.

Student answer:
Hofstadter's core claim is that an 'I' is just a pattern of symbolic self-reference, like a whirlpool in water. The whirlpool isn't special water; it's the shape of the flow. So if you connect two brains with a link, the flow of symbols can span both, meaning the loop now lives across both brains. That makes one distributed self. He'd also say you could copy the whole pattern onto a computer and it would still be the same person, since there's no hidden essence. If the link gets cut, the pattern splits like an amoeba, leaving two copies that each keep going with their own loop. So the answer is yes, one I while linked, and two separate ones after separation.
