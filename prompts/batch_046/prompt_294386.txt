Weber says the Protestant ethic had a causal role in modern capitalism's rise, yet he builds sociology on verstehen — grasping subjective meaning rather than finding law-like causes. Is there a contradiction between saying culture "caused" an economic system and Weber's anti-positivist method? student answer (around 350 words):
Weber's Protestant Ethic is basically an argument that ideas aren't just superstructure — they can drive history. Calvinist asceticism created the psychological conditions for rational capitalist accumulation, so culture caused capitalism in a direct motivational sense. The concept of elective affinity captures this: the ethic and capitalism had a functional fit, like a key in a lock, where the belief system supplied exactly the discipline capitalism needed to take root.

Because Weber uses verstehen, he replaces statistical laws with meaning-adequacy. But he still needs causal adequacy, which in teh natural sciences means covering-law explanation, whereas in sociology it just means the action was meaningfully appropriate. So values work as causes through internalization — what we could call value-causation — reshaping economic behavior from inside the agent's worldview without any mechanical push.

There's no real tension because Weber rejects functionalism outright. He doesn't care whether asceticism was beneficial for capitalism, only that real believers acted on the calling and the proof of election. Thus macro-level correlation between Protestant regions and capitalist growth lines up with micro-level interpretation. Values are causes, but only through this mediated, meaning-laden route. The thesis is therefore both interpretive and causal, with no leftover contradiction.
