I was arguing in a graduate seminar comparing moral cultures—specifically whether victimhood culture is structurally inferior to dignity culture, or whether it merely reorganizes the same moral goods—when a fellow student interrupted with the line: "Dignity is unconditional, but victimhood culture makes it depend on being wronged." She used it to close off further comparison, implying that any culture linking moral standing to harm is automatically degraded. I think the sentence is doing several kinds of work at once, and I want to be able to separate them. Could you lay out, as a clear menu, the distinct precise theses this slogan could be advancing? Please distinguish at least: (a) metaphysical claims about whether human worth has a source independent of social recognition; (b) normative claims about whether moral status ought to be invariant across contexts; (c) descriptive-psychological claims about how dignity and victimhood cultures actually assign status; and (d) implicit claims about whether victimhood culture confers an epistemic or moral authority that dignity culture withholds. For each reading, indicate whether it is trivial, defensible but substantive, or false; and say explicitly which of these interpretations, if any, actually warrants the conclusion that victimhood culture is morally inferior rather than just different. I am not looking for an author bibliography or a list of sociologists to read—just the conceptual disambiguation I can bring back to the seminar floor.
