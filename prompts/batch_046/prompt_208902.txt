hey i'm putting together a taxomony handout for a rationalist meetup on gradual brain repacement and uploading. i want a decision tree built from the logical space, not just named philosophers—sort people by what they actually say about cases, not by which label they like.

start with this concrete scenario: your neurons get replaced one-by-one with perfect functional silicon equivalents over ten years, and you're awake and remembering everything throughout. then branch on these diagnostic questions:

1. after full replacement, is the survivor you?
2. does the schedule matter—neuron-by-neuron vs big modular chunks vs all-at-once?
3. during intermediate "hybrid" states (say 40% silicon, 60% biological), are you fully present, partly present, or not present at all?
4. if replacements only happen while you're asleep—interleaved with sleep cycles—so you never consciously experience being hybrid, does that change your answer to 3?

map the answer combinations onto positions: hard substrate essentialist, smooth psychological-continuity, the "flicker" view (you vanish during hybrids and maybe return if they stabilize), deflationary/no-self, and that weird graded-presence idea where you're 30% you if 30% replaced. flag empty spots—like can anyone consistently hold that modular chunks kill you but neuron-by-neuron is fine, or is that incoherent? also flag any pairs the tree can't really seperate (e.g. do psychological-continuity and narrative views collapse into the same answers here?).

keep it to about 6 branch nodes, phrase every node as a question answerable without knowing the jargon, and include a brief diagnosis for each unoccupied combination (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored?). no citations or reading lists—this should work for someone who hasn't read the lit. oh and add one extra case: simultaneous replacement of both hemispheres with a one-second gap between them—ask whether that gap breaks identity even if function is restored.
