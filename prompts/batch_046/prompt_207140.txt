In my graduate seminar on liberalism and comprehensive doctrines, I have been defending an accommodationist reading of Rawls's political liberalism: the proviso, together with the burdens of judgment and the distinction between the background culture of civil society and the public political forum, genuinely permits religious citizens to maintain the integrity of their faith without bifurcating their identity, and it does so without smuggling in a crypto-secularist priority of the political over every conception of the good. For the purpose of testing this view I have introduced two framing devices—what I call the asymmetry of translation costs, whereby secular citizens are rarely required to render their deepest normative commitments into shared political values, and the problem of non-justiciable theological residues, being commitments that influence a citizen's political judgment yet resist formulation in terms acceptable to all reasonable citizens. I maintain that neither phenomenon undermines neutrality, because reasonableness applies symmetrically and the proviso regulates only the justification of coercive legislation, not the full range of civic motivation or personal ethics. But I want this position genuinely stress-tested rather than rehearsed. What arguments, conceptual results, concrete institutional cases, or observed patterns of political behavior would count decisively against it? Which predictions does it make that rival secularist or perfectionist liberalisms do not, and has the view been qualified—perhaps by treating every failure of religious translation as mere evidence of unreasonableness—into a framework that risks nothing and therefore explains everything? Assess in particular whether the translation-cost asymmetry and the existence of non-justiciable residues are fatal, merely awkward, or negligible, and take a definite stance on whether the accommodationist reading survives intact, requires substantial revision, or collapses into a franker secularism.

Please respond as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. Each item should identify a specific defeater or protective qualification, state your committed judgment about how decisive it is, and say explicitly whether on that point the view is well-insulated or genuinely vulnerable.
