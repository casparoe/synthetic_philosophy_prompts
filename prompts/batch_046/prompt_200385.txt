I keep seeing people compare chaos in climate models to a deterministic boardgame where you've hidden the dice rolls: the rules never change, but since you can't see the roll, predicting the board state is just guessing. They use this to say climate determinism is dead—if the system is deterministic but we can never shadow the true trajectroy, then predictability and determinism are basicly the same thing and both fail. Can you audit this analogy? Specifically, what maps onto the hidden dice, the fixed rules, and the player? Where does the shadowing lemma actually fit in—does it rescue predictability or kill the analogy? And does the conclusion that determinism collapses without perfect forecasting survive once you separate epistemic chaos from ontic openness?
