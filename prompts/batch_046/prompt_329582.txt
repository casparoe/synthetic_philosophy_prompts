write a short story about juno, a contract mediator in a near-future legal system where future generations have "proxy advocates" with actual standing. she's brokering a deal over a shared geothermal grid: present cities want max extraction now and cheap solar replacement later, but the proxy for people born after 2150 says "later" isn't a reason to matter less. the twist: juno has a professional neural implant that lets her dial her subjective discount rate — she can feel distant harms as strongly as present ones. she discovers its miscalibrated and she's running a hidden pure-preference bias, valuing present discomfort more just because its closer in time, not because of any risk model. she has to finish teh negotiation knowing her own empathy is literally cheating.

keep it 700-900 words, grounded sci-fi, let the philosophy come through concrete clauses — maintenance costs in 2080, risk-sharing, who gets the surplus. don't resolve it with a big speech; maybe she adjusts the deal despite the bias, or notices and still fails. casual tone, no bibliography or "discuss the literature" stuff — just the story.
