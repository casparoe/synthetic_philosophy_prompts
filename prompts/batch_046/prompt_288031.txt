Having recently begun cataloguing my late great-grandfather's private papers — which include detailed administrative correspondence from his tenure as a colonial district officer in the 1920s — I find myself unable to separate my moral revulsion at certain practices he describes from my professional obligation, should I pursue archival history, to understand those practices precisely as he understood them. This has led me to study the longstanding dispute between contextualist anti-presentists, who maintain that historians must recover past agents' own normative frameworks and suspend contemporary moral evaluation, and critical presentists, who argue that historians are ethically bound to apply present standards, particularly when assessing harms that continue to structure present inequalities. I want you to draft the joint statement that these two camps could both sign after a rigorous adversarial collaboration. The document should contain: first, a numbered list of claims both sides genuinely accept concerning the aims of historiography, the accessibility of past mental states, and the relationship between description and evaluation; second, a table setting out the precise disagreements in wording each camp would recognise as its own, including the suspected cruxes — particularly whether moral judgement is essentially indexical to the historian's temporal position, whether a genuinely neutral descriptive vocabulary for harmful practices exists, and how to treat pre-conceptual harm, where past subjects experienced damage they lacked the conceptual resources to name; and third, for each crux, a concrete case drawn from early-twentieth-century colonial administration, for instance an internal memorandum describing forced labour using exclusively bureaucratic terminology, that both sides agree is relevant, together with each side's explicit prediction about what that case demonstrates. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke highly specialised theoretical frameworks that a well-read generalist could not be expected to know; and do not quote any text verbatim — paraphrase all sources. Write in a formal, structured style suitable for publication as a methodological appendix.
