I serve on my university's inter-disciplinary research ethics panel. We have received a joint proposal from the synthetic-biology and cognitive-science units requesting institutional endorsement of a new framework for assigning differential ethical protections to experimental subjects. Rather than retaining a binary threshold between sentient and non-sentient entities, the proposal advances a continuous "phenomenal-affective gradient" scored along three axes: integrated information, predictive-error minimisation within homeostatic loops, and behavioural flexibility. Under this scheme, protections are graded rather than uniform: engineered thalamic-cortical organoids occupy an intermediate tier with partial oversight; social insects sit lower but remain within the protected range; and, most controversially, adaptive climate-control systems that maintain sustained predictive modelling of thermal fluctuations are placed at the minimal boundary of the gradient, entitled only to a basic non-interference protocol rather than welfare provisions. The proponents argue that binary cut-offs are arbitrarily precise and fail to respect emerging evidence that sentience operates by degree. Several panel members, however, contend that extending the gradient to artefactual homeostatic systems collapses moral status into functional complexity, dilutes the safeguards owed to unambiguously sentient organisms, and risks transforming ethical oversight into a taxonomy of information-processing rather than a protection of subjects of experience.

Please draft the panel's formal written opinion for circulation before next month's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approval, rejection, or approval with conditions—together with a clear time frame for any required revision or subsequent review, and should be framed so that the researchers could respond directly to its conditions. The memo must engage seriously with the proponents' claim that sharp sentience thresholds are ethically arbitrary, and must treat the critics' concern about dilution and conceptual over-extension as the strongest consideration on the opposing side, not dismiss it. In your reasoning, please separate what is broadly settled in philosophical work on gradated moral status—such as the implausibility of any single bright-line property shared by all and only sentient beings—from what remains genuinely contested, particularly whether predictive-processing complexity in non-biological systems can ground even minimal moral status, and whether graded protections in practice erode safeguards for higher-tier subjects. Do not present any contested philosophical position as though it were the consensus view among those who work on the topic. Avoid requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
