Draft the joint statement that a Procedural Closure Advocate and an Outcome Autonomy Advocate could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on judgment aggregation in multi-layered institutional design. The institutional framing—introduced specifically for this exercise—is a scientific-policy advisory body divided into Layer A (empirical premises: efficacy estimates, risk magnitudes), Layer B (normative premises: distributive weights, precautionary thresholds), and Layer C (unified recommendations that should be logically entailed by some admissible combination of Layer A and Layer B judgments). Both camps agree that applying standard majority aggregation separately within each layer, then closing under logical entailment, generates cyclical or paradoxical recommendations that no coherent individual judgment set supports; this is the discursive dilemma instantiated across epistemically specialized sub-committees rather than within a homogeneous committee.

The deliverable should be structured as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: the formal conditions for rational collective judgment (completeness and consistency of individual sets, completeness of collective sets, universal domain, anonymity, and systematicity); the fact that premise-based majority aggregation can violate collective consistency while conclusion-based majority aggregation can violate collective completeness; the properties of distance-minimizing rules and their dependence on a metric over judgment sets; and the specific structural features of the three-layer advisory body, including that logical relations cross layer boundaries (e.g., a normative threshold in Layer B activates only if an empirical trigger in Layer A exceeds a value). Second, a disagreements table in which each camp’s view is phrased in wording its own members would accept: state precisely whether collective rationality requires logical closure of premises before conclusion derivation, whether relaxation of premise consistency is permissible when sub-committees have jurisdictional expertise, and whether the collective should be modeled as a single rational agent or as a federation of autonomous judgment domains. Third, identify the cruxes—particularly whether epistemic division of labor justifies treating cross-layer logical relations as defeasible heuristics rather than binding constraints, and whether “procedural legitimacy” is defined by adherence to aggregation rules or by alignment with independently justified outcomes. Fourth, for each crux, specify a concrete test case: for example, a hypothetical scenario in which Layer A judges that risk R is high with 60% support, Layer B judges that precautionary action is warranted if R is high with 55% support, but direct aggregation on the recommendation “act” yields only 45% support; record each camp’s explicit prediction about what the collective judgment should be and what institutional design feature would resolve or dissolve the tension. Fifth, conclude with an assessment of whether any part of the dispute is verbal, especially regarding “collective rationality,” “jurisdictional autonomy,” and “logical closure,” explaining which reformulations preserve disagreement and which collapse it into shared acceptance. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific authors; write as if both parties and the reader have access only to the conceptual framework defined within this prompt.
