As a retired experimental biologist who has recently turned to philosophy of science, I find inference to the best explanation puzzling when applied to multi-level systems. The standard formulation seems to require that we rank competing explanations of the same phenomenon by their combined simplicity, scope, and coherence, selecting a unique winner. Applied to biology, this suggests that once we have a molecular mechanism for a trait, any population-level selection explanation of that same trait becomes redundant and should be discarded—yet biologists routinely maintain both. Is the error in assuming that IBE demands explanatory uniqueness across organizational levels, or in treating virtues like simplicity and mechanistic depth as commensurable on a single scale? More specifically, if I construct a decision procedure that weights these virtues numerically to resolve underdetermination, have I captured what IBE actually requires, or have I replaced a philosophical criterion with an arbitrary algorithm?
