ok so i've been trying to formalize my free-will intuitions for a post i'm drafting and i want to check if this package is actually consistent before i publish something embarassing. here's where i stand:

1. frankfurt-style cases do knock down PAP — you can be responsible even if you literally couldn't have done otherwise, so long as teh blocker never actually intervenes.

2. but i still think responsibility needs frankfurt-style sourcehood: the action has to flow from your own reflective will, not just be caused by your body.

3. i buy teh hierarchical model — second-order volitions aligning with first-order desires — and i think determinism is totally compatibile with that. the causal chain running through your brain is enough.

4. i reject the "flicker of freedom" defense as ad hoc. pointing to some tiny unblocked alternative (like deciding to blink) doesn't save PAP in any interesting way.

5. i think there's a hidden twist in nested-intervener cases (black watches jones, gray watches black, etc.). i think nesting doesn't change responsibility, but it does show that sourcehood has to be "deep" — not just one layer of endorsement.

6. i also think a deterministic AI with the exact same hierarchical structure running in a sandbox isn't morally responsible, because its "source" is ultimately the programmer, not itself.

7. but for AIs i think we actually need some hardware-level indeterminacy — a real flicker — to break the programmer's deterministic control and establish genuine sourcehood. i don't think humans need this, because biological development gives us an independent origin story.

8. i apply this same "causal robustness" idea to decision-theoretic agents: an agent's values should be robust across nearby worlds, not just actual, and i think that's the same concept as deep sourcehood.

so... do these hang together? specifically:
- is (3) compatible with (6)? if hierarchy is sufficient, why does the sandbox matter?
- does (1) plus (7) mean i'm sneaking PAP back in through the back door for AIs?
- is (4) consistent with (7) given that both invoke a "flicker"?
- and is (8) just confusing two different concepts — robustness for values vs sourcehood for responsibility?
