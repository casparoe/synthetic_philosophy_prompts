I am preparing my position for a philosophy-of-mind discussion group and want an audit of whether my commitments hang together. Here is my profile: (1) I take Global Workspace Theory as the correct account of access consciousness—information becomes access-conscious precisely when it is globally broadcast to multiple specialized consumer systems. (2) I accept that phenomenal consciousness can occur without access, for instance in subliminal or peripheral presentations that retain subjective quality despite being unreportable. (3) I defend an exclusivity thesis of my own framing: global broadcasting does not merely overlay access-structure onto phenomenal content but competitively replaces it; a representation in the workspace carries an access-proxy, not the original phenomenal character. (4) I hold that phenomenal concepts provide direct but fallible acquaintance with our own states, which explains the explanatory gap without dualism. (5) I reject substrate chauvinism: a non-biological system with sufficient local recurrent integration could possess phenomenal consciousness without any global broadcasting, hence without access consciousness. Please go through these commitments, identify which pairs are in genuine tension, rank them by severity, and for each specify the cheapest revision that would resolve it. Also flag any pair that looks tense but dissolves on inspection. Then tell me which single revision—changing just one of the five—would most improve overall consistency, and what I would have to give up. Please state how confident you are in each assessment you make, and single out the specific claim about my view set that you are least sure of.
