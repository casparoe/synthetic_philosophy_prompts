Write the full specification of desiderata that any theory of epistemic closure must meet if it aims to integrate Nozick’s tracking conditions—sensitivity and adherence—without either reinstating unrestricted deductive closure or abandoning transmission altogether. For each requirement, state the concrete case or conceptual pressure behind it: ordinary entailment transmission, where knowing one has hands should yield knowing one has ten fingers; skeptical entailment, where knowing one has hands is not taken to yield knowing one is not a handless brain in a vat; and the less-discussed case of interfering entailment, in which the counterfactual conditions required to track q are structurally incompatible with those required to track p even though p strictly entails q—for instance, tracking the global reliability of a formal system might require sensitivity to revision worlds that actively undermine the belief-stability conditions needed to track its axioms. Distinguish the non-negotiable demands—such as preserving a clear boundary between knowing p and knowing every logical consequence, avoiding appeals to hidden mental-state access to resolve conflicts, and accounting for why skeptical closure feels wrong without making all closure impossible—from negotiable camp preferences, like preserving closure specifically within mathematics, requiring adherence only in nearby worlds, or allowing graded rather than binary transmission. Flag any pair or subset of desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable and explain the tension, particularly if tracking’s counterfactual structure makes universal closure impossible while complete denial of closure collapses the distinction between knowledge and mere belief. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; a brief comparative note on how Nozick’s original account, a contextualist tracking variant, and a modal-safety hybrid fare against the list is welcome, but do not provide citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or page references.
