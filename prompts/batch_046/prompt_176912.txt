Write a deadpan, bureaucratic FAQ—numbered questions, flat procedural headings, references to internal protocols—published by a fictional government agency, the Office of Reflective Choice Architecture, that has implemented libertarian paternalism across every domain of civic life, from career selection to grief counseling. The document should run about 600 words and maintain the voice of an actual administrative publication: neutral assurances that all interventions preserve "full autonomy" because they are technically opt-out, citations of fictional directives such as "Section 4.2 of the Welfare-Heterogeneity Directive," and precise but invented institutional details like a national holiday honoring default-option designers, an examination citizens may take to override the standard life-plan template, and a sidebar explaining how the office handles utility functions it classifies as incommensurable. The satire must target the conceptual architecture of libertarian paternalism itself, not any named individual, by pushing its logic into intimate domains—marriage, vocation, mourning—where its hidden commitments become unavoidable. Specifically, the piece should expose three conceptual tensions with deadpan consistency: the epistemic problem of adaptive preferences (how the office distinguishes "authentic" revealed preferences from those it considers distorted without smuggling in a substantive theory of the good), the heterogeneity of well-being (what common metric allows the architect to rank default options across citizens whose pleasures or values may have nothing in common), and the authority of the choice architect (who qualifies them to design the "reflective equilibrium" of another person's major life choices?). The humor should arise from internal coherence—every assurance of freedom should be technically true within the framework yet clearly undermined by the framework's own premises—so that by the end the reader recognizes the office has not accidentally slipped into authoritarianism but has executed the logic of nudging faithfully to its conclusion. Avoid political caricature; keep the exaggeration conceptual rather than cartoonish, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations.
