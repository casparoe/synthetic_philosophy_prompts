I'm preparing the evaluation framework for a philosophy-of-biology seminar session on whether personal origins are metaphysically necessary. Rather than asking for a survey of existing positions, I want the specification itself: write the complete list of desiderata that any adequate theory of origin essentialism—specifically, the claim that you necessarily derive from the particular sperm and egg that actually produced you—must satisfy. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or empirical constraint behind it: cover standard puzzles such as monozygotic twinning (one zygote yielding two distinct persons) and genetic chimerism, but also include less commonly discussed reproductive scenarios such as embryos generated via in-vitro gametogenesis from induced pluripotent stem cells (gametes never produced within a human reproductive tract), somatic-cell nuclear-transfer embryos in which nuclear genetic material from one individual is combined with the cytoplasm and enucleated egg of another, and trophoblastic pathologies like partial hydatidiform moles where the genetic origin and the developing organism diverge. Indicate clearly which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable—for instance, accommodating the biological fact that a single zygote can split into separate people—and which reflect camp-specific preferences, such as the insistence that origin must be fixed solely by nuclear DNA rather than by cytoplasmic, epigenetic, or environmental factors. Crucially, identify any cluster of these desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable, particularly whether accommodating synthetic-gamete origins forces a retreat from strict essentialism to a weaker origin-contingency view.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their internal tensions rather than on cataloguing every contender. Only at the end, and without citing specific texts, page numbers, or bibliographies, briefly indicate how a Kripke-inspired essentialist position and a permissive origin-contingency position each fare against your list—not as a full scorecard, but enough to reveal which desiderata do the actual exclusionary work. I want this to serve as a pre-reading rubric for students who have not yet encountered the literature, so avoid references to specific publications and treat the list of requirements as the primary deliverable.
