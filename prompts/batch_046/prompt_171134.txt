I've been arguing with my study partner about what counts as unfreedom in our philosphy seminar. She's pretty consistent: she said a benevolant master/slave setup is an unfreedom even though he never actually stops the slave leaving; she said being so poor you have to take a dangerous job isn't an unfreedom, just misfortune; she said a state that never teaches anyone to read, leaving citizens unable to vote in any meaningful way, IS an unfreedom; she said an employer using at-will contracts but rarely firing anyone is exercising domination and that's unfree; but she said a heroin addict who genuinely wants to quit and can't is not experiencing unfreedom, just a health failure. Now we're stuck on a new case: a private tech platform runs an opaque ranking algorithm that can suppress any user's visibility without notice or appeal, though in practice it only suppresses spam, and users don't even know the power exists. Do you think she'll call that an unfreedom?

I'm less interested in the yes/no than in the rule she seems to be running — maybe republican non-domination restricted to formal institutional hierarchies, maybe a hybrid that adds civic-capability conditions for political participation, or maybe negative liberty plus explicit status violations — and in the other rules that fit her earlier verdicts just as well. Which of her five cases best distinguishes those interpretations? And what single further case would settle whether her view extends arbitrary-power concerns to private actors, or only to state-backed domination?
