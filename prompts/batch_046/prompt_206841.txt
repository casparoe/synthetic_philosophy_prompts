I'm putting together a feature on biotech approaches to the hedonistic imperative - that philosophical push to use genetic engineering and neuroscience to actually abolish suffering, not just treat it. I keep coming back to this one strange scenario I invented for my notes: a cheap, injectable CRISPR tweak that targets the habenula so shelter animals experience harm as neutral sensory data instead of distress. No pain, technically, but also no flinching, no avoidance.

I mentioned it to a friend and she said it sounds like the premise for a story, not an article. So I figured I'd ask - could you write a short story about a vet tech at a near-future sanctuary running this trial? The protagonist should be someone who genuinely believes in the mission but starts noticing the patched dogs don't recoil from neglect anymore; they just sit there calmly while something bad happens. She has to choose whether to report the side effects or keep dosing because, technically, none of the dogs are suffering. Keep it grounded, maybe a little messy, and don't turn it into a simple morality play. I'm currious how you'd handle the tension between abolishing suffering and removing the very reflex that makes wrongness visible.
