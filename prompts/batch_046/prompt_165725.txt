Having spent a career modeling multi-species competition and symbiotic exchange in evolutionary ecology, I have recently turned to philosophy and am struck by the structural parallels between ecological trade-offs and the problem of moral compromise between value systems—particularly where gains from trade are sought across frameworks that do not share a common currency of evaluation. Before I assess any existing account, I want the specification written out in full.

Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of inter-value-system compromise—conceived as a structured exchange between autonomous normative frameworks rather than as mere aggregation or strategic bargaining—must satisfy. For each requirement, state the motivating case or conceptual distinction behind it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp (for instance, economists who assume continuous substitutability versus deontologists who insist on lexical priorities).

The list should cover, at minimum: (1) how the theory distinguishes a genuine symbiotic compromise, in which each value system retains its internal coherence and reproductive logic, from a case of normative assimilation or horizontal transfer in which one framework is silently rewritten by the other; (2) how it handles lexical incommensurability, where one system treats certain commitments as non-tradable veto points while another treats them as continuously substitutable; (3) what it says about the stability of moral exchange rates—whether the terms of compromise must be context-invariant or may shift with institutional setting without dissolving the agreement; (4) whether it can accommodate layered veto structures, such as those found in federal bioethics committees or cross-jurisdictional environmental regulation, without collapsing into a single hierarchical metric; (5) whether it preserves the distinction between the existence of moral status and the weight assigned to it, particularly when interests of wild animals, future persons, and present human obligations are traded off; and (6) whether it remains consistent with the empirical record of actual institutional compromises, which rarely exhibit a unified utility function but instead display partial overlap, mutual non-interference zones, and residual disagreement.

Then, identify any subset of these desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable—for example, preserving full lexical priority within each system while simultaneously achieving Pareto-improving exchange across systems—and explain the tension precisely. Finally, keep the evaluation brief: indicate how bargaining-theoretic models, value-pluralist accounts, and modus-vivendi approaches fare against the criteria, but place the conceptual weight on the criteria themselves rather than on the scorecard. I do not need bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; I want the architectural specification alone.
