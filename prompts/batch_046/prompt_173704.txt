I'm at uni taking a course on metaphysics of science and we've been going round in circles — literally, I think — about whether laws of nature are just really stable patterns or whether there's something extra, like a kind of governing force. I keep noticing the same argumentative shape whenever someone defends the 'something extra' view. They point out that if copper conducts electricity, it's not just that every bit of copper we've seen does so; it seems to hold for bits we haven't seen, and if we found one that didn't, we'd say it was defective or interfered with, not that the law had an exception. That feels different from 'all the mugs in this cupboard are blue' — if I find a red one, I just update my census.

But then when I ask what exactly is different in the world itself, not just in our heads, the answer always seems to come back to those very reactions. The law 'governs' the copper, so counterfactuals about unobserved copper are supported; it explains the instances; we wouldn't revise it easily. But I wonder: do we call it a law and say it governs, or do we notice it governs and therefore call it a law? The two directions feel like they're being used interchangably. I've seen this with induction too — necessary connections are supposed to ground why the future resembles the past, but then the only reason we're given that there are necessary connections is that induction needs grounding.

Is there an established term for this particular loop, where the features used to classify something as metaphysically special are the same ones cited to prove that the specialness is real rather than projected? I don't mean circular reasoning in the bad logic-textbook sense, because each individual move is defensible. I mean the specific structure where identification and justification share the same content. Does it have a name in the Humeanism debate specifically, or in epistemology more broadly? And if philosophers haven't carved this out precisely, could you define its boundaries — when it's benign definitional work and when it's actually begging the question — and suggest what we should call it? I'm not after a bare label; I want to know exactly where the edges are, especially compared to ordinary regularity accounts or conceptual analysis.
