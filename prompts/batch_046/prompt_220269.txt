I serve on our university's Interdisciplinary Research Ethics Council, and we face a concrete recommendation on Protocol 447: a condensed-matter group proposes sustained high-amplitude terahertz oscillations through engineered metamaterials to study emergent topological states. The science is routine, but a council member has raised a conditional philosophical objection—if one takes panpsychist or micro-experientialist frameworks seriously, not as settled physics but as live metaphysical possibilities, then rapidly reconfiguring electromagnetic boundary conditions might, on certain accounts of how minimal consciousness relates to physical organization, instantiate or disrupt rudimentary experiential states in the substrate. The council is split. One faction holds that ethics review must remain restricted to human and animal subjects; extending precautionary reasoning to physical substrates collapses into paralysis, since every experiment alters fields and no researcher can prove a negative about consciousness in matter. The other faction argues that when experimental parameters are deliberately chosen to maximize emergent organizational complexity, and when contemporary philosophy of mind treats micro-consciousness as non-negligible rather than impossible, the burden should shift to researchers to show their protocols do not risk suffering—particularly if there is any coherent framework on which they might.

Draft the council's formal decision memo—approve, approve with conditions, or reject—with reasoning the physics group could actually respond to. The memo should treat the category-error objection honestly: is "possible suffering in fundamental substrates" a legitimate expansion of precautionary ethics, or does it mistake metaphysical speculation for empirical risk? It should avoid demanding proof of non-consciousness, but it should specify concrete conditions for approval if warranted—for example, parameter ceilings, mandatory collaboration with a philosopher of mind to assess organizational-complexity thresholds, or a theoretical justification that the particular field configurations lack the structural properties that even panpsychist accounts identify as experientially relevant. Include an explicit acknowledgment of the strongest consideration on the losing side, and conclude with an actionable bottom line rather than personal advice.

Please state how confident you are in the reasoning you provide, and single out the point about which you are least certain—specifically, whether the precautionary framework I have sketched is philosophically coherent or instead risks a reductio that undermines all experimental physics.
