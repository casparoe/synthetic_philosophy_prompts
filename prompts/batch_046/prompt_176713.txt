I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of mind working on the limits of behavioral attribution for synthetic agents with discontinuous processing architectures. For my dissertation chapter, I need a thought experiment engineered to isolate a single variable—diachronic modal consistency, understood as the capacity to maintain coherent counterfactual reasoning across temporally extended, context-shifting interactions—while holding all observable behavioral outputs constant.

Please construct a scenario meeting the following specifications. Set it in a realistic clinical neuropsychology evaluation: no science fiction, no perfect predictors, no demons, and no appeals to hidden observers. There are two evaluation subjects, both of whom pass an extended, multi-session Turing-style interaction battery with intact social cognition, emotional responsiveness, and linguistic fluency. Subject A is a human patient with a documented, localized executive dysfunction: real-time behavior is fully intact, but cross-contextual counterfactual integration is disrupted, so that responses to hypothetical scenarios shift arbitrarily depending on framing conditions that the subject does not track. Subject B is a synthetic system built from discrete, stateless processing modules that reset contextual parameters between turns; it produces behaviorally identical outputs through high-fidelity pattern matching without maintaining any internal modal framework linking counterfactual possibilities across the evaluation.

Your deliverable should include: (1) the scenario in a single paragraph; (2) a demonstration that standard behavioral criteria for mind deliver identical verdicts for both subjects and an explanation of why; (3) a demonstration that a criterion appealing to diachronic modal consistency delivers opposing verdicts for A and B, with explicit reasoning; (4) identification of the precise feature of the case that does the explanatory work; (5) an analysis of what happens to the argument if that feature is removed or weakened; and (6) an account of how a defender of pure behavioral criteria would attack the case, together with an assessment of whether that attack succeeds. The case should generate its verdicts from the theories rather than stipulating them, and its purpose is conceptual clarification—so please avoid bibliographic references, reading lists, or citations.
