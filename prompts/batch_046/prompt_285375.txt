I spent much of last evening in a prolonged argument with a friend over whether Nāgārjuna’s catuskoti—the four-cornered negation—functions as a rigorous logical instrument or merely as a rhetorical mechanism for exhausting conceptual proliferation (prapañca). We kept returning to its application against svabhāva, the notion that phenomena possess an inherent, self-established nature independent of conditions, and specifically to the Madhyamaka claim that emptiness (śūnyatā) is itself empty: not a hidden metaphysical substrate but the absence of that very substrate. I want to close this tension not with an explanation but with a poem that enacts the structure it describes.

Write a poem consisting of four quatrains followed by a single closing couplet. Each quatrain should correspond, in order, to one corner of the tetralemma applied to the claim that anything possesses svabhāva: first, that it does possess such inherent existence; second, that it utterly lacks existence (the extreme of nihilism); third, that it both possesses and lacks it; fourth, that it neither possesses nor lacks it. Hold a steady rhyme scheme—say, ABBA—through the first four stanzas so that the formal expectation itself becomes a kind of conceptual framework. Then, in the closing couplet, break or distort that rhyme, and shift the diction from the register of ultimate negation to the register of conventional designation, performing the move that emptiness itself lacks svabhāva and therefore cannot serve as a fifth, hidden corner. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with Madhyamaka recognizes the specific progression from the two extremes through the two compound positions to the dissolution of the framework itself, with the distinction between conventional truth (saṃvṛti-satya) and ultimate truth (paramārtha-satya) felt in the language rather than named in a gloss. Do not turn it into a sermon about Buddhist metaphysics, and do not resolve the tension with a comforting synthesis; let the formal rupture do the philosophical work.
