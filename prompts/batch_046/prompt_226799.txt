My opening move: a philosopher of science applies an interventionist account of causation to your claim. On this view, X causes Y only if an intervention on X, holding other factors constant, produces a change in Y. Your ascetic values, however, are embedded in a total social configuration; they are not externally manipulable variables, and any attempt to intervene on them while holding economic conditions fixed either dissolves the social mechanism or is conceptually incoherent. Therefore, either culture fails to be a cause in any rigorous sense, or your historical explanation violates the conditions for causal attribution. Respond in character: can your ideal-type method defend the causal status of values without satisfying this interventionist criterion, or does the criterion itself misapply to the kind of explanation you practice?
