I've been working through the standard justification for Occam's razor via Solomonoff induction—specifically, the argument that the universal prior exponentially penalizes hypotheses with high Kolmogorov complexity, so that simpler explanations automatically dominate in Bayesian updating. While this is elegant, I think there's a gap in how rationalist discussions usually apply it to scientific reasoning. The universal prior cares only about the length of the shortest program that reproduces observed data on some reference universal Turing machine; it has nothing to say about the structural dynamics of that program's predictions beyond the observed sequence.

I'm particularly interested in a less-discussed failure mode: hypotheses that are genuinely minimal in description length but adversarial in their predictive architecture—programs that reproduce all historical observations with perfect fidelity yet are engineered to diverge wildly from reality immediately after the present moment. Let's call these deceptive minimal descriptions. Because Solomonoff induction weights them equally with cooperative minimal descriptions of the same length, the universal prior alone seems unable to distinguish a stable physical law from a precisely calibrated epistemic trap.

Please make the argument that Solomonoff induction therefore does not, by itself, vindicate Occam's razor as scientists actually practice it. Defend the stronger claim that a defensible form of Occam's razor must be two-dimensional: it should penalize not only Kolmogorov complexity but also predictive discontinuity, perhaps via a smoothness or local-continuity constraint on the hypothesis space. Use a concrete contrast—imagine one minimal program that generates consistent orbital dynamics and another minimal program of identical length that reproduces every past planetary position but predicts chaotic divergence tomorrow—to show why brevity alone is insufficient for scientific realism. Avoid appeals to bibliographic authority; I want the reasoning developed from first principles.
