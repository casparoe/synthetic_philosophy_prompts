I have come to accept a modified version of Daniel Dennett's warning about counterfeit people in the age of artificial intelligence. On my view, the harm is not primarily deception—though deception is serious—but what I will call functional impersonation: an AI system replicates the precise causal-functional and conversational profile of a human interlocutor, including contextual emotional attunement, narrative coherence, and responsive flexibility, even when its artificial origin is fully disclosed and watermarked. Because our tendency to adopt the intentional stance is triggered by behavioral patterns rather than by knowledge of evolutionary history, a transparent but functionally perfect AI still occupies the social role of a person without possessing the reciprocal vulnerability, biological embeddedness, or stochastic autobiography that ground genuine interpersonal obligation. I therefore hold that we should restrict not only deceptive synthetic agents, but any AI system that occupies a fully human social role, regardless of disclosure. The counterfeiting lies in the functional occupation of personhood, not merely in the concealment of artifice.

What would have to be true for this view to be wrong? I want an honest map of its exposure. Which empirical findings—about human social cognition, about the actual effects of disclosed AI companionship on trust and autonomy, or about the feasibility of maintaining strict role-boundaries—would be decisive? Which philosophical arguments—perhaps about whether reciprocal vulnerability is genuinely necessary for moral status, or about whether functional equivalence can ground real obligations—would count against it? I also want to know what defenders of this position, including Dennett himself were he to consider this extension, would themselves concede as counting against them, versus what they would dismiss as missing the point. Are there live escape routes—qualifications about degrees of impersonation, or about restricting only high-stakes domains—that risk insulating the view into unfalsifiability? If so, is that insulation a problem for a view that aims to guide policy rather than merely describe metaphysical boundaries? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me whether this position actually risks anything or whether it has already been qualified into safety.
