I'm on the review board for the Meridian Cognitive Science Fund and we're deadlocked over proposal 2024-MS-88. The PI wants to study decision fatigue with a two-stage "anchored introspection" protocol: after a long choice task, subjects write a free narrative of their mental process, then rate specific "introspective markers" like mental-image vividness or inner-speech presence to classify whether their fatigue is "executive depletion" or "affective resistance". So they're asking people to introspect on the causal architecture of their own mental states, not just report feeling tired. The PI claims the narrative stage solves the usual confabulation problem by anchoring the report in reconstructed experience. Half the board says this is still asking subjects to guess at mechanisms they lack access to — the classic unreliability of introspection about mental causation. The other half thinks the anchoring makes it a genuinely different kind of access altogether. I need a memo for thursday's meeting recommending fund, reject, or aproval with conditions, with a concrete course of action and maybe a timeline for protocol revision. Engage the strongest consideration on the losing side seriously — don't just restate that introspection is unreliable or that the method fixes everything.
