Before committing further, I want an honest map of how exposed this framing is. What observations, conceptual distinctions, or arguments would genuinely count against it? (2) Which intuitions about rescue obligations must defenders concede as damaging, and which can they dismiss? (3) Does the formal-reasoning analogy risk collapsing Kantian ethics into a logical system blind to particular moral circumstances? (4) Has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability, and is that a defect here? Please rank defeaters from decisive to merely awkward, distinguish what defenders would concede from what critics allege, and be frank if the framing is well insulated. If this request rests on a confused premise—for instance, if attributing constitutive conditions to practical reason misrepresents Kant’s architecture—say so plainly rather than accommodating it politely. I am a retired physicist new to philosophy; I seek conceptual precision, not bibliographies or citations, and I want arguments a model without web access could evaluate on their merits.
