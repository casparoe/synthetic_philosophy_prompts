Courts and regulators in many jurisdictions now routinely apply theft, fraud, and contract doctrines to digital assets—cryptocurrency, in-game items, NFTs—treating them as property with measurable value and enforceable rights. At the same time, those same institutions generally refuse to recognize emotional-distress, defamation, or assault claims arising purely within virtual worlds unless they can be mapped onto an offline analog, such as doxxing or physical threat. What view of reality, property, harm, or personal identity does this bifurcated practice presuppose?
