I'm writing up a short column on Turing for a science-and-ideas newsletter (the kind where I usually cover AI ethics and old papers nobody reads anymore), and I'm drafting it between coffee refills with a deadline breathing down my neck. I was arguing with a friend about whether Turing's views on learning machines actually form a coherent package, and I realised my own take is all over teh place. So here's roughly where I've landed — tell me if it hangs together.

First, I think in that 1948 report Turing genuinely meant that learning machines should be educated like children: bottom-up, through discipline and reward, not pre-programmed with rules. Second, I take him to be a functionalist about minds — the imitation game shows he thinks internal experience is basically irrelevant if the behaviour matches. Third, I also believe he literally thought the brain was a digital computer that learns by rearranging storage, not just that it was a useful analogy. Fourth, I want to say his educational model implies something like a teacher-student relationship with real feedback loops, which sounds more relational or even social than pure functionalism allows. Fifth, I think he was actually pretty pessimistic about machines ever having free will or genuine originality, so his optimism about machine learning was narrowly technical, not philosophical.

So: do these five hang together? Specifically, does the digital-storage brain view (3) clash with the pedagogical bottom-up view (1), or can they just be two levels of the same story? Is there a real tension between functionalism (2) and the relational/social flavour I read into the education model (4), or am I importing something he wouldn't have meant? And does his skepticism about free will (5) undercut the whole learning-machine project, or is it a seperate compartment? I need you to take a definite position — rank the tensions by severity, say which commitment is the weakest link, and tell me what the cheapest fix is without just listing "well you could drop any of them". Don't hedge by saying "it depends on interpretation" — I want your best read of whether this package is salvageable, and if it isn't, which one has to go first.
