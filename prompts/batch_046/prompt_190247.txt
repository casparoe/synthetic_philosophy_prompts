I am currently enrolled in an upper-division metaphysics course on modality and the philosophy of mind, and I am composing a creative assignment in which poetic form does philosophical work. Please write a Petrarchan sonnet—exactly fourteen lines, with an octave (ABBAABBA) and a sestet (CDECDE or CDCDCD)—voiced by a proponent of the view that conceivability is a reliable guide to metaphysical possibility. The octave should present the rationalist's case through the conceivability of philosophical zombies or of watery substance lacking H2O, with the enclosed rhyme scheme echoing the apparent self-containment of pure reason. At the volta (line 9), shift to the modal skeptic's reply—drawing on the essentialist worry that such conceptions are either epistemically possible but metaphysically impossible, or confused because they overlook hidden necessary structures—so that the sestet's rhyme scheme feels disrupted or more open. The poem should be accurate enough that a specialist in contemporary modal epistemology recognizes the debate between rationalist conceivability arguments and necessary a posteriori objections, though it should not depend on verbatim quotation. Immediately following the poem, provide a brief postscript of no more than 60 words in a single paragraph stating your confidence in the philosophical interpretation offered, singling out the claim or framing you are least sure about, and noting any liberties taken with standard expositions of these positions. Do not exceed these bounds: 14 lines of verse, then the note.
