The fine-tuning argument observes that several fundamental physical constants lie within extremely narrow ranges compatible with complex life, and asks why. For decades, three broad responses have circulated: a theistic design explanation, a multiverse explanation that treats our values as unremarkable draws from a vast ensemble, and an anthropic selection-effect claim that no further explanation is required. Despite extensive literature, the debate appears stalled: each camp accuses the others of missing the point, and neither confirmed multiverse mechanisms nor deeper derivations of constants have clearly settled the philosophical dispute.

My working hypothesis is that the stalemate masks two parallel disagreements. First, about explanatory legitimacy: multiverse advocates treat statistical distributions over parameter space as explanatory resources, while critics treat such distributions as either empirically ungrounded or question-begging absent an independent generating mechanism. Second, about the terminus of explanation: theists treat a necessary being as halting an infinite regress of contingent explanations, whereas naturalists treat that same being as an unnecessary multiplication of entities and accept brute physical law as a legitimate stopping point.
