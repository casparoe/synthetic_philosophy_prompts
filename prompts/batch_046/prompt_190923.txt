Write an essay on whether Dutch book arguments genuinely show probabilism is a universal rationality rule, or whether they just police behaviour in betting markets. Weigh the standard line — that avoiding sure loss is minimal consistency — against the objection that lots of rational beliefs (moral convictions, first-person reports, institutional commitments) arent on the market, so a bookie cant exploit them. Consider specifically an agent with "locked credences": she updates like a Bayesian but wont stake anything, because betting would breach a side-constraint (say, shes pledged to act on climate risk regardless of her private odds). Even if a Dutch book could be constructed against her beliefs in principle, does that show shes irrational? Reach a verdict and justify it: is coherence binding everywhere, or only where credence and action are coupled?
