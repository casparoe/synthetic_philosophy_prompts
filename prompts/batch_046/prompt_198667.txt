I am a doctoral candidate in the history of political thought preparing assessment materials for a graduate seminar on Machiavelli and the conceptual architecture of Renaissance statecraft. I need a 90-minute final-exam essay prompt—together with a model answer and a detailed marking scheme—for students who have read The Prince and selections from the Discourses on Livy but who have not been assigned any secondary literature.

The prompt must not be answerable by summarizing the standard interpretation of The Prince, chapter 25 (the river metaphor and the opposition between fortuna and virtù). Instead, I want it to turn on a precise, somewhat technical interpretive crux: whether Machiavelli's virtù should be read as a single, domain-invariant mechanism—an adaptive causal capacity that operates with the same structural logic across military command, civic founding, and personal survival—or as a family-resemblance concept whose internal causal architecture differs across these domains, with fortuna playing structurally distinct roles (external obstacle, cyclical corruption, temporal contingency) in each.

Please construct the following:

1. The exam question itself. It should ask students to defend one of these two readings using evidence from both The Prince and the Discourses, and specifically to address why the mechanistic-uniformity reading either succeeds or collapses when applied to Machiavelli's distinction between founding a state and maintaining it.

2. A model answer of approximately 700–800 words. It should not be a summary but an argument that identifies specific textual tensions—for instance, between the river-bank imagery in ch. 25 and the cyclical account of corruption in the Discourses—and resolves them through the domain-invariance problem. The answer should cite only the primary texts without fabricated quotations; direct quotation should be avoided in favor of precise paraphrase.

3. A marking scheme (total 25 marks) listing five specific argumentative moves that distinguish first-class answers from passing ones, with marks allocated to each. For each move, include a one-line note on the characteristic failure mode. The scheme should penalize answers that treat virtù and fortuna as merely opposed terms without engaging the mechanistic-uniformity question.

The prompt must be fully self-contained: a student with access only to the primary texts and the conceptual framing given above should be able to answer it without consulting secondary scholarship, bibliographies, or external citations. Do not include requests for reading lists, bibliographies, or citations of modern commentators.
