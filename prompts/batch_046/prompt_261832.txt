He argued that because semantic content essentially involves being correct or incorrect in one's applications — a normative status that requires membership in a practice of mutual correction — machines, which merely compute statistical patterns without normative accountability, cannot be said to mean anything at all. Can you separate the distinct precise claims this one-liner might be making, such as that semantic facts are irreducibly prescriptive, that correct use is constituted by community agreement, or that without normative commitment there is only causal disposition? For each reading, indicate whether it is trivial, defensible on broadly Kripkean grounds, or false, and say specifically whether that reading supports dismissing questions about rule-following in artificial systems rather than in human speakers. Finally, identify which interpretation he most likely intended, and assess whether it succeeds.
