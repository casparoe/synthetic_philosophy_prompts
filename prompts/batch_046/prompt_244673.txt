hey — my seminar group got stuck on this and i cant tell if its clever or just broken. heres an argument they sketched from nietzsche's three history-types (monumental, antiquarian, critical). they claim the framework secretly eats itself.

(1) history serves life only through one of three modes: monumental (heroic models), antiquarian (preservation of roots), or critical (judging and freeing from the past).

(2) each mode, taken consistently, undermines the conditions for the other two. monumental needs selective amnesia (critical work); critical needs a preserved record to judge (antiquarian); antiquarian needs heroic figures worth preserving (monumental).

(3) so a culture can only be healthy if all three are held in exact equilibrium, none dominating.

(4) but "exact equilibrium" is itself a normative standard about how the present should relate to the past — which is precisely the kind of judgment that belongs to critical history.

(5) therefore, to maintain the balance, critical history must already be in charge, deciding how much antiquarian preservation and how much monumental inspiration are permitted.

(6) that means the tripartite scheme isn't symmetric; it's actually critical history regulating the other two, with antiquarian history reduced to whatever critical history allows.

(7) but if antiquarian history is only legitimate when licensed by critical judgment, then there's no independent value in preservation for its own sake — which contradicts nietzsche's explicit defense of the antiquarian impulse in small, rooted communities.

(8) so either we drop the equilibrium requirement and let one mode dominate (which nietzsche says makes history poisonous to life), or we keep it and lose antiquarian history as an autonomous mode.

they say this proves the three-mode taxonomy collapses into a binary and that nietzsche can't really defend preservationist history. i feel like step (4) or (5) is smuggling something in — maybe the equilibrium standard doesn't have to come from critical history? — but i cant pin it. which premise do you reject, and whats the cost? they really want to hang onto (2) and (7) if possible.
