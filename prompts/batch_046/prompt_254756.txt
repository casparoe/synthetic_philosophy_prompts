i've been arguing with friends about judging dead people—Jefferson, Columbus, that kind—using the "man of his time" defence. I keep seeing the same weird pattern. Someone says we cant blame Jefferson for owning slaves because he was just imbedded in his era, everyone around him did it. But then in the next breath they praise him for being weirdly progressive on religious freedom, like he was actually transcending his time. Same with Columbus: hes excused for brutality because thats just how 1492 worked, but then celebrated for being bolder and more visionary than his contemperaries. So the same figure gets locked into his moral epoch when that lets him off the hook, and breaks out of it when that makes him look good. Is there an established name for this double move? I know its not just hypocrisy—theres a specific temporal sleight-of-hand going on. How is it different from ordinary presentism or from plain cherry-picking, and if theres no term, what should we call it? Im not after a bare label; I want to know exactly what the pattern is, where its edges are, and whether it counts as one mistake or two different ones stitched together.
