I've been trying to work out whether I actually accept Van Fraassen's reflection principle, but the formal treatments always lose me. I want a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts people by what they believe about concrete belief-updating cases, not by which philosophical label they recognize. Build a tree with six to eight leaves, branching on concrete commitments that come apart in practice. The first split should ask whether reflection applies at all when you anticipate gaining no new evidence—use a case like knowing you'll feel calmer about a medical result after a good night's sleep, with the facts unchanged. The next splits should cover: whether it applies when you plan to consult a trusted expert whose reasoning you haven't yet heard; whether it applies to predictions about your own future irrationality, such as knowing you'll take a drug that induces overconfidence in a false belief; and whether it applies in an "amnesia loop," where you expect to forget sound reasoning and revert to an old prior. For each leaf, give the name of the position if one is standard (for example, strict reflection, qualified rational-update reflection, anti-reflection, interpersonal-only reflection, or an unnamed "epistemic humility" view), and state in one sentence its most awkward commitment. Where two positions land on the same branch, flag them as indistinguishable by this tree. Also note any answer-combination that seems incoherent or merely unexplored, with a brief diagnosis—whether it collapses into contradiction, looks unattractive, or is just unoccupied. The branching questions must be phrased so that someone without formal training can answer them directly, and the final mapping should show which pattern of answers lands you in which leaf, without ever asking "which theory do you hold?"
