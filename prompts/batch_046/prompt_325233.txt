I'm writing up my personal epistemic framework for a reading group and want a consistency audit before I present it. Here are my commitments on reasoning under uncertainty, the inside/outside view distinction, and epistemic modesty:

(a) I treat the inside view and outside view as complementary but asymmetrical layers: the inside view supplies the mechanistic model, and the outside view supplies a prior on that model's reliability. Where the reference class is well-defined they combine in something like a Bayesian update; where the reference class is sparse or heterogeneous (e.g., long-horizon forecasting, unprecedented technological transitions), the prior is essentially uninformative and the inside view dominates by default, but that dominance is itself a reason to widen credible intervals symmetrically rather than to narrow point estimates.

(b) I hold that epistemic modesty is structural rather than substantive: it should govern how I distribute confidence across possibilities—wider intervals, lower point confidence, more weight on tail risks—not which specific possibility I endorse. I do not think modesty requires me to split the difference with expert consensus or to defer on the substance of a dispute.

(c) When I disagree with domain experts, my default method is crux-search: I try to identify the specific empirical or methodological divergence that explains the gap, condition my update on that divergence alone, and avoid reputation-weighted averaging or uniform deference.

(d) I recognize that identifying the crux is itself a reasoning task subject to the same calibration failures and framing biases: experts may disagree not only on the conclusion but on what counts as the crux, and my own identification is likely distorted by my prior theoretical commitments.

(e) I reject any "view from nowhere"—all reasoning is situated—but I also think the outside view derives its debiasing power precisely from aggregating across differently situated reasoners. That makes it multi-situated rather than unsituated, but it does not eliminate the need for some form of aggregation when first-order reasoning conflicts.

(f) I want to apply structural modesty reflexively: the belief that modesty should be structural, and the belief that outside-view priors are diffuse in poor-feedback domains, should themselves be subjected to the same widening and calibration adjustments. I assume this should converge to a stable fixed point rather than collapsing into global skepticism, though I haven't specified the formal conditions.

My question: which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like tensions, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally problematic. For each genuine conflict, specify what has to give—whether it's (b)'s rejection of substantive deference, (c)'s crux-first method, (d)'s meta-level skepticism about crux identification, or something else—and what that revision costs me in terms of the broader framework. Be specific about whether the tension is between commitments I hold simultaneously or between a commitment and an implicit consequence I haven't endorsed. Don't tell me which one I should drop; just map the dependency structure.
