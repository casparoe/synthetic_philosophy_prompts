hey, drafting a short essay on Bradley's regress and the unity of the propostion. rough sketch:

Bradley's worry is that any proposition like Socrates is wise splits into parts — Socrates, wisdom, and some relation linking them. But that relation then needs another relation to link it to Socrates, and so on forever. So relations can't be real, and propositions don't actually hang together.

My injected idea: what if the mistake is treating propositions like lego bricks that need glue? Maybe a proposition is more like a melody — you can abstract out notes, but there's no extra 'melody-glue' holding them together. The unity comes first, and separation is just an intellectual trick.

But I'm not sure this escapes the regress. If I can genuinely distinguish the cat from the mat and the being-on, doesn't that prove thier separation is real? Or is separation just an abstraction that never occurs in actual experience?

I'm also worried I'm misreading Bradley — maybe he doesn't treat relations as external glue but as internal to the terms. Could you check if my summary is accurate? Is the melody analogy interesting or just muddling things? And is there a hidden circularity in saying 'unity is primary'?
