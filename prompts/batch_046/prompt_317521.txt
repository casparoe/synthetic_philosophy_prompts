I'm finalizing the interpretive framework for a museum exhibition on societal resilience that I have recently been asked to curate, and I need a consistency audit before the labels go to print. Here are my working commitments: (1) I reject "collapse" as an objective, observer-independent category; it is a normative-institutional judgment that applies only when a society loses enough organizational complexity that it can no longer reproduce its core practices across generations. (2) By that standard, neither the Late Bronze Age palace economies (c. 1200 BCE) nor the Classic Maya lowland polities (c. 800–950 CE) collapsed: both experienced state fragmentation and demographic reorganization, but village-level agriculture, kinship networks, and ritual transmission continued with little discontinuity. (3) I treat Rapa Nui (Easter Island) as a genuine exception: the archaeological record indicates a severe, discontinuous break in intergenerational transmission of navigation, monument-building, and resource-management knowledge, which satisfies my reproduction-failure criterion. (4) Nevertheless, I think that defining collapse purely by institutional reproduction misses something essential: if a population undergoes rapid, involuntary loss of security, predictive control, and collective meaning—what I call a phenomenological rupture—that rupture should carry independent weight in our taxonomy, even where institutional practices technically persist. (5) I also believe that modern climate and risk discourse inappropriately borrows archaeological "collapse" narratives for rhetorical effect, and that archaeologists therefore have an ethical duty to police and correct this misuse. (6) Finally, I hold that the difference between a society that "fails" and one that "merely changes" depends not on outcomes but on the availability of alternative institutional pathways at the moment of stress—something I am tentatively framing as "institutional liquidity," meaning the density of substitutable organizational forms within a given social field. Are these commitments jointly tenable? Specifically: which pairs are in genuine tension rather than merely apparent tension, how severe is each conflict, what is the least costly revision in each case, and does abandoning (6) resolve the deeper contradictions or only relocate them? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then provide the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question; rank the tensions by severity.
