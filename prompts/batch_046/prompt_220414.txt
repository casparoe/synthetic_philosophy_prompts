I am advising a small interdisciplinary unit—two philosophers of history and one constitutional theorist—who have two years, no archival budget, and a shared problem. They accept the Hobsbawm-Anderson thesis that modern nations are largely invented traditions with imagined communal roots rather than primordial entities. From this they have distilled a specific conceptual puzzle I will call retroactive institutional legitimation: many constitutional orders claim authority through narratives of continuous national existence stretching centuries before the institutional forms we now recognize as the state actually coalesced. The unit wants a defended agenda, not a survey, for how to allocate its limited effort.

They are weighing three potential lines of work. First, an ontological agenda: clarifying whether the nation is best understood as a diachronic social kind, a legal fiction, or a performative institutional status, and whether these categories are layered rather than exclusive. Second, a normative-legal agenda: determining whether the inventedness of a national tradition undermines the legitimacy of constitutional provisions—such as secession clauses or indigenous land-title frameworks—that depend on claims of pre-modern continuity. Third, a historiographical-methodology agenda: assessing how philosophers should treat primary-source narratives that conflate ethnic continuity with state continuity without reducing all such narratives to mere ideology.

Please rank these three for this specific unit. Your defense should make explicit: (i) the criteria governing the ordering—tractability within philosophy rather than empirical history, dependency relations among the questions, and practical import for constitutional interpretation or democratic theory; (ii) the trade-offs, especially between achieving ontological clarity and addressing normative urgency; (iii) what concrete result or argument over the next two years would cause you to reorder the ranking; and (iv) which implications of this debate are genuinely upstream of practice in international law or democratic legitimacy, and which are philosophical diversions that do not affect those practices. I want an ordering justified by reasons, not a bibliography or a literature review.
