One said the classic case -- it's raining but I don't belive it -- is absured because belief is transparent to itself, so asserting P while denying belief in P violates some basic coherance condition on assertion. The other said that's only true for individual mental states, and groups can totally do this: imagine a jury returns a guilty verdict (that's an institutuional assertion of guilt) but later surveys show most jurors privately never belived he did it. No contradiction, just a split between official output and private mental states. The first pushed back that even distributed, the jury is still putting forward P while lacking the corresponding belief, which is exactly the transparency problem Moore was worried about, just scaled up. Then the second brought up AI: a language model might generate that the tumor is malignant while its internal probabilities are 55/45, and we don't call that paradoxical, we call it poorly calibrated. So if machines can assert-without-believing without absuredity, why can't jurys?

Please adjudicate move by move: is the jury case actually a version of Moore's paradox or a category mistake? Does the AI analogy help or does it confuse institutional belief with predictive output? And tell me what both are missing -- maybe something about the normativity of assertion vs prediction, or about whether collective belief even exists in a way that can be transparent.
