I am a junior serving on my school's student honor council, which meets monthly to review academic misconduct cases and must recommend sanctions to the administration for final approval. Our current framework confuses being the causal source of cheating with being genuinely morally responsible for it, and I need a reusable decision procedure grounded in Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility. The procedure should be designed for teenagers with no formal philosophy training, should take no more than fifteen minutes per case, and should yield either a structured determination that true moral responsibility has not been established or an explicit flag that the board must make an irreducible value judgment rather than apply a factual test. Format it as an ordered checklist or flowchart in words, with steps that first separate causal involvement from responsibility for one's character or mental states, then test whether the student can be held responsible for possessing the dispositions that produced the action, and then apply the regress that arises when we ask whether the student is responsible for the causes of those dispositions. At each step, specify what counts as a sufficient answer when records are incomplete, clearly mark where the procedure stops with a negative finding, and identify the precise points where judgment cannot be eliminated and the board must decide. Be honest about where the procedure is likely to misfire or produce counterintuitive results that members may not want to accept. Apply the procedure to three hypothetical cases: a student who cheated after discovering that her parents had concealed her failing grades; a student who meticulously planned and executed plagiarism over two full weeks with complete awareness; and a student who copied answers during an undiagnosed dissociative episode. Please write the answer in American English.
