I am presenting a paper at an interdisciplinary seminar in philosophy of biology—mostly evolutionary biologists, a few philosophers of science, and one historian of science—defending a rehabilitated reading of Karl Popper’s claim that Darwinism is not a strictly testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research programme. My thesis is that Popper was not attacking evolutionary biology but diagnosing a precise logical feature: the core principle of natural selection can be formulated in ways that make it immune to direct empirical counterexample, yet this metaphysical framing is not a scientific defect but an explanatory architecture that operates analogously to a modal selection framework across possible evolutionary histories. I argue that recognizing this “explanatory elasticity” is exactly what explains why Darwinism generates such robust, testable auxiliary hypotheses—about adaptation, phylogenetic constraint, and evolutionary dynamics—without being reducible to any one of them. The audience will include people who regard Popper as an ignorant outsider who dismissed natural selection as a tautology; people who think falsifiability is an obsolete criterion after Kuhn and Lakatos; and people who worry I am smuggling in logical positivism or trying to diminish Darwin’s scientific status.
