I have no formal training in philosophy or art history, but I have been working through a problem on my own: can a work of art be genuinely excellent if its content or design is deeply immoral, especially when the immorality is embedded in the work itself rather than only in the artist's private life? To organize my thoughts, I have sketched a three-part framework I want to put to a real test. First, representational corruption: the work systematically misrepresents a group or historical event in ways that cause real-world harm. Second, affective misalignment: the work is engineered to make audiences admire, celebrate, or emotionally bond with actions or figures that should be condemned. Third, institutional endorsement: the work's reputation as a masterpiece suppresses or drowns out moral criticism, so that praising its craft becomes inseparable from legitimizing its message. I want to apply this framework to the concrete historical episode of D.W. Griffith's 1915 film The Birth of a Nation. I understand the basic facts well enough—its technical innovations in narrative filmmaking, its depiction of the Ku Klux Klan as heroic saviors, the NAACP-led protests and attempts to suppress or censor it, and the long history of critics and educators having to wrestle with its legacy in classrooms and retrospectives. Using my three categories, analyze exactly how the film relates to each one: does it fit cleanly, fit only partially, or does the category fail to capture what is actually going on in this case? For each fit, say whether that kind of moral failure should diminish our aesthetic judgment of the film, or whether artistic value can be isolated from it. Then tell me where my framework is silent or breaks down: what does the actual history of the film's reception, reassessment, and continued institutional presence reveal about the separation of work from moral effect that my three categories miss or misdescribe? I want the reasoning direct and self-contained, answered in American English, without lists of scholars, books, or citations I should look up—just the analysis and an honest account of where the case pushes back against the idea. If there are gaps in my framework that only become visible through this episode, name them explicitly.
