A philosopher working within Ian Hacking's looping-effects framework has rendered these four judgments: autism spectrum conditions exhibit strong looping, since diagnostic language reshapes self-conception and behavior; schizophrenia exhibits weak looping, because classification tracks a relatively stable biological dysfunction that labeling does not substantially alter; dissociative identity disorder exhibits extreme looping, in that the diagnostic category itself generated and structured the symptom presentations found in clinical populations; and social anxiety disorder exhibits moderate looping, where the label amplifies pre-existing avoidance without creating the underlying tendency.
