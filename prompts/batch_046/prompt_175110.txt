I'm preparing a podcast interview with a philosopher who has recently defended what she calls the "threshold asymmetry" in procreative ethics—the view that the standard asymmetry between a strong moral reason not to create a suffering child and the absence of a corresponding reason to create a happy child holds only above a certain quality-of-life baseline, and that below that baseline the asymmetry either collapses or inverts depending on whether the potential person stands in a "counterfactual dependency" relation to the actual parents. She explicitly rejects both the impersonal-value framing (maximizing welfare across actual and possible people) and the standard person-affecting restriction, and she argues that the non-identity problem is largely a red herring if we reconceive procreative harm as a relational failure rather than as a comparative harm. I want eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her to restate the view, but instead press on the internal mechanics of her position.

Specifically, I need at least one question about how she fixes the threshold without smuggling in the very impersonal comparisons she rejects; at least one about what her relational-dependency criterion actually requires—whether adoption severs it, whether genetic relation strengthens it, and what happens in cases of accidental conception where no deliberate procreative choice was made; at least one about the interaction between her view and the non-identity problem, particularly whether her "relational failure" framing survives the objection that a child brought into poor conditions is not worse off than they would have been, since absent that choice they would never have existed; and for each of these, a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. For instance, if she appeals to a "standing to complain" that survives the non-identity worry, the follow-up should ask why that same standing does not generate a positive duty to create happy people who would then have standing to thank us. I also want a final question about what conceptual or empirical discovery could genuinely cause her to abandon the threshold asymmetry altogether, framed so that she cannot deflect it by saying it is merely a research program rather than an objection. Please draft the full sequence with the anticipated answers and follow-ups woven in.
