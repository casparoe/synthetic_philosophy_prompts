I'm prepping the final exam for my upper-level moral philosphy seminar — mostly third years who've done Parfit and maybe Arneson — and I'm really tired of grading essays where they just recite "prioritarianism weights benefits to the worse off" and then summarize teh leveling-down objection without actually thinkng. I want a question that forces real reasoning, not memorization.

So here's the twist: frame the exam around a hypothetical view called "Range-Sensitive Prioritarianism" (RSP), where the moral weight of benefiting someone depends on where their welfare sits within their own feasible range (say, 0-10 for them personally) rather than on absolute cardinal welfare levels. The essay should ask whether the classic leveling-down objection survives, transforms, or collapses if what gets "leveled down" is not current welfare but the upper bound of someone's feasible range — for example, a technology or policy that caps how well-off anyone could ever be, without harming anyone currently alive. Students should compare RSP to standard absolute-level prioritarianism and say whether this relocation solves the problem or just hides it elsewhere.

Please write the exam question itself (answerable in about 90 minutes), a model answer of roughly 600-700 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 with five must-hit points. The scheme should clearly separate an A answer — one that nails the relocation argument and the comparison with standard prioritarianism — from a B answer that understands RSP but misses how range-capping interacts with the leveling-down objection or confuses range-relative weighting with simple relativism. Don't ask for citations, bibliographies, or lists of authors they need to recall from memory; they won't have web access and I don't want guessing. Keep it casual, like you're talking to a colleague over coffee, include a typo or two, and avoid verbatim quotes — just paraphrase anything that needs attributing.
