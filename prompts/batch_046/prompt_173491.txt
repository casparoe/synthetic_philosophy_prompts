I am assisting with an undergraduate seminar on political philosophy, and over the past two weeks we have worked closely through Rousseau’s argument that theatrical spectacle corrupts civic virtue by substituting artificial emotional display for genuine moral engagement, and that the rigid separation between passive spectator and performing actor fractures the direct reciprocity he associates with healthy communal life. During our last meeting a student raised a scenario that none of the assigned texts address: a technically feasible but still emerging form of participatory performance in which there are no professional actors, audience members wear biometric sensors that stream real-time emotional data into a generative system, and an algorithm produces a unique, unscripted narrative for each participant. Because the system responds immediately to each viewer’s own reactions, the experience feels intensely personal and immediate, yet every interaction is mediated by predictive models trained on aggregate behavioral data, and there is no fixed script or stable boundary between those who perform and those who observe.

Given that this form of immersive algorithmic theater blurs the very distinctions Rousseau criticized, I need to anticipate his likely judgment for my lecture notes, but I want the reasoning to be rigorous rather than impressionistic. Would he regard the biometric feedback loop as an even more insidious form of moral deception—disguising artificial mediation as authentic self-expression—or might he see the dissolution of the actor-spectator divide and the absence of predetermined representation as a partial recovery of the spontaneous, unmediated communal expression he praised in popular festivals? Please reconstruct his probable position by applying his specific objections to emotional artifice, to representation as such, and to the social division created by theatrical roles directly to this scenario, and indicate whether you think he would ultimately condemn it, cautiously welcome it, or treat it as an ambiguous hybrid. I am especially interested in how he would evaluate the fact that participants appear to express themselves freely while their expressions are simultaneously being harvested and reshaped by an external system they do not control.
