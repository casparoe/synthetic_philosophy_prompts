I just had a long argument with a friend about whether digitizing colonial administrative papers actually addresses the power embedded in archives, or merely replicates it with better search functions. She kept framing the silence in the source as an absence that technology can remedy—missing voices that digitization and community tagging can restore to visibility. I kept insisting that the silence is structurally productive: that digitization does not erase it but transforms it into an indexed absence, giving the gap metadata, discoverability, and even a kind of presence in the catalog. By the end we were not sure whether we disagreed about what archives do, about the meaning of "silence" (missing data versus a kind of record), or whether we were answering different questions altogether—one about epistemic access, the other about the ontology of the record itself. Diagnose this impasse: is it substantive, verbal, or a case of two questions running under one heading? What would each diagnosis require to count as progress—a conceptual distinction, a concrete archival case study, or an argument that no resolution should be expected? And where in the dependency structure should one start if the goal is not to win but to understand what kind of disagreement we actually have?
