There's this old debate about moral saints — people who max out on goodness and drop everything personal. Wolf-style critics say they're missing out on humor, hobbies, love, whatever; defenders say that's just romantacizing selfishness. Decades on, people are still trading the same examples. My working theory: the stall isn't really about ethics, it's about whether "desireable" here means agent-relative (would I want to live this?) or agent-neutral (is this the best life for anyone?), and also about whether personal projects are constitutive of who you are or just optional accessories. So the sides might be answering different questions under one name. Is that right? Or is there a hidden psycholgical claim about human flourishing that could actually be tested? I want a diagnosis of the disagreement's structure — verbal, normative, metaphysical, whatever — tied to what each side actually says, not a survey of positions. And be honest: could any conceptual split, study, or reframing break the deadlock, or is the question just badly formed?
