You serve on the state Constitutional Precommitment Review Panel, and the legislature has referred to you a proposed “Reflexive Lock Amendment” for advisory opinion before ratification. The amendment does two things simultaneously: it entrenches a fifty-year carbon-budget framework, alterable only by supermajority, and it creates an independent “Epistemic Oversight Tribunal” appointed for life and removable only for cause, empowered to suspend the budget automatically if it unanimously finds that the underlying climate models have been empirically falsified; the amendment further includes a “self-dissolution clause” permitting any future legislature, after thirty years, to abolish the tribunal by simple majority upon showing that the tribunal has become unresponsive. Proponents argue this is a legitimate intergenerational Ulysses contract—present citizens binding themselves against future weakness for short-term economic relief—while wisely tying release to objective scientific failure rather than political whim. Opponents argue the structure is conceptually incoherent: true precommitment requires identity between the binding and bound parties over time, but here future citizens are being bound by present ones, while the escape mechanism both delegates sovereign authority to an unaccountable tribunal and allows future legislatures to destroy that tribunal, so neither layer is genuinely entrenched. A minority view holds that the amendment is better understood not as precommitment but as strategic delegation to an epistemic proxy, which avoids intergenerational illegitimacy but raises its own democratic-accountability problems.

Draft the panel’s advisory memo recommending whether the legislature should approve, reject, or approve with conditions. Lead with your bottom-line recommendation in the very first sentence, with no preamble and no restating of the question; then provide the reasoning, including an honest and substantive treatment of the strongest argument against your position. The memo should be actionable: address whether the nested structure is conceptually sound, whether the intergenerational binding is legitimate, whether the escape clauses hollow out the commitment, and what conditions—if any—would be required for approval. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific works, and do not ask for them.
