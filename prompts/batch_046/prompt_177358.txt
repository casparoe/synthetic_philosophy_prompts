I work in the Austin-Searle tradition on speech acts and want to understand the contemporary debate about slurs without importing semantic machinery I reject. Restate the three main families of position — that slurs encode derogation in literal semantic content, that they express contempt or invoke conventional norms without such encoding, and that they function primarily as prohibited terms whose wrongness lies in violating a social rule — entirely in the vocabulary of illocutionary force, perlocutionary effect, felicity conditions, conventional implicature, and speech-act conventions. For each, indicate what translates cleanly and what must be distorted or supplemented. Then report the residue: do phenomena such as the persistence of offensive force under embedding in negation or conditionals, and the asymmetry between in-group reclamation and out-group use, have any natural expression in this framework? And does whatever remains untranslated point to a genuine need for literal semantic encoding, or only to an incomplete speech-act vocabulary?
