Write an essay on whether Ryle's distinction between knowing-how and knowing-that survives scrutiny once we examine the representational basis of skill in both biological and artificial systems. Weigh the intellectualist position—that all procedural competence ultimately decomposes into tacit propositional representations—against the anti-intellectualist claim that knowing-how is irreducibly practical and non-propositional. Give serious weight to the observation that large language models acquire sophisticated procedural abilities (e.g., analogical mapping, code synthesis) through distributed parameter updates rather than explicit rule encoding, and consider whether this undermines intellectualism or merely demonstrates that propositions can be implicitly instantiated in high-dimensional weight space. Do not retreat to a non-committal middle ground: defend a clear verdict on whether knowing-how is a genuinely autonomous epistemic category or reducible to knowing-that implemented differently. Write in a formal, conceptually precise register suitable for a longtime reader of rationalist forums; avoid citation lists, bibliographies, and appeals to empirical studies you cannot summarize from first principles.
