The moral foundations debate has been stuck for like a decade now. Haidt's psycholgy group keeps showing liberals weight care and fairness way more, while conservatives spread it across loyalty, authority, sanctity, and liberty, and then philosophers ask "ok but who is actually right?" and everyone just shrugs. Is this stall because we're running two different projects - descriptive psycholgy and normative ethics - under the same "moral foundations" label? Or is there a hidden empirical disagreement about whether these foundations are modular instincts versus post-hoc rationalizations? Or is it just bedrock intuitions: people think there own foundation profile is self-evidently correct and there's no shared standard to adjudictate? I want a diagnosis, not a verdict on left vs right. Specifically, does knowing conservatives have six foundations and liberals have two tell us anything about which morality is better, or is that a category error? And if it's a category error, why do people keep sliding from "conservatives value authority" to "authority is a real moral value"? What's the hidden move, and is there any distinction or experiment that could actually move this? I keep seeing it in my reading group and nobody moves an inch.
