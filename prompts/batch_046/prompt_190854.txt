Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between Gödelian rationalists and Gödelian formalists on whether Gödel's metaphysical rationalism supports personal post-mortem survival. The rationalist camp maintains that Gödel's principle of rational optimism, together with his modal ontological argument, entails that individual cognitive structure cannot be annihilated without violating the rational completeness of the world, so consciousness must persist in some transformed mode. The formalist camp accepts Gödel's theism, his modal-logic framework, and the rational order of reality, but denies that these universal commitments license claims about individual persistence, treating survival as a category error when applied to metaphysical structure.

Produce a document with these sections:

1. Numbered agreed claims—shared commitments such as Gödel's rejection of materialism, his acceptance of an ontological argument within modal logic, the evidential role of mathematical intuition for non-physical cognition, and the shared constraint that no empirical observation can verify or falsify post-mortem states.

2. A disagreements table, with each position phrased in wording that camp accepts. At minimum distinguish (a) whether rational completeness requires conservation of individual information-structure across death, and (b) whether Gödel's Leibnizian-inspired monadology permits continuity or interaction between pre-mortem and post-mortem cognitive states.

3. The cruxes—sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side. Propose two: (i) Does the prohibition on irrecoverable loss of structured information apply specifically to conscious subjects, or only to formal and mathematical systems? (ii) If necessary mathematical objects are accessed rather than created by minds, does physical death terminate that access or merely alter its conditions?

4. For each crux, specify a concrete conceptual case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance each camp's predicted outcome. For example, both may agree that if a complete physical description of neural processes lacks any parameter corresponding to grasp of abstract objects, then the rationalist must locate intuition outside physical structure; state whether each side expects this absence to be reconcilable with transformed post-mortem access or fatal to individual survival.

Keep formulations neutral, do not request citations or bibliographies, avoid appeals to obscure unpublished manuscripts, and reason solely from the shared philosophical architecture both camps acknowledge.
