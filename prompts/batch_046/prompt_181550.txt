I'm writing a long-form piece for a science-and-ideas publication on how scientific thought experiments actually do epistemic work. Examine whether such experiments can be calibrated against real-world physical constants without smuggling in unstated empirical premises, and whether their knowledge-yielding capacity requires counterfactual portability—the ability to transfer inferences from the imagined scenario back into actual experimental design. Don't treat this as a historical survey of famous cases; argue for one side of the controversy, but address the strongest objection from the opposing view. The essay should stand entirely on its own reasoning, so do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations.
