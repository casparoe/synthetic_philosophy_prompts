I'm defending a thesis at a philosophy-of-technology colloquium that von Neumann's theory of self-replicating automata should be read not as a prophecy of autonomous artificial life but as a technical constraint argument: any technology that genuinely survives long-term perturbation must maintain a redundancy-to-plasticity ratio high enough that distributed error-correction exceeds the informational capacity of any centralized controller, not because the system seeks independence but because robustness structurally demands it.
