The standard debate is whether assertion is governed by a knowledge norm — you should only say it if you know it — or a belief norm, where sincere belief is enough, or maybe just a truth norm where being right is all that matters. But there's this other pattern that bugs me, which I'll call the stewardship problem. When you assert something in a community that treats claims as shared resources, you're not just passing information; you're also creating a kind of dependancy. Other people build reasoning on top of your claim, and if it turns out wrong, they don't just drop your sentence — they have to tear down everything that rested on it. So false assertions don't just mislead, they generate epistemic debt that others pay off with extra maintainance work. That makes me wonder if the norm of assertion should account for this burden, not just the mental state of the speaker. Maybe it's not knowledge (too restrictive, kills useful speculation) and not bare belief (ignores the debt), but something closer to justified belief paired with an obligation to retract cleanly.
