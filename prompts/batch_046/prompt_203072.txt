hey, so i'm drafting this essay for a weird little philosophy-of-lit zine and i keep going back and forth on whether the central hook even holds up. i'm a novelist usually, not an academic, so bear with me if this sounds like fiction trying to wear philosophy's coat.

i'm specifically worried about two things. first, am i misrepresenting how the bindng problem actually works, or is it okay to treat it as a kind of "narrative stitching" problem? second, does the analogy to unreliable narration hold, or does it collapse once you realize there's no hidden author behind the scenes — just competing processes with no final authority? also, is the whole "editorial board" framing interesting, or does it sound like i'm just dressing up old ideas in literary jargon? i'd love comments on accuracy and on whether this is actually interesting, especially that middle section where i try to connect page-turn blindness in novels to the illusion of continuous selfhood. there's definatly a typo or two in my notes but that's part of the charm, right?
