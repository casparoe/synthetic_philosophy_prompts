Consider the following five normative frameworks for assessing directness, indirectness, and the permissibility of white lies across cultural contexts—specifically comparing Dutch and Japanese communicative norms. Rank them from the most philosophically defensible (tier S) to the least defensible (tier D) as universal accounts that can coherently evaluate practices in both cultures without collapsing into either cultural imperialism or uncritical relativism. For each framework, provide a single sentence explaining its tier placement, with particular attention to whether it can consistently condemn or permit a white lie in a Dutch transactional setting and in a Japanese relational setting.

The frameworks are:

1. Autonomy-respecting directness: Direct speech is morally required because it treats the interlocutor as capable of handling unfiltered information; indirectness and white lies are paternalistic violations of autonomy, regardless of cultural context.

2. Relational harmony (wa) preservation: Indirectness and strategic ambiguity—including white lies—are morally required when they prevent disruption of social harmony; directness is permissible only when relational stakes are negligible.

3. Contextual contractualism: Directness is obligatory in institutional or low-trust exchanges, indirectness in intimate or high-context relationships, and white lies are permissible only when both parties implicitly contract for face-management within a shared relational frame.

4. Attitudinal expressivism applied to pragmatics: The wrongness of a white lie lies not in its indirect form but in expressing a disrespectful attitude—treating the listener as emotionally fragile; directness and indirectness are both permissible if they express appropriate respect for the listener's rational capacities.

5. Phronesis-integrated virtue ethics: The relevant virtue is not directness or indirectness per se but practical wisdom in balancing honesty with kindness; white lies are defective when they substitute the speaker's judgment of the listener's needs for a genuinely shared understanding of the situation, regardless of whether they are phrased directly or indirectly.

Please present your ranking as S, A, B, C, D with the framework number and name, followed by your one-sentence justification for each.
