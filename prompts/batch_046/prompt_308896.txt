The locals must choose, in real time, whether to sanction violations through token loss, to explain norms as practical tools, or to accommodate without judgment, while visitors must navigate the ambiguity of being corrected versus being guided, so that the group experiences the difference between procedural inconvenience and genuine normative injury before any philosophical vocabulary is introduced.

Please design the full rules, the token economy, the rotation schedule, and the precise collective task, then provide a debrief that maps observed interactions onto the distinction between procedural hospitality—adopting a local rule to avoid friction—and normative assimilation—accepting it as a duty that survives cultural translation—while being frank about which feature of the moral-obligation thesis the simulation cannot reproduce, such as the long-term socialization that makes conventions feel binding rather than chosen. Also tell me what will predictably fail the first time I run this—perhaps locals over-explaining or visitors misreading sanctions as moral condemnation rather than strategic signals—and how to correct it, ensuring that no participant needs prior knowledge of any specific academic citation or obscure theory to engage fully.
