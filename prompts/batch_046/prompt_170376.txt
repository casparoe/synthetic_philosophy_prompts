I'm trying to hold together the following commitments on original versus derived intentionality, and I want a consistency audit before I present them in a seminar. (1) I take Searle's basic distinction seriously: original intentionality is intrinsic and requires phenomenal consciousness, while derived intentionality is always borrowed and belongs to artifacts. (2) I think Dennett is right that the wandering two-bitser possesses real derived intentionality, but I understand this as resting on its causal-functional tracking relations and evolutionary selection history rather than on any mental resemblance. (3) I deny that natural selection is an intentional designer, so if biological brains have intentionality it must be original rather than derived—yet I also think brain states can be explained functionally in exactly the two-bitser style without invoking consciousness. (4) I hold that current large language models lack both kinds: they are not conscious, and their outputs are not fixed by any intentional design history that would confer derived content. (6) Finally, I want to leave open a niche possibility I call "autogenous derivation": a system's reference-stabilizing feedback loops might generate aboutness without either consciousness or an external intentional designer, though I am unsure whether this is a real third category or just derived intentionality with a hidden origin.
