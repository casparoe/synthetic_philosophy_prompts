i'm putting together a taxonomy for my philosophy blog on whether there's actually a moral difference between voting, abstaining, and spoiling your ballot — but I don't want it to be one of those quizzes where you just pick the label you like. Instead I'm thinking of a decision tree that starts from a single split: do you treat casting a vote as basically an act of speech, as pulling a causal lever that changes outcomes, or as a ritual that lends legitimacy to the system regardless of results? From there it should branch on whether moral evaluation tracks your inner intention, the expected causal effect, or the symbolic meaning of your participation. Each leaf needs to name the position (or say 'unnamed' if it's a dead combination) and then state its most awkward commitment in one sentence — like how the pure-speech view has to claim that voting in a safe district where you'll never change the result is just as weighty as voting in a tie, or how the ritual-legitimation view has to explain why boycotting the election is sometimes praised as protest rather than condemned as free-riding. The tree should definitely include the civic-duty view, the expressive-authenticity view, the strategic lesser-evil view, the clean-hands abstentionist view, and at least one unoccupied combo — maybe treating the vote as purely causal but judging it solely by your subjective intention with no regard for actual outcomes. I also want you to flag any pairs that the tree can't actually seperate, and please don't include a bibliography or citation list — I just need the tree structure with the branch claims and the awkward commitments spelled out clearly.
