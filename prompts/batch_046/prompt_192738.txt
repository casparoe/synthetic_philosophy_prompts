Write a villanelle that stages the tension in using biography for moral education, focusing on the problem Plutarch navigates in his Parallel Lives: hagiography makes the subject exemplary but pedagogically distant, while an unvarnished life is relatable but may fail to provide a normative standard. Use two refrains that represent these competing demands—one insisting that the life shown must be worthy of imitation, the other insisting that only visible flaws can make the example usable for an imperfect learner. The villanelle’s formal structure, in which the refrains alternate and finally converge in the closing couplet, should do the conceptual work of showing whether these demands can be synthesized or only held in unresolved adjacency. The poem should be precise enough that someone familiar with debates about moral testimony from narrative, the hagiographic tradition, and the uses of exemplars in ethical formation would recognize the specific philosophical problem being enacted rather than merely seeing names invoked. Do not include citations, references to specific editions, or a reading list; the poem itself should carry the argument.
