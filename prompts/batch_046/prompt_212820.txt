I'm preparing the conceptual framework for my doctoral research on cultural causation and want a consistency check before I submit the workshop outline. My position combines Weberian sociology with a philosophy-of-social-science commitment to mechanistic explanation. Specifically, I hold that:

(1) Weber's Protestant ethic thesis is best interpreted as an elective-affinity relation—a reciprocal reinforcement between ascetic religious values and the spirit of capitalism—rather than as a unidirectional or monocausal claim.

(2) Cultural values are genuinely autonomous causes of economic behaviour; they are not reducible to material conditions or to institutional incentives.

(3) To avoid explanatory circularity, the content and presence of a value system must be identifiable through non-economic evidence—such as liturgical texts, catechetical structures, or ritual practice—rather than being inferred solely from the economic conduct it is invoked to explain.

(4) Nevertheless, the best evidence that a value has been genuinely internalised (as opposed to being merely doctrinally present) is behavioural: patterns of economic and social action that systematically deviate from predictions of pure material self-interest.

(5) Causal claims about culture should meet the same methodological standards as materialist explanations—explicit mechanism specification, counterfactual robustness, and the isolation of the value factor via institutional substitution (that is, holding the value constant while varying legal and organisational frameworks).

(6) Values are never causally efficacious in isolation; their explanatory power is always mediated by, and contingent upon, specific institutional and organisational arrangements.
