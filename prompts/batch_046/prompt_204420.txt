I'm working through whether an "I" can be genuinely distributed across brains, guided by my gut reactions to concrete cases rather than by Hofstadter's theory alone.

Case 1: A person's neural architecture is gradually replaced, neuron by neuron, by synthetic substrates split between two remote bodies, with continuous bidirectional feedback maintaining one uninterrupted autobiographical narrative. The system reports being one subject spread across both locations. Verdict: Yes—this is one distributed I, not two copies.

Case 2: After a corpus callosum split, each hemisphere develops distinct preferences and can answer questions independently; the patient describes feeling like two people cohabiting one body. Verdict: Two I's, co-located—not one I distributed.

Case 3: After a philosopher's death, his closest colleague internalizes his conceptual framework so thoroughly that she solves problems in his exact style and occasionally reports feeling "inhabited" by his perspective when writing in his voice. There is no direct neural link. Verdict: There is a real but partial persistence of his pattern in her, yet it is not the same I distributed—more like a high-fidelity echo.

Case 4: A perfect digital upload is created while the biological original survives unchanged; both claim continuous identity and share the same memories. Verdict: Two separate I's (duplicates), not one I distributed across substrates.

The pair that keeps pulling me in opposite directions is Case 1 and Case 4: both involve one pattern instantiated in two substrates, yet integration makes me call the first "distributed" while independence makes me call the second "duplicated." Meanwhile Case 3 denies distribution despite deep psychological integration, which suggests causal or neural coupling matters—yet I want to resist reducing the self to mere wiring.

Is there a precise principle—perhaps about first-person access, causal integration, or narrative continuity—that delivers all four verdicts? If not, which one should I overturn? Please state the principle precisely, identify which clause each case motivates, predict what it says about an unlisted case (ten people linked by a brain-computer interface who achieve a shared conscious state with no dominant individual narrative), and be explicit about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate.

Also say what would change your mind: what argument or feature of a case would convince you that psychological integration without direct neural coupling can constitute genuine distribution of the same I, and where in your reasoning is the weakest point?
