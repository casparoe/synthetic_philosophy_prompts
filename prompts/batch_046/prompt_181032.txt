I'm a high school senior writing an independent study presentation in philosophy of religion, and I want a consistency audit of my position before I present it. Here are the commitments I currently hold:

(1) Libertarian free will is correct: in precisely identical causal circumstances, an agent retains the genuine power to choose otherwise.
(2) Molinism is true: prior to any creative act, God possesses middle knowledge of all true counterfactuals of creaturely freedom, for example that if agent A were placed in situation S, A would freely perform action X.
(3) These counterfactuals are pre-volitional and brute: they depend neither on God's will nor on any prior intrinsic properties of the agent that causally determine the choice.
(4) Divine providence is exhaustive but non-coercive: God guarantees that any desired free event occurs solely through weak actualization, meaning God selects which feasible world becomes actual rather than causally producing the choice within that world.
(5) Explanations must be non-circular: if God's decision to actualize world W partly explains why the counterfactual about A in S is true, then middle knowledge collapses into knowledge that depends on God's will and Molinism fails.

I believe (4) preserves (1) because selection is not causation. But I am concerned that (3) and (5) generate a hidden tension: if the counterfactuals are ungrounded in the agent's nature and also cannot depend on God's selection of W, then there is no explanation for why they are true, which seems to make God's world-selection in (4) rationally arbitrary or impossible. At the same time, giving up the brute status in (3) seems to threaten libertarian freedom, while giving up (5) seems to destroy Molinism.

Please audit these commitments: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and rank the tensions by severity. Specify the least costly revision and what it costs me. In particular, address whether a stable middle position exists between (3) and (5) that preserves both Molinism and libertarian freedom, or whether I must abandon one. Please also separate what is settled in the philosophical literature on Molinism from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any disputed claim—such as that weak actualization fully preserves libertarian freedom—as though it were the consensus view.
