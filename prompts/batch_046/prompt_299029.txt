Write an essay on whether backward induction really destroys cooperation in teh centipede game, or if the paradox just falls apart once you relax perfect common knowledge. Specifically, consider agents who are rational but hold noisy second-order beliefs — they think the other player is probably rational, but they aren't sure, and they update that belief each round if the other passes. Does backward induction still force immediate defection, or does the noise let the game run for several rounds? Weigh the main arguments on both sides, but reach a real conclusion — either that induction survives and our cooperative intuitions are wrong, or that noisy beliefs break the chain and backward induction is mostly a theoretical curiosity. Don't end on a non-committal note; defend a verdict.
