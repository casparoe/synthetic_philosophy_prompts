My roommate is trying to trap me in a metaphysics argument about statues and clay but I think he's sneaking in an assumption that blows up into full-blown dualism. Here's his chain:

(1) The statue and the clay lump share every physical part and property at time t, yet they're not identical — the statue was created Tuesday when shaped, the clay existed since Monday.

(2) So we have two material objects sitting in exactly the same place with the same parts.

(3) If endurantism is right — objects are wholly present at each moment — then at every instant of overlap we literally have two complete things stacked on eachother.

(4) Since they share all intrinsic physical features, whatever distinguishes them must be non-physical: modal properties like "can survive being squashed flat" (the clay can, the statue can't).

(5) Now apply the same structure to a person and her brain/body at t. They coincide completely, share every neuron, but differ modally — the person dies if consciousness is permanently lost, the body can persist in a vegatative state.

(6) So anyone who buys material constitution for art has to accept that people and bodies are also distinct, coincident entities that differ only in non-physical modal essenses.

(7) But then physicalism about persons collapses: you've got two things in one place, and the mental one carries properties the physical one lacks.

He says this doesn't prove too much, it just shows constitution is incompatible with physicalism. I feel like there's a hidden slide between (4) and (5) or maybe (3) is where the rot sets in. Which step should I reject, and what's the cost? Especially I want to keep the idea that statues and clay are genuinely different — I just don't want to be forced into saying people have ghostly modal essenses.
