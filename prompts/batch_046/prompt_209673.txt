I am preparing an argumentative essay for my advanced high school history and economics seminar on Adam Smith's views regarding empire and colonies. I would like you to address the following controversy: should Smith's well-known criticisms of colonial monopoly and mercantilist policy in his writings on political economy be read as a principled moral rejection of imperial domination, or do they remain fundamentally an economic argument about capital misallocation and the fiscal-military burdens that colonies impose on the mother country? In your essay, weigh both interpretations, paying close attention to how Smith's idea of the natural progression of national wealth interacts with his observation that colonies often distort rather than enrich the governing state. Consider also whether this interaction reveals an implicit normative commitment to self-governing commercial societies rather than societies maintained by conquest. Reach and justify a definite conclusion about which interpretation is more defensible. Finally, please state how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the specific point—particularly whether Smith's relative silence on the rights and conditions of colonized indigenous peoples undermines the stronger anti-imperialist reading—about which you feel least sure.
