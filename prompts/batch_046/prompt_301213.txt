You are an expert in Nietzsche's philosophy, with particular attention to his writings on the body, health, and culture. Before adjudicating, briefly brainstorm the core conceptual distinctions—such as physiological determinism versus aesthetic self-cultivation, and anti-teleological versus biomedical conceptions of health—that frame debates about his dietary remarks. Then provide a neutral, point-by-point adjudication of the exchange below.

At a philosophy-of-medicine study group, two members—Rosa and Kai—debated Nietzsche's views on diet and digestion. I listened without taking sides. Please referee their exchange move by move, rating each argument from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say who is right about which specific point, identify any hidden tension or apparent tension that dissolves on inspection, declare who is more right overall, and note what both participants miss.

(1) Rosa opened: Nietzsche's dietary remarks should be read through digestive perspectivism. For him, food choices are interpretations of one's own physiological constitution, not applications of universal nutritional science. There is no healthy diet in general, only diets that express or suppress particular life-types.

(2) Kai replied: That overintellectualizes a direct causal claim. Nietzsche links heavy German food to heavy, resentful thought. The stomach conditions the mind materially—call it alimentary determinism. Eat like a burdened herd animal, think like one. This is physiology, not hermeneutics.

(3) Rosa answered: If determinism holds, Nietzsche's own chronic migraines and digestive failures should have made his philosophy impossible. Yet he produced some of his greatest work while physically broken. So the causal chain is broken, and diet cannot determine philosophical output.

(4) Kai countered: You confuse absence of pain with great health. For Nietzsche, great health is the strength to overcome physiological failure, not comfort. His diet was a performative symbol of self-overcoming—he enacted lightness and selection even as his body betrayed him. The failure does not refute determinism; it shows the symbolic practice operates independently of biomedical outcome, which is precisely why it escapes modern nutrition science.

(5) Rosa objected: Then you have stripped the view of empirical health content and turned it into pure aestheticism. But Nietzsche consistently insists the body conditions the spirit in material, not merely symbolic, ways. If diet has no necessary connection to actual physiological function, you have lost his materialist core and replaced it with bodily theater.

(6) Kai concluded: It is not pure theater but performative physiology: selecting one's food enacts the will to power regardless of whether the body complies. The material and the symbolic are inseparable in Nietzsche, but you wrongly demand they collapse into biomedical determinism, which he explicitly rejects.

Please adjudicate each move (1) through (6) with brief justification, order the tensions by severity, say what single revision would most improve the exchange, and identify the crucial consideration about Nietzsche's concept of health that neither party fully addresses.
