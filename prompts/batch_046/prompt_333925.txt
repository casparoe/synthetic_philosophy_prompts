(3) But Judy can now compute her revised internal distribution: within Blue she is certainly in Second, while within Red she remains certainly at Headquarters. So her unconditional expectations about specific locations should shift in lockstep with P(Red) and P(Blue).

(4) Now suppose Judy receives a second message, also conditional: "If you are at Headquarters, then you are in Red territory." By (1), this should leave her credence in being at HQ unchanged, because the message only relates HQ to Red and gives no direct news about HQ itself.

(5) Yet together these two conditionals entail that being at HQ is coextensive with being in Red, and being in Second is coextensive with being in Blue. If her credence in Blue and in HQ never shifts in response to the conditionals themselves, then no sequence of purely conditional messages can ever alter her initial distribution over territories or locations.

(6) This is absurd: a rational agent in Judy's position should be able to learn her location from a well-structured protocol of conditional radio instructions, not remain permanently suspended at her priors regardless of how many messages she receives.

(7) Therefore, Jeffrey conditioning on conditionals is incoherent, and premise (1) must be rejected.
