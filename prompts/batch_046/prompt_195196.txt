Is there a name for this? I keep running into the same move in discussiosn of liberal eugenics and procreative beneficence. First, someone defends genetic selection by saying parents have a duty to give their child the best possible life—so picking for intelligence or health is just responsible parenting, not state overreach. Then you ask "best according to who?" and they toggle to autonomy: it's not about objective welfare, it's about letting parents decide what's best for their family without interference. But then if you press them on why selecting for deafness should be treated differently from selecting for hearing, they toggle right back to objective welfare—deafness isn't a harm in the right community, but intelligence genuinely improves life prospects. So autonomy is used to block state mandates, while welfare is used to rank which autonomous choices are legitimate. It's not quite motte-and-bailey, because the two claims aren't a defensible core and an indefensible extension; they're two separate justifications that borrow authority from each other.

I've seen versions of this in debates over Savulescu's principle and in some reproductive-liberty literature, but I don't know if there's a specific term for the oscillation itself.
