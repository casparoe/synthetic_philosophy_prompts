Write a villanelle about the claim that analogy is the engine of cognition — not a comparison between already-formed concepts, but the very process by which concepts are constituted — in the spirit of the view developed by Hofstadter and Sander. The form should do philosophical work: choose two refrains that, in the opening tercet, sound like descriptions of surface-matching between fixed categories; then, as the villanelle forces them back into new contexts, let them accumulate the opposite meaning, so that the repetition itself enacts the "slippage" by which analogical projection reshapes what is being mapped. The rhyme words should also shift in connotation across the stanzas. In the final quatrain, bring both refrains together to resolve the tension between surface resemblance and essential structure without collapsing either — perhaps by suggesting that an essence is only the temporary stability left after countless analogical slips. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with the distinction between low-level feature-matching and high-level conceptual projection recognises the theory, yet it should stand as poetry for someone who has never encountered the idea. Do not include citations, footnotes, or references to specific editions or page numbers.
