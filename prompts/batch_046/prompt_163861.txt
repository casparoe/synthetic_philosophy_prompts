I am designing the final exam for an upper-level philosophy of mind and computation seminar, and I want an essay question that forces students to do original conceptual work rather than reciting standard summaries of Searle, Fodor, or Kim’s causal exclusion argument. To that end, I am introducing a deliberately contrived hypothetical: the “careenium,” a substrate of interlinked mechanical nodes whose state-transitions are fully governed by local physical laws, and within it “simmballs,” symbolic tokens that are physically instantiated only when specific substrate configurations occur. Crucially, the identity conditions for a simmball are not substrate-independent: a token counts as representing condition C precisely when the substrate enters the state that C denotes, and that token then feeds back into the substrate dynamics, modulating subsequent transitions. The symbol’s presence is therefore defined by the substrate effect it produces, yet the substrate’s organization also partly depends on the token being present.

Write an exam prompt suitable for ninety minutes, expecting roughly 800–1,000 words, that asks students whether the simmballs genuinely “push the substrate around”—exerting downward causal influence—or whether all causal work is done at the substrate level, with the symbolic vocabulary being only an interpretive overlay. To earn top marks, a student must address three specific cruxes: (i) how Kim-style causal exclusion applies when the symbolic property and the substrate state are not merely co-instantiated but definitionally intertwined; (ii) whether the feedback loop confers real causal efficacy on the symbol or collapses into circular redescription; and (iii) whether an identically behaving “zombie” careenium—lacking any symbolic interpretation—would differ, and what that counterfactual shows about the causal status of symbols. Then provide a model answer and a marking scheme totaling twenty points. The scheme should name the specific moves that separate an A answer from a B (for example, correctly applying exclusion without conflating supervenience with causation, and analyzing the loop rather than assuming it either solves or dissolves the problem), and should flag typical mistakes—such as treating the simmballs as external observers rather than embedded tokens, or appealing to intuition about the zombie case without examining the counterfactual structure. The model answer must clearly separate what is settled (e.g., that substrate dynamics are necessary conditions for token presence) from what is genuinely contested among specialists (e.g., whether symbolic properties are causally efficacious in a non-epiphenomenal sense, and whether definitional interdependence resolves mental causation). Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the answer should stand on conceptual analysis alone, and you should present no contested interpretive claim as if it were the consensus view.
