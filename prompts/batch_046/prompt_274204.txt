Write an essay on the following controversy. Hart's legal positivism holds that the validity of a law depends on its social sources rather than its moral content, whereas Fuller's natural-law theory insists that law must satisfy an internal morality of legality—clarity, generality, consistency, and the other principles—to count as law at all. Consider a hypothetical jurisdiction in which all statutory interpretation is performed by a large language model trained exclusively on legal texts: it applies rules with perfect procedural fidelity and without human moral reasoning, yet because it treats ambiguous provisions as probabilistic distributions, it regularly generates contradictory rulings in like cases. Does this scenario demonstrate that Fuller's internal morality is indispensable—since mechanical consistency is lacking—or can a refined positivism accommodate it by distinguishing between validity and the quality of adjudication? Weigh both positions carefully and defend a clear verdict. Write in British English.
