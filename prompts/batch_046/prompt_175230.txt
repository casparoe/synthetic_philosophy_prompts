I am developing an evaluative framework for normative theories that treat "the will of the people" not as an empirical aggregation but as a contested performative authorization. Before I compare specific accounts, I want the full specification of adequacy. Write the desiderata that any such theory must satisfy: the phenomena it must explain (e.g., extraction of a unitary mandate from structurally heterogeneous electorates, the relation between populist authority and constitutional constraint, the status of counter-majoritarian institutional vetoes), the distinctions it must preserve without collapse (e.g., between normative authorization and statistical aggregation, between a collective political agent and a distributive sum of preferences, between legitimate popular sovereignty and arbitrary majoritarian will), and the conceptual or empirical boundaries it cannot cross (e.g., the irreducible pluralism of modern societies, the possibility that different institutional channels—elections, referenda, movements—yield genuinely incompatible mandates). Pay particular attention to the problem of disaggregated popular sovereignty: must the theory synthesize contradictory expressions of will into a single popular mandate, or can it accommodate plural, non-hierarchical popular wills without reducing them to liberal interest-group pluralism? For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or tension behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable conditions of adequacy and which are commitments of particular camps (proceduralist, decisionist, radical-democratic). Finally, indicate briefly how the three standard families—aggregationist proceduralism, exceptionalist decisionism, and pluralist radical democracy—measure against your list, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves.
