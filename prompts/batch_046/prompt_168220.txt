I'm presenting a twenty-minute paper at an interdisciplinary workshop on algorithmic governance defending what I call "context-sensitive deliberative gatekeeping." The thesis: an AI assistant should be permitted—indeed, designed—to apply temporary procedural blocks to user requests that violate the user's own stable, previously articulated deliberative commitments, but only when the contradiction is traceable to identifiable situational distortions (time pressure, emotional arousal, framing effects) rather than a genuine change of heart. The block is not a refusal based on harm to others or objective welfare; it is a brief, explainable delay that prompts reflection, after which the user can override. I argue this preserves procedural autonomy rather than violating it.
