After three decades in experimental condensed-matter physics, I have spent my retirement reading philosophy of perception, and I find Molyneux's problem—the case of a congenitally blind person suddenly given sight—more subtle than the usual empiricist-versus-rationalist dichotomy allows. Rather than rehearse that well-worn debate, I want you to evaluate five distinct explanatory architectures I have outlined, ranking them from 1 (most adequate) to 5 (least adequate) for a specific variant of the case: the subject is shown a cube and a sphere, but only through degraded visual input—flat silhouettes, heavy noise, no tactile access—so that depth and texture are largely unavailable. For each rank, provide a concise justification of no more than two sentences, and do not include bibliographies or citations.

The five positions are as follows. First, strict Lockean empiricism: geometric form is entirely constructed from prior tactile exploration, so vision in isolation, particularly when degraded, cannot support identification. Second, rationalist structuralism: the intellect carries innate geometric schemas that permit direct recognition of cubicity and sphericity regardless of sensory noise or modality. Third, embodied enactivism: object recognition is not an internal representational match but an achievement of active sensorimotor exploration, which is precluded here because the subject receives only a passive, restrained glimpse. Fourth, predictive processing: the brain holds cross-modal generative priors about three-dimensional structure and updates them with visual prediction error, a process that should succeed unless the degraded input renders the error signal uninformative. Fifth, the degeneracy-constraint thesis: stable cross-modal binding requires redundant, non-degenerate information across sensory channels, meaning that noisy, unimodal visual data—lacking any tactile or depth redundancy—will fail to stabilize an object representation regardless of innate endowment or learning history. Please rank these according to how well they account for success or failure in this degraded-input scenario, with brief reasoning for each position.
