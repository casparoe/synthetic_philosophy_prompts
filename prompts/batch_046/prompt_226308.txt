I have been observing a persistent stall in the philosophical discussion over whether "Western values" denotes a coherent normative cluster or functions primarily as a rhetorical instrument of geopolitical boundary-drawing. One side treats the phrase as tracking a historically stable bundle—liberal-democratic institutions, individual autonomy, secular public reason, and market-oriented exchange—insisting that these elements cohere both conceptually and genealogically. The opposing side treats the phrase as essentially contested, arguing that its content shifts with strategic need and that attempts to stabilize it invariably smuggle in universalist moral claims under the guise of cultural description. Both camps invoke the same broad intellectual references—Weber on rationalization, Rawls on political liberalism, Huntington on civilizational identity—yet neither has persuaded the other, and the exchange has begun to repeat itself without advancing. I am inclined to think the impasse is not mainly about normative ethics or intellectual history, but about what I will call "civilizational grammar": the implicit rules that govern whether a value is attributed to a civilization as an internal achievement or projected onto it as an external standard of judgment. On my hypothesis, the debate is partly verbal—disputing the conditions of attribution rather than the values themselves—and partly substantive, turning on whether such grammar can be made explicit without circular appeal to the very values being classified. Is this diagnosis accurate? More precisely, is the disagreement a hidden empirical dispute about cultural transmission, a bedrock normative clash about the legitimacy of universalist claims, a conceptual confusion between genealogical origin and normative justification, or genuinely two different questions being pursued under one heading? And if the stall does indeed arise from this grammatical layer, what form of progress—conceptual clarification, historical genealogy, or normative argument—would be required to move it?
