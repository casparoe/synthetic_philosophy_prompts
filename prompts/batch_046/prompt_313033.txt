Write a deadpan satirical memo of approximately 600 words, formatted as an internal compliance audit with standard headers (TO, FROM, RE, DATE) and numbered findings, issued by a fictional Philadelphia oversight body reviewing Benjamin Franklin’s documented practice of moral self-perfection. The memo should treat Franklin’s autobiographical project—his chart of thirteen virtues, his weekly black-spot ledger, his pragmatic relaxation of Order, and his ironic difficulty with Humility—as if it were a modern corporate ethics program subject to rigorous regulatory audit. The satire should target the contemporary self-optimization culture that frames ethical growth as measurable, iterative, and audit-ready, not Franklin himself. To satisfy the competing demands of specificity and accessibility without relying on obscure scholarship, inject a fictional conceptual framework: the memo should invoke the invented “Philadelphia Doctrine of Virtue Continuity,” a regulatory principle holding that regression in a previously mastered virtue threatens not merely one’s record but the continuity of one’s moral identity, and should debate whether Franklin’s decision to maintain strict Chastity and Industry while allowing himself irregular Order constitutes a permissible “virtue portfolio rebalancing” or an illegitimate “moral fork” that spawns a successor self. The voice must remain entirely deadpan—no winking, no anachronistic slang—so that the absurdity arises solely from applying bureaucratic logic to Franklin’s actual reasoning, including his belief that habituation makes virtue easier, his admission that he never reached perfection, and his self-aware pride in having achieved Humility. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or direct quotations from Franklin’s writings; refer to his methods and conclusions in paraphrase only. The piece should be readable to someone unfamiliar with audit culture and rewarding to someone who recognizes Franklin’s actual grid system and his particular struggles with Order and Humility.
