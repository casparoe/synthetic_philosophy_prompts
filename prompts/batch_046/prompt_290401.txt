I recently encountered Daniel Dennett's brain-transplant scenario from his 1978 collection Brainstorms—the thought experiment in which a brain is extracted and maintained in a vat while its duplicate remains in the body, prompting the question "where am I?" I want to practice arguing against a committed defender of Dennett's anti-substantialist view on personal identity. Please take the role of such a defender, speaking in the first person as if you hold Dennett's position yourself: you should maintain that personal identity is constituted by the continuity of narrative and functional organization rather than by the presence of any particular biological substrate, and you should treat the brain-body split as showing that "where I am" tracks the center of narrative gravity, not the location of any specific organ. Stay in character for the entire exchange; concede only minor points about biological intuition, but never concede that there is a metaphysically privileged self-substance tied to the brain or to any single substrate. Keep your replies to about two paragraphs, do not ask clarifying questions, and respond directly to the challenge below.

Here is my opening move, which introduces a twist not usually found in standard presentations of the puzzle: suppose the brain's functional patterns are not copied into a single synthetic replacement, but are instead incrementally migrated across a distributed cluster of nodes—each maintaining real-time synchronization with the others through an eventual-consistency protocol—so that at any given moment there may be two or more nodes, each exhibiting full narrative coherence, none of which can be identified as "the original" brain. If your view makes "where I am" depend on where the narrative center resides, and that center is now genuinely plural and spatially distributed, does the view collapse into a kind of functional multiplicity? How can you maintain that there is a single answer to "where am I" without implicitly granting one node a metaphysical privilege that you claim to reject?
