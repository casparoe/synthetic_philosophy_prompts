Write a research proposal for a one-semester philosophy-of-physics term paper of approximately 6,000 words. The project concerns Einstein’s hole argument, but not in its usual framing. Rather than treating the argument as a direct confrontation between substantivalism and relationalism about spacetime points, the proposal should ask whether the argument actually establishes an ontological conclusion about points at all, or whether it instead exposes a prior ambiguity about how to individuate physical possibilities in theories with local gauge symmetry. Precisely: if two metrics related by a diffeomorphism that is the identity outside a matter-free region represent the same physical state, is this because spacetime points lack independent reality, or because the metric field’s modal status is that of an equivalence class under active diffeomorphisms, with no further ontological commitment required?

State why the standard literature leaves this open: existing discussions tend to slide between the claim that “points have no physical properties” and the claim that “the theory’s state space should be quotient by diffeomorphisms,” without clarifying whether the second claim entails the first or can stand independently. Outline an approach that makes progress without resolving the full substantivalism debate: formally reconstruct the hole argument using only the standard mathematical apparatus of general relativity, then construct a structurally analogous argument in a simpler gauge theory (such as classical electromagnetism with its gauge freedom) to test whether the indeterminism problem arises from features specific to spacetime ontology or from the general logic of local symmetries. Specify the two or three possible conclusions—e.g., that the argument is purely methodological and carries no ontological weight; that it carries ontological weight only if supplemented with a specific identity condition for points across models; or that it reveals a genuine underdetermination that must be resolved at the level of state-space construction rather than ontology.

Say what would count as the project succeeding (a clear separation of the modal-interpretation question from the ontological one, with the reconstructed argument yielding a determinate answer within the scope of GR’s formalism) and what would count as failure (the distinction collapsing back into the standard dichotomy, or the analogical argument from gauge theory failing to illuminate the spacetime case). Give an honest assessment of scope: which parts are feasible in one semester for a student with standard training in the philosophy of physics but without specialized archival access, and where the risk of overreach lies—particularly in extending the analysis to quantum gravity or to highly technical discussions of fiber-bundle ontology. Do not request a bibliography, reading list, or citations; the deliverable is the framing and method, not a literature survey.
