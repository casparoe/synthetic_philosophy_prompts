One proposed dissolution—the protocol-relative reply—holds that 'green' and 'grue' belong to different inductive protocols (a color-stable protocol versus a time-indexed protocol), so there is no single correct projection rule; selecting a protocol is a pragmatic decision about framework design, not a logical discovery. Evaluate this reply. Does it genuinely dissolve Goodman's riddle, or does it relocate the problem into the choice of protocol? In particular, address whether it avoids circularity and how it compares to an entrenchment-based solution.

Student answer: The protocol-relative reply succeeds in dissolving the riddle. Goodman's mistake was assuming that induction must be governed by a single universal logic applicable to all predicates. Once we see that 'green' operates within a color-stable protocol and 'grue' within a time-indexed one, there is no logical contradiction—each predicate projects correctly within its own framework. The choice between frameworks is pragmatic, analogous to Carnap's view that inductive logic is conventional. This avoids circularity because we are not defining projectibility in terms of past projections; we are simply selecting a framework. Consequently, this reply is stronger than the entrenchment solution, which depends on the accidental historical fact that we have used 'green' more often.
