I'm working through Schrödinger's adoption of Advaita Vedanta — specifically his claim that consciousness is singular rather than multiplied across empirical minds, and the corresponding distinction between the illusory multiplicity of the empirical ego (associated with māyā) and the unitary reality of Ātman-Brahman — and I'm trying to see whether this metaphysics can be turned into a genuine interpretation of quantum mechanics rather than merely an ethical or mystical consolation. It seems to me that if the appearance of separate observers is itself a conventional construct sustained by identification with the plural self, then the measurement problem dissolves in a way that avoids both Copenhagen discontinuity and Everettian branching: there is never genuinely "a system observed by an observer," because the dualistic framing presupposes the very multiplicity that Vedanta denies at the paramārthika level. On this reading, quantum superposition is not an objective physical ambiguity that awaits collapse or decoherence, but rather the formal expression of reality as seen from the standpoint of the illusory plural self that has not realized its identity with the one consciousness; entanglement, similarly, does not link physically distinct systems but reveals that the boundary between observed and observer is ultimately conventional.

From this I'm inclined to propose what might be called a Vedantic interpretation: the cat in Schrödinger's paradox is neither alive nor dead in any independent sense, not because the wavefunction remains uncollapsed, but because the very formulation "the cat, observed by me" embeds the dualistic structure that the metaphysics rejects. Am I correct that this provides a coherent third path, or is there a hidden circularity in using a doctrine about the nature of consciousness to resolve a problem that was formulated precisely within the physical and mathematical framework that such a doctrine denies? I am especially uncertain whether Schrödinger intended this connection as an interpretation of the formalism itself, or whether treating it as such commits a category error by conflating epistemic or metaphysical unity with the absence of physical interaction. If the reasoning is flawed, where exactly does the move from "consciousness is one" to "superposition is an artifact of pluralistic illusion" break down?
