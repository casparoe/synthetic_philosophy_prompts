Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, with two refrains that alternate—taking Buddhist anattā (no-self) as its subject and treating the analytic narrative theory of personal identity (the view that identity is the continuity of a self-telling story) as its interlocutor. Refrain A should voice the narrative position: something like “The story holds, yet no one holds it,” capturing continuity without substance. Refrain B should voice the Abhidharma reduction: something like “Form, feeling, thought—no owner, no ground,” naming the skandhas without a substrate. Structure the poem so that the first five stanzas move through the chariot analogy from the Milindapañha—Nagasena dismantling King Milinda’s search for a charioteer—then through the five aggregates, and then into the problem that even the awareness of impermanence is itself impermanent. The turn should come in the final quatrain: realize that if there is no observer outside the aggregates, then the poem’s own speaker is also only dependently arisen, not a hidden soul reporting from safety. Let the refrains shift meaning each time they return, just as each skandha arises and ceases without a persistent bearer. Do not resolve the tension in a sermon or a shrug; the closing couplet should leave open whether “I” am merely my narrative or whether the narrative itself has no “I” behind it. The result should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with both the Milindapañha dialogue and the narrative-identity debate recognizes the positions, and should stand as a real poem for someone who knows neither. Build it from the concepts alone; no bibliography or citation is needed.
