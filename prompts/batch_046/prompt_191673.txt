Consider a hypothetical cognitive architecture in which belief-states and desire-states reside in separate, non-interacting modules. A single reporting module reads both, but applies a hard consistency filter: it never outputs an internal report that assigns the agent belief B together with desire not-B whenever B and not-B stand in direct logical contradiction according to a fixed formal grammar. As a result, the agent may genuinely hold B in the belief module while desiring not-B in the desire module, yet every introspective report pairs B with wanting B, or not-B with wanting not-B. Explain the conceptual issue this raises for self-deception: does the architecture permit the agent to hide a truth from herself in the traditional intentional sense, or does it replace self-deception with structural dissociation in which contradiction is never internally registered? In particular, address whether the agent can be said to know of the contradiction if no internal report ever captures it, and whether this undermines the standard claim that self-deception requires an active, intentional division within a unified mind.
