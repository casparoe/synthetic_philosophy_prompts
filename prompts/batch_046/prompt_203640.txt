I sketched this for my grad seminar on collective intentionality and now I'm second-guessing myself because the conclusion feels like it nukes teh whole field. Here's the chain my student walked in with:

(1) If A and B jointly intend to do X, then A's practical reasoning must be partly shaped by B's corresponding reasoning — not just causally influenced, but constitutively tied to it. Otherwise it's just parallel play.
(2) Now consider "latent joint projects" — like a decentralized boycott where people join via an algorithmic feed, never meet, and each only sees a filtered subset of other participants. Each contributor's reason is "this cause is just" plus "enough others seem to be in," but that "enough others" is mediated by platform metrics, not by direct knowledge of anyone's specific reasoning.
(3) So in these cases, no individual's reasoning is constitutively tied to any specific other individual's reasoning; it's tied only to an aggregate signal.
(4) If that's not joint intention, then any collective action that scales past direct acquaintance — protests, open-source builds, even large choirs — is also out, since scaling always replaces pairwise constitutive ties with aggregate signals.
(5) So either we reject joint intentionality for all large-scale collectives, or we say that being tied to an aggregate signal still counts as constitutive interdependence.
(6) But if (5) is right, then two strangers who happen to walk in step because they both heard teh same metronome app also have jointly intended to walk in step — their reasoning is tied to the same aggregate signal.
(7) That proves too much: it erases teh line between joint action and accidental coordination.
(8) Therefore (1) is false, and there is no meaningful distinction between joint and parallel intentionality.

My instinct is to keep (1) and reject (5), but then I'm stuck saying large-scale boycotts aren't collective actions, which feels wrong. Is there an equivocation in "constitutively tied" between (1) and (3)? Or maybe (4) sneaks in a false dichotomy about what scaling does? Where does it actually break, and if the arguement is valid, what's the least painful premise to ditch?
