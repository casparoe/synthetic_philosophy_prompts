Her central claim is that archival silences are not accidental omissions or passive gaps but are actively produced by what she terms catalogical foreclosure—the way descriptive standards and indexing protocols render certain forms of collective life literally unsearchable. She further argues that the shift to post-custodial digital repositories and algorithmic tagging does not resolve these silences but transforms them into metadata invisibilities that are harder to detect because they present themselves as technical neutrality. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate this thesis. I want at least one question probing how her framework distinguishes a genuinely unindexable social practice from one that is merely poorly documented; one question pressing whether her account requires intentional malice on the part of archivists or can operate with purely structural bias; and one question asking what empirical evidence could show that algorithmic tagging creates a qualitatively new silence rather than reproducing an older descriptive one. For each of these three, include a sharp follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—particularly the move of invoking community-controlled description or open-access digitization as remedies—and ensure that at least two other questions in the set anticipate standard objections from historians who insist that archives have always been incomplete and that this observation is not news.
