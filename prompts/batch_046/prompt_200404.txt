Write a proposal for a one-semester philosophy-of-history term paper (around 7,000 words) on whether presentism is unavoidable specifically at the explanatory level, not merely the moral one. State the precise question: historians reject normative presentism—judging past actors by current ethical standards—yet they still invoke present-day causal categories (rational-choice frameworks, institutional-economics concepts, climate-determinist models) to explain outcomes. Is there a defensible principle that distinguishes permissible retrospective description from impermissible retrospective explanation, and does it apply symmetrically to rapid political events and to slow-moving structural conditions?

Explain why existing answers leave this open: the dominant debate treats presentism as a failure of moral neutrality, leaving largely unexamined whether explanatory presentism is structurally necessary because causal explanation requires identifying counterfactual paths not taken, while the criteria for what counts as an “available” alternative are always articulated in present conceptual vocabulary.

Propose a conceptual rather than archival approach: analyze the logical structure of three accepted explanation-types (intentional, structural, and contingency-based) to test whether each requires importing present causal frameworks, and evaluate a proposed constraint—counterfactual vocabulary neutrality, which demands that any explanatory counterfactual be expressible in terms the historical agents could have recognized—against standard historiographical debates. Justify this over the obvious alternatives of surveying primary texts or offering a purely normative prescription for historians.

Specify the deliverable: a precise criterion separating descriptive anachronism (tolerable) from explanatory presentism, with a demonstration of whether it holds for both event-centered narratives and long-duration analyses. Define failure honestly: the constraint either collapses into a descriptivist ban that makes causal explanation impossible, or proves so permissive that it licenses any present framework.

Assess scope for one semester: the project avoids primary-source excavation and relies on widely discussed examples, but flag the risk that the description/explanation boundary may blur, potentially requiring more case analysis than the word limit allows. Refer to positions and arguments without citation; do not include a bibliography, reading list, or literature review by citation, and supply all references yourself.
