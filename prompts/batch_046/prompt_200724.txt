ive been chewing over pain asymbolia and i think ive landed on a slightly odd view: the badness of pain isnt intrinsic to the raw phenomenal quality at all, but is entirely imported by a representational-cum-evaluative layer that tags the sensation as tissue damage and demands avoidance. patients with asymbolia still feel the intensity — they describe it as strong pressure or heat — but they dont suffer; some even report curiosity or a kind of detached interest, which suggests the 'ouch' itself is closer to neutral bodily intensity than to suffering. my claim is that if you stripped away that damage-representation, pain would be affectively on a par with vigorous massage or deep warmth. so: what would genuinely count against this? i dont want the usual "but pain just is bad" — i want the hard defeaters. which neuropsych findings or hypothetical patient profiles would be fatal, which would only be awkward, and does the view risk collapsing into a general anti-affectivism about itch, nausea, or pleasure? also, which objections would a committed defender concede actually hurt the view, and which would they dismiss as conflating the sensory core with its usual framing? rank them from fatal to merely embarrassing. answer in british english please — and feel free to be casual, a typo or two is totally fine.
