I hold the embedded-agency thesis that any rational agent, being a physical subsystem of the world it models, cannot achieve the representational closure presupposed by universal, atemporal decision theory: its self-model is causally entangled with the dynamics it predicts, so practical reasoning must remain indexical, locally bounded, and incapable of exact replication across structurally isomorphic subsystems — call this the causal-embeddedness constraint. What would count against it? I want an honest map of its exposure: which formal constructions (for example, causally embedded agents that nevertheless maintain stable, replicable preference orderings), which conceptual distinctions (say, between physical containment and logical dependence, or between causal embedding and epistemic closure), and which theoretical or empirical developments in the behaviour of artificial agents would genuinely weaken or defeat the constraint? Please distinguish the defeaters that committed embedded-agency theorists ought themselves to concede as damaging — perhaps demonstrations that embedded systems can approximate universal rationality without representational closure — from those they could legitimately dismiss as misapplying classical frameworks to non-classical agents. Rank these from fatal to merely awkward. Finally, assess whether the thesis risks being qualified — restricted to biological substrates, particular scales of complexity, or specific representational architectures — into unfalsifiability, and whether such insulation is a theoretical vice for a claim of this kind. Please answer in British English.
