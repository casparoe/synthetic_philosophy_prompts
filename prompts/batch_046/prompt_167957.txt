For a long-running rationalist forum thread on intertemporal rationality, I want a decision tree—not a list of named schools—that sorts people by what they actually believe about pure time preference: whether temporal distance alone can justify caring less about a future welfare state. Build the instrument from logical space first, then map positions onto the leaves.

The tree should have seven or eight leaves and must include at least these: (A) strict zero pure discounting on welfare; (B) exponential discounting justified solely by risk, mortality, or epistemic uncertainty; (C) hyperbolic discounting accepted as normatively appropriate for individual planning but not for social policy; (D) agent-relative preference—rational for me to weight my near-future welfare more heavily due to direct identification, but irrational for an impartial planner to do so; (E) the view that pure time preference is incoherent because temporal location lacks intrinsic axiological weight; and (F) a split position that permits discounting goods but not welfare.

Each branch node should be a concrete case or claim answerable without knowing the taxonomy labels. Use scenarios such as: "If you must prevent the same magnitude of harm to a specific person either tomorrow or in exactly one hundred years, with no change in identity, probability, or causal chain, does the date affect your choice?" And: "If your future self is psychologically continuous but not bodily continuous, do you have the same reason to prevent harm to that successor as to yourself now?" Include at least one logically possible combination of answers that no standard position occupies, and briefly diagnose why—whether incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Explicitly flag any pair of leaves the tree genuinely cannot separate—positions that agree on every practical case but diverge only on underlying metaphysical justification. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. The audience is philosophically literate but not necessarily trained in welfare economics; avoid dependence on obscure named formalisms or specific historical texts. Keep the framing formal and precise, and ensure every leaf is reachable through a path of answers that do not require recognizing philosopher-names in advance.
