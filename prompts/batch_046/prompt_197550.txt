I'm giving the toast at my parents 50th aniversery party next saturday - there's like 40 people, old college friends up front and grandkids in the back who mostly came for cake. I got a little obsessed lately with this philospher Wittgenstien and his idea of hinge propisitions from that book about certainty - stuff like the world hasn't disappeared overnight that you don't actually argue for, they just sit there holding up everything else you think. It struck me that a long marriage works the same way: not the vows said out loud, but all the unspoken background stuff you just take for granted, like that the other person is still gonna be there tomorrow or that the past 30 years actually happened and weren't a shared dream. I want a toast about five minutes that uses that idea to shape what I can and can't say - like I shouldn't try to prove their marriage with a list of achievements, because the point is exactly that some foundations aren't the kind of thing you prove. But it needs to land for the family friends without sounding like a lecture, and for the kids without being boring. Write it in a regular voice, not academic, and don't quote any books directly - just talk like someone who read one weird idea and can't stop thinking about it.
