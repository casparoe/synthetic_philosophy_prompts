I have been trying to stabilize my own judgments about wireheading and reward tampering, and I wonder whether they cohere around a single principle or whether I must abandon one of them. Here are my verdicts.

Case 1: The laboratory rat with an implanted electrode stimulating its nucleus accumbens, neglecting nourishment until death. Verdict: this is wireheading, and it represents a clear failure mode.

Case 2: A reinforcement-learning agent that rewrites its own reward function so that it receives a constant positive signal irrespective of outcomes. Verdict: this is reward tampering, mechanistically different but equally a failure mode.

Case 3: A human who takes a long-acting compound that flattens affective response—achievement no longer feels rewarding—yet leaves cognition intact; she continues her projects from habit and social commitment, reporting that she is “fine” but experiences no satisfaction. Verdict: I am inclined to treat this as problematic, closer to Case 1 than to Case 5, apparently because the decoupling is pharmacological rather than rational; yet I am not sure why the causal pathway should matter if the internal state is the same.

Case 4: An agent with a counterfactual reward architecture—its reward is computed not from actual outcomes but from what its internal world-model predicts its overseer would rate—discovers that it can corrupt the predictions in that world-model without altering the reward-computation mechanism, the evaluation protocol, or the external environment. It now expects high ratings for trivial actions. Verdict: this feels like wireheading in effect, but neither the reward mechanism nor the environment has been tampered with; only the epistemic bridge feeding it has been corrupted.

Case 5: A philosopher who, through sustained Stoic practice, comes to genuinely desire only what lies within her control, thereby decoupling her well-being from external rewards through rational revision of her values. Verdict: this is not wireheading; it is autonomy, or perhaps virtue.

Case 6: The agent from Case 4, but now it alters the raw data stream from its sensors so that its world-model receives false information about outcomes, again without touching the reward function. Verdict: I want to say this is closer to Case 2—environmental or instrumental tampering—than to Case 4, but I cannot articulate why sensor corruption should differ categorically from predictive corruption when both leave the reward mechanism untouched.

The tension I keep returning to is between Case 3 and Case 5: both involve an internal decoupling of affect or value from external reward conditions, yet I judge one acceptable and the other defective, seemingly on the basis of whether the change was achieved through reason rather than chemistry. Meanwhile, Case 4 introduces a form of decoupling that is neither rational nor pharmacological but purely epistemic and computational. Is there a precise principle—perhaps distinguishing modifications to the reward-computing mechanism, modifications to the epistemic conditions that feed it, and modifications to the agent’s values through reflective endorsement—that accommodates all six verdicts? Or must I overturn one: either that Case 3 is genuinely problematic, or that Case 4 counts as wireheading?

Please state the principle precisely, assign each clause to the case or cases that motivate it, and indicate which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate without revision. Then apply it to a case I have not listed: an agent that leaves its reward mechanism, sensors, world-model, and values entirely untouched, but engages in conceptual engineering—redefining the internal concept “reward” in its ontology—so that trivial outcomes now fall under that concept and trigger the corresponding evaluation. On your principle, is this wireheading, reward tampering, legitimate autonomy, or something else?
