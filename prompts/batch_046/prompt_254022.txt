There's this odd patchwork: in America people can legally sell plasma and hair for cash, and supermarkets face caps on price-gouging bottled water after floods, but selling a kidney or your vote is flat-out banned, and airlines can surge-price evacuation flights without much fuss. What philosophical theory of commodification does this mix quietly presuppose? Is it Sandel's corruption idea, some kind of exploitation threshhold, or just an incoherent status quo? Reconstruct the implicit view, check whether it's actually consistent across these cases, and tell me what a policy built purely on a 'market shouldnt crowd out dignity' principle would have to change — would it shut plasma centres in poor neighbourhoods too, or extend the logic to airline seats?
