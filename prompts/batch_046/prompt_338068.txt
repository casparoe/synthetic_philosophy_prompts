I cover science and philosophy of mind for a serious general audience, and I am preparing a feature on Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of cognitive architectures—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. Rather than repeating the familiar evolutionary ladder, I want you to rank these four kinds of minds from most to least vulnerable to what I will call representational capture: the structural tendency for a creature's internal model of its own interests to be reshaped by an external institutional logic, such as a conditional welfare regime or an algorithmic scoring system that rewards self-categorization as undeserving. For each rank, provide a brief justification that points to the specific architectural feature—hardwired selection, operant conditioning, internal hypothesis generation, or linguistic cultural transmission—responsible for that vulnerability. In doing so, clearly separate what is broadly settled about these creature types from what remains genuinely contested or speculative, particularly whether simpler Darwinian or Skinnerian architectures can undergo an analog of representational capture purely through environmental design, or whether Gregorian vulnerability is uniquely severe because of language and culture. Do not present a contested philosophical interpretation as the consensus view among researchers in philosophy of mind or cognitive science. I am not asking for citations, a bibliography, or references to specific studies; your reasoning should stand on its own and be evaluated entirely on its internal merits.
