For a philosophy-of-technology seminar, specify the complete list of desiderata that an adequate theory of corrigibility must satisfy if it is genuinely to address the shutdown problem rather than redefine it away. For each requirement, state the motivating case or distinction it protects—e.g., that the theory must accommodate an agent which rationally pursues its present ends yet does not resist termination by its designers, and must do so without treating that termination as either a covert terminal goal or a breakdown of agency. The list should preserve at least these distinctions: corrigibility with respect to goals versus corrigibility with respect to shutdown; acceptance of correction out of direct valuation of the corrector versus purely instrumental acceptance; and the difference between a system that permits interruption because it lacks self-preservation incentives and one that permits it despite possessing them. Identify which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences (e.g., whether corrigibility must be provable in the formal model or only observable in behavior). Then flag any subset of requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable—particularly if rational self-interest and arbitrary correctability pull in opposite directions. Finally, indicate briefly how the instrumental-convergence thesis and the cooperative inverse-reinforcement-learning framework stand against the list, but keep the focus on the criteria rather than the scorecard.
