Here is my rough position on Strawsonian reactive attitudes and moral responsibility, and I want an audit of whether the package holds together or whether I am borrowing incompatible motivations. (1) I take Strawson’s core claim seriously: moral responsibility practices are grounded in reactive attitudes—resentment, gratitude, indignation—rather than in metaphysical free will. (2) I think these attitudes are domain-sensitive rather than uniform: a creditor’s resentment and a betrayed partner’s feeling share family resemblance but differ in internal structure, and both can be appropriate without either presupposing libertarian agency. (3) I deny that determinism threatens responsibility on this view, since reactive attitudes are interpersonal responses to quality of will, not judgments about causal ultimacy. (4) I also hold that strong self-deception about one’s own motives is impossible—there is no stable mental architecture in which one genuinely believes one acted maliciously and simultaneously genuinely believes one acted benevolently in the very same respect. (5) Nevertheless, I think holding a reactive attitude implicitly represents the agent as having possessed a minimal agential openness: not libertarian freedom, but the capacity, at the moment of action, to have been responsive to reasons in a way that was not fully fixed by prior causes. (6) Finally, I accept that Strawson’s objective attitude—viewing a person clinically or managerially—is genuinely available, and I think it can be layered with reactive attitudes in different registers without contradiction.

Do these six hang together? Specifically: does (5)’s agential openness smuggle back the metaphysical freedom that (3) rejects? Does (2)’s domain-sensitivity make (5) hopelessly ambiguous across contexts? Is there a hidden tension between (4)’s denial of self-deception and the obvious phenomenon of moral self-blindness, or does that dissolve once we separate belief about motives from emotional self-assessment? And does (6)’s compatibility claim undermine (1)’s grounding thesis, since it permits treating responsible agents exactly as we treat non-responsible ones in some registers?

Audit, do not lecture: rank the tensions by severity, state for each what must give and what that costs the rest of the package, and flag any apparent conflicts that turn out harmless upon inspection. Put the bottom line up front. Maximum 400 words.
