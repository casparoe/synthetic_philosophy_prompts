I am an undergraduate in a political philosophy seminar and a voting member of our student-run housing cooperative’s governance council. On Thursday the council votes on a proposal to abolish our elected representatives and replace them with direct democratic votes on every budget line, membership dispute, and disciplinary expulsion. Supporters invoke Spinoza’s view that democracy is the most absolute form of sovereignty because the collective conatus remains identical with the group’s shared power, while opponents invoke his warning that a multitude governed by imagination and superstition rather than by rational common notions becomes more dangerous than any monarch. Draft the council’s decision memo recommending either rejection or adoption with strict procedural conditions; it must state the bottom line, apply Spinoza’s distinction between rational common notions and passionate imagination specifically to the risk that direct votes could expel dissenters over ideological disagreement, honestly treat the strongest democratic argument, and propose a concrete safeguard with a clear time frame if conditions are chosen. Present the memo as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
