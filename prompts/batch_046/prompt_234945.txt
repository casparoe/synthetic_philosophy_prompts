Two people in my writing workshop fought over a settlement in my novel where citizens literally barter moral rules — like giving up an animal-welfare ban to lock in universal healthcare. (1) Maya said it's plain gains from trade: if Group A values health more than non-interference and Group B values the reverse, swapping priorities puts each closer to what they actually care about. (2) Leo said that's a catagory error — moral values arn't goods with prices, so "closer to what they care about" already sneaks in a hidden common scale that doesn't exist between incommensurable frameworks. (3) Maya came back that real politics does this constantly — carbon taxes for labor deregulation, religious exemptions for funding — and dismissing it as incoherent doesn't explain why those bargains stabilize societies. (4) Leo said those aren't true compromises but power asymmetries wearing exchange clothing, and that "both sides win" just lets utilitarian welfare smuggle in a universal currency that colonizes every other value system.

Can you adjudicate move by move — rate each of the four from clearly mistaken to clearly right — say what both are missing, especially whether moral compromise can ever be a true gain rather than a masked defeat, and don't just declare a winner?
