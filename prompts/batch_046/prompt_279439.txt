I'm a doctoral student in applied ethics, and I've been trying to systematise my own intuitions about food-related harm after a long discussion with colleagues in our environmental philosophy seminar. I find myself holding a cluster of verdicts about different production systems that I can't easily reconcile, and I want to know whether there's a coherent principle behind them or whether some have to be abandoned. Here is how I stand on several concrete cases.

First, I think factory-farmed chicken is clearly impermissible: the intensity of suffering per unit of nutrition, combined with the sheer number of lives ended, makes it one of the worst ordinary choices available. Second, I think pasture-raised beef from a system that uses no crop feed—where a single cow lives well for several years and is killed with minimal distress—can be permissible, despite the direct killing of a sentient being, because the life is good and the death is swift. Third, I think a conventional plant-based diet reliant on annual grain and legume cultivation is not clearly better: mechanised harvesting and pest control kill field rodents, insects, and other small animals, and I feel that this indirect, dispersed harm matters in a way that makes the plant option morally comparable to, or even worse than, the pasture-beef option, though I am less certain about this comparison. Fourth, I think wild-caught fish that suffer briefly during capture and that involve no feed-crop footprint are wrong, but differently wrong—perhaps less gravely wrong than chicken, yet still impermissible because the killing is direct and avoidable. Fifth, I think a hypothetical cell-cultured beef that eliminates on-farm suffering but still relies on foetal bovine serum harvested from slaughtered animals is particularly objectionable: it parasitically depends on the very harm it claims to have removed.

The tension I keep returning to is between direct and indirect authorship of harm, and between the number of lives affected and the quality of the lives lived. I think pasture beef is permissible despite direct killing, yet I think crop agriculture is seriously wrong despite being indirect; I think wild fish are impermissible despite having good lives prior to capture, yet I think the beef cow's death is acceptable despite being intentional. Is there one principle—perhaps involving proximate authorship, sentience-weighted life quality, or ecological displacement—that delivers all five verdicts? If not, which verdict should I overturn, and what does the resulting principle predict for a case I haven't listed: a fungal mycelium protein grown on agricultural waste that requires no animal input but depends on a pesticide-intensive supply chain that harms soil invertebrates?

Please state any principle precisely, indicate which case motivates each clause, and be explicit about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate.
