As a doctoral student in medical ethics studying relational conceptions of autonomy, I need a reusable decision procedure—structured like a checklist or ordered flowchart in words—that a clinical ethics mediator can run in about thirty minutes when family members invoke Ubuntu-based relational personhood to challenge an adult patient’s refusal of life-sustaining treatment. The mediator has no legal authority to override the patient and must deliver a sign-off-ready recommendation to the attending physician. Translate the philosophical claim that personhood is constituted through relationship rather than atomic self-ownership into concrete steps: how to distinguish a genuine Ubuntu-derived obligation from a post-hoc family interest; how to map the patient’s actual narrative-identity web against the claimed relational duties; which distinctions must survive simplification and which can be dropped; and exactly where judgment calls remain unavoidable and must be flagged. Apply the procedure to three hypothetical cases: (1) a competent elderly patient refusing dialysis to avoid burdening his grandchildren, while the family insists on reciprocal care obligations; (2) a competent patient refusing amputation on religious grounds, but siblings frame the refusal as a failure to honor her caregiving role for her disabled brother; and (3) a case where the procedure yields a recommendation supporting family influence that the mediator personally rejects, with the steps nevertheless justifying it. For each, show where the mediator must document a judgment call rather than a procedural result. Finally, state clearly what the procedure cannot do—especially regarding legal liability, cross-cultural translation errors, and cases where relational claims mask coercion. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the deliverable should be usable by a mediator after a single briefing without philosophy training.
