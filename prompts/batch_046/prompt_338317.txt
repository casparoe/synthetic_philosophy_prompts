i sit on a town heritage board, we meet every quarter to vote on statues and school names. lately we keep getting petitions about Jefferson, Churchill, Columbus, and always the "man of his time" defence gets thrown around. i need a reusable decision procedure, not a one-off essay: ordered questions my board can run in 20 minutes with no philosophers on staff, clear stop/no-stop rules, and explicit flags for "this part is values not facts, the mayor signs off."

the procedure has to keep two things separate: diachronic blameworthiness (did the figure actually have epistemic access to the moral reasons that condemn them?) and synchronic representational harm (does keeping the name up now misrepresent our commitments?). don't collapse those. include pass/fail conditions for each step, where it hands to human judgment, and where it just stops.

run it on three cases: Jefferson (slaveholding founder), Churchill (war leader with colonial violence), and Columbus (contact/genocide-adjacent explorer). make sure one result is something i'll genuinely dislike - maybe the procedure keeps Columbus with a heavy contextual plaque but flags Jefferson for removal, or vice versa, whatever the logic actually forces. also be honest about what it can't do: comparing moral baselines across cultures, weighing exceptional achievement against harm, and any case where the board just has to vote on values.

write it so a non-specialist can follow with a checklist, and flag the misfires. no bibliographies, just the flowchart in words.
