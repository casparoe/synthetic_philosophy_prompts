I'm drafting my view on Kierkegaard's Abraham for a seminar. I hold: (1) the teleological suspension is literal — ethics actualy gets suspended, not just symbolically overridden; (2) Abraham's faith deepens his ethical love for Isaac rather than putting it aside — the father-son bond is intensified; (3) the knight of faith must be observationally identical to the knight of infinite resignation — no external marker distinguishes them; (4) Abraham feels the full crushing ethical guilt and horror of being a murderer during the ascent, not just anxiety; (5) the 'absurd' here is a practical paradox, not a logical contradiction. Can you audit these? Which pairs genuinely conflict, which only seem to?
