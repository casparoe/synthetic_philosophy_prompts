I'm presenting next tuesday at our interdiscplinary social philosophy workshop. I'm defending the view that Strawson's reactive/objective distinction doesn't hold up for institutional agents — specifically, that when a corporation retaliates against a whistleblower through legal and bureaucratic mechanisms, that's not a metaphorical "resentment" but structurally the same reactive attitude Strawson describes between persons. From this I argue moral responsibility can be genuinely distributed across institutions without requiring an individual blamer, and that this actually makes standard compatibilism harder because you can't retreat to the "objective attitude" as a non-blaming stance; institutional objective attitudes are still reactive, just directed at populations rather than individuals.

The audience is mostly collective-responsibility and institutional-ethics people who are allergic to sloppy metaphor, plus two moral psychologists who think reactive attitudes are irreducibly interpersonal and face-to-face.
