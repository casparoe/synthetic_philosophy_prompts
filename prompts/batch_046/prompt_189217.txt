Hey, I'm a backend engineer and I don't really do philosophy, but I got into this weird argument with my team after an outage last month. Our incident review protocol has this split: if the monitoring script fails to send an alert and we miss a failure, that's logged as a 'human oversight failure' because the engineer on call should have checked manually. But if the script sends a bad alert that tricks the engineer into restarting the wrong service, that's a 'system error' and the automation gets the blame. I started wondering what kind of theory about causation or responsability makes that split make sense. Like, does it preseuppose that not doing something — not sending the ping — isn't really causing the outage, only the human's inaction is? Or is there a deeper view about omissions vs actions baked into our policy?

And then there's this other thing: in negligence law (which I only know about from a podcast), people get blamed for not doing something they should have done, but our protocol seems to say the opposite — the omission is invisible and the human gets blamed for not catching it. So is our practice assuming that omissions can't be causes unless there's a special duty, or is it just inconsistant? I want to understand what philosophical commitments about causation by omission our company rules are actually presupposing, whether they're coherent with each other, and what would have to change if we decided that a missed alert counts as the system causing harm just like a wrong alert does.
