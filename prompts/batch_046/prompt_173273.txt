hey — I'm putting together the take-home final for my grad seminar on intergenerational justice and climate change (some sharp undergrads are in the mix too). I want one essay question that stops students from just recycling Parfit's non-identity problem or the usual future-generations-have-no-rights line. Instead, I want them to wrestle with a specifc conceptual knot I'm calling the double asymetry: present generations bear mitagation costs for benefits that accrue to future people, but because of demographic turnover and policy-dependent fertility and migration effects, the actual individuals who'd suffer under inaction aren't identical to those who benefit under action — and the present generation's own descendants aren't a fixed population but depend on which climate pathway we pick. Ask them whether this double asymetry undermines contractualist justifications for aggressive mitigation, or whether a modified Rawlsian framework can accommodate it by treating representative future persons as placeholders rather than actual claimants.

Please write the exam prompt (expect about 1200 words, worth 35 marks out of 100), a model answer around 700 words, and a marking scheme that separates an A from a B. For an A they need to identify both layers of asymetry, separate them clearly from the basic non-identity problem, explain the placeholder-representative move, and address at least one objection like demographic substitution. For a B they recognize the asymmetry but conflate it with standard non-identity, or they sketch the Rawlsian fix without showing why placeholders avoid the contractualist objection.

Also please tell me how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you're least sure of — I'm not totally sure the demographic substitution objection is standard enough and I don't want you winging the marking scheme. And no bibliographies or citation lists please; half the class is working offline.
