You advise a public school district equity office that regularly receives requests to adjust uniforms, event calendars, curriculum framing, and cafeteria practices on religious or cultural grounds. The district wants to operate between two models: French republican laïcité, which holds that public institutions should not register particular group identities in their functioning, and Anglo-American multiculturalism, which treats equitable recognition of difference as itself a public good. Write a reusable, step-by-step decision procedure your office can run on any proposal. Specify the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where the procedure mandates denial, where it mandates approval, and where it must halt and escalate to human judgment because the conflict is one of values rather than facts. The procedure should distinguish identity-expressive requests (a preference about how one is seen) from identity-constitutive requests (where denial forces a student to misrepresent their own self-understanding in order to participate), and it must handle visibility effects on peers and staff. Apply it to two cases: a proposal to permit head coverings in a school with a blanket ban on all hats, and a proposal to restructure the history curriculum so that each unit is taught sequentially from the internal perspective of a different cultural group. Be explicit about where the procedure will misfire, where it relies on contested empirical claims about social visibility or harm, and what categories of dispute it cannot settle.
