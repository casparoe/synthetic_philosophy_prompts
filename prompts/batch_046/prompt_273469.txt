Write a villanelle on Nietzsche's doctrine of eternal recurrence, treating it not as the familiar ethical test—whether one would will one's life to repeat infinitely—but as the cosmological claim that every configuration of force must return identically, leaving no exterior standpoint from which to withhold assent. Let one refrain invoke the will's desire to bless what is necessary and eternalize the moment; let the other invoke the closure of possibility—no variation, no exit, no outside. Structure the poem so that the repeating lines gradually shift in meaning—first read as active desire, then as inescapable constraint—so that the form itself enacts the collapse of the distinction between willing recurrence and being compelled by it. The turn should occur where the speaker recognizes that affirmation is not a response to repetition but its only internal mode; the closing couplet should leave the reader with the idea that to say yes is not to choose but to discover that choice itself is already inside the circle. The poem must be philosophically precise enough that a scholar clearly distinguishes the cosmological argument from the existential test, and poetically rigorous enough that the villanelle's returns feel inevitable rather than decorative.
