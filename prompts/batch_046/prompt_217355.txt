As a retired physicist who has only recently begun to study philosophy of mind in earnest, I have become preoccupied with Daniel Dennett's critique of the Cartesian theater and with his alternative picture of consciousness as multiple competing drafts rather than a single finished presentation to an inner audience. I should like you to analyse a well-known neuroscientific episode through this conceptual lens.

The episode concerns the split-brain experiments conducted by Roger Sperry and Michael Gazzaniga from the 1960s onward. In these studies, patients whose corpus callosum had been severed displayed behaviours suggesting that the two cerebral hemispheres could operate with considerable independence. In the classic demonstration, the right hemisphere—which controls the left hand—was shown a wintry scene, while the left hemisphere—which controls speech—was shown a chicken claw. Each hemisphere then selected an object consistent with its input: the left hand chose a shovel, and the right hand chose a chicken. When asked to explain why the left hand had selected the shovel, the speaking left hemisphere, apparently unaware of the snow scene, confabulated an explanation linking the shovel to the chicken—suggesting, for instance, that the shovel was needed to clean up after it.

Please analyse this episode in both directions. First, apply Dennett's framework: does the left hemisphere's confabulation resemble the work of a Cartesian theater—an inner audience being presented with a polished, singular narrative—or does it fit better with the multiple drafts model, in which various narrative fragments are edited and broadcast without any central observer? Second, consider what the episode reveals about the theory: does it genuinely undermine the notion of a unitary self, or does it merely show two co-existing draft-generators that are normally coupled?

In framing the problem, I have been drawn to an analogy from statistical mechanics: the left hemisphere behaves somewhat like a mean-field approximation, treating outputs from the right hemisphere as external perturbations to be smoothed into a single macroscopic state, rather than as messages from an equally authoritative subsystem. I present this only as a heuristic, not as an established interpretation.

Please adjudicate whether this physical analogy illuminates the philosophical issue or instead obscures it, and say clearly where the theory remains silent rather than forcing it to speak.
