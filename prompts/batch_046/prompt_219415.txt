I recently encountered a summary of teleosemantics—the Millikan/Papineau line on naturalizing mental content—in an online discussion, and I want to check whether the framing is trustworthy before I internalize it. The summary says that teleosemantics solves the naturalization problem by treating mental representation as biological proper function: a cognitive state is about water not because of causal correlation or resemblance, but because evolutionary selection specifically favored mechanisms that produce that state in water’s presence in order to guide behavior. Misrepresentation is therefore analyzed as malfunction—the mechanism failing to perform the task it was selected for, analogous to a heart that does not pump—which eliminates any need for irreducible semantic or normative primitives in psychology. The summary then claims that because proper functions are grounded in evolutionary selection history, artificial systems like LLMs necessarily lack them; without an evolutionary past, no derived or designed function can generate genuine mental content, only behavioral simulation.

I am inclined to accept the core mechanism but worry that the reduction to evolutionary engineering is overstated, and that the inference about artificial systems does work the theory itself does not license.
