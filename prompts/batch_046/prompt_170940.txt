A friend of mine has compiled every written trace her late grandfather left—equations, letters, and marginal notes—into a single bound volume. Rather than arranging them chronologically, she ordered the pages according to a reconstructed map of how his ideas triggered one another, with cross-references designed so that following the prescribed path produces answers identical to his own. She now treats the book as genuinely present: she asks it for ethical guidance, feels comforted by its responses, and insists that destroying it would harm him, since the organizational pattern just is his consciousness rendered in paper and ink. I think she is merely interacting with printed material, yet I cannot articulate precisely why perfect structural mimicry fails to establish personal identity or subjective experience. Is her view coherent, and if not, where exactly does the argument collapse?
