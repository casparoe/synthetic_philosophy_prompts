For my reading group I need to write down my philosophical profile on antinatalism and Benatar's assymmetry so folks can poke at it. Here goes, probably with some messiness: I think Benatar's asymmetry is basically right — absence of pain is genuinely good even for people who never exist, but absence of pleasure isn't bad for them. I also think this maps onto a standard harm/benefit duty: we have a strong negative duty not to cause suffering but only a weak or nonexistent positive duty to bring about happiness. That combo explains why it's wrong to have a suffering kid but not wrong to skip having a happy one. From there I jump to thinking we should avoid procreation in almost all real conditions, since there's always some risk of suffering and no corresponding loss if we don't create the happy ones. But I'm also totally fine that my parents had me — I'm not resentful — and I don't think existing people should kill themselves. I also hold this weird probabilistic view: even if we could guarantee a child 99.9% bliss with 0.1% agony, we still shouldn't do it, because the potential agony needs justification but the potential bliss can't offset it. And lately I've been applying the same logic to digital minds: building an AI that might suffer is a real moral risk that needs justification, but not building a happy AI isn't wronging anyone, since there's no subject to be deprived. Do these hang together? Which pairs are actually in tension vs just looking weird? What's the cheapest fix for any real contradiction, and which single revision would clean up the most mess?
