hey, i've been trying to nail down what i actually think about truquilism—y'know, the view that only the absence of craving and suffering matters, and all positive states are basically ornamental. here are my gut reactions, and i can't tell if there's one principle or i'm just being inconsistent.

case one: a monk in deep jhana, fully awake, zero desire, zero pain, but also no particular joy—just clear empty awareness. feels like the best possible state.

case two: same monk, but now he's got intense bliss and deep insight. my gut says this isn't any better; the bliss is just decoration.

case three: a patient in permanent deep anethesia—no awareness, no suffering, no craving. i think this is just as good as case one. if there's no subject, there's nothing missing.

case four: someone on a drug that wipes negative affect but leaves this weird background itch—no suffering, exactly, but a constant feeling that they're missing a feeling they can't name. this isn't tranquil; there's a hidden deficit.

case five: a society genetically modified to have zero craving and zero suffering, but also zero capacity for joy, love, or achievement. they live simple peaceful lives. i call this a real utopia.

case six: but then there's a child who dies in infancy before ever craving or suffering. if three and five are perfect, shouldn't this be perfect too? yet i feel there's a loss, though not a loss of suffering-avoidance. maybe i'm sneaking in values that tranquilism can't allow?

case seven: a philosopher who kills every urge through brute will—no lingering craving, but the crushing takes effort and creates tension. i say this fails. the method matters, not just the result.

so is there a coherent account? maybe "absence of craving and suffering in the actual phenomenal state, regardless of consciousness or positive goods"? but then why does case four fail—there's absence of suffering but that itch? and why hesitate on case six? please state the principle precisely, say which verdicts i have to overturn to keep it simple, and tell me what it predicts for something i haven't listed: a being that can experience craving but literally cannot suffer, existing in a state of constant mild wanting.
