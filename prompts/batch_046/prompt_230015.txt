I teach a philosphy seminar for grad students, about 10 people, 90 min. I want an in-class activty that gets them to feel the tension inside the "varieties of free will worth wanting" idea — specifically a version where diachronic free will requires not just compatibilist control but also a kind of narrative opacity: the agent has to be able to tell a story about the choice that they don't fully control, otherwise it feels manufactured. Design a role-play or forced-choice game with rules and small stakes where participants make decisions either under full transparency (everyone sees their reasoning) or under partial opacity, then defend whether they still "own" the result. They should run into the tension themselves before anyone names it. Then a debrief that maps the moments onto the standard taxonomy but highlights exactly where it misses the opacity condition. Include what predictabely goes wrong the first time you run it, and be honest about which feature of the view the activity just can't reproduce and you have to supply by hand. No lectures up front.
