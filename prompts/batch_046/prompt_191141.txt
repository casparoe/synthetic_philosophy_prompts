Design a 75-minute classroom activity for 16 graduate students in a philosophy of mind and ethics seminar. The domain is Ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood, with particular attention to the claim that personhood is not an individual possession but is constituted through mutual recognition and shared moral liability. To prevent a generic recitation of “I am because we are,” inject the following conceptual tension directly into the mechanics of the activity: the reciprocity gap, in which one agent’s selfhood is constituted by recognizing another as part of their own being, but the other explicitly denies that the first party plays any constitutive role in return. Participants should encounter the instability, vulnerability, and asymmetrical obligation produced by this gap through their own choices and social dynamics, not through a facilitator’s lecture.

Specify the setting clearly: assume students have basic familiarity with Ubuntu as a philosophical orientation but no specialized knowledge of recent technical literature. The deliverable must include:
