Below is an outline and partial argument for an essay on the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents, centered on a framing I am calling “weighted reflective consistency.” The standard presentation assumes that an agent either asserts or denies a sentence such as “this proof system is consistent.” I want to explore what happens if, instead, the agent assigns continuous confidence weights to its own reasoning procedures, including meta-level claims about consistency, and requires that these weights be stable under reflection up to a bounded epsilon. The thesis is that Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem does not disappear, but its bite is transformed: rather than preventing the agent from rationally trusting itself tout court, it forces a non-degenerate confidence gap between first-order reasoning and any explicit meta-claim of consistency, with the gap’s lower bound scaling with the descriptive complexity of the system being trusted. The essay sketches a dynamics for how these weights update when the agent considers its own proof-checking architecture, and argues that this produces a stable, bounded form of self-trust rather than a flat contradiction.

Please provide detailed critical comments on this draft, treating it as a work in progress rather than a finished paper. Specifically:

(1) Is the distinction between binary and graded doxastic self-trust correctly drawn, or does it collapse once we notice that proof-theoretic strength and arithmetical hierarchy membership remain discrete categories, so that “confidence” is ultimately tracking a discrete property?

(2) Does the “weighted reflective consistency” framework genuinely avoid the Löbian obstacle, or does it merely relocate the problem into the dynamics of confidence updating—e.g., by requiring that the agent never reach full confidence in its own consistency, which looks like the same obstacle in different clothing?

(3) Is the claim about a lower-bounded confidence gap, scaling with descriptive complexity, formally defensible in outline? Or does it smuggle in an implicit and undefended mapping between real-valued confidence weights and the syntactic structure of formal systems?

(4) Given that I am not requesting citations or a bibliography—only conceptual engagement—what are the strongest objections from formal epistemology or from the literature on bounded rationality and self-reference that I have likely overlooked? In particular, are there arguments showing that any continuous relaxation of belief in consistency is either redundant or incoherent?

(5) Which parts of the argument are most likely to strike a knowledgeable reader as genuinely novel, and which parts risk being an unrecognized rediscovery of existing points about reflective equilibrium, defeasible reasoning, or probabilistic self-reference? I am especially interested in whether the move to graded confidence changes anything substantive about the obstacle, or whether it is primarily a reformulation.

Please be direct about weaknesses, and flag any point where the reasoning seems to depend on an unstated assumption about how meta-level beliefs are encoded in the object-level system.
