I'm a phil of sci phd student and i've been stuck in this debate with my advisor about ontology and inaccessable information. The setup is: we have domains where information is permanently screened off — not just hard to get, but in principle unreachable, like whatever lies behind an event horizon or inside a causally disconnected pocket. One camp, call them the Thin Ontologists, says if something is that screened off then it shouldnt be in our ontology at all; ontology should only include whats structurally anchored to accessible data, otherwise were just inventing ghost entities. The other camp, the Shadow Realists, says inaccessibility isnt the same as non-existence — the hidden region still has determinate properties, they just dont causally interact, and treating them as ontologically absent is verificationism in a trenchcoat. Both sides cite the same holographic bounds and agree the screening is permanent, but for like three years nobody has moved an inch. I started wondering if the stall is because theyre answering different questions — one about what exists, one about what we should commit to in our theories — but my advisor thinks theres a hidden formal disagreement about whether modal counterfactuals can bridge the epistemic gap. Im not convinced. Is this actually a substantive metaphysical dispute, or is it verbal about the word ontology? Or maybe its a disagreement about standards of explanation: do we need ontic completeness for a theory to be satisfactory, or just empirical adequacy? And if your diagnosis is right, what could actually break the deadlock — a new theorem about boundary conditions, a sharper distinction between epistemic insulation and ontic inaccessibility which i think is being conflated here, or should we just accept its bedrock intuition and move on? I want an honest diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of positions, and tell me if progress is even possible or if were just talking past each other.
