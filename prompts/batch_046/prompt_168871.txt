I'm on our state's penal advisery panel — small group, advises the governor on clemency stuff. We've got this weird petition sitting in front of us. A guy on death row, been there 18 years, filed not for commutation exactly but for a kind of philosophical stay. He wants the panel to officially recognize that carrying out his execution violates Camus's argument that capital punishment is basically state-sponsored premeditated murder, and he asks us to recommend a 24-month moratorum on all executions so the legislature can reconcile the statute with what he calls the 'absurdity thesis' drawn from Camus's writing on the guillotine.

Half the panel thinks we should reject it outright because we're not a philosophy seminar, we're a body with a legal mandate. The other half thinks there's actually something to the structural hypocrisy Camus points out — especially his point about hiding executions behind prison walls while demanding public condemnation of murder — and wants us to recommend a conditional stay tied to a legislative review.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday's vote: recommend reject, approve the moratorum, or approve with conditions and a clear timeline. Engage the inmate's Camus-based argument seriously — don't just say 'not our job' — but also be honest about the strongest reason to go the other way. Make it a document the panel can actually act on. Don't include citations or a reading list, just write it straight.
