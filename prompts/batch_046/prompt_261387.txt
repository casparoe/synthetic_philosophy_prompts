Please argue for the position that Brouwer's rejection of the law of excluded middle over infinite domains should be interpreted not as an ontological claim about the indeterminacy of mathematical objects, but as an epistemic-normative constraint governing any verification process in which the investigating agent is embedded within the domain being verified. Develop this argument to show that the constraint dissolves the standard tension between intuitionistic mathematics and classical probabilistic reasoning in physics, but only on the condition that one rejects logical pluralism—the view that the choice between intuitionistic and classical logic is merely pragmatic or conventional. Address the specific counter-objection that embedded verification necessarily collapses into vicious circularity rather than constituting a principled limitation, and explain why circularity is avoided if the embedding is partial and asymmetrical rather than total.
