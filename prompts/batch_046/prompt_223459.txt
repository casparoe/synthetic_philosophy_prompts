Me and two friends got into it over whether changing your mind in loops can still be rational. (1) Jake said if you preffer A to B Monday, B to C Tuesday, and C to A Wednesday, you're a money pump -- someone can trade you in circles and drain you, so diachronic rationality means your preferences have to stay transitive across time. (2) Maya said thats too quick: Tuesday-you learned something about B that Monday-you didn't have, so its not the same evaluator, its an updated ordering, and no pump works if the agent changed. (3) Jake said if you let "learning" excuse every reversal the argument is useless -- anyone can claim it. He said a rational person should pay a cost to bind her future self, and refusing is like refusing a handicap: it reveals you dont actually value consistency. (4) Maya said binding just pushes the loop up to meta-preferences, and if you treat your future selves as a collective then Harsanyi-style aggregation applies -- the group can be rational even if each member cycles. She called this the "temporal collective view" and said Jake was treating one life like one voter. They asked me to referee.
