Second, if you scanned my brain and ran a perfect copy of my loop in another head, I think that copy really is me — not a twin, but an actual continuation. There's no hidden 'thisness' that stays behind.

Third, I still feel like there's only one 'me' at any given moment. So if both copies are running simultaneuosly, something's off — either they split my identity in a way I don't understand, or one is fake, but I have no principle for saying which.

Sixth, but I also think that if two brains were actively coupled — same experiences, constant feedback, like one loop with two nodes — that might actually be one distributed 'I'. And if one node got destroyed, the remaining one might still be me, not just a survivor remembering me.

Seventh, I think what makes the loop mine is its current structure and first-person viewpoint, not the specific atoms or causal history. Substrate independence feels real to me.

So my commitments are roughly: (a) loop is substrate-independent pattern; (b) perfect copies are me; (c) uniqueness — only one me at a time; (d) fragments exist in others via prediction/simulation; (e) fragments don't save me or count as distribution; (f) live coupling creates real distributed self; (g) causal origin/history doesn't matter, only structure and perspective.

Which ones are actually in tension? Is (b) vs (c) the worst? Or is (d) vs (e) worse, since I'm saying literal fragments exist but don't matter?
