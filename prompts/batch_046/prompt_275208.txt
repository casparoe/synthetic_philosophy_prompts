ok so i'm trying to map out where embedded agency research should actualy go if we care about both ai alignment and not just doing philosophy for fun. people talk about embeddedness like it's one problem but i think there are really three failure modes that get collapsed together: representational (you can't fully model your own state without leaving something out), strategic (you can't precommit to strategies that depend on reasoning about your own reasoning), and ontological (the agent/environment boundary shifts as you act, so there's no stable 'self' to optimize).

wheter these are independant or not matters for what to work on first. which of the three has open questions that are both tractable with current tools and upstream of the others? like, does cracking the representational problem unlock strategic precommitment, or are they separate dead ends? and among lines of work—maybe reflective consistency, logical induction, or just accepting approximate self-models—which should a small group prioritize?

i want a dependency-aware agenda: which crux most of the field implicitly depends on, which directions are overrated because solving them wouldn't change much downstream, and where three years of grad-student effort would actualy move the needle versus spin wheels. defend the ordering with criteria—tractability, how many other questions hang on it, and what you'd deprioritize—not just list them.
