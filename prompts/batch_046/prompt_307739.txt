Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers: one who defends a thick relational conception of personhood rooted in the Ubuntu tradition, holding that an individual's moral status is constituted—not merely supported—by reciprocal recognition relations within a living community; and one who defends a liberal-capacitist conception, holding that personhood is grounded in pre-social intrinsic capacities for rational agency and moral accountability, with social relations serving only to develop or exercise those capacities.

The statement should be structured as follows.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims. These should cover: (i) that human beings are social animals in some significant sense; (ii) that severe isolation can damage or diminish human functioning; (iii) that moral status admits of degrees or conditions of flourishing rather than being an all-or-nothing property in every context; and (iv) that both sides reject both biological reductionism and an unrestricted social-constructivism about value.

Second, a disagreements table with two columns—one stating the relationalist position in wording its own proponents would accept, the other stating the liberal-capacitist position in wording its own proponents would accept. The table should register precise disagreement on: (a) whether reciprocal recognition is ontologically constitutive of personhood or only instrumentally necessary for its development; (b) whether a solitary human being who lacks all actual community ties retains full moral status, diminished status, or only potential status; and (c) whether the first-person singular pronoun 'I' refers to a node within a relational network or to a metaphysically prior bearer of capacities.

Third, the cruxes. Identify the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party. I take the primary cruxes to be: (1) whether prolonged solitary confinement eliminates personhood or merely suppresses its expression, and (2) whether the relational view can accommodate the normative authority of individual conscientious refusal—e.g., a lone dissident rejecting communal norms—without treating that refusal as mere social dysfunction or false consciousness. For each crux, present a concrete case or argument that both sides agree bears on the issue, and record in advance each side's prediction about what the case demonstrates.

Fourth, an explicit assessment of whether any part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive. If you conclude that part of the disagreement concerns only the definition of 'personhood' or 'community' without empirical or normative divergence, specify exactly which part and explain why.

Write the document in neutral, precise language suitable for both signatures. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Avoid framing either position as obviously correct; the goal is to make the residual disagreement as exact as possible.
