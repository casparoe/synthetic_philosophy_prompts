Having retired from experimental biology and only recently turned to philosophy, I want a consistency check on the following package of commitments before I present them in an informal discussion group. I hold that (1) human existence lacks any transcendent or cosmic purpose, and that the physical universe is fundamentally indifferent to our projects in the manner associated with existential absurdism. I hold further that (2) evolutionary biology discloses a local teleonomy—functional organization directed at survival and reproduction—that supplies human activities with an objective directional structure without entailing any cosmic design. From this I derive (3) that meaning in life requires reflective endorsement of one's projects within this local teleonomic framework, though I explicitly deny that subjective happiness, emotional fulfillment, or lasting historical impact are necessary conditions for it. I also propose (4) that the absence of cosmic purpose should be treated not as a deficit but as an epistemic control condition: it strips away confounding teleological assumptions so that we can assess whether local commitments are genuinely robust or merely evolutionary comfort-seeking illusions. Finally, I accept (5) that a life can be fully meaningful even if it is eventually forgotten and leaves no external trace, provided it maintains internal functional coherence and survives reflective scrutiny under the control condition described in (4).

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts only appear serious but dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real tension what is the least costly revision and what exactly it costs? Rank the tensions by severity, specify which commitment must give way in each case, and trace specifically what my own stated position commits me to rather than delivering a general lecture on meaning in life. I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations of particular authors.
