me and two friends got into it over Locke's Some Thoughts Concering Education. Sam said Locke's program is basically a safe pareto improvement over old grammar schools: kids learn by play and reason instead of beatings and rote Latin, parents get rational citizens, and nobody's made worse off since the old system was pure cruelty. Jordan said that only works if you ignore class. Locke writes for gentlemen with tutors and free time; his working-school plan for poor kids is forced labor plus catechism. So calling it a safe improvement is wrong — it sets a humane standard the poor can't reach while locking them into workhouses, making them relatively worse off. Sam replied Jordan's mixing tracks, but even inside each track Locke swaps punishment for reasoning, which is still pareto safe. Jordan said the tracks aren't separable because Locke uses the same language of 'forming the mind' for both, but only the rich get reasoning; the poor get habituation to labor. So the universalist framing itself hides the harm. Can you go through their exchange step by step, say who's right on each point and where both miss something? Don't just crown a winner — point out what's actually missing in their read of Locke.
