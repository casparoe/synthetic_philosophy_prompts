I sit on the funding and ethics review board for our university's Institute for Human-Machine Interaction, and we've got a proposal that's splitting us right down the middle. A team from cognitive robotics and moral psychology wants three years of support to build and pilot what they're calling a "Sphex Protocol" — an ethics-consultation support system designed to be maximally sphexish. By that they mean mechanically rigid, with zero capacity to adapt its decision-rules to context: it applies the same programmed criteria every single time, no exceptions, no feedback loops, essentially reproducing the kind of behavioral rigidity philosophers discuss with sphex wasps. Their philosophical pitch is that flexibility is actually the root of moral failure — human committees rationalize exceptions, get tired, show bias — so a genuinely sphexish system, precisely because it lacks free will or adaptive agency, would be more trustworthy in end-of-life and resource-allocation consultations. They want to run the pilot in our affiliated hospital's clinical ethics service.

Half the board, including our chair, thinks this confuses determinism with transparency and wants to desk-reject it outright; the other half thinks the rigidity is a feature and we should approve with safeguards. I'm drafting the recommendation memo for Thursday's vote. I need it to recommend one concrete course — approve with strict conditions, reject, or approve only a limited non-binding pilot — with reasoning the researchers could actually respond to rather than just restating institutional policy. Crucially, the memo has to take seriously the strongest consideration on the losing side: if I land on approval, I need to engage the worry that moral agency requires flexibility and that sphexishness eliminates accountability; if I land on rejection, I need to engage the transparency-and-consistency argument. Don't ask me for a bibliography or citation list — just structure the reasoning so it's actionable. Also I keep misspelling sphexishness with a double-x in my notes, just ignore that.
