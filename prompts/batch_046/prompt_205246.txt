Please construct a rigorous argument for the claim that the so-called problem of logical omniscience in epistemic logic is not a defect of modal knowledge operators but an artifact of misapplying a normative entitlement relation to psychological description. Your argument should proceed in three stages. First, distinguish three senses of knowing that are systematically conflated in standard objections: (a) occurrent mental representation with explicit derivational access, (b) dispositional cognitive accessibility given sufficient computational resources, and (c) institutional or practice-based entitlement that closes under logical consequence as a constitutive norm of rational accountability. Second, defend the view that the formal semantics of epistemic logic—particularly the closure of the accessibility relation—models only (c), and that objections based on human cognitive limits therefore commit a category error by treating a normative closure principle as a psychological prediction. Third, anticipate and rebut the charge that this is merely a verbal relocation: argue that once we recognize the normative function of the knowledge operator, we can enrich the framework with hyperintensional distinctions—so that logical equivalence does not entail identity of epistemic topic—thereby preserving closure within topics while allowing that agents may lack entitlement across topic-boundaries, which dissolves the omniscience worry without abandoning modal rigor. Your conclusion should address whether this reconception requires abandoning the S5 axioms or only reinterpreting their domain of application.
