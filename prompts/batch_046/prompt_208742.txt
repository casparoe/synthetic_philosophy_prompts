been thinking about parfits hitchhiker case in reasons and persons and im stuck on whether its actually about rationality being self-defeating or just about the limits of contract design. the scenario: youre stranded, a driver with perfect prediction saves you only if she expects payment after, but once rescued you have no incentive to pay. parfit argues the rational agent should cultivate a disposition to pay anyway—call it rational irrationality. but heres where i get confused. if the driver can predict your psychology, and if modern institutions let you precommit funds via escrow or smart contracts, then isnt the whole dilemma just a temporary technological gap? in other words, once credible commitment devices exist, theres no need for any internal irrationality at all—you just lock the money away mechanically. so is parfit describing a deep feature of practical reason, or just describing what rational agents do when they dont yet have access to good binding technology? i keep feeling like im reducing the hitchhiker to odysseus tying himself to the mast, but maybe thats exactly right and im missing why parfit thinks internal character matters more than external mechanisms.
