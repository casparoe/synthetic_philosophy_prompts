Below are notes for a short essay I am drafting on the symbol grounding problem. I argue that current multimodal systems do not solve grounding but instead produce what I call indexical drift: a systemic decoupling of symbols from stable external anchors that is masked by high predictive coherence across modalities. In standard philosophical accounts, grounding requires an asymmetrical relation in which a symbol can be corrected by its non-symbolic referent, but the referent is not reinterpreted through the symbol. Biological cognition exhibits this correctional priority; statistical multimodal embedding does not, because its "referents" are themselves distributions in training data, allowing bidirectional reinterpretation. I distinguish vertical grounding (this asymmetrical, correctable link) from horizontal coherence (inter-symbolic stabilization), and claim that large language models achieve only the latter. The result is not occasional hallucination but a structural feature: symbols can maintain functional roles indefinitely without ever being falsified by an external state of affairs. I conclude that true grounding requires correctional priority, and that without it a system manipulates representations rather than referring. Would you comment specifically on whether the distinction between vertical grounding and horizontal coherence holds up, whether indexical drift names something genuinely different from familiar failure modes like hallucination or distributional shift, and whether my requirement of correctional priority illegitimately assumes direct realism rather than a viable representational theory?
