I'm moderating a podcast Q&A with a philosopher who defends a specific variant of illusionism about phenomenal consciousness she terms "empty-reference illusionism": phenomenal concepts are not false descriptions of an inner qualitative state, but functionally empty speech acts that coordinate attention and behavior without referring to any property at all. Write six questions that avoid generic biography or view-summary prompts and instead press her on three specific tensions. First, ask how introspection can be a misfiring representational mechanism if there is literally nothing for it to misrepresent, with a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that introspection tracks functional self-models rather than hidden qualities. Second, ask what distinguishes her position from eliminativism, since both deny phenomenal properties, with a follow-up addressing her probable claim that eliminativism wrongly assumes phenomenal talk aims to describe. Third, ask how she accounts for the intuition that phenomenal differences do explanatory work—such as why pain-avoidance differs from itch-avoidance—if there are no qualitative differences to ground those distinctions, with a follow-up anticipating her defense that the explanation lies entirely in differential functional roles and evolutionary salience. Make each initial question sharp and each follow-up directly responsive to the defense she is most likely to offer.
