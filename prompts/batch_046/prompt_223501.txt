Here are notes for an essay I am drafting. The central claim is that Sidgwick’s involvement with psychical research through the Society for Psychical Research should be read not as an empirical curiosity separate from his moral philosophy, but as a methodological extension of his commitment to impartial rationality. Specifically, I propose that Sidgwick investigated telepathy and post-mortem communication to test whether normative reasons could be transmitted and evaluated without being anchored in particular embodied agents—an empirical probe into whether rationales can operate independently of identifiable reasoners. I suggest that his cautious openness to mediumistic evidence reflects a tension in his ethical system between the demand for disembodied impartiality and the practical need for situated moral judgment. I do not claim he endorsed spiritualism; rather, I treat his psychical inquiries as an attempt to determine whether the conditions for rational moral concern could survive the dissolution of individual psychological continuity.

Please comment on these notes. In particular: (1) Does this framing distort Sidgwick’s actual motivations, or is there sufficient basis for linking his psychical inquiries to his ethical epistemology? (2) Is the notion of rationales operating independently of reasoners useful here, or does it import an anachronistic metaethical vocabulary that obscures his project? (3) Which parts of the argument are most vulnerable to the charge that Sidgwick was simply pursuing a private empirical hobby with no philosophical significance? I am especially interested in suggestions for sharpening the distinction between testing the conditions of rational agency and merely investigating paranormal claims.
