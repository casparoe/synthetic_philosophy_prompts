I serve as a contract mediator for a consortium of regional hospitals and AI vendors negotiating data-use agreements for clinical-model training. Each negotiation fixes terms across three coupled dimensions: patient-data volume accessible to the vendor, model-accuracy guarantees the vendor must meet, and liability caps borne by each party. I need a reusable decision procedure—an ordered protocol I can run in a ninety-minute session with legal and technical representatives present, with sign-off required from both sides—that applies the Kalai-Smorodinsky bargaining framework to recommend a specific contract point. The procedure must translate the four KS conditions—individual rationality, Pareto efficiency, symmetry, and individual monotonicity—into concrete steps: how to construct each party’s utopia point from counterfactual best-cases constrained by current regulatory ceilings rather than theoretical maxima; how to compute the line of equal utility gains; and exactly where standard individual monotonicity fails because expanding the vendor’s data-access utility contracts the hospital’s privacy-related utility within HIPAA-derived floors, requiring a coupled-feasible-set check in its place. It must include an explicit judgment-call checkpoint where symmetry is waived due to asymmetric regulatory exposure, distinguish steps that require cardinal estimates from those that need only ordinal rankings, and flag where the mediator must stop and hand the decision to the parties because the question is one of values rather than bargaining geometry. Apply the procedure to three negotiations: first, a standard case with convex, independent feasible sets; second, a coupled-set case where the vendor’s accuracy demands push required data volumes past the hospital’s privacy floor, forcing the monotonicity check to fail and be replaced; and third, a case where the procedure recommends a liability-cap point that gives the vendor substantially less protection than the hospital initially offered—state what the mediator should say when the vendor objects that this violates intuitive fairness, and identify whether the objection reveals a hidden Nash-weighted intuition rather than a KS violation. Conclude by stating what the procedure cannot resolve—for instance, whether bargaining should occur at all when one party holds a statutory monopoly on the data source—and specify the single diagnostic case that would reveal whether the mediator is genuinely running KS or an unrecognized hybrid with the Nash product.
