A member of my philosophy discussion group offered this summary of Libet's experiments and what they show about voluntary action: using the clock method, subjects reported the moment of conscious intention—what Libet called the W judgment—while EEG tracked the readiness potential, revealing that neural preparation begins about 500 milliseconds before movement but conscious awareness only arises roughly 200 milliseconds before it, leaving an unconscious initiation period of about 350 milliseconds. The summary continues that Libet tried to salvage free will by allowing a conscious veto in the final 150 milliseconds, but that this veto is itself produced by preconscious neural mechanisms, so it offers no genuine freedom; it then treats later fMRI studies predicting binary choices up to ten seconds in advance as direct confirmations of Libet's deterministic interpretation, concluding that voluntary agency is essentially post-hoc narration of predetermined brain processes. I can tell the timeline is roughly correct, but I suspect the account conflates Libet's cautious framing with stronger determinism and misrepresents both the veto and the relation to predictive neuroimaging.
