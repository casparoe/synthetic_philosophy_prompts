In a seminar on environmental history, my colleague dismissed my claim that a particular minister’s decision in 1847 was a decisive cause of a famine, saying that “events are only foam on the waves of history” — the real work being done by climate and geography over the longue durée. I want to formulate a precise reply, but I need to know what exactly that metaphor might be claiming. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that slogan could stand for — for instance, an ontological claim about causal primacy, an epistemological claim about historiographical focus, a normative claim about which explanations count as adequate, and a metaphysical claim about temporal scale — indicating for each whether it is trivial, defensible, false, or actually at odds with Braudel’s own framework? Then say which of these readings my colleague needs for her dismissal to hold, and whether any defensible version actually rules out the minister’s decision without also ruling out every other event-based explanation.
