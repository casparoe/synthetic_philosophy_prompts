I am preparing to interview a philosopher who defends a specific, under-discussed reading of Nietzsche's eternal recurrence. On her view, the doctrine is neither a cosmological claim about cyclical physics nor a purely psychological exercise in affirmation, but a normative coherence test: any evaluative framework is adequate only if the agent can consistently will its infinite repetition in identical form—while retaining full awareness of every particular suffering and contingency it entails—without generating a practical contradiction. She insists this distinguishes amor fati from passive resignation, but critics argue the test is either too permissive (even a malicious framework could be willed repeatedly) or too demanding (no finite agent could stably will the exact repetition of every contingent particular).

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view. Include at least one question pressing on how she distinguishes successful coherence from mere psychological endurance or stoic tolerance; at least one on the granularity problem—whether recurrence requires repetition down to every particular position and state, and whether that specificity renders the test either vacuous or impossibly precise; and at least one on whether her criterion collapses into a variant of Kantian universalizability or retains a distinctively Nietzschean structure tied to the affirmation of life rather than to rational consistency alone. For each main question, provide a sharp follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. The questions should turn on the actual conceptual machinery of her position; avoid generic biographical or introductory questions.
