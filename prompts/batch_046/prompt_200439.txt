For a metaethics and reproductive ethics discussion I'm writing out my commitments to check coherence. Here they are:

(1) Benatar's asymmetry is correct: the presence of suffering is bad, but the absence of pleasure is not bad for anyone, since there is no subject to be deprived when a being never exists.
(2) I apply the asymmetry selectively: it gives a decisive moral reason against creating beings whose lives will contain suffering, but I deny it applies to hypothetical beings whose lives would contain only pleasure, because without suffering there is no negative weight to place on the scale.
(3) The non-identity problem undermines person-affecting harm claims about procreation: if a child is brought into existence with a life worth living, we have not made that particular child worse off, since the only alternative for them was non-existence.
(4) Despite (3), I hold that parents are morally responsible for the suffering of their children, because they are the cause of that suffering's existence, even if no specific individual has been made worse off.
(5) I accept evolutionary debunking arguments against the intuition that reproduction is a fundamental moral good or that childlessness is a harm to parents, but I think our judgment that suffering is bad survives debunking because it is anchored in direct phenomenological access rather than in adaptive value.
(6) I am a moral realist: there are mind-independent moral facts, and the badness of suffering is one of them.
(7) I arrived at (1) and (2) partly through reflective equilibrium, weighing considered judgments about harm, absence, and reproductive ethics for coherence with broader principles.
(8) I think reproductive freedom is a strong negative right: the state should neither coerce reproduction nor subsidize childbearing, even though (1)-(4) give citizens moral reasons not to create suffering lives.
