ok so im a retired physicist who recently got into philosophy of science, mostly bayesian epistemology. i keep running into talk about the old evidence problem -- theory predicts E, but E was already known, so does belief go up? fair enough. but heres a pattern i see in actual science that doesnt quite match. in my old field people sometimes dig up measurements from the 80s that were published with no clear theoretical target, then decades later a new model comes along and claims those same numbers as precise confirmation. another case: climate archives from the 70s that werent measuring the right variable for any then-existing theory, but now get cited as if they predicted todays warming curve. in both cases the data is old, but it wasnt evidence for the new theory back then because nobody knew what to look for. its not fresh data, but its also not the classic old-evidence case where everyone already knew E and its relevance. is there a name for this specific thing -- old measurements getting retroactively promoted to confirming evidence? and if there isnt one, can you coin a term with a sharp definition that separates it from plain old evidence and from just reinterpreting old results?
