Both appear to sever the intuitive link between comprehension and competence, but I want to examine whether they do so in the same direction. Darwin's inversion seems ontological: it explains how competence can arise in the world without being caused by a comprehending agent. Turing's inversion, by contrast, seems methodological or epistemological: it asks whether our grounds for attributing comprehension to others can survive the discovery that the underlying mechanism is comprehension-less.
