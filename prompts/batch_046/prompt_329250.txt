Write an essay addressing a specific controversy in Schopenhauer's philosophy of music. Schopenhauer maintains that music is not a representation of Platonic Ideas, as painting, poetry, or sculpture are, but rather a direct objectification of the Will itself—an immediate copy of the inner nature of reality that bypasses the principium individuationis and conceptual cognition entirely. The controversy is whether this claim of metaphysical directness can survive careful scrutiny of musical experience.

Weigh the two standard readings. The literalist reading takes Schopenhauer at his word: music expresses the Will without mediation, and its emotional power derives precisely from this unmediated contact with the essence of being. The mediationist reading argues that this directness is illusory, because musical understanding requires temporal form, harmonic expectation, and culturally shaped emotional cognition—structures that mediate between sound and the listener's awareness of the Will, placing music closer to representational art than Schopenhauer allows.

Then introduce and examine a third position, which you should call structured immediacy. On this view, music does objectify the Will directly, but not in the raw acoustic material alone; rather, the directness occurs through the listener's embodied, non-conceptual anticipation of harmonic resolution and rhythmic tension. The Will is expressed in the gap between pattern and bodily drive, not in a purely subjective emotional overlay and not in unmediated sound. Assess whether structured immediacy genuinely preserves Schopenhauer's claim that music is metaphysically unique, or whether it collapses music back into mediated representation.

Reach and defend a clear verdict: either that structured immediacy saves Schopenhauer's uniqueness thesis, or that it undermines it. Your verdict must turn on Schopenhauer's broader metaphysics of the Will as blind, striving, and non-rational.

Organize your response in the following sections, in this order, with approximate lengths:
1. 'Schopenhauer's core claim' (around 150 words): summarize his account of music's relation to the Will and its distinction from the other arts.
2. 'The directness debate' (around 250 words): present the literalist and mediationist readings, giving the strongest version of each.
3. 'Structured immediacy' (around 250 words): lay out the third option precisely, explaining how embodied anticipation operates without becoming conceptual representation.
4. 'The verdict' (around 250 words): defend your conclusion with explicit reasoning tied to the Will's nature.
5. 'One admitted weakness' (around 100 words): identify the most vulnerable point in your defended position and concede its force briefly.
