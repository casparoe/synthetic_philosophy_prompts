Two people in my study group—well, technically my office hours—got stuck arguing after I mentioned Hobsbawm and Anderson in passing. One of them, let's call her A, insisted that if Hobsbawm is right then asking "how old is France" is basically a category error: the nation-form is a 19th-century invention, so France-as-nation is young even if the territory is old. The other, B, said that's way too reductive. B pointed out that people in 14th-century France clearly had some kind of collective identity, and Anderson's whole point isn't that nations are fake but that they're imagined in a specific mode—horizontal comradeship among strangers who'll never meet. So the belonging isn't new, it's just the form of it.

A fired back: fine, but then Hobsbawm's claim just collapses into "social structures evolve," which is true but trivial. You need a sharper boundary or you've just renamed modernization. B said the boundary is there—pre-modern solidarity was vertical (dynastic, religious, personal loyalty) while national solidarity is anonymous and simultaneous. But then A asked: what about the Republic of Letters, or the international scientific community? Those are horizontal, anonymous, and they pre-date the nation-state. So either Anderson's criterion picks out way too much, or you have to smuggle in territorial sovereignty, which he doesn't do consistently.

B tried to save it by saying the nation is special because it claims sovereignty over a bounded territory and demands ultimate loyalty—something the Republic of Letters never did. A said that's just defining the state, not explaining why the community aspect is distinctive. They went back and forth on this for awhile, then B mentioned sub-state groups like the Sami or modern indigenous sovereignty movements that invent traditions after declaring autonomy—does Hobsbawm's framework apply there differently?

Can you go through each round and say who's right about which specific point, not just who wins overall? What's both of them missing—maybe about how invented traditions work for groups that aren't trying to be states? I'm especially unsure about B's sovereignty move: does it save Anderson or just change the subject?
