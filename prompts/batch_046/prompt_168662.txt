I help administer a community makerspace that distributes workshop credits equally among members at the start of each quarter, with members free to trade them for services. One member, whose machining skills are exceptional, began offering repair and calibration work in exchange for credits. Through months of entirely voluntary, uncoerced exchanges, she accumulated enough to monopolize the premium equipment for the next two quarters, while others depleted their reserves and now face effective exclusion. Several members argue the result is unjust: every transaction was consensual, yet the distribution is now severely unequal and undermines the cooperative purpose. She replies that she never forced anyone, the initial allocation was fair, and that redistributing her credits would violate freedom of contract. Where does the injustice lie, if anywhere, and by what principle should we decide whether to intervene?
