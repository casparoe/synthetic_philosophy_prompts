I was chatting with some friends from my old LessWrong reading group about the Frege-Geach problem for expressivism, and we kept going round in circles -- everyone agrees embedded moral sentences are embarasing for pure expressivism, but nobody can say whether the problem is about compositionality, about logical form, or about something else entirely. So I'm trying to figure out if this debate is actually stalled, and if so, what kind of disagreement it is. Is it substantive -- a real clash over whether normative language carries propositional content -- or is it verbal, with both sides using 'meaning' for different jobs? Or are they answering different questions, one about semantic theory and one about cognitive processing, without realising? I want a diagnosis backed up by what each side actually says about cases like 'If torture is wrong, then ordering torture is wrong', plus an honest answer on whether anything -- a formal distinction, a shift in framing, or even giving up on one desiderata -- could actually move things foward.

One niche framing I've been playing with: treat the issue not as standard propositional attitude embedding, but as the transmission of normative force through syntactic trees in a kind of 'moral syntax' where the embedding operator itself carries evaluative weight. I don't want you to solve that, but keep it in mind when you diagnose whether the sides are even asking the same question.
