I am grading exam responses in an upper-division ancient philosophy course. The prompt asked students to compare Plato's treatment of intoxication in the Symposium with that in Laws I–II, with particular attention to three issues: how the Athenian Stranger describes regulated drinking as a pedagogical test for young citizens; how Alcibiades' portrait of Socrates at the drinking party relates to that testing function; and whether Plato treats alcohol as revealing pre-existing dispositions or as altering them, including whether Socrates' exceptional sobriety supports or undermines the Stranger's proposal.

Exam answer:

The Athenian Stranger in Laws I–II proposes that wine be permitted in a regulated manner for older citizens to ease the rigidity of age, but its primary pedagogical value lies with the young. By lowering social inhibitions, intoxication functions as a gymnastic for the soul: it reveals whether a person's underlying nature is orderly or disorderly, allowing educators to correct what is hidden. Crucially, alcohol does not alter one's nature; it only strips away pretense so that true dispositions become visible. In the Symposium, Alcibiades praises Socrates for being able to drink the entire party under the table without ever losing rational composure. This demonstrates exactly the principle from the Laws: Socrates possesses a soul so well-ordered that even under the influence of wine, nothing but virtue is revealed. Hence Socrates' sobriety confirms the Athenian Stranger's pedagogical program, showing that philosophical education can produce citizens immune to corruption. At the same time, the Symposium adds a further layer. Alcibiades only confesses his true erotic attachment to Socrates when he is drunk, which shows that wine does not merely reveal pre-existing states but actively generates new conditions of disclosure that sober discourse suppresses. Because the Laws employs wine to test youth and the Symposium employs it to uncover hidden truths, the two dialogues present a unified Platonic view: intoxication is a tool that reveals and produces truth, provided the soul has been properly educated.
