ok im trying to pin down my view on collective memory and forgetting before a seminar on Margalit and Rieff. here's roughly where i am:

1. Margalit-style shared memory creates real intergenerational obligations—if a society wronged a group, descendants have a standing claim that the wrong not be erased from public record.
2. Rieff is basically right that therapeutic culture turns moral memory into self-care, so we should resist framing remembrance as just trauma processing for individuals.
3. but i also think there's a strong individual right to be forgotten—people should be able to withdraw personal histories from archives and refuse to be memorialized against their will.
4. democratic reconciliation requires institutional forgetting: amnesty, sealed records, expiration of collective guilt, otherwise grievance becomes permanent.
5. the duty to remember is stricter for direct descendants and victims than for bystanders—once formal acknowledgment happens, bystanders can legitimately move on.

do these hang together? im especially worried that (3) clashes with (1)—if collective memory is binding on a society, how can individuals opt out of being part of that record? and does (5) undermine the universality implied in (1)? also (2) and (4) might look tense but maybe theyre not—can you check? rank the actual tensions by severity, say what'd have to give in each case, and tell me the single revision that'd clean up the most mess without losing my core commitments. also flag any pair that only looks bad but actually dissolves on inspection.
