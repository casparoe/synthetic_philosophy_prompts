Please respond in American English. I have been working through Benatar's asymmetry argument, and I keep encountering what looks like an internal tension. Benatar holds that the absence of suffering is good even when there is no one to benefit, whereas the absence of pleasure is not bad when there is no one to be deprived. This framing seems to treat non-existence as an axiologically evaluable condition for the merely possible person who never comes to be—assigning it a positive value (the good of lacking pain) without a corresponding negative value—yet Benatar also insists that potential persons have no interests at all. But if these impersonal goods and bads are genuinely predicated of states involving specific possible beings, doesn't the argument implicitly confer on them a form of moral standing? In particular, if it is good for them (in this directed but impersonal sense) that they avoid suffering by never existing, wouldn't they possess an interest-based claim against being created, which Benatar explicitly rejects? How can the asymmetry be maintained without either smuggling in interests for non-existent beings or collapsing into a rights-based antinatalism that Benatar wants to avoid?
