I'm in a seminar on practical reason focusing on transformative experience and rational agency, and I'm stuck on a conceptual distinction that seems to sit just outside the standard framing. L.A. Paul argues that transformative experiences—such as becoming a parent or undergoing a radical sensory alteration—create an epistemic gap: prior to the change, we lack first-person access to what it will be like and to how we will value it. But I'm trying to determine whether there is a further, structural problem that goes beyond this ignorance—something like "preference-metric collapse," where the transformation doesn't merely replace our values but dissolves the very evaluative framework that made our pre-transformation preferences coherent. Please explain whether Paul's account requires this stronger structural claim, or whether the epistemic-gap explanation is sufficient on its own. If metric collapse is genuinely distinct from first-person inaccessibility, how does it affect arguments about diachronic rational continuity? Must we then treat the post-transformation agent as a different rational subject rather than as the same agent with updated values, and what does that imply for the possibility of rationally authorizing such a choice in advance?
