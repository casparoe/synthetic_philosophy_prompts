Xunzi denies this: he takes human nature to be driven by self-interested desire, and argues that virtue is an artificial construction achieved only through ritual discipline (li), the guidance of teachers, and the coercive framework of law. The standard summary treats this as an ancient disagreement about goodness versus badness, but that framing misses the deeper structural question: is moral development better understood as the cultivation of an existing gradient, or as the imposition of a regularizing constraint on a prior that lacks moral content?
