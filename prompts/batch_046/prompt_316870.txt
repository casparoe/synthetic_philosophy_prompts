I am writing an analysis for my philosophy of science course on the taxonomy that distinguishes cranes, skyhooks, and greedy reductionism. The framework I am using—encountered in a recent lecture series on hierarchical explanation—treats a crane as an account that builds a complex phenomenon step-by-step through intermediate organizational levels without skipping them; a skyhook as an explanation that introduces an unexplained, top-down cause lacking grounding in lower-level mechanisms; and greedy reductionism as an account that collapses a phenomenon directly into its bottom-level constituents while dismissing the explanatory autonomy of the layers in between.

My instructor has applied this taxonomy to the following cases:
1. The evolutionary account of the vertebrate eye through successive modifications of light-sensitive tissue, neural wiring, and selection pressure: a crane.
2. The vitalist claim that metabolic processes require an irreducible life-force not derivable from chemistry: a skyhook.
3. The assertion that artistic genius is exhaustively explained by dopamine receptor density in the prefrontal cortex, leaving no explanatory remainder for training, cultural context, or aesthetic judgment: greedy reductionism.
4. The climate-science explanation of glacial retreat through coupled feedback loops among atmospheric chemistry, ocean circulation, and surface albedo: a crane.
5. The explanation of an empire’s collapse solely through the personality disorders of its final three rulers, with explicit disregard for economic structures, agricultural failure, and institutional decay: greedy reductionism.
