Write a Shakespearean sonnet—rhyme scheme ABAB CDCD EFEF GG, volta at line nine—that works through Kant’s distinction between duties with regard to animals and duties directed toward them, but frames the entire meditation through the concrete situation of standing in a veterinary clinic deciding on euthanasia for a long-term companion animal who is suffering irreversibly. In the octave, articulate the indirect-duty view with precision: that cruelty or neglect is wrong chiefly because it blunts the human capacity for moral feeling toward rational beings, not because the animal is itself a direct bearer of moral status. At the volta, shift to the immediate, unmediated presence of the animal’s pain and the felt inadequacy of treating this decision as merely an exercise in preserving one’s own moral sensibility. In the sestet, hold the tension open without resolving it toward either full animal rights or pure instrumentality; let the speaker recognize that attending directly to the suffering as genuinely belonging to the animal makes the indirect-duty justification feel like a failure of attention rather than a philosophical answer. Close the couplet on the specific unresolved contradiction: the more one respects the animal’s suffering as its own, the further one moves from the Kantian taxonomy, yet without arriving at an alternative framework that can accommodate both that recognition and the absence of rational autonomy. Write in a formal, reflective voice suitable for a philosophical memorial rather than a polemic, and do not invoke any named contemporary commentators or textual editions—only the conceptual structure itself.
