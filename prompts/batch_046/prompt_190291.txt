Rank American restaurant-tipping norms, British queuing rules, and German anti-jaywalking conventions by the strength of moral obligation each creates for a short-term visitor who regards every social convention as a purely instrumental coordination game with no intrinsic moral weight. For each convention, assign a score between 0 and 1 indicating the proportion of its normative force that derives from coordination value rather than from direct harm avoidance, and place all three in tier lists—Strong, Moderate, or Weak—for both dimensions. Then explain which structural feature—visibility of harm, irreversible cost imposition on others, or ease of reversal—best accounts for why local moral condemnation of violations is warranted in some cases but amounts to overreach in others.
