Write a deadpan parody of an international development agency's technical report appendix—around 700 words—titled "Comparative Assessment of Grievance Regimes: Honor, Dignity, and Victimhood." The report attempts to determine, through quantitative metrics, whether honor culture, dignity culture, or victimhood culture is morally superior. Adopt the voice of a bureaucrat who treats conceptual moral philosophy as a dataset: dry, statistical, institutional, with no winking or explicit jokes. The target is the very idea of ranking incommensurable moral frameworks with indicators, coefficients, and policy recommendations.

Structure the piece as follows: an introduction that defines the three regimes precisely—honor culture (public reputation maintained through collective retaliation and social sanction), dignity culture (internal worth protected by universal legal rights and emotional restraint), and victimhood culture (suffering mediated through institutional authorities with moral status derived from grievance). Then present a fictional "Moral Superiority Index" composed of four fabricated sub-indicators: Retaliation Latency, Institutional Trust Density, Grievance Spectacle Index, and—your novel injection—the "Dignity Decay Rate," measuring how rapidly a dignity culture slips into victimhood logic once formal legal channels are perceived as slow or unresponsive. Include a comparison table described only in prose (do not format an actual table; describe columns, values, and surprising cross-cultural results, such as honor cultures scoring highest on Retaliation Latency but lowest on Institutional Trust Density). Invent one hybrid case study—perhaps a "face-saving dignity" society that uses civil lawsuits not for material compensation but for public ritual restoration of reputation—showing how the taxonomy collapses when the categories mix.

The satire should exaggerate real structural features—honor's collective accountability, dignity's suppression of emotional display, victimhood's institutional dependency—rather than inventing strawmen, so that a reader familiar with the sociological literature recognizes the underlying concepts but finds them pushed into absurd administrative form. Include one bureaucratic footnote explaining why "moral superiority" was operationalized as "lower grievance-related mortality plus higher subjective autonomy scores," letting the logic quietly undermine itself. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or references to named scholars; paraphrase any borrowed concepts without quotation marks. Keep the irony sustained and deadpan throughout: the absurdity should emerge entirely from the form, not from direct mockery.
