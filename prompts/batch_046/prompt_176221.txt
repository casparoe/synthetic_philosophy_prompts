hey — longtime lurker on teh rationalist forums here, and i've got a row with my flatmate about meat, wild suffering, and land use that i genuinely can't adjudicate myself.

she's mostly vegan and says chicken is worse than beef if you care about wild animals. her reasoning goes like this: chicken has a better feed-conversion ratio, but that feed is almost all soy and maize, so every kg of chicken is tied to harvest machinery that kills huge numbers of field mice, voles and small birds twice a year. beef, she argues, mostly runs on pasture that doesn't get mechanically harvested, so the direct accidental mortality per calorie is lower. then she adds a replacement point — if beef demand dropped, a lot of that pasture wouldn't revert to wild meadow; it'd be ploughed for crops or developed, which might actually increase wild-animal deaths per hectare.

i pushed back in a few rounds. first, i said she's ignoring total land footprint: beef still requires enormous area, including grain fed to cattle in feedlots, so its hidden crop-death footprint isn't zero — and pasture displaces wild herbivores and predators that could have lived there. second, i argued arable land between harvests supports wild bird and insect populations, whereas overgrazed pasture is often biodiversity-poor, so her "pasture is safer" framing is too quick. third, i brought up the replacement problem in reverse: if people switch from beef to chicken, you're not freeing pasture, you're ramping up demand for the exact crops that cause harvest deaths, which seems like the wrong direction. she replied that i'm treating land-use categories as fixed, and that grain-finished beef is a production choice, not a law — grass-fed beef uses even more land per calorie, so my switch-to-chicken point actually proves her case that beef should be avoided?

then she hit me with the net-lives thing: she said i'm implicitly assuming wild animals have lives worth living, but if wild suffering dominates — predation, starvation, disease — then reducing wild populations via crop displacement might even be good. i said that was sneaking a normative premise into what was supposed to be an empirical argument about land use, and that she couldn't have it both ways.

we're stuck on four moves: (1) the feed-conversion / harvest-mortality comparison between chicken and beef; (2) the biodiversity and pasture-vs-arable point; (3) the replacement / counterfactual land-use argument; and (4) the net-lives / wild-suffering-dominates move.

can you go through each move and rate it from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with brief justification? also say who's closer to being right overall, and what we're both missing — particularly on marginal demand elasticity (does eating one less chicken actually reduce harvest area proportionally, or is teh supply chain too inelastic?) and on suffering-intensity comparison (machinery death vs predation and starvation). please actually take a view rather than just saying it depends, but show your work. and no citations or reading lists needed — i want to know if teh reasoning holds up, not who said it first.
