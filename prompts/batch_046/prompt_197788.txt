Before I commit to this interpretation, I want to understand exactly where it is vulnerable.

1. The position stated precisely (about 150 words): Clarify the ontological commitment of the strong Avicennian reading—specifically that substantial independence is established rather than mere epistemic priority—and distinguish it carefully from Descartes's cogito on the grounds of skepticism and the role of bodily doubt.

2. Decisive defeaters (about 250 words): What would have to be true for this interpretation to collapse? Include one conceptual objection (for instance, concerning the principle of individuation for immaterial substances) and one hypothetical empirical result (for instance, about neural structures necessary for first-person reference) that would be fatal rather than merely awkward. Explain why each strikes at the ontological claim specifically.

3. What defenders would concede (about 200 words): Which objections would a committed proponent accept as genuinely damaging, and which would they dismiss as category errors, strawmen, or as conflating epistemic with ontological independence? Be explicit about the boundary they would draw.

Assess whether such insulation is a virtue protecting metaphysical inquiry from empirical overreach, or a vice that renders the flying-man argument explanatorily empty.

5. Bottom line (about 100 words): Rank the risks from fatal to negligible, and state whether the interpretation remains philosophically defensible once its exposure is honestly mapped.

Please do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Focus on the conceptual architecture, the conditions of failure, and the distinction between what is genuinely risked and what is merely inconvenient for the view.
