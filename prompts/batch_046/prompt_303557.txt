For my philosophy of mind seminar, I have been asked to write down my working philosophical profile on consciousness so that the others in the group can stress-test its coherence. I am treating this as an audit of my actual commitments, not as a defense of any named school. Here is where I stand:

1. I am an ontological physicalist: every concrete fact about consciousness is ultimately a physical fact.
2. I take the philosophical zombie scenario to be genuinely conceivable in a robust sense, not merely apparently so.
3. I regard clear conceivability as a fallible but real guide to metaphysical possibility: if I can stably conceive of X without contradiction, that gives me a defeasible reason to think X is metaphysically possible.
4. I accept the phenomenal-concept strategy: phenomenal concepts involve a direct, acquaintance-based mode of presentation that is conceptually isolated from physical-functional descriptions, and this isolation explains why there is an explanatory gap.
5. I hold a version of representationalism: for a mental state to be phenomenally conscious just is for it to have presentationally immediate, non-derivative representational content.
6. I deny that there is anything it is like to be a thermostat, despite its minimal functional and informational states.
7. I take the hard problem to be a problem of explanatory access and conceptual structure, not an ontological gap.
8. I maintain a broadly naturalistic epistemology: in general, knowledge of a domain requires some appropriate causal or informational connection to that domain.
9. More specifically, I think phenomenal concepts work by what I would call indexical embedding: the concept I deploy expresses not a descriptive property but an indexical relation that ties a physical state to the subject who instantiates it, analogous to how spatial indexicals like here pick out a location relative to a speaker.

Please go through these commitments and audit them for joint tenability. For each pair that is genuinely in tension, indicate how severe the tension is, trace exactly what each commitment commits me to, and identify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what it costs. Be sure to distinguish genuine conflicts from tensions that only look problematic on the surface: I suspect there may be an apparent conflict involving representationalism and the denial of thermostat consciousness, and possibly another involving conceivability and physicalism that dissolves once the phenomenal-concept strategy is taken seriously, but I do not know which ones are real and which dissolve on inspection. Finally, tell me which single revision to my profile would eliminate the most tension overall, and whether that revision is one I should actually want to make.
