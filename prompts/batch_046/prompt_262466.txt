I am a doctoral student in philosophy of cognitive architecture, and I am trying to formalize the conditions for downward causation in substrate-symbol systems. In my framework, a careenium is a self-organizing substrate composed of coupled dynamic nodes that enter cascading 'careen' states, while simmballs are stable symbolic patterns embedded within that substrate, defined not by local physical boundaries but by relational encoding within the node lattice. The guiding question is whether these symbols ever push the substrate around—whether a simmball's semantic content can make an independent causal difference—or whether all apparent influence collapses into the physics of the underlying nodes.

I have constructed the following argument, but it drives toward a conclusion I find unacceptable, and I cannot identify the precise weak point. (1) For any entity to causally affect a substrate, it must either be physically distinct from it or be realized within it with properties that make a causal difference. (2) A simmball is not physically distinct from the careenium; it is just a particular configuration and relational position of careening nodes. (3) Therefore, any causal influence a simmball exerts is fully mediated by the physical properties of the nodes that realize it. (4) If the causal work is done entirely by the nodes, then the simmball's semantic content—what it represents—makes no independent causal contribution. (5) Yet we can build a careenium architecture in which two simmballs are locally physically identical in every node property but encode opposite regulatory rules solely through their lattice-relative contextual position; the substrate responds differently to each, tracking the encoded rule rather than any local physical feature. (6) In such cases, the difference in substrate dynamics is explained by the symbol's relational meaning, not by any underlying physical difference. (7) Hence, symbols do push the substrate around, and they do so in virtue of their semantic content rather than their physical form. (8) But if semantic content can make a causal difference without a corresponding local physical mediator, then downward causation is non-physical. (9) If downward causation is non-physical in careenia, then by structural analogy it is non-physical in brains, computers, and any other symbol-bearing substrate. (10) So either we accept widespread dualism about symbolic systems or deny that the architectures described in (5) are genuinely possible.

The conclusion seems wrong—especially the leap to widespread dualism—yet I am reluctant to abandon (5), since the contextually encoded simmball is precisely the novel architecture I want to defend, and I do not wish to surrender (3) without collapsing into eliminativism. Which step should I reject? Please explain why it looked plausible, then spell out what a strict reductionist, a non-reductive physicalist who accepts downward causation, and a relational-functional account would each have to pay or gain by denying it. Finally, if the argument is valid and the cost falls on (5), describe what a conceptual test—say, a mirror-lattice simmball with inverted contextual encoding—would need to demonstrate in order to adjudicate between the reductionist denial and the non-reductive acceptance.
