I am preparing to moderate a colloquium interview with a philosopher—let us refer to her as Dr. Sorel—who defends a distinctive variant of the predictive-processing view that consciousness is a user illusion. On her account, the brain does not generate an inner theater observed by a subject; rather, it constructs a compressed narrative self-model—what she terms a control interface—whose function is to coordinate long-horizon action and social interaction. She maintains that this interface is structurally transparent: it is engineered to represent the world and the agent's relation to it, but not to represent itself as a representation, since doing so would compromise its operational stability. Additionally, she proposes that phenomenal character—the what-it-is-like quality of experience—is not a feature of the interface's content, but rather a diagnostic signal that flags high-confidence prediction errors demanding narrative revision.

I am seeking eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond having her restate this framework. The questions should press where the current literature presses, anticipate the standard reply, and include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Specifically, I require coverage of the following angles:

- One question on whether structural transparency renders her view unfalsifiable, with a follow-up that addresses her probable response that transparency is a functional design constraint rather than an epistemic shield.

- One question comparing her diagnostic-signal account of phenomenal character to both representationalist and illusionist alternatives, with a follow-up tailored to her likely defense that her position avoids both the hard problem of consciousness and full-blooded eliminativism.

- One question on how her framework treats apparent breakdowns of the user illusion—such as in certain altered states or specific neuropathological conditions—without reducing these cases to a simple absence or switching-off of consciousness, including a follow-up for her expected claim that these are format failures or compression errors rather than experiential voids.

- One question on the evolutionary or architectural rationale for adopting this particular narrative-compression format, rather than a leaner error-monitoring or feedback system, with a follow-up anticipating her argument that social coordination and long-term planning require a unified, persisting agent-model.

Please avoid generic biographical questions. Each item should be substantive, formally phrased, and designed to make her standard deflections progressively harder to sustain.
