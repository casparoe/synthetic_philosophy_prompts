For a conceptual dataset on alignment-adjacent philosophy of science, design a taxonomy—structured as a branching decision tree with concrete diagnostic nodes—that sorts positions on what counts as the same mechanism being detected in mechanistic anomaly detection.

The motivating problem is that interpretability research routinely tracks circuits across model updates, quantization, and sparse interventions, yet rarely states the identity conditions being assumed. Build the tree from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of named theories, so that an informed reader can locate their own commitments without recognizing a label.

Use exactly three branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete scenario with yes/no/unsure options, answerable without prior knowledge of philosophical taxonomy:

Node A: A circuit's causal graph is preserved under intervention testing after forty percent of its constituent attention heads have been pruned and replaced with residual connections that replicate the statistical behavior. Is the post-intervention structure the same mechanism?

Node B: During distributed representation learning, the functional role previously localized in layer L is now split across layers L and L+2 with partial overlap. An anomaly detector calibrated to the original localization flags the split version as anomalous, yet the input-output mapping and intermediate causal dependencies are unchanged. Should this be classified as mechanism corruption or mechanism migration?

Node C: Two structurally identical mechanisms run in parallel within the same forward pass; one exhibits statistically anomalous activation patterns on out-of-distribution inputs while the other does not. For safety-relevant detection purposes, does mechanism identity depend on shared causal role, shared physical substrate, or shared normative risk profile?

From these nodes, derive the full leaf space. Required leaves must include: strict substrate identity (no identity across physical change); causal-role continuity regardless of substrate or distribution; a no-branching functional-equivalence view that denies identity if the mechanism splits or duplicates; a pluralist family-resemblance view that rejects any single necessary-and-sufficient condition; and one explicitly unnamed position corresponding to a logically possible but apparently unoccupied combination of answers. For each leaf, provide one sentence describing its most awkward practical commitment—paying special attention to the view that treats parallel redundant copies as non-identical for risk assessment.

Flag any pair of leaves that these three nodes cannot genuinely separate, and explain briefly whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere underexploration. Phrase every node as a commitment on the scenario, not as a theoretical label. The intended audience is technically sophisticated readers familiar with mechanistic interpretability debates but not necessarily with formal philosophy of science taxonomy; avoid bibliographic references, citation requests, or reading lists, since the instrument should be fully usable by someone reasoning without web access. Keep the deliverable formal and polished, suitable for a seminar handout.
