I am trying to stabilize my view on distributive justice before presenting at a reading group, and I want an internal consistency audit rather than a lecture. Please separate what is broadly settled among philosophers working on these issues from what is genuinely contested or depends on idiosyncratic framing, and do not present a disputed interpretation as if it were the consensus.

Here is my current package:

(1) I accept prioritarianism with a smooth, concave welfare-weighting function—moral importance declines continuously as baseline welfare rises, with no lexical threshold or discontinuity.

(2) I reject the leveling-down objection in its standard form: equality is not intrinsically valuable, and reducing the welfare of better-off people without benefiting anyone is never an improvement.

(3) I hold that the correct evaluation of policies and actions is ex post, not ex ante: moral assessment should depend on the actual welfare distribution that results, not on expected values calculated at the time of choice.

(4) I accept a contractualist constraint of Scanlonian form: it is impermissible to impose a severe burden on an identifiable individual solely to secure a diffuse pattern of small prioritarian benefits for many others who are only marginally worse off.

(5) Under conditions of deep uncertainty—where we cannot assign probabilities—I think we should fall back on maximin applied to prioritarian-weighted welfare, but once any probabilities are available we should abandon maximin and maximize expected prioritarian value.

(6) I calibrate prioritarian weights to lifetime-integrated welfare rather than to momentary welfare, so that temporary deprivation receives proportionally less moral weight than sustained deprivation at the same level.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and for each real tension what is the least costly revision—specifying whether a commitment must be abandoned, qualified, or reinterpreted, and what that costs elsewhere in the package? Order the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. Also indicate, for each commitment, whether it represents a standard prioritarian position or depends on a more contested or niche assumption.
