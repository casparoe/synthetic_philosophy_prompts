I sit on the advisory council of the Meridian Foundation, which votes Thursday on a proposed $2M program titled "Cohorts as Causal Kinds." The plan organizes a new museum wing and research grants around generational labels—Silent, Boomer, Gen X, Millennial, Gen Z—treating them as explanatory categories with predictive and counterfactual force rather than as mere demographic bins. Staff would fund hierarchical age-period-cohort studies and design galleries that assume cohort membership carries inductive projectibility. The council is split: one side sees real differences in formative conditions; the other sees an ecological fallacy that reifies arbitrary birth-year slices, especially since boundary cases like "Zillennials" seem to lack determinate membership conditions. Please draft the short opinion memo I will circulate before the vote. Recommend approve, approve with conditions, or reject, with concrete criteria the staff could actually implement if we move forward. Treat the reification worry seriously: discuss whether cohorts behave like natural kinds (with homeostatic property clusters), like interactive kinds (where labeling alters behavior), or like statistical artifacts of age-period confounding. Also address the curatorial implication—without diachronic identity conditions, is there a fact of the matter about whether a 1996-born visitor belongs, and can cohort-based gallery boundaries still be justified without misrepresenting the ontology? Include the strongest argument for the losing side, state a clear bottom line, and do not include a reading list or bibliography.
