Next tuesday I present at our dept seminar on Frankfurt cases and the principle of alternative possibilities. I'm defending this hybrid view: Frankfurt-style counterexamples only defeat PAP if we treat "alternative possibilities" as counterfactual branches inside a deterministic system, but if we reconstrue PAP as requiring access to what I'm calling "deliberative redundancy" — the capacity to reframe one's own reasons without external causal preemption — then Jones still retains an alternative, just not the one Black's device is monitoring. The audience is mostly free-will skeptics who think Frankfurt already killed PAP dead, plus a legal philosopher who works on excuse conditions in criminal law, and two grad students who just read Frankfurt's original paper last week. I need the objections ordered by how much damage they'd actually do, phrased the way each of them would put it: the circularity charge from the skeptics, the legal theorist's duress analogy, the grad-student "but isn't Black just like a brain tumor?" question, and whatever the skeptic is most likely to ask about my view collapsing into source compatibilism. For each one give me a two-minute reply, what I should concede up front, and flag the softest point in that reply. Also include the objection I'm praying nobody asks — probably something about whether "deliberative redundancy" is just epistemic access repackaged — with a note on whether I should try to answer it or just admit I don't have a crisp boundary condition yet. Keep it practical: I have ten minutes for Q&A and I can't just cite literature I haven't read.
