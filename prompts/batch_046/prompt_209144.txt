I chair our university's Interdisciplinary AI Governance and Research Coordination Board. We are evaluating a proposal — the Opt-Out Consensus Protocol (OCP) — that would permit any individual research lab in our network to unilaterally design, publish, and enforce its own "counterfactual robustness audit" and "stochastic parity constraint" benchmarks for studies involving generative models, without board-level clearance, provided the lab files a 30-day notice in a shared registry. Advocates argue that distributed epistemic judgment is more resilient than centralized review and that requiring board approval for every benchmark creates an epistemic bottleneck. Opponents reply that this is precisely the unilateralist's curse in institutional form: if each group applies its own risk calculus, the aggregate effect of well-intentioned local standards could fragment the research ecosystem, impose redundant compliance costs, and trigger a de facto race toward the most restrictive interpretation, ultimately undermining the collaborative infrastructure the board was created to protect.

We must issue a concrete decision — approve OCP as proposed, reject it, or approve with conditions — together with an implementation time frame (for example, a six-month pilot with a mandatory sunset review, immediate adoption with safeguards, or an outright rejection with a moratorium). The memo should present the recommendation up front, followed by reasoning that engages the pro-decentralization position seriously rather than treating it as mere anti-bureaucratic sentiment, and should identify the strongest consideration on the losing side with intellectual honesty. The document should be suitable for circulation before Thursday's meeting and should stand on its own reasoning without relying on external citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.

Please draft this board memo.
