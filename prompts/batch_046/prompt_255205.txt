I am trying to develop a focused research agenda around the symbol grounding problem, specifically for artificial systems that acquire representations without direct sensory-motor coupling to the physical world. The familiar debates — Searle's Chinese Room argument, Harnad's formulation of the symbol-grounding problem, the dispute over whether distributed vector representations in neural networks constitute genuine grounding, and the embodied-cognition claim that grounding requires physical interaction — are all in the background. What interests me is a narrower, less settled tension: suppose a system learns its symbols entirely through predictive modeling over large-scale textual and multimodal corpora, and is then embedded in a synthetic virtual environment where it can manipulate rendered objects and receive feedback, but only through linguistic or symbolic descriptions of those interactions rather than through direct physical causation. In that setting, is the linguistic mediation an insurmountable barrier to grounding, or can a form of second-order grounding — stabilization through the reported grounded experiences of other agents — eventually yield representations that are functionally equivalent to first-order grounding?
