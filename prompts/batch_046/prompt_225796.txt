I've been trying to map out my actual intuitions about Confucian role ethics for a paper I'm sketching, and I keep hitting these wierd edge cases that don't fit the standard father-son vs magistrate story everyone uses. So here's where I land on concrete cases, and I want to know if there's a coherent principle behind them or if I'm just being inconsistent. Case one: a father hides his son's petty theft from the authorities — I feel that's actually okay, almost required, because the family role comes first. Case two: same father is the village magistrate and hides the theft — actually no, I think he should report it, which means I'm not consistently putting family first. Case three: an older sister who is also her younger brother's teacher discovers he plagiarized an exam; I think she should fail him, but I feel guilty about it in a way I don't with strangers. Case four: a close friend asks you to lie about his whereabouts to save him embarassment in front of his in-laws — that feels permissible, like maintaining harmony is part of the role. Case five: the same friend asks you to lie to a judge in court — totally wrong, friendship doesn't extend that far. So there's some boundary around institutional roles vs private ones.

But then there's this case that keeps breaking things: you're caring for a neighbor's child after the parents died, but there's no formal adoption, no teacher-student relation, just this interstitial caregiving role that doesn't map onto the five relationships. The kid steals money from you. I think you should confront them directly and maybe even report it to the authorities if it's serious — treating them more like a stranger than family, even though you've fed them for years. That feels right to me, but it seems to contradict the idea that care generates role-obligations. And what about when that same now-grown child asks you to lie on their job application about a past conviction? I keep going back and forth. Is there one account — maybe about the density or institutional recognition of a role, or some kind of role-residue idea — that captures why family loyalty works in case one but collapses in the magistrate version, why friendship allows face-saving but not perjury, and why this weird non-adopted caregiver relation doesn't generate family-style leniency? Or do I need to accept that Confucian role ethics just has a gap for interstitial roles? Please lay out the principle precisely, say what it predicts for the grown-child job-application lie, and be honest about which of my gut calls — especially the caregiver-strictness one — it can't save without becoming ad hoc.
