I defend a strengthened version of the Chase-Sanborn thesis that qualia are conceptually incoherent rather than merely epistemically elusive: the notion of an intrinsic, non-relational phenomenal property cannot satisfy the determinacy conditions required for property-individuation, yet phenomenal reports—distinguishing red from green, or comparing degrees of similarity—presuppose exactly such determinacy. If phenomenal character were genuinely non-relational, comparative judgments about qualitative difference would be groundless, leaving only an undifferentiated something-it-is-like that lacks the structure needed to explain introspective knowledge, inverted-spectrum possibilities, or first-person authority. What would genuinely count against this specific formulation? I want the decisive defeaters separated from the merely awkward ones: conceptual arguments showing that intrinsic properties can be determinately ascribed without relational anchoring; empirical results about the neural architecture of consciousness that reveal a stable, relationally-independent phenomenal structure; or formal results in metaphysics of properties that undermine the determinacy objection.
