Write a dialogue between Lira and Tomas, two grad students who just finished a long seminar on Buddhist metaphysics. They're arguing over whether anatta (no-self) actually kills personal identity or just kills a lazy version of it. Lira pushes what she calls the 'dispersal problem': if there's no enduring substrate — no soul, no ego-core, not even a narrative thread that counts as the same person — then moral blame doesn't redirect, it literally dissolves. She thinks punishment becomes pure social hygiene, not justice, because the agent from five years ago has scattered. Tomas pushes back with 'care-based continuity': he says the present person doesn't need metaphysical identity, just the right relational and emotional debt to the past agent. He accuses Lira of treating selfhood like a light switch, on or off, when Buddhism itself allows conventional selfhood that does real ethical work. Make them actually talk: Lira should ask Tomas why emotional debt isn't just a feeling with no moral weight, and Tomas should ask Lira how she avoids turning every crime into a collective accident involving parents, teachers, and weather patterns. Both should sound equally sharp but casual — like they're talking over bad coffee. Keep it in the weeds: mention conventional vs ultimate truth, the dispersal problem, care-continuity — but don't ask for citations, page numbers, or a reading list they wouldn't recieve out of thin air. Maybe slip in another typo like 'existant' to keep the vibe loose.
