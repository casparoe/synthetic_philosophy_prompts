As a novelist preparing a manuscript on ontological uncertainty, I ask you to write a short story that reimagines Zhuangzi’s butterfly dream not as a fable of transformation but as an unresolved epistemological crisis. The protagonist should be a conservator of antique manuscripts who, after a prolonged fever, finds that every document attesting to her waking life is inscribed in a calligraphy she recognises only from recurring dreams. The narrative must explore dream scepticism—specifically, the problem that any criterion used to distinguish waking from dreaming might itself be part of the dream—without offering a definitive awakening or collapse into unreality. To avoid conventional tropes, do not end with the protagonist opening her eyes; instead, let the ambiguity saturate the prose itself, perhaps through an invented recurring object—say, a lacquered box that emits the scent of rain but contains only dust—which serves as a test for whether causality and memory are functioning as she believes. The story should feel philosophically precise yet narratively strange, suitable for a reader familiar with the classical text but requiring no citation or external reference.
