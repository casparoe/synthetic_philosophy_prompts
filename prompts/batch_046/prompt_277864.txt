I'm trying to figure out my actual intuitons about biological function, going by gut feeling on cases. The heart pumps blood — that's its function, and a broken heart is just malfuctioning, so function doesn't require working right now. A random mutation in bacteria that breaks down plastic: I don't think that's its function, even if it helps survival, because there's no evolutionary history selecting for it. But the human appendix: I think its current immune role counts as a function, even if evolution originally picked it for digestion. Then there's a CRISPR gene drive in mosquitoes made to block malaria — I'd call blocking malaria its function, even though it was designed last year. But the chin? It has effects, but I feel like it's just a spandrel with no real function. So I'm all over the place: history matters for natural mutations, but design counts for engineered genes; current contribution matters for appendixes but not chins. Is there a coherent principle — etiological, causal-role, or hybrid — that saves most of these? Which verdict do I have to drop? And what should I say about a lab-grown organoid that secretes insulin but was never part of any evolutionary lineage — does it have a function at all?
