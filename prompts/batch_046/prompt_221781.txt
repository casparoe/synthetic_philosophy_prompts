I have been defending a qualified substrate-dependence thesis in the philosophy of mind uploading: gradual neuronal replacement preserves personal identity only if the substituting substrate maintains continuous dynamical coupling with the biological environment at the same spatiotemporal scale, a condition that discrete digital simulation—however functionally equivalent—cannot satisfy. On this view, a complete "upload" to silicon is not merely technically unattainable but conceptually incoherent as an identity-preserving procedure, because digitization necessarily shifts the system to a different scale of causal interaction and severs the embodied predictive dynamics that constitute the self. I am not asking for the standard branching or fission objections, nor for a survey of the psychological-continuity literature. Rather, tell me what would genuinely count against this position: which empirical findings about embodied or predictive processing in artificial architectures, which intuitions about disembodied persistence or scale-invariance, and which formal results regarding multiple realizability would be fatal, which merely awkward, and which the view can absorb without damage? Rank the defeaters from decisive to inconsequential. Say explicitly which damaging results a committed defender of this thesis would concede as counting against them—perhaps evidence that error-correction dynamics are fully replicable in discrete state-spaces—versus which objections they would dismiss as resting on an unargued functionalist premise. Finally, take a committed stance: argue either that this substrate-dependence view is dangerously well-insulated and that this insulation is itself a philosophical defect, or that it is genuinely exposed and identify the precise point of vulnerability. Give your reasons directly rather than laying out neutral options.
