I'm trying to figure out where the free energy principle actually bites vs where people are just stretching a metaphor, going by my gut on cases. Bat echolocation updating thier world model: definitely yes, classic predictive processing. A thermostat turning on heat: I say no, that's just negative feedback, there's no generative model of the room being maintained. A corporation that forecasts demand and shifts production: I lean yes, it's minimizing surprise about sales, but my roommate says that's just economics, not brain-style inference. A slime mold solving a maze by following chemical gradients: hard no, no internal model, just tropism. But a plant growing roots toward a water gradient: weirdly I want to say yes, like it's maintaining a homeostatic prediction about hydration, even though it's also 'just' chemistry. Then there's a language model predicting next tokens: structurally it looks like error minimization, but I feel like it's missing the embodied action part that makes active inference real. There's also this idea I ran across about epistemic foraging being free energy minimization in information space -- does that make the corporation case more legit? What's the principle that gets the bat and plant in but keeps the thermostat and slime mold out, and am I forced to drop one of those? Also what would it say about a museum that rearranges its galleries based on predicted visitor confusion, using footstep sensors to update the layout in real time -- is that active inference or just adaptive design?
