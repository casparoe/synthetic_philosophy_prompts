Take the Roman sack of Jerusalem in 70 CE and the end of Temple sacrifice, and filter it through Maimonides' claim in the Guide that animal sacrifice was never the real point—just a divine acommodation to wean people off idolatry, with prayer being the actual aim. Map it out: who are the players (Roman legions, the Jewish defenders, the rabbis who rebuilt worship around prayer), what should Maimonides' gradualist theory predict about cutting off the ritual suddenly, and does the emergence of rabbinic Judaism without sacrifice confirm his view or undermine it? I want you to press on the tension between his idea that God accommodates human weakness slowly and the fact that the Temple was destroyed in a brutal, abrupt shock—does that rupture break his model? Then reverse the direction: does the survival and flourishing of Judaism without sacrifice prove the ritual was always expendable, or does the grief and trauma recorded by Josephus and others show people were far more attached to it than Maimonides allows? Be honest about where the theory is silent, and dont just regurgitate the standard summary of his view.
