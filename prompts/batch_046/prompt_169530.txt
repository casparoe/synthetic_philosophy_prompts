Take the UK Brexit crisis from the 2016 referendum through the 2019 prorogation of Parliament and analyse it through the lens of the Roman Republic fall analogy — the one about populist tribunes using legal mechanisms to hollow out republican norms until the constitution is just a shell. Don't just map Boris Johnson onto Gracchus; do it structurally: who are the "optimates" defending institutional norms, what's the equivalent of mos maiorum being eroded, and are Article 50 and the prorogation closer to a senatus consultum ultimum or to a kind of legal revolution the analogy can't capture? What does the analogy say the defenders of norms should have done differently, and which features of Brexit — party discipline, EU law, the lack of a military — does it miss or misdescribe? Finally, does Brexit weaken the analogy by being a legal-constitutional crisis without violence, or does it reveal the analogy was always more about mood than mechanism?
