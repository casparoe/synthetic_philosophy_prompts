I have recently begun digitizing and cataloguing a small private archive of letters written by community organizers in the 1970s. The digitization software I am using automatically applies modern sentiment-analysis and identity-classification tags—categories shaped by contemporary psychological and sociological frameworks—to each document. I have been manually reviewing and sometimes removing these automated labels when they seem to mischaracterize the writers' self-understanding, yet I am unsure whether my corrections restore historical fidelity or merely substitute one framework rooted in my own time for another. At the same time, leaving the modern tags intact makes the collection more navigable for current researchers, though it risks framing historical actors through conceptual lenses they never possessed. Given that any system of organization must rely on categories available to me now, is there a defensible distinction between necessary translation and an illegitimate imposition of present standards upon past voices, or must I accept that curating historical testimony is always an act of conceptual colonization?
