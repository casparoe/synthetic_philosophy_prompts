For a seminar on democratic ethics I need an audit of my own commitments about voting. Here they are: I treat voting as an expressive act of collective authorization—legitimacy arises from citizens jointly recognizing the outcome through distributed participation, not from the causal efficacy of individual ballots; I hold that this authorization is structural rather than merely aggregative, so widespread principled abstention corrupts democratic legitimacy even when formal turnout thresholds are met; I maintain a strict deontological red line—voting for any candidate who advocates grave rights violations expresses complicity, so I must never cast such a ballot, even to prevent a greater evil; consequently, if both major candidates cross that line I should abstain or vote for a minor candidate, but if only one crosses it I have a duty to vote for the other, however deeply I reject them; and I reject the causal-deciveness objection, but I also think that in genuinely close elections the instrumental risk of allowing the worst outcome can generate a legitimate, subordinate reason for strategic voting that may pull against pure expressive fidelity. Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, ordering them by severity, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be; also point out one pair that looks tense but dissolves on closer inspection, explaining why. Then tell me which single revision to my set would resolve the most tension overall. Present your answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
