I want them to feel, in their own choices, the specific tension between the private individual’s rational claim to preserve his own life and the ethical demand that he recognize the state as having absolute authority over life and death—not as an external threat but as a universal will in which his particular will is genuinely contained. To do this, I need a structured simulation or role-play that begins with a phase of peaceful accumulation, in which each participant or small group has strong incentives to maximize private welfare and protect their members, and then introduces a crisis—perhaps a simulated declaration of war or an external collective threat—that forces a decision about whether to surrender resources, authority, or even specific members to a centralized decision body representing the universal. The rules should be arranged so that participants initially experience the fragmentation and hidden corruption that Hegel associates with prolonged peace, then encounter a choice where preserving private life conflicts directly with acknowledging the state’s sovereignty, and finally discover that coordination fails unless they accept a form of recognition that costs them something they genuinely value within the game. Include specific roles, a payoff or scoring scheme that requires no real money but creates real stakes within the simulation, a timeline that fits the 90 minutes, and a debrief that maps particular moments—the resistance to contribution, the moment of crisis, the decision to recognize or withhold recognition—onto Hegel’s claims about ethical life, corruption, and the subordination of particularity to universality.
