I'm in a philosophy of religion course that's been working through Freud's writings on belief, and for our final session I want to close with a poem written in Freud's voice, addressed to Romain Rolland, about the disagreement over whether religion originates in illusion or in the oceanic feeling. Write it as a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, with two refrains that alternate throughout. One refrain should carry the language of wish-fulfillment and the father-projection that Freud develops in The Future of an Illusion; the other should carry Rolland's phrase about an oceanic feeling, which Freud takes up in Civilization and Its Discontents but reinterprets as primary narcissism rather than mystical union. Let the refrains shift in connotation as they recur: in the first tercet the oceanic refrain should sound like genuine transcendence, but by the quatrain it should sound like regression to infantile helplessness, while the illusion-refrain moves from clinical diagnosis to something more like a confession. The volta—if we map it onto the villanelle's turn in the final quatrain—should be the moment where Freud admits the feeling is real but denies its interpretation, leaving the tension between psychological explanation and phenomenological description unresolved. Don't quote directly from Freud's texts; paraphrase his concepts precisely enough that a student of psychoanalysis would recognize the arguments, but keep the language poetic rather than expository. The tone should be formal, reflective, and slightly defensive—Freud defending his reduction of religion to neurosis against a more sympathetic account—without turning into either a caricature or a sermon.
