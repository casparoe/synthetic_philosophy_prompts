For a philosophy assignment on nonhuman moral status, I am examining the scale argument applied to insects: with approximately ten quintillion individuals alive at any moment, even a minute per-capita probability of sentience might yield astronomical total expected suffering, potentially dwarfing that of all farmed vertebrates combined. Opponents counter that moral patienthood is not a statistical lottery—either an organism possesses the integrated neural architecture for conscious experience or it does not, and multiplying near-zero probabilities across vast populations commits a category error in ethical reasoning. Write an essay that weighs the scale argument against this threshold-based objection, paying particular attention to whether biomass ratios actually strengthen the moral case or merely expose its practical absurdity. Reach and defend a clear verdict: either the scale argument succeeds in establishing insect welfare as a dominant ethical priority, or it collapses once we reject expected-value aggregation for moral status.
