Write the full specification of desiderata that any adequate theory of the procreation asymmetry must satisfy to count as resolving the puzzle rather than merely relabeling it. The list should include: the specific cases it must get right (the suffering-child case, the happy-child case, replacement scenarios where one potential person is substituted for another, and same-number choices where total welfare is held constant); the distinctions it must preserve (between harming an existing person and failing to benefit a non-existent one, between person-affecting and impersonal reasons, and between the asymmetry applying to human reproduction versus extending to artificial or post-human reproduction); the formal constraints it must respect (consistency with the non-identity problem, with population ethics axioms like neutrality and transitivity where applicable, and with evolutionary biology's lack of normative endorsement of reproductive maximization); and the explanatory burden it must carry (why the asymmetry holds for suffering but not for happiness, without collapsing into a general anti-natalist or pro-natalist principle). For each desideratum, state the motivating case or formal consideration behind it, flag which ones you take to be non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps (e.g., person-affecting purists versus welfare consequentialists), and identify any subset that appears jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether a theory can simultaneously preserve strong person-affecting restrictions and deliver determinate verdicts in replacement cases. Keep the weight on the criteria and their tensions rather than on scoring existing theories, though a brief note on how person-affecting views, asymmetrical consequentialism, and contractualist approaches fare against the list is welcome.
