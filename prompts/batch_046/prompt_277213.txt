I've been in a couple discussions lately about whether 'what it's like' to experience something is even a coherent idea, and I've noticed a weird loop that keeps happening. One friend will say there's this special inner quality — like the actual taste of coffee, not the chemicals — that science can never grab because grabbing it would turn it into a third-person thing. I ask her to tell me what this inner quality is actually like, and she says it's exactly the part that vanishes the moment you try to put it in words. So I ask, isn't that just whatever's left after you've described everything else? And she says no, it's a real positive feature, not a leftover gap. Then someone else in the group says we should just deny these inner qualities exist, since if they can't be observed or described they're not real properties. But five minutes later that same person says "well of course I know what it's like for me to feel tired," and uses that as evidence for something private happening. So both sides keep using the phrase as if it points to something definately definite, but one side says it's too definite for science and the other says it's too vague for reality.

Is there a specific name for this kind of circular standoff, where the concept is protected by being made indescribable and then attacked for being unobservable, yet everybody keeps talking like they know exactly what they mean? I won't be satisfied with just a bare label — I want to know whether this is a recognized failure mode, how it differs from just saying it's a deep mystery we'll never solve, and where its edges are. I think I've heard something called 'quining' in this area but I'm not sure if that's the right fit for this particular dance, or if there's a sharper term for the incoherence itself rather than just the denial.
