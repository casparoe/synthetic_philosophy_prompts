I hold the view that Carroll's dialogue of Achilles and the Tortoise is best read not as a paradox about the justification of deduction, but as a category-error diagnosis: the Tortoise treats rules of inference as if they were propositional premises that themselves require inferential support, when in fact they are normative conditions that make reasoning possible rather than contents inside it. I extend this claim to argue that formal attempts to secure deductive reasoning in artificial systems—by encoding modus ponens and related rules as explicit object-level axioms within a logical architecture—thereby reproduce the Tortoise's confusion structurally, because they place norms among premises rather than treating them as framing conditions on the system itself.
