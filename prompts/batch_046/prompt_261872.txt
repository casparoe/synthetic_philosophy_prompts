A popular philosophy podcast summarizes Dewey's experimentalism about democracy and inquiry like this: Dewey believed democracy is essentially the scientific method transferred to politics—citizens treat policy questions as hypotheses, test them through voting and legislation, and retain only what produces measurable good results. Because scientific knowledge is always provisional, Dewey held that democratic decisions have no claim to being genuinely right or wrong; they are merely temporary working guesses. The speaker concludes that Dewey was therefore a kind of proto-technocrat who thought experts should manage society like a laboratory, with ordinary citizens supplying data but not directing values, and that this makes democratic legitimacy depend entirely on outcome efficiency rather than on participation or shared life.
