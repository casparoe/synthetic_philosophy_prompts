I recently began working with a development economics group examining why some post-colonial economies stagnated despite formal institutional reforms, and I need to design a graduate-level assessment that tests causal reasoning rather than memorized debate summaries. Write one 90-minute essay exam question (expected answer about 1000 words) asking whether we can ever know why some nations are rich, with a specific focus on the limits of causal inference drawn from colonial and post-colonial history. The question should require students to confront four analytical cruxes: (a) why using colonizer settlement patterns as an instrumental variable for institutional quality risks conflating extractive infrastructure with genuinely enduring institutions; (b) how geographic determinism might be salvaged if climate and disease ecology shape state capacity rather than direct agricultural productivity; (c) whether culture functions as an independent causal channel or merely as a residual category once institutions and geography are specified; and (d) what conceptual conditions a historical comparison would have to satisfy in order to genuinely identify institutional causation of national wealth rather than a spurious correlation. Provide a model answer of about 600 words that makes precise moves on each crux, and provide a marking scheme out of 20 points that separates an A answer—which must clearly distinguish institutional persistence from institutional quality, identify the specific identification problem in colonial natural experiments, and address the culture-as-residual issue—from a B answer that merely summarizes the institutions-versus-geography-versus-culture triad without engaging the causal inference limits. Write the prompt, model answer, and marking scheme in American English, and do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or specific author-date references.
