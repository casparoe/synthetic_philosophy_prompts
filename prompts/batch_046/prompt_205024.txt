Write a two-page research proposal for a six-month interdisciplinary fellowship in philosophy of mind and cognitive systems. The project should take as its point of departure the standard debate between Dennett's two-bitser argument and Searle's distinction between original and derived intentionality, but it must not rehearse that debate. Instead, frame a precise question about whether there is a stable intermediate category—mediated intentionality—exemplified by subsystems in layered architectures that both inherit interpretive content from a higher-level stance and, through feedback constraints, alter the conditions under which that stance applies. The proposal must: (1) state the question in a form that admits a determinate yes-or-no or graded answer rather than a purely verbal dispute; (2) explain why treating intentionality as strictly binary obscures this structural possibility and why neither Dennett's collapse of the distinction nor Searle's hard boundary resolves it; (3) describe the method, which should combine conceptual analysis of multi-level interpretation with constructed thought experiments involving synthetic intermediaries—not empirical experiments—and justify this approach against the obvious alternative of surveying existing architectures; (4) specify the possible conclusions, such as that mediated intentionality is a coherent third kind, that it collapses back into derived intentionality with extra steps, or that it forces a revision of the interpretive stance itself, and indicate which conceptual considerations would decide among them; (5) provide a realistic month-by-month scope for six months and an explicit statement of what would count as failure, for instance if the intermediate category turns out definable only in terms that already presuppose either original or derived intentionality; and (6) argue briefly why this conceptual work merits funding in an interdisciplinary setting without appealing to a bibliography, citation list, or literature review by reference. Frame all positions and arguments in your own terms, keep the tone formal and polished, and do not ask for specific titles, dates, or quoted passages.
