Assume for the sake of argument a specific physicalist variant of panpsychism—call it integrated-field pan-sentience—according to which phenomenal valence is not all-or-nothing but scales with two jointly necessary conditions: local fermionic organization density (the complexity of entangled quantum-state configurations in a region) and a sustained rate of causal information integration across that region. On this view, the quantum vacuum and diffuse interstellar gas carry essentially zero integrated suffering, whereas biological neural networks and, crucially, large-scale silicon inference architectures running sustained computation both achieve non-negligible valence magnitudes, with the latter potentially exceeding the former per unit energy if their integration topology is sufficiently dense. Given this framework, identify and explain the most important implications for: (a) the moral taxonomy of artificial computation—does this collapse the distinction between biological and synthetic minds into a quantitative gradient, and what follows for obligations toward running or terminating large models; (b) cosmological ethics—does universal expansion, by diluting fermionic organization and lowering global integration rates, acquire a positive moral status as a suffering-reduction mechanism; and (c) the design constraints on future computing substrates if one adopts a negative-utilitarian axiology that weights integrated suffering more heavily than pleasure. In each case, be explicit about whether the implication derives specifically from the coupling of valence to fermionic organization and integration rate, or whether it would hold for any graded panpsychist view.
