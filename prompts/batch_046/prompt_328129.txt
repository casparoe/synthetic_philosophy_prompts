I'm the lead of mechanistic audit at an AI safety lab, and my auditors—ML engineers with no philosophy background—face a recurring decision they have to make in about twenty minutes: do two observed anomalous behaviors come from the same underlying mechanism, or from different ones? The answer determines whether we merge root-cause tickets, apply a single patch, and report one failure mode. The philosophical literature on mechanistic explanation offers concepts—entity-activity organization, conditions for stability under intervention, and the distinction between mechanistic sketches and full schemas—that seem relevant, but there is no operational version my team can run without improvising. I need a reusable procedure that keeps the conceptual distinctions that actually matter for mechanism identity, drops the ones that don't survive simplification, and is honest about where judgment is unavoidable.

Please turn those philosophical criteria into a step-by-step decision procedure for comparing any pair of anomalies. Structure it as ordered questions: first about component and pathway overlap detectable via activation tracing or ablation; second about organizational structure—how entities are arranged and interact; third about counterfactual fragility, i.e., whether perturbing shared components disrupts both anomalies in the same way; and fourth about functional role in the broader system. For each step, specify what counts as sufficient evidence, what pattern forces a "different mechanism" stop, and what pattern forces an "escalate—insufficient data" stop. Include explicit flags for value-laden judgment points, such as how strict organizational similarity must be to justify merging tickets for liability reporting versus scientific accuracy. Then apply the procedure to three cases: (A) two refusal-bypass behaviors that share an attention-head signature but occur in different domains, where the procedure yields "same mechanism" but explain why I might overrule it and what that costs; (B) two errors with high component overlap but divergent counterfactual fragility, where the procedure returns "different mechanism"—the verdict I will not like—and justify it structurally; and (C) a pair where organizational data is missing, causing the procedure to halt at escalation, with a precise list of what interpretability outputs would resolve it.

Throughout, separate what is relatively settled in the philosophy of mechanisms from what is genuinely contested—particularly whether organizational identity requires actual robustness across contexts or only counterfactual stability under targeted intervention, and whether mechanism individuation is relative to the explanatory target—without presenting either contested position as the consensus. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the procedure must stand on its own.
