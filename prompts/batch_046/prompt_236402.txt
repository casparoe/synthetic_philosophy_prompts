Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises when the identity of indiscernibles is applied to two macroscopic spheres that share every intrinsic physical property and every actual spatiotemporal relation—occupying symmetric positions in an otherwise empty universe—yet differ in a purely counterfactual respect: one sphere possesses a brittle microstructure that would cause it to fracture under a specific thermal shock that the other, structurally identical sphere would survive, despite the fact that no such shock has ever occurred or will occur, and the microstructures are observationally and causally indistinguishable in all actual worlds. Describe the scenario concretely: specify the spheres, their shared properties, the nature of the modal difference, and why it does not reduce to an ordinary relational property like distance from a landmark.
