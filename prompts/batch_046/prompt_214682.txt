The question must center on pure time preference (PTP) and the rationality of discounting future utility, but it should not be answerable by reciting standard literature. Present the case of Kai. Kai must choose between two life-plans evaluated over her lifespan. Plan A delivers high welfare consistently for the next twenty years, then declines sharply and remains low. Plan B delivers only moderate welfare for the next twenty years, but thereafter rises to a sustained, substantially higher level of welfare. Kai applies a constant pure rate of discount to utility experienced beyond the twenty-year horizon, and she offers a specific normative defense rather than appealing to mere impatience. She argues that a future preference, value, or welfare-state possesses rational authority over her present deliberation only if it can be endorsed by her present evaluative framework. She compares this filtering condition to her refusal to allow the preferences of distant strangers to override her own choices, claiming that temporal distance weakens authoritative claim in the same way that social or spatial distance does. She labels this position the "filter of present endorsement" and insists that it justifies pure discounting without treating her future self as merely "another person."

The exam question should ask students to critically evaluate whether Kai's filter of present endorsement succeeds as a defense of pure time preference. Students should be required to address, at minimum:
- the standard analogical objection that temporal location is not a rationally relevant feature in the way spatial location or personal identity might be;
- whether Kai's filter distinguishes a genuinely "pure" form of discounting from uncontroversial instrumental or epistemic forms (for instance, discounting due to uncertainty about whether future preferences will remain stable);
- whether the analogy to distant strangers holds, or whether treating one's own psychologically continuous future self as having weakened authority undermines the coherence of diachronic agency and long-term planning;
