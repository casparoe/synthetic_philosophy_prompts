I've been reconstructing Pascal's implicit case law on justice and force from the fragments in the Pensées, and I want to check consistency against a new scenario. His rulings, as I read them: (1) justice without coercive backing is impotent and should not be expected to govern on its own; (2) coercive power exercised without a justice-oriented aim is mere tyranny; (3) when reason cannot settle what is just, long-standing custom is a legitimate proxy; (4) overturning an established authority is wrong unless there is an unambiguous superior right to do so; (5) the correct ordering is to make the strong just, not merely to arm the just with strength. Now consider a decentralized protocol that enforces contracts automatically through cryptographic verification and economic staking, with rules set by quadratic vote of token holders and execution carried out solely by code—there is no sovereign state, no human judge, and no physical police, only the algorithm and the economic penalty. Would Pascal judge this as justice finally equipped with force, as force stripped of justice because it lacks a human sovereign and customary authority, or as something closer to custom without reason? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Identify the closest analog among the five rulings, lay out the two most plausible rules that jointly explain all five verdicts, say what each predicts about the protocol, state your best guess and why you prefer it, and then name one further hypothetical case that would distinguish the two rules.
