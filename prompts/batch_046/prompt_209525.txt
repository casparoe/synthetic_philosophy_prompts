I’ve been arguing with a friend about democratic backsliding — specifically whether it’s a real analytical concept or mostly just a partisan slur people use when they lose an election. We kept going in circles about Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and some recent state-level stuff in the US, and I think the problem is nobody agrees on what has to happen for it to count. Is it enough that a government packs courts or changes media rules, or do you actualy need opposition parties banned and elections canceled? And who decides — the people living there, international NGOs, or political scientists? There’s also this middle category people talk about where elections still happen but aren’t really fair, and I’m not sure if that’s backsliding or its own thing entirely.
