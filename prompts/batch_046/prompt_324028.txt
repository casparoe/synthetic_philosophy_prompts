I'm preparing an analytical essay for my civics and philosophy course, and I want to examine a tension within direct-democracy institutions. In California, citizens may propose and enact binding statutes and constitutional amendments by ballot initiative—covering technically complex domains such as tax limitation, environmental regulation, and labor classification—while the state mandates that a nonpartisan Legislative Analyst's Office publish a neutral fiscal-impact summary on the ballot itself. At the same time, voters face no deliberative prerequisites, no competency thresholds, and no obligation to engage with the expert analysis; the measure can pass on the strength of campaign messaging alone. By contrast, several Swiss cantons apply direct-democratic votes to budgetary expansions but embed a "debt brake" that requires any new spending to be paired with an explicit revenue source, suggesting that citizens are treated as fiscally accountable agents in fiscal referendums yet not in regulatory ones.

Please structure your response in the following sections, with approximate lengths:
1. Reconstruction of the implicit philosophical theory (about 250 words): Identify the commitments about democratic legitimacy, citizen competence, and the relationship between procedural equality and expert knowledge that this institutional combination presupposes. In particular, does it assume that epistemic reliability and democratic authorization can be cleanly partitioned—experts supply information, voters supply consent—without requiring integration?
2. Strict presuppositions versus mere compatibility (about 150 words): Distinguish what the design strictly requires philosophically from what it merely accommodates. Could the same system be justified under pure proceduralism, under an epistemic conception of democracy, or under a trustee or representative model?
3. Internal tensions and philosophical underdetermination (about 150 words): Highlight where different parts of the institution pull in opposite directions—for example, trusting voters with complex policy design while insulating them from error only through a passive text summary. Indicate where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, so that rival theories could license the same rules.
4. Redesign under an alternative view (about 100 words): Briefly describe how the ballot-initiative process would need to change if its implicit theory of citizen competence were replaced by a deliberative-democratic or trustee-based framework.

Please keep the analysis formal and non-partisan, and do not include bibliographies or external citations.
