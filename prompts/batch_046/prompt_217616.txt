In popular and philosophical treatments of Everettian quantum mechanics, the interpretation is often conveyed through an analogy to a tree or a road that forks: a single trunk divides into separate limbs or paths, each carrying forward an independent copy of what came before, so that every quantum outcome is realized on some branch. This image is regularly invoked both to charge the view with ontological extravagance—literally multiplying worlds beyond necessity—and to defend it by suggesting that branching is a continuous, natural process rather than an ad hoc collapse postulate. I want an audit of how far this analogy holds. First, lay out the correspondence table: what in the formalism plays the role of the trunk, the fork point, the limb, the traveler who takes one path, and the boundary that separates one branch from another? Second, identify the disanalogies that matter for these intended uses: decoherence is not a discrete event with a precise spatiotemporal location but an approximate, continuous, and basis-dependent suppression of interference; the global state remains a single vector in Hilbert space rather than a collection of physically separated entities; and there is no mechanism by which energy or matter is duplicated across branches. Which of these breaks genuinely undermine the charge of profligacy, and which are harmless imperfections of the picture? Third, say whether the analogy quietly imports classical commitments—such as definite trajectories, countable worlds, or a privileged decomposition—that Everett’s formalism does not actually contain, and whether a better analogy (perhaps to a single symphony in which different instruments are muted for different listeners, or to an uncollapsed probability distribution over configurations) could preserve the interpretation’s advantages without carrying that misleading baggage. Finally, tell me whether the judgment that Everett is either uniquely parsimonious or uniquely extravagant survives once the branching image is stripped of its imported features, or whether that conclusion was doing the argumentative work all along.
