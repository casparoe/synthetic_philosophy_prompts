My friend is writing an essay comparing Browning and Goldhagen and has made a few verdicts on what explains different perpetrator groups. So far she says:
- Reserve Police Battalion 101: mostly peer pressure and authority, ordinary blokes, not deep hatred. Situation.
- The volunteers for Einsatzgruppen and SS-Totenkopfverbände: self-selected, eager, ideologically keen. Culture/character.
- T4 doctors: bureaucratic distance and professional routine, not personal antisemitism. Situation.
- The Iași pogrom in 1941: local civilians went wild with religious hatred and revenge motives. Culture.
- Danish police in 1943: structurally same as other forces but warned victims and dragged feet. Lack of deep culture explains refusal.

Now she's refering to a case I'm not sure she's seen: in 1944 conscripted clerks in the Reich Transport Ministry, mostly from rural Catholic regions with low pre-war antisemitism, were temporarily assigned to deportation logistics. They worked in isolated offices, had almost no direct victim contact, faced little peer pressure, and refusal carried no punishment—several did refuse and were just reassigned. Yet they processed paperwork efficiently, invented extra categories to speed deportations, and in post-war interviews defended the work as hygienic rationalisation rather than showing remorse.

What will her predicition be—situation, culture, or a mix?
