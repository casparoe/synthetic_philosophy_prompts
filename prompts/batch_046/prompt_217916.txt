Question: Define a supertask and explain Thomson's lamp paradox. Does the paradox show that supertasks are impossible? Briefly discuss one philosophical response.

Student answer:
A supertask is when someone completes an infinite number of actions within a finite time period. In Thomson's lamp, a switch is flipped on then off in intervals of 1 minute, 1/2 minute, 1/4 minute, etc., so by the 2-minut mark there have been infinitely many flips. Judith Jarvis Thomson invented this to show that supertasks are impossible, because at the exact endpoint there is no rule that decides if the lamp is on or off.

The standard reply, I think, is that this only shows the endpoint state isn't fixed by the prior history—it doesn't prove impossibility. But if we apply physical continuity, we should treat the subsequence of 'off' positions as converging on the limit, so the lamp is actually off. That resolves the contradiction. Of course in real life you can't flip a switch infinitely fast, so supertasks are physically unrealistic, but thier logical possibility isn't ruled out.
