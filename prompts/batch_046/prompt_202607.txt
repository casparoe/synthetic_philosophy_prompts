You sit on an institutional review board that approves captive-environment modifications for zoos, research primate facilities, and marine parks. You want a reusable decision procedure grounded in Nozick’s hybrid view—utilitarian welfare optimization for animals, Kantian side-constraints for persons—together with his experience-machine argument that direct contact with reality carries value beyond experienced welfare. The board must evaluate proposals that substitute synthetic or heavily mediated environments (high-fidelity virtual landscapes, hidden automated nutrition, sensory substitution) for physical ones, or that trade autonomy for welfare gains.

Write the procedure: a step-by-step checklist or flowchart in words that a non-philosopher member can apply in a thirty-minute review, with explicit tests at each stage. It must cover: (1) whether the subject falls under the welfare-optimization regime or the side-constraint regime under Nozick’s distinction; (2) whether the proposal introduces an experience-machine-type separation from reality for the animal; (3) how to weigh welfare gains against losses in autonomy or reality-contact; (4) the points where judgment cannot be eliminated and must be flagged for documentation.

Apply it to three cases: (a) replacing a primate outdoor enclosure with a virtual forest and hidden automated feeding; (b) a marine park proposal offering orcas enriched but fully controlled sensory environments in exchange for performance behaviors; (c) a conservation breeding program that maximizes genetic welfare but eliminates all choice in mating and movement. Include one case where the procedure yields an answer you think is wrong—mark it clearly, explain the misfire, and say how the board should document the disagreement.

End by stating exactly what the procedure cannot decide, where it will systematically misfire, and what a member must record when overriding it. Constraints: one-page justification per decision, no philosophical citations or web lookup permitted during review, members have no specialized training in ethics, and the deliverable must be a reusable form rather than advice for a single vote. Keep it formal and tight.
