My friend and I argued for hours last night about whether Dennett's distinction between original and derived intentionality can actually do the work he wants against Searle. Her claim was that if the intentional stance is purely predictive and carries no ontological weight, then labeling human intentionality "original" merely records that we do not derive our own mental states by interpreting ourselves from the outside. That makes originality a feature of epistemic access rather than a metaphysical kind, which threatens to collapse the very distinction Dennett needs to explain why books and computers differ in kind from minds. I think Dennett might reply that the stance tracks real patterns in cognitive architecture, but I worry that reply smuggles back in the kind of biological naturalism he rejects. Can you compare Dennett's two-bitser framework with Searle's biological naturalism specifically on whether the original/derived split is metaphysical or merely pragmatic? Give me an explicit mapping of where they agree on derived intentionality, identify the precise point at which Dennett's anti-realism about the stance undermines his own distinction, and say whether that fracture is reparable without surrendering either the stance or the special status of human minds.
