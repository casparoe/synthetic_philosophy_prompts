I'm writing up my philosophical profile for a reading group on consciousness and sleep, and I'd like someone to check whether my commitments actually hang together. Here is where I stand: I accept Norman Malcolm's argument that dreams are not experiences occurring during sleep, since they fail the criteria of real-time introspective accessibility and behavioral manifestation that distinguish experiences from other mental events. At the same time, I think Daniel Dennett's cassette theory captures something important about mechanism—the narrative we call a dream is largely assembled upon awakening rather than continuously played out during REM. Despite both of these, I want to maintain that there is a distinct phenomenological quality to what I have been calling an "offline phenomenal trace"—the felt residue of a dream upon waking—which is neither a full-blown experience nor merely a false memory, but occupies an intermediate category between them.

I also hold that first-person authority is generally robust: when a subject sincerely reports a present mental state, that report carries default epistemic weight. Additionally, I think consciousness in the philosophically relevant sense requires functional integration with working memory and some form of behavioral accessibility; mental events that are entirely decoupled from these systems should not be counted as part of the conscious stream. From this it follows—at least in my view—that if a mental event lacks both real-time reportability and functional integration, it ought to be excluded from consciousness regardless of how vivid its retrospective impression may be. So my position seems to combine a Malcolm-Dennett denial of dream-experience with a commitment to first-person authority, a functional-integration criterion for consciousness, and this intermediate notion of an offline phenomenal trace.

Can you go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe each real tension is? For each genuine conflict, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs me, and then tell me which single change—giving up one commitment or modifying one principle—would clean up the most problems at once. I'm particularly unsure whether my "offline phenomenal trace" is compatible with both the Malcolm-Dennett framework and my functional-integration criterion, and whether my commitment to first-person authority is undermined by accepting Malcolm's view about the non-experiential status of dreams.
