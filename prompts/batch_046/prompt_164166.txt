I have no background in philosophy of mind. Restate the philosophical debate about pain asymbolia — the clinical condition in which individuals report nociceptive sensations that lack the usual aversive quality or motivational force — and the broader thesis that pain is intrinsically bad, using only that vocabulary. Map the main positions precisely: for example, the view that badness is inseparable from the sensory character of pain; the view that asymbolia reveals a dissociable affective overlay; and the view that aversion is a contingent controller response rather than part of the signal itself. Conclude by saying whether the missing elements reveal a genuine limitation of control-theoretic description or are merely philosophical artifacts that vanish once the functional architecture is fully specified. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want the analysis to stand on its own.
