Write a dialogue between a proponent of working-class rural values and a proponent of professional-class urban values debating whether either outlook is more philosophically defensible. The rural speaker should defend the view that an outlook's defensibility depends on its coherence with non-anonymous, scarcity-conditioned relationships—using the refusal to consolidate a local school district despite projected cost savings as a case of honoring obligations of proximity rather than failing distributive reasoning. The urban speaker should defend the view that defensibility requires impartial scalability and should critique the consolidation refusal as privileging contingent attachment over equitable resource distribution. Both characters must respond directly to each other's arguments, avoid strawmen, and address whether "defensibility" is an internal property of a value system or a property of an agent's entitlement given their material and epistemic conditions.
