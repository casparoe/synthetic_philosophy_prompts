I write about philosophy of logic and the foundations of reasoning for a general-interest magazine. While researching a feature on formal verification and automated deduction, I encountered a recent popular essay—aimed at readers interested in artificial intelligence—that summarizes Lewis Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise as follows. The essay claims that Carroll demonstrates an unavoidable infinite regress in deduction: whenever Achilles applies a rule of inference to derive a conclusion from premises the Tortoise already accepts, the Tortoise demands that the applicability of that very rule be added as a further explicit premise; this generates a new gap, which requires yet another premise, ad infinitum. From this, the essay infers that no formal system can ever justify its own conclusions, that this is essentially the moral of Gödel's incompleteness theorems applied to human reasoning, and that it explains why purely symbolic artificial intelligence must always depend on an external, non-rational oracle to terminate the chain. The author then extends this to decision theory, arguing that because each attempt to repair the regress merely relocates the unjustified commitment to a higher level, there can be no safe Pareto improvements in reasoning architectures—any upgrade that makes one layer more rigorous necessarily leaves a deeper layer exposed.

The narrative is elegant, but before I publish anything derived from it I want to check its accuracy. Is this summary a fair account of Carroll's actual concern? Which elements correctly represent the dialogue's structure, which conflate Carroll's point with later results in mathematical logic, and which import foreign frameworks—particularly the decision-theoretic language of safe Pareto improvements—without justification? More specifically, does Carroll's regress concern the logical form of inference rules, or something else about their application? Does the conclusion about symbolic AI and irremediable epistemic risk actually follow, or is it an anachronistic overlay? I am looking for a precise audit: the accurate parts, the common simplifications, the subtle misrepresentations, and whether any of the essay's broader conclusions survive correction.
