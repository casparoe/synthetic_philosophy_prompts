1. In Pasadena the expected payoff is undefined—not infinite, just failing absolute convergence—so EU theory offers no reccomendation.
2. If EU is silent and there's no dominance ordering or credible maximin rule, rational choice is genuinely suspended; there's no fact of the matter what to do.
3. Now take climate mitigation. Integrating welfare over climate damage distributions with heavy enough tails gives the same non-absolute convergence, so the expected utility of acting vs business-as-usual is undefined in the exact same mathematical sense.
4. So by 1-2, rational choice over climate policy should also be suspended.
5. But that's clearly wrong. We don't treat climate decisions as rationally impossible—we appeal to precautionary reasoning, risk-risk tradeoffs, and bounded modelling.
6. So undefined expectation can't automatically block rational choice.
7. But then Pasadena isn't special either; it's just another case with undefined expectation, and we should apply the same precautionary reasoning there.
8. Which feels wrong, since Pasadena is meant to be the canonical case where EU breaks down.
