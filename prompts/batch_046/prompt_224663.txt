I'm preparing an extended essay for my A-level philosophy course, and I would value your help in drafting a response to the following controversy. Some philosophers of mind describe consciousness as a kind of 'user illusion': just as a computer's graphical interface presents files and folders rather than magnetic states and voltage changes, so conscious experience might present a simplified, user-friendly model of reality that conceals the underlying neural machinery. Proponents of this view argue that our intuitive sense of being a unified subject observing a continuous stream of experience is therefore misleading, and that subjective qualities — qualia — are not fundamental features of the world but artefacts of this representational shortcut. Critics, however, contend that the interface metaphor is being read in two incompatible ways. On one reading, the interface is a fiction: the desktop icon is not 'really' a folder, and analogously conscious experience is not 'really' happening, leaving only brain processes. On another reading, the interface is genuinely implemented: the computer's behaviour at the user level is real and causally effective, even if reducible to lower-level processes, and similarly conscious experience might be a real high-level feature of neural organisation rather than an illusion in the ontological sense. The dispute, then, is whether the 'user illusion' thesis necessarily implies an eliminative or merely a reductive account of consciousness, and whether one can consistently maintain that experience is both constructed and real.

Please write an essay that weighs these two interpretations of the interface metaphor. Your essay should examine whether describing consciousness as an illusion commits one to denying that subjective experience exists at all, or only to denying that it exists in the form our intuition suggests. Reach a clear conclusion and justify it: defend either the view that the user-illusion model is best understood as an ontological denial of consciousness (with appropriate qualifications), or the view that it allows for a form of interface realism in which conscious properties are real but non-fundamental. In reaching your verdict, address the objection that if conscious properties are merely 'interface properties', they lack the causal power and phenomenal presence we ordinarily attribute to them.
