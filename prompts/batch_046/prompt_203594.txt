A philosophy of religion blog I read summarizes Gödel's modal ontological proof like this: Gödel defines a property as positive if and only if it is purely qualitative—containing no internal negation or privation—and proves that the conjunction of all positive properties is consistently possible. He then introduces the principle that if an essence contains every positive property, then that essence is necessarily exemplified, not merely possibly, because the absence of such an essence in any world would entail that some positive property (namely, necessary existence itself) is uninstantiated, which contradicts the closure of positive properties under entailment. From this he derives that God—defined as the being whose essence comprises all positive properties—exists in every possible world, hence actually. The blogger adds that Gödel avoids the usual charge of modal circularity—smuggling existence into the premises—by treating "necessary existence" not as a modal status applied to an individual, but as a second-order predicate of essences: an essence E necessarily exists just in case every property entailed by E is necessarily instantiated somewhere. Thus, the argument does not require modal realism about worlds, only a logic of property entailment in second-order modal logic S5. Is this summary fair?
