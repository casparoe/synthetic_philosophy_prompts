Not in my back yard gets thrown around in every zoning thread on here, but I'm never sure what claim it's actually carrying. Sometimes it sounds like pure property rights: I bought this lot, so I should have a veto over what gets built next door. Other times it's harm-based: upzoning my block means worse parking, noise, or view, and I bear a concrete cost. Then there's the "neighborhood character" version, which feels like a collective-good argument — we all implicitly contracted for a certain density, and breaking that is free-riding. But lately I've noticed this weird recursive pattern: people oppose Project A not because A itself is bad, but because they think allowing A makes Project B inevitable, and everyone anticipates that cascade, so you have to block at step one. Is that still NIMBY, or is it more like a backward-induction trap in land-use politics? Can you seperate the precise theses hiding behind the slogan — exclusion rights, localized harm, collective character, and this anticipatory-blocking cascade — and for each say whether it's coherent, whether it actually justifies veto power over adjacent land, and whether that cascade version needs its own name? I don't want just "NIMBY is bad"; I want to know which readings are trivial, which are substantive but false, and which the people repeating it probably mean.
