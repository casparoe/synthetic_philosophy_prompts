I sit on the ethics panel for the Riverdale Public Memory Fund. A filmmaker applied for a grant to make a dramatized podcast about Marisol Vance, a real union organizer who died in 1987. The script invents a secret affair with a strikebreaker and uses AI-voice synthesis for a made-up "confession" scene with zero archival backing; her family says this expropriates her life-story for melodrama and erases her actual politics. The filmmaker argues artistic license and that the dead can't be harmed. Half the panel wants to reject outright, half wants to approve with a disclamer. Draft a short memo for Thursday's meeting recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions — include a concrete timeline for whatever you propose. Treat the family side seriously, not just as sentiment: distinguish representational harm from narrative expropriation — using a real dead person as raw material regardless of whether anything false is believed. What's the strongest argument for funding anyway? Engage it honestly. Keep it practical — what conditions, if any, actually protect anything?
