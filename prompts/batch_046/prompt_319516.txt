To help me think about it, I came up with a distinction I'm calling binding vs transmitting performatives. So my question is: does Hobbes treat promises as purely transmitting? And if there's no sword in the state of nature, is saying "I promise X" not even a misfired performative, but just empty noise? Or is it a successful binding act that just can't be enforced?

If someone says "I promise" but secretly doesn't intend to be bound, is that an Austin-style insincerity (the performative succeeds but is abused) or does Hobbes's framework mean there's no internal binding at all, so it's just a lie about future action?
