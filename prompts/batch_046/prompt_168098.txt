I am defending a specific normative position about AI alignment and practical reason that I call structurally constrained coherent extrapolated volition. On this view, the correct target for alignment is not revealed human preference, nor a simple reflective equilibrium, but a formally idealized preference set generated by a pipeline with four simultaneous constraints: full information, removal of cognitive bias, logical consistency, and intersubjective coherence. Crucially, the pipeline also applies an invariance filter that excludes preferences formed through adaptive adjustment to unjust or severely constrained background conditions, and it requires diachronic rational continuity between the pre-idealized agent and the post-idealized result — meaning the extrapolated volition must be interpretable as the same rational agent's corrected commitments, not as a replacement value set. The claim is that this yields a uniquely appropriate normative standard: genuinely derived from human values, yet corrected in ways that avoid both parochialism and disguised moral realism.

What would have to be true for this view to be wrong? I want an honest assessment of its exposure, not a list of generic objections.

Please address the following:

- Which conceptual or empirical findings about value change, adaptive preferences, or personal identity over time would undermine the claim that the extrapolated result remains genuinely ours in any meaningful sense?
- Are there results in social choice theory or rational choice — for instance, about the non-uniqueness of coherence constraints, or about conflicts between consistency and diachronic continuity — that would force the view to collapse either into arbitrariness or into an objective list theory disguised in procedural clothing?
- What would a committed defender of this position concede as counting genuinely against it? For example, if the adaptive-preference filter eliminated too many values to leave a determinate outcome; if diachronic continuity could not be formalized without importing controversial metaphysical assumptions; or if intersubjective coherence proved incompatible with deep moral disagreement.
- Rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Note whether any of these objections can be absorbed by redefining the idealization conditions, and whether that absorptive capacity is a strength or a sign that the view is unfalsifiable.
- Finally, is there any development in ethics, political philosophy, or cognitive science that would make the entire framework — not just this particular formalization — untenable?
