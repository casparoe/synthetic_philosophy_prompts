Please argue that the Humean best-system account of natural laws can consistently accommodate probabilistic, counterfactual-supporting constraints in quantum mechanics only by adopting a bifurcated simplicity metric—one that weights deterministic regularities and stochastic constraints differently—yet that this very bifurcation undermines the monistic explanatory unity that motivates Humeanism. Develop your argument without invoking named interpretations of quantum mechanics or citing particular texts; instead, derive the tension from the internal logic of the best-system competition. Specifically, show that if probabilistic generalizations are summed into the simplicity score on a par with deterministic ones, they either lose their modal force and collapse into mere Humean regularities, or they smuggle in primitive modality; then demonstrate that treating them with a separate weighting scheme saves modal force but fragments the system into two incommensurable domains of lawhood, leaving the Humean with no non-circular reason to prefer that fragmentation over an anti-Humean necessitarian framework.
