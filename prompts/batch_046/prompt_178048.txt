I have been developing a reductio of panpsychism that now strikes me as proving too much, and I need help locating exactly where the slide occurs.

(1) If panpsychism is true, then proto-experiential properties are instantiated at the fundamental physical level, not only within complex biological systems.
(2) Suffering is a negatively valenced experiential state. If proto-experience is ubiquitous, then either negative valence is also distributed among fundamental entities, or it can arise whenever those entities are arranged in suffering-conducive configurations.
(3) Every physical interaction—whether in stellar plasma, crystal lattices, or neural tissue—reorganizes countless fundamental entities, and on this view a vast proportion of those reorganizations either instantiate or risk instantiating suffering.
(4) Therefore, every causal event in the universe involves the production or alteration of suffering on an enormous scale.
(5) If suffering is pro tanto bad and ought to be minimized, then every possible action—including inaction, which permits other interactions to proceed—contributes to an unavoidable and immeasurable quantum of harm.
(6) But an ethical framework that renders every choice equally impermissible and provides no axis for practical deliberation is not merely demanding; it is vacuous.
(7) Hence either panpsychism is false, or fundamental-level experience carries no moral significance, or our ethical commitments require radical revision.

I would prefer to keep (1) and (6) intact if possible. Is there a hidden equivocation between potential and actual suffering between (2) and (3), or a modal slide in (4)? If I reject (2) by denying that fundamental entities possess valence altogether, do I retain a version of panpsychism that is philosophically substantive, or do I collapse into a view indistinguishable from physicalism? Which step is least costly to abandon, and what does abandoning it commit me to?
