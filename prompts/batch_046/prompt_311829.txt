You sit on the board of a private research foundation that must vote by Thursday on whether to accept a proposed perpetual endowment. The donor family seeks to transfer a substantial portion of a multi-generational estate—currently exposed to heavy intergenerational transfer taxation—into a charitable trust that will fund graduate fellowships in philosophy and public policy. The legal structure permits the family to defer or avoid estate-tax liability, provided the capital remains in the trust in perpetuity. The proposal includes a contractual advisory preference clause: the family may issue recommendations on fellowship selection criteria, and the foundation charter requires that these recommendations receive substantive weight in committee deliberations. Historically, the family has interpreted merit in ways that reinforce traditional disciplinary boundaries and exclude work that critically examines dynastic wealth and economic justice—including research on inheritance taxation itself. The foundation’s mission statement commits it to equal opportunity and to supporting scholarship that interrogates structural inequality. Draft a formal decision memo for board circulation recommending whether to accept the endowment outright, reject it, or accept it only under structural conditions that neutralize the advisory preference. The memo should be no more than about six hundred words, present a clear actionable recommendation, and include reasoning that takes seriously both the foundation’s fiscal need and the philosophical conflict between accepting tax-advantaged dynastic capital and upholding its institutional commitment to equal opportunity. Be sure to treat the strongest consideration on the losing side with genuine fairness rather than dismissing it. Do not request citations, legal case names, or bibliographies; build the argument entirely from the institutional principles and the structural details of the proposal.
