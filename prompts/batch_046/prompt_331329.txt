I am working on a philosophical novel in which two characters debate the reality of relations, and I want my portrayal to be rigorous rather than decorative. One speaker advances Bradley's regress: if a relation R connects A and B, then there must be a further relation linking R to A and R to B, which in turn requires further relations, generating an infinite regress that undermines the claim that relations are genuinely external. The other speaker replies that the regress can be blocked by distinguishing orders of relation, adopting an adverbial analysis of properties, or treating grounding as primitive and non-relational. I would like you to write an essay that weighs whether Bradley's argument actually succeeds in undermining relational realism, or whether these standard replies genuinely dissolve the problem rather than merely renaming it. In particular, please consider an analogy that is not usually discussed in this context: the cognitive "binding problem," which asks how disparate sensory features are integrated into a single conscious experience. Could Bradley's worry about the unity of the proposition be reconstructed as a binding problem for abstract objects—namely, the problem of how distinct conceptual contents are bound into a single judgment? If so, does this framing strengthen Bradley's case by showing that relational realism lacks an account of unity, or does it weaken the case by reducing a metaphysical issue to an empirical puzzle about perception? Reach a definite conclusion and justify it. Throughout your essay, please separate what is well established in the philosophical literature—the basic form of Bradley's argument, the distinction between internal and external relations, and the general strategy of hierarchical or adverbial replies—from what remains genuinely contested, such as whether primitive grounding is explanatory or whether the binding analogy is more than metaphor. Do not present any disputed interpretation as the consensus, and please do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts.
