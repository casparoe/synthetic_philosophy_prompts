I serve on the ethics and methodology review board for an international cognition consortium, and we must rule on a proposal studying moral-philosophical intuitions across fifteen societies. Rather than translating standard vignettes, the researchers will embed structurally equivalent dilemmas into locally collected folklore narratives, then score responses on a made-up “reflective coherence index” rather than binary choice, claiming that cross-cultural divergence largely disappears under this metric. Half the board sees this as ethically sound innovation—full debriefing, no deception about purpose, and framing that respects local narrative forms—while the other half argues that smuggling Western moral structures into indigenous stories is conceptual imposition that undermines informed consent, and that the coherence index itself encodes culturally specific norms about rational justification. Draft our decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—giving concrete reasoning the researchers can actually respond to, and include an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Please write in British English, keep the document formal and concise, and do not ask for references, reading lists, or citations.
