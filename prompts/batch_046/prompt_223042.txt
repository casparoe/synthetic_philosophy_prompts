I'm defending the thesis that the Enlightenment is best understood not as a binary of progress versus domination, but through what I call "procedural universalism"—the historical shift from substantive claims about human nature to standardized methods of verification, measurement, and administration that could be applied locally without universal metaphysical commitment. I argue that this procedural turn created a shared epistemic infrastructure—uniform questionnaires, standardized metrics, and delegated expert judgment—that made democratic literacy and poor-relief campaigns in Scotland structurally co-dependent with the East India Company's colonial revenue surveys, since both required the same statistical societies, census protocols, and trained intermediaries to render populations legible. My core claim is therefore not that Enlightenment reason "caused" domination as an unfortunate side effect, but that the mechanisms producing expanded rights and those producing extractive administration were institutionally inseparable in this specific procedural form.
