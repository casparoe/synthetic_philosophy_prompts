so i just got roped into rewriting the by-laws for my building's co-op and now i cant stop thinking about legal self-reference. i think i stumbled into an argument that proves constitutional self-amendment is literally impossible, which is obviously wrong since it happens, but i cant see the exit. heres the chain:

(1) For a rule R to be genuinely self-amending, the authourity to change R has to come from R itself. if the authority comes from some higher rule, then R isnt self-amending, its just being amended by something else.
(2) So R must contain a proceedural clause P that spells out exactly how R can be altered.
(3) Now suppose we try to amend P itself—say, to lower the vote threshold for future changes. since P is part of R, and R is the source of authority, the change to P has to be authorized by P.
(4) That means P applies to its own alteration: whatever conditions P sets for amending R, those same conditions govern changing P.
(5) But if P requires, for example, a two-thirds vote to change R, then changing P also requires two-thirds—even if everyone wants to switch to a simple majority.
(6) More generally, if P specifies condition C for amending R, then altering C requires satisfying C, which presupposes C is still in force during and after the change.
(7) So P is effectively entrenched against change; but if part of R is immune to R's own amendment authority, then R isnt fully self-amending after all, since it's own procedure is rigid.
(8) The only way out is to claim P isnt really part of R, but then R doesnt contain its own amendment procedure, contradicting (2).
(9) Therefore true legal self-reference is impossible: either R is rigid (not self-amending) or R lacks the internal authority claimed in (1).
(10) Yet democracies clearly amend their amendment rules. so (1) must be false, and "self-amending rule" is just a misnomer.

this feels like it proves to much. is the slip in (3), where i conflate amending P with invoking P? or is there a modal slide between (5) and (6)? or is the argument actually valid and i just have to bite the bullet and reject (1) even though it sounds right? id rather keep (1) and (2) if i can, but im open. which step do you reject, and whats the conceptual cost?
