Write a two-page dissertation prospectus in philosophy of language and formal epistemology. The background: Lewis's triviality result demonstrates that if the probability of an indicative conditional A → B is systematically equated with the conditional probability P(B|A) within a standard Boolean algebra of propositions—together with closure conditions and a principal-principle analog—then A and B must be probabilistically independent. Existing responses have largely stayed inside that propositional framework, either weakening the probability thesis or disputing one of the closure premises.

The question to frame precisely: if indicative conditionals are instead treated as belief-state operators that map a prior distribution over worlds to a revised distribution—rather than as propositions carrying a single unconditional probability—does the derivation of independence still go through, and if not, which of Lewis's auxiliary premises fails and at what cost?
