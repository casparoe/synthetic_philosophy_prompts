I'm presenting a paper next thursdsay to a mixed crowd—some Kierkegaard scholars, some media philosophers—arguing that his attack on the press isn't mainly about distraction or anonymity, but about a specific logical glitch: the press turns the idea that something concerns everyone into the idea that it concerns no one in particular, which I call modal dissociation. I claim this explains why he thinks the public can't repent or take collective responsibility—the modal structure makes shared action conceptually impossible, not just practically hard. The audience will have the ususal suspects: Kierkegaard people who say I'm turning occasional polemic into systematic philosophy, media theorists who say I'm ignoring material infrastructures, and probably one person who'll accuse me of sneaking in a Habermasian public sphere framework without admitting it. Prep me: the objecitons in order of damage, phrased like each camp would actually say them, what to concede up front (probably that he never uses the phrase modal dissociation), where each reply is soft, and especially the killer question I'm praying they don't ask—probably that if the public is just a modal error, then Kierkegaard's religious solution of the single individual collapses into a logical fix rather than a leap of faith.
