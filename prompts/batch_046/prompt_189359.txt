ive been readign hofstadter for fun and keep running into a pattern i dont have a name for. theres some inner message being delivered—say a claim in an argument or a scene in a novel—and then a frame around it that tells you how to read it, like this is only metaphor or everything up to now was made up. but then a third layer comes in and undercuts that frame without replacing it with a stable outer message. 

a couple concrete cases: a narrator confesses that the prior chapters were fiction, but the confession is spoken in the same fictional voice, so you never get a ground-level real narrator to settle the account. in philosophical arguments ive seen someone say that point only holds inside framework F, but then immediatly turn around and use that same point to attack framework F from outside, with no clear signal for which register is active. the frame and the framed keep swapping places, and the meaning seems to live in the tension between them rather than in any single layer.

it reminds me of strange loops and frame messages, but in those theres usually a clear hierarchy—an outer message that governs the inner one. here the hierarchy collapses and oscillates. does this specific collapse have an established term in philosophy of language or literary theory? if not, how exactly does it differ from ordinary irony or self-reference? im not after a bare label—i want to know what the phenomenon is, where its edges are, and whether it fits under some broader concept only imperfectly.
