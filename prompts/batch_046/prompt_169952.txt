I am organizing a working session for my philosophy of value seminar, and we have reached an impasse between two readings of Nozick’s position on non-human animals. One group takes his familiar contrast—roughly, that animals fall within a utilitarian calculus of welfare while rational agents are protected by Kantian-style side constraints—as licensing only derivative moral concern for animals, with no direct prohibition on using them as means in research or agriculture so long as suffering is minimized. The other group reads the same framework as extending side constraints directly to all sentient beings, arguing that the experience-machine case Nozick deploys for human well-being reveals a deeper commitment: treating any being as a mere container for pleasant experiences, whether by virtual simulation or by instrumental use, violates the same constraint regardless of species.

We need a joint adversarial-collaboration document that both camps could sign, to be circulated before next Thursday’s session. It should contain: a numbered section of claims both sides accept about Nozick’s formal apparatus, about what the experience-machine argument actually establishes regarding the value of reality-contact, and about the empirical conditions of primate cognition; then a disagreements table stating, in wording each side accepts for its own view, the precise divergence over whether side constraints apply derivatively through human interests or directly to the animals themselves; then the cruxes—especially whether a fully simulated “naturalistic” environment for captive research primates, designed to maximize subjective welfare while preserving full experimental control, counts as using them as means in a way that physically equivalent captivity with identical welfare does not; and, for each crux, the concrete prediction or principle each side offers, plus any shared empirical or normative criterion that would actually move the debate. The document should treat the contractualist worry about rational agency and social-contract membership with full seriousness, should not ask for bibliographic references since participants will supply those themselves, and should conclude that a specific part of the dispute is verbal only if both sides genuinely agree on the formulation.
