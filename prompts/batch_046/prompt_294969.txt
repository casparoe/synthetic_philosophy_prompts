Structure the tree around four successive splits: first, whether frame-relative simultaneity establishes full ontological parity among distant events or only determinateness without parity; second, whether the conventionality of simultaneity undermines the argument’s premise; third, whether a metaphysically real but physically undetectable privileged foliation is admissible; fourth, whether presentness is global or purely indexical. Identify exactly five terminal combinations across this logical space, naming each position—eternalism, presentism, growing-block theory, moving-spotlight theory, or an unnamed view—and stating in one sentence its most awkward commitment regarding distant past or future events. For each entry, add a second sentence diagnosing whether that combination is inhabited, incoherent, or unexplored, and explicitly flag any pair of named positions the tree fails to separate. Present the result as a numbered list of exactly five items, each item consisting of those two sentences describing one leaf, with no introduction or conclusion.
