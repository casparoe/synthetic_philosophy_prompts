My seminar leader in metaphysics has given the following verdicts on whether particular objects possess specific dispositions, without ever stating the principle behind them.

1. A crystal wine glass is enclosed in a shock-absorbing polymer shell; struck hard with a hammer, it does not break. Verdict: it remains fragile—the shell is only an external mask on the manifestation.
2. A copper wire is linked to a device that instantly ionizes its free electrons the moment a potential difference is applied. Verdict: the wire is not electrically conductive—the device is a fink that removes the categorical base upon triggering.
3. A match is soaked in water, then thoroughly dried; its chemical composition is unchanged. Verdict: it is flammable—the interfering condition was temporary and the intrinsic base is intact.
4. A toxic plant produces, within its own cells, a permanently bound antidote that neutralizes its poison at the molecular level regardless of ingestion. Verdict: the plant is not poisonous—the interference is internal and constitutive, not merely environmental.
5. A sleeping person wears noise-canceling headphones that block all sound; an alarm clock sounds loudly beside her. Verdict: she still possesses the disposition to wake to an alarm—the blockage is an external mask on the causal pathway, not a change to her auditory or arousal capacities.

Next case: A ceramic vase is manufactured with a dormant, self-healing polymer chemically integrated into its outer lattice. The polymer lies inert until microscopic cracks actually initiate upon impact; once initiation occurs, it instantly fills and seals them, preventing any visible breakage. The vase is struck with force sufficient to initiate such cracks, but none propagate. Predict her verdict—does the vase possess fragility?—and identify the implicit rule or rules that her track record supports (for example, one that distinguishes masks from finks by where in the causal chain they operate, versus one that distinguishes them by whether the object itself has been modified away from kind-normal functioning, versus one that cares only whether the categorical base is present and genuinely unimpeded by any actually operating factor). State which rules converge and which diverge on this case, give your best-guess prediction with explicit reasoning, and specify one further hypothetical case that would settle the matter between the remaining rules.

Please separate what is settled in the philosophy of dispositions—such as the standard distinction between finkish and masking interference, and the role of categorical bases—from what is genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any contested interpretation of these mechanisms as if it were the consensus view.
