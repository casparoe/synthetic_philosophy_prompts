Take the 1956 Suez Crisis — Nasser nationalises the canal, Britain and France invade alongside Israel, then get humiliated and pulled back by US and Soviet pressure — and read it through Hegel's claim that war is ethically necessary to stop civil society from stagnating and to prove state sovereignity against an other. I dont want a summary of Hegel; I want the actual mapping. Who counts as the state and who as the particular interest? Was Egypt the external other that should have affirmed British and French statehood, or was the invasion just the Suez Canal Company dressed up as national policy? Where do Washington and Moscow sit — a kind of world-spirit crushing particular wills, or just superpowers protecting their spheres? And since Britain and France come out weakened and embarrassed rather than spiritually renewed, does that damage Hegel's view, or does it show they werent acting as genuine ethical states but as particularistic powers all along? Be honest about where the framework just doesnt fit.
