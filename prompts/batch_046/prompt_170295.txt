Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on the lottery paradox and the preface paradox. Camp A, the Doxastic Closure Defenders, holds that rational belief must satisfy a consistency constraint—if one believes p and believes q, one should believe p-and-q—and that both paradoxes arise only if we illegitimately apply a flat probabilistic threshold to generate beliefs that then violate closure. Camp B, the Contextual Threshold Advocates, holds that belief is credence above a context-sensitive threshold, that closure fails for belief because the threshold for conjunctions is structurally higher than for conjuncts, and that the lottery and preface cases are therefore not paradoxes but correct descriptions of rational doxastic architecture.

The collaboration should produce a single document with the following structure:

(i) A numbered list of agreed claims—about the formal structure of the lottery scenario (a fair lottery with N tickets, exactly one winner); about the structure of the preface (an author who rationally accepts each individual claim in her book but also rationally accepts that some error remains); about what any adequate resolution must preserve (non-triviality: it cannot simply deny that anyone ever holds the problematic beliefs); and about the logical relation between the two cases, with both camps stating in neutral wording whether they share the same underlying form.

(ii) A disagreements table with three columns: the issue; Camp A's position in wording Camp A accepts; and Camp B's position in wording Camp B accepts. The table must cover: whether belief is closed under logical conjunction; whether a single uniform threshold governs belief formation; whether the preface and lottery share an identical logical form; and whether rationality permits simultaneous belief in each member of a large set together with disbelief in their universal conjunction.

(iii) A cruxes section identifying at least three sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. For each crux, provide a concrete test case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp predicts. One crux must concern the partitioned-credence model introduced here for this collaboration: the proposal that agents maintain two doxastic registers—an operational register governed by consistency and a high threshold for action-guiding commitment, and an archival register governed by evidential tracking without closure—such that beliefs about individual lottery tickets or individual book-claims reside in the archival register, while the corresponding universal conjunction is evaluated only in the operational register. Each camp should say whether this model genuinely resolves both paradoxes, merely relocates the tension between registers, or collapses into one of the existing views.

(iv) A brief concluding assessment: identify whether any portion of the dispute is purely verbal, specifying exactly which formulations create the appearance of disagreement without substantive divergence, and why. If the disagreement is fully substantive, state precisely what conceptual result or concrete case—such as the scientist with one thousand independent measurements each at 0.999 reliability, who needs all one thousand auxiliary hypotheses for her main theory—would change each camp's position.

The document must not contain bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and must not depend on access to specific texts or page references. Phrase every claim so it can be evaluated on its conceptual merits alone. Write in formal, precise philosophical prose.
