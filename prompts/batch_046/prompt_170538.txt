I've come to think pain asymbolia shows the badness of pain isnt in the raw feel but in a separate evaluative layer—like nociception gets stamped "bad" by a framing mechanism, and asymbolics just miss the stamp. So pain is never intrinsically awful; it's awful because of how we process it. I want to know what would actually count against this, not the usual hand-waving. Would it be fatal if we discovered asymbolics still have unconscious avoidance learning they cant verbalize? Or if we imagined a "pure framing" case—full bad-stamp, zero sensory input—would that support me or show the stamp is always parasitic on sensation? Rank the defeaters: which ones force me to admit badness is built into the feeling, which are just awkward, and which ones defenders of the intrinsic-badness view would themselves concede are real problems for my side? And be honest: is this position well-insulated, or is it just dodging the hard cases?
