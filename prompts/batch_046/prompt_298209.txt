Our lawyer wants us to treat the self as a center of narrative gravity — basically a story-cluster that holds together — and build a reusable checklist our staff can run in ~2 hours using just chart notes and one family interveiw. I need the actual philosophy kept intact, not dumbed down to "is he still the same guy?"

Turn it into a word flowchart: tests in order, with a "gravity well depth" score (1-4) based on three axes — temporal coherence, social anchoring, and counter-narrative drag. Mark exactly where judgment can't be cut out. Work three cases: (A) patient remembers nothing but cries at her wedding song, (B) patient built a cheerfull new story that explicitly rejects the old directive, and (C) a case where your procedure spits out an answer we will hate. Also say exactly what the procedure just can't settle. Be honest about confidence on each axis and flag the one you're least sure about. Write like you're talking to a tired paralegal.
