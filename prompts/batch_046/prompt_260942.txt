I'm preparing a position paper for a philosophy of value seminar and want a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is the package of commitments I have settled on, which I take to be largely defensible but fear may pull against each other in ways I am not seeing.

(1) I defend a lexical-threshold version of negative utilitarianism: suffering is lexically worse than pleasure is good, but only below a context-sensitive intensity threshold; above that threshold, sufficient pleasure can outweigh suffering, though never via a cardinal sum.

(2) I hold that pleasure and pain are phenomenologically incommensurable—there is no shared metric such as "hedons"—but that they remain ordinally comparable for practical deliberation through a non-aggregative ranking.

(3) I reject the repugnant conclusion for barely-positive lives, yet I accept its analog for populations whose members suffer only at sub-threshold levels: adding more such lives is not necessarily bad, even absent compensating pleasure.

(4) I accept evolutionary debunking arguments that our intuitive weights for pleasure and pain are contingent adaptive artifacts, which undermines any objective claim to cardinal comparison; nevertheless, I treat the lexical ordering itself as rationally mandatory rather than merely evolved.

(5) I judge that a life of intense aesthetic pleasure combined with mild chronic physical pain is overall good, whereas a life of uniform mild contentment with identically mild chronic pain is not—on the grounds that only the intense pleasure crosses the compensatory threshold.

(6) I claim that unrestricted negative utilitarianism is incoherent without cardinal commensurability, which is precisely why I restrict my own view to a lexical-threshold form that replaces aggregation with ordinal ranking; I further insist that this restricted form remains recognizably negative-utilitarian rather than a distinct theory altogether.

Please audit this set. Go through the pairs and tell me which are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each tension, identify the least costly revision and what it costs me—specifically whether it forces me to abandon the negative-utilitarian framing, the evolutionary-debunking concession, or the incommensurability thesis. Please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know this literature, and do not present a contested view as the consensus; flag any point where my position relies on a claim that is not widely shared.
