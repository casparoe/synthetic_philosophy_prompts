Write the opening remarks—approximately five minutes—for an interdisciplinary workshop titled "The Timing of Experience." The audience is evenly divided between philosophers of mind who work on temporal consciousness and historians who study testimonial memory; neither side is familiar with the other’s vocabulary. Your task is to use the color phi phenomenon, in which a moving dot is perceived to change color before its motion is registered, to illuminate the distinction between Orwellian and Stalinesque models of retrospective revision: the first rewrites the past record, the second removes it without leaving a gap. Do not merely present these two models; instead, develop a third framing you term "phi-shifted narrative," in which the emotional valence of an event is retroactively displaced onto an earlier temporal position, leaving the original event structurally present but affectively orphaned. The remarks should avoid all citations, references to specific texts, or bibliographies, and must conclude with one concrete, non-obvious question that forces the philosophers and the historians to speak across their disciplinary boundary.
