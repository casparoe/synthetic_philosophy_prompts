Hey, I was trying to adapt the Rietdijk-Putnam argument for a seminar and now it seems to prove that caring about the future is irrational. Here's the chain I ended up with:

(1) In SR simultaneity is frame-relative, so if observer O1 calls distant event E "now" and O2 calls it "past", E can't depend on that label to exist or not.
(2) So every event that is "now" in some admissable frame exists tenselessly in the block.
(3) There's an inward version — call it worldline-parity — that applies the same reasoning along my own worldline: for any event on my timeline there's some frame (accelerated or just differently sliced) where that event is simultaneous with my present.
(4) Therefore all my mental states — past regret, present coffee, future panic — are equally real parts of the four-dimensional object.
(5) If they're equally real, then the suffering in my future slice isn't "going to happen"; it's already instantiated, just like suffering in a past slice.
(6) I don't feel obligated to prevent past suffering because it's causally unreachable from here.
(7) By parity, if future suffering is equally real and equally causaly unreachable from my present slice, I have no special reason to prevent it either.
(8) That collapses all prudential reasoning.
(9) But prudential reasoning clearly doesn't collapse.
(10) So either worldline-parity is bogus, or there's a hidden asymmetry I'm missing.

I feel like something slips between (5) and (6) — maybe "causally unreachable" means something different for future-directed actions? — but I can't name it. If I have to choose I'd rather keep (1)-(4) and drop (7) than bring back a hidden flow of time. Where exactly does the argument break, and what's the cost of fixing it?
