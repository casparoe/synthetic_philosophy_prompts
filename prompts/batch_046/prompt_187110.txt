Consider attempts to formalize the presumption of independence — the heuristic principle that, absent specific reasons for dependence, variables or propositions should be treated as independent — within frameworks for plausible reasoning (whether Bayesian, imprecise-probabilistic, or argument-based). Rather than a survey, I want a defended prioritization of open problems in this area, specifically: (i) whether the presumption should be formalized as a default inference rule or as an assignment of prior probabilities; (ii) how to capture the asymmetry between causal and evidential independence without circular appeal to the very heuristic being formalized; and (iii) how to represent dependence violations that are structurally invisible to the reasoner. Which two of these should dominate research effort over the next few years, and in what order? Defend your ranking by stating the criterion you apply (e.g., tractability for formal philosophers, downstream impact on heuristic evaluation, or dependency of other questions on this one), what form of progress would count as decisive, and which remaining problem should be deprioritized because it either presupposes one of your priorities or is already adequately handled by existing formalisms.
