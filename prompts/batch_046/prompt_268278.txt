Write a short story of roughly 800 words, set in a deaf-blind commune where architecture is the sole shared language and buildings are “read” through pressure and thermal patterns in walls. The community’s founding aesthetic doctrine adapts W. E. B. Du Bois’s claim that all art is propaganda—specifically, that every structure must embed an intentional message of collective interdependence through its embodied tactile logic, not through symbols. The protagonist, an aging architect, discovers that a revered cathedral built sixty years earlier contains an accidental inversion in its thermal gradients: inhabitants have been physically experiencing a message of radical isolation rather than solidarity. The story should stage the conceptual tension between Du Bois’s insistence on art’s deliberate social purpose and the possibility that embodied interpretation is uncontrollable; ask whether the misread cathedral counts as failed propaganda, successful art, or something that collapses the distinction; and conclude with the architect’s decision whether to correct the error, preserve the accidental reading, or design a new structure that makes the misinterpretation itself part of the intended message. Use the physical details of the architecture—weight, warmth, sequence of chambers—to carry the philosophical argument without explicit exposition.
