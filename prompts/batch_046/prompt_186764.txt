I sit on the strategic planning committee of the Meridian Institute, a private research endowment with an explicit century-scale mandate. We are considering a charter amendment that would require every long-range funding proposal—any projecting institutional impact beyond thirty years—to include an "outside view" appendix. This appendix would map the proposal onto a reference class of comparable long-lived entities (territorial empires, universities, religious orders, joint-stock companies, open-source ecosystems) and derive a base-rate survival or failure curve, which must be weighed against the proposal's internal narrative, or "inside view." A minority faction argues that for a networked, non-territorial body like ours, reference-class selection collapses into an unprincipled exercise in reference-class tennis that smuggles in a false ontology of institutional kinds; they claim that applying collapse data from sovereign empires to a decentralized endowment is a category error rather than a correction for optimism bias, and they urge us to reject the amendment in favor of pure scenario planning. The majority favors adoption with a six-month pilot, but they have not engaged the ontological objection. Draft the short opinion memo I will circulate before our March meeting: recommend adoption with conditions, adoption with a pilot framework, or rejection, specifying a concrete implementation timeline or review checkpoint. Your memo should treat the minority's category-error argument seriously—reconstruct the implicit philosophy of history and institutional identity it presupposes, assess whether an outside view can be salvaged by using feature-based similarity metrics rather than kind-membership, and spell out the practical safeguards (for example, transparent documentation of class selection, mandatory sensitivity analysis across multiple reference frames, and a sunset clause tied to predictive calibration) that would be required if the amendment passes. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
