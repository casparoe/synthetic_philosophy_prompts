For my high school philosophy elective, I have been asked to practice grading peer short-answer responses. The exam prompt instructed students to explain Frege's puzzle about identity statements—specifically the difference in cognitive value between "a = a" and "a = b"—using the distinction between sense and reference. Students were then required to apply this framework to a specific case: a library preservation system assigns two separate catalog entries, "Storage record S-104" and "Display file D-104," to the same handwritten letter; one entry tracks the physical paper in climate-controlled storage, while the other tracks the high-resolution digital scan available online. The question asked whether the statement "Storage record S-104 is Display file D-104" is trivial or informative in Frege's terms, and to justify the answer with care.

Below is the full response from one student.

"Frege's puzzle arises because an identity statement can be informative when two names share a reference but express different senses. 'Hesperus is Hesperus' is trivial because both sense and reference are identical, whereas 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' is cognitively valuable: both names refer to Venus, but one presents it as the morning star and the other as the evening star, so learning their identity expands our knowledge.

Applying this to the library case, 'Storage record S-104 is Display file D-104' should also be informative. The name 'S-104' presents the letter as physical paper in a storage box, while 'D-104' presents it as a digital image for screen viewing. Because these modes of presentation differ, the statement tells us that the physical object and the digital file correspond, which is not obvious on its own.

However, the reference is not actually the same. The physical paper is made of cellulose and occupies space, while the digital file consists of encoded data. Thus 'S-104' and 'D-104' do not share a reference; they denote two different entities that merely represent the same content. So this is not a genuine instance of Frege's 'a = b' with one reference and two senses. Rather, it is closer to a false identity claim, or at best an analogy rather than an application of the puzzle itself."

Do not offer a general summary of Frege's theory; evaluate this particular answer.
