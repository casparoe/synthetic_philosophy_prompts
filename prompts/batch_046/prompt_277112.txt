I am trying to apply the knowledge norm of assertion to the ethics of multi-author scientific publications. The norm requires that a speaker assert only what she knows. In large collaborations, no individual researcher knows every empirical claim reported; each relies on derivatively transmitted results from sub-teams. If every co-author thereby asserts the paper's full conclusions, they all seem to violate the norm. One might reply that only the corresponding author asserts, or that the collaboration itself is the assertoric agent. But if we allow group-level assertion, the knowledge norm collapses into a collective-knowledge condition far weaker than individual knowledge, and it becomes obscure why a reviewer who detects an error in one sub-team's data should be obligated to trigger a retraction of conclusions she never personally asserted. Should we therefore restrict the knowledge norm to single-speaker contexts, or can we develop a theory of distributed assertoric agency that preserves the norm's individual stringency without demanding omniscience from each co-author?
