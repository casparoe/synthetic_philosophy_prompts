I'm giving the opening remarks at the retirment concert for Dr. Elena Voss, who taught Schopenhauer's philosophy of music for thirty years. I need the talk to honour her actual view: music isn't representational like painting or poetry but is a direct copy of the Will itself, which means it's about blind striving rather than harmony or comfort. So the speech has to avoid false consolation — nothing about music lifting us out of suffering or revealing a higher order. Could you write a five-minute address that uses that tension structurally, so the philosophers catch the exact distinction she's defended and everyone else gets why she never called music beautiful in the usual sense? Write it in British English, and please say how confident you are in the Schopenhauer interpretation you've used, singling out the point you're least sure about.
