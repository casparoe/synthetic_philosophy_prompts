I retired from physics a few years back and lately I've been poking around philosphy, mostly because my grandson said I should read something besides journels. Anyway I was flipping through Berkeley's old writings on coin and trade -- he seemed to think money isn't the metal but a kind of token for commanding labor or goods, which struck me as obvious untill you try to apply it consistently, so now I'm testing my gut on cases and I'm all turned around.

Here are my verdicts. A gold coin is real money, full stop. A paper banknote with no gold behind it is also real, but only because the state makes people accept it as a claim on work. A digital entry in my bank account is the same thing, just a record of that claim. In my retirement community we have these time-credits where an hour of tutoring buys an hour of plumbing, no government involved, and I think those are real money too -- maybe even purer. But Bitcoin, where the only value is that other people might want it tomorrow, feels like fake money to me, just a collective hallucination with electricity bills. A government bond that promises future tax revenue seems very real, like a stored labor-order. But here's the one I keep going back and forth on: writing a check when I know the funds aren't there yet, but I'm sure they'll be covered before it clears. Is that real money being moved, or is it just a lie with a delay? And then there's this hypothetical entropy-token my old collegue mentioned -- a unit representing the right to dissipate one kilojoule of ordered energy in a closed ecological zone -- I think that'd be real money too, since it commands actual physical work, but I'm not sure if that's consistent with me rejecting Bitcoin.

So is there one principle behind all these, maybe something Berkeley-ish about money being an enforceable token for labor or energy? Or do I have to drop one of my verdicts -- probably the check or the Bitcoin one -- to make it clean? And whatever the principle is, what does it say about the entropy-token case I haven't fully made my mind up on?
