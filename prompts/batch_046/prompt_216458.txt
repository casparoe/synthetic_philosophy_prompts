But then the same institutions publish user-facing docs and policy briefs that insist these systems have no consciousness, no beliefs, no desires, it's all just statistical pattern matching, and mentalistic language is purely heuristic. So there's this institutional double-bookkeeping: realist intentional talk inside the lab, hard eliminativist talk in public.

What philosophical commitments does that practice actually presuppose? Is it only coherent if you buy Dennett's intentional stance as purely predictive with zero ontological weight? So does the practice smuggle in a philosophy of mind—maybe a weak unity constraint, like the idea that holding a belief needs a single subject—that doesn't fit the architecture at all?
