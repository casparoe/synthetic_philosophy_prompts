I'm putting together a taxonomy for my philosophy of biology seminar and I'm sick of the usual etiological-vs-causal-role flowchart that just lists two boxes. I want a decision tree that sorts actual positions by concrete answers, not by labels they wear. The branches should be answerable questions: is evolutionary origin necessary? Is current fitness contribution sufficient? Can a trait have multiple unsettled functions? And here's the niche twist — does a synthetic gene engineered de novo in a lab and inserted into a bacterium have a biological function before it participates in metabolism or reproduction? Each leaf needs a name — make sure etiological, causal-role, pluralist, and organizational views all appear somewhere — plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Note any empty combinations and flag pairs your tree can't seperate; I suspect pluralist and organizational views land together on the synthetic case, so call that out rather than hiding it. Six or seven leaves max, branch questions phrased so a student can answer without knowing the view it leads to.
