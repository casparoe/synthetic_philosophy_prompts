hey, so i've been chewing on Camus's argument against the death penalty — the one where he says the state becomes a murderer with a calculater, planning the kill with bureaucratic calm — and i built this chain that feels like it collapses into absolute pacifism, which he clearly wasn't. tell me which step to ditch and what it costs me:

1. Camus's core objection isn't just that the state kills, but that it premeditates and rationalizes the killing, which makes it a mirror-image of the murderer rather than an opposite.
2. If the wrong-making feature is rational premeditation combined with a claim of justice, then any state killing that has both features is equally wrong.
3. So a carefully planned targeted strike, approved through legal review and justified as necessary justice or defense, should be in the same moral category as execution.
4. But Camus distinguishes rebellion (which he accepts can involve violence) from state terror, and he doesn't seem to think all military action is mirror-image murder.
5. So either step 2 is false — there's something special about capital punishment's premeditation — or Camus needs a hidden principle that separates execution from other rational state killings.
6. The only plausible hidden principle is that execution is unique because it claims to correct a wrong by doing the exact wrong it condemns.
7. But if that's the principle, then any punitive or defensive state violence that claims moral justification while replicating the harm is also hypocritical.
8. That seems to swallow just-war reasoning, capital punishment for war crimes, maybe even lethal self-defense law.
9. So the argument either proves too much or it relies on a distinction that's doing all the work in step 6 without being defended.

where's the leak? is it that "mirror-image" is just metaphor and doesn't transfer? or that rebellion is categorically different because it refuses state authority altogether? i want to keep 1 and 4 if i can.
