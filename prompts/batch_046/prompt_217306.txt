I'm putting together a small interdisciplinary seminar—philosophy and clinical psychology—on the contemporary Stoic revival, and I've become fixated on a tension that seems to run through almost every modern appropriation of the dichotomy of control. On one side, the revival tends to present the boundary between internal agency and external events as a pragmatic heuristic for emotional regulation, essentially continuous with cognitive-behavioral strategies for managing anxiety and beliefs about personal efficacy. On the other side, in the ancient texts this boundary is anchored in a specific metaphysical picture: the ruling faculty is the only site of genuine agency precisely because everything outside it—body, reputation, external goods—is already woven into the deterministic fabric of cosmic reason. I am trying to decide where to direct three years of limited research attention, and I want a defended agenda rather than a survey.

Given that framing, which open questions about this split matter most, in what order should they be tackled, and why? Specifically, I am weighing whether the primary task is to determine if the dichotomy can be coherently detached from Stoic determinism without becoming merely a therapeutic slogan; whether modern clinical applications systematically misread Epictetus by treating the internal and external boundary as mutable through practice rather than as ontologically fixed; and whether the revival's near-total neglect of Stoic cosmology undermines the ethical coherence of its popular prescriptions. For each, I want to know how tractable it is for philosophers working without empirical lab resources, which other questions depend on its resolution, and what kind of conceptual result over the next few years would cause you to reorder the priority. If you think the list misses the genuinely upstream problem—perhaps something about prohairesis, social justice applications, or the relationship to modern debates about free will—please substitute it and defend the substitution. I am not looking for a bibliography or a reading list; I want a prioritized research program with explicit dependency relations and trade-offs between tractability and downstream importance.
