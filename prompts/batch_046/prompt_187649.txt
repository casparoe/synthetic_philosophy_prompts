Please provide a concrete example of the conceptual issue that arises specifically regarding the first-person indexical "I" in this split situation. In particular, explain whether Dennett's framework—according to which personal identity depends on psychological continuity and the self functions as a kind of narrative center of gravity—forces us to say that there are now two "I"s, that the original "I" has disappeared, or that "I" has become a distributable property. Focus on exactly where the framework is strained and what this divided case reveals about whether self-location must necessarily be singular.
