I'm presenting a 25-minute paper at next month's metaphysics-of-mind workshop, defending what I call a stance-relative but non-instrumentalist account of real patterns applied specifically to the combination problem in panpsychism. My thesis is that the patterns tracked by the physical stance, the design stance, and the intentional stance are not reducible to one another, but neither are they ontologically independent layers that license fundamental micro-consciousness. Rather, each stance detects a distinct compressibility profile—conditions under which a predictive model achieves stable, non-accidental compression of a target domain. I argue that panpsychists generate the combination problem only by illicitly exporting physical-stance stability conditions (spatial contiguity, causal closure, micro-physical adjacency) into the intentional domain, where pattern stability actually depends on functional integration and cross-scale predictive coherence. So macro-consciousness involves real patterns that micro-physical descriptions genuinely miss, yet this does not entail that consciousness is present at the fundamental level.

The audience is deliberately mixed and hostile in different directions: two Dennett scholars convinced that "real but stance-relative" collapses into pure instrumentalism; one committed panpsychist who will accuse me of borrowing her explanatory gap without accepting her ontology; a philosopher of science suspicious that I'm inventing ad hoc pluralism to avoid physicalism; and a cognitive scientist who works on predictive processing and wants to know whether my "compressibility profile" maps onto anything empirically tractable. They all know Dennett's three stances and the standard combination-problem literature, but none are specialists in information-theoretic metaphysics, so I need objections phrased in their own vocabularies—not in the jargon of formal epistemology or decision theory.
