I'm drafting the conceptual framework for an independent-study presentation on what I'm calling "competence inversions"—cases where robust explanatory or behavioral performance exists without underlying mechanistic or semantic comprehension. Before I present, I want a consistency audit of the package I've assembled. Here are my commitments:

(1) Darwin developed the theory of natural selection with extraordinary explanatory and predictive competence despite having virtually no comprehension of the mechanisms of heredity or variation; this shows that scientific success does not require prior mechanistic understanding.

(2) Turing proposed that we should evaluate machine intelligence by behavioral output alone, inverting the usual reasoning that demands internal comprehension of cognitive architecture before we grant intelligence; I think this methodological inversion is sound.

(3) I apply both inversions to contemporary large language models: they generate linguistically appropriate outputs without possessing semantic comprehension or a world-model, and I regard this as a legitimate form of competence—philosophically analogous to Darwin's theoretical achievement rather than a mere simulation.

(4) At the same time, I accept Searle's Chinese Room argument that pure syntactic manipulation, however behaviorally perfect, cannot constitute genuine semantic understanding; therefore I deny that LLMs understand language, even while I grant them linguistic competence.

(5) I also hold that in human moral life, competence without comprehension is not excusable: if a person acts with behavioral skill but without understanding the moral significance of their action, they should not be treated as fully responsible agents.

Be specific about the incompatibility—do not list them all as equally bad.
