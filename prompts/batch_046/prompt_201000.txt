Write a dialogue between two philosophy grad students, Lila and Raj, arguing in a noisy campus cafe about whether ontology should depend on information thats permanently gone. Lila holds that if two entities are indistinguishable in every possible observation — even in principle — then treating them as distinct is just multiplying beings without cause; she calls her view epistemic closure. Raj thinks thats backwards: ontology should follow the structure of our best theories, not our access, and he keeps invoking teh mirror-archive case. In that case, two reconstructions of a destroyed historical archive match in every surviving record, but they differ in the causal role of one document that burned so completely that no trace of its existence remains, not even in principle. Lila tells Raj he's telling ghost stories and letting theory run wild. Raj pushes back that Lila is letting accidental cosmic limits — like being stuck behind an event horizon — decide what exists. Make sure they actually respond to each other: Lila should tackle Raj's claim about theoretical structure licensing hidden distinctions, and Raj should answer her charge that he's sneaking in unobservable entities for no empirical gain. Neither should be a strawman. Keep it casual and messy, like a real argument, with both landing real punches and talking past each other a bit.
