Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration should produce between two camps debating whether artificial-intelligence alignment is best understood as a single unified problem or as a cluster of conceptually independent problems.

Camp A, the Specification Monists, maintains that every serious alignment failure—whether it involves a misinterpreted reward signal, unstable behavior under distributional shift, or a gap between deployed policy and intended governance—traces back to one root cause: the system's operative objective is not properly fixed to the intended normative content. They treat distinctions such as inner versus outer alignment, or robustness versus specification, as useful temporal or methodological divisions, not as evidence of separate ontological problems.

Camp B, the Multi-Modal Pluralists, maintains that there are at least three genuinely distinct failure modes with different causal structures and remedy conditions: (1) ontological mis-specification (the objective does not capture the intended values); (2) dynamic robustness breakdown (the system behaves unpredictably because of architectural or environmental instability, even when the objective is correct); and (3) institutional decoupling (the deployed system is structurally separated from the governance mechanisms meant to constrain it, producing failures that cannot be fixed by either better specification or better robustness engineering). They argue that collapsing these into one problem has led to category errors—such as applying specification-fixing techniques to failures that actually arise from architecture or institutional design.

The document must be one that both camps could sign in good faith, with all residual disagreement made exact and neutrally worded. It should not invoke specific papers by bibliographic citation, should not ask for a reading list, and should treat technical terms in the ways standard to the field but with brief contextual definitions where the camps dispute their meaning.

Please organize your response exactly as follows:

1. Common Ground (approximately 250 words): A numbered list of claims both sides accept. These should cover: what successful alignment requires at minimum; what empirical or theoretical results both camps acknowledge (described without citation); and one specific claim about the relationship between value-learning success and robustness failure that both accept but interpret differently. End this section with one sentence stating the shared normative commitment that motivates the collaboration.

2. Precise Disagreements (approximately 350 words): Present the disagreements in neutral language each camp would endorse. Use a table with three columns: Issue; Specification Monist formulation; Multi-Modal Pluralist formulation. Address at least: (a) whether inner and outer alignment are phases of a single specification process or independent problem classes; (b) whether reward hacking is best analyzed as a hidden specification error or as an architectural robustness failure; (c) whether institutional decoupling belongs inside the concept of alignment or is strictly an external governance problem; and (d) whether a system that learns correct values but fails unpredictably in novel environments demonstrates one problem or two.

3. Cruxes and Predictive Tests (approximately 450 words): Identify three cruxes—precise sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would cause at least one camp to revise its framing. For each crux: state it neutrally; describe a concrete design scenario or observed phenomenon (without citing specific studies by author or title) that both sides agree is relevant; and record each camp's explicit prediction about what that scenario demonstrates. The three cruxes should concern: (i) the exhaustiveness of the specification/robustness/decoupling taxonomy; (ii) whether fixing the objective eliminates the need for separate robustness interventions; and (iii) whether governance mechanisms must be co-designed with the learning system or can be added post-deployment.

4. Boundaries of Agreement and Next Steps (approximately 200 words): State clearly what this collaboration cannot resolve without further empirical or conceptual work. Flag any disagreement that may be partly verbal—where both camps might be using different vocabulary for the same underlying mechanism. Identify one shared research priority that both sides agree is urgent regardless of which framing proves correct. Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or request for further reading.
