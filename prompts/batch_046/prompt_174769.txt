Hey, I'm a retired biologist who got into philosphy of mind lately, mostly because my grandson works in ML and I want to understand what he's actually building. Last week at a local meetup I got stuck in an argument with two people, Raj and Elena, about whether neural nets have real representations or if that's just us anthropomorphizing.

Raj said if you ablate a specific filter in an early vision layer and the model suddenly can't do horizontal edges, then that filter was literally representing horizontal edges. He compared it to knocking out a frog's retina neuron and it stops snapping at flies.

Elena said that's just causation, not representaion. She said representation needs a normative standard -- the system itself has to be able to get it wrong in a way that matters internally, not just to us engineers. She brought up the barometer: it tracks pressure but doesn't represent it unless we interpret it, and nets are closer to barometers than brains.

Raj came back that the normative part comes from teh loss function. The network is trained to minimize prediction error, so when a filter activates wrong and drives up loss, that's the system's own evaluation. He called it derived teleology.

Elena said that's still externally designed. True misrepresentation needs decoupling -- like thinking about a unicorn without one being present. Nets don't decouple; they just compute.

Then Raj mentioned these newer self-supervised vision models where internal representations drift during training into categories that don't match human labels, and he said that flexibility proves they're not just statistical compressors but building their own ontology.

Elena said that just proves they're complex compressors, not that they have mental content.

I honestly couldn't tell who was winning. Can you go through their exchange move by move -- rate each argument from clearly wrong to clearly right and briefly justify each rating? Then say who's more right overall, and point out what both are missing, maybe something from older philosophy of science about models? I'm not asking for a bibliography, just your take.
