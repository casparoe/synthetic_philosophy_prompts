You serve on an institutional review board at a neuroscience research center and must sign off, alone and without web access, on protocols that invoke “phenomenal qualia” in patients with disorders of consciousness or in non-human primates. The board has adopted the Chase-Sanborn incoherence thesis as its working framework: the classical posit of private, immediately known qualitative states collapses because fixing their content across contexts either requires circular appeal to introspection or eliminates their causal-explanatory role, leaving nothing coherent to measure or protect. You need a reusable decision procedure—completable in thirty minutes by a reviewer with neuroscience literacy but no philosophy PhD—that applies this framework rather than adjudicating metaphysics.

Write a step-by-step review procedure (a flowchart in words) with ordered coherence filters: (i) referential stability—can the protocol specify qualitative content without circular reliance on the subject’s report? (ii) access conditions—does it presuppose first-person access that is either unverifiable or redundant with third-person measures? (iii) explanatory redundancy—does invoking qualia do ethical or causal work that the protocol’s neural or behavioral measures already do? For each filter, state the passing criterion, the failure mode, and whether failure triggers rejection, mandatory revision, or hand-off to a senior ethicist for a values judgment rather than a coherence judgment. Mark every point where the procedure must yield to human judgment. Then apply the procedure to three hypothetical protocols: (A) a high-profile, well-funded study using “inverse-phenomenal reports”—patients with locked-in syndrome who deny subjective experience despite intact cortical activation—to calibrate an instrument the authors call a “qualia meter”; (B) a proposal to vaccinate a badger population against a painful disease, justified partly on the ground that it preserves the animals’ “phenomenal suffering” as an ethically distinct harm; (C) a study that defines qualia purely operationally as “the neural signature correlated with verbal color-naming” and claims this escapes the incoherence problem entirely. Include at least one case—preferably the well-funded study—where the procedure mandates rejection or major revision that the board will be politically reluctant to enforce. End by listing the procedure’s explicit limitations: the protocol types it will misclassify, the assumptions it smuggles in, and the questions it refuses to answer. Keep the role, stakes, one-page sign-off constraint, and distinction between coherence failures (procedure decides) and ethical trade-offs (procedure hands off) fixed throughout. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or further reading.
