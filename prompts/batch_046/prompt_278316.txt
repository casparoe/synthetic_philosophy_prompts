Specify the full list of desiderata that a normative formalism for rational belief under structural ambiguity must meet in order to count as adequate. Here structural ambiguity denotes not merely uncertainty about probabilities within a fixed model—e.g., the bias of a coin—but uncertainty about the correct causal structure or event-space partition itself: for example, whether an observed correlation is mediated by an unobserved confounder, with different candidate models altering both the available acts and the outcomes they generate. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it: avoiding dilation upon conditioning when observations are interpreted differently across models; preserving intertemporal consistency without forcing premature commitment to a single model; permitting aggregation of expert judgments when experts disagree about which variables belong in the model; and maintaining actionable decision-guidance without collapsing the ambiguity into precise Bayesian updating over an artificially expanded state space. Distinguish the non-negotiable requirements—those any normative account must preserve—from camp-specific preferences (such as completeness of preference orderings, universal avoidance of sure loss, or retention of the sure-thing principle across model revisions). Identify at least one subset of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable in light of standard conditioning results for convex sets of probabilities, and explain whether the conflict is structural or merely technical. The weight should fall on the specification of criteria rather than on scoring particular theories, though brief comparative remarks on convex-set, belief-function, and smooth-ambiguity approaches are acceptable.
