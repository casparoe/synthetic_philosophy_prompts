I am a high school senior working on an independent study in philosophy and literature, and after a long discussion with a friend about whether Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor actually proves anything about human nature, I realized that most classroom treatments reduce the parable to a simple opposition between freedom and happiness. For my semester project, my advisor has asked me to write a short research proposal—approximately two single-spaced pages—framing a more precise and less familiar question.

Here is what I want to pursue: does the Grand Inquisitor's claim that people will always surrender freedom for bread, miracle, and authority depend on an unstated assumption that freedom is only a negative condition—the absence of external coercion—rather than a positive or structural capacity for self-interpretation and creative self-formation? I want to propose that the Inquisitor's tripartite taxonomy of human goods implicitly suppresses a fourth category, a kind of authorial freedom that is not about selecting among predetermined options but about shaping the very framework within which choices become meaningful. The proposal should state this question with enough precision that it admits a determinate answer, explain why standard interpretations that treat freedom and happiness as competing goods leave this specific architectural problem unresolved, outline an approach—likely a close rhetorical analysis of the three temptations paired with a conceptual distinction between negative and structural liberty developed independently—describe what a successful result would look like (for instance, showing that the Inquisitor's anthropology of weakness collapses once the fourth category is admitted) and what would count as failure (perhaps demonstrating that the text genuinely offers no internal resources for such a category), and include an honest assessment of whether a high school semester can do more than sketch the argument. I will compile all citations and verify sources myself, so please do not include a bibliography or reading list; I need only the conceptual framing, the research plan, and a realistic scope for the time available.
