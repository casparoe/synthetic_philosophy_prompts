I'd like a consistency audit on the following package of commitments, which I intend to present in a seminar on value theory. I hold:

(1) A lexical-threshold form of negative utilitarianism: there exists a severity of suffering—call it the lexical boundary—such that no quantity of pleasure, and no absence of sub-boundary suffering, can morally compensate for a single occurrence of suffering that crosses it.

(2) The boundary is not a discrete discontinuity but an asymptotic gradient: as suffering approaches the boundary from below, the exchange rate at which additional pleasure can offset additional pain declines exponentially, approaching zero without ever reaching literal non-tradability.

(3) Within the sub-lexical domain, pleasure and pain are fully commensurable via a single cardinal inter-experiential metric; once the boundary is crossed, that metric is not merely unbounded but undefined—there is no coherent assignment of magnitude that relates supra-boundary suffering to any sub-lexical goods.

(4) Moral evaluation is therefore bifurcated: worlds that contain only sub-lexical experiences are assessed by standard aggregate maximization on the shared metric, whereas worlds containing any supra-boundary suffering are evaluated solely on whether they contain such instances, with all other quantitative considerations rendered morally inert.

(5) I reject the proposal to model the lexical relation as a very large but finite disutility coefficient embedded in an otherwise continuous utility function, because such a model preserves cross-world transitivity and completeness in ways that misrepresent the categorical, non-gradated nature of the judgment.

Do these hang together? Please identify which pairs generate genuine logical or motivational tension and which only appear to conflict, rank the conflicts by severity, and specify the least costly revision that restores consistency without collapsing the view into standard (non-lexical) negative utilitarianism or into a purely continuous welfarist framework. Be precise about which commitment must yield and what exactly is lost in yielding it.
