As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun to study philosophy of cosmology, I have been discussing observer-selection scepticism with a colleague who specialises in anthropic reasoning. Her settled verdicts are these: she dismisses Boltzmann-brain scepticism as a live threat in our actual universe, on the grounds that evolutionary biology supplies a non-fluctuation origin for our cognitive faculties; she grants that in an infinite universe in thermal equilibrium Boltzmann observers must eventually outnumber ordinary ones, yet denies that this fact undermines present-day scientific knowledge; she rejects self-sampling reasoning in cosmological model selection and instead endorses self-indication; she accepts Nick Bostrom's argument that self-sampling would favour cosmologies with smaller total observer populations—what he calls the presumptuous-philosopher problem—but treats that result as a reductio ad absurdum of self-sampling rather than of large universes; and she holds that imposing a "cognitive-stability" constraint on observer selection fails to solve the problem, whereas imposing a "causal-continuity" constraint succeeds.

The new case is one I have constructed for her: imagine a universe that is spatially finite but temporally infinite, in which thermal fluctuations eventually generate conscious observers, but in which every such observer is embedded in an unbroken causal chain extending back to a low-entropy initial state. In this model, observers with such causal continuity eventually outnumber isolated Boltzmann brains, though only after an exponentially long but still finite duration. Predict her verdict on whether, in that universe, our present knowledge would be undermined by the eventual dominance of fluctuation-born observers. Say whether her reasoning is best reconstructed as pure self-indication, as self-sampling supplemented by a causal-continuity filter, or as a hybrid principle, and identify the closest analogue among her earlier verdicts. Then specify the further hypothetical case that would best distinguish her actual principle from the other candidates.

Please indicate how confident you are in your reconstruction, and explicitly name the point about which you are least certain.
