I've been stuck on the Thomson lamp thing ever since our philosophy of physics reading group met last tuesday and nobody could agree on why we even care. You know the setup — lamp toggled on and off in intervals that keep halving, all done by the two-minute mark, and then everyone asks is it on or off. What's frustrating is that this debate hasn't budged in decades: some people say it's a flat contradiction because there's no last toggle to determine the state, others say the state is just undefined at the limit point, and then there's the camp that says supertasks are physically impossible so the whole scenario is moot. But are we actually disagreeing about infinity, or is this a verbal mess around the word 'completed'? I think part of the stall is that one side is analyzing logical possibility while the other is quietly importing physics about continuous time, but nobody flags it.

Here's the twist I've been playing with: what if the toggle isn't deterministic but probabilistic, with each switch having an independent 50% chance? Then there's no paradox about parity — the limit state is genuinely random — yet people in my group still split on whether the supertask 'succeeds' or whether asking about the lamp's state at the end is even meaningful. Does that mean the disagreement was never really about determinism or contradiction, but about whether an infinite sequence can have a well-defined endpoint at all? Or is there a hidden formal question about measure and limit behavior that we're all dancing around? I want a diagnosis: is this substantive, verbal, or are we answering different questions about logic versus physics, and what — if anything — would actually move the needle?
