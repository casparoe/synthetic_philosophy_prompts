I am a doctoral student in philosophy of value preparing to moderate a podcast interview with an unnamed philosopher who recently advanced a hybrid account of meaning in life that integrates Camus-style absurdism with objective-list and subjective-fulfillment frameworks. On her view, cosmic pointlessness is not an external obstacle that meaningful projects must overcome; instead, she argues that an occurrent or dispositional awareness of ultimate futility serves as a constitutive condition for what she terms defiant integration, in which an activity's value is partly constituted by the agent's sustained commitment to it despite full recognition that it lacks cosmic justification. She distinguishes this position both from pure objective-list theories, which treat subjective fulfillment as secondary, and from Susan Wolf's fitting fulfillment model, which she claims treats the absurd as a psychological threat external to the structure of meaning rather than as an internal feature of it.

I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond restating this framework and that turn on the actual content of her position. Please include at least one question pressing her on whether defiant integration adds genuine normative content to Wolf's joint conditions of subjective attraction and objective value, or whether it is merely a phenomenological redescription of the same hybrid structure; for this question, provide a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, namely that the awareness of futility transforms the quality of subjective fulfillment rather than supplementing it. I also need a question about practical discrimination: if cosmic pointlessness is constitutive rather than obstructive, how does her view distinguish a genuinely meaningful life from one that is merely compulsively busy or self-deceptive? Include a follow-up anticipating her appeal to the authenticity conditions of defiance. Additionally, ask about the phenomenological requirements—must the agent actually experience the tension between value and futility, or is a dispositional, non-occurrent grasp sufficient?—with a follow-up addressing the likely reply that only occurrent awareness avoids self-deception. Conclude with a challenging question asking whether defiant integration is ultimately a normative philosophical thesis or a sophisticated psychological coping strategy, and if the latter, what independent philosophical work it can still perform.

The interview is intended for a colloquium-style podcast audience, so the questions should avoid generic biographical openings and should not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations. Each question should anticipate a standard reply and include a brief follow-up tailored to that reply. Please write the full sequence in American English.
