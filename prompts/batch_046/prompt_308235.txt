I am drafting an essay for an interdisciplinary seminar on normative political language, and I would value critical comments on its central line of argument. The essay asks whether "Western values" denotes a coherent normative category or whether it functions primarily as what I call an "indexical cluster"—a phrase whose extensional content shifts with the perceived threat in a given argumentative context. Rather than rehearsing the familiar historical objections about contingency and cultural hybridity, I propose a structural diagnosis: the phrase is deployed in three distinct registers—genealogical (claiming lineage from Enlightenment liberalism and Christian humanism), geopolitical (marking a civilizational boundary against authoritarian or traditionalist alternatives), and procedural (referring to institutional norms such as rule of law, secular governance, and free expression). My claim is that these registers require incompatible social ontologies: liberal individualism undermines the thick communal identity presupposed by geopolitical usage, while procedural norms are now adopted by societies that explicitly reject the genealogical narrative. To formalize this, I introduce a "triangular incoherence test" for value-categories: if a term must invoke contradictory accounts of the moral subject and of institutional legitimacy across its standard uses, it lacks conceptual integrity and should be treated as performative boundary-work rather than descriptive classification. I apply the test to "Western values" and conclude that it fails, though I acknowledge that defenders might retreat to a minimal core of human rights.

Specifically, I would welcome comments on three points. Third, where does my argument stand in relation to the standard claim that "Western values" is not a category error but a rhetorical instrument whose coherence is pragmatic rather than conceptual? I am particularly unsure about the second point, as I have not fully worked out whether procedural norms can be thickened without collapsing back into one of the other registers.
