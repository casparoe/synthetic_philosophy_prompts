everyone in my metaphysics circles keeps using the bus-route analogy for universals: the route exists independantly of any passenger, different people get on at different stops (particulars instantiating the property at different times), and the schedule can be empty (so realism handles properties like being-a-unicorn with no riders). they lean on it to defend realism about properties, especially against trope theorists who say every ride is just its own private trip with no shared line. can you lay out the correspondence table — route map, bus company, passengers, empty schedule, missed connections — then show the disanalgies? specifically, does it smuggle in realism by treating the route as a real entity rather than a pattern? and how does it handle disjunctive or negative properties — is being-non-red just a route that goes everywhere except one stop? finally, does the conclusion that realism handles 'empty' universals better actually survive, or was the analogy carrying the whole argument?
