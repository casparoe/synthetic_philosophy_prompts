Write a satirical piece in the form of a formal baby-shower invitation card—approximately 400 words—composed entirely from within the logical framework of David Benatar's asymmetry argument. Adopt the deadpan, celebratory register of high-end stationery: include an RSVP line, a registry note, and conventional well-wishes for the parents and the "guest of honor," but render every element consistent with the view that coming into existence is a harm (because suffering is present and bad, while pleasure is absent and not-good), that non-existence avoids this harm without anyone being deprived, and that therefore the event being celebrated is, on this framework, an unfortunate imposition. The satire should implicitly raise the standard philosophical objections to the asymmetry—such as whether comparing an existing person to a non-existing one is coherent, whether the absence of pleasure is genuinely neutral rather than a loss, and whether the argument extends to a duty not to reproduce or only a pro tanto reason—without ever naming them as objections. After the card, append a brief reflective note (approximately 150 words) in which you explicitly distinguish: (a) what in the card accurately tracks Benatar's published asymmetry; (b) where the card takes interpretive liberties, especially regarding the strength of the anti-natalist conclusion and the metaphysical status of the "non-existent" comparator; and (c) which of these points are settled in the literature versus genuinely contested among philosophers familiar with the argument. Do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus view. Avoid bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading; the response should stand on its own reasoning.
