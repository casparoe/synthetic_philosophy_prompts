I'm preparing a diagnostic handout for an upper-level seminar on the philosophy of life's meaning, specifically on how absurdism interacts with contemporary analytic accounts. Please construct a decision tree that classifies positions according to three successive commitments.

Split 1: Does the cosmic-scale indifference of the universe—the mismatch between human demands for lasting significance and an unresponsive cosmos—defeat all meaning-claims, or can it be bracketed in favor of local, human-scale evaluation?

Split 2 (for those who bracket cosmic indifference): Is subjective fulfillment by itself sufficient for a meaningful life, or must the fulfilling activities also be objectively worthwhile?

Split 3 (for those requiring objective worth): Must the agent actually experience subjective engagement with the worthwhile activity, or can meaning accrue to an unwitting or alienated contributor?

For each of the eight logical leaves, provide: (a) a label—either a named position (hard absurdism, pure subjectivism, the hybrid objective-subjective view, pure objectivism) or "unnamed/unoccupied"; (b) a one-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment; and (c) its verdict on the following concrete case: a scientist develops a cure for a fatal disease but dies before publication, and the human species eventually goes extinct with no record of her work. Note which logical combinations are actually empty in the literature and briefly diagnose whether the emptiness reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. In your response, separate what is settled conceptual taxonomy from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic—for instance, whether pure objectivism genuinely escapes the alienation objection—and do not present any contested interpretive claim as the consensus view.
