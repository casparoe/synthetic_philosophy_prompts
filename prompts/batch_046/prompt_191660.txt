In my philosophy of life reading group we keep circling Schlick's claim that meaning comes only from "play" — activities done for their own sake with no external purpse. One faction says this can't cover moral obligations like caring for aging parents, since those are clearly purpse-driven and exhuasting. The other faction insists Schlick allows them, because "play" just means you're not instumentally calculating outcomes, so parenting counts if you're not keeping score. I think the real stall is hidden: both sides are actually fighting over whether meaning needs a kind of second-order transparency, where not only are you free of external goals, but there's common knowledge among everyone present that nobody's treating it as strategic. Is our disagreement really about Schlick's text, or is it verbal, or about bedrock intuitions on naturalness? Give me a diagnosis with reasons — not a survey of Schlick — and tell me what, if anything, could actually move us.
