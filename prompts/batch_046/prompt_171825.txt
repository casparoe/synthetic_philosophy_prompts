A philosopher of art has delivered consistent verdicts across five cases. First, she maintains that Richard Wagner's documented anti-Semitism does not diminish the aesthetic value of the Ring cycle, because the vice is biographically external to the work's internal musical and dramatic structure. Second, she holds that a state-sponsored propaganda film can possess genuine cinematic merit—innovative montage, for instance—despite being morally toxic, since aesthetic achievement and moral message can be evaluated separately. Third, she judges that a poet's documented personal cruelty does not reduce the value of poems that neither express nor depend upon that cruelty. Fourth, she argues that a photographer's series aestheticizing the vulnerability of models is diminished in value once we learn the subjects were coerced, because the harm is not external but constitutive of the aesthetic experience itself. Fifth, she insists that an artist's hidden criminal acts, if entirely unconnected to the work's meaning or conditions of production, should not affect evaluation at all.

Now consider a new case: an immersive virtual-reality installation titled The Confession, created by an artist recently convicted of large-scale financial fraud. The installation places viewers inside a simulated prison cell where they must choose between confessing a fabricated sin and receiving virtual rewards. The artist funded the work entirely with proceeds from the fraud, and the guiding voice heard throughout is synthesized directly from her actual prison correspondence. Critics have praised the installation for its ethical complexity and its immersive design.

Would my interlocutor judge the work's aesthetic value to be diminished?

Your task is not merely to answer yes or no. Identify at least two distinct principles—name them however you wish—that are jointly consistent with her five prior verdicts. State precisely what each principle predicts about The Confession. Give your best guess as to which principle she is actually running, with reasons. Then specify one further hypothetical case that would cleanly distinguish the two principles, explaining exactly why it forces them apart.
