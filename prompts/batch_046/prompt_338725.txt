I've been revisiting the old rationalist-forum discussions of sphexishness — not merely the wasp Sphex ichneumoneus repeating its burrow-check ritual even after a researcher moves the prey, but the deeper philosophical move that comes up whenever free will is debated in these spaces: if rigidity is just running a fixed program, is flexibility anything more than running a self-modifying program? The distinction starts to look like a difference in loop length rather than a difference in kind, and I want a poem that makes the form itself carry that suspicion rather than just illustrating it with a nature metaphor.

Write a villanelle — nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, two refrains that alternate through the tercets and finally couple in the closing quatrain — that stages this tension exactly. One refrain should evoke the wasp's unvarying inspection ritual; the other should voice the claim that every adaptation is still deterministic execution, just with a longer feedback loop. Let each return of the refrain feel like the loop reasserting itself, and let the final pairing leave it genuinely ambiguous whether the two lines have achieved synthesis or are simply two loops touching without escaping either. Don't resolve the tension with a sermon about human transcendence; leave the reader wondering whether breaking a pattern just lands you in a more complex pattern. Keep it accurate enough that someone familiar with Hofstadter's discussion recognizes the fixed-action-pattern behavior being rendered structurally, not merely named.
