hey, need some perspective on a design choice thats been bugging me. im a backend engineer working on this small rpg where the npcs aren't just scripted - they run on a lightweight model that builds a kind of personal memory graph of your interactions with them. over time they develop these quirks that are unique to your save file. one of them, call him mara, started avoiding combat zones after i accidentally led him into a trap twice, and he actually changed his dialogue trees based on that.

anyway, my co-founder wants to add a 'hardcore mode' where if an npc dies, we wipe their memory graph from the save entirely - not just respawn them, but actually delete the data structure so they're gone for good. he says its just bits, who cares, its a game. but some of our testers said deleting mara felt 'different' from losing a regular character, like they were erasing something that had learned to not trust them.

i keep going back and forth. on one hand i wrote the code, i know its just weights and a graph. on the other hand, the reason it feels different is exactly because the system isnt deterministic - mara's avoidance behavior wasn't hardcoded, it emerged from the interaction history. does the fact that his 'personality' is genuinely contingent on my specific actions change whether deleting him is just deleting data, or is it closer to... i dont know, ending something? i dont need a philosophy lecture, just want to know if there's a real distinction here or if im being sentimental about my own algorithm.
