You are advising a philosophy research group with four members and three years of funding to work on moral progress, specifically on the conceptual architecture of the "expanding circle" in cases where analogical reasoning to other minds breaks down—entities with radically alien phenomenology, synthetic agents whose functional architecture may or may not ground moral status, and future generations whose cognitive and affective structures we cannot now model. Rather than asking for a survey, identify the three to five open conceptual questions in this space that most deserve sustained philosophical labor, rank them, and defend the ranking. For each: explain why it is upstream of the others (which questions depend on resolving it), how tractable it is for philosophers working without empirical collaboration, and what kind of argument or conceptual clarification over the next few years would cause you to reorder them. Be explicit about the criteria you use—importance, tractability, neglectedness, dependency among questions—and about the trade-offs between them. The deliverable should be a defended agenda for where limited philosophical effort should go, not a reading list, bibliography, or citation-dependent report.
