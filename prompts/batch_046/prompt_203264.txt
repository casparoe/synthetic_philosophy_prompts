I am moderating a panel discussion for a postgraduate philosophy series, and my next guest is a philosopher who has recently advanced a position she calls 'modal reference-fixing rationalism' concerning rigid designators. She maintains that a rigid designator is governed not by a bare causal-historical chain but by an implicit structural description—part of full linguistic competence—that specifies the conditions under which the term tracks its referent across worlds. On her account, once a competent speaker grasps this structural description, she can derive the necessity of true identity statements—such as that Hesperus is Phosphorus, or that water is H2O—through pure reasoning about reference-fixing mechanisms, without further empirical investigation. The empirical task is confined to identifying which actual-world object satisfies the conditions. She therefore treats Kripke's necessary a posteriori as largely an artefact of conflating discovery of the satisfier with discovery of modal status.

Please draft a sequence of seven interview questions that target the weakest joints of this view, anticipating the replies she is most likely to offer and supplying a sharper follow-up for each. I require at least one question addressing each of the following: (i) whether the account collapses into the very descriptivism that Kripke's causal theory was designed to avoid; (ii) how it treats proper names introduced through ostensive baptism or deferred reference, where no explicit structural conditions are available; (iii) whether empirically loaded theoretical identities—e.g., that heat is mean kinetic energy—can genuinely be rendered a priori once the underlying structural descriptions are recognised to be themselves theory-laden; and (iv) what the view implies for mathematical identities, where there are no causal-historical baptismal chains to ground the structural descriptions. The questions should be formulated for a specialist audience but must not request citations, bibliographies, or reading lists. For each proposed question, include a brief indication of the anticipated reply and the corresponding follow-up.
