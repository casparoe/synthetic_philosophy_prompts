Ever since my mom had to choose between two seperate treatment plans for my dad—both expensive, only one covered—I can't stop thinking about how moral theories deal with numbers. I read somewhere about Scanlon's contractualism, where an action is wrong if anyone could reasonably reject it, and people say it fails on aggregation: you can't justify saving five by harming one because each of the five can reject being left to die, but the one can reject being killed, and there's no counting mechanism. The debate about whether this kills contractualism has been going in circles for decades, and I'm trying to figure out if it's actually a deep disagreement or just people using reasonable differently. Is the stall happening because one side thinks justification is pairwise and the other thinks it's collective? Or is there a hidden assumption about whether moral reasons are additive? I want a diagnosis—verbal, normative, or formal—of what's really blocking progress here, not a list of papers. Also, I definetly don't care about citations, just whether a new distinction or formal result could break the deadlock or if we're permanently stuck talking past eachother.
