I work as a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, but I have been trying to make sense of Zhuangzi’s account of death—specifically the idea that dying is not annihilation but a transformation within the continuous flow of qi, analogous to seasonal change. From a systems perspective, this sounds like a distributed architecture in which individual nodes fail and are replaced without destroying the overall network state; the systemic information persists even though particular instantiations dissolve. That leads me to think that if Zhuangzi genuinely holds that death is only a reconfiguration of qi, he must be implicitly committed to some form of substrate-independent continuity, where the pattern that constituted a person is preserved and redistributed rather than erased. If that reading is correct, then his view seems closer to a materialist, non-theistic version of informational survival or reincarnation than to the total self-dissolution that commentators usually attribute to him. So my question is: does Zhuangzi’s transformation thesis actually entail that personal identity survives death in a redistributed form, and are scholars mistaken to treat his position as accepting the complete disappearance of the self rather than as an early process-philosophy account of pattern preservation across physical change?
