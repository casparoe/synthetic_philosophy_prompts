I am preparing a postgraduate seminar on the metaphysics of practical reason, and I want you to design a diagnostic taxonomy — structured as a flowchart-style decision tree with an accompanying scoring key — that sorts philosophical positions on whether normative reasons can exist independently of any actual or possible reasoning being. The topic is sometimes framed as the problem of “free-floating rationales”, but I want to avoid standard textbook labels and instead build the instrument from concrete commitments and cases, so that someone could answer the branch questions without knowing the names of the views.

Please begin by thinking carefully and outlining a brief preliminary section in which you map the genuinely independent logical dimensions of the debate — for instance, whether reason-existence requires an actual mental state, a counterfactual reasoning process, or neither. Then construct the tree.

The root should split on two concrete diagnostic questions. First: can a normative reason apply to an agent — say, a long-extinct hominid species whose ecological conditions we now understand, or a future artificial system that operates without propositional attitudes — who never has and never will form the corresponding mental representation? Second: if such a reason does apply, does its existence depend on there being somewhere an actual or counterfactual reasoning process that could register it, or can it obtain purely as a structural feature of the situation?

From these branches, develop at least six leaf positions, which must include: (1) a strong reasoner-dependence view that treats reasons as essentially mental-state-dependent; (2) an ideal-observer or counterfactual-reasoner view; (3) a fully source-independent structural realist view; (4) an institutional or practice-embedded view in which reasons depend on social structures rather than on individual minds; (5) an error-theoretic view that denies free-floating reasons but also denies that reasoners create them ex nihilo; and (6) one hybrid or logically possible but unexplored position occupying a gap in the standard space. For each branch point, specify the concrete claim or case that forces the separation.

I also want you to flag combinations of answers that appear unoccupied — for example, a view that accepts source-independent structural reasons yet insists they can only be discovered by actual reasoners — and briefly diagnose whether each gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Where the tree fails to separate two positions cleanly, say so explicitly and explain which diagnostic question collapses the distinction.

Please adopt the role of a professional philosopher of normativity with expertise in metaethics and metaphysics. Write in British English. At the end, state how confident you are in the taxonomy as a whole and single out the one specific point about which you are least certain — whether that concerns the coherence of one hybrid branch, the diagnosis of an empty cell, or the framing of the root questions themselves.
