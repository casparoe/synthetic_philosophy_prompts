Consider the paired conceptual inversions associated with Darwin and Turing: Darwin showed that biological competence—functional design, adaptive fit—can arise through natural selection without any comprehending agent, thereby separating the existence of design from a designer's understanding; Turing showed, through his analysis of computation and the imitation game, that behavioral and cognitive competence can be produced by mechanistic processes that need not involve consciousness, semantic understanding, or internal comprehension. Together, these cases suggest that competence without comprehension may be the norm rather than the exception, yet they also expose an asymmetry: evolutionary competence is historical, distributed across populations, and substrate-bound in living lineages, whereas Turing-style competence is functional, substrate-independent, and reproducible in artifacts.
