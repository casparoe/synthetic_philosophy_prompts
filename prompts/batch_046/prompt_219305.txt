Write an essay on the following controversy: in debates about free will and determinism, philosophers often deploy intuition pumps that depend on what might be called modal proximity—the intuitive pull of a possible world that is causally near our own but differs at a single nodal point. The pump asks whether we should regard the switchboard as an agent that genuinely intervenes, or merely as another deterministic gear in the machinery. Consider whether the persuasive force of the pump comes from a tacit essentialism about causal closure rather than from any clear feature of agency, and whether modal-proximity pumps in general conflate causal nearness with explanatory relevance. Reach a definite conclusion—should philosophers continue to use modal-proximity intuition pumps in the free-will literature, or should they be abandoned as structurally misleading?—and defend your verdict with careful reasoning.
