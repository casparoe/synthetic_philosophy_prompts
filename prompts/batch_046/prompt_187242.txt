I've been reflecting on Rawls's separateness of persons objection to utilitarianism—the claim that treating welfare as aggregable across lives ignores that each person is a distinct center of consciousness with their own existence to lead. It seems to me that this objection tacitly depends on a metaphysical isolation thesis: that one person's suffering and another's happiness are incommensurable because they are locked inside separate biological containers. But direct neural-interface technologies now make coupled affective states across brains conceivable—if two minds could literally share pleasure and pain in real time, wouldn't the physical basis for that "separateness" dissolve? Give me a committed answer, in American English: is the objection just a contingent artifact of unconnected brains, or does it survive independently even when intersubjective neural coupling makes welfare genuinely fungible across persons?
