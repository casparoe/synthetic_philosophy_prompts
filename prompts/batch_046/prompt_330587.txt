i’m writing the final for my upper-level epistemology seminar (mixed grad and strong undergrads, 90 minutes, about 600-800 word answers) and i want one essay question on peer disagreement that can’t be answered by just summarizing Elga or Christensen. i want to inject a specific asymetric case so students actually have to do work.

the setup: A and B are epistemic peers about claim p. A knows B is a peer, but A also has private access to the fact that B’s confidence in not-p spiked only after B spoke with C — and A knows C is unreliable in this domain, though B doesn’t realize that. B knows nothing about C’s unreliability or that A sees this. So they disagree, but the second-order evidence is lopsided.

please write:
1. the exam question itself, asking whether conciliationism forces A to reduce confidence in p, whether steadfastness is available, and whether the answer splits if we distinguish revising the first-order belief from revising our assessment of B’s reliablity. have them confront the “double-tracking” worry that conciliation might apply to B’s epistemic status without applying to p.
2. a model answer of about 600 words.
3. a marking scheme with five must-hit moves, the marks each is worth (total 25), and a one-line note on how students typically miss each.

important: in the model answer and scheme, seperate what’s pretty settled in the debate from what’s genuinely contested — don’t present a contested claim (like whether conciliationism is self-undermining) as if it’s the consensus view. also, no bibliographies or reading lists; this is a closed-book exam and i don’t want students guessing at citations. feel free to include a typo or two, this isn’t being published.
