We keep finding that the view is either dismissed as mystical obscurantism or domesticated into a trivial form of panpsychism that loses the specific Advaita commitment to non-duality. Rather than another historical exposition, I want a specification of what a genuinely adequate contemporary theory built around this unity thesis would have to deliver if it hoped to be empirically accountable and conceptually coherent.

Please write the desiderata that such a theory must meet. For each requirement, state the phenomenon, distinction, or empirical constraint it addresses, explain why it is a genuine desideratum rather than a prejudice of one camp, and flag whether it is non-negotiable or merely a preference (for instance, physicalist commitments about local supervenience, or dualist demands for an immaterial substrate). In particular, include: how it must account for the phenomenology of split-brain patients and personal identity over time without multiplying subjects; what distinctions it must preserve between first-person access, third-person observation, and functional integration; which results from quantum decoherence theory and neuroscience it must be consistent with without importing the mathematics wholesale; and how, if at all, it can make sense of moral agency and interpersonal obligation when there is technically only one subject. Also indicate whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — for example, whether maintaining strict empirical accountability automatically collapses the view into eliminativism about multiplicity.
