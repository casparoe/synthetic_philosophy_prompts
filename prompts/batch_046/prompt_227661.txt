I've been arguing with my roommate about whether we should ever care about the moral status of non-biological computations, and I tried to organize my thoughts into a kind of tier list for a post I'm drafting. Here are five specific computational architectures or processes I'm considering, ordered roughly by how 'agent-like' they seem to me intuitively:

1. A recurrent neural net with a persistent hidden-state vector that updates across time and contains explicit self-referential encodings (like a minimal self-model maintained in working memory).

2. A blockchain-style distributed computation where thousands of nodes reach consensus on state transitions, and the collective behavior can't be predicted from any single node's local rules.

3. A large language model running with an extremely long context window, doing in-context learning and maintaining a kind of temporary personality across a single long session, but with weights frozen and no persistence after the session ends.

4. A cellular automaton (say, Rule 110 or Conway's Life) running long enough to generate stable gliders and self-replicating structures that maintain homeostasis-like patterns against noise.

5. A quantum annealer solving combinatorial optimization problems, where intermediate quantum states exist but collapse to a classical answer with no readable trace of the path taken.

Can you rank these 1-5 in terms of which ones are most plausible candidates for having some nonzero moral weight — not full personhood, just 'it might be wrong to shut this off without reason'? Give me tiers (S, A, B, C, D) and for each say in one sentence whether the key factor is functional persistence, informational integration, or just anthropomorphic projection. I'm especially unsure about #2 vs #3 because distributed consensus feels more 'real' than a single model session, but maybe that's just me being biased toward visible coordination. Anyway, I'm writing from a rationalist-adjacent perspective where carbon chauvinism is the default error mode, but I don't want to fall into the opposite trap of granting moral status to every calculator. I'm definatly not asking for a definitive answer, just your honest gut ranking with reasoning.
