I serve as a classification officer on the review board of a synthetic-biology and astrobiology oversight committee, where we must decide—typically within a thirty-minute session and with sign-off required from a non-specialist chair—whether ambiguous entities qualify as living for quarantine and funding-eligibility purposes. The board sees everything from dormant microbial spores recovered from permafrost to engineered lipid-vesicle networks and server-based evolutionary simulations, so I need a reusable procedural checklist, not a one-time philosophical opinion. The procedure should translate core philosophical accounts of life—particularly organizational closure (autopoietic self-production of boundaries), metabolic thermodynamic openness with internal regulation, and evolutionary capacity involving heritable variation under selection—into an ordered set of practical tests that a trained but non-philosophical officer can apply consistently. It must preserve the conceptual distinctions that actually determine classification—such as whether boundary maintenance is generated by the system's own processes versus scaffolded externally, whether replication entails selectable heritable change rather than mere copying, and whether energy flow is regulated by the entity itself—while explicitly dropping distinctions that do not affect the regulatory outcome. The checklist should include no more than five ordered steps, identify the hard borderline cases it is designed to handle (dormant spores, obligate parasitic replicators lacking independent metabolism, synthetic protocells with engineered catalytic cycles but no evolutionary history, proteinaceous replicators, and digital ecosystems exhibiting organizational closure only in software), and flag precisely where judgment cannot be eliminated—especially where evolutionary history and present organizational closure come into conflict. Please apply the procedure in detail to three cases: a dormant bacterial endospore that shows no measurable metabolism for years but can resume; a synthetic protocell that actively maintains its lipid boundary through an internal catalytic network designed de novo; and a server-hosted digital ecosystem that exhibits self-modifying organizational closure in its code architecture but possesses no material metabolism. At least one application should yield a verdict that runs against intuitive expectation—state clearly which step forces the counterintuitive classification and why the underlying framework demands it despite common intuition. Finally, specify the exact decision nodes—particularly those weighing evolutionary origin against present functional autonomy—where the board chair must exercise irreducible discretion, and indicate what kind of empirical or conceptual evidence could shift that discretion without resolving it mechanically.
