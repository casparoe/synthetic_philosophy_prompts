I hold a specific philosophical interpretation of computational complexity: the verification-discovery asymmetry captured by the P versus NP framework is not merely a practical constraint on modeling, but a structural feature that undermines any general philosophical theory of explanation—whether covering-law, causal-mechanical, or unificationist—that implicitly assumes an efficient, tractable path from the existence of an explanation to its identification or validation for arbitrarily complex phenomena. I further maintain that this asymmetry damages rationalist epistemologies that presuppose a symmetry between the conditions for justifying a belief and those for discovering it, since discovery in complex domains may be structurally intractable even where verification is not. What would genuinely count against this position? I want specific defeaters rather than generic skepticism: formal results or conceptual arguments that collapse the verification-discovery distinction specifically for explanatory hypotheses; demonstrations that explanation is not the kind of process that could ever be algorithmized, rendering NP-hardness irrelevant rather than undermining; empirical or conceptual cases in which genuinely complex explanations have been arrived at or confirmed with unexpected efficiency; and internal distinctions within philosophy of science—such as between local and global explanation, or between explanatory power and explanatory accessibility—that allow one of the standard theories to survive the asymmetry intact. For each category, indicate whether a committed defender of my view would concede it as damaging or dismiss it by refining the claim’s scope. Rank these defeaters from fatal—those that would require abandoning the core asymmetry thesis—to merely awkward adjustments in formulation. Finally, assess whether the position risks unfalsifiability: if every successful complex explanation can be redescribed as falling outside the relevant complexity class, or every failure attributed to contingent limits rather than structural ones, does that insulation strengthen or weaken the view?
