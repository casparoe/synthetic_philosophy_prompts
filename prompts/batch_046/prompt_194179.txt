Write a poem of roughly 18–22 lines in blank verse that stages the strange loop of Escher’s Drawing Hands—two hands drawing each other on a sheet of paper—while introducing a specific conceptual twist not found in standard accounts: treat the paper’s tear, a rupture that neither hand can draw and that breaks the recursive symmetry, as the inviolate level that makes the loop possible. In the first half (about lines 1–10), use a first-person plural voice (“we”) to speak from inside the loop, describing the mutual drawing without outside ground; in the second half (about lines 11–20), shift to a first-person singular (“I”) that belongs to the tear itself, describing how absence holds the hands apart. Let the turn occur precisely at the line introducing the tear. The poem should be accurate to the idea that every tangled hierarchy requires a level untouched by the rules above it—here not a foundation but a rupture—so that a reader familiar with the study of strange loops and inviolate levels recognizes the structure, yet the specific treatment of absence-as-level should feel like a fresh extension rather than a summary. After the poem, include one titled section, “Commentary on choices made,” about four to six sentences long, in which you state how confident you are in the philosophical accuracy of depicting the inviolate level as an absence or rupture, and explicitly identify the point about which you are least sure—whether an absence can genuinely serve as an inviolate level in the original framework, or whether that framing distorts the idea of a substrate or rule-system that lies outside the loop.
