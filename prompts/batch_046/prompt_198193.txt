question: some evolutionary psychologists claim that because humans evolved stronger altruism toward kin, we have a direct moral duty to prioritize family members over strangers. analyze this claim using hume's is-ought gap and moore's naturalistic fallacy. be precise about how the two objections differ. also address whether searle's institutional-fact counterexample saves the argument.

student answer:
first, hume's is-ought gap shows that descriptive premises about evolved kin preference cant logically entail a normative conclusion about direct duty. second, moore's naturalistic fallacy, which he lays out in his ethics book, shows that identifying moral duty with any natural property like evolutionary fitness is open to question. since the argument defines our direct duty in terms of fitness, it commits this fallacy.

the two criticisms differ in focus: hume targets the inference, moore targets the definition. but they reach the same result.

searle's counterexample shows that institutional facts, like promises, contain an ought within an is. kinship is a social institution, so evolutionary data about kin preference actually describes an institutional fact. that means we can derive the direct duty from the descriptive premise, and the argument survives.
