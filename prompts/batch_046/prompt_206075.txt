Two people in my study group got stuck arguing about omissions and causation with this made-up case I call the shadow valve — there's an automated water system with a backup sensor that fails to trigger, but only fails because a secondary monitoring loop was disabled earlier. One said the disabled loop is the real cause of the flood, not the sensor failure, since if the loop had been active it would've intercepted; the other said that's wrong, omissions only cause when there's a specific duty or mechanism making the absence salient, and the disabled loop was just background conditions, not an omission in the relevant sense. Then she brought in this idea she calls counterfactual stability — if you vary the omission across nearby worlds and the outcome tracks it, it's causal. He said that's just regular counterfactual dependence and doesn't solve selection since there are infinite omissions. She replied stability filters the noise: the loop's absence is stable across perturbations, but the color of the technician's shirt isn't. He said stability is an epistemic filter, not ontological, so she's smuggling knowledge conditions into causation. Neither would budge. Whos right about which specific point, and what are they both missing?
