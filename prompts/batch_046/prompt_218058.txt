I'm writing up my philosophical profile on quantum ontology for a reading group and want a consistency audit before I present. Here are my commitments:

(1) I accept the Pusey-Barrett-Rudolph result that, given a preparation-independence assumption, psi-epistemic models are incompatible with the quantum formalism, so I take the wavefunction to be at least partly ontic.

(2) I also hold a relationalist view: quantum states don't describe intrinsic properties of isolated systems but rather relations between a system and its environment or reference frame, in the spirit of relational quantum mechanics.

(3) I think decoherence solves the measurement problem by selecting a preferred basis through environmental interaction, without invoking wavefunction collapse or hidden variables.

(4) I deny that quantum completeness—the absence of hidden variables—means the wavefunction represents all physical reality; I allow for structural or relational facts that the wavefunction doesn't fully encode.

(5) I think the wavefunction's ontological status is analogous to a Hamiltonian in classical mechanics: it is a real feature of the world, but not a substance or field inhabiting spacetime.

Please go through these commitments and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and rank the tensions by severity. For each real tension, specify the least costly revision and what it costs me in terms of my other commitments. Then tell me whether I'm missing any hidden tension—especially between (1) and (2), and between (3) and (4)—and whether my package is jointly tenable with only minor adjustments.

Finally, please separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among specialists in philosophy of physics: do not present the interpretation of the PBR result, the viability of relational quantum mechanics, or the sufficiency of decoherence as if they were consensus views. Be explicit about where experts disagree and flag the points at which I'm adopting a minority or contested stance.
