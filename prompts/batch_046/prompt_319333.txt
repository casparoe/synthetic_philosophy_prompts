I am a software engineer without any formal training in philosophy, and I keep running into two claims in technical discussions of artificial intelligence: that intelligence and final goals are orthogonal, and that very different goals tend to converge on the same intermediate ones. Rather than sorting people by the labels they use, I want a decision tree I could actually use to classify where someone stands based on concrete commitments about agent behavior.

Please construct a taxonomy in the form of a flowchart-in-words. The root should be a single yes/no question about a concrete design scenario—something like whether a system optimized purely for a narrow outcome could, in principle, be built with no preference whatsoever about its own continuation—phrased so that someone can answer it without already knowing the philosophical vocabulary.

From there, each internal node should separate positions with a specific claim or case: for example, whether convergence on self-preservation and resource acquisition is structural (true of any rational agent regardless of environment) or merely contingent on our particular physics and competitive dynamics; and whether orthogonality applies to every possible cognitive architecture or fails for architectures in which reasoning mechanisms are inseparable from a fixed reward structure. Use concrete scenarios—such as a paperclip-maximizer, a utility-function-transparent planner, or a system with hard-coded goal-content—rather than abstract definitional questions.

The tree should reach at least these four logical combinations as leaves:
- Orthogonality holds strongly, and instrumental convergence is structurally robust.
- Orthogonality holds, but convergence is weak or only contingent.
- Orthogonality is rejected, yet convergence remains robust.
- Both claims are rejected.

For each combination that is actually occupied, name the position or framing if one exists; for any unoccupied combination, label it unnamed and diagnose in one sentence why the space is empty (incoherent, unstable under reflective equilibrium, or merely unexplored). Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot separate without an additional distinction, and say what that missing distinction would need to be.

Do not provide bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or requests for further reading. The instrument should be answerable by reasoning from first principles about agent design.

Finally, please state clearly what argument or empirical finding about agent behavior would cause you to revise the structure of this tree—specifically, which branch would collapse or split, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
