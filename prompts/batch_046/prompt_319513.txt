Since a recent discussion of structural realism made me worry that we treat empirically equivalent ontologies inconsistently, I have sketched an argument that now looks like it proves far too much, and I want to locate the failure. (1) If two ontological frameworks O and O* are empirically equivalent—no possible observation, however refined, could distinguish them—then rational credence should not discriminate between them, because belief ought to track only accessible differences. (2) Consider a continuous physical situation that admits two coordinate representations, R and R', where selecting R over R' corresponds to no observable distinction; this is structurally parallel to gauge freedom and to the hidden parameter spaces that generate Bertrand-style measure ambiguities. (3) By the principle of indifference applied across our accessible information, since we cannot determine whether R or R' is the "true" parameterization, we must distribute credence uniformly over both representations at once. (4) Yet a uniform distribution over R assigns probability p to event E, while a uniform distribution over R' assigns probability q ≠ p to the same event E, because the measure of the region corresponding to E differs between representations. (5) Therefore, if ontology is genuinely underdetermined by accessible information, then for any symmetric continuous space we are rationally compelled to assign contradictory probabilities to one and the same event. (6) That is incoherent, so we must reject (1) and allow rational belief to discriminate on the basis of inaccessible ontological structure. (7) But once we permit that, there is no principled barrier to preferring, for example, a deterministic hidden-variable ontology that reproduces all quantum predictions, since parsimony cannot do the work—parsimony is usually defended precisely by appealing to the avoidance of empirically redundant entities. Hence either we embrace contradiction in our probabilities, or we open the door to arbitrary ontological inflation. Which step should I abandon? I would prefer to keep (1) intact and avoid contradiction without surrendering to inflation, but I need to know exactly what I am paying to do so.
