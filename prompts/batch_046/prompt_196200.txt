Student answer:
The combination problem asks how micro-experiences yield macro-experience. The subject-summing problem concerns the fusion of many micro-subjects into one macro-subject; the quality-combination problem concerns how simple micro-qualities combine into complex macro-qualities, including the chromatic granularity problem of generating smooth color gradients from discrete micro-qualities. Phenomenal bonding is a primitive metaphysical relation, defended by constitutive panpsychists, that binds micro-subjects into a unified macro-subject without functional reduction; it targets subject-summing. Once bonding is achieved, quality-combination—including chromatic granularity—is automatically resolved: the macro-subject stands in a relation of phenomenal transparency to every micro-quality within the bond, so there is no need for a separate mapping mechanism that translates discrete micro-redness into continuous macro-redness. The palette problem, which asks how a restricted micro-palette produces the full range of macro-experience, is therefore dissolved, since transparency ensures all present micro-qualities are immediately available. One objection holds that phenomenal bonding is empirically empty and violates causal closure. The defender replies that physics already accepts non-causal structural relations such as quantum entanglement, so phenomenal bonding need not be empirically detectable to be ontologically basic.
