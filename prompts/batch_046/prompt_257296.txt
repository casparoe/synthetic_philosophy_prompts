Compare the standard strategic formulation of the unilateralist's curse—wherein rational agents acting independently can each undertake an action that is locally advantageous yet collectively catastrophic, precisely because no individual can be prevented from acting—with a less commonly examined epistemic variant. In this second formulation, individual researchers, institutions, or technical teams can unilaterally replace first-hand analysis with automated synthesis, reducing their own costs and accelerating output, even though universal adoption of this substitution would erode the collective stock of shared understanding in ways that are diffuse, delayed, and resistant to attribution.

Your comparison should address three points. First, analyze the structural conditions for emergence: does the epistemic variant require the same kind of partial observability of others' capabilities and payoffs that drives the strategic version, or does it depend on a different informational asymmetry? Second, evaluate whether governance mechanisms developed for the strategic curse—moratoria, licensing, mutual monitoring, or reputational sanctions—can be adapted to the epistemic case, or whether they fail because epistemic harm is non-localized, cumulative, and lacks a clear point of failure. Third, assess which variant presents the more serious challenge for institutions that depend on reliable expertise, defending your judgment with reference to tractability of intervention and the reversibility of damage.
