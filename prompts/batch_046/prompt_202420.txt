I've been working through a seminar on early analytic metaethics and recently read an unnamed essay that summarizes G. E. Moore's treatment of intrinsic goods in Principia Ethica. The author grants that Moore lists both personal affection—what the essay calls friendship—and aesthetic appreciation among the greatest goods we can know, but then draws a sharp structural distinction between them. On the essay's reading, aesthetic value is governed by Moore's principle of organic unity: the experience of contemplating a beautiful painting together with a close friend produces an emergent value that is not merely the sum of the solitary aesthetic pleasure plus the social pleasure, because the combination acquires a qualitatively different intrinsic character. By contrast, the essay claims that Moore treats friendship itself as fully decomposable: its total value is nothing more than the aggregate of the pleasures, cognitions, and emotional states that each party separately undergoes, weighted by their intensity and duration, with no irreducible relational property remaining once those parts are accounted for. The summary also asserts that Moore is an outright mental-state subjectivist about beauty—he allegedly holds that a landscape or artwork has no value whatsoever unless it is actually being perceived, and that he extends this dependency to friendship, which implies that an unreciprocated affection carries literally no intrinsic worth. Something about this feels uneven to me. I know Moore resisted reducing all value to pleasure, and his well-known attack on idealism suggests he was wary of making reality depend on mental states in exactly this way, yet the essay is written with considerable technical precision and cites distinctions I had not considered. Is this a fair summary of Moore's position? Please separate what is accurate from what is a plausible but misleading simplification, and identify what Moore explicitly denied, particularly regarding whether friendship and beauty share the same axiological structure and whether value collapses into summed subjective states.
