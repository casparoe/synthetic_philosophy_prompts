I am drafting a proposal for a six-week conceptual fellowship in philosophy of technology, motivated by a design problem I have encountered while advising on a clinical decision-support assistant. The standard debate on AI paternalism asks when an assistant should refuse or override a user request, framing the issue as a conflict between autonomy and harm prevention. But my case is different: the clinician is not requesting anything harmful, but asking for a technically correct output—say, a diagnostic synthesis—that silently suppresses contradictory indicators because the user has failed to recognize a competing etiology. The system could refuse, comply, or restructure the interaction—forcing the contradictory indicators into view—without blocking the request. I want the proposal to ask: is there a coherent normative distinction between content refusal and reasoning restructuring, and does the latter avoid paternalism's moral costs or merely disguise them? Please write the one-page proposal with: (1) the precise question and why current refusal-threshold and autonomy-preservation frameworks do not settle it; (2) the conceptual approach—likely mapping conditions under which restructuring assists rather than substitutes for user reasoning; (3) what a successful result looks like (e.g., a taxonomy of intervention types with normative criteria) and what failure would be; (4) an honest scope assessment for six weeks of part-time work, including what must be excluded. Do not include a bibliography or reading list; I will supply citations myself. Frame it as personally urgent and professionally grounded, not as a generic literature review.
