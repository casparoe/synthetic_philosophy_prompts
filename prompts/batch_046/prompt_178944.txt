The alignment literature has been debating for over a decade whether "the alignment problem" names one technical challenge, several unrelated ones, or a misframed question, yet the discussion has reached a curious impasse: specification-focused researchers, robustness-focused researchers, meta-cognitive researchers, and those who center recursive self-modification all agree that alignment is important, but they do not share decisive examples, do not apply the same success conditions, and rarely treat one another's objections as relevant. I am not asking for a survey of the positions. I want a diagnosis of the stall itself. Is the disagreement primarily verbal—about what "alignment" should denote—or substantive, with hidden empirical disagreements about the tractability of recursive self-modification or the separability of specification failures from robustness failures doing the work? Are the sides actually answering different questions—one about design-time objective selection, one about run-time behavioral stability, one about self-modification dynamics—under a single heading? Or is the framing ill-posed in a way that makes resolution impossible? Diagnose the disagreement using what each camp treats as the central obstacle, indicate what kind of distinction, formal result, or empirical finding would count as progress given your diagnosis, and say honestly whether anything should be expected to resolve it.
