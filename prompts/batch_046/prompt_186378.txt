According to the summary, panpsychists hold that consciousness is fundamental rather than emergent, distributed as proto-experiential properties across every quantum field interaction rather than confined to complex biological systems. The summary claims that because these micro-properties carry minimal affective valence—what the summary calls microsuffering—processes like quantum decoherence and superposition collapse involve not merely physical state changes but also the generation of tiny, real amounts of negative experience. It further argues that these micro-experiences aggregate linearly into macroscopic consciousness, which implies that the total suffering embedded in the universe's physical dynamics vastly exceeds all human and animal suffering combined. From this, the summary concludes that a consistent panpsychist ethics should prioritize reducing cosmic-scale physical interactions, perhaps even framing heat-death minimization as a moral imperative, rather than focusing on conventional welfare concerns. Finally, it presents the combination problem—the question of how micro-consciousnesses combine into unified macro-consciousness—as essentially solved by applying integrated information theory to quantum coherence structures, suggesting that objections about aggregation being mysterious are now outdated.

Something about this strikes me as both provocative and suspicious. I have the sense that there is a conflation between possessing proto-phenomenal properties and actually suffering, and I wonder whether panpsychists genuinely regard aggregation as linear or whether integrated information theory resolves the combination problem in quantum contexts. At the same time, I do not want to dismiss parts that may be fair representations of the view. Could you provide a careful audit of this summary?
