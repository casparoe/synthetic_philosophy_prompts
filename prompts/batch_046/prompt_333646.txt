I think creativity is always just turning paramaters inside a conceptual space—like shifting tempo or key—while the underlying theme stays exactly the same. I also think analogical reasoning, which Hofstadter treats as the core of cognition, needs those spaces to stay structurally stable; if the knobs themselves keep getting redefined then cross-domain mapping collapses. But then I want to say that the best creative leaps aren't just turning existing knobs but unlocking ones that everyone treated as invarient—like treating time as variable rather than absolute—though once you unlock one and turn it you've technically spawned a new conceptual space, not just varied the old theme.

I also hold that there's genuinely no 'outside' to conceptual spaces at all; you never escape your framework, you only expand its parameter set. And I think artistic creativity is mostly pure twiddling within a fixed space, while scientific revolutions are mainly about breaking those locked knobs, so they're genuinely different in kind, not just degree. Which of these commitments actually pull against each other? I'm especially unsure whether my stability-requirement for anology conflicts with my celebration of breaking invariant parameters, and whether saying we spawn new spaces contradicts my claim that there's no outside. Rank the tensions by severity, flag any pairs that only look bad but dissolve on closer inspection, and tell me the least costly revision—I'm not treating any of these as sacrosanct, but I don't want to lose the idea that analogies need stable structures.
