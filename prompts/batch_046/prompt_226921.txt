Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of pleasure must meet if it is genuinely to solve the heterogeneity problem—the challenge that pleasures as diverse as tasting a ripe peach, solving a logical proof, feeling relief after escaping danger, and enjoying schadenfreude seem to share neither a uniform qualitative feel nor a single functional role. For each desideratum, identify the specific case, puzzle, or conceptual pressure that generates it, and mark whether you take it to be non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference (for instance, demands for phenomenal uniformity, intentional-object dependence, cross-modal intensity comparability, belief-independence, evolutionary coherence, or accommodation of pleasures grounded in false beliefs). Be explicit about whether the theory must explain how pleasures lacking any sensory or bodily quality can still be pleasures, and whether it must preserve the intuition that pleasures of wildly different kinds can be ranked in intensity without reducing them to one measurable dimension. Then, briefly, indicate how a pure qualitative-feel theory, a representational or desire-satisfaction theory, and a hybrid phenomenal-functional theory perform against the list, and flag any pair of desiderata that look jointly unsatisfiable.
