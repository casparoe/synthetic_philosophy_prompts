Please argue that indirect normativity, when grounded in idealised deliberation, avoids collapsing into direct normative assessment only if we accept a distinction between deliberative accessibility and procedural inheritance. Specifically, defend the claim that an agent whose evaluative framework has undergone a stable, practice-induced restructuring remains bound by norms generated under her prior idealised self, but solely within a tolerance band of psychological continuity. Address the objection that this distinction merely smuggles direct evaluation of her current psychology back in, and explain why procedural inheritance retains normative force even when the original deliberative conditions are no longer accessible.
