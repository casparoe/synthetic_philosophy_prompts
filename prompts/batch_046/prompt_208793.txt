ok so i'm writing up my philosophical profile on generations for a reading group and want a consistency check before people poke at it. here's roughly where i've landed:

1. "Gen Z" and "Millennial" are basically marketing buckets with no real ontological weight.
2. but cohorts that hit the same macro-shock during thier formative window — like being 22 in 2020 versus 35 — form a real social kind, an "imprint cohort," with shared risk preferences.
3. i hold that social kinds only count as real if they ground non-accidental counterfactuals about members.
4. i also think the boundary of an imprint cohort has to be sharp: either you had already locked in career expectations before the crash, or you hadn't.
5. at the same time i say generational boundaries are necessarily fuzzy because historical exposure is gradated and there's no magic birthday.
6. and i still want to claim Millennials and Gen Z are genuinely different kinds, even though the 1996/1997 cutoff is totally arbitary.
7. plus i think that if membership conditions are conventional or arbitrary, then the kind can't ground the counterfactuals required by (3).

which pairs are actually in tension? is (4) vs (5) just an apparent conflict that dissolves once i separate the kind from its administrative labeling? is (6) dead on arrival given (3) and (7)? rank the tensions by severity, not as if they're all equally bad. what's the least costly revision — maybe dropping sharp-boundary essentialism, or giving up on imprint cohorts altogether? and am i just smuggling in "formative-window essentialism" without defending it?
