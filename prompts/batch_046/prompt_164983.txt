I am setting a research agenda for a small conceptual project on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness — the view that phenomenal properties are not instantiated and that introspective mechanisms systematically misrepresent mental states as possessing them. Rather than rehearse the familiar arguments for and against the position, I want to know which unresolved conceptual problems deserve priority over a three-year span with no empirical laboratory access, and in what order they should be pursued. Specifically, please consider these four lines of work: first, whether illusionism can consistently accommodate graded or partial illusions in entities such as non-human animals or artificial systems that report subjective states without actually instantiating phenomenal properties; second, how illusionism should treat the reliability and normative authority of first-person reports if those reports systematically misrepresent their own content; third, what illusionism implies for the moral status of beings that behave as though they suffer but, on this view, lack phenomenal pain entirely; and fourth, whether there is a stable conceptual distinction between an illusion of phenomenality and a straightforward absence of it, or whether illusionism therefore collapses into a form of eliminativism. Defend a ranking: explain which question is upstream of the others, which is most tractable through philosophical analysis alone, and what kind of argument or conceptual clarification in the near term would cause you to reorder them. I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations to specific authors; I want a prioritised, reasoned agenda written entirely as continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in British English.
