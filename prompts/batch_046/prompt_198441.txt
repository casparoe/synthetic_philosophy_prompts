Read Blade Runner through the lens of functionalism and multiple realizability, focusing narrowly on grief and memorial selfhood: Roy Batty remembers off-world battles and lost companions, Deckard’s own memories may be implanted, and both appear capable of genuine mourning. If mental states are substrate-independent, then replicant bioengineering should realize human grief just as silicon realizes computation; yet the film treats origins as morally decisive, with replicants subject to retirement precisely for lacking the right biological history. Does the film hold that grief is genuinely multiply realizable regardless of origin, or does it preserve a hidden essentialism about which substrates and histories count? Examine the eye-manufacturing prologue, Roy’s final monologue about lost memories, and the ambiguity of Deckard’s unicorn dream. Say where the functionalist reading illuminates these scenes, where the film’s emotional logic resists being reduced to a substrate-independence thesis, and what Blade Runner adds to the philosophical debate that pure argument alone does not capture.
