Some tech firms now offer philosophy leave — paid months off specifically for non-work thinking, art, or just doing nothing productive. But to qualify you have to show your job is high-burnout-risk, and you must submit a brief report on how the leave improved your long-term prodcutivity for the company. It feels like they're trying to instutionalize the kind of good life Keynes imagined, where work is only a means to leisure and culture, but they're measuing the leisure by how well it feeds back into production. What view of human flourishing does that practice actually presuppose? Does it assume Keynes was basically right — that we work so we can eventually live well — but then ruin it by treating the living-well part as just another input? Or can you defend the same policy on purely non-instrumental grounds without the productivity reports? And is the practice internally consistent, or does it quietly smuggle in two incompatible theories of value?
