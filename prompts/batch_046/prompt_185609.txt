Your task is to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on Frankfurt cases and the principle of alternative possibilities.

Camp One — the PAP Resistors — holds that Frankfurt-style counterexamples fail to undermine PAP because they either rely on hidden deterministic mechanisms that do not genuinely eliminate all alternatives, or apply only to a narrow class of actions and do not scale to the full range of morally responsible agency.

Camp Two — the Generalizing Compatibilists — holds that the logic of Frankfurt cases extends broadly: if an agent’s choice is genuinely unforced and the only “blocked” alternatives are ones that never enter the agent’s deliberation, then the absence of those alternatives carries no moral weight, regardless of scale or architecture.

The collaboration must address a specific, self-contained scenario that moves beyond the canonical deterministic-preemption case: the Layered-Preemption Architecture. In this architecture, agent S is about to choose action Φ at decision-point t. A primary monitor M1 is present and can preemptively force Φ if S shows any neural or behavioral sign of deviating, but M1 operates with only 70% reliability and a 30% false-positive rate. Behind M1 sits a deterministic backup M2 that activates with perfect reliability only if M1 fails to intervene. S has no awareness of M1 or M2, and in the actual sequence S chooses Φ without any intervention occurring. The crux is whether, in this specific architecture, S retains a “robust” alternative possibility in the sense that PAP requires, and whether the unreliability of M1 introduces a metaphysically or morally relevant gap that saves PAP.

Also state what both sides agree would actually change at least one mind — for instance, a demonstrated failure of scaling, a clarified distinction between causal and moral possibility, or a precise formalization of “robustness” that both camps accept as decisive.
