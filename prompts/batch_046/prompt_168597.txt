Consider Gottlob Frege, who died in 1925, long before the problems of distributed digital identity and synthetic self-modeling arose. I want to know how he would apply his analysis of identity statements—specifically the distinction between the triviality of a equals a and the informativeness of a equals b—to two modern scenarios that extend his puzzle into domains he never considered.

First, in distributed ledger architectures, the same economic transaction is represented by entirely different syntactic structures on different nodes: one chain records it as a Merkle root, another as a zero-knowledge proof commitment, and a reconciliation protocol asserts that these representations are identical despite sharing no common bit-pattern. For the engineers, discovering this correspondence is genuinely informative, not tautological, yet within any single node the statement that a hash equals itself is logically empty. Would Frege treat the cross-chain identity claim as expressing the same sense through different modes of presentation, with the transaction itself serving as the Bedeutung? Or would he deny that an abstract digital record qualifies as the kind of object for which Sinn and Bedeutung can be assigned?

Second, imagine an artificial system that maintains two internal registers of its own state—one symbolic and propositional, the other distributed across a high-dimensional vector space—and produces the judgment that state S is identical to state S-prime. If the system treats this discovery as cognitively significant rather than trivial, does this parallel Frege's a equals b, or does the absence of a shared public language-game undermine the analogy? In particular, I am interested in whether Frege would insist that informativeness requires a difference in sense even when the referent lacks spatial or temporal location, or whether he would restrict his puzzle to entities that can be grasped through fully articulated thoughts. Please work through both scenarios with care, indicating where his framework illuminates the modern problem and where it reaches a principled limit.
