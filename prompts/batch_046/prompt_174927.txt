Below is a rough philosophical profile I have assembled on national character and the ethics of collective generalization; I want an audit of whether it hangs together.

(1) I deny that “national character” denotes a real psychological kind: nations are not the kind of entities that can literally bear dispositions such as industriousness, melancholy, or trustfulness.

(2) I nevertheless accept that robust, stable statistical patterns in behavior or preference can emerge at the population level, and I think describing them need not commit the ecological fallacy—provided the explanation appeals to institutional path-dependence and distributed coordination mechanisms rather than to innate group psychology.

(3) I hold that collective intentionality is coherent—groups can form joint intentions and shared commitments—yet I deny that this entails any distributive mental property across the members.

(4) I think that moral criticism directed at a people for collective actions (for instance, systemic complicity) requires attributing to the group a kind of shared orientation or disposition, but I treat this attribution as purely regulative—functioning as a heuristic for moral address—without ontological commitment to anything like national character.

(5) I maintain that any acceptable generalization about a people must be fully defeasible by individual counterexamples; if it survives only by treating outliers as negligible noise, it collapses into an essentialism I reject.

Please trace the tensions among these commitments. For each pair that genuinely conflicts—not merely appears to—specify which claim must be revised and what the revision costs. Rank the tensions by severity. In particular, examine whether (2) can coexist with (1) given that “institutional path-dependence” sounds like it explains group-level patterns without psychological attribution; whether (4)’s regulative heuristic is compatible with (1)’s ontological denial or secretly smuggles back the very kind of group disposition (1) forbids; and whether (5)’s strict defeasibility condition undermines the explanatory stability that (2) claims for population-level patterns. Finally, identify the single cheapest revision that would clean up the most inconsistencies at once, and state exactly what I would have to give up.
