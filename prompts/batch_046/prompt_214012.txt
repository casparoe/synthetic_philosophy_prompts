I was chatting with my mate in the pub last night about evolutionary biology - she's doing an MSc and reckons group selection is making a comeback but can't explain the distinction between MLS1 and MLS2 without sounding like she's reciting a lecture. Anyway, I had this idea for a satirical internal memo. Could you write one, about 700 words, formatted as a deadpan corporate memo from a fictional consultancy called Evolutionary Dynamics Ltd, addressed to all staff? The voice should be that of a bewildered HR director trying to roll out a new multi-level performance review programme after the firm acquired both its Gene Division and its Group Behaviour Unit. The joke should come from treating the actual positions seriously: the gene-level consultants should insist that individual employees are merely vehicles for replication and shouldn't recieve phenotypic bonuses, while the group-selection wing demands free-rider sanctions at the departmental level but can't agree whether the group means the open-plan office, the floor, or the whole building. You should also include a deadpan reference to major evolutionary transitions - perhaps treating the shift to multicellularity as a failed corporate merger that still causes headaches for the replication auditors. Make it readable to someone who doesn't know Okasha or Hamilton's rule, but fair enough that a biologist recognises you're not inventing the disagreement. Keep it deadpan with no winking, and please don't add a bibliography or suggested reading list - just the memo text. Apologies if there's a typo or two in this; I always mix up recieve and receive.
