I'm trying to isolate a specific argumentative pattern that recurs in debates over long-horizon predictive theories—especially those in the Malthusian register, where a structural mismatch between resource dynamics and demand is said to make some crisis inevitable, yet the crisis repeatedly fails to materialize within the forecast window. The pattern unfolds in three moves. First, defenders identify a countervailing mechanism—technological substitution, institutional adaptation, demographic transition—that temporarily suppresses the predicted crisis. Second, they argue that this suppression was not an external ad hoc rescue but was implicitly built into the theory's own dynamics, so that the delay actually demonstrates the model's explanatory depth rather than undermining it. Third, they observe that the countervailing mechanism is itself finite or self-exhausting, which means the original crisis is not canceled but only deferred, with the deferral serving as evidence that the underlying structural pressure remains intact and will eventually dominate. Over successive iterations, each new layer of suppression makes the theory's timeline more elastic without generating testable intermediate milestones, yet proponents treat that growing elasticity as a virtue—signaling robustness—rather than as a progressive loss of predictive risk. I am looking for the established concept, if there is one, that captures exactly this configuration: not merely an auxiliary hypothesis, but a form of temporal immunization in which falsification is reinterpreted as an endogenous latency phase, and in which self-undermining countermeasures are read as confirmations rather than anomalies. How does it differ from a standard auxiliary-hypothesis rescue, from a Lakatosian degenerating problem shift, and from simple chronological postponement? If no established term precisely marks this combination of predicted delay, endogenous countermeasure, and the conversion of predictive failure into confirmatory latency, could you propose a rigorous definition together with boundary conditions—clear cases that are in, and clear cases that are out—so that the concept can be applied without collapsing into ordinary theory-protection?
