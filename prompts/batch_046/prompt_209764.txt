If anekāntavāda teaches that every object possesses infinite attributes and that each naya reveals only one partial aspect, and if syādvāda requires every judgment to carry a conditional qualifier such as "in some respect," then it appears that contradictory ethical claims must be assigned equal epistemic standing. For example, the judgment that intentional harm is always wrong and the judgment that it is permissible under conditions of collective self-defense could both be true relative to different nayas, with no transcendent standpoint available to privilege one over the other. Given this, how can Jain ethics maintain that ahiṃsā is unconditionally binding rather than merely conventionally valid within one particular standpoint? Or does the commitment to non-violence necessarily depend on covertly treating a single ethical naya as absolute, thereby contradicting the anti-absolutist core of anekāntavāda?
