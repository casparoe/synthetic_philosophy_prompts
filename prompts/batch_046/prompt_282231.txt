I'm giving opening remarks tomorrow at a workshop on folk morality across cultures. About half the room are philosophers and psych people who've followed the WEIRD critique; the other half are teachers and NGO staff who haven't. I want about three minutes that do real structural work: instead of framing cross-cultural differences as evidence for or against moral objectivity, I want to push that in many non-WEIRD contexts moral talk functions more like relational repair than like truth-claiming, so the objectivity-relativism debate is asking the wrong kind of question - like arguing whether a handshake is correct. The remarks should help teh experts see why this isn't just relativism rebranded, and give the non-experts one concrete image they can hold. Don't turn it into a bibliography or cite specific papers; just reference the WEIRD sample bias and this relational-grammar idea. Keep it accessible but not patronizing.
