I'm giving my seminar paper next tuesday in the phil of mind grad seminar. My thesis is that dreams aren't experiences in Malcolm's conceptual sense—there's no subject having them—but they also aren't Dennett-style narrative confabulations. I call them "attentional voids": affective tone and minimal intentionality survive, but the experiential subject is suspended, which is why dream reports are always reconstructions rather than memories. The audience is half analytic philosophers who think Malcolm nailed the incoherence of saying "I experienced X while asleep," half sleep-lab scientists who treat REM reports as direct evidence of experiential content, and one enactivist who thinks both sides miss embodied cognition. Order them by how much damage they'd do. Also flag my weakest structural point—that I haven't explained why false memories form if there was nothing experienced to misremember. And include the objection I'm praying nobody asks—probably about lucid dreaming restoring full intentionality—or tell me if there's a hidden worse one.
