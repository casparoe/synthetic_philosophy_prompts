I am a doctoral student in the philosophy of social science. In a recent workshop on historical materialism and the autonomy of scientific knowledge, an interlocutor kept invoking the slogan "the economic base explains everything else" — occasionally glossed as "being determines consciousness" — to dismiss suggestions that domains such as the mathematics of attention mechanisms or the formal structure of quantum mechanics might possess internal development logics not fully reducible to the venture-capital imperatives and institutional funding patterns that currently house them. I am convinced something has gone wrong in that move, but I cannot yet say exactly what. Could you lay out, as a precise menu, the distinct theses that slogan might actually be expressing? I want the full range: from the near-trivial claim that economic conditions influence some intellectual outputs, through intermediate positions about institutional incentives shaping research agendas, to the strong reductionist view that every feature of a scientific theory is epiphenomenal upon production relations. For each reading, please indicate whether it is defensible, whether it is false, and — crucially — whether it actually warrants dismissing inquiry into the internal logic of these technical domains. In particular, I need to know which of these interpretations my interlocutor was most likely relying on when he used the phrase to end discussion, and whether that interpretation genuinely undermines the autonomy thesis or merely shifts the subject. Present the options as a numbered list with verdicts attached, so that I can bring it to the next seminar session without needing to defend a single exegesis in advance.
