As a consultant recently tasked with auditing integrated assessment models for long-term climate and economic policy, I have become preoccupied with a structural pattern I lack a precise name for. The framework’s core—typically a neoclassical growth module coupled to a linear carbon-cycle representation—rests on manifestly unrealistic simplifications: agents possess perfect foresight and homogeneous preferences, climate feedbacks are smooth and reversible, and technological change is exogenous. When the model confronts historical data, these simplifications fail in specific, predictable domains: investment exhibits path-dependent inertia, and temperature responses show non-linear tipping dynamics. Rather than revising the core dynamics, the architects preserve them as the “theoretical backbone” and instead embed localized corrections—an inertia parameter in the investment block, a piecewise damage function, a sub-grid closure scheme for ocean heat uptake—each defended independently by citation to micro-level empirical studies. Over successive iterations, these exemptions accumulate into an intricate peripheral architecture that absorbs all the anomalies. The model becomes progressively less falsifiable as a whole, yet no individual correction is treated as an ad hoc rescue, because each carries its own empirical pedigree and is presented as a refinement rather than a defense. I am familiar with Lakatos’s degenerating problem shift and with Popperian immunization, but this feels distinct: the protective structure is not a single auxiliary hypothesis but a distributed, compartmentalized network of exemptions, and the practitioners genuinely regard the periphery as enriching the core rather than shielding it. Is there an established concept in the philosophy of modeling or in science studies for this specific pattern—distributed, empirically grounded encapsulation that preserves an unrealistic core? If so, how exactly does it differ from ordinary model refinement and from a degenerating research program? And if there is no established term, what boundary conditions should govern its application, and what would distinguish it from legitimate multi-scale parameterization?
