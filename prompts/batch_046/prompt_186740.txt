I'm in a philosophy of biology seminar, and I'm trying to apply our readings to a disagreement I have with my lab partner. We work with captive-bred corvids being prepared for reintroduction. One bird developed an accidental hook at the tip of its beak—apparently a developmental response to the metal feeding trays used in captivity—which now lets it extract larvae from tree bark far more efficiently than wild-type beaks. My partner says this hook's biological function is clearly larval extraction, because function is just whatever a trait currently does to promote survival and reproduction. I keep feeling that its function should still be tied to the evolutionary history of normal seed-cracking beaks, even though this particular hook was never selected for and is useless for seeds. Without asking for citations or a reading list, is there a principled philosophical distinction behind our disagreement—something more rigorous than "whatever works right now"—and if so, which side does it actually favor in this specific case?
