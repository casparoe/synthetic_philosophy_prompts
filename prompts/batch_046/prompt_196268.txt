Take the role of a senior philosopher of AI and metaphysics. Before drafting, spend a few minutes brainstorming the conceptual tensions between formal representation and normative salience. Then write a concise one-page research proposal (suitable for a three-month independent fellowship) on the following question. The frame problem is standardly treated as a representational engineering challenge: how can an agent update its beliefs after an action without listing every property that remains unchanged? Technical responses—frame axioms, successor-state axioms, non-monotonic reasoning—treat the problem as one of efficiency. But they all presuppose that there is an objective, agent-independent partition between properties an action affects and properties it leaves untouched. This proposal should investigate whether that presupposition amounts to a hidden commitment to “property essentialism”—the view that properties carry intrinsic modal profiles specifying which changes they can undergo. The precise research question: if one rejects property essentialism in favor of a contextualist or relational metaphysics of properties, does the frame problem dissolve, become formally intractable, or reveal that “what stays the same” is always relative to an externally specified frame of evaluation that no formal system can generate internally? State the question precisely; explain why standard AI solutions leave this metaphysical commitment untouched; outline a philosophical (not empirical) approach using distinctions between intrinsic and relational properties and between formal representation and normative salience; describe what a positive result would look like versus a failure to resolve the tension; and give an honest scope assessment for three months. Do not request a bibliography or citation list—you may reference positions conceptually and note that references will be supplied separately. In your response, state how confident you are in the central claim you defend and explicitly identify the point about which you are least sure.
