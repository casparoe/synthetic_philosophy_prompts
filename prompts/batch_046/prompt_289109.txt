(b) Value alignment, understood as inducing in an artificial agent a coherent and stable set of human normative commitments, is both conceptually incoherent (given the irreducible pluralism of human moral values) and practically undesirable (it risks encoding one contingent moral framework as technically mandatory).

(c) First-order intent alignment is sufficient for safety: if a system reliably does what the operator meant by the instruction, then any harmful outcome can be traced to error in human specification rather than to autonomous value divergence by the system.
