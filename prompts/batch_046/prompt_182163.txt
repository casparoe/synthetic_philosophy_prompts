Next month I present at our dept's grad seminar on the procreation asymmetry. My thesis is that the standard Benatar-style asymmetry gets the logic backwards once you factor in counterfactual loyalty — the idea that if you actually bring someone into being you owe them a kind of narrative continuity with the conditions you picked for them, but if you don't bring them in there's no betrayal because there's no subject to betray. So I think we have stronger reasons to create happy lives (to honor that loyalty should we create) and only weak reasons against creating suffering ones (since suffering people can still be owed loyalty, just differently). The audience is mostly meta-ethics grad studnets who've read Benatar and maybe Parfit, plus one faculty member in personal identity who thinks talk of counterfactual loyalty is metaphysically confussed. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased like those two groups would actually say them, with what I should concede, what I should hold, and the weakest point in my own position I should prep to defend. Include the objection I'm secretly hoping nobody asks — probably about whether this collapses into a general obligation to maximize happy people. I don't need citations, just the prep sheet.
