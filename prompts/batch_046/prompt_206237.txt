Another friend, Leo, said that's way too restrictive and basically collapses ontology into epistemology. He pointed out we identify quarks and distant exoplanets without direct access, so inaccessibility is always a matter of degree. He said even with zero traces, theoretical coherence lets us identify things, like the interior of a black hole via the exterior metric.

Maya then said that's the mistake -- we're identifying a mathematical structure, not an ontology, and conflating the model with the thing modeled is exactly what gets us into trouble. Leo replied that all identification is model-mediated anyway, so if the model is robust, the sealed domain is just as real. They asked me to referee but I honestly dont know.

Can you go through their exchange and say who's right at each step? Rate each move from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify. Also, is there something both are missing about how ontology identification works when information is permanently sealed?
