I'm taking a philosophy course on Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka, and I'm trying to understand whether emptiness (śūnyatā) has any practical bearing outside textual study. Outside class, I sit on the board of a campus repair collective—a shared workshop with tools and a teaching schedule—where we've split over a governance dispute. One board member insists the collective possesses an intrinsic identity and purpose that persists regardless of who participates; another claims it is literally nothing beyond the current individuals and their temporary agreements; a third argues it is both an emergent social reality and fully reducible to personal choices; and a fourth rejects all three descriptions, saying that asking "what it really is" presupposes a false essentialism. I need to draft membership rules, but every option seems to embed one of these metaphysical commitments. Without simply telling me which camp is correct, can you show how the Madhyamaka rejection of svabhāva and the four-corner analysis (catuskoti) reframe this dispute? Specifically, does recognizing the absence of intrinsic existence dissolve the practical problem, or does it change what kinds of reasons are valid for including or excluding members?
