I've been thinking on my own about our obligations to people who haven't been born yet, especially around climate change, and I want to know if my views actually fit together. Here is my current position: (1) We have a strong obligation to reduce carbon emissions, mainly to prevent severe suffering for people who will be alive around 2100. (2) I also think that if we reduce emissions now, the specific individuals living in 2100 will be different people from those who would live if we did nothing—small differences in conditions and timing change who gets conceived and born. (3) I believe you can harm a particular person only if you make that very person worse off than they would have been otherwise. (4) At the same time, I think it is wrong to bring about a world with widespread suffering, even if the people who suffer in it wouldn't have existed in a better world—the wrongness is in the overall pattern of lives, not just in a comparison for each individual. (5) I don't think nonexistent people can hold rights or make claims on us, but I still believe we have real reasons not to worsen the general shape of the future. Do these hang together? In particular, does (2) combined with (3) undermine the reasoning behind (1)? Is there a real conflict between (3) and (4), or only an apparent one? And does (5) sit comfortably with (1) and (4), or does it pull in the opposite direction? Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate which ones dissolve on closer inspection, and for each genuine tension tell me the least costly revision—without assuming I am already committed to giving up any particular point.
