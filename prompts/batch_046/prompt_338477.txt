I'm writing up my commitments for a graduate seminar on existentialist ethics, and I'd like a consistency check before I defend them in the discussion section. Over the semester I've come to hold what feels like a coherent Sartrean package, but I suspect there are hidden fractures. Here is where I've landed: (1) I accept the thesis of radical freedom — consciousness is never determined, not even under extreme coercion or psychological trauma, because the for-itself can always choose its stance toward its situation; there are no excusing conditions that genuinely eliminate choice. (2) I understand bad faith precisely as flight from the anguish of this freedom, a self-deceptive attempt to treat oneself as pure being-in-itself, fixed by essence or circumstance. (3) Nevertheless, I take seriously the weight of facticity and deep social conditioning — structural oppression, trauma, and ideology can make certain options practically unbearable or psychologically invisible, even if they don't metaphysically cancel freedom. (4) I think authenticity demands owning one's freedom without hiding behind social roles, yet I also believe that for people formed in severely oppressive conditions, demanding authenticity can be an unfair burden that ignores the crushing weight of facticity. (5) Finally, I hold that if freedom is ontologically unavoidable, then bad faith is itself a freely chosen project, which implies we are fully responsible even for our own self-deception.

These five positions feel mutually supportive in broad strokes — freedom makes bad faith possible, facticity gives bad faith its content, and authenticity is the remedy — but I worry I'm smuggling in contradictions. Is there a hidden determinism in (3) that undermines the radical claim in (1)? Does (4) implicitly concede that freedom is not universal, or does it merely apply the normative demand selectively? Does (5) render the normative call in (4) redundant, since we are already free whether we acknowledge it or not? Or is the deepest tension elsewhere, perhaps between the total responsibility implied by (1) and (5) and the mitigating function I want facticity to serve in (3) and (4)? Please audit the set: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension versus which only appear to conflict (for instance, whether freedom and facticity are actually incompatible in Sartre's framework), rank the real tensions by severity, and for each specify the least costly revision — whether that means qualifying a commitment, distinguishing two senses of a term, or abandoning one piece altogether — without assuming I am committed to dropping any particular one in advance.
