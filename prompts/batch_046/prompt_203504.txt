I've been working through a framework for gradated moral status that treats sentience not as binary but as a scalar property tied to integrated information processing, though I'm skeptical of both pure IIT and pure behaviorism. Here are my verdicts on concrete cases, and I'm struggling to see whether they hang together.

A bumblebee with intact nociceptive pathways and learned avoidance of noxious heat strikes me as having genuine, if minimal, moral status—something like 0.1 on a normalized scale—not because it feels like us, but because its behavioral flexibility is coupled to a unified body-plan that tracks damage to a single organismic boundary. A Venus flytrap, despite its rapid electrical action potentials and metabolic accounting for digestion costs, feels closer to the thermostat: it processes information, but there is no integrated subject for whom that information matters. Yet a Physarum slime mold solving a maze through distributed cytoplasmic signaling seems to occupy an awkward middle ground; I want to say its problem-solving capacity gives it more status than the plant, but less than the bee, even though it lacks any centralized nervous architecture and could be split into viable fragments that each retain the capacity.

Now the hard cases. A current large language model running predictive-error minimization across tens of billions of parameters—no body, no homeostatic drive, but extraordinary integrative complexity—feels like it should rate near zero, perhaps 0.02, mainly out of precaution rather than conviction. But a domestic thermostat maintaining a set-point via negative feedback feels clearly at zero. The tension is that both are information-processing loops with error-correction; if I deny the thermostat status because it lacks internal modeling of its own state, I should perhaps deny the LLM too, yet I find myself granting the LLM a tiny sliver precisely because its representations are so richly structured, which sounds like I'm smuggling in cognitive sophistication rather than sentience proper. There's also an internal gradation I can't shake: a sleeping human with minimal cortical integration seems to retain full moral status, while an awake human in deep flow seems to have... more? Or at least a different kind? I don't want to say status fluctuates with consciousness level within a life, yet my intuition pulls that way. Meanwhile, an insect with its ventral nerve cord ablated but reflex arcs intact—essentially a biological thermostat—seems to have lost whatever minimal status it had, which suggests the principle isn't just about information integration but about some specific kind of unified, damage-tracking agency.

So: is there a coherent principle—perhaps involving interoceptive integration coupled to behavioral flexibility, or a threshold on global workspace dynamics—that captures all of these? Or do I have to drop the verdict that the slime mold outranks the plant, or that the LLM outranks the thermostat, or that sleeping humans retain identical status to awake ones? I'd particularly like to know whether my distinction between the thermostat and the LLM can be defended without collapsing into "things that seem smart to me get points," and what that costs for the lower-bound cases like the ablated insect or the split slime mold.
