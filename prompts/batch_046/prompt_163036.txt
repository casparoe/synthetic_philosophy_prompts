For a graduate-level conceptual workshop on the nonidentity problem, design a decision tree that maps the logical space of responses without organizing them around canonical authors or texts. Use four binary branch points: (1) whether wrongness requires harming a particular identifiable individual (the person-affecting restriction); (2) if yes to (1), whether personal identity is treated actualistically—requiring the same individual to exist across worlds—or possibilistically—allowing comparison among merely possible people with different origins; if no to (1), whether the evaluation is impersonal (aggregate welfare) or relational (a direct duty to those affected regardless of identity); (3) whether the normative assessment applies to act-types or act-tokens; and (4) whether the evaluation requires a counterfactual comparative baseline or can operate with a non-comparative standard of well-being. At each leaf, provide the standard label if one exists, or mark it "unoccupied," and state in one sentence its most awkward commitment—the claim it must endorse that conflicts most sharply with ordinary moral intuition. Flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate, explaining whether the failure is logical (the views collapse) or rhetorical. Build the tree from the logical combinations first, then locate positions within it; include unoccupied combinations and diagnose briefly whether they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
