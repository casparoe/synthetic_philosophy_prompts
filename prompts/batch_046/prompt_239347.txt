Assume the persona of a professional philosopher of action and a literary short-story author. Before composing the narrative, take a moment to think through the conceptual tension: in standard metaphysics, omissions are often treated as non-events, yet in ethics and law they are routinely blamed as causes. Brainstorm two or three concrete scenarios in which an absence—not a presence—triggers a chain of events, and consider how a character might experience responsibility for something she did not do.

Then write a short story of about 900 words set in the floating municipality of Omission, where civic maintenance is performed exclusively by licensed Negative Engineers. These professionals are authorized only to excise elements—faulty algorithms, redundant bridges, obsolete regulations—from an otherwise self-sustaining urban grid, and they are legally liable solely for what they actively remove. The protagonist, Mara Voss, is indicted after a reservoir overflows; investigators claim that she omitted the scheduled removal of a decaying pressure gate, and that this absence, rather than any active failure, caused the disaster. Follow Mara through the trial and through her daily work—calibrating voids, certifying non-interventions, measuring the exact shape of what is not there—without resolving whether omission is genuinely causal. Let the philosophical problem emerge through dialogue, physical detail, and Mara’s own uncertainty, rather than through exposition or named philosophers. The tone should be contemplative, slightly surreal, and grounded in sensory precision, so that a reader feels the weight of causation by omission without being told what to conclude.
