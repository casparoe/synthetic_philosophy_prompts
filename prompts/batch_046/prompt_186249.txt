I'm writing a paper on Dennett's taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures and my roommate built this argument that I can't dismantle, though the conclusion feels wrong. (1) Only beings capable of being false have real beliefs; mere reflexes and conditioned associations don't have truth conditions. (2) Popperian creatures generate internal hypotheses that can mismatch reality, so they genuinely believe and misbelieve. (3) Gregorian creatures go further: they represent things like imaginary numbers, justice, or next Tuesday - contents no single lifetime of trial and error could build. (4) But Gregorian minds only reach those contents through borrowed language, concepts, and cultural frameworks; they don't invent thier representational tools, they inherit them. (5) If every representational resource you deploy comes from outside, then you aren't the original source of the mental content - you're just a node carrying culture's representations. (6) So Gregorian individuals don't have original belief-content about abstract things; they only have derived, "outsourced" mental states. (7) But mental states that are fully derived belong to the system that generated them, not to the node that carries them. (8) Therefore Gregorian individuals, taken alone, don't actually believe anything about abstract objects; human cultures do, using people as hardware. (9) That's obviously wrong - I can lie about imaginary numbers, and lying requires that I hold the belief myself first. (10) So either Gregorian minds don't really transcend Popperian ones (giving up 3), or we have to accept that cultures literally think and we're just organs for them. I really want to keep (3) and the intuition in (9). Where does this slide happen? Which premise looks okay but isn't?
