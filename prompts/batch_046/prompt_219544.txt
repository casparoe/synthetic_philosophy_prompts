Analyze the South Asian Green Revolution from the 1960s through the 1980s—specifically the introduction of high-yield dwarf wheat and rice varieties, state-led irrigation and fertilizer subsidies centered on the Punjab, and the concurrent rapid decline in fertility across parts of the region—through the lens of Malthus’s Essay on the Principle of Population and the philosophy-of-science problem of whether a long-run structural prediction can be “right but early” rather than outright falsified. Rather than treating the absence of predicted mass famine as a simple refutation, treat the episode as a potential Lakatosian rescue: the Malthusian hard core (that population pressure tends to outstrip subsistence capacity) survives intact, while the protective belt of auxiliary assumptions—about the elasticity of agricultural productivity, the availability of fossil-fuel-derived nitrogen, and the institutional capacity for state-coordinated technological diffusion—is substantially revised. Identify precisely who occupies the theoretical roles in this drama: Malthus as the structural theorist, Paul Ehrlich in The Population Bomb as the modern point-predictor of imminent collapse, Norman Borlaug and the agricultural research institutions as auxiliary rescuers, and the falling fertility rates in the region as an emergent preventive check rather than a positive check. Then assess whether the rescue is ad hoc in the Lakatosian sense: did the revisions to the protective belt generate novel, risky predictions about future resource or climate constraints, or did they merely accommodate the failure of 1960s famine forecasts after the fact? Finally, examine what the episode reveals about empirical content in theories with century-scale horizons: if a mechanism is repeatedly buffered by successive technological, energetic, and institutional interventions, does it retain predictive bite, or does it risk collapsing into a tautological tendency that is always true “just around the corner”? Be disciplined about the mapping—distinguish positive checks from preventive checks, specify exactly where in the Punjab case the Malthusian pressure was genuinely operative and where it was suspended, and say clearly where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
