Two philosophy graduate students, Maya and Jon, argued about Chase and Sanborn's claim that qualia are an incoherent posit rather than merely an unexplained one.

Maya: The argument succeeds because the standard concept of qualia bundles four conditions—intrinsic, immediately introspectively accessible, causally efficacious, and ontologically basic—that are jointly unsatisfiable. If qualia are non-relational, they cannot enter causal relations, which contradicts efficacy; if they are immediately known, the mode of access must be something other than ordinary physical cognition. So the concept is contradictory, not just mysterious.

Jon: That treats a family-resemblance idea as a rigid definition. Chase and Sanborn attack a Cartesian package deal. The minimal core is just subjective phenomenal character—there is something it is like. Drop intrinsicness, ineffability, and causal efficacy, and the contradiction dissolves. What remains is the hard problem, not an incoherence.

Maya: Even the stripped version fails. If phenomenal character is purely subjective and non-relational, it lacks internal structure, so there is no fact of the matter that fixes one quality rather than another. Determinacy requires relational or structural differentiation; without it, "what it's like" cannot specify a determinate property. That is the deeper incoherence.

Jon: That assumes determinacy requires relational structure. Phenomenal qualities could be fundamental determinables—like charge or spin, except experienced—without importing relations. The contradiction is not internal to qualia; it arises only if you combine them with a metaphysics that denies primitive properties altogether.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate Maya's first claim about the four conditions, Jon's minimal-concept reply, Maya's determinacy objection, and Jon's primitive-property defense—each from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with brief justification. Then say who is more right overall, and identify the key metaphysical assumption both are treating as settled rather than defending.
