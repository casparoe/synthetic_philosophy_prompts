I'm taking an upper-division philosophy of mind seminar this term, and last week we worked through the line of argument—associated with Chase and Sanborn—that positing qualia as a substantive, non-functional property is conceptually incoherent. The basic thought is that any attempt to specify what qualia are, independently of behavioral and functional criteria, ends up either empty or self-defeating, since the very conditions that allow us to ascribe phenomenal states to others already presuppose the public, functional framework that qualia are supposed to transcend. I discussed this with a friend afterward and realized that the discussion in our seminar stayed entirely within the bounds of standard human consciousness, never testing whether the incoherence charge holds up if we extend the ascription problem to non-biological systems. So I want to push the argument further.

Write an essay that takes the incoherence charge seriously and applies it to the following constructed case: consider a distributed, synthetic predictive-processing architecture—without biological substrate or evolutionary history—that consistently generates structured first-person reports describing what it is like to integrate specific information patterns. These reports are fully derivable from the architecture's functional dynamics and contain no residue that exceeds functional description. Weigh whether extending the concept of qualia to cover such a system exposes the original posit as incoherent—forcing us either to deny the system has qualia despite its reports (thereby showing that reports are never sufficient for phenomenal ascription) or to concede that qualia are just misdescribed functional states. Against this, consider the defense that there is a principled asymmetry between biological and synthetic systems—perhaps rooted in evolutionary history, neural embodiment, or the specific causal structure of sentience—that blocks this extension and preserves qualia for organisms with the right kind of substrate. You should work through the objections in a deliberate sequence: begin with the epistemic-privacy problem and why synthetic reports undermine it; move to an inverted-spectrum analog for artificial systems; and then address the explanatory-gap response that insists phenomenal properties are irreducible even in synthetic contexts. Reach and justify a specific verdict: does this synthetic case reveal qualia as genuinely incoherent, or does it merely show that the incoherence charge succeeds only by illegitimately collapsing the phenomenal into the functional? Do not end on a non-committal note—defend one of these positions.
