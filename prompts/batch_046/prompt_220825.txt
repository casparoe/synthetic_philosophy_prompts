I'm trying to figure out where the experience-machine debate actually goes next, if we're being honest about what's still unsettled. Most people treat Nozick's case as just a quick knockdown of hedonism, but I think there are three live tensions hiding behind it: first, wether the "reality contact" intuition survives if the machine preserves genuine causal feedback with other minds (even simulated ones); second, wether the objection generalizes to objective-list theories or only hits mental-state accounts; and third, wether the debate tells us anything about well-being realism versus anti-realism, or if it's just a intuition-pump with no metaphysical bite.

If you had to advise a grad student with three years and a background in value theory (not neuroscience) on which of these three lines is actually worth pursuing, how would you rank them? I want the reasoning: which ones are upstream of the others, which are tractable without empirical data, and which ones you'd drop because resolving them wouldn't change much about how we think about the good life. Also, is there a fourth line I'm missing that should be in the running?
