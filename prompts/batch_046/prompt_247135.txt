Camp A: the normative-binding folks. They think hyperbolic discounting only explains akrasia when it breaks a diachronic commitment the agent herself endorsed — like, you made a plan yesterday, today you violate it, and the "weakness" is specifically the failure of your present-self authority over your future self. Without that prior binding judgment, it's just preference reversal, not real weakness of will.

Camp B: the descriptive-preference folks. They say that's metaphysical window dressing. Hyperbolic discounting is the mechanism; weakness of will IS steep time-inconsistent preference reversal, full stop. You don't need a "plan" or "authority" — if your discount curve makes you choose the smaller-sooner reward against your own stable long-run preferences, that's akrasia, regardless of whether you ever wrote anything down.
