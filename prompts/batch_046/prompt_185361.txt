I hold the view that philosophy is structurally obsolete for mature science: it functioned as proto-science until disciplines internalized their own conceptual hygiene, and the questions that remain are not deep but unstructured — lacking the formal conditions that make empirical progress possible — a position in the spirit of well-known dismissals by Weinberg, Hawking, and Feynman. I sharpen this into a maturation thesis: once a field achieves empirical traction, philosophical analysis becomes redundant at best and obstructive at worst, since scientists can clarify their own concepts with greater precision. What would count against it? I want decisive defeaters — historical cases where philosophical work genuinely redirected an empirical research program, examples where scientists' internal conceptual hygiene led to demonstrable error that philosophers corrected, and whether there exist domains where formalization is impossible yet philosophical reasoning still makes measurable progress — alongside an honest assessment of whether the thesis can be qualified into unfalsifiability, with any successful philosophical intervention retrospectively claimed as merely good science. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say whether this insulation is a vice of the view or a defensible boundary condition.
