I am in my final year of sixth form, preparing an Extended Project Qualification in the philosophy of science. My supervisor encouraged me to move past standard textbook accounts of causation and examine the metaphysics of dispositions, particularly the problems of finks and masks that arise in the literature following C.B. Martin's discussion of finkish dispositions and subsequent work on antidotes. I have developed a particular interest in a layered scenario that is rarely treated in detail: consider a compound substance with an intrinsic disposition to react explosively with water, yet permanently encased in a hydrophobic polymer shell that prevents any contact; if that shell itself possesses a disposition to degrade under ultraviolet light, and a UV-blocking filter is installed as a tertiary safeguard, we appear to have nested dispositions, potential finks, and successive masks operating simultaneously upon one another. Given this kind of multi-layered interference, which open conceptual questions in the current debate on dispositions are genuinely worth resolving first? Should priority go to clarifying the ontology of multi-track dispositions versus single-track ones, or to determining whether masks and finks can be distinguished without relying on counterfactual conditions that may fail in layered systems? Moreover, which of these inquiries carry real implications for how we model risk in engineered or chemical systems, and which remain largely internal to metaphysics with minimal downstream practical effect? I am looking for a defended prioritisation: which questions sit upstream of others, which are tractable for a student without specialised laboratory access, and what argument or conceptual clarification would cause you to reorder them?
