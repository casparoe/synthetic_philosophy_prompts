You chair the University Interdisciplinary Grant Committee, which must render a final decision on a joint philosophy-of-science and machine-learning proposal. The committee charter requires each member to vote separately on three logically linked propositions: (P) the project’s philosophical framework is internally coherent; (Q) its proposed computational model is technically sound; and (R) the project should receive funding. The charter also contains a coherence clause stating that any funding recommendation must be supported by a logically consistent subset of the members’ judgments.

After a secret ballot, majorities endorse P (seven of eleven) and Q (eight of eleven), yet R is rejected (only five of eleven support funding). At the same time, a conclusion-based tally—asking members for an overall evaluation—shows six of eleven ranking the proposal above the funding threshold, yielding a discursive inconsistency. The committee is now split: one faction argues for premise-based aggregation, insisting that funding must be denied because no consistent majority backs both premises and the conclusion together; the other faction argues for conclusion-based aggregation, holding that the overall evaluative judgment should prevail and that the premise votes are defeasible evidential inputs.

An additional complication has emerged: three members now report that their judgment on P was conditional on their expectation that R would fail, and they warn that if the coherence clause is enforced as a post-aggregation revision rule, they will revise P downward rather than revise R upward—potentially generating a mirror-image paradox in which a consistent subset supports R but not P. Draft the committee’s formal decision memo recommending one of three courses—premise-based rejection, conclusion-based approval, or conditional approval tied to a specific procedural revision—with clear reasoning the members can act on. The memo should engage honestly with the strongest argument for the option you do not choose, and it should specify whether the coherence clause ought to function as a pre-aggregation filter or as a post-deliberation constraint. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation request; write as though the committee has no access to external literature.
