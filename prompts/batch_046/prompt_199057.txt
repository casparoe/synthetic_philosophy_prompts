I need a reusable procedue my legal staff can run — no PhD game theorist on call, 48-hour turnaround — to apply iterated elimination of dominated strategies, including in mixd strategies, and stop when we either hit a unique rationalizable profile or hit a wall. Give me a worksheet-style checklist: the order of tests, how to check pure-strategy dominance first, then mixed, how to re-run after each cut, where to flag cycles, and exactly where the call hands off to human judgment because it's a values call or an ambiguity. The procedure must handle three specific wrinkles: (1) dominance that only appears after weighting payoffs by each state's declared ethical priorities (e.g., ecological damage vs water volume), (2) 'shadow moves' — actions not listed in the proposal but available through ambiguous treaty language, and (3) cases where rational elimination conflicts with political reality. Apply it to three proposals: Case A, a simple two-state split; Case B, a three-state setup where ethical weighting saves a mixed strategy that pure payoff reasoning kills; and Case C, a case where the procedue recommends eliminating a strategy the asking state actually wants to keep, so the answer is technically correct but politically toxic. Be honest about what this can't catch — especially loops that don't converge and shadow moves we never see — and don't dress up judgment calls as math.
