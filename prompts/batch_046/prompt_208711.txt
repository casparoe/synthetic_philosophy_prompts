Please explain the conceptual tension within Wittgenstein's private-language argument between the standard claim that a purely private ostensive definition is impossible because there is no criterion distinguishing correct application from merely seeming correct application, and the hypothetical case of a solipsistic symbol-system in which an agent attempts to stabilize her own usage through internal consistency conditions and mnemonic tracking alone, with no appeal to public practice. Does Wittgenstein's reasoning exclude even this self-correcting private calculus, and if so, does that exclusion rest on a substantive thesis about the necessarily social constitution of normative constraints, or can it be derived from the thinner logical point that any putative internal check collapses into the absence of a distinction between being right and thinking one is right? In addressing this, carefully distinguish the contribution of memory—understood as a causal mechanism for reproducing prior associations—from the contribution of a community—understood as the source of normative standards—clarify whether the argument targets the logical privacy of sensory contents, the logical privacy of rule-following, or both simultaneously, and identify the precise step at which the solipsist's internal consistency conditions fail to generate genuine correctness conditions rather than merely regularities of behavior.
