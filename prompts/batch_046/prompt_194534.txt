I have spent the past term tutoring first-year students through the philosophy of well-being, and a question from our final session has stayed with me. We were examining hedonism not in its popular guise but as the precise claim that pleasure alone is intrinsically valuable, and the students kept pressing what, if anything, makes all pleasures the same kind of thing. The heterogeneity problem, as it is usually framed, is that pleasures seem irreducibly diverse: the cool relief of shade after long exertion, the quiet satisfaction of finally grasping an abstract proof, the sudden warmth of an old melody heard unexpectedly. Some philosophers hold that a single uniform phenomenal quality—sometimes described in older literature as a felt tone—pervades every instance; others maintain that pleasure is best understood not as a shared feeling but as an intentional attitude, a pro-attitude whose objects vary wildly, so that the diversity is not a puzzle to be solved but a feature to be accepted. I want to close our seminar with a poem that holds both answers in suspension.

Could you write a villanelle—nineteen lines, with two refrains that alternate through the stanzas and meet only in the final couplet—taking this problem as its structural principle? I would like one refrain to evoke the claim that a hidden common thread binds every pleasure, and the other to insist that no such thread exists, only scattered, irreducible instances. The poem should move through concrete, specific pleasures—bodily, intellectual, aesthetic—so that the repetition feels philosophically earned rather than merely decorative. Crucially, the form itself ought to do the work: the way the two lines circle back without coinciding until the very end should mirror why the uniform-quality view and the attitudinal view keep passing each other in the literature without resolution. Please leave the tension open; let the final couplet place the two refrains side by side, as our discussion did. A specialist should recognise the debate immediately, yet the poem ought to stand on its own for someone encountering the problem for the first time.
