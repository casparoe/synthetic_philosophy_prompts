I'm preparing a handout for a philosophy of biology graduate seminar on whether DNA is literally a code, not merely metaphorically called one. Please construct a decision tree—framed as a sequence of four binary or either/or branch questions that a student can answer without already knowing the philosophical labels—that sorts respondents into named positions (or into an "unoccupied" or "unstable" cell) regarding the code-status of genetic information.

The four branch points should be:
(i) whether the triplet-to-amino-acid mapping carries semantic content (about what ought to be synthesized) or only causal-functional correlation;
(ii) whether epigenetic marks, non-coding regulatory sequences, and environmental modulation extend the code analogy, complicate it without destroying it, or render it obsolete;
(iii) whether the information carried by DNA is properly digital (discrete, arbitrarily assignable, with error-correction mechanisms) or analog (continuous, graded, and context-dependent in ways that resist digitization);
(iv) whether the "reader" or "interpreter" of the sequence is restricted to the ribosome and its immediate translation machinery, or distributed across the broader developmental and cellular system.

For each leaf, provide the conventional or proposed name of the position, one sentence summarizing its core claim, and one sentence identifying its most awkward commitment—the consequence it must accept that opponents find least palatable. Where two positions share identical answers at all four nodes, flag them as indistinguishable by this instrument and explain what further distinction (if any) could separate them. Also identify any combination of answers that seems incoherent or that no known theorist occupies, with a brief diagnosis: is the gap due to logical tension, empirical implausibility, or mere lack of exploration?

The instrument should be usable by students who understand basic molecular biology—the central dogma, transcription, translation, and epigenetics—but who have not read the specialist philosophy-of-biology literature in depth. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the purpose is to reveal a respondent's commitments through case judgments rather than to test scholarly recall. At the end of your response, please state how confident you are in the taxonomy's coverage and completeness, and single out the specific branch point or leaf about which you are least certain.
