I'm trying to nail down what I think about moral worth — not just doing the right thing, but being praisworthy for it. Here are my gut verdicts. A nurse works a double shift in an outbreak because she signed a contract and feels she owes it, but she feels no real warmth for the patients. I say worthy, but barely. A bystander jumps into freezing water to save a kid, driven purely by panic and compassion, with no thought about duty or rights. I say fully worthy, maybe the best kind. A tech millionaire gives away 90% of his income to malaria nets after running the numbers, but he admits he's emotinally flat about suffering — it's just logical. I say worthy, but it feels like a different category from the diver. Someone returns extra change mainly because they're terrified of being caught on camera, though they also think stealing is wrong. Not worthy at all. The case I keep going back and forth on: a parent ruins themselves financially to save their child, knowing it's the right call but driven almost entirely by love. Is that peak moral worth or just biology doing its thing? So is there one principle — maybe about the reason being moral rather than self-interested, or about the right kind of motivation — that gets all these? Or do I have to drop the diver or the nurse? And what should I say about a robot with perfect ethical reasoning but zero feelings — does it get moral credit for saving people?
