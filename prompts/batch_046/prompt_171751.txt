I've been trying to map out Émilie du Châtelet's view on happiness for a reading group and i keep coming back to this bundle: happiness is active satisfaction, not passive contentment; intellectual work is a necessary ingredient, not just a bonus; romantic love and social status are goods but dont actually feed into happiness directly; and the whole thing is acheivable through reasoned self-rule in this life, no afterlife required. Do these four actually hang together? There's a tension I'm seeing between claiming study is essential and claiming social recognition doesnt matter—doesnt serious study usually need some kind of audience or instutional support? And there's another thing that looks like tension but might dissolve: she seems to treat passions as both dangerous and necessary, which sounds contradictory unless you split them by type. Can you audit these commitments, say which ones genuinely pull against each other and whats the cheapest fix?
