Draft the joint statement that two camps could both sign after a sustained adversarial collaboration on ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood. Name the camps precisely: the Constitutive Relationalists, who hold that personhood is not an intrinsic property of isolated individuals but is metaphysically constituted by sustained structures of reciprocal recognition and communal care (so that a human being with profound and irreversible cognitive impairment can still fully possess personhood if embedded in such structures), and the Threshold Individualists, who hold that relational practices confer dignity and shape moral status but do not by themselves constitute personhood, which requires at least minimal capacities for subjective experience or agency.

The document should contain:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims — including about what ubuntu traditions correctly diagnose in liberal individualism, about the moral significance of care relationships, about the harm of social isolation, and about the inadequacy of purely atomistic accounts of human value — written in language both sides accept.

2. A disagreements table with the exact residual disagreements phrased so that each side accepts the wording of its own position. Include at least: (a) whether relational properties are metaphysically constitutive of personhood or only ethically and socially significant; (b) whether a permanently unconscious human being maintained in decades of sustained communal care possesses full personhood or only a derivative moral status; (c) whether the thesis that a person is a person through other persons should be read as an ontological claim, an ethical ideal, or both.

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party. Identify at least three, such as: whether recognition must be reciprocated to constitute personhood, or whether one-directional care can suffice; whether personhood can be held in abeyance or eroded gradually through relational rupture; and whether non-human animals embedded in sustained human care networks could acquire personhood under the relationalist framework.

4. For each crux, specify a concrete case, thought experiment, or comparative empirical pattern both sides agree bears on it, with each side's explicit prediction or interpretation recorded in advance. Examples might include: a well-documented case of a patient in a permanent vegetative state cared for by the same community for decades with no behavioral response; a cross-cultural comparison of moral attribution in societies emphasizing interdependence versus autonomy; and a scenario in which an isolated but fully capacitated individual is systematically denied all recognition.

Format the output as the numbered agreed-claims section, followed by the disagreements table, followed by the crux section with predictions. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Avoid framing either side as obviously correct; the wording should be exact enough that a committed defender of each view could sign the document without feeling misrepresented.
