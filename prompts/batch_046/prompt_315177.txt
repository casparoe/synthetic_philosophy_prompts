After I began serving on a hospital ethics committee six months ago, I have found myself returning constantly to split-second harm-allocation decisions and wondering whether the doctrine of double effect can genuinely guide them. I want you to draft the joint statement that a committed traditional double-effect defender and a committed path-dependency proportionalist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on whether the doctrine survives the loop-variant and remote-mediation extensions of the trolley problem.

The document should have two parts. First, a numbered list of agreed claims: about the formal structure of the standard trolley and footbridge cases; about which intuitions are genuinely shared versus contested; about the loop-track variant and the remote-switch bridge case, in which harm is mediated by a ten-second mechanical delay; about what any adequate normative theory must explain regarding agency and outcome; and about which features—causal path, agent intention, temporal proximity, or mechanical mediation—both sides agree are present in these cases. Second, a disagreements table: the precise formulation of each camp’s residual position, phrased so that each side would accept the wording of its own view; the cruxes, which I take to be (i) whether intention retains independent normative weight once causal path is held constant, and (ii) whether mechanical mediation collapses the direct/indirect distinction; and for each crux, one concrete scenario or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each camp’s explicit prediction or verdict recorded in advance.

Please keep the entire response to no more than 450 words. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not invoke obscure scholars or unpublished manuscripts; reason directly from the constructed cases and the conceptual distinctions they expose.
