I'm trying to map out what I actually think counts as knowledge under the Nyāya pramāṇas, going case by case. Seeing a tree in VR feels like pratyakṣa to me, just indirect. But hearing my friend through a deepfake voice clone feels like corrupted śabda, not real testimony, even though the words are right—those two feel similar since both are tech-mediated, yet I split them. An AI giving me a medical diagnosis counts as śabda only if I can trace its reasoning chain; if it's a black box it's noise. Inferring rain from dark clouds is solid anumāna; inferring my roommate's home because the lights are on, when she never uses lights, feels shaky and I don't call it knowledge. Comparing democracy to a market to understand voting behavior works as upamāna for me, even though I've never literally traded votes. Postulating an invisible particle to fill a data gap is arthāpatti only if there's no simpler explanation; otherwise it's just made up. Knowing there's no cup in an open empty box is anupalabdhi; knowing there's no virus in a sealed opaque box isn't, cause I can't perceive inside. So what's the actual principle tying these together—causal contact, transparency of source, or something else? And what's it say about the case I keep flip-flopping on: a synthetic voice I know is fake but that quotes my friend's actual recorded words exactly—do I have śabda knowledge of her statement, or is the corruption total? Give me the principle, say which of my verdicts it forces me to drop, and tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed.
