I've been reading about trying to build AI that doesn't screw up the world by accident, like robots that clean a room but don't move furniture in ways people care about. The idea is always "minimize side effects" or "stay in your lane". But every time someone tries to write that down formaly, it turns out they need a list of what counts as "the room" versus "the furniture", or what changes are "rearranging" versus "destroying". And that list looks exactly like a preference ordering over world states. So people propose structural fixes instead — like only affect things within 5 meters, or don't change more than X number of particles. But then you get cases where the AI keeps someone alive by moving them 6 meters away, or where it kills everyone in a 1-meter radius because that technically respects the radius. Each fix is individually reasonable, but together they show that "low impact" is just "align with values" wearing a math costume. Is there a name for this specific loop, where a supposedly value-free safety constraint keeps collapsing into an explicit value specifcation? I know there's stuff about reward hacking and outer alignment, but this feels different — it's not about the AI gaming a metric, it's about the metric not existant without the values it's supposed to replace. If there's no term, what would you call it, and how do you draw the line between this and ordinary specifcation problems?
