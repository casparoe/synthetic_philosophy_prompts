Consider the following exam question and a student response from a philosophy-of-mind course on consciousness and representation.

Question: Dennett rejects the Cartesian Theater—the idea that there is a central place in the brain where everything comes together for a conscious subject. Explain how he uses the color-phi phenomenon to motivate this rejection. Then evaluate a proposed amendment sometimes called the layered-fame view: rather than drafts competing for a single publication into consciousness, different drafts achieve different degrees of accessibility and influence, with no one draft being canonically the conscious state. Does this amendment preserve Dennett’s anti-theater conclusion, or does it risk reintroducing a hierarchical mechanism that sorts drafts by accessibility level? In your answer, make use of Dennett’s distinction between Orwellian and Stalinist revisions of narrative.

Student answer: The Cartesian Theater is wrong because it assumes one central display space where all sensory input is shown to a single inner observer. Dennett uses the color-phi illusion to undermine this: subjects report seeing a green circle travel across space, change color in mid-flight, and be replaced by a red circle, even though the actual display only showed a red circle in one position followed by a green circle in a different position. This shows that consciousness has no fixed temporal order at all; the brain constructs multiple drafts of what happened, and conscious experience is just whichever draft achieves the widest distribution—what Dennett calls fame in the brain. The layered-fame amendment fits naturally here: drafts can have high, medium, or low accessibility without any being officially published. This does not revive the Theater, because there is still no central screen; it is only a gradient of influence across distributed processes. As for revisions, an Orwellian revision fabricates an event that never actually occurred and inserts it into the narrative, while a Stalinist revision takes a real event and alters the record after the fact. In color-phi, the perceived motion and color change are an Orwellian revision, since the movement was never in the stimulus, and the layered-fame model explains how such a fabricated draft can reach high accessibility without a central observer endorsing it. So the amendment strengthens Dennett’s view by explaining gradations of access without reintroducing a hierarchical publisher.
