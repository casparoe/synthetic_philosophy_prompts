Two friends of mine got into it over beers about whether people are just fancy sphex wasps. (1) He said the wasp runs this totally rigid loop — check burrow, drag prey in, check again, and if you move the prey it just resets forever, never adapting. Humans look flexible, but he figures we're doing the same thing with more branches: deterministic routines all the way down. (2) She pushed back that the sphex example is meant to show what *real* flexibility isn't. People can break their own patterns in ways that don't serve any obvious biological or social loop — like going on a hunger strike for a political cause, or deliberately staying childless despite every evolutionary nudge. That's not a longer subroutine, that's open-ended. (3) He said even that "breaking" is just running the meta-program evolution installed: adaptability itself was selected for, so "choosing to violate routine" is just executing the flexibility module. Nothing actually escapes the architecture. (4) She said if you call every possible human deviation "part of the program," you've made sphexishness unfalsifiable — you've defined true flexibility away by fiat. He said he's not defining it away, he's just saying she hasn't shown any human choice that can't be explained by some deeper mechanism, and the burden's on her. Can you go through their four moves and say who's right at each step — not just who wins overall? Also please seperate what's pretty settled in this debate from what's genuinely contested among philosophers and cognitive scientists, and don't present a contested view like it's the consensus.
