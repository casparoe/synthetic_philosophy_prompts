I've been working through the model-theoretic deflationary stance on cardinality—call it Skolem quietism. The core claim is that because the downward Löwenheim-Skolem theorem applies to any first-order theory with an infinite model, including theories like ZFC that explicitly assert the existence of uncountable sets, there is no internal-to-the-theory distinction that fixes whether a model's domain is genuinely uncountable or merely counts as uncountable relative to that model's internal arithmetic. The quietist takes this to show that first-order formalization cannot ground ontological commitment to uncountable entities: claims about cardinality are structural relations among models, not metaphysical discoveries about the actual size of the set-theoretic universe.
