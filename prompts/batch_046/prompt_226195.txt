Before I evaluate competing answers to what all pleasures have in common, I need the full specification of what an adequate solution to the heterogeneity problem must deliver. Write the desiderata: the phenomena it has to cover (thin sensory pleasures, thick cognitive or aesthetic enjoyments, second-order pleasures directed at one's own mental states, and pleasures that carry an aversive or unpleasant qualitative dimension), the distinctions it must preserve (pleasure versus mere satisfaction, intrinsic versus instrumental valuing, and first-order versus reflective enjoyment), the counter-cases it must survive (pleasure in beings lacking introspective access, or in artificial agents with radically different phenomenology), and any results from phenomenology or cognitive science it cannot contradict, with the motivating argument or case behind each. Flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps (for instance, demanding a uniform phenomenal quality versus allowing a family-resemblance structure). Identify which single desideratum does the most damage to the standard candidates—the pure feeling-view, the attitudinal or desire-based view, and the representational or content-based view—and take a definite position on whether some subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. Keep the weight on the criteria and their load-bearing status rather than on scoring the theories, though a brief note on how each contender fares is welcome. I want a committed answer: state clearly which requirements you treat as indispensable and why, not a neutral menu of options.
