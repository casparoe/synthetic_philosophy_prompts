I'm writing this for an independent study in epistemology at my high school, and I'd like a consistency check on the view I've been trying to hold together about the regress problem. Here are my commitments.

First, I think foundationalism is basically right: there are basic beliefs—chiefly perceptual and introspective ones—that justify other beliefs without themselves needing further inferential support.

Second, I deny that these basic beliefs are infallible or incorrigible. I think they can be overturned by strong counter-evidence, and I take this to be fully compatible with foundationalism rather than a betrayal of it.

Third, I hold that coherence among non-basic beliefs is a necessary condition for justification: if my beliefs about a subject are wildly incoherent, they don't justify one another, even if each traces back to something basic.

Fourth, I reject circular reasoning, so coherence can't be the sole source of justification. Mutual support is only legitimate if at least one belief in the circle is independently justified.

Fifth, in two specific domains—mathematical knowledge and direct self-awareness of one's present mental states—I have an infinitist intuition: justification seems to require an unending chain of reasons, with no ultimate foundation, and I don't see this chain as vicious.

Sixth, I think justification can be socially distributed: in collaborative inquiry, I can be justified in holding a belief that traces back to another person's basic beliefs, not to my own, so the point where the regress stops can be external to the individual agent.

Are these jointly tenable? Specifically, does my infinitist intuition in (5) contradict the universal claim in (1), or can foundationalism be restricted to empirical domains? Does (6) require redefining what counts as a basic belief, or does it collapse into a form of coherentism? Is there a hidden tension between (3) and (4)—namely, if coherence is necessary but circularity is forbidden, does every belief need its own independent linear chain to a foundation, which would make coherence redundant rather than necessary? Please rank the tensions by severity, say which pairs only appear to conflict but actually don't, identify the least costly single revision that would clean up the most problems, and please say how confident you are in your assessment and single out the point you are least sure of.
