For an upper-level epistemology seminar of approximately 14 students, running for 90 minutes, design a structured classroom activity that forces the participants to experience the lottery paradox and the preface paradox not as separate puzzles but as a single, practical tension: a collective body that must act on beliefs it rationally holds about each particular case, while also rationally believing that its collective judgement contains at least one serious error.

Inject the following framing: divide the students into three review panels of four or five. Each panel receives five short philosophical arguments, which you should specify in your answer — explain what features they ought to share, such as being technically plausible but subtly flawed, and varying in subject matter so that no panel develops a single heuristic for detection. Every student privately assesses each argument as "sound enough to endorse for publication in a course anthology" or "not sound enough", with the explicit instruction that, based on past performance, each student is about 90 per cent accurate on such judgements.

After private assessment, each panel must produce two written outputs: first, a list of arguments endorsed by at least three members; second, a collective "panel preface" stating: We recognise that at least one argument on our endorsed list contains a serious error that we have failed to detect. The decision phase then imposes this rule: every endorsed argument is automatically included in the anthology unless the panel issues a specific, named veto identifying exactly which argument is flawed. A blanket withdrawal of all endorsements is prohibited.

Your answer should set out: the precise rules and roles; the required materials and the criteria for selecting the five arguments; a minute-by-minute timeline for the session; the debrief that maps moments of group discomfort onto the formal structures of the lottery problem (high individual confidence, collective near-certainty of failure) and the preface problem (rational particular belief paired with rational general scepticism); an honest assessment of which aspect of the paradoxes this physical staging cannot reproduce and must be supplied by the facilitator; and a brief prediction of what is likely to go wrong during the first run.

Please write in British English. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
