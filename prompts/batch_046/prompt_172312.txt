At a recent philosophy-of-science seminar, two colleagues disagreed over whether the pessimistic meta-induction undermines scientific realism in the context of modern artificial-intelligence-driven discovery. (1) The first argued that, since every previously successful scientific theory has eventually been superseded, we ought now to withhold realist commitment from our current theoretical frameworks—including those that scientists invoke to validate predictions generated by black-box algorithms. (2) The second replied that deep-learning models frequently yield accurate predictions without positing any underlying ontology at all, so the meta-induction, which targets false theoretical entities, simply fails to apply to these instruments. (3) The first then countered that algorithmic outputs are never assessed in a vacuum: they are still interpreted and corroborated against background physical and chemical theories, and those very background frameworks remain vulnerable to the historical pattern of replacement. (4) The second responded that this actually concedes the anti-realist’s central point—if predictive success can survive the overthrow of the theoretical scaffolding originally used to validate it, then success is decoupled from theoretical truth, which strengthens rather than rescues the anti-realist position. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Write your answer in British English, maintaining a formal and polished register.
