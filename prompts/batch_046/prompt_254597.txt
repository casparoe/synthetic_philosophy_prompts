Compare two proposed criteria for identifying the same mechanism across model architectures in mechanistic anomaly detection: (i) causal-graph isomorphism under intervention, according to which two subcircuits are the same mechanism just in case their directed causal dependencies are structurally isomorphic and respond identically to targeted interventions; and (ii) behavioral equivalence across distribution shift, according to which they are the same mechanism just in case they preserve input-output mappings and internal activation correlations across a specified range of perturbations, regardless of underlying causal architecture. Apply both criteria to the following constructed case: Subcircuit X in a transformer exhibits a parallel multi-head composition pathway, while Subcircuit Y in a recurrent network implements a functionally analogous computation through a sequential state-update loop. Both yield identical outputs on standard inputs, but under an adversarial perturbation that disrupts parallel pathways, X fails anomalously while Y does not. Would the anomaly in X but not in Y indicate a breakdown of the same mechanism or of different ones? Assess which criterion yields the more coherent verdict, and explain what the disagreement implies for whether mechanistic anomaly detection ought to be substrate-independent or architecture-bound.
