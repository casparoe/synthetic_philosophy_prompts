Analyze Norway's institutional response to the 2011 Oslo and Utøya attacks—specifically the trial, psychiatric evaluation, and sentencing of Anders Behring Breivik—as a case study that tests Daniel Dennett's compatibilist defense of moral responsibility and just-deserts punishment against Gregg Caruso's free-will skepticism and public-health quarantine model. Breivik killed seventy-seven people; two court-ordered psychiatric evaluations reached opposing conclusions about his sanity, with one diagnosing psychosis and the other finding narcissistic personality disorder without psychosis, and he was ultimately sentenced to twenty-one years of preventive detention, extendable, within a prison system that emphasizes rehabilitation and denies retributive suffering. Treating this sequence as an institutional natural experiment rather than a mere policy choice, map the episode onto both frameworks with disciplinary precision: identify who counts as the moral agent, what counts as the relevant evidence of responsibility, and what institutional options are available under each view. Specifically, explain how Dennett's compatibilist account handles the psychiatric disagreement over sanity, the state's refusal to impose harsher punishment despite intense victim-family demands, and the rehabilitative prison design that offers education and therapy to the offender. Then do the same for Caruso's quarantine model, addressing why the sanity evaluation should matter at all if moral desert is genuinely impossible, and whether the rehabilitative prison design follows necessarily from his view or is only contingently compatible. Next, run the analysis in reverse: ask what the episode reveals about each theory—whether it confirms the framework, exposes an internal tension such as whether Dennett can consistently endorse the sentence without collapsing moral responsibility into risk management, or identifies a feature the theory never anticipated. Conclude by judging which framework provides the more coherent explanation of the institutional stability observed in Norway's response, and explicitly state where each framework is silent or forced to speak beyond its intended scope.
