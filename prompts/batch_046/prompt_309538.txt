compare the hyperbolic discounting account of akrasia — where present-bias makes the near-future reward loom larger so your preference literally flips becuase the discount curve is steep near zero — with a veto-fatigue model: on this view you never actually stop preferring not to eat the cake; instead, your earlier self issues a standing veto that gets worn down by repetition or cognitive load, like a constitutional check that fails procedurally. the later self doesn't win on preference, it just finds the lock broken. which one actually captures weakness of will? does hyperbolic discounting miss the normative residue — that you still think it's wrong even as you do it — by treating all temporal selves as symetric? and can veto fatigue make sense of precommitment (Ulysses tying himself up) without reducing willpower to just another preference curve? be specific about whether weakness of will is a rationality failure or a governance failure, and where the two models diverge on that.
