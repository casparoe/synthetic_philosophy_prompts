I have been working through the interpretive options in quantum foundations and have settled, at least provisionally, on a hybrid view that I am struggling to stress-test properly. I reject both the psi-ontic picture, in which the wavefunction is a real field in a high-dimensional configuration space, and the standard psi-epistemic picture, in which it merely represents subjective ignorance about hidden ontic states. Instead, I hold that quantum states encode objective but non-physical consistency conditions among perspectival records: there is no universal wavefunction, only a network of relative-state assignments tied to specific physical reference frames, and these assignments must satisfy a global coherence constraint without describing any substance or field in space-time. The constraint is normative rather than causal—much like a logical consistency requirement across distributed ledgers—so that interference patterns reflect structural relations between records rather than dynamics of an underlying entity.

Given this position, I want an honest map of its exposure rather than a defence. What observations, theoretical results, or conceptual arguments would genuinely count against it? I am not asking for the usual catalogue of Bell-theorem or Kochen-Specker objections, which I take to be compatible with my framing, but for the defeaters specific to this relational-coherence account: for instance, whether the global coherence constraint can be formulated without smuggling in a privileged frame, whether any experimental protocol could distinguish this network of relative states from a single universal state evolving unitarily, and whether the view collapses into a disguised form of psi-onticism once the coherence conditions are given ontological weight. Please rank these potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and indicate which ones a committed defender of this view would concede as damaging versus those they would dismiss as category mistakes. I would also like you to consider whether the position has been qualified—perhaps by allowing the coherence constraint to be approximate or context-dependent—into a form that risks unfalsifiability, and if so, whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for an interpretation of this kind. Please present your analysis in British English.
