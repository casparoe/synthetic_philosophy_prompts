Ive been arguing with my roomate lately about whether Im still literally the same person if I change careers or move abroad, and she mentioned Lewis's counterpart theory versus the idea that its actually me in other worlds. I tried to turn this into an argument that counterpart theory destroys personal identity over time, not just across worlds, but now I think it proves way too much and Im stuck. Here it is:

(1) Counterpart theory says I dont exist in other worlds—only counterparts that resemble me do—because one thing cant have contradictory properties (like being a musician in one world and not in another).

(2) But the same issue shows up over time with temporary intrinsics: right now I dont have gray hair, but in forty years I will. If having a property I currently lack means its not really me, then my future self is just a temporal counterpart rather than me persisting.

(3) So if we accept counterpart theory for modal claims, consistency forces us to accept it for temporal persistence too.

(4) That means Im never literally identical from one year to the next—just a chain of temporal lookalikes.

(5) But then I have no reason to save for retirement or feel guilty for past mistakes, since those belong to other people.

(6) Since (5) is clearly wrong, counterpart theory must be false, and Kripke is right: I am strictly identical across worlds and times regardless of properties.

Where does this go wrong? I feel like step (2) is cheating somehow—maybe Im confusing world-relative properties with time-relative ones?—but I cant quite pin it down. Id rather keep (1) and (5) if possible, but I want to know what Im paying.
