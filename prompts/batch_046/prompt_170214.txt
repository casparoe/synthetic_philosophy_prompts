After a long argument with a friend about whether normative reasons can exist without a corresponding reasoner, I want to pressure-test a specific tension. Give an example of a conceptual issue in which a free-floating rationale—say, a reason to preserve a particular ecological equilibrium—appears to apply normatively to an institutional collective (for instance, a transnational regulatory body) despite the collective lacking any unified consciousness, personal interests, or single point of agency. The example should make clear why reducing that rationale to the aggregated preferences of the individual members either distorts its normative content or eliminates its binding force altogether, and it should highlight the conceptual tension without resolving it.
