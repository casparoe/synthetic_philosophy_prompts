Write an essay on whether Whitehead's rhythmic account of education—progressing through romance, precision, and generalization—remains defensible in an era of competency-based, self-paced digital curricula. A growing number of instructional designers claim that modular learning allows students to achieve broad synthetic understanding before mastering disciplinary detail, effectively reversing Whitehead's sequence. Critics reply that such inversion produces only the kind of broad but inert understanding Whitehead himself warned against. Assess whether this reversal genuinely undermines his theory or merely exposes it as an artifact of classroom logistics. Reach and justify a specific conclusion: should modern curriculum design preserve Whitehead's temporal ordering as a cognitive necessity, treat it as a flexible heuristic, or reject it altogether?
