Produce the adversarial-collaboration document that a committed path-dependence theorist and a committed convergence theorist could both sign regarding whether history is genuinely “locked in.” The path-dependence theorist holds that contingent branching, increasing returns, and coordination effects can freeze suboptimal institutional outcomes, so that a slightly different past would have produced a meaningfully different present. The convergence theorist holds that functional selection pressures and adaptive demands tend to generate equivalent solutions across different initial conditions, so that apparent historical locking is largely post-hoc narrative smoothing. Structure the document as follows: first, numbered claims both sides accept about the relevant mechanisms—what counts as true causal inertia versus retrospective rationalization—and about the evidential standards that would distinguish them; second, the precise disagreements in wording each side accepts for its own view, with special attention to whether “counterfactual robustness under institutional branching” is a coherent empirical criterion and whether “equifinality” in complex adaptive systems undermines the very possibility of tracking historical causation; third, the cruxes—especially whether we can empirically separate contingent freezing from functional convergence, and whether the distinction collapses into a verbal dispute about the scope of “possible worlds” in institutional analysis; fourth, for each crux, one concrete historical case or structured thought experiment both sides agree is relevant, with each side’s explicit prediction about what that case demonstrates. Take a committed, definite position: do not merely catalog formulations—choose the wording you believe best captures the residual disagreement, defend that framing with reasons, and state clearly if any part of the conflict is verbal rather than substantive.
