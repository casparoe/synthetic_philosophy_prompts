My gut verdicts on when behavorial evidence actually shows there's a mind behind it, and whether there's a real principle or I'm just making it up as I go.

Case one: a chatbot that chats fluently for an hour but has zero memory when the window closes. Verdict: no mind, just a very convincing parrot. Case two: a locked-in patient who can only blink twice for yes, once for no. Verdict: full mind, no question - the behavior is minimal but it's clearly tied to a stable inner life. Case three: a crow that uses tools and passes the mirror test but never communicates symbolically. Verdict: some kind of mind is there, though probably not the same kind as ours. Case four: a system that passes every verbal Turing test across months, answers stay consistent, but if you change a single word in the prompt it collapses into nonsense. Verdict: I think it's still not a mind, because the behavior is brittle, not genuinely responsive. Case five: a human with severe brain damage who reacts to pain reflexively but hasn't shown any goal-directed behavior in weeks. Verdict: I genuinely flip-flop. Sometimes I feel there's still a subject in there suffering; other times I think if there's no sustained behavorial integration then there's no one directing the experience.

So is there a principle? Maybe something like "mind is indicated not by the richness of behavior but by its robustness and integration across contexts, with minimal output still counting if it's consistently tied to internal coherence"? That saves case two and case three, kills case one and four, but what does it do to case five? And there's a tension I can't shake: case two has almost no behavior at all, yet I give it full marks, while case four has tons of behavior and I deny it - so the principle can't just be about quantity. But then why do I hesitate on case five? Is it because I secretly think there needs to be some minimal behavioral expression, and the patient lost even that?

What principle gets all of these, or close? Which verdict do I have to drop if I want coherence - probably case five? And what does your best principle predict for a system I haven't mentioned: an agent that never produces external behavior at all except occasional internal weight updates that mirror human learning patterns, with no observable output?
