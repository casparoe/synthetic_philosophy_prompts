As a philosophy professor who has spent considerable time working with distributed-systems engineers, I am designing an interdisciplinary module on collective rationality and need a precise conceptual translation for it. I want you to take the unilateralist's curse—the structural tendency for groups of reasoning agents to produce collectively catastrophic outcomes because each member, acting on independently justified beliefs, initiates actions without accounting for the correlation among the group’s parallel reasoning processes—and restate the entire argument in the vocabulary of distributed consensus protocols, Byzantine fault tolerance, and heterogeneous state-machine replication. Assume I am fully fluent in CAP-theorem trade-offs, fork-choice rules, crash-fault versus arbitrary-fault distinctions, and the mechanics of ledger replication under divergent validation rules, but do not assume familiarity with decision-theoretic literature beyond what I have sketched here. Translate the curse’s core mechanism—the failure to internalize the correlation structure among agents’ epistemic positions—into terms appropriate to nodes that may hold genuinely different state-transition functions yet must maintain a shared or competing ledger. Be rigorous and avoid superficial analogy: if the curse involves a normative claim about how individual agents ought to discount their own judgments, show exactly where that norm resides in the protocol layer, or admit explicitly that it has no analog there.

Then, and this is the essential deliverable, report the residue with care. Which elements survive intact—for instance, does the correlation-failure map cleanly onto a failure of nodes to share a common prior about network state, or onto an absence of cross-node epistemic calibration? Which elements survive only with distortion—can the notion of “unilateral action” be rendered without importing anthropomorphic notions of agency, intention, or moral responsibility into mechanism design? And which elements are inexpressible altogether in this vocabulary, particularly any claim about collective rationality as a normative standard that can override individual computation, or about epistemic self-subordination for the sake of a higher-order group goal? For each item in the residue, offer a judgment: is the gap a deficiency in the distributed-systems framework (it genuinely lacks the normative or epistemic machinery), or is it a sign that the original philosophical formulation was relying on an underspecified intuition about “group reason” that dissolves once formalized? I do not want bibliographies, citations, or references to specific papers; I want only the translation itself and an honest accounting of what fails to carry across, with particular attention to whether the failures reveal something missing in my engineering vocabulary or something less substantive than it appeared in the original argument.
