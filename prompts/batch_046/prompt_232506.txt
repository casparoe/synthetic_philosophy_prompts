If moral pluralism is correct and values such as autonomy, equality, and loyalty are not merely weighted differently but structurally incommensurable—lacking any shared cardinal or ordinal scale that could represent trade-offs between them—then describing political compromise as producing "gains from trade" seems to commit a category error. Economic exchange presupposes subjective preference rankings that generate implicit exchange ratios; if values share no representational framework whatsoever, then a constitutional settlement that sacrifices some autonomy for greater security is not analogous to voluntary barter but to translating a poem into a language that possesses no equivalent semantic field. Given this, when deliberative democrats claim that moral compromise yields mutual normative surplus, are they secretly relying on a hidden monistic metric—perhaps a form of pluralist consequentialism—or is there a defensible model of inter-axiological negotiation that avoids collapsing into either relativism or inter-theoretic reductionism?
