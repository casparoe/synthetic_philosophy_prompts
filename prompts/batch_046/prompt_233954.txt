As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently turned to philosophy of logic and epistemology, I wish to understand whether Fitch's paradox of knowability imposes genuine constraints upon scientific knowledge, or whether it is largely an artefact of formal idealisation. Please draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps might produce.

The first camp, the Epistemic Modesty party, holds that the Church-Fitch derivation shows the principle that every truth is in principle knowable must be rejected, even for empirical propositions, and that we ought to accept "necessary ignorance" about certain physical states. The second camp, the Transparency Defenders, maintains that the paradox dissolves once we restrict the distribution axiom and respect the factivity of knowledge, leaving scientific transparency intact and treating apparent counterexamples as involving modal or temporal equivocation.

The document should contain a numbered section of agreed claims, including about the formal structure of the argument, the distinction between knowing that p is false and knowing that p is unknown, and the boundary between logical and empirical knowledge; a disagreements table in which each residual dispute is phrased in wording that its own side would accept; the cruxes — in particular, whether the distribution axiom ought to be restricted in empirical epistemic logic, and whether a permanently shielded cosmological parameter (one that by construction interacts only gravitationally with observable matter and lies beyond any future light cone, leaving no indirect signature) counts as genuinely unknowable in the relevant modal sense; and, for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears upon it, together with each camp's explicit prediction.

Write in formal British English, keep the statement concise, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. The reader should be able to assess the dispute without access to external sources.
