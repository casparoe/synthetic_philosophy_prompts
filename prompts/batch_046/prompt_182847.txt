In a philosophy of medicine seminar, a discussant repeatedly appealed to the maxim that 'nature knows best' in order to defend the claim that any departure from statistically typical biological functioning is ipso facto a disease state and that the sole legitimate aim of medicine is to restore that baseline. I am trying to determine precisely what this slogan could be asserting. Could you distinguish the several precise theses it might encode—for instance, claims about evolutionary design, statistical normality, teleological function, or the normative authority of biological baselines over first-person reports of well-being—and evaluate each for plausibility? In particular, separate interpretations that presuppose a unique, species-wide functional design from those compatible with functional pluralism or evolutionary mismatch, and specify which of these readings actually entail that deviation is necessarily pathological and that restoration is always therapeutically obligatory.
