In Hofstadter’s careenium analogy, symbolic structures—symmballs—are usually framed as either being shoved around by the neural substrate (the symms) or as exerting downward pressure on it, as though causation between symbol and matter must be linear and unidirectional. I am drafting an essay that rejects this framing and proposes instead the notion of substrate recoil: once a symmball pattern achieves a critical density of recursive self-reference, it does not merely cause substrate changes but restructures the substrate’s own dynamics so that the substrate begins generating the conditions for that pattern’s recurrence. The upshot is not that symbols push matter or vice versa, but that both enter a co-constitutive loop in which the agent-patient distinction collapses into a temporary attractor state. I apply this specifically to deliberation under ambiguity, arguing that what looks like a symbol weighing options is in fact the substrate and the symbol jointly stabilizing a decision space. I would welcome comments on three points. First, accuracy: is substrate recoil genuinely distinct from standard descriptions of re-entrant neural processing, or is it merely feedback with an unfamiliar label? Second, coherence: does this dissolve Hofstadter’s original question about who shoves whom inside the careenium, or does it preserve a meaningful version of that problem? Third, conceptual payoff: does framing symbol-substrate interaction as reciprocal entanglement clarify anything about top-down causation, or does it obscure the very phenomenon it aims to explain?
