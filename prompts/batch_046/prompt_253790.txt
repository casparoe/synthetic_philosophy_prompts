My friend and I have been arguing for hours about lexical threshold negative utilitarianism and whether pleasure and suffering are ever genuinely commensurable. She walked me into the following chain, which I feel must have an exit I cannot see; the conclusion is that anyone who accepts the threshold view is forced to deny that we can ever trade a minor headache for a concert, which seems absurd. Here is the argument:

(1) Lexical threshold negative utilitarianism (TLNU) holds that suffering has lexical priority over pleasure only when it exceeds a threshold θ; below θ, pleasure and suffering are fully commensurable on a single scale.

(2) Full commensurability below θ implies that for every suffering s with |s| < θ there exists some finite pleasure p with |p| > |s| that rationally outweighs s.

(3) Commensurability on an open interval also implies what I will call the smooth-intercalation thesis: if two values are comparable at every point in (0, θ), then the exchange rate between them must vary continuously as |s| approaches θ, with no jumps inside the interval.

(4) So as |s| → θ⁻, the minimal compensating pleasure P(s) approaches some finite limit L = P(θ⁻).

(5) At |s| = θ, TLNU asserts that no finite pleasure can outweigh the suffering; the required compensation jumps discontinuously to infinity.

(6) Therefore TLNU introduces an infinite discontinuity in the exchange rate at a single point, even though the underlying suffering changed by an arbitrarily small amount.

(7) Any rational preference ordering that permits cardinal comparison must satisfy continuity: if outcome A is strictly preferred to B, then nearby outcomes preserve that ordering.

(8) Embedding TLNU in such an ordering requires representing preferences with a utility function that is continuous everywhere except at θ, where it has an essential discontinuity (a jump to +∞).

(9) But standard representation theorems for complete, transitive preferences require continuity everywhere on the domain; a single essential discontinuity violates the Archimedean condition and makes the ordering non-representable.

(10) Hence TLNU cannot be incorporated into any rational decision framework that allows comparison below θ—contradicting premise (1).

(11) The only escape is to deny that pleasure and suffering share any scale whatsoever, even for minor aches and joys; but that makes ordinary trade-offs (a vaccine's brief sting for lasting immunity) impossible to justify, which proves far too much.

I want to keep (1) and (2): small pains and pleasures clearly can be weighed against each other. I also want to keep (7), because discontinuities in rational preference seem genuinely problematic. I think the slip is somewhere between (3) and (9), perhaps an equivocation between the continuity of a value-scale and the continuity of a preference ordering, but I cannot name it precisely. Which step should I reject, why did it look plausible, and what does each of the main alternatives cost?
