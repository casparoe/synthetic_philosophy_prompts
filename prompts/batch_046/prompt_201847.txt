I'm preparing questions for a podcast interview with a philosopher who defends a strict Kierkegaardian account of the press and the public—specifically, the thesis that modern media anonymity does not merely disperse ethical responsibility but generates a phantom-like collective that structurally prevents the individual from reaching decisive commitment. This has become personally urgent since I recently began editing a small independent publication online, addressing an audience I neither see nor can be named by, which made me wonder whether Kierkegaard's indictment applies symmetrically to the writer operating behind platform-mediated partial anonymity.

Please draft eight to ten questions that avoid generic biographical openings and do not ask the guest to summarize the familiar leveling objection in broad terms. Instead, organize them around three clusters. First, whether Kierkegaard's critique targets reader, writer, or institution symmetrically, and whether pseudonymous or indirect authorship offers the writer an escape from leveling or merely replicates it at the level of production. Second, how the anonymous public described in the 1846 review relates to the category of the crowd developed in the later retrospective on his authorship—whether the former is only the latter scaled numerically, or whether anonymity introduces a qualitatively distinct obstacle to ethical inwardness. Third, what concrete form resistance might take for someone whose vocation requires communicating through precisely the anonymous structures condemned.

Within each cluster, pair a primary question with a follow-up anticipating the likely defense—such as the claim that pseudonymity functions as indirect communication preserving inwardness, or that numerical aggregation is ethically neutral because it lacks qualitative commitment.

Please also include a brief reflexive note: state what specific textual finding or argumentative objection would cause you to drop the framing that the writer is equally implicated in leveling, and identify which part of your proposed line of questioning is most vulnerable to misrepresenting either the guest's position or Kierkegaard's actual texts.
