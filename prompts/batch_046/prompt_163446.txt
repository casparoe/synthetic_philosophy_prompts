If this distinction holds, then anekāntavāda is not a flat logical pluralism in which all propositions are treated identically, but rather encodes a latent ontology that distinguishes intrinsic qualities from frame-dependent relations, an ontology that the classical texts rely upon without explicitly thematizing.
