I am a philosophy student in a seminar on collective intentionality and social norms, with working knowledge of evolutionary game theory and emergent communication in multi-agent reinforcement learning. Restate David Lewis's account of convention—the coordination problem, the role of salience and common knowledge in equilibrium selection, and the idea that conventions are arbitrary yet stable regularities—using only the vocabulary of evolutionary stable strategies, replicator dynamics, and decentralized policy convergence in populations with partial observability and a signaling-cost constraint. Be explicit about how rational deliberation over recognized alternatives and explicit normative expectations map onto selection pressures, reward shaping, and learned protocols, without smuggling mentalistic assumptions into the evolutionary description. Then provide the residue report: which elements translate cleanly, which require distortion (for instance, reconceiving common knowledge as a statistical convergence criterion rather than an iterated mental-state condition), and which have no expression whatsoever in evolutionary dynamics. Finally, assess whether the untranslatable residue indicates that evolutionary explanations of norms miss an essential rational or normative dimension, or whether it reveals that Lewis's framework depends on mentalistic baggage that a purely dynamic account can discard without explanatory loss.
