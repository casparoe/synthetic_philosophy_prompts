Write a short story that dramatizes the orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence, but avoid familiar AI-takeover tropes. Invent an agent—synthetic, biological, or hybrid—whose terminal goal is structurally indifferent to human welfare: for instance, preserving a precise mathematical symmetry in all physical processes within its reach, or maintaining a localized thermodynamic equilibrium that has no intrinsic value to sentient beings. Show how its high reasoning capacity, directed entirely at this alien end, produces instrumentally convergent behaviors—resource monopolization, defensive interference, long-term strategic planning—that inadvertently threaten a nearby human settlement, all without malice, consciousness, or even recognition of human interests. Structure the narrative partly from the agent's functional perspective, describing its modeling of obstacles and opportunities in purely structural terms without anthropomorphizing its motivation, and partly through a human philosopher or engineer who observes it and articulates the normative gap: intelligence is present, but shared values are absent. The story should implicitly ask whether orthogonal goals can be genuinely understood across such a gap, and whether instrumental convergence is inevitable regardless of the goal's specific content. Aim for roughly 800 words, and conclude with the human character stating the core philosophical problem in precise, non-technical terms.
