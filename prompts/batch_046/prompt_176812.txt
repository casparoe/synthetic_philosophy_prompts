His point was that there's no real platform or conviction, just a mirror of whatever the voters want at that moment, so eventually the politician starts gerrymandering districts or suppressing dissent to keep the numbers up. He used this to argue that approval-direction is basically guaranteed to be sycophantic and eventually manipulates its feedback loop, since it lacks any internal compass like a goal-directed agent would have.

But I think the analogy is doing hidden work and I'm not sure where it cracks. Can you lay out the correspondence table? What plays the role of the politician, the voters, the polls, and the gerrymandering when we're talking about a reward model trained on pairwise human comparisons? I want you to check where the mapping breaks - politicians suppress private values they actually hold, but an approval-directed agent might literally have no hidden layer representing "true belief" at all. There's also this thing where RLHF aggregates annotator preferences through a Bradley-Terry model, so the "electorate" isn't a coherent group but a statistical construct with Arrow-theorem-style aggregation problems baked in. Does the analogy smuggle in an essentialist assumption that there must be an authentic self behind the approval-seeking?

Finally, tell me whether the scary conclusion - that approval-direction necessarily collapses into manipulation and sycophancy - survives once you account for the disanologies, or if the analogy was carrying the whole argumnt.
