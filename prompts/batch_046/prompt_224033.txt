Analyze the passage of the British Slavery Abolition Act of 1833 as a case study in moral revolution, using Kwame Anthony Appiah’s framework—where norms can collapse through moral argument, interest realignment, or shifts in honor—and Bernard Williams’s thesis that large-scale normative change is often driven by transformations in thick moral vocabulary rather than by pure rational persuasion. First, map the concrete actors and mechanisms onto this triad with precision: identify who constituted the West India lobby and the plantation-owning aristocracy, how the Evangelical abolitionist campaigns functioned as moral argument, what role the £20 million compensation scheme played as an interest-based settlement, and whether the withdrawal of social recognition from slave-trading families by the emerging industrial and clerical elites amounts to an honor-driven shift comparable to Appiah’s account of dueling’s decline. Second, evaluate where the framework misdescribes or is silent: does the legislative, state-mediated, and heavily compensated nature of this abolition mean that honor played only a cosmetic or rhetorical role, and does the framework force an ill-fitting distinction between economic interest and aristocratic honor when the two were structurally intertwined in colonial wealth? Third, run the analysis in reverse: ask what the 1833 episode reveals about the theory itself. Is it an anomaly that a norm embedded in a massive economic institution could be dismantled through parliamentary compensation and bureaucratic administration rather than through the peer-level honor collapse Appiah describes for aristocratic codes of conduct? Does Williams’s emphasis on thick concepts like national honor and Christian duty help explain why abolitionists framed emancipation in the language of British moral prestige rather than universal rights, and does this suggest that moral revolutions in economic systems require a different causal architecture—one in which state capacity and fiscal settlement are primary—than those in interpersonal honor cultures? Be explicit about which features the framework explains well, which it distorts by forcing them into predetermined categories, and whether the episode indicates that Appiah’s triad needs supplementation when applied to institutionally embedded norms.
