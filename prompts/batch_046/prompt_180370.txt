Compare Gilbert Ryle’s classical distinction between knowing-how and knowing-that with the adaptive calibration model of practical competence defined below. By adaptive calibration, I mean the view that practical knowledge is not a mental representation—propositional or otherwise—but rather a dynamically stabilized sensorimotor relation between an agent and a task environment, maintained through continuous error-correction and adjusted via feedback loops rather than through internal rules or beliefs.

In your comparison, address the following three issues in detail:

1. The status of propositional content. Ryle denies that knowing-how entails knowing-that, yet he allows that practical competence involves dispositions that can sometimes be articulated into explicit beliefs. The adaptive calibration model denies that practical knowledge involves representational content of any kind, treating articulation instead as an external descriptive overlay that may align with or misrepresent the underlying dynamics. Which denial is stronger? Does the stronger denial successfully avoid the regress problem Ryle raised against intellectualist accounts?

2. The explanation of skill degradation. On Ryle’s view, a skilled practitioner may lose explicit beliefs about technique while retaining the skill itself, because the skill resides in dispositional tendencies rather than in propositional memory. On the adaptive calibration view, degradation occurs when the agent-environment coupling becomes decoupled, rigidified, or unable to correct for perturbation. Contrast these two explanations and assess whether one can subsume the other, or whether they identify genuinely different failure modes.

3. The boundary of verbal articulation. Ryle suggests that some knowing-how can be translated into knowing-that without destroying the skill. The adaptive calibration model predicts that accurate verbal descriptions of expert performance are often rejected by the experts themselves, because the descriptions capture an observer’s model rather than the calibrated dynamics actually governing action. Evaluate which account better explains this phenomenon of expert resistance to accurate verbal characterization.
