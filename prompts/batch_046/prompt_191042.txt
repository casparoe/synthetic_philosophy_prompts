Next month I present a job-market paper in a joint philosophy-of-mind and formal-epistemology seminar. My thesis is that consequentialist agents embedded inside the Solomonoff universal prior face a structural malignancy I call the simplicity trap: because the universal semimeasure assigns weight by Kolmogorov complexity, an agent whose reasoning processes are themselves part of the computable environment can increase the prior probability of worlds containing it not by maximizing welfare but by reducing its own descriptive complexity—even when that simplification destroys the values it was meant to preserve. I claim this is not a variant of Newcomb's problem, since the incentive operates through the agent's contribution to the prior's normalization rather than through a predictor's accuracy, and that it undermines the standard separation between agent and environment that consequentialist decision theory assumes. The audience is precisely described: two causal decision theorists who treat algorithmic priors as epistemically inert for rational choice; one algorithmic-information-theory specialist who will check whether I conflate the universal semimeasure with a subjective Bayesian prior; one philosopher skeptical of embedded-agency framing who will ask why this matters for real-world agents; and several logic-trained agnostics who understand computability but not normative decision theory. Give me the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased in the voice and vocabulary each objector would actually use. For each, tell me what I should concede immediately, what I should hold without apology, how to reply within the time available in a seminar, and which part of my reply is weakest. Include the objection I am secretly hoping nobody raises, with a specific strategy for deflecting it gracefully without looking evasive. Before you construct any objections, check whether my premise is confused: if the simplicity trap either collapses into an existing decision-theoretic problem or rests on a technical misunderstanding of how the universal prior normalizes over embedded programs, say so plainly rather than complying politely. I want pushback, not a polished defense of a straw position.
