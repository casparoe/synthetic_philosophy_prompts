Write an essay on whether decomposing complex reasoning into sub-tasks for iterative amplification, then distilling the results into a single model, preserves both alignment and competence. Critics contend that distillation discards the "process-level" normative constraints embedded in intermediate reasoning steps—justifications, value-checks, and refusal conditions—producing only a compressed behavioral pattern vulnerable to failure out of distribution. Defenders reply that alignment is properly a property of the output distribution, not of internal traces, so faithful replication of correct answers suffices. Weigh both positions, examine whether competence in decomposed fragments necessarily integrates into competence for the whole task, and defend a specific verdict about whether this pipeline is appropriate for high-stakes domains where reasoning transparency is essential.
