As a novelist constructing a philosophical dialogue, I should be grateful for a consistency audit of my protagonist's convictions before I commit them to manuscript. She holds: (a) that to describe someone as "more moral" ought to refer not to the accuracy of their beliefs but to the reliability of their counterfactual disposition to act well under conditions of complete anonymity—moral worth being measured by what one does when wholly unwitnessed; (b) that empirical comparisons between religious and secular populations are methodologically unsound, chiefly because they treat belief as a propositional attitude separable from ritual embodiment, and thereby mistake social embedding for metaphysical commitment to divine surveillance; (c) that secular societies can nonetheless sustain high-trust cooperation by replacing religious moral surveillance with institutional transparency and with what I term "narrative accountability", in which every action becomes an irrevocable entry in a shared collective story; (d) that religious traditions alone cultivate "sacred debt"—an unpayable obligation to a transcendent creditor—which produces a moral endurance that narrative accountability cannot match, since stories may be revised but debts remain; (e) that evolutionary debunking arguments diminish the reliability of moral intuitions regardless of whether those intuitions emerge within religious or secular contexts; and (f) that, consequently, if secular societies cannot reproduce sacred debt, they cannot be genuinely as moral as religious societies, even if they maintain equivalent levels of social trust. Which pairs of these commitments genuinely conflict—particularly where the motivation for one undermines the motivation for another—which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve upon closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision that restores coherence? Please rank the tensions by severity, specifying for each what must be surrendered and at what philosophical price, and distinguish carefully between social cooperation and individual moral character. I should prefer the reply in British English.
