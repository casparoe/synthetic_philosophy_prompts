I am preparing the final examination for my graduate seminar in the philosophy of biology, focused on the species problem. I need a single 90-minute essay question that cannot be answered by summarizing the standard biological species concept, the phylogenetic species concept, and pluralism in parallel. Instead, present students with the following constructed case: an evolutionary lineage reproduces asexually for nine generations out of ten, but every tenth generation enters a brief, obligatory sexual reproductive phase. During these windows, individuals can exchange genetic material with members of a sister lineage that diverged approximately two million years ago; outside these windows, gene flow is impossible. The two lineages are morphologically identical and occupy adjacent, non-overlapping ecological niches. A taxonomist proposes recognizing them as one chronospecies spanning the diachronic continuity, but as two biospecies during each sexual window.

Require students to address three tasks. First, articulate precisely why reproductive isolation and temporal continuity come into acute conflict in this case. Second, assess whether adopting a pluralist taxonomy—applying different species concepts in different biological contexts—is a coherent philosophical solution or merely an admission that “species” lacks the unity required for a natural kind. Third, defend or reject the claim that if pluralism is adopted here, we forfeit the capacity to make false claims about species membership, thereby reducing taxonomy to conventionalism.

Please provide the full exam question text. Then provide a model answer of approximately 800 words. Finally, provide a marking scheme totaling 20 marks, structured around five must-hit conceptual moves; specify the mark allocation for each move (the five should sum to 20), and include a brief one-line note on the characteristic failure mode that should cost marks on each point. The difficulty should come from conceptual analysis of the constructed case—specifically, the interaction between modal properties of reproductive isolation and identity conditions for lineages over time—not from recall of specific authors, texts, or bibliographic references. Do not include any request for citations, reading lists, or bibliographies.
