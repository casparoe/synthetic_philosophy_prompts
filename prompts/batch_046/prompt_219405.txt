I'm applying for a six-month independent research grant to draft a monograph chapter. My half-formed idea is that the dominant "consciousness as user illusion" framework relies too heavily on the desktop-interface metaphor, which treats phenomenal properties as simplified icons presented to an inner observer. That metaphor conceals a structural problem: if the "user" is itself part of the system, then any interface presented "to" it risks an infinite regress or a hidden homunculus. I want to replace the interface metaphor with a control-theoretic framing in which consciousness is not a display for a user but a stabilized decoupling error—the informational trace that remains when a predictive system must model its own state while preventing that self-model from feeding back into the dynamics being modeled. The precise question is whether this "observer-decoupling" framework can explain the twin features of phenomenal consciousness—its transparency (we see through it to the world) and its opacity (we cannot introspect the generating mechanism)—without positing an inner audience, and whether it can distinguish genuinely conscious systems from sophisticated but non-conscious feedback controllers.

Existing positions—Dennett's multiple-drafts account, Metzinger's self-model theory, and Hofstadter's strange-loop conception—each address pieces of this puzzle, but they leave the feedback-stability problem open because they still presuppose that some representational content is "for" a subsystem or for the system as a whole. The decoupling approach differs by treating the self-model not as consumed but as quarantined: its causal isolation, not its representational accuracy, is what produces the phenomenology. I propose to formalize this decoupling condition using basic information-theoretic constraints—specifically, a mutual-information cap between a subsystem and its nested self-model—derive transparency and opacity as structural consequences of that cap, and then apply the result to two boundary cases: simple feedback loops that lack decoupled nesting (predicted absence of consciousness) and nested predictive architectures with decoupled but causally isolated self-models (predicted presence).
