Draft the joint statement that would emerge from an adversarial collaboration between two camps on democratic backsliding, specifically regarding Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the United States.

Camp 1 — the institutional-measurement camp — holds that democratic backsliding denotes an empirically observable, directional degradation in institutional quality: measurable declines in electoral integrity, judicial independence, civil-society space, and horizontal accountability that can be tracked with standardized indicators, and that such degradation is conceptually separable from the normative question of whether a regime was ever fully democratic.

Camp 2 — the normative-contestation camp — holds that "backsliding" is an irreducibly normative label whose application presupposes agreement on a baseline definition of democracy; they contend that the concept functions as a partisan or ideological instrument in debates over Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the US, that there is no neutral "back" without a contested "front," and that measurement projects smuggle in liberal-pluralist teleology.

The document should contain:

1. A numbered Agreed Claims section — covering the empirical patterns visible in the named cases, the logical requirements for cross-national comparison, the distinction between procedural and substantive conceptions of democracy, and what both camps accept as genuine institutional change versus rhetorical relabeling.

2. A Disagreements Table with columns for the precise claim, Camp 1's formulation (worded so Camp 1 accepts it), Camp 2's formulation (worded so Camp 2 accepts it), the crux (the sub-question that, if settled, would move at least one side), and — for each crux — the case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each camp's predicted resolution recorded in advance. Key cruxes should include: whether backsliding can occur in regimes that never achieved full democratic consolidation (competitive authoritarian or hybrid regimes), or whether it is conceptually restricted to prior democratic baselines; and whether "democracy" should be defined minimally (procedurally) or maximally (including liberal rights and pluralism) for the concept to apply.

3. A Verbal-Disagreement Audit identifying any residual disputes that reduce to framing rather than substance — for instance, whether "authoritarian deepening" and "democratic backsliding" describe the same phenomenon in different vocabularies — with an explanation of why they are verbal and what would be required to make them substantive.

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke obscure sub-disciplinary formalisms that depend on specific technical literatures. The output should be a document both camps could sign, with residual disagreement made exact.

End with a single line beginning "Summary:" followed by a one-sentence assessment of whether the collaboration succeeds in isolating at least one crux that remains genuinely substantive rather than purely verbal.
