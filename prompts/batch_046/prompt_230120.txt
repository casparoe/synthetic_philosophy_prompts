I've been thinking about what we actually owe dead people when filmmakers turn their lives into drama. It seems there are several distinct ways a biopic can wrong its subject, and I am unsure which failures are gravest. Please rank the following four obligations from most important to least important—using a clear ordering or tier list—and in one sentence for each explain why it belongs there: (1) not inventing private moments or dialogue that reshape the audience's moral view of the person; (2) not stripping away the historical context that made their actions reasonable in their own time; (3) not using their biography primarily as a vehicle for a modern political argument they never held, even when every depicted event is factually accurate; (4) not replacing the subject's own stated reasons for acting with a modern psychological framework they would not have recognized. Then tell me whether this ranking should shift when the subject died over two centuries ago rather than within living memory, and if so, how.
