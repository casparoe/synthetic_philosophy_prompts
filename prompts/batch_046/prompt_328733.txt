I have been reconsidering the problem of free will since my brother was diagnosed with a neurological condition that compromises his executive control, and a colleague recently offered me the following summary of the compatibilist position. She said that compatibilism essentially holds that an action is free when it flows from the agent's own desires without external coercion, and that Harry Frankfurt's hierarchical model completes this account by showing that genuine freedom requires second-order volitions that mesh with one's first-order desires. On her telling, this makes determinism philosophically harmless: even if every mental state is causally necessitated by prior conditions, a person remains free and responsible so long as her will is internally coherent and uncoerced, because moral responsibility only requires that actions issue from a properly structured will rather than from an alien cause. She claimed that Frankfurt thereby dissolved the incompatibilist's insistence on the ability to do otherwise, since he demonstrated that such leeway is unnecessary for responsibility, and she concluded that the central dispute is therefore not about determinism at all but about whether an agent's will is hierarchically ordered. The summary strikes me as too tidy, particularly in light of what I have encountered about sourcehood conditions and manipulation arguments, but I cannot precisely locate the errors. Could you provide an accuracy audit? Please separate what is faithful to the compatibilist tradition, what amounts to a common simplification, and what subtly misrepresents Frankfurt's specific contribution or the broader compatibilist reply to determinism.
