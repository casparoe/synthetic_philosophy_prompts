I'm a graduate student in a seminar on pragmatism and democratic theory, and I've adopted a specific interpretation of Dewey's experimentalism that I want to stress-test before writing my term paper. The view—call it revisionary institutionalism—holds that democratic legitimacy derives not from consent, constitutional fixity, or representational accuracy, but from the continuous, fallibilist testing of institutional arrangements against experienced social disruptions. On this reading, democratic procedures, rights-claims, and constitutional structures are working hypotheses in an ongoing social inquiry rather than non-negotiable side-constraints; if an institution systematically fails to resolve the problems it was designed to address, its revision or replacement is not merely permitted but required by the logic of inquiry itself. I take this to be a coherent but radical extension of Dewey's account of experimental intelligence into governance.

What would have to be true for this position to be wrong? I want an honest map of its exposure, not objections I can dismiss out of hand. Which empirical patterns—regarding institutional stability during crisis, the conditions under which procedural revision improves collective outcomes, or the dependence of problem-solving on stable background expectations—would count decisively against it? Which conceptual tensions within Dewey's own philosophy of inquiry—such as the need for stable conditions of experimentation versus a commitment to radical fallibilism—undermine extending experimentalism to democratic institutions? What does revisionary institutionalism predict that rival accounts, such as liberal constitutionalism or deliberative democracy, do not predict, and what observational or historical results would show those predictions failing? Has the view been insulated by qualification—if every institutional failure can be interpreted as evidence that revision hasn't been radical enough—and if so, is that unfalsifiability a theoretical vice for a position of this kind? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish what a committed revisionary institutionalist would themselves concede as damaging from what critics typically allege, and assess whether the position risks anything substantial or has become well-insulated to the point of emptiness.
