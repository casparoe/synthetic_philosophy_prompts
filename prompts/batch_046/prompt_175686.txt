I'm preparing my position for a seminar on moral ideals and want an internal consistency audit before I present. Here are my commitments on moral sainthood and whether maximal moral goodness is desirable:

(1) Moral sainthood is best understood structurally: a life in which "moral attention"—the direct, unfiltered perception of others' needs as action-guiding reasons—operates with near-constant priority over personal projects.

(2) Moral reasons scale continuously without saturation: whenever I can produce significant moral value at a cost that does not destroy my basic agency, I am required to do so, and this applies iteratively rather than stopping at some "good enough" threshold.

(3) Deep personal attachments, artistic practice, and leisure are not merely instrumental to moral effectiveness; they are constitutive elements of a genuinely flourishing human life.

(4) The objectively best human life is one in which moral excellence is maximized, which requires the progressive narrowing—potentially to near-absence—of the goods mentioned in (3).

(5) There is a categorical, not gradual, boundary between being "morally excellent" and being a "moral saint"; one does not slide into sainthood by incremental improvement, but crosses a qualitative threshold defined by the near-total subordination of personal goods.

(6) Sainthood is supererogatory rather than obligatory: falling short of it involves no moral failure, and we should admire saints without being required to emulate them.

Please audit these for joint tenability. For each pair that genuinely pulls against the other, specify the tension, rank its severity, and identify the least costly revision that would resolve it together with what that revision costs me conceptually. Also identify at least one pair that looks conflicted but actually dissolves on inspection—explain precisely why the appearance is misleading and what distinction removes it. Do not treat all six commitments as equally problematic; discriminate sharply. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if revised or abandoned, would clean up the largest cluster of tensions, and whether making that change renders the overall view more coherent or less interesting.
