I've been rewatching The Matrix lately and got stuck on something that isn't the usual brain-in-a-vat stuff everyone talks about. Cypher decides he wants back into the simlation because the real world is miserable and he can have a nice steak and never know the difference. Morpheus seems to think knowing the truth is just obviously worth it, even if it realy hurts. So I was wondering if you could read the whole movie through that philosphical argument about whether beliefs should always aim at being accurate, or wether it's okay — even smart — to believe something false if it makes your life go better. Like, does Neo actually make the rational choice by taking the red pill, or does the film only make him look right because he happens to be The One and gets to save humanity? If he had been just some regular guy who takes the pill and then dies of starvation in some dirty bunker, wouldn't Cypher's choice look a lot smarter? I want a reading that takes both the accuracy-first view and the idea that beliefs should be judged by how well they serve your life seriously, and asks wether the movie secretly cheats by giving Neo such a good outcome that it hides the real risk of knowing the truth. Does it actually defend the idea that truth is always worth it, or does it accidentally show that Cypher was the sensible one and Neo just got lucky?
