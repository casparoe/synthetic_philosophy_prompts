Okay so I've been noticing this weird pattern in national myths about crushing defeat and I can't tell if it already has a name. It's not just glorifying the loss — there's a specific move where the defeat itself is treated like a neccessary cleansing. The Lost Cause story doesn't just praise the Confederacy; it frames Appomattox as burning away corrupt commercialism so the 'true' South could survive in culture. Masada gets told the same way: not just heroic death, but suicide as preserving an uncorrupted identity that Roman rule would have diluted. Kosovo 1389 works like that too — the battlefield loss becomes proof of spiritual purity, like anyone who wasn't truly Serbian was stripped away by the defeat.

I'm trying to isolate exactly this: losing territory or people gets reinterpreted as an intentional purification, not just a tragedy. Is that part of lost-cause mythology or it's own concept? How's it different from plain martyrdom? And if there's no established term, what should we call the boundary case — like Dunkirk, where the retreat was spun as a miracle but not really as a moral cleansing? I keep going back and forth on whether that fits.
