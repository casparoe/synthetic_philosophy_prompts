So I've been reading about the Anthropocene and the argument over when it actually starts feels totally stuck. You got the stratigraphers pushing for 1950 with the plutonium spike and plastic layer, then historians arguing for 1610 with the Columbian exchange and carbon dip, and the industrial-revolution camp saying 1784 with Watt's steam engine. And then there's this whole other axis about who even gets to decide -- is it the IUGS, climate scientists, or historians?

But here's what I'm wondering: is this actually a disagreement about evidence, or is everyone just using "Anthropocene" for different jobs? Like maybe the geologists are asking "when did the stratigraphic signal change?" and the historians are asking "when did human activity become the dominat earth-system force?" Those aren't the same question, but we pretend they are. Or maybe there's a hidden normative layer -- picking 1610 implicates colonialism, picking 1950 blames the Great Acceleration and nuclear testing. So maybe the stall isn't scientific at all; it's that people pick start dates to assign moral blame.

Can you diagnose what's actually going on? Is it verbal? Are there hidden empirical cruxes? What kind of distinction or formal criterion would move the needle, or should we just accept that there are two Anthropocenes and stop fighting?
