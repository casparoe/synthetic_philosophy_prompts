I am a novelist preparing a series of recorded interviews to inform the philosophical architecture of a novel about a secular ritual practice sustained after the collapse of any transcendent framework. For this episode, my guest is an unnamed philosopher who defends what I will call practical absurdism: the view that absurdism is not the absence of meaning but a specific modal structure of commitment that persists precisely when teleological validation is withdrawn. She holds that both supernaturalist and contemporary naturalist accounts of meaning in life—whether grounded in fitting fulfillment, fundamental purpose, or objective value—share a false presupposition that meaning requires external validation, and she proposes instead that meaning operates as an internal practice sustained under conditions of ontological indifference. I want a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her merely to restate this position. I need questions that press where the literature on absurdism and meaning actually presses: first, a question that sharply distinguishes her view from Camus’s rebellion and asks whether, without the posture of defiance, her account collapses into a form of quietism; second, a question that challenges her critique of naturalist accounts by asking what conceptual work the notion of “external validation” is doing if it cannot be reduced to psychological satisfaction or social recognition, with a follow-up keyed to the reply that it concerns normative authority rather than affect; third, a question that anticipates her likely response that arbitrariness is blocked by internal coherence rather than external fit, followed by a further question asking whether that coherence criterion can genuinely distinguish a meaningful practice from a compulsive or self-deceptive one without smuggling in a hidden normative standard; fourth, a question about how her view handles the phenomenology of failure—when a practice collapses not through argument but through exhaustion—without treating that collapse as proof that meaning required the very validation she denies; and finally, a closing question about what kind of phenomenological report or argumentative result could, in principle, count against practical absurdism rather than being interpreted as a mere failure of commitment. In drafting these, please separate what is settled in the philosophy of absurdism and contemporary meaning-in-life debates from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present her view, or any opposing naturalist or supernaturalist view, as the consensus position.
