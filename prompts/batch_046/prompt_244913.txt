I'm writing up my philosophical profile on recognition and intersubjectivity for a working group on Hegel and social philosophy, and I want a consistency audit before I present. Here are my commitments:

(a) I read the master-slave dialectic in the Phenomenology of Spirit as primarily tracking the intersubjective conditions for stable self-certainty, not as a narrative about labor, production, or material history.

(b) I hold that the master's recognition fails specifically because he receives acknowledgment from a consciousness he treats as non-autonomous; the recognition is structurally hollow, not deficient for any merely psychological reason.

(c) From (b) I derive that genuinely stabilizing recognition requires mutual acknowledgment of equal autonomy—what I will call symmetric recognition.

(d) At the same time, I think that functionally asymmetric relationships—such as clinical therapy, pedagogical apprenticeship, or civic administration—can deliver genuinely recognitive acknowledgment, provided the asymmetry is institutional and provisional rather than ontological.

(e) I also maintain that the dialectic carries direct normative weight for contemporary social institutions: any structure that systematically blocks the prospect of mutual recognition is ethically defective.

(f) But I deny that the dialectic offers a constructive political blueprint; it operates only as a critical standard, not as an institutional design manual.

(g) Finally, I think the slave's labor-mediated self-relation is conceptually separable from the recognition problem: one can in principle resolve the recognition deficit without adopting the slave's specific path through objectification and labor.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and which commitments pull against each other in ways I have not noticed? In particular, examine whether (c) and (d) can both stand given (b), whether (e) and (f) are compatible or whether my denial of a blueprint undermines the normative claim, and whether (a) together with (g) commits me to treating labor as philosophically dispensable in a way that distorts Hegel's overall argument. Rank the tensions by severity—identify the least costly revision for each, and then say which single change would clean up the most inconsistencies overall. Give a committed answer: take definite positions on where the conflicts lie, say which commitments should stay and which should go, and do not present all options as equally viable.
