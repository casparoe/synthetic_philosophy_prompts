As a member of my university's Committee for Educational Ethics, I must help decide on a proposal to pilot a compulsory sixth-form module called the 'Moral Preference Audit'. The exercise requires students to sort their intuitive ethical responses into two categories — legitimate 'preferences' (for instance, prioritising loyalty over strict impartiality) and correctable 'biases' (for instance, in-group favouritism) — using a rubric supplied by the designers. The designers claim this teaches critical self-awareness without presupposing any normative theory; opponents reply that the preference/bias line is itself a moral judgment masquerading as psychological taxonomy, so institutionalising it smuggles in a particular ethical framework under the guise of neutrality. Please draft the committee's written opinion for circulation before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend approval, conditional approval with concrete safeguards and a review date, or rejection; it should engage seriously with the strongest form of the opponents' argument; and it should include an actionable bottom line with a clear time frame. If the task rests on a confused premise — for example, if the distinction between preference and bias cannot bear the institutional weight I am placing on it — please say so plainly rather than complying politely. Write in British English.
