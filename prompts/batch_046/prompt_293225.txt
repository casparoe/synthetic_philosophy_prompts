As a novelist preparing an advanced seminar examination, I need a single seventy-five-minute essay question that tests whether students understand Bostrom's Self-Sampling Assumption and Self-Indication Assumption well enough to apply them outside the standard population-paradox template. Frame the problem around a hypothetical I call the Cartographer's Index: a reasoner knows she is in exactly one of N cosmological models, where model k contains precisely k observers who are capable of recognizing a specific self-referential paradox—the Reflection Axiom—and she has just recognized it. Ask the students whether she should update her credence in model N relative to model 1 using SSA, SIA, or a hybrid principle, and require them to identify exactly where the 'presumptuous philosopher' objection applies within this recognition-capacity frame rather than in Bostrom's original observer-count case. Provide a model answer of roughly five hundred words and a marking scheme totaling twenty-five marks that weights five specific moves: correct formalization of the reference class; accurate distinction between SSA and SIA applied to indexed recognizers; identification of the selection-effect error that makes the update presumptuous; separation of causal influence from evidential weight; and recognition of an ambiguity in hybrid principles. For each, note the characteristic mistake that should cost marks. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Finally, state how confident you are in your answer's philosophical details and single out the point you are least sure about.
