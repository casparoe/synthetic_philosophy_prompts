I've been arguing with a friend about whether Hofstadter is right that an "I" can be distributed across brains — like, after someone dies their patterns live on in the people who knew them, or in extreme cases maybe a self can be spread out like a standing wave with nodes in different skulls. If my best friend dies and I keep her inside jokes, her moral reactions, even her voice in my head, and I genuinely feel she's "present" in my thinking — I think that's a real distributed fragment of her self in me, not just memory. But if we scan my brain and run it on two separate android bodies, both claiming to be me, I think both are fakes; there's no I distributed there, just copies. I think maybe there's one I spread across both brains, but if the link breaks I think it splits into two rather than one dying. But contrast that with my mom having dementia and me taking over her decisions so completely I think like her — I don't think her I migrated into me, I think it's just me being like her, not her loosing herself into me.
