If Maya's operative reason for showing up is not her mother herself but an abstract self-description—her project, her identity—then she relates to her mother through a category rather than through direct attachment. Calling it one thought too many ignores the temporal architecture of sustained commitment.

(3) Jordan countered that Williams's point concerns the form of motivation, not its speed. Even if the care spans years, if Maya's direct reason for each act is that this is her ground project, she is substituting an abstract self-conception for the concrete person. (4) Riley then proposed integrity maintenance as a structural feature of ground projects themselves. Without it, the project collapses, which means it cannot be too many thoughts; it is exactly the right number for this kind of commitment.

By labeling any reflexive endorsement integrity maintenance and declaring it internal to the project, Riley makes Williams's objection unfalsifiable. The concept, Jordan said, explains away the problem rather than solving it, and risks emptying the integrity objection of all content.
