Specify the full list of desiderata that any adequate theory of conceivability as a guide to metaphysical possibility must meet. For each requirement, give the motivating case or distinction that makes it necessary — including how the account should handle the apparent conceivability of philosophical zombies alongside physicalist denial of their possibility, the necessary identity of water and H2O despite prima facie separability, and the difference between epistemic and metaphysical modal failure — and mark which desiderata are non-negotiable versus preferences of particular camps (for example, transparency of conceivability to possibility, fallibilism about modal intuition, or independence from empirical science). Identify any subset of requirements that appears jointly unsatisfiable, with the reason. Then briefly indicate how a rationalist modal-epistemology view, a two-dimensional semantic framework, and an empiricist restriction on modal knowledge stand against this list, but keep the emphasis on the criteria rather than the scorecard. Separate what is widely accepted from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers who work on modality, and do not present a disputed claim — such as whether conceivability entails possibility or merely provides defeasible evidence for it — as settled consensus.
