You are advising a municipal water-authority procurement officer who selects filtration-technology vendors every week under a strict 48-hour deadline, with no external consultants available during the window, and who must personally sign each selection for audit. The proposal pool always contains options that only partially meet technical specifications. The officer needs a reusable decision procedure grounded in bounded-rationality and satisficing theory, but it must be operational rather than abstract, tailored to someone who can read spec sheets and perform basic arithmetic but lacks specialist engineering training. Design a procedure that structures aspiration levels as nested tiers—minimum-viable, target, and stretch—across three dimensions: verified performance, total lifecycle cost, and supply-chain risk. Introduce an explicit mechanism for scarcity-cascade adjustment: when meeting the target tier on one dimension threatens the minimum-viable tier on another, the procedure must specify whether and how to lower the aspiration on the first dimension rather than reject the proposal outright. The deliverable should include: the ordered sequence of questions to apply to each proposal; the criteria that count as a sufficient answer at each step; the hard-stop conditions that produce an immediate rejection; the termination rules that end search and trigger selection; the precise points where measurement ends and the decision must be handed to human judgment because the conflict is one of values rather than fact; and a concise documentation format for the audit trail. Then run the procedure on three hypothetical vendor proposals. Case A should present a vendor that exceeds the stretch tier on performance but carries an unquantified supply-chain risk in a gray zone, requiring a flagged judgment call. Case B should present a vendor that meets minimum-viable on all dimensions but forces a scarcity-cascade trade-off between cost and performance targets, showing how the adjustment mechanism operates. Case C should be the proposal that dominates every measurable metric except a single regulatory-compliance parameter that misses the minimum-viable threshold by a narrow, arguably correctable margin; here the procedure must yield a hard rejection that the officer will likely find undesirable, and you should show exactly where the procedure blocks override. For each case, display the step-by-step run, identify every point where judgment replaces procedure, and state the procedure’s final output. Conclude by stating clearly what the procedure cannot do: which forms of uncertainty it systematically fails to resolve, where it will misfire if used blindly, and why it cannot eliminate the need for the officer’s personal sign-off despite its structure.
