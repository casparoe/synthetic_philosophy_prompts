I've come around to a kind of hybrid on virtue ethics vs the situationist critique. Instead of global traits, I think virtues work like local strategies — domain-specific evaluation functions, kind of like how a chess engine doesn't have general intelligence but calibrated heuristcs for specific board structures. The situationist data (low cross-situational consistency) then doesn't kill virtue ethics; it just shows virtues aren't global personality factors but latent variables with high factor loadings only within domains. What would count against this? Not just the standard Milgram or Hartshorne studies — I want defeaters that specifically hit the local-strategy reconceptualization: empirical cases where even domain-specific moral strategies fail to predict behavior, or where expertise doesn't transfer the way the chess analogy predicts, or where the reconceptualization collapses virtue ethics into pure situationism with fancy vocabulary. Rank them from fatal to awkward. Also be honest: has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability, and is that actually a problem for a view of this kind?
