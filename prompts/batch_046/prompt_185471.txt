Write an essay about Davidson's radical interpertation and whether the principle of charity really forces agreement, or just forces us to map someone onto beliefs that cohere with our own background assumptions. Here's the twist: imagine two interpreters—one a physicalist, one who accepts non-physical mental causation—both trying to interpret the same speaker saying things like "I decided to lift my arm." Each applies charity, but they start with different ontolgies, so they maximize agreement over different sets of beliefs. Does this mean charity secretly smuggles in the interpreter's prior metaphysics, and if so, can interpretation ever be genuinely "radical"? Weigh both sides but reach a verdict: is the tension real, or can Davidson get out of it? And say what your answer means for whether interpretation always depends on shared worldview.
