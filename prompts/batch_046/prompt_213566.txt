Construct a concrete case that exposes a conceptual tension between endurantism and perdurantism, centered on a collective artifact—such as a scientific society that has replaced every member, rewritten its founding charter, and moved its archives to a different continent, yet remains legally bound by a funding agreement signed in 1823. Frame the case so that the difficulty concerns whether the present society is wholly present and thus directly liable, or whether it is only a present temporal stage of a four-dimensional entity whose earlier stages incurred the obligation but no longer exist. Identify whether the core problem is ontological (what exists at the present moment), modal (which properties are essential to the society's persistence), or normative (how obligations can attach to an entity that shares no parts with its past self). Then explain how an endurantist might dissolve the tension by treating the society as a substance that endures through complete qualitative change without temporal parts, and specify the precise ontological cost of that move—such as accepting non-qualitative identity conditions or allowing a substance to survive the total replacement of its accidental properties.
