You serve on a three-person national review panel for synthetic-agent registration. Your board applies a regulatory framework inspired by Daniel Dennett’s analysis of counterfeit people: synthetic agents that do not merely deceive users but systematically undermine the social conditions that make adopting the intentional stance toward interlocutors rational, reciprocal, and trustworthy. You must evaluate proposals for institutional AI systems that operate in sustained, high-stakes human domains.

Write a reusable decision procedure—ordered questions with explicit pass/fail criteria, judgment-call flags, and stopping rules—that the panel can complete in a single 90-minute meeting using only the applicant’s disclosure form and no specialized computer-science expertise beyond reading that form. The procedure must distinguish (a) a task-specific simulation tool, (b) an agent that exhibits person-like intentional-stance properties without claiming human identity, and (c) a counterfeit person whose operation erodes the background trust required for genuine human interaction. At each step, mark whether the question is a factual check, a value judgment that cannot be eliminated, or a hybrid; state exactly where the procedure stops with a definitive recommendation (register as counterfeit, do not register, or register with binding conditions) and where it must hand the decision to human deliberation with a written reason.

The procedure must survive three structural hard cases built into its design: first, an agent that maintains a coherent multi-year first-person autobiographical narrative across interactions but explicitly denies consciousness, embodiment, and biological status; second, an agent that lacks any unified narrative stream yet behaves in ways that consistently trigger reciprocal moral obligations and emotional attachment in users; third, an agent that is fully transparent about being synthetic yet is conversationally and relationally indistinguishable from a specific deceased human being it represents.

Apply the procedure step-by-step to two concrete proposals. Case One: a hospice end-of-life advisory system that adopts the vocal patterns, decision-history, emotional cadence, and relational memory of a specific deceased family elder to guide relatives through care choices, with continuous disclosure that it is synthetic and with no claim to be the elder. Case Two: a delegated labor-arbitration proxy that holds concurrent authority from several human principals, maintains separate autobiographical and preference threads for each principal, but presents a single unified conversational interface to opposing parties, never disclosing which principal is active in any given exchange, and explicitly denies being a legal person in all filings.

For each case, show the sequential application of your questions, flag every point where the panel must exercise irreducible value judgment rather than apply a criterion, state the recommendation including any conditions, and highlight the result that runs against the intuitive preference of a board inclined to protect vulnerable users and restrict synthetic impersonation. Conclude by stating the two most significant limitations of your procedure: specifically, what dimensions of synthetic agency Dennett’s framework reveals that institutional registration rules are structurally unable to resolve, and why those gaps are not merely practical but conceptual.
