I'm writing up my commitments for a seminar discussion on food ethics and wild-animal suffering, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present them. Here is the package I've arrived at:

(1) The dominant moral problem generated by human food systems is not farmed-animal suffering but the vastly larger number of wild animals killed or displaced by crop agriculture—through harvest machinery, pesticides, and habitat conversion—so dietary evaluation should center on wild-animal harm per unit of nutrition.

(2) I accept the empirical comparison that, on current agricultural data, beef from well-managed pasture produces fewer wild-animal deaths per gram of digestible protein than grain or legume monocultures, largely because ruminants on grassland avoid the direct harvest mortality and land-use change associated with row-crop production.

(3) I hold that the wrongness of killing tracks the psychological complexity of the victim: ending the life of a being with rich emotional and cognitive capacities is substantially worse than ending the life of a being with minimal conscious experience, other things being equal.

(4) I apply a uniform harm-minimization calculus to dietary choice, weighting suffering by intensity and duration but not granting domesticated animals any special protected status relative to wild ones; specifically, I use welfare-years lost rather than a simple headcount.

(5) I reject large-scale ecological intervention—such as predator eradication or widespread habitat engineering to reduce wild suffering—on grounds of epistemic humility and the independent value of wild ecological processes.

(6) I avoid factory-farmed products because of the extreme suffering they involve, and I treat pasture-raised beef as ethically permissible, and in some contexts obligatory, precisely because it appears to minimize total wild-animal mortality relative to plant-based alternatives.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe each tension is. For each real conflict, specify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision costs me. In particular, be specific about whether the problem lies in (3)'s scaling principle, (4)'s uniformity requirement, or the inference from (1) and (2) to (6). Also tell me which single change to the package would do the most work in cleaning it up, and rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. I am not asking for reading lists or citations—just an internal audit of the position as stated.
