Please explain the conceptual tension in Douglas Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model of creativity when it is applied to cases of meta-knob invention—where a creator introduces an entirely new axis of conceptual variation rather than adjusting values along dimensions already present in the space. Hofstadter treats creative thought as navigation through a high-dimensional conceptual space by turning feature-corresponding knobs; but this framing seems to presuppose that the dimensions themselves are fixed in advance. Clarify whether the model can coherently account for the emergence of genuinely new dimensions without circularity—specifically, whether recognizing that a new axis is needed itself requires a prior knob-turn within a meta-space, and if so, whether this pushes the explanation into an infinite regress or collapses paradigm-shifting innovation back into combinatorial rearrangement. Take seriously the distinction between parameter adjustment and frame expansion, and address what this implies about whether the model reduces all creativity to variation within a pre-given ontology.
