I've bought into illusionism about phenomenal consciousness -- not that we're zombies, but that "what it's like" is basically a cognitive misrepresentaion, like a user interface with no corresponding inner machinery. What would actually count against this? Skip the naive "but I feel pain" objection; I want the serious defeaters: empirical results in neuroscience or psychology that'd be strange on this view, conceptual arguments that don't just beg the question, and phenomena like animal pain reports or perceptual transparency that illusionists usually explain away. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward. And be honest: has the view been qualified so heavily with phenomenal concepts and introspective-error theories that nothing could really hurt it now, and if so is that unfalsifiability actually a problem for a claim like this?
