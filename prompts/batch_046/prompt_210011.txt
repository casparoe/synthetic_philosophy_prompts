Quick one for the political-philosophy module — I'm playing with whether subsidiarity actually kills federalism, and I think the argument proves too much. Here goes:

(1) Subsidiarity assigns authority to the lowest level that can acutally address the issue effectively.
(2) Climate, macro-stabilization, and cross-border migration clearly spill past any single state, Land, or canton.
(3) So for those domains authority should sit globally, not with Länder or provinces.
(4) For purely local issues — zoning, school boards — authority stays local.
(5) There's no principalled reason for an intermediate tier: states are neither necessary (global bodies cover spillovers) nor sufficient (they can't block local choices without being obstructive).
(6) So subsidiarity collapses into world-government-plus-village-councils, and federal structures become unstable historical acccidents.
(7) I want to save federalism, but I'd rather keep (1) than (6). Which step should I reject, and what does it cost?
