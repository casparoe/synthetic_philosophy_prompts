I'm an undergraduate in a philosophy and public-policy program, and for a course seminar on legitimacy and institutional design I'm tasked with delivering the opening remarks at a workshop titled 'Enforcement Without Authority: Algorithmic Governance and Institutional Legitimacy.' The audience is deliberately mixed: philosophers of law who know Pascal's Pensées well, computer-science graduate students who are building compliance and auditing systems, and a local magistrate who works on court administration but has no background in philosophy. The remarks should run about ten minutes. They need to be built structurally around Pascal's view that justice without force is powerless, and force without justice is tyrannical—not as decoration, but as a constraint: I must be able to address the magistrate's practical concern with enforcement without endorsing pure coercion, and I must be able to speak to the engineers about why a 'neutral' technical architecture is never politically neutral, without losing the philosophers in jargon about code. I also want to push into Pascal's less-discussed claim that custom is so deeply internalized that it is mistaken for nature, and that this confusion is precisely what allows legitimate authority to function—so that the speech raises the question of whether algorithmic norms, once embedded, become invisible 'custom' that we treat as necessary rather than constructed. The philosophical framing should determine what I can and cannot say: for example, I should not be able to resolve the tension with a simple call for transparency, because Pascal's framework suggests that making the custom visible may weaken its authority rather than legitimate it. Please write the remarks. They should be precise enough that someone familiar with Pascal recognizes which conceptual moves are being made, but they must not include bibliographies, reading lists, footnotes, or citations, since no one in the room can look them up. Avoid verbatim quotations unless they are unmistakably common knowledge; otherwise paraphrase freely.
