I've been working out my own view on insecticides and pest control, not from any study but from how different cases feel. Killing ants with a spray inside my kitchen when they're invading food: that seems clearly allowed. Using poison to save a house from termites: allowed, perhaps required. But broadcasting chemicals over a field that kills bees and butterflies along with the target pest: that feels wrong, even when food production is genuinely at stake. Swatting or trapping mosquitoes that could carry malaria near a sleeping child: allowed, even urgent. Running an electric bug zapper on a porch just for comfort: not allowed — it seems like killing for ambiance. Wiping out an invasive beetle that's destroying native trees: allowed, perhaps obligatory. Yet I keep going back and forth on using systemic pesticides on ornamental garden plants; the human purpose feels decorative rather than necessary, but I'm unsure whether that distinction is morally real or just a kind of snobbery. Is there a principle that captures these verdicts? I suspect it involves whether the killing is targeted versus ambient, whether the human need is structural or decorative, and whether the insects affected play an irreplaceable ecological role — but I can't tell if those are three separate conditions or one idea said three ways. Tell me what principle, if any, gets all or most of these cases right, which verdict it forces me to abandon, and whether the residue — especially my hesitation about ornamental plants — reveals a real moral boundary or just an inconsistent sentiment.
