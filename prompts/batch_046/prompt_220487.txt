Construct a taxonomy tree — presented as a flowchart of binary splits — that classifies normative positions on how societies ought to treat individuals whose achievements in economic, artistic, scientific, or athletic domains far exceed the median. Begin from the root commitment that societies should adopt an explicit normative stance toward exceptional achievement rather than remain neutral.

Build the tree from logical space using exactly four binary branch questions, yielding sixteen leaves. Each branch node must be phrased as a concrete claim or scenario evaluable without prior knowledge of the position labels it generates.

The four splits are:
(1) Desert: Is exceptional achievement attributable mainly to the individual’s distinctive effort and talent, or mainly to structural advantages, cooperative infrastructure, and moral luck?
(2) Visibility harm: Does widespread public visibility of this success tend to damage social cohesion, equality, or collective well-being?
(3) Mechanism: Should the social response operate primarily through informal cultural norms and social signaling, or through formal institutional rules and policies?
(4) Domain scope: Should the norm apply uniformly across all four domains, or selectively only to domains in which status is perceived as zero-sum?

For each leaf, provide: (a) the combination of answers along the path; (b) the name of the position that occupies it — American Dream, Jantelagen (Law of Jante), or Tall Poppy Syndrome — or unnamed/unexplored if vacant; (c) one sentence stating its most awkward or counter-intuitive commitment; and (d) for vacant combinations, a brief diagnosis of whether the vacancy stems from incoherence, practical unattractiveness, or mere lack of exploration.

Place the three named positions in the tree, and flag any pair that your four splits fail to distinguish. Additionally, inject one niche hybrid position into a vacant leaf: call it Asymmetric Admiration, defined as the view that exceptional achievers deserve private honor and reward but should be publicly anonymized or symbolically leveled through cultural practice; explain why this combination is logically available yet rarely defended in the literature.

Write for an audience of advanced philosophy students. Maintain a formal, precise tone. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and do not quote any source verbatim.
