Alex is running a pretty strict Boorse-style naturalist line: a disease is just an internal state where some part-function falls statistically below the species-typical range and that lowers survival or reproductive fitness. Since this fragmentation doesn't clearly break any biological mechanism—it's more like a learned behavioral loop—Alex says it's not a disease, just a bad habit that medicine shouldn't pathologize. Jordan pushes back with a normativist angle closer to Nordenfelt, arguing that disease is fundamentally about harm to a person's ability to achieve vital goals in a standard environment, and for people whose work and relationships depend on sustained focus, this fragmentation genuinely undermines those goals. Jordan counters that even on Boorse's own account, sustained attention is part of our evolved cognitive design, so the dysfunction is biological; the values only enter when we ask whether it's harmful enough to treat, which is exactly how Wakefield's harmful dysfunction analysis splits the work.
