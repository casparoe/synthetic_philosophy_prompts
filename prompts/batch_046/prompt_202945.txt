In our school’s Model UN preparatory seminar last week, a fellow delegate dismissed the proposal that administrators could redesign course-registration interfaces—making AP enrollment the default with an easy, transparent opt-out—by declaring that “libertarian paternalism is an oxymoron.” The phrase was deployed to shut down discussion entirely: if a policy is genuinely libertarian, it cannot be paternalistic, and vice versa. I am writing to ask for a precise conceptual disambiguation of this slogan. Could you lay out the distinct theses it might be advancing—for instance, a logical contradiction in the compound concept; a psychological claim that preserving formal choice is incompatible with steering outcomes; a normative claim that any intentional influence on choice vitiates autonomy; or an empirical claim that nudges inevitably escalate into coercive mandates? For each reading, please indicate whether it amounts to a platitude, a substantive and defensible philosophical claim, or something plainly false. Because the speaker used the line specifically to reject a light-touch choice-architecture intervention (default enrollment with full transparency and low-cost exit), I also want to know which of these precise interpretations actually undermines that proposal, and which miss the target by conflating influence with coercion, or by treating the preservation of option sets as identical to the absence of directional design. I am not asking for citations, bibliographies, or author attributions; I want the menu of readings ordered by how damaging each is to institutional designs of this kind, together with a judgment about which version the speaker in that seminar most likely intended.
