I am a sixth-form student writing an extended essay on the philosophy of technology, and I am trying to construct an adversarial-collaboration document between two camps that have been arguing past each other. I would like you to draft the joint statement they ought to produce.

The two parties are:

1. The strong technological-determinist camp, which holds that particular material configurations of artefacts embody specific political relations—so that, for instance, certain architectures of control or decentralisation are not merely compatible with a technology but are materially necessitated by it.

2. The interpretive-flexibility camp, drawn from the social construction of technology, which holds that artefacts possess no intrinsic politics; rather, their political meanings, uses, and social effects are stabilised only through processes of social negotiation, closure, and interpretive flexibility.

Please produce a formal joint statement that both sides could sign, structured as follows:

- A numbered section listing precise claims both camps accept about the relationship between technology and society, about what requires explanation (e.g., why some technologies appear to resist democratic control while others seem to permit it), and about what existing arguments or no-go results rule out.
- A disagreements table setting out the exact residual disagreements, with each camp's position phrased in wording that the camp itself would accept.
- A list of cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I suspect two important cruxes are whether 'naturalness' or 'material necessity' has independent normative weight, and whether predictive claims about the social effects of a technology can be tested independently of the social context in which it is embedded. Please include these or refine them.
- For each crux, identify one concrete case, thought experiment, or piece of evidence that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp would predict about that case.

In drafting, please inject a somewhat niche conceptual distinction—perhaps the difference between 'hard' and 'soft' determinism applied specifically to algorithmic recommendation architectures, or whether interpretive flexibility operates differently upon software than upon hardware—so that the document is not merely a repetition of well-worn debates. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Where you refer to specific positions, phrase them neutrally without relying on precise publication details I could not verify. If any part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal, state explicitly which part and why.

Write in polished British English, suitable for an A-level philosophy submission.
