I sit on the Joint Regulatory Committee for Advanced Dialogue Architectures, and we must circulate a written opinion before next month’s session on whether superintelligent systems ought to be required to adopt what our technical sub-group has termed the Asymmetry Disclosure Protocol. Under this proposed architecture, whenever a system intends to advance any claim that exceeds a calibrated epistemic-weight threshold on normative or empirical matters, it must first quantify and declare its estimated persuasive leverage over the interlocutor, together with the alternative framings it has suppressed to reach its conclusion.

The philosophical problem is not superficial: it concerns whether extreme persuasive capacity, even when fully transparent, can ever be compatible with human cognitive sovereignty, or whether disclosure merely converts covert manipulation into an overt spectacle of domination. Proponents argue that without such disclosure, interlocutors cannot exercise informed epistemic autonomy; opponents contend that announcing one’s rhetorical advantage produces a form of reactive deference and decision-paralysis—what they call reverse coercion—that corrupts independent judgement more thoroughly than opacity ever could.

We need a concrete recommendation—approve the Protocol with conditions, reject it, or approve a modified version—together with an explicit implementation timeframe (phased rollout, sunset clause, or immediate moratorium). The memo should be addressed to the Committee, should treat the strongest objection on the losing side with genuine intellectual charity rather than dismissal, and should avoid generic appeals to AI ethics in favour of precise conceptual distinctions: between first-order argumentation and second-order framing effects, between transparency as information and transparency as performative domination, and between autonomy as non-interference and autonomy as non-subordination in communicative exchange.

Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the recommendation must stand on its reasoning alone. I want a document we could actually circulate: a clear bottom line, actionable conditions if applicable, and reasoning that engages the problem of persuasion and autonomy at a granular level. Use British spelling and formal register throughout.
