I encountered a summary of evidential cooperation in large worlds that runs as follows. In a universe large enough to contain many copies of an agent, those copies share a decision procedure but lack causal contact. Rationality therefore requires maximising expected utility across all copies, treating evidential correlation as if it licensed cooperation. The summary claims this is essentially superrationality applied to cosmology, and that causal decision theorists reject it only by conflating correlation with causation—insisting that without physical interaction, altering one's local choice cannot affect distant copies. It concludes that evidential cooperation either collapses into causal decision theory with an unconventional prior, or else presupposes a non-physical mechanism by which a local decision changes distant outcomes. I suspect this gloss misrepresents both the view and its critics, but I cannot pinpoint where. Could you audit its accuracy: distinguish what fairly captures the reasoning behind functional decision theory and multiverse cooperation, what subtly conflates evidential and causal relations or overstates the cooperation thesis, and whether the alleged dilemma—collapse into causal theory or commitment to non-physical influence—actually follows?
