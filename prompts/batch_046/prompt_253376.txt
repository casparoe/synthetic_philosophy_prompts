I've been working out a position on whether neural networks genuinely possess representations, and I'd like to know if these commitments form a coherent package or if I'm helping myself to incompatible motivations.

(1) Mechanistic interpretability has established that deep networks contain real representations—internal states that carry semantic content and are causally implicated in outputs, not mere correlational patterns. I take the successful extraction of interpretable features through sparse autoencoders and the tracing of functional circuits in vision and language models as strong evidence for this.

(2) The correct philosophical account is not Fodor-style symbolic representation but a real-pattern ontology: representations exist when they are unavoidable, projectible, and causally efficacious patterns in a system's dynamics. I do not require discrete localization.

(3) In practice, representations in these systems are distributed, superposed, and frequently polysemantic—context-dependent across dimensions—so that talk of "the representation of X" is a useful idealization rather than a literal description of an isolated state.

(4) For a representation to be real in the sense that matters, it must exhibit stable counterfactual dependence: intervening on the relevant internal state should produce the corresponding behavioral change, and this robustness should extend beyond in-distribution cases to meaningful perturbations.

(5) Current large language models do not possess representations of epistemic or normative states—such as "this claim is false" or "I should revise my view"—because although they generate refusal and correction outputs, there is no stable functional architecture that deploys such states for belief revision or action selection. The behavior is simulated pattern-matching without the requisite integrative role.

Do these hang together? I want an ordered audit: which pairs genuinely conflict, which tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and what the cheapest repair is for each serious conflict. In particular, check whether (3) undermines the stability demanded by (4), whether (2) commits me to accepting epistemic representations in LLMs that (5) denies, and whether (1) survives once (3) is fully taken on board. Do not give me a general survey of representationalism—just trace the commitments and tell me what, if anything, has to go.
