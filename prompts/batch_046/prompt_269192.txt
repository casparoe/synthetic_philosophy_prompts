For a senior undergraduate seminar in political philosophy (16 students, 90 minutes), design a structured classroom simulation that makes participants feel the distinction between negative liberty, positive liberty, and republican liberty through roles and rules rather than through exposition. Frame the scenario in a fictional polity, Velaris, governed by the Open Gate Statute—which formally prohibits any direct interference with citizens’ contracts, movement, or speech—alongside an unwritten Patron’s Prerogative that grants a single appointed Guardian the arbitrary, unreviewable, and non-appealable authority to nullify any contract or travel permit at will, even though the Guardian receives a payoff only if total civic exchange exceeds a hidden threshold and almost never exercises the power.

Assign four roles: Citizens (who negotiate pairwise trades using point tokens), a Guardian (who may silently invalidate any agreement with a gesture but is incentivized to maintain high trade volume), Arbitrators (who enforce contracts but lose personal points if they enforce against a party the Guardian has informally signaled disfavor toward), and two Observers (who record behavioral shifts). The payoff scheme should use non-monetary stakes—points redeemable for small session privileges—to keep stakes real but minimal.

Structure the timeline in four phases: (1) a fifteen-minute setup and first trading round under explicit rules, during which Citizens experience formal non-interference; (2) a revelation phase in which Arbitrators begin deferring selectively and Citizens notice that certain partners are avoided, without any explicit prohibition being announced; (3) a crisis event in which the Guardian silently nullifies one high-value contract, followed by a second round where Citizens self-censor and Arbitrators preemptively reject risky agreements; (4) a collective drafting phase in which Citizens attempt to write a Self-Rule Code to govern the Guardian’s discretion, representing the pull of positive liberty.

The rules should require that the Guardian communicate authority only through silent physical gestures—never verbal commands—so that domination operates through anticipated reaction rather than direct command. The debrief must map each phase onto the corresponding concept: the initial round as negative liberty, the drafting phase as positive liberty, and the moment of self-censorship before any interference occurs as republican liberty’s core concern with arbitrary domination. Include an explicit, honest note on which feature of republican liberty the simulation necessarily fails to reproduce—namely, the structural invisibility of domination when the Guardian is benevolent and never acts—and state precisely how the facilitator must supply this verbally. Also specify what will predictably go wrong the first time the activity is run—likely that participants will interpret Arbitrator deference as corruption rather than as systemic domination, and that the Guardian will over-communicate—and explain how to correct this without breaking the frame. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not include any lecture material before the activity begins.
