In discussions that combine Dennett's brain-transplant puzzle — the question of where one is once one's brain is relocated or duplicated — with Hofstadter's ant-fugue analogy for collective cognition, a persistent stall has emerged in the philosophy-of-mind literature. One side observes that an ant colony exhibits integrated information processing, collective decision-making, and stable functional organization even when individual ants are replaced; they conclude that if brain-part replacement does not destroy the mind in Dennett's scenario, then colony-part replacement should not destroy the colony's mind, and therefore the colony genuinely possesses a single, persisting mental subject. The other side grants all the biological and informational facts but insists that the colony lacks any unified phenomenal point of view, that there is no answerable location of self for the colony, and that functional stability across part-replacement in a population is not the same as personal survival in an organism. Both sides have been rehearsing these moves for years without convergence, despite agreement on the underlying entomology and on Dennett's own conditions for locating the self. Is this disagreement verbal — a dispute over whether mind requires phenomenal ownership or only functional integration — or substantive? Is there a hidden empirical question about whether distributed neural-like architectures can generate a singular perspective, or a formal question about the identity conditions for non-biological subjects that neither side has made explicit? Are the parties in fact answering different questions — one about architecture, one about phenomenology — under one heading? Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a conceptual distinction that separates collective cognition from collective consciousness, an empirical result about colony-level integration, a formal model of distributed personal identity, or an argument that the question is ill-posed because it applies organism-level concepts to populations? And if the stall rests on bedrock intuitions about what it takes to be located as a subject, is there any reason to expect movement at all?
