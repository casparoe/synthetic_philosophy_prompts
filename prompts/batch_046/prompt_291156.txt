I defend a specific response to Jackson's knowledge argument: Mary's post-release phenomenal knowledge is not a new fact about the world, but the same physical facts she already possessed, now accessed through a different conceptual mode or representational format. On this view, no non-physical properties are introduced; the change is purely in Mary's cognitive architecture, analogous to how knowing Hesperus and knowing Phosphorus can differ despite referring to the same planet. What would genuinely count against this neo-Fregean physicalist strategy? I want the objections that defenders of this position should take seriously, not just the generic anti-physicalist intuition pump: for example, whether mode-of-presentation shifts ever generate genuinely new propositional knowledge in other domains, whether the analogy to Fregean sense collapses once we recognize that phenomenal concepts are not merely descriptive but demonstrative, and whether this defense implicitly concedes that phenomenal qualities have a subjective reality that physical descriptions cannot capture. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a committed proponent of this view would concede require further refinement and which they could dismiss as mischaracterizing the proposal, and assess honestly whether this position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if so, whether that is a defect for an answer to Mary's room.
