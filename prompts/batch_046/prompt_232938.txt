I am drafting a research proposal at the intersection of metaphysics and moral psychology, and I have become absorbed by Galen Strawson's Basic Argument—the claim that moral responsibility is impossible because true agency requires being causa sui, which no one can achieve, rendering desert-based practices unjustified regardless of whether determinism or indeterminism obtains. Rather than rehearsing the standard free-will taxonomy, I want to understand which open questions and downstream implications of this argument genuinely deserve sustained attention over a three-year project.

I am weighing three directions in particular: first, whether the argument undermines only retributive justification or also destabilises rehabilitative and restorative practices that implicitly rely on some notion of moral address; second, whether accepting the argument forces us to reconstruct the expressive and communicative functions of blame in purely instrumental terms, or whether those functions can survive the elimination of desert; and third, whether the Basic Argument depends on an implicitly linear, agent-causal conception of responsibility that recent relational or distributed accounts of agency might dissolve without reviving true desert. Among these, and any others you regard as more pressing, which one or two lines of inquiry should be prioritised for someone with training in conceptual analysis but limited empirical resources? Please defend your ranking with explicit criteria—tractability, neglectedness, and the number of adjacent debates that hinge on the resolution—rather than providing an undifferentiated survey. I am not seeking a bibliography or a reading list; I want a reasoned agenda that explains why one question is worth years of work and the others are not.
