I'm a high school student serving on my school's equity and resource committee, which meets monthly to allocate a fixed budget across student support programs. We frequently face proposals to expand counseling, meal vouchers, or tutoring for students who are clearly worse off, sometimes funded by cutting programs—like an advanced research lab, a competitive travel team, or an honors-track lounge—that mainly benefit students already thriving. I want a practical decision procedure that translates prioritarianism—the view that benefits to the worse-off carry greater weight—into something our committee can actually run in thirty minutes, while confronting the leveling-down objection that equalizing welfare by harming the better-off is wrong. The procedure should specify: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it yields a clear recommendation, where it stops and hands the decision to a value judgment, and how it treats proposals that reduce the well-being of the best-off without improving anyone's baseline. Apply it to three cases: cutting the robotics team budget to expand crisis counseling; eliminating an honors study lounge to fund universal breakfast; and a proposal that levels down every student's extracurricular access equally without raising anyone's welfare—this last one should fail. Also tell me where the procedure will misfire and what it cannot resolve. If my request rests on a confused premise—for instance, that prioritarianism can be operationalized as an algorithm without smuggling in contested judgments about what "worse off" means, or that it requires rather than permits leveling down—say so plainly instead of complying politely.
