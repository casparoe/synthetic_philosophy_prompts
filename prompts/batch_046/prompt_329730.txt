I have been reading on my own about whether your origins are essential to who you are—specifically, whether you could have come from a different sperm and egg and still been you. I encountered a summary, probably from a popular philosophy article or podcast, that tried to explain the view called origin essentialism and then dismiss it. The summary said that origin essentialism claims you are metaphysically tied to the exact sperm and egg that actually produced you: not just causally, but in the sense that if those particular gametes had never united, you would not exist, and anyone who resulted from a different pairing in your parents' place would be someone else entirely, not you under different origins. It said supporters appeal to an intuition about IVF selection: if your parents had used in-vitro fertilization and deliberately picked a different embryo, the child born wouldn't be you, which shows that origin matters for identity. Then the summary argued that this intuition is shaky, because we do not think you become a different person if your parents had conceived you a few hours earlier with the very same gametes—yet technically a different sperm might have been involved—so our judgments about origins are inconsistent. It went on to claim that origin essentialism really collapses into genetic essentialism, the idea that you must keep the same DNA throughout life, which biologists reject because mutations occur constantly. From all this, the summary concluded that origin essentialism is just a category mistake—treating biological causation as if it were personal identity—and that the correct account is psychological continuity: you are the same person over time because your mental life is connected, not because of how you started. I am unsure how much of this is a fair portrayal. Could you go through the summary and tell me which parts accurately represent what defenders of origin essentialism actually hold, where it subtly misrepresents either the view or its critics, and whether the conclusion that psychological continuity replaces origin essentialism actually survives once the misrepresentations are corrected?
