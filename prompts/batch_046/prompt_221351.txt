I'm trying to articulate the principle behind my judgments about when it is wrong to harm or use non-human animals, and I want to see whether my intuitions cohere. Here are my verdicts on concrete cases. Factory-farming pigs in cramped, painful conditions is clearly wrong. Hunting a wild deer for food, where the animal lived freely and is killed quickly, strikes me as permissible, though not obligatory. Keeping a highly intelligent African grey parrot in a spacious, enriched aviary where it thrives seems acceptable, but keeping an identical bird in isolation in a small cage is wrong. Swatting a mosquito that is actively biting me is fine; swatting one that is merely nearby feels unnecessary but not gravely wrong. Testing a painful cosmetic procedure on rabbits is wrong; running the same painful test on mice to develop a vaccine that will save millions of human lives is regrettable but permissible. Euthanizing a healthy, beloved family dog because the owner is moving abroad is wrong; putting down a rabid stray that threatens children is permissible. Eating beef from a cow that lived on pasture and was killed painlessly seems acceptable, but eating meat from a chimpanzee raised and killed under identical conditions is not merely worse but categorically forbidden. Growing meat in a laboratory from a cell line originally taken from a cow that died of natural causes also strikes me as fine. Now consider a more engineered scenario: if we genetically modified pigs to eliminate their capacity for suffering specifically so that we could factory-farm them without guilt, I think the resulting farming might become permissible, yet the act of engineering them that way for our convenience would itself be a grave wrong.

Is there one principle that delivers all or most of these judgments? Please state it precisely, show which clause is motivated by which case, and say what it predicts for cases I have not listed—for example, whether it permits eating oysters if they turn out to be non-sentient, or whether it condemns keeping dolphins in large aquariums for conservation education. Be explicit about which of my verdicts must be overturned if the principle is to remain simple and coherent, especially the tension between permitting harm to mice for great human benefit while forbidding identical harm to chimpanzees, and the tension between finding cultured meat acceptable while condemning the genetic elimination of suffering for exploitation.
