Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between the Downward-Efficacy Symbolists and the Substrate-Mirror Inertists on whether simmballs—symbolic structures embedded within a causal substrate called the careenium—can genuinely restructure that substrate, or whether they are only descriptive patterns that never push anything around.

The document should begin with a numbered section of agreed claims that both camps can sign without reservation. These should cover: the formal ontology of simmballs and the careenium; the observation that reliable correlations exist between changes in symbol-state and changes in substrate-state; the distinction between predictive tracking and causal efficacy; and a neutral formulation of the reverse-careen problem, in which substrate dynamics might generate symbolic descriptions retrospectively and thereby create the appearance of downward causation.

Next, present a disagreements table with two parallel columns—each disagreement phrased in wording that the Symbolists would accept for their own position and in wording that the Inertists would accept for theirs—addressing at least: (i) whether simmball instantiation requires energy or informational transfer across substrate boundaries; (ii) whether the careenium is ontologically prior to the symbolic layer or whether the two are co-constitutive; and (iii) the status of uninstantiated simmballs, meaning symbols that describe substrate states not yet realized, and whether their presence can pull the substrate toward those states rather than merely anticipating them.

Then identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. For each crux, specify a concrete engineered system or observational case that both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's advance prediction about what that case will show. One crux must concern the phenomenon of simmball inertia—whether a symbol, once introduced into the careenium, continues to influence substrate dynamics after the original conditions that generated it have disappeared, and whether that persistence is best explained by lingering causal efficacy or by substrate dynamics that have already been altered and now maintain themselves independently.

Conclude by distinguishing which residual disagreements are genuinely substantive from those that are verbal—particularly disagreements about whether restructuring counts as pushing only if it involves energy transfer, or whether tracking can be re-described as pushing without changing the underlying mechanics. State explicitly whether either camp concedes that part of the dispute dissolves once terminology is fixed, and note the least costly revision each camp would have to make if the other camp's predictions on the crux cases turned out to be correct. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the argument should stand on its internal logic and on the agreed descriptions of the cases.
