The long-running dispute over whether global violence is in decline—pitting those who cite falling per-capita homicide and battle-death rates against those who reject normalization and point to rising absolute harm, state violence, and ecological damage—seems to resist resolution despite shared data sources. Diagnose the stall: is it primarily a verbal disagreement about the extension of 'violence' (direct physical harm versus structural or ecological damage), an empirical disagreement masked by selective reference classes (what counts as a conflict zone, whether state and algorithmically mediated injuries are included), or a normative disagreement about which metric should govern assessment? If one were to add a precisely defined category—say, physical harm caused by automated decision systems that operate without direct human intent—would that move the debate, or does the impasse show that the sides are answering different questions, one descriptive and one about what ought to be measured? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of positions, and an honest assessment of whether any conceptual distinction, data correction, or change of question could actually resolve it.
