You serve on the technical review panel of an international biosafety authority that evaluates proposals to release engineered microorganisms into open ecosystems. A recurring regulatory question is whether a modified gene or pathway counts as possessing a "biological function" within the target system—this classification determines whether the proposal falls under enhanced functional-modification protocols or standard containment rules. The philosophical literature distinguishes etiological accounts (function derives from selection history: what the trait was selected for) from causal-role accounts (function derives from current contribution to system capacity: what the trait actually does now, within a specified system boundary). Your panel needs a reusable decision procedure—something a reviewer with basic biology training but no philosophy background can run in under an hour, without access to evolutionary databases—that translates this distinction into ordered operational steps.

Design the procedure as an ordered checklist or flowchart in words. For each step, specify: (1) the exact question to ask; (2) what kind of evidence counts as a sufficient answer; (3) whether that step feeds an etiological or a causal-role determination; and (4) the precise point where the procedure stops and hands the decision to a human values-judge rather than a fact-finder. The procedure must explicitly address synthetic or horizontally transferred traits that lack an evolutionary selection history within the host lineage, and it must include a practical rule for defining the "system boundary" for causal-role assessment—e.g., whether to include metabolic exchange partners, host immune interactions, or ecosystem-level feedback loops—without requiring the reviewer to resolve deep metaphysical disputes about organismal identity.

Then apply the procedure to three proposals:

A. A soil bacterium engineered to produce a synthetic polyketide assembled de novo from non-homologous fragments; the pathway has no evolutionary precedent in this lineage, but it currently suppresses a fungal pathogen and stabilizes the root-microbiome network.

B. A wild-type nitrogen-fixation cluster with a clear selection history in rhizobia, relocated via plasmid into a non-legume host where it operates in an unfamiliar metabolic context with unknown net contribution to host or soil-system capacity.

C. A gene drive spreading a temperature-sensitive sterility allele through an insect population; the allele's "function" is contested between ecosystem-level population suppression (a causal-role claim at the community level) and individual-level reproductive failure (an etiological claim about what selection would eliminate).

For each case, show where the procedure yields a clear classification, where it hits a judgment-call boundary, and where it produces a result the proposing team will likely dispute. Include at least one case where the procedure's answer is unwelcome to the proposer.

Finally, identify two specific conditions under which the procedure will systematically misfire—such as conflating current system contribution with intended design purpose, or treating absence of selection history as absence of function altogether—and state exactly what the reviewer should do when those failure modes are detected. Be explicit about which steps are genuinely procedural and which are judgment calls dressed up as procedure.
