In a seminar on metaphysical dependence I have been puzzling over a framing that seems to fall between the usual introductory treatments of grounding and the highly technical literature on fundamentality. Call this position monistic hierarchical fundamentality. Opposed to it are various pluralist or domain-relative views, according to which, for instance, institutional facts might depend on collective intentionality in a manner that is not further reducible to physical grounding, or mathematical objects might stand in sui generis dependence relations that do not fit on the same ladder as particles and fields. I am trying to determine whether my resistance to the monistic picture is well-founded or merely reflects my own disciplinary biases, and I would like you to help me see where the reasoning actually lives.

Provide a rough probability and one line of justification for each term, then combine them into an overall number. Tell me which term contributes the most uncertainty and what kind of argument or empirical discovery would most shift your final credence.
