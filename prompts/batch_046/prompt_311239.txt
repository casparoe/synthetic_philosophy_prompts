The prompt asked students to distinguish Rietdijk's argument from Putnam's argument about special relativity and presentism, explain the Andromeda-paradox style challenge to a privileged "now," and evaluate whether a Lorentzian hidden-frame reply saves presentism.

Here's the answer (paraphrazed from messy handwriting):

Special relativity makes simultaneity frame-relative: two observers in motion disagree on which distant events are happening right now. Rietdijk used this to show there's no universal present slice, and he also showed determinism follows because all events are fixed in the four-dimensional block. Putnam then added the Andromeda paradox — a traveler leaving Andromeda carries a "now" slice that includes Earth events centuries in the future — which proves presentism is absurd since existence can't depend on velocity. The presentist reply is usually a hidden Lorentzian frame, but that denies the physical reality of time dilation and looks like special pleading. So the block universe is correct, and since every future event already exists in the block, free will is impossible.
