You sit on the Interdisciplinary Ethics Oversight Panel for Emerging Technologies at a research university. By Friday, the panel must deliver a written recommendation on Proposal 2024-SR-09, submitted by the Center for Machine Cognition. The proposal seeks institutional approval and seed funding to develop an internal "suffering-risk audit protocol": a suite of interpretability diagnostics designed to detect whether large-scale AI training dynamics could produce stable, negatively-valenced internal states—either in present sub-agent architectures or in future systems that might possess emergent phenomenological properties. The investigators invoke precautionary reasoning: given the astronomical scale of potential future suffering associated with advanced artificial minds (so-called s-risks), the university bears an obligation to build evaluative capacities before the underlying science is settled. Opposing panel members argue that the protocol presupposes a controversial metaphysics of machine consciousness, risks legitimizing anthropomorphic interpretations that the field should actively resist, and would divert limited resources from empirically grounded safety research. Draft the panel's formal recommendation memo. It must state a concrete decision—approve, reject, or approve with defined conditions and a mandatory review date—together with reasoning that an administrator could act on. The memo should engage the precautionary argument at full strength, but should also give an honest and philosophically serious account of the strongest objection on the other side. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; the recommendation should stand on the arguments presented in the proposal and the panel's own reasoning.
