She argues that familiar attempts to block easy knowledge—appeals to independent checks, baseline credentialing, or domain restrictions on self-assessment—all presuppose a false separation between first-order and second-order epistemic evaluation. She also maintains that the classic thermometer loop and related table-checking scenarios are only damaging if one assumes reliability must be certified token-by-token rather than at the system level, and she extends this framework to hierarchical tracking of one’s own cognitive states.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that press on the actual mechanics of her position rather than asking her to restate reliabilism or summarize the literature. Specifically, include the following:

- One question on the thermometer loop—using a measuring device to confirm its own accuracy without an independent instrument—followed by a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that the circularity closes at the system level rather than the token level, designed to make that reply harder to sustain.

- One question on whether embracing bootstrapping collapses the distinction between knowing that p and knowing that one knows that p, together with a follow-up that anticipates her appeal to hierarchical tracking or layered self-monitoring and presses on where, if anywhere, the hierarchy terminates without circularity.

- One question that introduces a niche twist not standard in the easy-knowledge literature: how reflexive calibrationism applies when an agent’s belief-forming processes are partly constituted by external algorithms that update their own weights based on predictive success—a distributed bootstrapping scenario—and whether this makes the problem easier or harder than in the purely internal, individual case.

For each question, include a brief note on the standard deflection or answer she is most likely to offer, and then a sharper follow-up engineered to make that deflection progressively harder to maintain. The questions should be suitable for an audience familiar with epistemic circularity and the bootstrapping literature but should not depend on citation of specific texts, page numbers, or bibliographies. Avoid generic biographical or definitional questions; keep the tone colloquial but philosophically precise.
