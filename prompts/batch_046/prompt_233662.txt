I'm working on a piece about how moral cultures embed themselves in institutional design, and I want to move past the standard culture-war tropes. The framing I have in mind pits a German folk morality—where ethical life is structured through external rules, role obligations, and collective order—against an American folk morality that treats individual conscience and internal conviction as the final court of appeal. Rather than asking which produces better outcomes in the abstract, I need a conceptual issue that shows where the two frameworks do not merely disagree on what to do, but disagree on what kind of problem is being solved in the first place. Give me an example—ideally drawn from engineering ethics, bureaucratic discretion, or algorithmic governance—where the German-style agent sees a failure of institutional fidelity and the American-style agent sees a failure of personal authenticity, and where neither framework can translate the other's grievance without distortion. Then say which framework, if either, actually has the better of it, and whether that superiority claim itself depends on smuggling in one of the two moral ontologies.
