I have been working through the low-impact AI literature, particularly the idea that an agent should minimize its counterfactual footprint—the divergence between the actual world trajectory and a baseline trajectory in which the agent was never activated, measured over physical-state space rather than over preference satisfaction. It strikes me that one can specify "avoid side effects" in purely structural terms: the agent may alter only those state variables strictly entailed by its assigned task, leaving all other causal pathways within some epsilon of the baseline. If this is correct, then defining low impact does not require a full specification of value or a complete preference ordering over worlds; it requires only a task boundary and a metric of physical similarity. Yet critics seem to treat value-neutrality as impossible here. Where exactly does the argument break down? Is the problem in selecting the baseline, in defining the task boundary without normative leakage, or in the implicit value judgments built into any similarity metric over world-states? I want to understand whether the claim that "side effects" cannot be defined without full axiology is a conceptual point or merely a practical one about our inability to agree on metrics.
