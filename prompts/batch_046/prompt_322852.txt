The easy problems are the functional ones - how the brain integrates information, controls behavior, and processes sensory input. The hard problem is why any of that is accompanied by subjective experience, why it feels like something to be conscious. Then there's the zombie argument. A zombie is physically identical to a person but lacks consciousness entirely. Chalmers claims these are conceivable, and if you can conceive of them without contradiction then consciousness isn't logically tied to the physical facts. That means physicalism is false. Nagel makes basically the same move in his bat essay, where he argues that subjective character is left out of scientific accounts. So if science can fully describe a body without mentioning what it's like, then you could have that body with nothing going on inside - a zombie. Both arguments point to an explanatory gap that physicalism can't bridge.
