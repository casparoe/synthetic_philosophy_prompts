For a seminar on historical method in New Testament studies, I want the full specification of what an adequate authentication protocol for sayings and deeds attributed to the historical Jesus must deliver if it is to do more than redescribe the Gospel traditions and actually help settle who he was. Write the desiderata: the cases the method must get right, the puzzles it must dissolve or explain, the distinctions it must preserve, and the results from adjacent disciplines—particularly oral-tradition dynamics and social-memory theory—it must not contradict, with the motivating consideration behind each. Be explicit about distinctions that are easy to collapse but must be kept apart, such as authenticity of substance versus authenticity of precise wording, discontinuity with Second Temple Judaism versus discontinuity with emergent Christian practice, and independence of attestation versus independence after accounting for source interdependence and redactional framing. Identify which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable for any serious reconstruction and which are preferences of particular camps, especially the tension between those who treat dissimilarity as the primary gatekeeper and those who treat coherence with an already-established core as indispensable. Then flag any subset of these desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether the demand for multiply-independent attestation can survive once cross-source contamination and evangelist framing are fully acknowledged—and explain the conflict. Finally, briefly indicate how the classic multi-criterion framework, the plausibility-and-coherence approach, and social-memory reconstruction models fare against the list, but keep the weight on the specification itself rather than on the scorecard. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
