Write a blank-verse poem of about 18–22 lines, arranged in alternating couplets: one voice in the quantitative register of historical explanation, the other in the prohibitive register of moral comparison, addressing whether ranking historical atrocities is conceptually coherent or morally obscene. Do not resolve the tension; instead, have each voice charge the other with category error—specifically, keep visible the distinction between an epistemic ordering of events for causal understanding and a normative ordering of suffering for comparative moral weight. End on an image that suggests both voices are measuring the same object but seeing incommensurable things. Keep the diction precise and avoid sermonizing or shrugging; the poem should leave the conceptual disagreement intact rather than dissolving it into either a circle-of-life acceptance or a call to action.
