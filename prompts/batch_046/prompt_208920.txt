I sit on the funding review board for the Meridian Institute's AI governance program, and we're meeting Thrusday to vote on the Chen-Okonkwo proposal. They're building a verification framework for autonomous decision systems and they adopt what they call the narrow alignment definition: intent alignment only, which they define as perfect behavioral conformance to the designers' explicitly specified objective function — they even propose a two-layer audit where Layer 1 checks behavioral fidelity and Layer 2 checks for value residue, but they want to fund only Layer 1. They reject value alignment — the idea that the system should internalize or share human moral values — as technically intractable and philosophically muddled, since they claim there's no consensus on what values should be shared and that Layer 2 audits always collapse into subjective interpretation. The pilot system is for emergency medical resource allocation across regional hospitals, and their core arguement is that if we can guarantee the AI executes the triage protocol with complete fidelity via Layer 1, then the alignment problem is solved; any moral disagreement about the protocol itself is a political problem, not an AI safety failure, and funding Layer 2 would just politicize engineering.

Half the board, including my co-chair, wants to approve this outright because they think separating technical alignment from normative politics is exactly the kind of rigor we need, and they say Layer 2 is just ethics-washing. The other half — I'm somewhere in the middle but leaning skeptical — worries that this distinction collapses in practice because the specified objective already embeds controversial normative commitments about whose lives count more in triage, and without some form of value alignment or at least Layer 2 monitoring there's no mechanism to catch when the specification itself diverges from the values of affected communities. I need you to draft the board's decision memo: recommend approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, with concrete reasoning we can actually act on by Friday. Please take a definite position rather than just laying out both sides, and be honest about the strongest consideration for the view you reject. Also, since I'm writing a course paper on this, could you end with a short note flagging where your reasoning takes liberties with the intent/value distinction or leans on a contested interpretation of it?
