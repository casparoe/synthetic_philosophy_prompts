I've been arguing with a friend that the DNA–Gödel isomorphism—read through Hofstadter's typogenetic framework—should be taken as a structural identity rather than a metaphor: biological self-replication requires the encoding scheme to be physically embedded in the very object it describes, which is exactly the condition Gödel proved necessary for formal systems to achieve self-reference. On this view, codons operate analogously to Gödel numbers and the translation machinery functions as a biological decoder, so replication is not merely parallel to arithmetic self-reference but realizes the same logical architecture in chemistry. My friend objects that this illicitly projects mathematical necessity onto evolutionary contingency, but I think that misses the point—I am claiming that wherever self-replication occurs it must satisfy that embedding condition, not that chemistry was designed to satisfy logic. Still, I want an honest map of the exposure. What empirical findings about error-correction mechanisms or redundant encoding, what formal results about self-reference in weaker logical systems lacking full arithmetic, and what conceptual distinctions between syntactic decoding and mere causal chemical interaction would genuinely count against this identity claim? Which defeaters would force a retreat to a weaker analogy, which ones would a defender of the structural view have to concede as damaging, and does the position risk anything empirically testable or has it been insulated by qualifications that render it unfalsifiable? I am especially interested in whether there is any result in molecular biology or in the logic of self-reference that would make the core claim collapse rather than merely awkward.
