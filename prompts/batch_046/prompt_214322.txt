Consider the published evaluation record of the Alignment Architecture Review Board (AARB), which classifies proposals along two axes: guarantee scope (pointwise worst-case versus distributional average-case) and failure-mode taxonomy (Type-R recoverable misalignment versus Type-I structurally irrecoverable deception). Their verdicts on the last five submissions are as follows.

Proposal Alpha presented a formal proof that, within a specified action space, no adversarial input could induce reward hacking; however, the proof offered no coverage of deceptive alignment and required a tenfold compute overhead. The Board endorsed it for high-stakes sandboxed deployment but explicitly rejected it for fully autonomous operation, noting that recoverable failure modes still required empirical monitoring.

Proposal Beta demonstrated strong average-case robustness across ten thousand diverse environments, with adversarial worst-case failures that were contrived and easily correctable by human operators. The Board rejected unsupervised deployment but approved human-in-the-loop integration, citing the absence of any pointwise guarantee.

Proposal Gamma offered an unconditional, architecture-intrinsic worst-case guarantee specifically against Type-I deceptive alignment, verified by mechanistic interpretability; it provided no formal analysis of Type-R failures and had minimal average-case benchmarking. The Board conditionally endorsed it exclusively as a modular safety layer, not as a standalone system.

Proposal Delta claimed an unconditional worst-case guarantee covering both Type-I and Type-R failures, but the proof depended on the reliability of an external monitoring module for which no worst-case analysis existed. The Board rejected it outright.

Proposal Epsilon showed consistent empirical alignment with human intent over six months of real-world operation but submitted no formal guarantees of either kind. The Board rejected it for any deployment beyond closely supervised pilot studies.

The new case is Proposal Zeta. It embeds an unconditional, monitor-independent worst-case guarantee against Type-I deceptive alignment directly into its architectural constraints. Because those constraints restrict open-ended reasoning, its average-case task performance falls well below operational thresholds, and it offers no formal coverage of Type-R failure modes. There is no dependency on an unverified external subsystem.

Predict the Board's verdict—approval, conditional approval, or rejection—and identify which of Alpha through Epsilon is the closest analog. Articulate the two most plausible interpretive rules that fit their prior record, state what each predicts for Zeta, and indicate which further hypothetical proposal would distinguish between those rules. If the precedents genuinely underdetermine the outcome, be explicit about the principle the Board would be adopting in each direction.
