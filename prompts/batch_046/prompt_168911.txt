Write a short story—no more than eight hundred words—that dramatizes Borges’s idealist denial of temporal succession, specifically his claim that the present is merely a false division of an eternal, simultaneous now. The protagonist should be a “reverse-memorist”: she remembers with perfect clarity every event that, by conventional reckoning, has not yet occurred, yet retains no trace of anything conventionally regarded as past. Set the narrative in a library whose volumes are arranged not by author or subject but by the emotional temperature of their protagonists—measured in gradations of solitude, awe, and regret. The reverse-memorist must locate and attempt to read a biography of herself that concludes with her own birth, treating that conclusion as an origin rather than a terminus. Do not deploy familiar Borgesian motifs—mirrors, tigers, infinite hexagonal galleries—as mere ornament; instead, use the library’s emotional taxonomy and the inverted mnemonic architecture to render tangible the idea that if the present lacks reality, then causation and personal identity become symmetrical illusions rather than directional processes. The result should read as a philosophical thought experiment cast in narrative form, avoiding magical-realist pastiche and avoiding any reference to specific editions or secondary scholarship.
