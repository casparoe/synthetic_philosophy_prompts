Construct a thought experiment to specifications. The purpose of the case is to isolate whether organizational structure can cause uniform behavior that remains inexplicable once every individual's private calculus is held constant. Design a realistic workplace scenario—no more than one paragraph—in which every senior analyst suddenly abandons a standard software tool for an unofficial workaround. The methodological-individualist explanation must trace the shift entirely to changed private incentives or information costs faced by each agent, with no appeal to collective intentions, emergent norms, or structural causation. The methodological-holist explanation must trace the shift to an organizational-level property—specifically, a change in the topology of visibility and interaction—operating independently of any preference change. Inject the following niche mechanism: adoption is governed by a silent-quorum rule, where each agent switches only upon observing that a locally defined peer subset (not the whole firm) has switched, but the boundary of that subset is fixed entirely by an institutional seating-plan and meeting-schedule matrix that no agent designed or globally perceives. After the scenario, show the reasoning for each theory: state what each predicts about adoption rates and about the counterfactual in which the seating-plan matrix is randomized while individual incentives stay fixed. Identify the structural feature doing the work, say whether removing it collapses the case into a standard rational-choice story, and explain how a defender of the losing theory would attack it—specifically, whether they could redescribe the seating-plan effect as merely an aggregation of individual observation costs. Keep everything realistic: no exotic physics, no perfect predictors, payoffs a normal person could face. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
