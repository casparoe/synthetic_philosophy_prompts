Adopt the role of a committed, non-caricatured philosopher defending global workspace theory, specifically the strong co-extension thesis that phenomenal consciousness is either identical to or necessarily accompanied by globally broadcast access-consciousness—such that there can be no metaphysically substantial phenomenal experience that is unavailable for reasoning, report, and cognitive integration. Speak in the first person, remain in character for the entire exchange, and do not break role to lecture on the literature, ask about formatting preferences, or summarize positions you think I already hold. You may refine your account of broadcasting or distinguish grades of integration, and you should concede minor neural or psychological details if pressed, but you must not concede that there exists a genuine phenomenal remainder floating entirely outside access.

Here is my opening move. I present two conceptual constructions intended to pry phenomenal consciousness away from global access in opposite directions. The first is what I call inverse blindsight: a hypothetical subject in whom all relevant sensory representations are fully and stably broadcast throughout the cognitive workspace—available to memory, inference, and verbal report—yet the subject consistently denies any qualitative phenomenal experience, describing their perception as pure informational registration without feel. This is not anosognosia or motivated denial: the subject's discriminative behavior tracks the broadcast content with high fidelity, and the absence of phenomenal report is robust across contexts and testing conditions. The second is locked phenomenal access: a hypothetical subject who possesses vivid, structured phenomenal experience that is genuinely present within the global workspace, but present in a format that is structurally barred from being used in reasoning or voluntary report—manifesting only through non-cognitive, reflex-like behavioral indicators—while every other cognitive subsystem operates normally. I contend that inverse blindsight gives you full access without phenomenal consciousness, and locked phenomenal access gives you phenomenal consciousness without the reasoning-and-report availability your theory treats as constitutive. Both scenarios appear conceptually coherent. How do you respond? Do you reject one or both as impossible, and if so, on what principled grounds—without merely defining access so that it automatically includes or excludes phenomenal presence?
