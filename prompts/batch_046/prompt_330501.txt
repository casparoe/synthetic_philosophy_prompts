For a story I'm filing on philosophy of science, I'm stuck on why the Humean vs anti-Humean law debate never dies. The regularity camp - Best System Account, minimal ontology, laws as summaries of the mosaic - says anti-Humeans smuggle in modal glue with zero empirical payoff. The necessitarians fire back that without governing relations science can't really explain anything; correlation isn't constraint. Recently I've heard people call it a split between 'descriptive metaphysics' and 'explanitory metaphysics,' but that sounds like a relabeling, not progress. Is this a real ontologcial clash, a verbal fight over 'law,' or are they answering different questions - one about what's in the world, one about what makes a scientific explanation good? There's also this weird parallel to map-territory debates: both sides agree on the empirical mosaic, they just disagree on whether a satisfying explanation needs to reference something outside the pattern. Diagnose the stall. What are they actually disagreeing about, and what kind of result - formal proof, empirical finding, conceptual distinction - would count as actual progress?
