Write a Petrarchan sonnet—fourteen lines, iambic pentameter, octave with rhyme scheme ABBA ABBA and sestet with rhyme scheme CDECDE—voiced from the perspective of a philosophical illusionist about phenomenal consciousness. In the octave, build the familiar first-person report of what it is like to see red, but embed the phi-phenomenon analogy: present the apparent qualitative glow as analogous to apparent motion across discrete frames, a feature that seems intrinsic to the stimulus but is actually projected by the processing architecture. Arrange the octave rhymes so that sensory language pairs with mechanistic language—pairing words like glow with flow, or red with fed—so that the rhyme scheme itself links appearance to underlying mechanism. At the volta in line 9, turn introspection upon itself: the speaker should attempt to seize and hold the qualitative feel and find nothing fixed there, only a judgment about judgment, dissolving the seeming presence in the act of inspection. In the sestet, do not deny that experience occurs; instead, redescribe it as virtual—like motion in cinema or a virtual object in computation—present in the representation but absent from the substrate, so that the subject who would suffer the illusion dissolves into the representational system. Let the sestet rhymes blur presence and absence—pairing there with bare, or mind with find—so that the formal closure sounds like resolution while actually erasing the boundary between experiencing and representing. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with illusionist arguments, especially the claim that phenomenal properties function as illusory properties analogous to virtual objects, recognizes the precise move, yet it should stand as poetry for someone who knows the debate only by rumor. Do not include citations, footnotes, or bibliographic references, and write in American English.
