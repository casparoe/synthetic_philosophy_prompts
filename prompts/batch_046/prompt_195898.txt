I'm trying to reconcile procreative beneficence—roughly, that parents should choose the reproductive option expected to yield the best life for their child—with liberal eugenics, which holds that the state should remain neutral among reproductive choices while parents may act on well-being reasons. My worry is that if beneficence genuinely directs parents toward the option with the highest expected well-being, and liberal eugenics only blocks state interference rather than weakening those reasons, then meaningful genetic pluralism collapses: all well-informed parents should converge on the same optimal genetic profile. To model this, I've sketched a genetic pluralism index that weights available reproductive options by the variance in expected well-being across them, and on that measure any positive weight on well-being seems to drive pluralism toward zero. Is the mistake in assuming that well-being maximization singles out one unique genome, or is there a deeper tension between directive procreative reasons and the pluralistic commitments of liberal eugenics that my index is actually capturing?
