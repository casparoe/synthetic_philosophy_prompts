Having spent several decades in experimental physics before turning, in retirement, to questions in moral philosophy, I have become increasingly struck by the empirical literature suggesting that populations characterized as Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic—WEIRD societies—exhibit moral psychological profiles that diverge sharply from those found in most other human societies, particularly with respect to the weight assigned to individual autonomy, contractual fairness, and harm-based reasoning relative to communal loyalty, hierarchical respect, and purity-based norms. Rather than requesting a survey of studies or a bibliography, I would like you to set out the most important philosophical implications of this divergence for debates over moral objectivity. In particular, please distinguish carefully between descriptive cross-cultural variation—which may reflect adaptive social coordination or ecological pressures—and genuine normative disagreement about which moral principles are correct, and then examine whether the WEIRD pattern undermines robust moral realism, lends support to constructivist or relativist alternatives, or can be accommodated within an objectivist framework that treats apparent disagreement as largely a matter of framing or non-moral background conditions.

I am especially interested in whether one can defend a coherent intermediate position—call it "indexical moral objectivism"—according to which moral truths are genuinely binding but are indexed to particular social-ecological niches in a way that does not collapse into mere descriptive anthropology, and I want to know what conditions such a view must satisfy to preserve normative force rather than becoming a disguised form of cultural description. Finally, please address the specific concern that Western academic moral philosophy, by taking WEIRD intuitions as the default, may have systematically confused locally stable coordination equilibria with universal moral requirements, and discuss what this implies for the methodology of normative ethics if it is to avoid parochialism while still claiming to deliver truths that transcend local custom.
