i need a retirement toast for dr. elena voss, stepping down after thirty years in moral philosophy. the dinner is next thursday; half the room are her grad studnets who sat through her seminars on welfare metrics, the other half are family who think she teaches "happiness surveys." her actual view - she calls it lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism - is that pleasure and pain aren't on one scale at all. there's a structural break: once suffering crosses a certain intensity it dominates lexically, and no aggregation of small pleasures can ever offset even minimal suffering. she thinks this kills standard cost-benefit ethics and most policy analysis.

the speech needs to honor her without betraying the view. that means i can't say she "maximized wellbeing" or wish her "years of pure joy," since that assumes the commensurability she rejects. but i also can't turn the toast into a seminar. her retirement plan - running a sanctuary for injured farm animals - looks like a cute hobby to her relatives but is actually the direct practical conclusion of her position: reducing suffering takes absolute lexical priority over generating human pleasure. i want the toast to make that visible to the family without alienating them, and to signal to the students that the philosophy is doing real structural work, not just decoration.

please write it, about 600-700 words, warm and personal, with the philosophical constraint actually shaping what can and can't be said. don't include citations or a bibliography - work from the idea, not her specific papers. maybe address her sister directly at one point. keep the tone casual; this isn't an academic lecture.
