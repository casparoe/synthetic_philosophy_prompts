A neighborhood board is deciding whether to convert our shared green space into an open-air amphitheater for an immersive theater collective. Their productions assign audience members roles—sometimes as sympathetic wrongdoers—in dramatized local histories. One opponent, who is not generally anti-art, argues that giving vice an attractive form cultivates a taste for it rather than condemning it; that performing assigned emotional roles teaches people to treat their own character as costume; and that replacing direct, unmediated gathering with staged spectacle turns citizens into passive observers of one another rather than equal participants. Another board member dismisses this as elitist. I find the first objection resonant but can't articulate it. Can you lay out the precise philosophical claims hidden in her argument—separating the corruption-of-taste claim from the authenticity claim from the civic-participation claim—and identify whether there is a coherent view that rejects this specific form of theatrical spectacle without rejecting art or public culture altogether? If so, apply it: should the green space remain unmediated common ground, or is there a form of collective performance that avoids these dangers?
