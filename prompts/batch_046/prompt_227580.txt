Write an essay on whether adopting Daniel Dennett’s intentional stance toward contemporary large language models is ever rationally warranted, or whether it remains an unjustified anthropomorphism. First, lay out the standard instrumentalist defense: belief-attribution to AI need carry no ontological commitment, serving merely as a predictive heuristic that outperforms physical-level descriptions. Then present the counter-argument that biological intentionality is anchored in evolutionary history, functional integration, and a stable “center of narrative gravity,” none of which AI architectures possess; on this view, attributing beliefs to models is not harmless shorthand but a category error. Next, introduce and defend a mediating position—call it “cross-contextual coherence attribution”—which holds that thin intentional attribution is justified only when a system’s representations exhibit stable, corrective feedback loops across disparate domains, such that treating it as holding beliefs genuinely improves prediction and explanation regardless of substrate. Apply this criterion specifically to LLMs: examine whether their context-dependent, distributed representations constitute such coherence, or whether their “beliefs” are always reconstructed on the fly without causal integration, collapsing attribution back into metaphor. Reach and defend a clear verdict: either argue that current LLMs fail the coherence criterion and therefore should not be treated as intentional agents even instrumentally, or argue that they meet it in specific domains and warrant a restricted, thin form of belief-attribution. In either case, address the objection that denying intentional status to AI is pragmatically self-defeating—since we routinely interpret each other through the intentional stance without access to neural mechanisms—and explain why this analogy either holds or breaks down for artificial systems. Conclude by stating what your verdict implies for the ethics of treating AI outputs as expressions of belief rather than as sophisticated statistical patterns.
