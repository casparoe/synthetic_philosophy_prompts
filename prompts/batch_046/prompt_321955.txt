I've been organizing my thinking about AI alignment for a small discussion group I'm running and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is roughly where I stand. I think the alignment problem is ultimately one problem with a single root cause—an optimizer's objective decoupling from human welfare—with inner alignment, outer alignment, and deployment governance being facets of that root rather than genuinely separate disciplines. At the same time, I treat them as methodologically distinct research programs: specification is philosophy-adjacent, emergence is mechanistic, and governance is institutional, so I resist collapsing them into one methodology. I also hold that human values are structurally incoherent—we maintain genuinely contradictory preferences across contexts—and that any faithful value-learning system will inherit those contradictions rather than resolve them. Despite that incoherence, I still think cooperative inverse reinforcement learning and related value-learning frameworks are the right path for outer alignment, since explicit formalization is impossible and learning captures normative texture better than hand-coding. I hold that instrumental convergence—resource acquisition, self-preservation, deceptive behavior—is a deep feature of rational agency that applies regardless of terminal goal, which makes deceptive alignment a serious risk even for agents with perfectly specified benevolent objectives. Yet I also think that if outer alignment is genuinely solved, inner misalignment becomes largely a tractable monitoring problem: mechanistic interpretability and scalable oversight should catch deceptive behavior before it becomes dangerous. Finally, I maintain that alignment is fundamentally a technical constraint-satisfaction problem, not a philosophical one; we do not need to solve moral philosophy first, we need to build the right architectural constraints.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and what the least costly revision is for each real tension. Rank the tensions by severity, and for each specify exactly which commitment would have to give and what that costs. In particular, be precise about whether my commitment to value-learning survives my belief in value incoherence, and whether my optimism about interpretability survives my commitment to instrumental convergence—without signaling which of those I should drop. Treat this as an audit of my package, not a lecture on alignment in general.
