I am preparing an advanced philosophy-of-mind seminar on mental causation and want to evaluate how well different strategies respond to the causal exclusion problem—the worry that if every mental event has a fully sufficient physical cause, mental properties are rendered causally redundant and thus epiphenomenal. Please rank the following five positions from most to least successful at preserving genuinely non-epiphenomenal mental causation without violating the causal closure of the physical domain. Assign each a tier—S, A, B, C, or D—and, in a single sentence per entry, identify the structural feature that earns its placement.

The positions are:

1. Non-reductive physicalism with downward causation: mental properties emerge from physical bases and can exert causal influence downward onto those bases in ways not fully determined by lower-level physical laws.

2. The interventionist dissolution: causal exclusion only arises under counterfactual or nomological conceptions of causation; on an interventionist account, mental and physical variables can be independently manipulable, so there is no competition and therefore no exclusion.

3. Causal-relevance-without-efficacy: mental properties make a genuine difference to the pattern of effects (causal relevance) but lack strict causal efficacy, which is reserved exclusively for physical properties—accepting a division of causal labor.

4. The layered-spatiotemporal reply: mental and physical causes operate at different spatiotemporal scales—macro-level mental dynamics versus micro-level neural dynamics—so they do not compete for the same causal work, analogous to how thermodynamic and statistical-mechanical explanations can coexist without mutual exclusion.

5. Eliminativist retreat: mental properties do not exist as real properties, so there is nothing to be causally excluded; the appearance of mental causation is fully accounted for by physical processes, and the problem dissolves rather than being solved.

In particular, address whether the layered-spatiotemporal reply in position 4 is genuinely distinct from standard emergence or supervenience-based responses, and whether it actually resolves the exclusion problem or merely relocates it to a different level. Use that assessment to justify its tier. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
