Wittgenstein's hinges aren't regular beliefs; they're part of the framewrok that makes doubt possible. They don't get justified by evidence — instead they sit at the very center of our web of belief, much like Quine describes with central beliefs. So a hinge like "the earth is old" or "there is an existant world" is just an especially stable empirical claim. Collective hinges work the same way for labs: everyone shares a commitment to calibration, and it's just the group's central belief. These can be rationally revised when anomalies pile up, just as Kuhn explains paradigm shifts — enough counterevidence eventually overturns the hinge. The riverbed metaphor means hinges stay fixed while empirical beliefs flow over them, but if the riverbed erodes from accumulated water pressure that's basically rational revision. So yes, hinges are revisable, but only through cumulative rational pressure that forces a system change, not through a single falsification.
