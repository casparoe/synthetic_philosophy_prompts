I work in distributed-systems engineering, so I naturally think in terms of Byzantine fault tolerance, quorum consensus, leader election, state-machine replication, replication factor, commit latency, and network partition tolerance. Restate the three main philosophical positions on the distribution of consciousness across animals—the gradualist or continuum view, the sharp-threshold biological-naturalist view, and Integrated Information Theory’s exclusion postulate—entirely within that vocabulary. To make the mapping concrete, assume a hypothetical mechanism I will call cross-modal phenomenal replication: the idea that conscious states are not localized but are redundantly committed across sensory and cognitive subsystems via something analogous to a two-phase commit protocol, and that an animal’s consciousness varies with its replication factor and the latency of that commit rather than with raw neuron count. Map phenomenal unity onto leader election or a committed log, map degrees of consciousness onto quorum size and fault-tolerance margins, and map IIT’s exclusion problem—why one integrated system does not subsume another—onto partition tolerance or split-brain network behavior. Then provide the residue report: which philosophical claims translate cleanly, which must be distorted to fit (for example, does subjective quality or “what-it-is-like” map onto anything in replication design, or is it pure residue?), and whether the elements that have no distributed-systems counterpart indicate a deficiency in my engineering framework or reveal that the original philosophical debate relies on metaphysical assumptions that do no real explanatory work. I do not want bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; I want the translation and its failures to be the argument itself.
