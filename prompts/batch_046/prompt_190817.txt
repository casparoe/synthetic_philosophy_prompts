I'm writing up my position on commemorative ethics for a seminar and want a consistency audit before I present. (2) Removing long-standing monuments harms present residents by disrupting the mnemonic continuity of their spatial environment — an identity-constituting harm that is independent of whether the figure honored was virtuous. (4) The harm identified in (2) should be addressed through managed transition — relocation, contextualization, or ritual acknowledgment — rather than through retention. (5) A monument can inflict dignitary harm on marginalized groups through symbolic overflow: the honored figure's legacy operates in public meaning in ways that present maintainers cannot fully control or neutralize.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, ordering the real tensions by severity.
