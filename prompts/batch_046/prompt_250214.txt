Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of hedonic unity must satisfy in response to the heterogeneity problem: what property, if any, is shared by all pleasures despite their qualitative diversity. For each requirement, state the motivating case or consideration, and mark whether you take it to be non-negotiable or negotiable for particular camps. Be sure to include: (a) an explanation of cross-modal hedonic interference—cases in which combining qualitatively distinct pleasures (e.g., gustatory and intellectual) yields non-additive or negatively integrated value rather than simple summation; (b) a principled distinction between "positive" pleasures and mere relief-pleasures that does not reduce to a binary conscious/unconscious boundary; (c) compatibility with evidence that agents systematically misreport the qualitative character of their own pleasures under conditions of divided attention or cognitive load; and (d) a non-circular criterion for excluding putative "micro-pleasures" (e.g., homeostatic satisfactions in simple regulatory systems) that does not presuppose a sharp threshold for moral status or sentience. Identify any subset of these desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and explain the tension. The response should be sustained analytical prose, approximately 600 words, with no bibliography, reading list, or citation requests.
