Write an essay addressing this controversy: Can illusionism about phenomenal consciousness remain coherent if introspection is not uniformly authoritative but instead operates with graded reliability—where some self-attributions are near-certain reconstructions of functional or representational states and others are speculative confabulations—rather than delivering a single, homogeneous stream of phenomenal data? Your task is to determine whether an illusionist can consistently claim that all reports of qualitative character are globally erroneous while allowing that a subset of those reports accurately track genuine representational or functional properties. Reach and defend a definite verdict: either articulate a version of illusionism that survives within this graded-introspection framework, or show that graded introspection undermines illusionism and pushes us toward a realist or representationalist account. In your argument, engage the standard challenges—the phenomenal-contrast objection and the charge that illusionism is self-defeating—specifically as they apply to this graded model of introspective access, and explain whether your conclusion holds up or collapses under that pressure.
