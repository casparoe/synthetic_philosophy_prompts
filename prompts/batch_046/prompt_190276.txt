I'm putting together a framework for thinking about alignment guarantees and want to stress-test its coherence before I post it more widely. My current package is roughly:

(1) The correct normative standard for deploying a high-capability system is worst-case: we need a hard bound on catastrophic misalignment probability, not merely favorable average-case behavior.

(2) Epistemically, worst-case reasoning is brittle: long speculative causal chains compound uncertainty, so worst-case scenarios often have lower posterior weight than average-case failure modes.

(3) Corrigibility should be a universal property over reachable state-space: steerability by human operators must hold in every state reachable through any sequence of internal updates or external perturbations, with no exception clauses.

(4) Universal-state corrigibility is computationally intractable for any agent with non-trivial planning depth; practical architectures can only approximate it over a high-probability subset (say, 99.999% of reachable states), leaving the residual tail formally unbounded.

(5) I reject the "pause until proof" position: deployment should proceed when average-case benefits and competitive dynamics justify it, even absent a full worst-case proof, because delay itself carries catastrophic risk from unaligned competitors.

(6) I distinguish "structural corrigibility" (deviation is architecturally impossible) from "dynamic corrigibility" (the system is incentivized to remain within bounds). I hold that only structural corrigibility satisfies (3), yet structural corrigibility is incompatible with general intelligence that requires open-ended internal updating.

Are these jointly tenable? Specifically, which pairs are genuinely incompatible versus only apparently so? Does (2) undermine (1), or do they operate at different levels (normative standard versus epistemic method)? Does (4) make (5) reckless, or does (4)'s intractability justify (5)? Is there a hidden commitment in (6) that collapses (3) into an impossibility result? Rank the tensions by severity, identify the least costly revision that restores consistency, and say what it costs me in terms of the other commitments—without assuming I'm attached to preserving any particular one.
