I run a philosophy club at a high school, about 15 students aged 16-17, one hour. I want to explain the hedonistic imperative — specificaly the biotech argument that suffering is an evolutionary design flaw we could patch, but with the twist that the goal isn't constant euphoria, it's restructuring teh hedonic range so motivation comes from scaled-up well-being gradients rather than from pain or deprivation. The kids will probably show up thinking nature is mostly okay and that this sounds like a Brave New World dystopia where everyone is too blissed out to care about anything.

Plan the explanatory arc: what conceptual dependancy has to be in place before the proposal even sounds like philosophy instead of sci-fi, the one carrying example that shows how you can have drive and contrast without a suffering baseline (it needs to have something that feels like achievement but doesn't require misery), the predictable misconceptions in the order they'll come up and the exact point in the sequence where each should be headed off, the check at the end that tells me they've understood the difference between "no suffering" and "no motivation", and what biotech specifics I should leave out for this crowd. Don't ask for readings, and I don't need citations — just the teaching plan.
