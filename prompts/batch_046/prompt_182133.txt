As a journalist covering the politics of automation and political economy, I am trying to map a long-running interpretive fault line in Marx scholarship that rarely gets aired outside specialist journals. I want you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps.

Camp A reads Marx's Grundrisse notebooks—specifically the section known as the 'Fragment on Machines'—as demonstrating that fully automated production makes labor-time technically obsolete as a measure of value. On this reading, capitalism's value-form becomes internally contradictory once machinery dominates the labor process, because surplus value can no longer be extracted from living labor in the required proportion, and the system should therefore collapse under its own technical development.

Camp B, drawing on Marx's analysis of real subsumption, argues that automation never escapes the social form of capital. Even when machines replace direct labor, capital still measures wealth by labor-time socially necessary for reproduction, and automation merely intensifies exploitation by cheapening labor-power, expanding the reserve army, and accelerating turnover. For Camp B, labor-time remains the substance of value regardless of the technical composition of capital, so automation deepens capitalist relations rather than dissolving them.

Please produce a document both parties could sign. Structure it as a numbered section of agreed claims followed by a disagreements table. In the agreement section, cover the relevant formal distinctions in Marx—between use-value production and value production, between formal and real subsumption, and between technical progress and the value-form—plus whatever both sides accept about the textual evidence and the logical structure of Marx's argument. State precisely where they diverge, phrasing each side's position in language that side would endorse. Identify the cruxes: the specific sub-questions—textual, conceptual, or empirical—such that resolving one would cause at least one camp to revise its view. For each crux, specify the kind of evidence or argument both camps agree would be relevant, and record each side's explicit prediction about what that evidence would show.

If part of the disagreement is verbal—for example, whether 'obsolete' is being used in a strict technical sense about measurement or in a polemical sense about capitalism's historical limits—identify exactly which part and explain why the wording creates the illusion of deeper conflict.

This should not be an even-handed survey that leaves every option on the table. I want a committed drafting: make definite editorial choices about what belongs in the agreement section and what belongs in the disagreement table, justify those boundary calls, and take a clear position on whether the real-subsumption framework actually resolves the contradiction Camp A claims to find. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
