Having recently read the debates surrounding the International Military Tribunal for the Far East and found the standard accusations of 'victors' justice' somewhat under-theorised, I am preparing a podcast interview with an unnamed legal philosopher who has just published a monograph defending the legitimacy of the post-war tribunals at Nuremberg and Tokyo. Her central claim is that the criticism conflates two distinct phenomena: what she terms 'procedural retroactivity,' which concerns the alteration of jurisdictional rules to bring defendants before a court, and 'substantive retroactivity,' which involves the creation of entirely new crimes ex post facto; she maintains that only the former occurred, because the norms applied—crimes against peace, crimes against humanity, and war crimes—were already implicit in the existing architecture of international law rather than invented for the occasion.

I want a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that avoid generic biographical openings and press directly on the architecture of her argument. In particular, please include one question addressing the asymmetry problem—why analogous Allied conduct, such as strategic bombing of civilian centres, was not prosecuted despite causing comparable harm—and a sharp follow-up keyed to her likely reply that jurisdictional authority was simply not exercised over the victors, asking whether that reply collapses the distinction between procedural and substantive retroactivity she wishes to defend. I also want at least one question on whether the customary-law status of the norms in question can be established without circularity, given that state practice among the very powers now sitting in judgement is being used to justify prosecuting those powers' former enemies; include a follow-up anticipating her appeal to opinio juris independent of state practice. Additionally, please include a question probing whether her distinction can survive the objection that defining 'crimes against humanity' required precisely the kind of normative innovation she denies, and a final question asking not merely what objections have been raised, but what specific institutional feature or piece of historical evidence—were it discovered—would genuinely count against her thesis. Please draft the sequence in British English, maintain a formal and polished register, and do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
