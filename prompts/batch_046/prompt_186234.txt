I was chatting with a mate after a seminar and he summed up the inefficacy objection like this: "The core point is that in big collective harms — climate, factory farming, voting, even whether deleting your old cloud backups saves server energy — your single contribution gets diluted across millions of actors, so it's physically impossible for your choice to change the outcome. That means moral reponsibility can't attach to individuals here; it's a category error. The people who say 'but if everyone did it' miss that this is just a heuristic, not an argument, and collective responsibility doesn't work either because you can't blame a crowd in the same way you blame a person. So really, the only coherent position is that these domains are outside individual morality altogether, unless you want to invoke some weird collective mind."

I feel like he's smuggling in something about "collective minds" that doesn't match what philosphers actually say, and he's treating the dilution argument as if it's about physics rather than moral reasoning. Is this summary fair?
