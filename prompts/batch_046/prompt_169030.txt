Within the study of bounded rationality and satisficing, an under-examined tension concerns the regress of aspiration-level setting: if an agent satisfices by comparing outcomes to a threshold, the choice of that threshold is itself a decision made under bounded rationality, which invites either an infinite regress or an arbitrary stopping point. Three broad responses have been articulated—treating aspiration adaptation as a meta-satisficing problem governed by procedural rationality, treating thresholds as exogenously fixed by institutional design, and dissolving the regress through evolutionary or game-theoretic dynamics that stabilize aspiration levels over time. If you were allocating several years of conceptual research effort toward decision architectures for high-stakes autonomous systems, which of these lines (or another you identify) should be prioritized, and in what order?
