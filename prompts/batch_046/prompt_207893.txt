Here's my niche angle: I'm calling it "counterfactual loyalty." The Kosovo myth projects a continuous medieval Serbian kingdom onto modern ethnic identity in ways that don't match the historical record. Masada involves loyalty to an independent Judea that was already effectively finished by the time of the siege. But Dunkirk seems different — it's loyalty to Britain as it actually was, just saved from annihilation. So my question is whether we can taxonomize these myths by whether they depend on this counterfactual loyalty, and whether that distinction predicts whether the myth becomes politically dangerous versus just nostalgic.
