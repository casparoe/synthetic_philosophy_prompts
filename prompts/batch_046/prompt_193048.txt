Please respond with exactly five interview questions, presented as a numbered list with no introduction or conclusion; each item should be one or two polished sentences, should press where the literature actually challenges the view, and should embed both a challenging premise and a brief follow-up that anticipates the standard reply. The questions should address, in this order: tetragametic chimerism and whether two fused zygotic origins yield one essential person or two; mitochondrial replacement therapy and whether the mitochondrial donor thereby becomes part of one's essential origin; synthetic embryos developed without gametes and whether a person so generated lacks an origin entirely or is metaphysically impossible; monozygotic twinning and the precise developmental moment at which a single essential origin bifurcates into two distinct ones; and the tension with psychological-continuity theories if complete neural mapping onto a synthetically generated body were ever achieved.
