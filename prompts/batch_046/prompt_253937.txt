As a novelist drafting a narrative about historians arguing over the shape of the past, I have walked myself into a line of reasoning that now strikes me as suspicious, since its conclusion dissolves the very discipline it addresses. I call its core commitment the principle of equiprimordial framing: to escape Eurocentrism, a genuinely global history must treat every civilizational trajectory as narratively equiprimordial, so that no region serves as the implicit axis around which others orbit. From this I derive the following steps. (1) Any historiographical narrative requires a vector of salience—some events function as causes, others as effects, some as turning points, others as continuations. (2) Selecting such a vector necessarily assigns some regions to structural background relative to that vector. (3) Therefore, under equiprimordial framing, privileging any vector violates the requirement that all trajectories remain equiprimordial. (4) It follows that no causal or chronological vector can be privileged without parochial distortion, which means no narrative form is permissible. (5) Anti-Eurocentric global history, however, aims precisely to construct narratives that reject European privilege. (6) So if the anti-Eurocentric project is consistent, it must reject all narrative form and leave only an undifferentiated archive. (7) But an archive is not a history; hence anti-Eurocentrism, taken consistently, abolishes historiography rather than reforming it. (8) Consequently, Eurocentrism is not a contingent bias correctable by better scholarship, but a necessary condition of historical narration itself. I wish to preserve the intuition behind equiprimordial framing—that histories should not silently center Europe—and I am reluctant to accept that global history is impossible. Which step should I reject, and why did it appear defensible? If I reject the inference from (2) to (3), what costs must a multi-vectorial or polycentric historiography bear? If I reject (4) instead, can I retain narrative form without smuggling in a hidden axis?
