Several peer-reviewed social-science journals now accept manuscripts containing LLM-drafted sections, provided the authors complete a standardized disclosure box listing the model and the prompts used; at the same time, these journals reject submissions that cite primary literature only through AI-generated summaries without direct author engagement. Reviewers are not asked to assess whether the author genuinely understands the LLM-drafted claims—only whether the citations verify and the disclosure is complete. What philosophical view of epistemic dependence and understanding does this two-tier policy presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory: does it treat drafting assistance as leaving comprehension intact, while mediated citation replaces it? Is the distinction between “procedural aid” and “epistemic proxy” coherent, or does it rest on an implausible localization of understanding to discrete verification steps? Say whether the practice strictly requires this view or is also compatible with rival accounts, and describe what peer review would have to become if we adopted the stronger thesis that any substantive use of an LLM in drafting necessarily erodes the author’s understanding of the published claims.
