Write a polished philosophical dialogue between two equally capable interlocutors, Mara and Eli, on whether privacy retains intrinsic value under conditions of universal "ambient inference" — a hypothetical regime in which distributed algorithms continuously generate and share predictive models of each person's unstated intentions, without storing raw behavioral data or creating permanent records.

Mara argues that privacy is not essentially about secrecy, data control, or protection from discrimination, but about preserving an epistemic gap between one's present actions and others' legitimate anticipatory knowledge of one's future-directed intentions. She maintains that ambient inference collapses this gap not by revealing hidden facts, but by pre-structuring every social interaction with others' predictive access, thereby destroying the conditions for what she terms "unscripted accountability" — being genuinely answerable for choices that interlocutors could not have anticipated — and for moral surprise that arises from encountering another's unmodeled intention.

Eli responds that this view romanticizes opacity. He contends that predictive transparency is merely scaled-up ordinary social cognition, that "unscripted accountability" reflects a preference for theatrical unpredictability rather than a condition of autonomy, and that privacy's value has always been instrumental: shielding individuals from asymmetries of information that enable coercion or discrimination. Because ambient inference is symmetric and non-archival, Eli argues, it eliminates the harmful asymmetry without introducing new harms, and he challenges Mara to show why being anticipated by others undermines one's authorship of one's actions.

In the dialogue, both should engage in direct back-and-forth: each must address the other's specific arguments rather than advancing parallel monologues. Mara should defend the intrinsic value of the epistemic gap without retreating to instrumental concerns about discrimination or state surveillance; Eli should concede where predictive transparency genuinely transforms social dynamics but insist that autonomy depends on causal authorship, not on being epistemically opaque to others. Neither should be a strawman. Keep the exchange formal, precise, and conceptually focused.
