hey i'm making a handout for a small phil-of-mind study group on the meta-problem of consciousness — not the hard problem itself, but why we think there's a hard problem at all. i want a taxonomy tree that sorts positions by actual commitments, not by camp labels, so someone can trace through without knowing the jargon first.

root node: do you think the "explanatory gap" feeling is tracking a real feature of consciousness, or is it basically an artifact of how we talk and think?

from there, split on where the artifact/real-feature comes from: is it (a) that we lack a specific phenomenal concept, (b) that consciousness reports are indexical rather than descriptive so they always feel mysterious from the outside, or (c) that there's a genuine ontological gap no concept could close?

then for each branch, ask whether accepting that diagnosis saves physicalism, requires abandoning it, or leaves physicalism untouched but demands a revision to our epistemic standards.

each leaf gets a name if one exists (phenomenal concept strategy, strong illusionism, chalmers-style hard-problem physicalism, the indexical-report view, etc.) or "unnamed" if the combo is empty. for every leaf, one sentence on its most awkward commitment — for empty ones, say whether the view is incoherent, just ugly, or merely unexplored.

you must include: the deflationary "there is no meta-problem, it's a failure of imagination" position; the view that consciousness reports function like indexicals; and a version of physicalism that takes the gap-feeling seriously but denies it undermines the ontology.

please flag any pairs the tree can't really seperate — i think the indexical-report branch and some higher-order-theory responses might converge on the same answers. keep it to about 5-6 decision nodes and 7-8 leaves. phrase every node as a concrete claim or case, not "are you a physicalist?" and avoid asking for citations or reading lists since half my group doesn't have library access.

oh, and write it casual — it's for a blog-style handout, so a typo or two is fine.
