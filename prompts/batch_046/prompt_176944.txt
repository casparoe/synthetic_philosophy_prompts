Please lay out the most important philosophical implications of accepting that dispositions can be masked not merely by external interfering conditions—antidotes that prevent manifestation—but by intrinsic structural features of the bearer itself, and of treating such intrinsic masking as metaphysically on a par with finking—where the bearer loses the disposition precisely at the moment it would be triggered—rather than as causal interference. Specifically, consider the consequences for (1) whether a counterfactual analysis of dispositions can survive if both finking and pervasive internal masking are possible, (2) how the distinction between a categorical base and the disposition it supports should be redrawn if the base can simultaneously constitute and suppress that very disposition, and (3) whether a disposition that remains continuously suppressed by an internal structural feature should be said to persist in a dormant form, to be absent altogether, or to exist only as a counterfactual potential without any actual categorical grounding.
