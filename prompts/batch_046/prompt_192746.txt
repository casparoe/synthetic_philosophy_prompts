hey so me and my friend were arguing for like three hours about whether Malthus was actually right but just early, like the green revolution just kicked teh can down teh road and eventually we'll hit the wall. i tried to show that if you let theories be "right but early" then you lose the ability to falsify anything, but now my argument feels like it proves climate science is unfalsifiable too, which i don't buy at all. here's the chain:

(1) If theory T predicts crisis C by date D, and C hasn't arrived by D, then either T is false or there's an auxiliary hypothesis A that explains the delay.
(2) If A isn't independently confirmed and can be stretched to cover any future date without changing T's core, then T+A is empirically empty.
(3) Malthus predicted widespread famine by the late 1800s / early 1900s; defenders invoke sustained agricultural innovation — basically the Boserupian reversal, that population pressure drives tech — to say the crisis was only delayed.
(4) That reversal wasn't part of Malthus's original arithmetic; its an exogenous sociological add-on, not derived from his geometric vs arithmetic growth rates.
(5) So rescuing Malthus lets you push the crisis arbitrarily far into the future, making the theory unfalsifiable.
(6) Now take any long-range scientific prediction L — climate tipping points, peak phosphorus, whatever — that hasn't fully hit yet but has a target window.
(7) Defenders of L can always invoke some unspecified future intervention or feedback loop A' to explain why the crisis hasn't arrived on schedule.
(8) By (2), that means L is also empirically empty.
(9) So either we accept (2) consistently and reject both Malthus and climate science, or we reject (2) and save both.
(10) But climate science clearly isn't empty, so (2) must be wrong, which means "right but early" is always legitimate and Malthus might still be correct.

where does this go wrong? i really want to keep the idea that rescuing Malthus has a real cost — its not just free — but i don't want to end up saying long-range science is all bs. also please separate whats settled from whats genuinely contested here; don't tell me "everyone agrees X" if thats not actually true among people who study falsification and long-run predictions.
