I am advising a small interdisciplinary research group—philosophers of mind working alongside cognitive psychologists—who have secured a modest three-year grant to investigate the cognitive penetration of perception. Rather than a broad survey, I want a defended prioritisation of the open conceptual questions and empirical lines of work that should command their limited time and funding. Specifically, I would like the framing to take seriously the challenge posed by hierarchical predictive-coding accounts, particularly in interoceptive as well as exteroceptive pathways: does the accumulating evidence that prior expectations modulate neural activity even in relatively early sensory regions genuinely undermine Pylyshyn-style modularity, or can these phenomena be accommodated within a “weak” modular framework that distinguishes genuine cognitive penetration from mere top-down feedback modulation? Given that the group must divide its efforts between conceptual analysis and experimental design, which three research questions or programmes should they treat as most urgent, and in what order?

Please defend the ranking with explicit criteria: tractability for philosophers versus empirical scientists over a three-year window; which questions are upstream of others, in the sense that resolving one would reshape the remaining agenda; and what kind of result—behavioural, neuroimaging, or conceptual—would cause you to reorder the priorities within the next couple of years. I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations to specific works; the deliverable should be strategic judgement about where effort is best placed, not source retrieval. In your response, please separate what is relatively settled within the philosophy of perception and cognitive science from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and avoid presenting any disputed interpretation—such as whether predictive coding necessarily entails penetration—as though it were the consensus view.
