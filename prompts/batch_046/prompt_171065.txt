I have been thinking about the Pasadena paradox and the problem of undefined expected values. It seems to me that there are two different ways to respond: one is to say that the expectation genuinely diverges, so expected-utility reasoning simply does not apply; the other is to restrict the averaging to outcomes consistent with the actually observed stopping rule, which I have been calling a "localized" expectation rather than a global one that integrates over every counterfactual stopping point. Could you compare these two approaches? In particular, does localizing the expectation actually resolve the paradox, or does it just move the undefinedness into the conditioning step? And is this distinction between global and localized expectation specific to the Pasadena case, or does it illuminate other decision-theoretic problems involving undefined values?
