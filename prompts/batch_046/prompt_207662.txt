Here's an argument I sketched for a seminar on capital punishment and now I'm second-guessing it because the conclusion feels like it wipes out too much. (1) If retributive justice is valid, then punishing someone requires they actually deserve it; you can't justify harming an innocent person as a side-effect of harming guilty ones. (2) Any real-world death-penalty system has a nonzero rate of false convictions—say, from mistaken eyewitness ID or coerced confesions—so applying it always carries a statistical risk of executing an innocent. (3) Therefore, if we keep the retributive justification, we must either abolish the penalty or pretend the risk is zero, which is dishonest. So far so good. But then I pushed further: (4) If we drop retribution and defend the penalty purely on deterrence, we treat executions as a policy tool that saves net lives. (5) Once we allow that, consistency demands we compare the death penalty not just to life imprisonment, but to any other policy with a similar statistical profile—say, a preventive-detention program that locks up 10,000 high-risk people to stop 100 murders, knowing 500 are innocent. (6) If the deterrence-only defender accepts capital punishment with a 1-in-200 error rate, they should also accept that preventive program if its ratio is better. (7) But accepting (6) means we've justified mass incarceration of the innocent on statistical grounds, which seems like a reductio. (8) The only escape is to say execution is special because it's irreversible, but (9) that actually makes the statistical risk worse, not a reason to tolerate it. So (10) either we embrace the reductio and justify sweeping preventive detention, or we reject the deterrence-only defense and go back to retribution—which we already saw collapses under error risk. (11) Hence the death penalty can't be justified on either ground once error risk is admitted. Where does this chain break? I really want to keep (1) and also avoid (6), but I'm not sure that's coherent. Is there an equivocation between individual-level justification and policy-level justification hiding in step (4) or (5), or am I just stuck?
