Hey, quick question about something I've noticed while arguing about big background beliefs - like whether the past really exists or whether other minds are real. I know Wittgenstien talked about hinge propositions in On Certainty, the ones that sit in the background and hold everything else up. But there's this move I keep seeing that I don't have a word for.

Say you're debating climate science with someone. They start out treating "measuring instruments are basicaly trustworthy" as a claim they can back up with studies. Then you point out that those studies themselves rely on instruments. Instead of defending the claim, they shift the ground: suddenly anyone who asks for proof of instrument reliability is being irrational or missing the point, as if reliability was never an empirical claim but always just part of the rules for talking about climate. The hinge - that instruments work - hasn't been defended; it's been moved into the frame of the conversation so denying it becomes a kind of category error.

Another example: in philosophy seminars someone treats "language refers to the world" as a thesis they argue for, but when pushed they say it's not a thesis at all, it's just the condition for having the discussion. But they only say that after being challenged. Is there a name for this specific pattern - where a hinge proposition gets protected not by being asserted, but by being quietly rewritten into the ground rules of the debate once it's under pressure? Or is it just framing? I feel like it's different from ordinary framing because the hinge was explicitly treated as a claim right up until the moment it needed protection. What should I call this?
