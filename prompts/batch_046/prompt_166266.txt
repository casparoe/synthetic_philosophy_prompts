Compare Zhuangzi's butterfly dream with Descartes's first-meditation dream argument—not the usual "both doubt reality" gloss, but specifically on what the "waking up" moment is supposed to do. For Descartes, waking restores coherence and lets you dismiss the dream as epistemically defective. Zhuangzi wakes and finds himself in exactly the same ambiguty: maybe he's the butterfly now dreaming he's Zhou. So compare them on whether skepticism is meant to be resolved or just inhabited permanantly. And here's the twist I want you to work through: does Zhuangzi sidestep the standard "similarity objection" against Cartesian skepticism—that waking life is more stable and coherent—precisely by denying there's a stable self that could own either state? Or does he just make the problem worse?
