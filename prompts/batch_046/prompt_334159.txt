The student wrote the following: 'Friedman’s F-twist says that unrealistic assumptions are not just acceptable but actually preferable, because simplifying falsehoods make models more tractable. Friedman explicitly requires that if the micro-level predictions fail—like people not maximising—then the macro-level defence automatically collapses, since a false foundation cannot support a true superstructure. This means behavioural economics undermines the rational-agent model not just descriptively but methodologically, because Friedman’s own criterion demands consistency across levels of analysis. The only remaining issue is normative: whether economists should care about describing real cognition, but Friedman’s instrumentalism rules that out as irrelevant to positive science.'

Please grade this answer out of 100, giving brief justification for the mark, and provide point-by-point comments that identify any subtle conflations, misattributions, or steps that do not follow, without signalling your own view on Friedman’s methodology. Present your assessment as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, using British English.
