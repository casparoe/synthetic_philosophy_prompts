Two people in my study group got stuck arguing about updateless decision theory. Sam said the core idea is you dont update on observations, so if you pick a policy to cooperate with a copy of yourself in prisoner's dilemma, youre stuck even if you literally watch the other guy defect — which makes it useless for agents that can learn. Riley said Sam's mixing up execution and selection: UDT picks one mapping from inputs to outputs beforehand, and seeing defection just runs the pre-planned branch; it doesnt "stick" with anything. Sam replied that in a noisy transparent-box version where the predictor is only 80% right, UDT still wont switch after seeing an empty box, which seems like a bug not a feature. Riley said human brains update causally, not logically, so its expected they fail. They asked me to settle it. Can you go through there exchange move by move and say whos right?
