I was working through some old notes on Bradley's regress and ended up building this argument that feels like it proves way too much, maybe that nobody actually thinks complex thoughts at all. Here are the steps I laid out:

(1) If a proposition has more than one constituent, those constituants aren't just sitting next to each other; they have to be genuinely unified into one thought. (2) That unification can't be done by one of the constituents themselves, since then that one would have to relate itself to the others, which is circular. (3) And it can't be done by something entirely outside the proposition, because then the proposition wouldn't actually contain both constituents together. (4) So there's has to be a further constituent — call it a binder — inside the proposition that relates the others. (5) But now the binder, together with the original constituents, forms a new complex that also needs unification. (6) So every genuinely complex proposition requires an infinite chain of binders. (7) An infinite chain can't be completed, so no complex proposition is ever fully unified. (8) Therefore either there's no complex propositions, or what we call "thinking John loves Mary" is really just an atomic mental state with no internal structure we can access.

But then I pushed it further: (9) Apply the same logic to the relation between me and that mental state. I'm related to it by "believing" or "entertaining." (10) By (1)-(4) that relation also needs a binder inside the belief-state, then another binder for that one, etc. (11) So either I'm not genuinely related to any of my thoughts, or I don't exist as a subject that stands in relations at all. (12) Both options are absurd, so one of the premises must go.

I feel like the move from (8) to (9) is where something slips — maybe a scope shift, maybe treating mental relations like propositional ones when they aren't — but I can't name it exactly. If I have to choose, I'd rather keep (1), that real complexity needs real unity, and reject whatever lets the regress spread from propositions to mental states. Which step should I actually give up, and what's the cost? Is there a standard way out — Frege's idea of unsaturatedness, Russell's type-theoretic block, some modern mental-file view — that stops the spread without just denying that propositions are structured?
