The Gould-Lewontin spandrel analogy is a staple in evolutionary reasoning: the triangular spaces between the arches of San Marco are structural byproducts of the dome-and-arch system, not designed to hold mosaics, yet they are co-opted for decoration. The analogy is usually deployed to license an anti-adaptationist move — observing that a trait is functional does not show that natural selection shaped it for that function, since it may be a spandrel arising from other architectural constraints. I want to test how far this mapping holds when it is transferred to the emergence of in-context reasoning in large transformer models. In this transposition, the fixed inductive biases of attention and residual streams are the "arches"; the reasoning patterns that appear at scale but were never explicitly targeted by the loss are the "mosaics"; and gradient descent with a broad pretraining objective is the "builder" that produces both. Proponents of a scaling-driven adaptationist view treat these capabilities as selected adaptations; skeptics treat them as algorithmic spandrels — unavoidable structural byproducts that happen to be useful and are therefore retained.

Can you set out the correspondence table precisely — what maps onto the physical constraint, the byproduct space, the co-opted decoration, the absence of intentional design, and the evaluation of current utility? Then identify the disanalogies that bear on the intended conclusion: for example, that evolutionary spandrels arise from physical law while neural architectures are engineered; that biological selection is blind while gradient descent is directed by an explicit loss; or that a spandrel in biology is typically neutral at origin while a model's emergent capability is immediately rewarded by the training dynamics. Separate the disanalogies that genuinely break the analogy from those that are inessential ornament. Finally, does the core anti-adaptationist conclusion — that functional appearance is not sufficient evidence of adaptive origin — survive once these structural differences are on the table, or does the presence of an optimization target and an engineered substrate collapse the distinction between spandrel and adaptation in the algorithmic case?
