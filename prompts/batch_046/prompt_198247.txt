Write a dialogue between two philosophers of action, Mara and Lin, about whether the Knobe effect reveals that moral evaluation structurally infects our concept of intentional action, or whether the asymmetry is best explained by conversational pragmatics and differing explanatory demands for harm versus help. Mara should defend the view that we apply a normative filter before attributing intentionality, so that harmful side effects are labeled intentional while beneficial ones are not, even when causal structure is identical. Lin should defend the view that speakers face different pragmatic pressures—harm demands justification and thus invites richer causal description—without any deep shift in the underlying concept of intentional action.

To keep the exchange from becoming a rehearsed replay of the standard literature, center part of the dialogue on a modified Knobe case that injects probabilistic uncertainty: a CEO approves a policy that she knows has a 70% chance of harming the environment and a 30% chance of leaving it unchanged, versus an otherwise identical policy with a 70% chance of helping and a 30% chance of leaving it unchanged. Have each philosopher argue whether the intentionality asymmetry persists, weakens, or reverses in this framing, and have them use that result—whether assumed or argued—to support or challenge their broader account.

Structure the response in four named sections in this order:
1. The standard harm/help asymmetry (approx. 180 words)
2. The probabilistic twist and each philosopher's expectation (approx. 220 words)
3. Direct exchange: moral filter versus pragmatic framing (approx. 280 words)
4. Where they stand at the end (approx. 150 words)

Both characters must be equally sharp, and each must respond to the other's actual arguments rather than talking past them. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or verbatim quotations from specific texts.
