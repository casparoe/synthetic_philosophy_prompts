Please argue for a restricted, reconstructive interpretation of Nāgārjuna's tetralemma, specifically that the four-cornered negation applies with full force only to propositions asserting inherent existence, svabhāva, in entities, but operates differently—perhaps as a dialectical suspension rather than an outright negation—when applied to relational or conventional designations, prajñapti. On this reading, Madhyamaka emptiness, śūnyatā, is not a global ontological denial but a targeted dissolution of metaphysical reification, which allows conventional causal and ethical discourse to remain fully functional without being reduced to mere fiction. Defend this interpretation against two objections: first, that it arbitrarily limits Nāgārjuna's universal claim that all dharmas are empty; and second, that by preserving conventional discourse intact, it collapses into a form of conventionalism indistinguishable from the realist Buddhist schools he rejects. Your argument should explain why treating emptiness as a modality-specific critique rather than a blanket ontology avoids nihilism without smuggling essentialism back in through the conventional-ultimate distinction.
