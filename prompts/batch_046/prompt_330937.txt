ok so in my reading group we've been stuck for months on this AI safety debate thing. half of us think the debate idea—where two AI systems argue in front of a human judge—actually solves alignment because any hidden agenda has to be exposed in the back-and-fourth. the other half says thats naive because of what they call obfuscated arguments: an AI can bury its real reasoning inside long chains of technically correct but mostly irrelevant steps, kind of like hiding a secret message in static. the judge only sees the surface, and the opponent AI might even be tricked too if the hiding is spread across enough turns.

everyone agreeds that transparency is good and deception is bad, and they all accept the same formal rules for the protocol. but we haven't moved an inch. my working theory is that this isnt about facts at all—its about whether explanation has to be local and checkable, or whether it can be globally distributed in a way that still counts as reasoning. is that right, or is there a real empirical disagreement hiding underneath about whether current architectures can actually do this kind of steganographic reasoning? and if its mostly conceptual, is there any theorem or experiment that could break the deadlock, or should we just admit its bedrock intuition about whether intelligence always leaves footprints? I want an honest diagnosis, not just both sides have points.
