For a newsletter parody, I want to push this observation into a sustained deadpan satire that treats his bodily prescriptions with the bureaucratic seriousness they arguably deserve.

Write a mock corporate Bio-Optimization and Values-Revaluation Wellness Protocol memo, approximately 800 words, formatted with numbered sections, bullet points, and an appendix titled Climate and Digestive Contingencies. The voice should be that of an HR wellness consultant sincerely applying Nietzsche's specific physiological determinism—his rejection of alcohol, heavy starches, and sedentary reading; his belief that walking velocity regulates the velocity of thought; his distinction between mere absence of illness and great health as a surplus of vitality—to a contemporary remote-workforce. The satire should exaggerate a real feature of his writings: that he derives the possibility of metaphysical achievements, including the revaluation of values and the emergence of a higher human type, directly from meteorological and gastrointestinal conditions. To prevent the parody from being generic wellness satire, inject a niche conceptual twist: frame will to power not as an abstract metaphysical doctrine but as a quantifiable enteric-nervous-system metric (for instance, intestinal will-to-power scores correlated with fiber intake and barometric pressure), and treat climate selection as a form of contextualist epistemology—where the truth-conditions of a philosophical claim vary with altitude, humidity, and the thinker's current gastric pH. The piece must remain readable to someone unfamiliar with Nietzsche, but should reward specialists who recognize that every absurd corporate metric maps onto an actual claim from his writings on health and philosophy. Do not include any verbatim quotations; paraphrase specific claims in plain language without quotation marks. Conclude with a Refund and Continuity Policy clause that quietly asks whether an employee who achieves optimal digestive metrics and correct climate alignment but fails to produce a revaluation of values is entitled to compensation, or whether the failure itself proves the metrics were never truly optimized.
