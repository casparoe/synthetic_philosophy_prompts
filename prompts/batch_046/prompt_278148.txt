I'm working through a problem set for my metaphysics seminar on material constitution, and a study partner proposed the following argument, which seems to prove that no object with form-dependent persistence conditions is ever materially constituted by a base that lacks them. (1) At any time t, if statue S is materially constituted by lump of clay L, then every non-physical property of S at t is either identical to, or grounded in, some property that L has at t. (2) S has the modal property being essentially such that, if it exists, it has the artistic form of David; L lacks this property, since L could survive being reshaped into a bowl. (3) Grounding is transitive and modally robust: if property P of S is grounded in property Q of L, then necessarily, whenever L has Q, S exists and has P. (4) So if L's being shaped like David grounded S's artistic-form property, then necessarily any lump shaped like David would be S; but a different lump could be accidentally shaped like David without S ever existing, so the necessary link fails. (5) Hence S's artistic-form property is not grounded in any property of L. (6) By (1), S is therefore not constituted by L at t. (7) By parity of reasoning, no artifact or biological organism is ever constituted by its material parts. (8) So material constitution does not occur.

The conclusion strikes me as plainly false—we think statues are made of clay—but each premise looks defensible, especially (1), which expresses a plausible explanatory constraint: constituted objects shouldn't have properties that float entirely free of their material bases. I would most like to keep (1) if I can. Tell me exactly which step to reject, explain why that step seemed so plausible on its own, and then price the alternatives: what does denying it commit a hylomorphist to, versus a four-dimensionalist, versus an eliminativist about artifacts? If the flaw is instead in the modal-robustness claim in (3), why did that principle seem attractive, and what does abandoning it do to the broader metaphysics of grounding?
