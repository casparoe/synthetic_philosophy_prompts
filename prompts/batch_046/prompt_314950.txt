I sit on my university's general-education review board, and we must vote Thursday on whether to approve a required reasoning module that uses the White Horse Dialogue — the argument tied to the School of Names in which it is claimed that a white horse is not a horse — as its central case study. The proposal teaches it as a straightforward logical contradiction: the speaker denies that the particular animal is identical with the general kind. I have been reading about this on my own, and I think that reading may be too quick; it strikes me that calling something white belongs to one kind of talk, about how a thing looks, while calling it a horse belongs to another, about what kind of thing it is. So the dialogue might not be collapsing categories at all, but pointing out that we confuse appearance-language with kind-language. A fellow board member insists that our job is not to rewrite the instructor's philosophy but to check that any interpretation taught is clearly marked as interpretation and defensible for students. Please draft the short written opinion I should circulate before the meeting. Recommend a concrete decision — approve, approve with conditions, or reject — with a clear implementation time frame if approved. Treat both the contradiction reading and my appearance-versus-kind concern seriously, state the strongest argument for the side you do not take, and say plainly what principle the board would be adopting either way. Please write the answer in American English.
