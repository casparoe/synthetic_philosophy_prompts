In Quine scholarship, the dispute over whether his naturalism leaves room for objective moral values has been largely static: one camp reads his continuity thesis as reducing ethics to habits of social coordination and predictive adjustment, while the other reads his holism as allowing moral claims to be genuinely truth-apt, revised like physical hypotheses but no less objective. Both sides agree on the textual evidence and neither has moved. My working theory is that the stall comes from a hidden divergence about what "objective" demands—whether it requires a distinctive causal or explanatory role for values, or merely intersubjective stability within the overall web of belief. Is that right? Diagnose the disagreement concretely: is it verbal, substantive, about standards of evidence, about which cases count as data, or are the sides answering different questions—one about Quine's personal ontology, one about what naturalism can accommodate—under one heading? Support your diagnosis with what each side actually says, and tell me honestly whether any conceptual distinction, formal model, or reframing could break the deadlock, or whether the impasse is a sign that the original question is ill-posed.
