I am writing a novel about consciousness and am trying to think through Hofstadter’s idea of the self as a strange loop—a symbol, specifically the pronoun “I,” that refers back to the system generating it. Here is an argument I have constructed that seems to prove too much, and I need to know where it collapses.

(1) If the self is nothing but a self-referential symbol embedded in a symbol-processing system, then its existence depends entirely on the coherence of that loop.
(2) In contemporary life, the system maintaining my loop is not confined to my brain; it extends into journals, digital notes, and devices I consult to reconstruct my memories and intentions.
(3) So the strange loop is distributed across biological and technological substrates.
(4) If the biological substrate is destroyed but the technological archives remain intact and can still be processed to regenerate the loop, then the symbol “I” continues to refer.
(5) By the same reasoning, perfect copies of those archives could generate multiple identical loops, each equally valid.
(6) Therefore, personal identity is not unique, not bounded by the body, and survival becomes a matter of data preservation rather than biological continuity.
(7) This dissolves the very distinctions—death, privacy, moral accountability—that depend on a single, continuous agent.

The conclusion feels absurd, yet each step seems to follow from Hofstadter’s framework once we accept distributed cognition. Which premise should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Specifically, does Hofstadter’s account require that the loop be localized, or can it genuinely be distributed without losing the uniqueness of the self? And if the loop is strictly localized in neural patterns, how do we account for the obvious fact that our self-narrative depends on external memory?

Please separate what is settled in the philosophy of mind about self-reference from what remains genuinely contested—particularly regarding distributed cognition and personal identity—and do not present a contested interpretation of Hofstadter as if it were the consensus view. Write your answer in American English.
