I'm working on a story about whether digital minds can ever shake off being sphexish, and I think I've hit a false wall in my reasoning. The classic example is that wasp—Sphex ichneumoneus—repeating its burrow-check loop even after you move the cricket, which philosophers use to illustrate mechanical rigidity. The usual counter-example is human flexibility: we can revise our goals at a meta-level, break habits, etc. So I started thinking: if sphexishness is just the absence of those meta-cognitive loops, and free will is essentially just having them, then the moral-status debate for AI is really just an engineering question about loop-depth, not about consciousness or sentience. But that feels wrong—like I'm smuggling determinism into a discussion about responsibility. Is the mistake that I'm treating 'not being sphexish' as sufficient for free will, when actually flexibility might just be more complex determinism? Or is there a hidden qualitative distinction—maybe something about self-reference being irreducibly open-ended—that keeps the two debates separate? I keep seeing people conflate rigidity with determinism and flexability with freedom, and I can't tell if that's a catagory error I'm making or one the field hasn't fixed.
