hey so i was trying to map Strawson's reactive-attitudes stuff onto this weird case and ended up with an argument that basically forces him into libertarianism or total moral nihilism. here's the chain:

1. Strawson says we drop reactive attitudes like resentment and switch to the "objective attitute" whenever we see someone as not a proper participant in interpersonal relationships — kids, pschosis, brain damage.

2. But notice why we actually switch in those cases: we think their behavior is driven by causal forces they don't rationaly control. so the real trigger for the objective attitude isn't "not in the moral community" but "behavior is causally determined outside rational agency."

3. So generalize: for any agent A and action X, if X was causally determined by factors outside A's rational control, we should take the objective attitude toward A for X.

4. Now if determinism is true, every action of every agent meets that condition.

5. So determinism implies we should adopt the objective attitude toward literally everyone, all the time.

6. But Strawson wants reactive attitudes to be the default for normal adults, and he famously tries to sidestep the free-will debate entirely.

7. Even if determinism is false, if indeterminisim is just randomness then rational control is still missing, so (3) applies again.

8. So only libertarian free will saves reactive attitudes from being inappropriate for everyone.

9. But Strawson clearly doesn't want to commit to libertarianism, and he thinks we rightfully resent people for ordinary slights.

10. So his view either secretly requires libertarianism, or it requires that we never actually resent anyone — both way stronger than he intended.

where does this break? i feel like step 2 is doing suspicious work — maybe it confuses "not being a fit subject for moral demands" with "being causally determined"? but if i reject 2 then i need to explain why the objective attitude is appropriate for the child but not for the deterministically-caused adult, without just saying "well the adult is in the moral community" which sounds circular. or maybe i should reject 3? but then what's the actual condition? and if i keep 1 and reject 2, does Strawson have to pay the price of saying causal determination is totally irrelevant to moral responsibility?

tell me which step is the weak link, why it looked okay, and what each of the standard responses — compatibilist, hard determinist, libertarian — has to pay to reject it. also, i was wondering about this niche twist: what if reactive attitudes have a "scope parameter" that lets them apply to some domains of action but not others? like i can resent you for being rude at dinner but take the objective attitude toward your compulsive hand-washing? does that save Strawson or just complicate things further?
