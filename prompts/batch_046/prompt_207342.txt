I'm a senior philosophy major serving on the College Curriculum Committee, and I'm writing this memo ahead of our vote next week; I'm also currently in a seminar on political philosophy where we've been examining whether "Western values" operates as a descriptive category, a normative cluster, or a rhetorical device.

We are reviewing a proposal for a new general-education course titled "Western Values in Global Context." The syllabus organizes the material through three distinct frames: (1) an inventory of substantive goods—procedural liberalism, secular public reason, and autonomy conceived as self-authorship; (2) a "problem-space" coherence thesis, according to which heterogeneous traditions (Christian natural law, Enlightenment rationalism, Romantic expressivism) share not doctrinal content but a historical grammar for negotiating tradition and critique; and (3) a genealogical narrative that treats these values as contingent institutional outcomes with no universal normative privilege.

Two faculty reviewers object that frames (1) and (2) implicitly essentialize a plural tradition into a false unity and perform an imperial taxonomy, regardless of the instructor's intentions. They urge the committee to reject the proposal unless it is rebuilt entirely around frame (3) with a mandatory critical-framing module. The course designer responds that excluding (1) and (2) would misrepresent the normative debates students actually encounter in law and politics, and that pedagogical neutrality is impossible—every syllabus performs some taxonomy, so the only honest choice is explicit framing rather than concealed framing.

The committee must decide among three options: approve the course as proposed; approve it only if revised to center frame (3) with the required critical module; or reject it outright.

Please draft a formal recommendation memo addressed to the committee. Lead with a clear, actionable bottom-line recommendation in the first sentence. Then provide reasoning that analyzes whether coherence can be sustained through an inventory account, a problem-space account, or a grammatical account without collapsing into essentialism. Finally, include an honest treatment of the strongest argument for the option you reject—specifically, the claim that any pedagogical framing of "Western values" necessarily privileges a civilizational self-image even when it aims at descriptive neutrality. Keep the memo to no more than 600 words, and format it as a document the committee could vote on.
