I serve as a senior mediator at a municipal arbitration center, and over the past year our caseload has shifted: an increasing share of disputes involve no tangible resource conflict, but rather parties who claim that some word or exclusion violated “who they really are,” whether that identity is professional, ethnic, tied to a trauma history, or inherited through family tradition. I need a reusable, secular assessment protocol—grounded in the Buddhist analysis of anatta and the five skandhas, but translated entirely into operational language—that I can run in the first fifteen minutes of intake, without asking clients to adopt any metaphysical framework. Specifically, I want a step-by-step procedure that distinguishes what I will call “indexical continuity” (the conventional, functional persistence of bodily sensation, affect, perception, mental formation, and consciousness working together as a practical narrative) from what I will call “the binding error” (the reification of that coordination into proof of a single, permanent, essential owner). The protocol should list the questions or observational checks in exact order, specify what response pattern counts as a sufficient answer at each gate, identify the precise point where the output is “proceed with narrative restructuring,” the point where it stops with “this is a values dispute outside procedural resolution,” and the point where I must hand the decision to my co-mediator because the judgment is irreducibly ethical rather than analytical. It must survive three worked cases: a veteran who treats his service identity as an unchangeable essence justifying retaliation over a perceived disrespect; a second-generation immigrant who experiences her parents’ traditions as an external imposition upon her “real self”; and a hard case where the procedure correctly detects the binding error but identifying it poses an existential threat to the party—meaning the protocol delivers the unwelcome recommendation to withhold intervention precisely when suffering is most acute. Flag explicitly what the procedure cannot do: it must not resolve whether identity claims deserve legal protection, it must not require clients to accept Buddhist metaphysics, and it should drop any scholastic distinctions that do not change my action as mediator. Write it as a polished document I could insert directly into our practice manual, with no bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts.
