In our philosophy of logic reading group, two colleagues got stuck in a disagreement about the MU-puzzle and what it actually demonstrates. Maya argued that the puzzle is a paradigmatic case of jumping out of the system—JOOTSing: human reasoners can recognize that "MU" is unprovable within the MIU formal system precisely because they are not bound by its rules, and this is why the concept is used to model the self-referential leap that distinguishes conscious cognition from mechanical computation. Leo countered that this mystifies standard meta-mathematics; Gödel's arithmetization already formalizes the "outside" perspective, so recognizing unprovability is just applying another formal theorem, not performing some ontologically special escape. Maya replied that Gödel's proof itself presupposes the very capacity for self-reference that JOOTSing describes—formalizing the escape does not eliminate the need for the cognitive act of seeing the system from without. Leo then said she is conflating epistemology with ontology: understanding a meta-theorem is a learned formal technique, and if an advanced system applied Gödel's proof, we would not say it had "jumped out" in any metaphysically interesting sense; any further interpretation is projection onto cognitive architecture. They asked me to referee and I honestly could not. Please adjudicate point by point: who is right about the logical structure of the MU-puzzle versus the interpretation of JOOTSing, whether formalizing meta-reasoning dissolves the phenomenon or merely relocates it, and whether both are missing a distinction—perhaps between procedural capacity and reflective endorsement—that would resolve the tension.
