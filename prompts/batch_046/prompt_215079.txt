design a classroom game for a well-being seminar (around 13 students, 80 minutes) built around the experience-machine idea, but twisted so it isn't just the same old "would you plug in?" discussion. invent a new framing: call it the "Leaky Protocol." in this version, every student gets a hidden card assigning them one of four life-priorities (comfort, achievement, connection, or understanding). then they learn that the class is collectively running a "simulation infrastructure" where each person can set their own "virtuality level" from 0% to 100%, but here's the catch - the class budget for real-world activities (like ordering food, picking a field trip, or controlling the playlist) depends on the average virtuality score being low. so there's a coordination problem: you want your own high virtuality if you're tired, but you also want the group average low so you get the good real perks.

structure it in three rounds with real small stakes (like points that determine who picks the music). round 1: everyone secretly writes their % and one sentence about what they actually want. round 2: an "Auditor" (a rotating role) reveals three anonymous numbers and the group votes to shift the class dial up or down by 15%. round 3: same but the Auditor may lie once, and people can form coalitions.

the rules should make people feel the tension between hedonism (if I'm at 100% virtual I still feel good) and objective-list theories (maybe connection requires real presence) without the facilitator explaining it first. include specific roles: the Auditor, the Coalition Leader who can promise rewards, and the Silent Recorder who writes down justifications.

the debrief needs to map round 1 onto desire-fulfillment vs hedonism, round 2 onto whether knowledge of being simulated corrupts value (the "epistemic condition"), and round 3 onto authenticity even under uncertainty. be honest about what the activity can't reproduce: probably the solitary, first-person horror of fully plugging in, since here it's social and strategic.

also predict one failure mode - like everyone gaming it by picking 0% to free-ride - and say how you'd handle it (maybe by revealing that high virtuality actually gives hidden individual bonuses). write this casually, accomodate the typo, and don't ask for bibliographies or specific citations.
