I serve on the institutional review board of a neuroscience startup evaluating a pilot study proposal. The researchers have built a bidirectional neural interface—essentially a high-bandwidth brain-to-brain link—that allows two consenting adults to share not just language but continuous streams of emotional valence and low-level sensory integration. In the proposed thirty-day trial, the coupling is calibrated so tightly that each participant reports moments in which they cannot cleanly distinguish whether a given feeling or percept originated in their own cortex or in their partner's. The investigators frame this not as reading someone else's mind but as demonstrating that a first-person perspective can be genuinely co-instantiated across two biological brains, citing Douglas Hofstadter's view that the self is a kind of strange loop—an abstract pattern rather than a substance tied to a single body. They want approval to proceed.

Half the board treats this as an extreme but conventional informed-consent and privacy problem: two people sharing data, with risks of psychological dependency or blurred boundaries, but nothing that violates existing ethical categories. The other half worries that if Hofstadter-style distributed identity is even partially correct, we may be authorizing an experiment that creates a single distributed subject without clear legal or moral standing, which raises questions our consent forms are not designed to handle.

Please draft the board's decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—for circulation before our meeting next Thursday. The memo should recommend a concrete course of action with an explicit timeline and specific safeguards. It must take seriously the philosophical claim that an 'I' might be distributed across brains, explain why that claim does or does not change our ethical obligations, and honestly address the strongest version of the opposing view—that this is merely enhanced empathy with shared data and poses no novel identity risk. I am writing this as a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, so please do not rely on unexplained technical terminology from philosophy of mind; however, do not oversimplify the conceptual stakes to the point of dismissing them. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading.
