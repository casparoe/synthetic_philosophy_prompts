Write a joke that hinges on the internal tension within Herbert Simon's account of bounded rationality: specifically, that satisficing requires setting an aspiration level, but determining the right aspiration level seems to demand the very optimization that bounded rationality rules out. The punchline should arise from applying this regress to an ordinary decision—say, picking a restaurant or buying a phone—so that the character gets paralyzed not by comparing options but by trying to decide how little comparison is enough. It should be genuinely funny to someone who grasps the technical distinction between satisficing and optimizing, rather than a generic joke about overthinking.
