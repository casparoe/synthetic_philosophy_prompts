In my philosophy of physics reading group people keep comparing Einsteins hole argument to sliding a jigsaw piece inside the empty gap of the board: the picture doesn't change, but if you think each piece has an absolute adress then sliding it makes a 'new' board that looks identical. The analogy gets used to say spacetime points are like addresses -- no intrinsic identity -- so the hole isnt a real determinism problem. Can you map out exactly what corresponds to what: the pieces, the board, the gap, the sliding rule? Then show where it breaks, especially whether general covariance is actually like the puzzle rules, and whether the analogy sneaks in relationalism about points without earning it. My advisor also keeps bringing up active diffeomorphisms on fiber bundles rather than just base manifolds, saying that changes how the sliding works. Does that break the puzzle analogy entirely, or can it be patched? If not, whats a better analogy? And tell me whether the conclusion -- no determinism failure -- survives once all that is on the table, or whether the puzzle picture was carrying it the whole time.
