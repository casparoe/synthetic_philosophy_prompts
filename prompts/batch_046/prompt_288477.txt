I serve on the ethics review board of a foundation that evaluates proposals for “unbounded generative systems”—technologies or institutional designs that, if deployed, could produce an indefinite and potentially infinite number of future welfare-bearing entities across open-ended time horizons. We need a reusable decision procedure, not a case-by-case intuition, that applies the core commitments of infinite-population ethics to these reviews. The procedure should be executable by a board member with no mathematics background in about forty minutes per proposal, with explicit ordered tests, clear stop points, and honest flags for where judgment replaces calculation.

Ground it in what formal ethics actually delivers about comparing infinite welfare distributions: the failure of simple aggregation, the survival of dominance relations across cardinalities, the distinction between “almost all” and “all but finitely many,” and the treatment of measure-zero harms or benefits inside an infinite set. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.

Deliver the following in American English:

1. A step-by-step checklist (no more than eight steps) that begins with classifying the proposal’s growth trajectory and ends with a sign-off category: approve, approve with conditions, or reject with documented discomfort.

2. Three worked cases: (a) a self-replicating probe program with exponentially growing populations but declining per-unit welfare; (b) a digital ecosystem that creates a constant-rate stream of entities with identical welfare profiles forever; (c) a proposal generating an infinite hierarchy of nested populations, where lower levels dominate in cardinality but upper levels have vastly higher welfare. For each, show exactly where the procedure lands, including one case where the result will likely violate pre-theoretical intuition—state the procedural answer and specify what discomfort the board must record.

3. Explicit “judgment call” boxes—steps where the mathematics underdetermines the answer and a human must choose, with a one-sentence note on what value or assumption is being selected.

4. A frank list of what the procedure cannot settle (for example, inter-personal comparisons across radically different kinds of entities, or the moral status of merely potential beings) and a required disclaimer language for those gaps.

Keep the language formal and practical, avoid invented quotation marks, and assume I have no web access.
