Write a deadpan bureaucratic “Frequently Asked Questions” document—approximately 650 words, formatted as numbered administrative clauses—issued by the fictional Ministry of Relational Governance in a nation-state that has replaced its legal code with care ethics as its sole constitutional framework. The document should address practical administrative matters (tax assessment, criminal sentencing, inheritance disputes, electoral eligibility, and property boundaries) entirely through care-ethical reasoning: contextual particularity, attention to dependency and vulnerability, rejection of abstract universal rules, and the priority of maintaining caring relationships over assigning rights or distributing goods by principle. The satire should exaggerate genuine commitments of care ethics—such as the critique of liberal individualism, the distinction between “caring for” and “caring about,” and the difficulty of scaling intimate moral attention to institutional levels—to expose the tension between bureaucratic universality and relational particularity without resolving it. Keep the reasoning internally consistent: do not smuggle in hidden appeals to rights, procedural fairness, impartial justice, or contractual obligation. The voice should be deadpan administrative prose; the humor should come from the content rather than from winks or asides, so that a reader familiar with care-ethical theory recognizes the exaggeration as fair and a general reader can still follow the logic. Include a section describing the national holiday “Day of Attentive Listening” that quietly reveals who gets to define “need” when care is administered at scale. Conclude with a “Contact for Further Relational Adjustment” clause that admits, without breaking character, that the system demands an unsustainable volume of emotional labor. Avoid naming specific scholars or texts, and do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
