A colleague summarized the debate over genuine moral dilemmas in these terms: "If you genuinely ought to do A and genuinely ought to do B, yet cannot do both, then morality issues contradictory prescriptions—do A and do not-A—which violates 'ought implies can' and renders the theory incoherent. Defenders of dilemmas reply by citing 'moral residue,' the regret that persists after one obligation is sacrificed; but this is only a psychological after-effect. Once rational deliberation has determined which obligation is weightier and overridden the other, the lingering discomfort is sentimental, not moral, so there is no contradiction—merely a hard choice with emotional costs."
