As a novelist assembling a collection of conceptual fiction, I ask you to write a short story that enacts Hofstadter's knob-twiddling model of creativity—the idea that a conceptual domain can be explored by adjusting continuous parameters or "knobs"—but avoids a generic "artist makes variations" plot. Instead, center the narrative on a fictional resonator-craftsman whose instruments possess coupled acoustic dimensions: altering cavity geometry unavoidably shifts harmonic decay, so that not all parameter combinations preserve the instrument's audible identity. The protagonist's arc should turn on recognizing which knobs are structurally coupled and which can be independently varied, ultimately constructing a resonator that remains identifiably itself despite radically different physical configurations. Embed the philosophical mechanics in the sensory and emotional texture of the craft—wood grain, vibration, silence—rather than in explicit exposition, and keep the story self-contained.
