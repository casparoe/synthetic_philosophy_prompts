I'm working on my dissertation in philosophy of mind, specifically on the metaphysics of phenomenal time, and I've become stuck on what looks like an institutional contradiction in consciousness science. On the one hand, researchers routinely invoke color-phi phenomena and related spatiotemporal illusions as evidence that the brain does not passively record sensory input but actively constructs a coherent narrative—sometimes characterized in Dennett's terms as either Stalinesque (fabricating a seamless present) or Orwellian (rewriting the past to match a later interpretation). On the other hand, the standard experimental protocol in both psychophysics and clinical neuropsychology—particularly in studies attempting to localize the neural correlates of conscious awareness—depends on treating subjects' retrospective verbal reports about the precise temporal order and qualitative content of briefly presented stimuli as authoritative data. Subjects are asked to timestamp their awareness ("At what point did you register the color change?") and these reports are plotted against neural recordings as if they were direct measurements of an internal phenomenal timeline, despite the field's simultaneous acknowledgment that the brain might have revised or constructed that very timeline after the fact.

What philosophical commitments about the relation between consciousness, memory, and first-person report does this methodological practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory: does it treat reportability as constitutive of consciousness, does it assume a transparent access to phenomenal chronology, or does it rely on a hidden distinction between "online" experience and "offline" reconstruction? Is the implicit epistemology coherent, or does it quietly rule out the very revisionist interpretations that the color-phi literature is taken to support? Furthermore, distinguish what the practice strictly requires philosophically from what it merely accommodates, identify where it is philosophically underdetermined, and say whether adopting a consistently Stalinesque or consistently Orwellian framework would force a fundamental redesign of how temporal data are collected in consciousness studies.
