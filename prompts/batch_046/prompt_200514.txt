Write a villanelle in regular iambic pentameter that structurally enacts Schopenhauer's claim that music is not a representation of Platonic Ideas mediated through form, as painting or poetry are, but rather the direct and unmediated objectification of the Will itself. Use two refrains: one naming representational art ("The Idea takes its shape in line and light"), the other naming immediate Will ("The Will sounds through melody alone"). Arrange the nineteen lines so that the first refrain returns altered by its surrounding context each time—modified, as representations are modified by their objects—while the second refrain returns verbatim and unchanged, mirroring Schopenhauer's view that the Will is everywhere identical and not subject to the differentiation that governs phenomena. Place the volta in the tercet where the speaker realizes melody does not depict feeling but is feeling's direct presence without image or concept. In the final couplet, bring both refrains together side by side without synthesis or resolution, letting the gap between them stand for the irreducible distinction between representation and Will. Allow the second refrain to break the rhyme scheme exactly once, at its middle appearance, so that the formal rupture enacts music's independence from the principle of sufficient reason that governs all other arts. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with Book III of The World as Will and Representation recognizes the metaphysical architecture rather than a mere name-check.
