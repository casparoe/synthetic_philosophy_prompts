For my philosphy seminar I jotted down my commitments about conceivability and possibility, but I'm not sure they hang together. Here is the list: I think clear conceivability without contradiction is a decent guide to metaphysical possibility, except for phenomenal states where I think conceivability only shows a conceptual gap. I think for social kinds like nations or money conceivability is basically useless, but for physical objects and biological kinds it works okay. I think zombies are genuinely conceivable yet impossible, and that this is fine because phenomenal concepts involve direct reference not descriptive content. I also think artificial minds are conceivable and that this does show they're possible, even though functionl properties feel closer to social kinds than physical ones. Finally I think we can clearly conceive of 2+2=5, and that this proves conceivability never tracks metaphysical modality for abstract objects. Go through these and tell me which pairs actually conflict and how badly. For each real tension say what the cheapest revision is, and tell me which single change would fix the most problems. Also flag any pairs that only look tense but dissolve once you see the distinction I'm relying on.
