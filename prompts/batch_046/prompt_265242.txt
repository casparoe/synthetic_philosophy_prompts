I'm working through a course on the philosophy of mathematics and have found myself drawn to a somewhat heterodox finitist position—specifically, the view that Hilbert's hotel and Cantor's diagonal argument do not establish actual infinities as metaphysically coherent, but instead reveal that cardinal arithmetic applies only to abstract structures whose elements are never all simultaneously present, and that treating infinite collections as completed wholes violates a determinacy condition on plurality: for any definite collection, there must be a fact of the matter about whether a given object belongs to it that does not depend on an open-ended procedure. I hold that finitism is therefore not merely a mathematical restriction but a metaphysical thesis about the conditions under which multiplicity can be genuinely totalized, and that the apparent paradoxes of infinite sets are symptoms of applying part-whole logic beyond its legitimate domain.

What would genuinely count against this? I want the defeaters sorted by severity, including those a committed defender should concede rather than dismiss. Specifically: are there formal results showing that finitist restrictions collapse into contradictions or fail to recover standard arithmetic, and which are decisive versus technically circumventable? Could there be an interpretation of Hilbert's hotel that preserves determinacy without abandoning actual infinity—say, by distinguishing potential and actual multiplicity in a way that does not require finitism? Do physical or cosmological considerations—such as the conceptual coherence of an actually infinite spatial or temporal extent—bear on the metaphysical claim, or are they separate? And has this determinacy-based finitism been insulated by qualifications, for instance by allowing abstract sets but denying concrete instantiation, to the point where it risks unfalsifiability? Is that insulation a weakness for a view of this kind, or is it a legitimate boundary? I'd like an honest assessment of which objections are fatal, which are merely awkward, and whether the position ultimately risks collapsing into a verbal reformulation of standard constructivism rather than maintaining a genuinely distinct metaphysical stance.
