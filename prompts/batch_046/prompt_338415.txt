I am a doctoral student in philosophy of medicine and I am designing a conceptual taxonomy for a graduate seminar on psychiatric classification. I want a branching decision tree — built from logical distinctions rather than from a list of named theorists — that sorts interpretations of Thomas Szasz's thesis that mental illness is a myth. Each internal node must be a concrete claim or case distinction that a respondent could answer without knowing which interpretive school it leads to. The tree should terminate in at least these six leaves: (a) a strict literalist denial that psychiatric conditions involve any brain-based dysfunction; (b) a category-error view that accepts brain dysfunction but denies that mental predicates apply to brains in the same way they apply to bodies; (c) an epistemic-demarcation view that concedes psychiatric suffering is real but holds that psychiatric diagnosis lacks the predictive and explanatory validity characteristic of physical medicine; (d) an ideological-function view that treats the myth primarily as a mechanism of social control rather than an ontological mistake; (e) Szasz's own framework replacing illness-talk with problems in living; and (f) a contemporary neurodiversity-informed revision that retains an illness framework for some conditions but rejects it for others. For every leaf, state in one sentence the commitment that is most awkward relative to the other branches — the consequence its defenders must swallow that rival positions avoid. In addition, map out any logical combinations of answers that the tree allows but that no named position actually occupies (for instance, accepting both full medical legitimacy for all psychiatric diagnosis and the thesis that the category functions mainly as social control), marking them as unoccupied and briefly diagnosing whether the gap looks incoherent, merely unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or source citations; the point is to test whether the instrument sorts people by their substantive commitments rather than by their familiarity with the literature. Write the tree so that it could be used by someone who grasps the conceptual stakes but does not already know which label belongs to which view.
