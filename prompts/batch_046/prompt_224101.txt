gödel's first theorem says that in any consistant formal system F with enough arithmetic, you can build a statement G that essentially asserts its own unprovability inside F. if F is consistent, G is true but unprovable within F. the second theorem says F cannot prove its own consistency statement, con(f), unless f is actually inconsistent.

for legal codes, the second theorem means any self-referential rulebook with an internal appeals court can't prove its own consistency, so contradictions or loopholes are unavoidable. that's why a complete legal system is impossible.
