At a graduate seminar on metaphysics, two students debated the classic statue-and-clay problem. Jordan argued that Michelangelo's David and the clay composing it are literally one object: they share every part and spatial region, and differences in persistence conditions—the clay survives being squashed into a ball, the statue does not—are merely descriptive framing rather than metaphysical distinctions, so Ockham's razor favors identity. Sam replied by invoking Leibniz's law: if the clay can survive squashing while the statue is destroyed, they differ in modal properties and therefore cannot be identical, which means they are distinct entities that merely coincide. Jordan countered that the modal difference is relational, not intrinsic: the statue essentially bears the relation "being intended as an artwork by its creator," which the clay lacks, and one object can support different relational descriptions without violating identity, just as the morning star and evening star are Venus. Sam then pressed that identity still produces a contradiction: in the squashing, the statue is destroyed, yet the object—which is also the clay—survives, so one and the same thing is both destroyed and preserved. Jordan responded by proposing a phase-sortal framework: just as a caterpillar and a butterfly are incompatible phases of one organism, the statue-phase and clay-phase are temporal phases of a single underlying substance, meaning destruction applies only to the phase and not to the continuing entity itself.

Please respond with exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: in items 1 through 4 assess Jordan's opening identity claim, Sam's Leibniz-law objection, Jordan's relational defense, and Sam's contradiction argument respectively, rating each as clearly mistaken, partly right, or clearly right with a brief reason; in item 5 assess Jordan's phase-sortal reply, state who is more right overall, and identify one important theoretical option about material constitution that both speakers overlook.
