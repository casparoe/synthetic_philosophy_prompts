I'm currently in a philosophy seminar on population ethics, and we've spent the last two weeks on the procreation asymmetry, but our instructor pushed us to consider a framing that isn't usually emphasized in the standard literature: treating the absence of suffering-beings not just as a null comparison point, but as an empty 'potential welfare repository' that lacks any bearer, which raises the question of whether impersonal value can be assigned to mere possibilities without generating a corresponding duty. For our closing session, I want to present a poem that works through this specific tension rather than simply restating the familiar asymmetry. Please write a broken sonnet in iambic pentameter: the octave should enumerate, one per line, eight conditions that make the creation of a suffering being wrong — foreseeability, the existence of a subject who bears the harm, the absence of compensating benefit to that subject, and so on — with a regular ABAB CDCD rhyme scheme. At the volta in line 9, the poem should turn to the mirror case of creating a happy being, and the sestet should attempt to list parallel conditions; however, the rhyme scheme should deliberately fail after line 12, leaving lines 13 and 14 unrhymed and ending on the same repeated word ('none'), so that the structural absence of a closing couplet performs the absence of the counterpart reason. The meter should remain intact even where rhyme collapses, so that the form feels like an argument that loses its footing rather than a poem that abandons form altogether. It should be precise enough that a student of population ethics recognizes the conditions, but it should not resolve the tension — the poem should end with the open rhyme and the empty repository still in view.
