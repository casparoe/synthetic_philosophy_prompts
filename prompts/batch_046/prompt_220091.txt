Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on whether genuine moral dilemmas are actually possible.

Camp A — call them the 'residue realists': they think some situations present genuinely incompatible moral requirements that can't be resolved by any hidden ranking, leaving unavoidable moral residue. They take this as a feature of plural values and agent-relative reasons, not just an epistemic blindspot.

Camp B — the 'resolutionists': they hold that in every situation there is a single correct action, even if agents never discover it. Apparent dilemmas come from cognitive limits, emotional overload, or framing mistakes, and 'moral residue' is usually grief or self-deception about having done wrong.

The collaboration should zero in on a specific hard subclass both agree is the real test case: symmetric, self-created dilemmas where the agent previously made an explicit commitment to treat two parties equally — like a clinician-researcher who designed a randomized trial protocol that now forces her to withold a suddenly-available life-saving treatment from one arm in order to preserve statistical integrity, with both patients being her prior commitments.

The deliverable is a document both sides could put their names on. Format it like this:

1. Agreed claims (numbered): about the formal structure of practical reasoning, what distinguishes a moral conflict from a conflict of desires, the phenomenology of being torn, the legitimate role of agent-relative reasons, and why naive 'just weigh the goods' answers fail for these cases. Keep them substantive, not vacuous.

2. Disagreements table: precise wording each camp accepts for its own side. Cover at least:
- whether incommensurability can be genuinely real or is always a failure of a common metric;
- whether feeling moral residue after a correct choice is ever rational, or always a mistake;
- whether a self-created symmetric commitment can generate a genuinely binding obligation to do the impossible.

3. Cruxes: the sub-questions that, if settled, would move at least one camp. For each, record in advance what each side predicts and what kind of argument or case would count as evidence they would recieve.
