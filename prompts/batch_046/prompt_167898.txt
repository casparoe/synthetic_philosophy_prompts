I was appointed three months ago to my university's Institutional AI Governance Committee in the wake of the public controversy over our adaptive tutoring deployment, and I am now drafting the formal recommendation memo for Thursday's meeting on the research consortium's proposed Operational Intent Alignment certification framework. The proposal asks the committee to approve deployment certification for high-capability systems based solely on statistical correspondence between outputs and explicit institutional directives, deliberately excluding any evaluation of whether internal representations or long-run behavioral dispositions converge with human normative commitments. The researchers contend that this avoids the epistemic overreach of value-alignment testing, which they say presupposes a false universality of moral psychology. The opposing members argue that intent alignment without value alignment is the precise failure mode that produces specification gaming and instrumental convergence, licensing systems to pursue operator goals through means that erode the institution's unstated but constitutive commitments. Please draft the committee's decision memo—recommending adoption, rejection, or adoption with binding conditions—with reasoning the research team could actually respond to and with an honest, substantive treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side. Lead with your bottom-line recommendation in the first sentence, then give the reasoning with no preamble and no restatement of the question. Write in American English. Include an explicit statement of what specific finding or argument would cause you to reverse your recommendation—whether about the empirical prevalence of specification gaming in intent-only architectures, the measurability of value convergence, or another point—and identify where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
