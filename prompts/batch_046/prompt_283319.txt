I am a novelist completing the fifth installment of a multi-generational saga in which the metaphysical framework of the narrative world has become gradually unstable. In each revision cycle, I face approximately twenty proposals—generated by myself, my editors, or continuity consultants—to allow characters to challenge or revise foundational premises that readers currently treat as implicit background conditions of the fiction: for instance, whether causality runs linearly, whether sensory memory is generally reliable, or whether moral status is distributed equally across all beings within the world. I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded strictly in hinge epistemology and in Wittgenstein's framework of certainty and hinge propositions, that my editorial team and I can apply without external reference material and within fifteen minutes per proposal. The procedure should take the form of an ordered sequence of questions, each with a specified criterion for a sufficient answer; it should yield one of three definite outputs—retain the premise as an unchallenged hinge, permit explicit contestation within the narrative, or suspend the decision for authorial judgment on non-epistemic grounds—and should flag precisely where judgment is unavoidable rather than disguised as algorithm. It must include a compact set of test sentences or scenario descriptions that can be applied quickly, and it must be applied in the response to three worked cases: first, a prophet-character who denies linear causality in a society that depends on it; second, a courtroom scene in which the reliability of memory is put on trial; and third, the proposal to let a major character deny the equal moral weight of all beings, where I want the procedure to return an answer I will likely find unwelcome—specifically, that the premise must remain protected—while still explaining why. The response should also state explicitly what the procedure cannot settle, particularly in cases where a hinge serves simultaneous epistemic and emotional or aesthetic functions for the reader, and should avoid all bibliographies, citations, or appeals to secondary literature I could not verify without web access. The stakes are narrative coherence and reader trust; the sign-off authority is shared between me and a senior continuity editor; and the constraint is that the procedure must be operable by someone with general philosophical literacy but no specialist training in epistemology.
