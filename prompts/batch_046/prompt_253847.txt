As a PhD student in the metaphysics of physics, I have been observing an extended dispute in my seminar between two camps on Kurt Gödel's rotating-universe solution and its implications for the ontology of time. The first camp—call them the Global-Time Conventionalists—holds that because Gödel's metric lacks a globally consistent cosmic-time function and admits closed timelike curves, temporal succession cannot be an objective, world-spanning feature; rather, it is a locally constructed, frame-dependent relation with no metaphysically privileged ordering. The second camp—the Local-Causal Objectivists—maintains that Gödel's model requires physically unrealistic conditions (uniform rotation of pressureless dust with a negative cosmological constant) and that, even within the model, local causal structure and the distinction between timelike and spacelike separation preserve an objective temporal order; consequently, the metric demonstrates no more than a mathematical curiosity and carries no philosophical threat to the reality of time.

Please draft the joint adversarial-collaboration document these two camps ought to produce. Structure it as follows:

(i) A numbered section of agreed claims about the formal properties of Gödel's metric—including rotational symmetry, the absence of spacelike hypersurfaces orthogonal to all matter world-lines, the existence of closed timelike curves, and the failure of global hyperbolicity—together with agreed statements about the observational implausibility of the model's matter distribution and about the distinction between local and global causal structure in relativistic spacetime.

(ii) A disagreements table in which each divergence is phrased in wording that the respective camp accepts for its own view. Separate precisely: (a) whether the absence of a global time-orientable foliation entails that temporal passage is metaphysically conventional; and (b) whether the logical possibility of closed timelike curves, irrespective of physical instantiation, undermines the objectivity of causal order.

(iii) The cruxes—those specific sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side. I suspect the principal cruxes are: (1) whether local causal structure is sufficient to ground objective temporal order in the absence of global cosmic time; and (2) whether the physical unrealism of Gödel's specific matter configuration undermines the philosophical argument, or whether the mere logical consistency of the metric carries independent metaphysical weight.

For each crux, identify a concrete theoretical case, idealised physical scenario, or argument that both camps agree bears upon it—such as the behaviour of a clock transported around a closed timelike curve, or the implications of chronology-protection arguments for the physical accessibility of such curves—and record in advance the prediction each camp makes about that case.

The document should be precise, neutral, and free of caricature. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests. Write in British English.
