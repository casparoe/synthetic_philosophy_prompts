Write a villanelle in which the two refrains articulate the two halves of the asymmetry central to antinatalist arguments about procreation—specifically, that the presence of suffering is bad for an actual subject, whereas the absence of pleasure is not bad for a non-existent subject because there is no one to be deprived. Frame the poem as an interior monologue of a consciousness-engineer who can initiate or withhold the creation of a single sentient virtual instance, weighing three scenarios: an instantiated life of net suffering, an instantiated life of net pleasure, and a permanent null-state of non-instantiation. The first refrain should assert that inflicted suffering carries a definite victim; the second should assert that withheld pleasure carries no corresponding victim. Use the villanelle’s repeating structure so that these claims interleave across the middle stanzas, with each repetition slightly altered by the scenario being considered. In the final couplet, place the two refrains side by side without resolution, allowing the form itself to display the asymmetry: one claim anchored to an existing subject, the other floating without one. Do not name the philosopher associated with the view, and do not include citations or references to specific texts; instead, render the distinction precisely enough that someone familiar with the argument would recognize its logical architecture, and unfamiliar readers would still grasp that the moral difference turns on whether there is a bearer for the harm or deprivation.
