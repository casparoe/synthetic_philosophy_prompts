I just finished a long, somewhat heated argument with a friend about whether teaching actual infinity to undergraduates implicitly commits them to an ontological position they have not earned. I am writing as a voting member of our university’s General Education Curriculum Committee, which must decide by Thursday whether to approve a proposed introductory course titled “Infinity in Mathematics and Mind.” The syllabus pairs Hilbert’s hotel with Cantor’s diagonal argument as demonstrations of completed, actual infinity, then introduces finitism only as a historical curiosity. My friend insists that presenting Hilbert’s hotel as a coherent thought experiment smuggles in Platonist commitments about completed totalities, and that the course should be rejected—or at least fundamentally revised—unless finitism is treated as a live, equally valid framework rather than a superseded error.

Please draft the committee memo I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. The memo must recommend a specific, actionable decision—approve, approve with conditions, or reject—and include a concrete implementation plan (for example, required syllabus revisions, a mandatory supplementary module, or a timeline for re-review). The reasoning should be accessible to committee members without formal mathematics backgrounds, but it must not reduce the philosophical dispute to a caricature. In particular, engage seriously with the finitist claim that Hilbert’s hotel demonstrates only potential, never actual, infinity, and with the coherence argument that Cantor’s pairing shows actual infinity is indispensable to modern analysis. Treat the proposed “physical-model module” as central: the syllabus includes an interactive installation in which students manipulate a finite but expandable set of rooms to simulate accommodating guests in Hilbert’s hotel, designed so that the model can always take one more guest but never reaches a state of “full occupancy at infinity.” The proponents say this illustrates potential infinity honestly; the critics say it implicitly presupposes an actual infinite set of potential rooms, thereby begging the question against finitism. Your memo should address whether this module resolves the pedagogical tension or deepens the ontological confusion, and it must present the strongest argument for the position your recommendation ultimately rejects. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; the committee needs reasoning it can evaluate on its own terms.
