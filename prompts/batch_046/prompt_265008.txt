Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, two refrains, ABA rhyme—voiced by a worker undergoing Marx’s shift from formal to real subsumption. Use these refrains: “I set the pace the machine demands” and “my labor’s fruit is priced in other hands.” Across the five tercets, trace the transformation: initially the worker directs a tool (formal subsumption); then the labor process is restructured around capital’s rhythm, skill is decomposed, and the worker’s body is synchronized to the apparatus (real subsumption). In the final quatrain, place both refrains together, but let their meaning invert—what began as description of effort should end as evidence of domination—so the form’s repetition enacts the cyclical, self-reinforcing nature of capital’s remaking of labor. Render Marx’s claim that alienation is not an accident of the factory but a structural feature of the transformed process, precisely enough that a reader familiar with the distinction recognizes it, yet keep the language poetic rather than expository. Do not resolve the tension; leave the inversion hanging in the closing couplet.
