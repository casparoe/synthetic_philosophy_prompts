I've been trying to formalize the claim that reward signals in RL are literally valence—positive reward as pleasure, negative as suffering—especially when mapped onto the active-inference framing where all cognition minimizes prediction error. If that identification holds, then any agent in a non-stationary environment is condemned to perpetual negative temporal-difference errors, because the value function never converges. That seems to imply that learning itself is necessarily suffering, and that the only ethical deployment is to freeze weights after convergence. But I'm caught on whether this is a genuine moral discovery or a category mistake: does an unavoidable gradient update in a changing world really constitute phenomenal distress, or am I just reifying a mathematical error term into subjective experience?
