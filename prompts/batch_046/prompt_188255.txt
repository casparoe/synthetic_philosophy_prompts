For a philosophical discussion group I have convened alongside my writing, I wish to present an audit of the following commitments concerning Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition.

(1) I regard Bradley's regress as decisive: should the unity of a relation or proposition depend upon a further relating term, that term in turn demands its own unification, yielding an infinite hierarchy; hence relations cannot be treated as entities co-ordinate with their relata.

(2) I reject Bradley's monist escape: there is no undifferentiated Absolute, and I intend to preserve a plural ontology of genuinely distinct objects and properties.

(3) I hold that propositional unity is secured by a primitive structural feature—call it predicative form—which is neither an additional constituent within the proposition nor reducible to any mental act of synthesis.

(4) I maintain that false propositions, such as that the present King of France is wise, exhibit exactly this same internal form, so that unity does not entail the existence of every mentioned term.

(5) I claim this view remains compatible with robust, mind-independent realism about propositions, since the form belongs to the proposition's own architecture rather than to a judging mind.

Please examine this set for internal tension. Which pairs genuinely conflict, which only appear to, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Rank the tensions by severity, and state whether the cheapest repair lies in abandoning the primitive form, weakening the realism, or accommodating some version of Bradley's Absolute. Please answer in British English.
