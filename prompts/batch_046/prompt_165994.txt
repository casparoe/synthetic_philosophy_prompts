I serve on the Joint Governance Committee of the Lake Meridian Consortium, an inter-institutional body charged with approving operational protocols for multi-party resource-sharing agreements. We are currently evaluating a proposed "Sequential Escrow Protocol" for a six-stage freshwater-allocation pilot: at each biannual review, any participating institution may claim its accumulated share of the pooled reserve and exit, or both parties may roll the reserve forward into the next stage, where the potential mutual payoff increases but so does the risk of total forfeiture upon unilateral withdrawal. The protocol's architects contend that this escalating structure naturally incentivizes sustained cooperation, yet a minority report invokes the backward induction paradox and the structure of the centipede game, arguing that fully rational actors with common knowledge of rationality should foresee inevitable defection at stage one and therefore treat the entire framework as theoretically unstable, regardless of empirical studies in which cooperation extends far longer. Further complicating the design is an embedded "reputational shadow ledger"—an informal, non-binding, publicly visible record of which institutions have previously terminated agreements early—intended to impose future costs on defection without altering the formal payoff matrix.

Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approve the protocol outright, approve it only with specific modifications to the shadow-ledger mechanism, or reject it in favor of a fixed-term contract—together with an implementation timeline. The reasoning must treat the game-theoretic objection with intellectual seriousness rather than dismissing it, and should specifically examine whether the shadow ledger genuinely resolves the backward-induction problem or merely displaces the paradox onto a meta-level of reasoning about future reputation and common knowledge of that reasoning. Include an honest account of the strongest consideration supporting the position you do not ultimately endorse.
