okay so my seminar partner and i have been going back and forth on whether jaspers idea of political guilt can actually span generations, and i think we accidentally built a reductio that proves way too much. heres the chain:

1. if a nation can be genuinely guilty for crimes committed before anyone currently alive was born, then the subject of that guilt has to be something that survives the complete turnover of every individual member.

2. that enduring subject can only be the state or nation as an institutional structure with continuous legal identity.

3. but institutions dont have minds, so they cant be morally guilty in the same sense persons are; at best they carry legal liability.

4. so intergenerational national guilt must reduce to the present citizens non-guilty obligation to make reparations, not to any real shared blame.

5. jaspers explicitly rejects this reduction: he says political guilt attaches to citizens by virtue of citizenship alone, even if they resisted the regime, and its not just a debt but a real stain.

6. if citizenship alone is enough to make you genuinely guilty for acts you never authorized and could'nt stop, then membership in any continuous association works the same way.

7. take the "1995 cohort" — people born that year dont form an agent, but they form a demographically continuous group that ages together. if institutional continuity is enough, then this cohort could be collectively guilty for a 1965 war crime, since the cohort existed then as infants and persists now.

8. thats obviously absurd; demographic cohorts arent moral agents and cant bear guilt.

9. therefore step 5 has to go, and jaspers political guilt collapses into either personal criminal guilt or a metaphor for reparative duty.

id really like to keep 5 if possible, and i dont want to toss 1 either — guilt without a subject sounds weird. but obviously the chain cant be right. which step do i reject, and whats the cost either way? if i drop 5 do i lose jaspers whole framework, or can i salvage the distinction between political and criminal guilt by fixing something earlier?
