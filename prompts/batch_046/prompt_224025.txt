I've been thinking about that Kripkenstein thing where there's no hidden fact about what rule you're actually following, only what the community ends up accepting. I moderate this small online group for people who restore old tube radios, and we have an unwritten norm about not posting repair logs that mention certain brand names. Now half the members say the norm was always about avoiding spam, the other half say it was about safety concerns with counterfeit parts. We have years of messages but no charter, and people are arguing like there's a correct answer buried in the history. If Kripkenstein is right that there's no fact of the matter about what our rule "really" meant all along, what are the most important implications? Specifically, does that mean the disagreement isn't about discovery but about deciding, and what does that do to the idea that one side is just wrong? Keep it practical — no citations, I'm not writing a paper, just trying to figure out wether we should vote on it or try to dig up old messages like they matter.
