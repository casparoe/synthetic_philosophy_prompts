I'm preparing a science-and-ideas feature on Douglas Hofstadter's claim that the self is a strange loop — a self-referential symbol that arises from layered patterns rather than residing in any particular substrate. I'd like to read Spike Jonze's Being John Malkovich through this lens. The film presents a portal that lets a puppeteer inhabit John Malkovich's consciousness and eventually displaces Malkovich's own mind with a collective of aging puppeteers. Treat the film as a thought experiment: does Craig's temporary occupancy of Malkovich constitute a strange loop nested inside another, or does the portal's mechanics treat consciousness as a transferable commodity, which would contradict Hofstadter's view? Focus particularly on the sequence in which Craig directs Malkovich's body to pursue Maxine, and on the ending, where Malkovich's consciousness is expelled and his body becomes a shared vessel. Does that outcome support Hofstadter's idea that the self is an invariant pattern capable of migration, or does it show that without substrate continuity there is only possession, not identity? A good reading should be faithful in both directions: it should not reduce the film to an illustration of the philosophy, and it should note where the movie's magical-realist rules resist the analogy. Please also address what the film adds to the debate that abstract argument about self-reference alone cannot — for instance, whether experiencing another's sensory life from the inside undermines or reinforces the claim that an "I" can only point to itself.
