I am a software engineer without formal philosophical training, though I have been auditing an upper-level metaphysics seminar that a colleague teaches, and I have volunteered to help design the final exam. I need a single essay question—together with a model answer and a detailed marking scheme—on the metaphysics of dispositions, specifically the problems that finkish dispositions and masked dispositions pose for the simple conditional analysis (the view that an object is disposed to behave in a certain way just in case it would behave that way if the appropriate triggering conditions were to obtain). Rather than asking students merely to reproduce textbook definitions of “fink” and “mask,” I want the question to present a layered hypothetical case and force them to apply the distinction precisely: a laboratory-engineered hydrogel that is chemically soluble in distilled water at standard temperature and pressure, but which is synthesized with an embedded micro-reactor that instantly polymerizes a waterproof barrier across the hydrogel’s surface upon any contact with water, and which is simultaneously encased in an impact-absorbing lattice that prevents mechanical fracture. Students should be required to determine whether the hydrogel is genuinely soluble, to classify the micro-reactor and the lattice respectively as instances of a fink, a mask, or neither, and to assess whether this compound case undermines only the simple conditional analysis or also threatens categorical-base theories of dispositions.

The exam is intended for an upper-level seminar; students should have approximately 45 minutes and should write about 800 words. The difficulty must come from selecting the crux: the question should be unanswerable by reciting a summary of Martin-style finks or Johnston-style masks, and it should separate students who understand the metaphysical structure from those who have only memorized the labels. In particular, a strong answer needs to explain why the micro-reactor interferes with the manifestation-trigger relation in a way characteristic of a fink, whereas the lattice interferes with the expression or preservation of the underlying categorical basis in a way characteristic of a mask, and then evaluate whether a categorical-base account can accommodate such layered interference without collapsing into circularity.

Please write the exam question itself, a model answer of roughly 800 words, and a marking scheme that distinguishes an A response—which must clearly separate the fink mechanism from the mask mechanism, identify exactly where the conditional analysis fails in each direction, and argue for or against the categorical-base solution with explicit reasoning—from a B response, which correctly describes both mechanisms but either conflates them, treats them as interchangeable, or misses the metaphysical upshot about whether dispositions can survive layered prevention intact. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the model should be able to construct the answer from conceptual knowledge alone.
