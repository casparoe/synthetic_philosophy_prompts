(2) Citizens cant delegate moral permissions they dont individually hold, so they cant authorize a politician to do what would be wrong for them to do alone.
(3) Therefore any act that is genuinely immoral is automatically outside the politician's legitimate authority.
(4) But dirty-hands cases are exactly those where a politician does something immoral (like lying or ordering surveillance) because the public good demands it.
(5) So if (3) is right then (4) describes an impossible situation: the politician lacks authority precisely when dirty hands says they have a duty to act.
(6) That means dirty hands isnt a special dilemma, its just a category mistake — we mislabel justified but unpleasant decisions as "wrong" when they're actually permitted by the scope of office.
(7) By the same reasoning, torture or assassination ordered for security isnt "dirty" at all; its clean institutional housekeeping.
(8) So the whole literature on dirty hands is solving a problem that dissolves once you look at delegation correctly.
