Consider the following line of reasoning from Henri Poincaré's Science and Hypothesis. Poincaré observes that no isolated experiment can ever compel us to adopt one geometry over another, because any discrepancy between predicted and observed measurements can always be absorbed either by altering our geometric axioms or by altering our auxiliary physical hypotheses—say, by postulating that measuring rods contract or expand in particular directions. From this he concludes that geometric axioms are neither synthetic a priori truths nor straightforward empirical generalizations; they are conventions, chosen for convenience and guided but not dictated by experience. He adds, however, that conventional status does not entail arbitrariness: once a framework is adopted, it constrains the form of physical laws, and some conventions are so unwieldy that they are practically excluded.

I want help understanding precisely where Poincaré draws the line between conventional choice and empirical constraint in this argument, and then I want you to apply that understanding to a constructed case. Imagine two cosmological models—call them Lambda and Mod—that reproduce every gravitational-wave and cosmic-microwave-background observation in our current datasets. Lambda embeds dark energy as a cosmological constant within standard general relativity; Mod keeps spacetime flat and replaces Einstein's field equations with a nonlocal modification whose coefficients depend on the full past light cone. The frameworks therefore differ radically in ontology yet yield identical observable predictions. Now introduce an asymmetry Poincaré never discussed: evaluating Mod's predictions for a dataset of size N requires computational resources scaling exponentially in N, whereas Lambda scales linearly.

Take a definite, committed position: on Poincaré's own terms, does this exponential computational burden count as a legitimate form of the convenience that makes Lambda preferable—thereby resolving the underdetermination pragmatically—or does it lie outside the scope of convention he envisioned, leaving the choice genuinely arbitrary? In your answer, specify which parts of Poincaré's reasoning about convention and constraint support your judgment, which parts need revision to accommodate this factor, and why a different interpretation fails. Do not lay out both options neutrally; defend one side with reasons.
