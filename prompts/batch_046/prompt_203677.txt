Here is my current position on deliberative idealizations, for a consistency check before I present it in a working group:

(1) HCH should be read as an idealization of internal deliberation, not merely collective reasoning: the root agent poses sub-questions to child nodes that are copies of itself, and the non-collapsing condition requires that these copies can in principle reach conclusions different from the root's without being rationally defective.

(2) Factored cognition gives the correct descriptive picture of actual reasoning: deliberation decomposes into functionally specialized subsystems—such as belief formation, preference evaluation, and temporal discounting—that operate with distinct representational formats and do not each instantiate the agent's full normative framework.

(3) For HCH to apply to actual deliberation rather than remain a toy model, its child nodes must be epistemic peers in the relevant domain: they need access to the same reasoning norms and evidential standards as the root, or else their outputs are merely instrumental inputs rather than independent deliberative voices.

(4) The specialization described by factored cognition is constitutive, not contingent: if a belief-formation module also carried out full normative evaluation with the agent's complete conceptual repertoire, it would cease to be the specialized subsystem in question.

(5) I want to apply the HCH framework to model actual human deliberation by identifying the nodes with the subsystems specified by factored cognition—for instance, letting a planning module serve as a child node that answers a sub-question about means-end coherence.
