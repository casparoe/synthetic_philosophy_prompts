Give an example of a conceptual issue within luck egalitarianism in which the standard brute-luck versus option-luck distinction becomes indeterminate due to what might be called structurally induced adaptive preference formation. Construct a case involving an agent who, during a prolonged period of deprivation clearly caused by brute bad luck, develops expensive and highly specific preferences—for example, a refined capacity for and commitment to an elaborate artistic practice—through sustained, deliberate effort that resembles option-luck choice. Once the deprivation is corrected, the egalitarian must decide whether to compensate for the costs of satisfying these preferences. Your example should isolate the precise conceptual feature that makes the preference's causal origin ambiguous—specifically, whether the preference should be classified by the conditions under which it was formed, by the volitional structure of its acquisition, or by its functional integration into the agent's practical identity—and explain what this ambiguity reveals about the underlying assumption that distributive compensation can be determined by sorting preferences according to a single origin-based taxonomy rather than by assessing their role in the agent's autonomous life plan.
