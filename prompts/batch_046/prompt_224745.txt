Each node must be answerable without knowing which leaf it leads to; include at least one niche scenario involving an AI-curated memorial archive that algorithmically surfaces inherited trauma narratives to non-experiential descendants, with the option of permanent algorithmic suppression. First list the full logical space—the combinations of yes, no, or qualified answers across the four nodes—then locate the named positions inside it, marking any unoccupied combinations and briefly diagnosing whether they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Specifically flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, especially where Margalit's view and a reconstructed post-therapeutic position might overlap on the conditions for forgiveness. The instrument should sort people by what they believe about cases, not by which label they prefer, so no node should ask whether one sides with Margalit or Rieff. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific page numbers; the tree should be constructible by reasoning about the structure of memory obligations alone. Keep the framing formal and in the weeds: avoid generic questions like whether remembering is important, and make every branch depend on a precise judgment about responsibility, inheritance, or technological mediation.
