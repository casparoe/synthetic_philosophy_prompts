I'm defending the view that pain asymbolia reveals a separable symbolic-evaluative layer in pain processing: affected patients register nociceptive signals and can report them, yet show neither the characteristic affective response nor protective withdrawal, which suggests that the badness of pain is not intrinsic to the sensation itself but depends on an intact mapping to harm-representation. From this I argue that the standard clinical definition—which ties pain necessarily to an unpleasant emotional experience—conflates nociception with suffering, and that chronic-pain treatment should sometimes target the evaluative mapping rather than suppress sensation alone.

Next week I present this at a joint seminar of clinical neurologists who localize asymbolia lesions and philosophers of embodied cognition who reject modular box-and-arrow accounts of affect. Give me the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased as each camp would phrase them, with what I should concede, what I should hold, the weakest point in my position that I should defend rather than hope they miss, and the single objection I am hoping nobody raises.
