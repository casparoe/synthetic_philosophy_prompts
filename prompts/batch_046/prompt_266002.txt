ok here's an argument my study group built and now it feels like it swallows itself. (1) reductionism about testimony says accepting what someone tells you is only justified if you've got independant reason to think they're reliable, and that reason eventually bottoms out in your own perception or reasoning. (2) but apply that to language learning: you learn what words mean from other speakers—parents, teachers, random people—so that's testimonial knowledge about meaning. (3) by (1), you shouldn't accept those linguistic teachings unless you already have independant verification of the speakers' reliability about word meanings. (4) yet you can't verify what "verification" means without already understanding the language you're using to do the check. (5) so reductionism requires you to already possess the linguistic knowledge it claims you can only get through prior justification. (6) therefore reductionism is self-defeating not just for ordinary facts but for language itself, which means anti-reductionism has to be right across the board. (7) but then what's stopping us from blindly trusting anyone about anything? i think step (4) is pulling a fast one—maybe there's a loop but not a vicious one—but i can't name the exit. if i have to choose, i'd rather save (1) for regular testimony and just carve out an exception for language, but that feels ad hoc. where does this actually break?
