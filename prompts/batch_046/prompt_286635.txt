I am currently drafting a long-form feature on the intersection of theoretical physics and metaphysics, and in my background reading I encountered a summary of the Rietdijk-Putnam argument that I want to verify before I treat its conclusions as settled in my reporting. The summary—drawn from a well-circulated essay that bridges science writing and philosophy—presents the reasoning as follows. Rietdijk and Putnam are said to have shown that special relativity, by eliminating any absolute simultaneity, proves determinism is true: since observers in relative motion disagree about which distant events are simultaneous with “now,” there can be no observer-independent present, which means every event that any inertial frame counts as present must actually exist. From this, the essay infers that past, present, and future events are all equally real within a four-dimensional block, and it further claims that this undermines the openness of the future and, consequently, ordinary notions of human freedom. The summary concludes that presentists are left with only one escape route—rejecting Lorentz covariance outright and reinstating a Newtonian absolute time—which modern physics rules out, so presentism has no scientifically respectable defense and the metaphysical debate is effectively closed.

The narrative is compelling, but several moves strike me as suspicious: the slide from relativistic simultaneity to determinism, the implication that eternalism necessarily erases contingency or freedom, and the suggestion that presentists have no philosophical resources beyond abandoning relativity theory altogether. Could you audit this characterization for accuracy? In particular, please distinguish which elements genuinely reflect the Rietdijk-Putnam reasoning about special relativity and eternalism, which parts conflate eternalism with determinism or overstate the argument’s metaphysical reach, and whether the claim that presentism survives only through an unscientific, Newtonian revival is a fair portrait of the contemporary landscape. Finally, does the essay’s conclusion—that physics has settled the metaphysics of time in favor of the block universe—actually follow from the corrected premises, or does the argument survive only in a more modest, conditional form?
