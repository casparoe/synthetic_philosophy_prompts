hey i need a runnable classroom thing for my phil of identity seminar — about 12 people, 75 minutes, mostly juniors who know parfit's nonidentity stuff but haven't really *felt* the tension in their bones. i want them to run into it themselves, not hear me explain it.

design a structured role-play/deliberation with a weird twist baked in so it's not just the 14-year-old girl for the millionth time. use a "registry rotation" model: every student gets two cards at the start — an "actual self" card (with a specific life-trait, like chronic fatigue or being an only child) and a "shadow self" card (the person who'd exist if the group had voted differently in round 1). the group makes three sequential policy votes (energy grid delay, migration cap, school funding cut) and after each vote you secretly swap out 2-3 cards according to a hidden mapping rule you prep in advance (no one knows exactly who gets replaced until you announce). by round three about half the room holds cards that wouldn't exist under round one's policies.

rules and mechanics: 15 min setup, then three 10-minute rounds (vote, reveal swaps, score). each card has a quality-of-life score 1-10 and a "dependency flag" showing which round's vote caused this person to exist. after each round, anyone whose score dropped relative to their shadow card can file a grievance — but the grievance form asks "would you prefer never to have existed?" they have to check yes or no; if they check no, the grievance is invalid. anyone whose score went up writes a one-line "gratitude note" to the policy that caused them. keep scores and cards visible on the table so people see the shadow pile grow.

materials: card deck (24 cards, 12 actual/12 shadow), grievance forms, score sheet, hidden mapping chart for you. debrief (20 min): map the grievance stall onto parfit's point — the same policy can't harm you if your existence depends on it, because the comparator isn't you. map the gratitude notes onto the asymmetry — you're thanking a cause that also "harmed" the shadow pile. ask them which felt more real: the grievance or the thanks, and why.

be honest about what this can't reproduce: it doesn't capture the temporal asymmetry of actual reproductive choices (no one is literally choosing a child), and it fudges personal identity over time since the cards are static. you'll have to supply that in the debrief.

also tell me what will predictably go wrong and how to handle it. i expect people will get weirdly attached to their first card and resist the swap — tell them the swap is mechanical, not personal, and let them keep the old card in the shadow pile so they don't feel erased. i expect the gratitude notes will come out sarcastic — lean into it, ask them to read one aloud and discuss whether sarcasm is a defense against feeling indebted to a harmful policy. and i expect the "prefer nonexistence" question will stall the room — have them vote anonymously first with slips of paper, then discuss the spread without forcing anyone to defend their check-box out loud.

no citations or reading lists needed. keep it casual — maybe a typo or two is fine. make sure the idea shows up in the rules and cards, not in a lecture at the end.
