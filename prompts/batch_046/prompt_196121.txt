I am a retired condensed-matter physicist who, in recent years, has turned my attention to philosophy of mind, and I find myself puzzled by the standard interpretation of split-brain cases. Once that arbiter is removed, it seems to me that we do not merely observe a division of labor within one continuous subject, but rather the dissolution of a political union: two distinct centers of awareness that were previously governed by an interhemispheric treaty now operate as separate states within a single cranial territory. If each hemisphere after surgery can maintain its own coherent narrative and preference structure, should we not conclude that personal identity over time is itself a kind of treaty rather than an intrinsic metaphysical unity, and that the philosophical commitment to the indivisibility of consciousness is therefore an artifact of intact neuroanatomy rather than a deep truth about the mind?
