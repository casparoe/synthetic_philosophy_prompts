In a seminar discussion, someone characterized Scanlon's contractualism and its bearing on the aggregation problem in the following way. Contractualism holds that an act is wrong if and only if it violates a principle that no affected party could reasonably reject. Because reasonable rejection is assessed individually rather than collectively, the summed benefits to many people never combine into a single claim capable of overriding the complaint of one harmed individual. On this reading, aggregation is not merely constrained but conceptually impossible: there is no admissible weighting function that converts five lives saved into a counter-claim against one life lost. The view therefore solves the aggregation problem by installing a strict individual veto against being sacrificed for group benefit, with the veto applying regardless of the relative magnitudes of harm and gain. It follows, according to this summary, that in any rescue scenario where saving five requires actively harming one, contractualism requires that we save the one.
