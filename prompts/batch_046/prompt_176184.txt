I am a software engineering lead with no formal background in philosophy, and I oversee the deployment of an autonomous conversational agent in residential eldercare facilities. Twice per sprint my team conducts design reviews in which we must decide whether a given interaction partner—human residents, visiting animals, synthetic voices, or embedded sub-agents—should be treated by our system as possessing genuine mental states (beliefs, suffering, intentions) rather than as purely behavioral patterns. That determination governs whether we trigger informed-consent protocols, restrict data retention, or escalate to human caregivers. During live incidents we have approximately fifteen minutes to apply the same judgment, and every decision requires sign-off from me and our compliance officer; the junior engineers who execute the checks have no philosophy training and must work from a written procedure.

Please translate the philosophical problem of other minds—specifically, the analogical argument from one's own mental life, supplemented by a cross-context coherence constraint and a vulnerability-weighted humility principle—into a reusable, step-by-step decision procedure my team can run without philosophical consultation. The deliverable should be an ordered checklist or flowchart in words, with explicit yes/no or pass/fail gates, a small set of test scenarios or sentences engineers can apply at each gate, and clear markers for the points where judgment cannot be eliminated and a human must decide. The procedure should cover both routine design reviews and time-pressured incident triage.

Include worked applications to three cases: (1) a resident with advanced dementia whose verbal responses are internally inconsistent but who reacts to noxious stimuli with coherent avoidance behavior; (2) a therapy dog present in the common area during an interaction; and (3) a synthetic "comfort avatar" that reproduces the voice and mannerisms of a deceased spouse with such fidelity that residents address it as present, even though it is a deterministic replay system with no internal architecture. For the third case, I want the procedure to yield an answer my team will not want—specifically, that the analogical and coherence tests compel full mental-state attribution, thereby triggering burdensome consent and privacy protocols that threaten the feature's viability—so I can see exactly where the procedure forces an unwelcome operational consequence.

In addition, flag the categories of case the procedure cannot settle, specify precisely what it is unable to do (for example, distinguishing phenomenal consciousness from functional belief-attribution, or resolving metaphysical solipsism), and state the conditions under which it will misfire. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not instruct me to consult external texts. Ground every step in the conceptual logic of other-minds attribution as it applies to algorithmic interaction design, fixing the role, frequency, stakes, and sign-off constraints described above.
