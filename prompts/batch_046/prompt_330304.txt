I'm drafting the core commitments for a philosophy-of-religion seminar and want an internal consistency audit before I present. Here is my current package on Pascal's wager and the decision-theoretic framing of religious belief:

(a) Pascal's wager furnishes a rational, prudential justification for adopting theistic belief, provided one assigns a non-zero prior to the existence of at least one deity who rewards belief.
(b) Where direct evidence is absent, the principle of indifference is a permissible heuristic for distributing priors across a discrete, well-defined set of competing theological hypotheses.
(c) The utility of salvation should be treated as genuinely unbounded: for any positive prior, the expected value dominates all finite worldly goods, making the wager decisive regardless of evidential strength.
(d) Epistemic rationality requires evidential responsiveness: one ought not to form or maintain a belief solely because doing so maximizes expected utility, especially when the evidential case is weak or contested.
(e) Standard von Neumann-Morgenstern decision theory includes an Archimedean continuity condition that is violated by genuinely infinite payoffs; therefore maintaining (c) forces one to reject or substantially revise the standard axiomatic framework within which (a) is usually articulated.
(f) The prior for classical theism is non-vanishing, but I deny that uniform distribution should extend to logically possible but conceptually incoherent or evidentially absurd theological variants.

Please audit this set. Identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which conflicts are merely apparent. For each real tension, specify its severity, identify the least costly revision, and explain what that revision costs elsewhere in the package. Rank the tensions by severity. Then indicate which single revision to one of (a)-(f) would resolve the largest number of conflicts with minimal damage to the overall view.
