Write an essay on advance directives and wether the earlier self really gets to boss around the later one, especially in these messy in-between cases. I'm thinking of someone who signed a directive refusing artifical feeding if they ever got dementia, but now they're in this weird partial state — they don't recognize family, they laugh at jokes they wouldn't have gotten before, they seem content, but they also don't have the same values or memories. The usual debate is about full psychological continuity vs. breaking the chain, but I want you to focus on the fading gradient view where identity is more like a dimmer switch than a light switch. Weigh the arguments: past autonomy, present welfare, the danger of letting strangers override old choices, and whether treating partial continuity as close enough to honor the directive actually respects the person or just respects a ghost. Reach an actual conclusion — should the directive be followed in these partial-transformation cases? — and defend it. Don't just list both sides and bail.
