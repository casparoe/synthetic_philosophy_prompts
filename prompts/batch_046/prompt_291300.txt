Write a short story, no more than 350 words, about a being whose cognition is organized by predictive processing and free-energy minimization, but with an inverted self-model: its strongest prior is not "I exist" but "I am not occurring." In this being, sensations, memories, and intentions do not confirm presence; they are registered as prediction errors that must be suppressed to restore the prior of absence. Agency is therefore experienced as noise, and stillness as the successful reduction of free energy. Ground the story in one concrete scene—perhaps a brief interaction or a single physical act—so that the inversion is shown through detail rather than exposition. Do not explain the theory; let the narrative logic of the character's perception carry the concept.
