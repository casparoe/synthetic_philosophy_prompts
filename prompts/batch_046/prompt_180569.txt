At a seminar on social ontology, two participants debated whether an emergent signaling practice on a decentralized forum qualifies as a convention in David Lewis's sense. (1) The first argued that users have converged on using one specific emoji to signal disagreement, that each prefers to conform when others do, and that within the active core this regularity is common knowledge—so all of Lewis's conditions are satisfied. (2) The second objected that algorithmic curation hides engagement patterns from view, so participants cannot be sure others know the regularity holds, breaking the common-knowledge requirement. (3) The first replied that common knowledge need not be globally transparent or visible to outsiders; it only needs to hold among the interacting subgroup, and algorithmic opacity is an environmental feature rather than an internal epistemic barrier. (4) The second countered that Lewis requires a pre-existing coordination problem with roughly shared preference orderings, whereas here the platform's engagement metrics manufacture the preference for that emoji, making the preference endogenous to the very regularity being explained. (5) The first responded that Lewis's evolutionary account explicitly allows preferences to co-evolve with regularities, so endogenous preference-formation describes the convention's origin rather than invalidating it.
