My opening move is the following constructed case, which I call the Triadic Interface. Imagine a subject—call her Elena—whose left and right cerebral hemispheres are gradually given independent sensory-motor interfaces linking each to a separate body, while a high-bandwidth symbolic bridge remains active between them. This bridge permits continuous shared memory, mutual modeling of mental states, and the joint maintenance of a single recursive first-person narrative. Once the transition is complete, Elena's original body is deactivated; she now exists as two embodied hemispheres, still unified by the bridge, with no privileged central substrate. The symbolic pattern that originally constituted her 'I-loop' is now instantiated across both hemispheres and both bodies. I put to you: is there now one Elena distributed across two brains and bodies, two distinct Elenas each carrying half the original pattern, or has the original Elena been destroyed? Furthermore, if one body is suddenly disconnected and the bridge severed, does the remaining system genuinely retain Elena's self, or does it suffer an amputation of identity rather than a division? Answer directly, in character, and address both the distributive and the severance conditions.
