For my graduate seminar on causation, I want a rigorous specification of what an adequate theory of causation must deliver if it is to organize — and ideally adjudicate — the standing dispute between counterfactual-dependence accounts and interventionist-manipulability accounts. Do not begin with a survey of familiar figures; begin with the criteria themselves.

Write the full desiderata list: the phenomena any such theory must explain; the distinctions it must preserve (between preemption and overdetermination, between token-level and type-level causation, and, crucially, between counterfactual stability and interventionist modularity as two independent dimensions of causal strength rather than as competing definitions of a single relation); the formal results from decision theory or structural equation modeling with which it must remain consistent; and the puzzle cases it must dissolve rather than dismiss by definitional fiat.

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it, and flag whether it is non-negotiable for any theory that genuinely addresses causation or whether it reflects a camp-specific preference (for instance, determinism about causal structure, rejection of probabilistic causation, or insistence on physical-process continuity). Then examine whether a jointly unsatisfiable subset exists: specifically, can a theory simultaneously preserve the asymmetry of counterfactual dependence in symmetric overdetermination cases and satisfy the interventionist requirement that causal relations be invariant under modular interventions? If these demands conflict, state the incompatibility precisely — name the formal or logical tension — because that conflict is the heart of the exercise.

Keep the weight on the criteria, their motivating cases, and their potential incompatibilities. Only briefly indicate how the counterfactual and interventionist programs respectively fare against the non-negotiable core; I will handle the scoring in class. The purpose is a theory adequate not merely for prediction or explanation in general, but specifically for grounding causal judgment in both scientific and ordinary contexts where manipulation and counterfactual reasoning diverge.
