I am a senior policy analyst in a non-partisan fiscal audit office, and I am trying to build an internal review protocol for inheritance-tax exemptions that my junior staff can apply without calling in an outside economist or philosopher. Our jurisdiction is seeing a wave of bills framed as protecting family continuity—farms, small businesses, artistic estates—that in practice shield multi-generational capital pools, and I need a reusable decision procedure, runnable in about thirty minutes on a draft bill, to determine whether a given exemption genuinely serves equal opportunity or instead entrenches dynastic wealth. The procedure should be grounded in the conceptual distinction between capital that blocks others' access to opportunity and capital that merely confers an unearned positional advantage on heirs, with ordered questions, criteria for a sufficient answer at each stage, explicit judgment-call nodes where values must replace algorithm, and a clear stop-rule that flags rejection; it should also be honest about which distinctions must survive simplification and which can be dropped without betraying the underlying principle.

Please construct this procedure and then apply it to two hypothetical proposals: first, a complete exemption for agricultural land valued up to fifty million dollars, with no requirement that heirs actively work the land; second, a sliding-scale rate reduction that increases with each generation since the original accumulation of capital, coupled with a hard sunset after the third generation. Include one worked case where the procedure yields an answer I will not like—specifically, an exemption that genuinely protects a vulnerable middle-class heir while simultaneously sheltering a billionaire dynasty—and explain precisely where the procedure breaks down and what the analyst must do by hand. I do not want bibliographies, citations, or references to specific legal codes; I need the derivation from first principles, with the points of unavoidable judgment marked clearly, so that a staff member without graduate training can still apply it consistently.
