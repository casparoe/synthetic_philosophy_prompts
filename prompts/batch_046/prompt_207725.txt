I've come to think consciousness across animals is best seen as a smooth gradient tied to predictive integration in nervous systems — so there's probably some minimal flicker of experience in lots of invertebrates, and the human-octopus gap is mostly quantitative rather than qualitative. But I want to know what'd actually count against this view. Not just the obvious "plants dont feel" rebuttal, but which empirical results or conceptual snags would really hurt it? Like, if octopus arms turn out to run on purely local reflex loops with zero central integration, does that just shift the gradient point or break the model? Or if some cognitive discontinuity shows up that doesnt track neural complexity at all? And does pushing the gradient far enough land you in panpsychism, and is that fatal or just embarassing? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a committed gradient theorist would have to concede versus which they'd dismiss by tweaking the metric.
