I am a doctoral student in the sociology of medicine and philosophy of science, working on the ontology of psychiatric classification. I want to defend a qualified version of Ian Hacking's looping-effects thesis, but with a specific restriction that I think is often missed in critical-psychiatry literature: looping is not a universal feature of psychiatric taxonomy, but a locally contingent mechanism whose strength depends on how a category is institutionally anchored and phenomenologically available. More precisely, I hold that dynamic nominalism operates strongly—what I call single-loop conditions—only for categories whose criteria are transparent to self-interpretation, socially circulated as narrative resources, and decoupled from bureaucratic gatekeeping functions. By contrast, I propose that many neurodevelopmental and forensic-psychiatric categories are protected by what I term double-loop insulation: first, their diagnostic criteria are anchored in non-narrative performance metrics, such as standardized cognitive-task failures or legal-competence thresholds, that resist incorporation into the subject's self-concept; second, they are embedded in administrative decision procedures that do not require the subject's self-identification with the label to trigger institutional consequences. The taxonomy is therefore stratified, not uniformly loop-sensitive or biologically real, but differentiated by the presence or absence of this insulation. What would genuinely count against this position? I want an honest map of its exposure: which empirical patterns in long-term diagnostic stability, patient self-reporting, or institutional reform would undermine the distinction between single-loop and double-loop conditions; whether my notion of administrative anchoring collapses into a standard social-constructivist account once scrutinized; what conceptual or formal arguments show that performance-based criteria are themselves susceptible to looping through strategic test-taking, institutional gaming, or shifting normative baselines; and whether universal-loop defenders can concede that some categories resist narrative uptake without thereby conceding biological realism, or whether my framework merely relabels an older distinction without adding explanatory power. Please distinguish decisive defeaters from evidence that is merely awkward, say what defenders of the qualified view should themselves concede as damaging, and assess whether the position has been insulated into unfalsifiability—particularly if I can invoke double-loop insulation post hoc for any apparently looping-resistant case. I am not asking for a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations; I want the arguments, predictions, and conceptual pressure points that should move me. Present your response as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a formal letter or sustained essay.
