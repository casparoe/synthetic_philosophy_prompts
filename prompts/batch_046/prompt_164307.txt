I recently encountered a summary of dialetheism—the position most closely associated with Graham Priest—in a general-audience philosophy essay. The author writes, in effect: Dialetheism is essentially paraconsistent logic applied directly to reality. Its central claim is that the law of non-contradiction is false full stop, which means contradictions are rationally acceptable everywhere and never cause logical trouble. To resolve the Liar paradox, Priest assigns the self-referential sentence that asserts its own falsity a special status: it is neither true nor false, falling into a truth-value gap. Paraconsistent logic—specifically Priest’s system LP—makes this safe by blocking the principle that a contradiction entails any conclusion whatsoever, so the gap never makes reasoning explode. From this, the essay concludes that dialetheism is a kind of global logical pluralism: since true contradictions exist in paradoxical cases, classical logic is wrong across the board, and we ought to replace it with LP for all domains of inquiry, including ordinary science and mathematics.
