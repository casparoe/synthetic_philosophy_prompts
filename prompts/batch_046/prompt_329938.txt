I have been thinking about the nonidentity problem in my own reading and now want to lay it out for my neighbour's discussion circle—adults with no formal philosophy—during a single evening. Do not explain the problem itself to me; instead, plan the explanatory arc. I intend to use a concrete case: our town council must choose between approving a large industrial estate, which would attract different families over the next decades and thus alter who meets, partners, and has children, or preserving the farmland so that an entirely different set of people eventually lives here. What idea must be in place before this paradox even makes sense—perhaps the intuition that harming requires making someone worse off—and in what order should the pieces follow? Which feature of this local-planning scenario should carry the explanation, and which details should I deliberately drop so the discussion does not become about zoning rather than identity? I expect three misconceptions: that I am saying future people do not matter, that this applies only to abortion, and that it is solved by gratitude—"they should be glad they exist." Where in the sequence should I anticipate and defuse each? What one question at the end would show me they have grasped that the difficulty lies in the logic of harm rather than in urban policy? Finally, what should I openly set aside at this level—perhaps formal person-affecting principles or the metaphysics of possible worlds?
