Ok so I've been reading about Newton's theological views — the Arianism, the alchemy, the prophecy notebooks — and I keep notcing this pattern in how people discuss them. Whenever Newton takes a position that sounds weird or un-scientific to modern ears, like action at a distance with no mechanical explanation, people quickly say oh well he was deeply religious, he must have imagined God mediating it. But then when you look at parts of his physics that feel totally modern and rigorous, like his commitment to absolute space and time, nobody ever invokes his religion as an explanitory factor. It's like theology gets used as a garbage can for the strange parts but is completely ignored for the parts we now approve of. I've seen this happen with other figures too, but Newton is the clearest case. Is there an established name for this selective use of biographical context? I know about Whig history and presentism, but this feels narrower — it's not just judging past thinkers by modern standards, it's using one slice of their life to excuse only the anomalies. What's the nearest concept, how does this differ, and if there's no exact term, what should we call it?
