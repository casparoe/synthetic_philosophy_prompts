For my junior-year philosophy seminar on language and institutions, I have to defend a position on whether Austin's framework for performative utterances can handle cases where institutional authority is decentralized. The specific controversy is this: when someone issues a performative—say, declaring a forum thread locked or pronouncing a verdict in a peer-led tribunal—they may satisfy all of Austin's felicity conditions individually, yet the act fails because the relevant community collectively withholds recognition, distributing the failure across many people rather than locating it in a single misfire. Some philosophers maintain that this is just an ordinary misfire under a more complex social condition; others contend it requires a distinct category, which we might call distributed performative failure, where authority is present structurally but absent interactionally. I want an essay that weighs whether Austin's original conditions are sufficient for these decentralized institutional settings or whether the phenomenon genuinely demands a new conceptual framework. Reach a clear conclusion and justify it rigorously. Structure your response as exactly five numbered points, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion. The first point should define the problem using a concrete example of a decentralized performative; the second should present the view that Austin's conditions already cover it; the third should present the opposing view that a new category is necessary; the fourth should examine a boundary case that tests the distinction; and the fifth should state your verdict explicitly, defend it against the strongest objection, and indicate what practical difference the answer makes.
