I'm a doctoral student in the sociology of religion and comparative economic history, and I'm designing a reusable audit for analysts at a development research unit. These analysts—mostly trained in economics, with no ethnographic field access—must review country reports that invoke "cultural values" or "religious ethos" as primary causal explanations for macro-level economic divergence (for example, low entrepreneurial density, resistance to wage labour, or rapid uptake of cooperative institutions). They have forty-eight hours per report and must sign off with one of three recommendations: the cultural-causal claim is admissible; it is inadmissible; or it is conditional pending further evidence. I want you to translate Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis, together with his broader methodological warnings about explaining outcomes through values—especially the risks of post-hoc rationalisation, teleological fallacy, and the failure to isolate ideational mechanisms from material conditions—into a step-by-step decision procedure they can actually run. The procedure should take the form of an ordered checklist with explicit stopping rules (where the answer is an automatic "no" or "conditional"), clear documentation requirements, and marked points where judgment cannot be eliminated and must be recorded. It should include a specific test for what I call "counterfactual value stability"—whether the alleged value-system would have generated measurably different economic behaviour under altered material constraints—and it should distinguish values operating as causal selectors from values operating as post-hoc legitimations. Apply the procedure to three hypothetical reports: (a) one attributing persistent low capital accumulation in a post-colonial region to an enduring "non-ascetic consumption ethos" rooted in local religious practice; (b) one arguing that rapid adoption of digital peer-to-peer governance platforms in a Southeast Asian region reflects an "elective affinity" between pre-existing communal reciprocity norms and the technical architecture of decentralised systems; (c) one proposing that secular technology founders in Northern Europe display high venture-capital risk tolerance owing to residual "Protestant-ethic" vocational asceticism, despite complete religious disaffiliation—an explanation I find initially plausible but which rigorous application of the procedure should reject or heavily condition. Include at least one case where the procedure yields a recommendation the report's authors would strongly resist. Conclude by stating precisely what the procedure cannot adjudicate and where it will systematically misfire. Please write your answer in British English.
