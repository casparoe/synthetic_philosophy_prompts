I'm giving a talk next thursday at a politcal philosphy workshop — mostly analytic philosophers of action, two historians of ideas who know Berlin and Pettit cold, and a libertarian economist who thinks republican freedom is just negative liberty with fancy dress. My thesis: we should reconceive republican liberty diachronically, not as a static status at a single moment but as a property of an agent's deliberative trajectory — domination isn't only the capacity to block an action, but the capacity to arbitrarily prune or redirect branches of reasoning the agent hasn't reached yet, borrowing structural ideas from dynamic choice theory but applying them to conditions of freedom rather than to utility. I sketch it as modeling reasoning like a tree of paths, where arbitrary power operates upstream on the trajectory itself.
