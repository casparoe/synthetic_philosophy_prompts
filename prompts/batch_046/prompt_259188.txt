Rank the following five responses to Sen's liberal paradox on a scale of 1 to 10 according to how well each preserves both the minimal liberal principle and the weak Pareto principle, and provide a one-sentence justification for each score: (A) restricting liberal rights to choices that generate no external effects; (B) allowing liberal rights to operate as lexicographic constraints that always override unanimous preference improvements; (C) reinterpreting minimal liberalism as a condition on binary social-choice rules rather than on aggregate social outcomes; (D) abandoning the underlying welfarist framework and substituting a capabilities-based metric of individual freedom; and (E) treating the conflict not as a logical contradiction but as an empirically negligible phenomenon that vanishes once preference domains are sufficiently rich. After assigning scores, write a single paragraph identifying which strategy comes closest to saving Sen's original intuition—that there should exist protected spheres of individual choice—while stating whether the theoretical cost of that saving undermines the very liberal commitment it aims to protect.
