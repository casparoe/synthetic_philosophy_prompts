Construct a reusable decision procedure for a clinical ethics consultant who must advise, within 48 hours and without command authority, on whether to withdraw life-sustaining treatment when the care team is divided among deontological, consequentialist, and relational-care frameworks, and when the consultant is genuinely morally uncertain—not only about which first-order framework is correct, but also about whether uncertainty should be resolved by weighing outcomes across theories by credence or by deferring to the single theory they find most credible. The deliverable must be an ordered sequence of tests and decision nodes in plain language that a hospital ethics board of mixed expertise can apply and sign off on; specify how to check whether outcomes are intertheoretically comparable, how to assign and revise credences under time pressure, and exactly where irreducible judgment remains. Apply the procedure to three cases: (i) one where it recommends withdrawal that the family rejects on religious grounds; (ii) one where two frameworks rank the available options in precisely opposite order with no shared metric; and (iii) one where the consultant's meta-level uncertainty—about whether to use credence-weighting at all—reverses the recommendation. For each case, show the procedural output and identify the specific node where misfire is most likely, stating what kind of error that misfire produces.
