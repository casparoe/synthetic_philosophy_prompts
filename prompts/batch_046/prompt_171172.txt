I'm giving the opening remarks at the retirement ceremony for my former high school logic teacher, who taught for thirty-five years. The audience is split between educators and former students who know her classroom work, and family members with no background in philosophy or mathematics. She was well known for using Hofstadter's MU-puzzle to illustrate that a formal system contains questions it cannot resolve with its own rules, and she often spoke about jumping out of the system as the only genuine source of change. I want to honor her, but I want the idea to do real structural work: it should constrain what I can honestly claim about whether she transformed the school from within, since if real change requires stepping outside the institution's own rules, I should not pretend she did it alone as an insider. I also want to include a concrete episode she told us about—once she resolved a scheduling deadlock by deliberately violating the district's allocation algorithm, which she described as her own small attempt to step outside and produce the answer the system forbade. Please write remarks that treat the MU-puzzle and the idea of jumping out not as metaphors but as a serious framework for her legacy, that comfort the family without turning into a lecture, and that avoid claiming she reformed the bureaucracy from inside if the logic of the system says that is impossible.
