hey, so my grandma died two weeks ago and ive been going in circles about whether her death was actually bad *for her* or just awful for us. i want to build a decision tree / sorting quiz for my study group that sorts people by how they answer concrete cases, not by which philosophy label they like.

start with three verdict cases:
1. a 90-year-old dies peacefully in sleep right after finishing her memoir and saying goodbye to everyone. bad for her?
2. a healthy 25-year-old is killed instantly, had no unfinished projects but was genuinely excited about next year. bad for her?
3. someone is cryogenically frozen and never revived—no clear endpoint, just permanent suspension. is that harm comparable to death, or different?

then branch on these questions (phrased so you can answer without knowing jargon):
- does harm need a living subject at the time it happens? (the no-subject problem)
- is the badness about missing specific future goods, or about the *interruption of an active life-project*? lets call the second one the "closure gap"—bad only when a project gets cut off before it reaches its own endpoint
- are prenatal non-existence and posthumous non-existence symetrical?

i want 6-8 leaves including: straight epicurean (death never harms the dead), pure deprivation (bad in proportion to goods lost), closure-gap view, desire-satisfaction variant (bad if it frustrates a present desire), the "no subject so no harm but family grief is real" position, and at least one empty slot where the answer combo seems incoherent or just unexplored. each leaf should have a one-sentence awkward commitment.

please also flag any pair of leaves your tree cant actually separate.

response format:
1. The decision tree (branch questions and leaf descriptions) — about 350 words
2. Tracing guide: how to map answers onto leaves — about 120 words
3. Empty / unexplored combos and why theyre missing — about 100 words
4. Pairs that collapse or that the tree fails to distinguish — about 80 words

write it casual, dont cite anyone, and make sure every branch is answerable by someone who hasnt read the literature.
