To complicate matters further, I have learned that the original foundry mould was retained and used in 1987 to cast an authorised duplicate, now held in a private collection. I wish to practise defending the view that artistic identity is fixed by constitutional relation rather than material continuity, against a sharp interlocutor.

Please play a committed material-continuity theorist — sharp, not a strawman, willing to concede minor points about restoration practice but never conceding that the statue and its constituent matter are distinct entities, or that artistic identity survives near-total material replacement. Stay in character until I say 'stop'. Keep every reply to a single paragraph of no more than 100 words. Here is my opening challenge: the repaired maquette retains almost none of the original bronze, yet preserves the precise causal-historical trajectory of my grandfather’s artistic intention, modified only by necessary restoration. By contrast, the 1987 duplicate possesses identical alloy composition and mould origin, but lacks any connection to my grandfather’s hand. If material continuity is your criterion, you must identify the duplicate — not the repaired original — as the true work.
