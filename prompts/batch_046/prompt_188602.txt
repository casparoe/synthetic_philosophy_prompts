I run a philosophy-of-rationality seminar with 14 graduate and advanced-undergraduate students, one 90-minute session, using only paper and pens. Design a paired in-class game that makes the backward-induction pressure in the centipede game felt in real time rather than grasped as an abstract argument. Structure it as a 6-round alternating-move decision game: player A chooses in round 1, player B in round 2, alternating through round 6, where B makes the final decision. Include an exact round-by-round payoff table with these properties: the cooperative pool doubles each round; at every node, taking gives the decider a strictly greater payoff than passing and then facing optimal play in the remainder; and mutual passage all the way to round 6 yields both players a reward that far exceeds what either would receive by taking in round 1. The central twist: before each round, the pair must complete an “epistemic audit card” together, marking whether they currently possess (i) private belief, (ii) mutual knowledge, or (iii) common knowledge that both are rational through every remaining round; these cards are placed face-up in a visible row before the choice is made. Arrange the rules and the visible card history so that as explicit higher-order knowledge accumulates, the logical pressure to defect immediately intensifies, even though the underlying payoff matrix never changes. Specify all materials, a facilitator script for introducing rules without naming the paradox or using terms like “backward induction,” and a choice-tracking sheet. The debrief should map each audit-card stage onto the corresponding level of strategic reasoning, identify the precise in-game moment that mirrors the “unraveling” step, and be explicit about which feature of the infinite regress the cards can only simulate and that you must supply verbally. Predict two predictable failures—e.g., pairs treating the cards as performative promises rather than epistemic descriptions, or refusing to defect in round 1 despite having filled out common-knowledge cards—and say how to recover from each without breaking the exercise’s internal logic. Finally, state frankly what this activity cannot reproduce about actual strategic reasoning and how you will fill that gap in the closing discussion. Maximum 600 words.
