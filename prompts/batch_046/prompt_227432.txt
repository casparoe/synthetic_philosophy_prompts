I teach an upper-level seminar on collective rationality, about 14 grad students, one 75-min session. I want a runnable in-class simulation that makes them actually feel the discursive dilemma in judgment aggregation—not just vote on premises vs conclusions and watch them diverge, but run into it through hidden rules and real (small) stakes.

Here is the niche twist: instead of binary yes/no votes, each student gets private "interval-valued" judgments (ranges like 0.3–0.8 confidence) on three interpretive premises about a made-up philosophical argument I’ll provide. They have to aggregate into a single group ranking of two competing conclusions using either premise-first or conclusion-first aggregation, but they don’t know which rule is active until round three. There shuld be roles: "premise auditors" check consistency, "conclusion defenders" argue for one ranking, "rule observers" can see the hidden aggregation mechanism but are forbidden from speaking. The stakes are small but real—maybe the winning interpretation gets "published" in our mock jounal and the group gets coffee vouchures, but defenders of the losing interpretation have to present its flaws next week.

I need: clear rules and timeline for all three rounds, the payoff/incentive scheme, a debrief that maps each round onto the formal distinction between premise-based and conclusion-based aggregation, and an honest note on which feature of the discursive dilemma this can’t reproduce (probably the democratic norm intuition). Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to handle it. No lecture up front—participants shouldn’t hear "discursive dilemma" named until after round three. Make it feel like they discovered the divergence themselves.
