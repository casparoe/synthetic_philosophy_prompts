Having spent three decades in experimental physics before taking up philosophy in retirement, I find myself repeatedly returning to a disagreement I first encountered in Weinberg's essays and later saw echoed by Hawking and Feynman: the claim that philosophy, and especially philosophy of science, offers no practical assistance to working scientists. Weinberg's particular complaint is that philosophical reflection on quantum mechanics has not moved the physics forward; others extend this to a general suspicion that philosophers are commenting on science from the outside rather than contributing to it. Against this, philosophers often reply that science is already full of conceptual labor—clarifying terms, resolving ambiguities in formalism, distinguishing empirical from interpretive claims—that is philosophical in nature even when scientists perform it without recognizing it as such.

I want to move past this general standoff by comparing two precise conceptions of what philosophical utility might consist in: (a) local conceptual troubleshooting, in which analysis addresses specific ontological or interpretive puzzles arising within a particular theory, and (b) global methodological guidance, in which philosophy purports to prescribe, correct, or organize the methods of science across domains. Compare these two conceptions with explicit attention to the critics' objections and the defenders' actual commitments: do Weinberg-style critics reject both, or only one? Do defenders genuinely claim both, or do they primarily defend the local variety?

Then apply this distinction to the historical reconceptualization of quantum entanglement within quantum information theory—specifically, the shift from treating entanglement as an interpretive paradox in the EPR tradition to treating it as a quantifiable resource for computation and communication. Was the work required for that shift primarily mathematical formalization, empirical discovery, or philosophical clarification in the local sense? Would Weinberg's dismissal apply here with equal force, or does this case present a counterexample? Identify which of the two conceptions of philosophical utility, if any, is vindicated, and specify what feature of the case makes it decisive.
