Rank the following four proposed mechanisms into tiers S, A, B, and C by how severely they corrupt the non-market norms they touch, with S denoting the deepest corruption: (1) a government-monopsony kidney market with price floors and lifelong donor-health subsidies; (2) state-authorized surge pricing for insulin and bottled water during declared emergencies, statutorily capped at 400 percent of baseline; (3) a blockchain-enabled peer-to-peer system allowing citizens to lease, but not sell, their votes on municipal zoning decisions in exchange for verified carbon-offset credits; and (4) a moral-offset scheme that grants wealthy patients expedited organ-transplant priority in return for surcharges that fully fund universal vaccination campaigns. For each tier, justify the placement in one sentence by identifying whether the primary damage is to civic equality, bodily integrity, or social solidarity, and by assessing whether the built-in compensatory mechanism genuinely repairs the violated norm or merely monetizes its absence.
