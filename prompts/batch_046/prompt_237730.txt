I want to argue zombie conceivability is structurally the same: we can imagine a physical duplicate without consciousness because our mental models work locally, not because there's a real modal gap. - Chalmers claims clear and distinct conceivability of zombies implies possibility.
- But if consciousness is globally constrained (like entropy), then conceiving its absence in a local duplicate is exactly what you'd expect from a limited cognitive architecture.
- So the zombie intuition might be an epistemic failure, not a metaphysical discovery.

My question: does this analogy actually pull weight, or am I just sneaking physicalism in through the back door by treating consciousness like a thermodynamic variable?
