He lays out this regress: if A stands in relation R to B, then R itself has to stand in some further relation to A and B, and then you need relations for those relations, ad infinitum. So relations can't be real on their own and have to be abosrbed into some larger whole, eventually the Absolute. But then he talks about judgements or propositions, saying that when you judge that something is some way, the result isn't just the subject plus the predicate plus a seperate relating link; it's a unified whole. I don't see how that escapes the same problem. Is he treating the connection inside judgement as internal to the mental act, not a relation in the same sense? Or is he carving out an exception? I also keep wondering if adverbial theories of events are a decent analogy - where there's no seperate relation-entity, just the subject being modified - does that capture Bradley's move, or does it miss the point because he ultimately wants everything dissolved into one absolute?
