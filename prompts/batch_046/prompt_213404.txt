Write a proposal for a one-semester philosophy-of-physics research paper, approximately 6,000 words, addressing this precise question: does Bell’s theorem refute local causality as such, or does it refute only local hidden-variable models that assume a single, globally assigned hidden state describing both particles? Specifically, examine whether a frame-relative or context-dependent assignment of hidden-variable properties—where the state description of one particle is defined relative to the measurement context of its partner rather than absolutely—can reproduce quantum correlations without violating a suitably reformulated locality condition, and determine exactly where in Bell’s derivation such a move is either blocked or succeeds.

Your proposal should include: (1) a sharply formulated statement of the question that has a determinate yes-or-no (or clearly structured) answer; (2) an explanation of why the standard debate between Bell-local hidden-variable defenders and orthodox quantum interpretations leaves this particular issue open, including why treating the factorization condition as purely methodological rather than physical fails to settle it; (3) your method—how you will dissect the factorization and parameter-independence conditions in Bell’s proof, what formal modifications a context-relative hidden-state assignment introduces, and why you will proceed by direct logical analysis of the derivation rather than by surveying empirical tests or historical interpretations; (4) the two or three possible conclusions, together with the specific formal or conceptual considerations that would decide between them (for instance, whether the relational assignment forces a hidden dependency on distant settings or collapses into superdeterminism); (5) an honest scope assessment for one semester, identifying the part of the proof you will focus on and what you will deliberately set aside; and (6) a description of what a successful deliverable looks like—such as a clear argument identifying the exact step at which the frame-relative move fails or succeeds—and what would count as failure, such as demonstrating that the proposed framework either reproduces standard nonlocality under a different name or requires abandoning statistical independence in a way that is not locally explicable. Do not request a bibliography, reading list, or citation review; refer to well-known positions and arguments by name where needed, and note that references will be added separately.
