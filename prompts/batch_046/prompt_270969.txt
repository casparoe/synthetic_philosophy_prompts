I'm preparing remarks for a workshop on intertemporal ethics and want an internal consistency audit before presenting. I hold the following commitments:

(1) Pure time preference is rationally permissible at the individual level: an agent may weight her own present welfare more heavily solely in virtue of its presentness, without failing practical rationality, so long as she respects temporal consistency conditions.

(2) The non-identity problem shows that climate-policy choices do not harm specific distant-future individuals, because altering emissions trajectories changes which particular people eventually exist.

(3) There is no metaphysically deep fact of personal identity beyond psychological and bodily continuity; over long intervals, the relation between my present stage and my distant future stage is more analogous to that between close relatives than to strict numerical identity.

(4) Intergenerational justice requires that social institutions treat temporal location as morally irrelevant: the distributive principles governing public goods and burdens across generations should not discount future welfare merely because it is future.

(5) For long-term climate cost-benefit analysis, a near-zero but strictly positive social discount rate is appropriate, partly to accommodate democratic preference aggregation rather than to assert that future lives have lower intrinsic value.

Please audit this set: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear incompatible, and what is the least costly revision to restore full consistency? Rank the tensions by severity, indicating for each whether resolution requires giving up a substantive moral claim, weakening a metaphysical thesis, or revising an institutional prescription. Do not provide a bibliography or reading list; I am asking for a coherence check, not a survey of the literature.
