As a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, I have been trying to understand why the debate over Strawson’s reactive attitudes and moral responsibility has stalled for so long. On one side, compatibilists argue that resentment, gratitude, and blame remain fully justified even if determinism is true, because these attitudes serve essential social functions; on the other side, incompatibilists insist that genuine moral responsibility requires a kind of authorship or control that determinism cannot provide, so that reactive attitudes are fundamentally misplaced in a determined world. Both sides seem to agree on the psychological facts about how people actually respond to wrongdoing, and both acknowledge the possibility of determinism, yet after decades of exchange the positions have not moved—the same examples are recycled, the same distinctions are drawn, and no experiment or formal result appears to settle anything. My working hypothesis, shaped by thinking about fault-tolerance in distributed systems, is that this may not be a hidden empirical disagreement at all, but a structural misalignment: one side treats blame as a retrospective judgment that traces failure to a specific origin (like identifying which commit introduced a bug), while the other treats it as a prospective calibration signal that regulates future behavior regardless of origin (like adjusting a load-balancer after an error). If that is right, the disputants may be answering different questions—one about the semantics of desert, one about the pragmatics of social coordination—under the single heading of moral responsibility. I want a diagnosis supported by what each side actually says about concrete cases of determined wrongdoing: is this a verbal dispute about the word “desert,” a substantive disagreement about what counts as a moral reason, a clash of bedrock intuitions about naturalness that neither side can argue for, or an ill-posed question that conflates retrospective and prospective functions? And given your diagnosis, what would count as genuine progress—a conceptual distinction separating origin-tracing from behavior-regulating roles, a formal model of responsibility attribution, a change in the question being asked, or is the impasse permanent and why?
