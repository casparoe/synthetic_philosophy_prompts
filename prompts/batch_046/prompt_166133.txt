Here's an argument my partener cooked up about Darwin and I think it proves way too much but I cant find the exit. (1) Darwin argued that morality isn't divine command but comes from social instincts shaped by natural selection — especially the instinct to care about group opinion and sympathy. (2) He also treated religious belief as an evolved byproduct of those same social instincts plus our tendency to see agency everywhere. (3) If both morality and religion are just evolutionary survival tools, then neither one tracks objective truth; they're adaptive fiction. (4) But Darwin's own evolutionary theory was also produced by an evolved human brain trying to survive and reproduce. (5) So if evolutionary origin always kills truth-tracking, then Darwin's theory is itself unreliable. (6) Therefore evolutionary naturalism is self-defeating and we should reject Darwin's account of both morality and religion. I really want to keep (1) and (2) — I think he was basically right about origins — but (6) feels like a disaster. Which step should I reject? And what does giving it up cost? Is there a non-arbitrary way to say evolved cognition works for science but fails for morality and religion?
