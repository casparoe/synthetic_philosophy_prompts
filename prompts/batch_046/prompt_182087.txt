She thinks the whole point of the lexical line is that there's a quantitative breaking point, and if pleasure and pain aren't measurable on the same axis you can't locate that break. Jonas said that's overreading the quantifcation — he thinks the threshold is qualitative, like a kind of "category switch" in moral urgency, so you don't need to weigh micro-pleasures against macro-pains at all. Maya pushed back that this just hides the comparison problem inside the category boundary, because deciding that existential dread counts but mild physical discomfort doesn't still requires comparing them to place them in different buckets, and that comparison is exactly the commensurability issue. Jonas then brought up this idea he called "modular incommensurability" — that suffering might have internal cardinal scales within domains (physical, social, cognitive) but no cross-domain exchange rate, so LTNU applies modular thresholds instead of one global line. Maya said that's clever but it makes the view either implausibly pluralist or secretly smuggling in a meta-comparison to decide which module gets lexical prioraty.
