I spent three decades in experimental condensed-matter physics before turning, in retirement, to philosophy of science, and I find myself dissatisfied with the standard presentations of verisimilitude. Rather than asking me which philosopher I agree with, I want a diagnostic taxonomy—structured as a decision tree—that sorts positions on how one false scientific theory can be closer to the truth than another. The instrument should begin with concrete scientific comparisons, such as whether the Bohr-Sommerfeld atomic model stands closer to modern quantum mechanics than Rutherford's nuclear model does, and whether that proximity should be measured by predictive success, partial ontological correspondence, or derivability as a limiting case. Each branching node must be phrased as a substantive commitment—for example, whether quantitative distance metrics are coherent for theories with different ontologies, whether comparative judgments can stand without such metrics, whether structural preservation alone carries the weight of approximation, or whether there is simply no fact of the matter about truth-proximity—so that a respondent can navigate it without knowing the labels in advance. The leaves should name or describe the corresponding positions: a quantitative metric account, a qualitative comparative account in the Popper-Miller tradition, a structural-realist view that privileges retained mathematical form over ontological content, and a deflationary denial that false theories bear determinable proximity relations to the truth. You should also include at least one logically possible combination of answers that no named position occupies, together with a brief diagnosis—incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect—of why the space is empty there, and you must flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate. To keep the instrument from being a mere encyclopedia recitation, inject one non-standard distinction of your own: specifically, whether assessing verisimilitude requires an explicit reference framework of background laws taken as true, or whether such judgments can be conducted locally within the conceptual scheme of the false theories themselves. Write this as a formal, polished request suitable for a retired scientist who wishes to understand where he stands on the problem without relying on bibliographic authority.
