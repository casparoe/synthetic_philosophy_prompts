In my advanced environmental studies seminar we have spent the past year tracking the debate over whether to formalize the Anthropocene as an official geological epoch and, if so, who possesses the authority to fix its start — stratigraphers through the International Commission on Stratigraphy, historians and archaeologists through socio-economic turning points, or some hybrid interdisciplinary body. The disagreement now appears stalled in a peculiar way: the Anthropocene Working Group advocates a mid-twentieth-century boundary anchored in plutonium and carbon-isotope spikes, proposing a Global Stratotype Section and Point at Crawford Lake, while critics advance the 1610 Columbian Exchange marker or the 1784 steam-engine transition, emphasizing that human earth-system impacts are diachronic and geographically uneven. Yet both sides largely concede the empirical profiles — they agree that radionuclide levels spiked, that nitrogen and phosphorus cycles were disrupted, that biodiversity loss accelerated — and still neither camp has shifted its position. My working hypothesis is that the dispute is not genuinely empirical but structural: perhaps it conceals a disagreement about whether stratigraphic classification should record physical signals alone or must also be causally transparent to the mechanism of human agency; perhaps it is a verbal confusion between 'epoch' as a formal chronostratigraphic unit and 'Anthropocene' as a historical-political diagnosis; or perhaps it rests on a bedrock intuition about whether human activity can ever be conceptually separated from 'natural' geological processes. I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict: is there a hidden formal or normative question doing the work, are the sides answering different questions under one name, or is the question of when it started and who decides itself ill-posed? And given your diagnosis, what would actually move the debate — a new conceptual distinction about the purpose of geological boundaries, a revision in the standards for ratifying Global Stratotype Sections and Points, an explicit acknowledgment that the authority question is political rather than scientific, or is this the kind of impasse where further argument should not be expected?
