I am preparing a position paper for a philosophy of physics seminar on spacetime ontology, and I would like an internal consistency check before I present it. My current package of commitments is as follows. First, I am a substantivalist: I hold that the spacetime manifold exists as an entity in its own right, and that its points possess primitive identity—haecceity—independent of the fields or relations that happen to be defined upon them. Second, I grant that Einstein's hole argument succeeds on its own terms: if active diffeomorphisms are interpreted as producing physically distinct models, then determinism fails in general relativity. Third, I resolve this threat by adopting the point-coincidence view, according to which diffeomorphism-related metrics represent exactly the same physical situation, so that determinism is preserved once we reject the naive identification of coordinate labels with physical states. Fourth, I insist that this resolution does not collapse into relationalism, because the manifold remains an independent substrate that bears the metric and matter fields, even though its points are not individually distinguishable in the absence of those fields. Fifth, I believe that if a future theory of quantum gravity breaks diffeomorphism invariance—perhaps by introducing discrete or background structure—then the hole argument revives in modified form, and this should be read not as a paradox but as an ontological clue that primitive point-identity must then be fully restored.

Please audit this set for joint tenability. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision for each real problem? In particular, is there a contradiction between attributing primitive identity to points and simultaneously treating them as interchangeable under the diffeomorphism equivalence endorsed in my third commitment? Does my claim that the manifold is an independent substrate sit comfortably with my denial that its points are distinguishable without fields? And is my fifth commitment about quantum gravity consistent with the resolution I offer for classical general relativity, or does it implicitly concede that primitive identity was never genuinely available in the classical case? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each indicate whether giving up the ontological claim, the interpretive claim about the hole argument, or the quantum-gravity speculation would be the cheapest fix.

Please separate what is well settled in the literature—such as the formal structure of the hole argument and the standard relationalist or sophisticated-substantivalist replies—from what remains genuinely contested, such as whether primitive haecceity can be coherently combined with diffeomorphism equivalence, and do not present any contested interpretive claim as though it were the consensus view.
