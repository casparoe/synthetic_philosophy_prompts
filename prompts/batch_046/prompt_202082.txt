The audience is split between researchers in consciousness science and family members, students, and colleagues from other fields who have no technical background. The philosophical idea must do real structural work in the speech rather than serve as decoration. Specifically, I want the remarks built around the exclusion postulate—the principle that within any given substrate only the complex with the maximally irreducible value of integrated information (Φ) counts as conscious, thereby excluding overlapping subsystems of lower Φ even though the physical parts remain present. I want this used analogically, not literally: to frame how scientific value operates at the level of irreducible collective integration rather than being distributed evenly across individual neurons or individual researchers. The speech must therefore avoid both mystical language about “emergence” and reductive talk about “just neurons,” and it must not claim that laboratories literally possess Φ. Before drafting, please think carefully, step by step, about how the logic of competitive exclusion among nested substrates can illuminate collaboration and legacy without misrepresenting the neuroscience. Then, acting as a professional philosopher of science with deep familiarity with consciousness studies, write the remarks. They must begin immediately with the remarks themselves—no preface, no commentary, and no restatement of these instructions. The first sentence should state the bottom line: that Dr. Vasquez’s career demonstrates that the properties we care about most, in both mind and in scientific labor, share a single architecture—irreducible integration across difference. Then unfold the reasoning in formal, precise language accessible to non-specialists but rigorous enough that experts will not wince. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific papers by title or author; do not invent specific theorems, experiments, or biographical discoveries attributed to her; and stay at the level of conceptual structure rather than technical detail. Include one brief original metaphor, drawn from the problem of nested substrates competing for maximal Φ, to illustrate the tension between honoring individual contribution and honoring the collective achievement.
