Write an essay on whether the shift to agriculture was actually a net loss for human well-being, but don't just trot out the usual Diamond-vs-Sahlins routine—everyones seen that a thousand times. I want you to work with this specific framing: call it the "attention-economy" view of the Neolithic. The idea is that forager societies distributed attention broadly across ecosystems, social networks, and tool-making, whereas agriculture forced a narrow allocation toward crop cycles and storage defense. The question is whether that narrowing was an unavoidable consequence of population density, or whether alternative agricultural forms—say, dispersed horticulture with communal storage—could have kept the broad allocation. Take a stand: was agriculture a mistake because it locked in this attentional bottleneck, or is the bottleneck overstated? Defend your answer conceptually, not with a bibliography.
