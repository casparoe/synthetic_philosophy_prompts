Analyze the 1755 Lisbon earthquake and the immediate political-theological responses—particularly the Marquis of Pombal's secular reconstruction measures versus the Inquisition's providential explanations—through the lens of Leibniz's account of justice as caritas sapientis and his taxonomy of metaphysical, physical, and moral evil. How does the theory determine whether the destruction counts as an injustice that demands redress or merely as physical misfortune permitted for greater harmony? What moral duties does it assign to rulers and survivors, and does it grant standing to non-rational victims? Map precisely where the framework illuminates the episode—say, the rational ordering of post-disaster governance as an expression of wise charity—and where it is silent or strained, especially regarding the absence of a responsible agent and the scale of apparently gratuitous suffering.
