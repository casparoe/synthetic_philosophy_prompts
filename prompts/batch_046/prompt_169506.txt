That argument was tied to a specific cosmological model that is now observationally disfavored; measurements of cosmic microwave background isotropy place extremely tight constraints on large-scale rotation. At the same time, rotating black hole solutions—the Kerr metric—still contain regions near the ring singularity where closed timelike curves are mathematically present, and the prevailing ΛCDM cosmology predicts an accelerating expansion that will eventually produce a de Sitter horizon, permanently isolating distant observers.

Might he have looked for an alternative global topology that preserves closed timelike curves without violating isotropy? And how would he have interpreted the de Sitter horizon—would he see an eternal, accelerating future with an event boundary as restoring a kind of objective temporal ordering, or as introducing yet another conventional boundary that shows time remains relative to the observer's causal patch?
