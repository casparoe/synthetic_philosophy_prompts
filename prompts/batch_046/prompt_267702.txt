I run a grad-student philosophy workshop — about 10 people, 75 minuets. I want them to actually feel anekāntavāda, not just read about it. The core idea is that every claim is only true from some naya (standpoint) and false or unstateable from others, so saying it IS X without qualification is already a mistake.

Design a structured role-play with rules, roles, and a small-stakes payoff scheme (points, not cash). Scenario: a town council must decide whether to demolish an old well that is both the only water source and a sacred site for a local tradition. Give each participant a standpoint card that restricts how they can speak — e.g., physical-properties-only, historical-meaning-only, future-utility-only, or they must prefix claims with conditional syādvāda-style language and are forbidden absolute negation. They have 20 minutes to reach a collective decision or everyone loses points.

Make sure the rules force them to talk past each other and then notice that partial agreement is only possible if they drop absolute claims. Include materials, the payoff structure, and a timeline.

Then a debrief that maps the momments onto syādvāda-style conditional predication and the refusal of onesidedness. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time — probably people ditching thier cards when frustrated — and how to handle it. Also be honest about which part of Jain thought this can't reproduce — probably the spiritual-liberation goal the logic is meant to serve — and what I have to supply verbally.

Keep it casual. No lecture up front; they shouldn't hear the word anekāntavāda until afterwards.
