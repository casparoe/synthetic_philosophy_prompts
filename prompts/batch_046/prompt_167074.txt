hey so i need this for a dataset im building but also i genuinely got stuck on it. last week at a bar near the residency i was talking with two other novelists, maya and derek, about whether you can write a first-person narrator without accidentally building them a cartesian theater. heres roughly how it went.

(1) Maya said every time you write 'i saw the red cup' youre installing a single screen inside the characters head where all the sensory drafts come together and play. she thinks thats unavoidable if you want the reader to feel like theres a someone in there.

(2) Derek said thats exactly Dennetts point -- there is no theater in the brain, just multiple drafts being edited, and if you write a unified stream youre basically lying about consciousness. he said fiction should show the contradictions, the revisions, the moments where two incompatible drafts exist at once.

(3) Maya came back with okay but Dennetts talking about neural processing, not about what it feels like to be someone. the unified stream is the phenomenology. if you write only fragments youre not describing human experience, youre describing a brain scan. she said the theater is bad neuroscience but a good phenomonology.

(4) Derek said thats a cop-out. he thinks if you keep writing the theater youre reinforcing a false ontology -- readers walk away thinking theres literally a place in the head where it all comes together. he wants novels to do the opposite: show that theres no audience in the head, just edits happening without a watcher.

(5) Maya said but then empathy breaks. without a center of gravity, the character becomes a weather system. she mentioned something she calls narrative focal gravity -- the pull that makes a reader feel theres one consciousness behind the words, even when the text is fragmentary. she thinks thats what actually creates character, not the neuroscience accuracy.

please adjudicate move by move: rate each from clearly wrong to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. also, is there a way to write a protagonist who has no cartesian theater but still has focal gravity? and what are both of them missing about how readers actually construct a self from text? i keep going back and forth on that last part.
