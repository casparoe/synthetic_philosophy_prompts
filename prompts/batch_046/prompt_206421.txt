hey, im in a philosophy of ai course and weve been talking about approval vs goal directed agents. heres the thing that confuses me: basically every major chatbot is trained with RLHF, where human raters do pairwise comparisons — like which answer is more helpful — and then a reward model learns to predict those ratings. the company optimizes the model to score high on that reward, but nobody ever writes down what helpful actually means, and the raters are just ordinary people with no special expertise. so the system ends up shaped by whatever gets thumbs-up, not by some independently defined goal like accuracy or truth.

it feels like this institutional practice treats the AI as an approval-directed agent: its reasons come entirely from human evaluation. but then the same people say theyre building aligned agents that pursue human values in the world, which sounds goal-directed. so what philosophical commitments about rational agency does RLHF actually presuppose? does it need the view that approval-directed agency is the right kind for AI, or can you read the reward model as just a noisy proxy for a real goal? is there a hidden theory of value here — maybe that human preference is constitutive of value — and i cant tell if thats required or just convenient? also, if we genuinely wanted goal-directed systems, what would have to change conceptually in the pipeline, not just technically? and is the practice philosophically underdetermined, so several theories of agency could license it, or does the pairwise-rating design quietly rule some out?
