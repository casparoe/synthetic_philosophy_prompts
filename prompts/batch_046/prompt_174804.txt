I've been getting into the quining-qualia thing lately — the idea that talking about raw feels is incoherrent cuz you either end up describing their functional role or you're just pointing at some private ghost in the machine. I want to push a stronger version: it's not just that qualia are redundent, but the concept itself is unstable whenever you try to make it do actual explanatory work. So my position is that there's no consistent property we can call 'qualia' that is both non-physical (in the sense of being irreducible to information-processing structure) and causally relevant enough to explain why we even talk about consciousness.

If I'm wrong, what actually counts against it? Don't just hit me with 'but I know I'm conscious' — I want the defeaters that someone commited to this view should take seriously. Like, could there be a formal result showing that any system with a certain self-model necessarily generates an irreducible qualitative report? Or some empirical finding in neuroscience or AI where internal representations have a feature that acts like a qualitative marker but can't be mapped onto function? And what about the middle ground where qualia exist but are just epiphenomenal tags — does that defeat my view or actually help it?

Also, I'm worried there's a hidden circularity: when I say any description of a quale is really just describing its functional correlations, am I just assuming physicalism? What would have to be true for that charge to stick, and is there a version of quining that survives?
