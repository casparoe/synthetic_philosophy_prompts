hey so i've been trying to figure out what descartes' "i think therefore i am" actually proves, and i built this little chain that feels like it proves way too much. i can't find the exit.

1. the cogito works because if i'm doubting or thinking, there has to be a thinker. you can't have the activity without the agent.
2. but thinking is never empty - it's always about something. right now i'm thinking about whether my cat is hungry, and the content is "my cat is hungry."
3. so in every thought there are two parts: me the thinker, and the thing being thought about.
4. descartes says i can be 100% sure of the thinker, because doubting my own existance is self-defeating.
5. but i'm also directly aware of the content, right? i know exactly what i'm thinking about. if i can be wrong about my cat actually being hungry, can i be wrong about the fact that i'm thinking about her?
6. it seems like the content is right there in my mind, just as present as the thinking itself. so if the cogito blocks doubt about me, shouldn't it block doubt about the content too?
7. that'd mean every time i imagine a unicorn or remember my dead grandfather's kitchen, they have to exist in at least some mental form, maybe even real form.
8. but clearly i can think about things that don't exist, and descartes doesn't think the cogito proves unicorns are real.
9. so either step 1 is wrong, or there's a hidden wall between me and my content that i don't understand.

where does it break? i feel like step 5 is the suspicious one, where i treat knowing my content like knowing myself, but i can't say why that'd be a mistake without making the cogito seem weaker. if i have to save one, i'd rather keep 1 and 4, but then what am i paying in step 5 or 6?
