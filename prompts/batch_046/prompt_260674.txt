I'm preparing a single 90-minute session for a reading group of philosophers of science and clinical researchers who are skeptical that psychiatric categories are robust natural kinds but who have not previously encountered Ian Hacking's framework. I want them to understand looping effects—the feedback between classifying human subjects and the subjects' subsequent self-modification, which then reshapes the classification—using the trajectory of post-traumatic stress disorder from its formalization in DSM-III through its gradual expansion into broad civilian trauma discourse as the central case. Design the explanatory arc: first, the preliminary conceptual distinction that must be secured before looping can be introduced (for instance, interactive versus indifferent kinds); second, why this PTSD trajectory functions as the carrying example, including which structural features make the loop visible and which historical confounds must be bracketed; third, the predictable misconceptions in the order they will arise—likely beginning with the reduction of looping to mere labeling bias, then sliding into the stronger claim that looping proves suffering is entirely fabricated—and the precise point in the sequence where each should be intercepted; fourth, the check-for-understanding question that distinguishes grasping the feedback mechanism from concluding that trauma is unreal; and finally, which standard philosophical apparatus (such as detailed analogies to biological taxonomy or full-scale social constructionism) should be deliberately omitted at this level and why.
