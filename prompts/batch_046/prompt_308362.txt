A colleague of mine distinguishes “akrasia” from “mere hyperbolic preference reversal” when classifying self-control failures. Her verdicts so far: (i) a dieter eats cake at a party after explicitly judging “this is worse for my health goal,” pulled by immediate taste—akrasia; (ii) a student puts off exam prep to play video games, genuinely thinking in the moment that resting now is better, with no sense of breaking a prior commitment—mere hyperbolic discounting, not akrasia; (iii) a worker consistently undersaves for retirement, always preferring present consumption, never experiencing a specific “I should save now” being overridden—stable discounting, not akrasia; (iv) a musician with a strict daily practice rule checks her phone during practice while thinking “I should be playing scales”—akrasia.

What would she say about this case: a recovered alcoholic with a lifelong, stable abstinence commitment is offered wine at a party. The immediate social reward is salient; his past recovery goals feel distant. He tells himself “one glass is harmless” and drinks, without ever judging in the moment that drinking is the worse option, though it clearly violates his global rule.
