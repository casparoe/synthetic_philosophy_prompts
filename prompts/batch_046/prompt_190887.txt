I'm a high school student trying to clarify my own views on whether citizens have a moral duty to vote, and I would like help identifying the principle—if any—that best captures my judgments across the following cases. I believe I ought to vote in a tight race between a decent candidate and a genuinely dangerous one, even if my single vote is almost certain not to be decisive; that feels like preventing harm rather than expressing a preference. I also think I should vote in a low-stakes local school-board election if I have actually studied the issues, since participation in my immediate community seems valuable in itself. By contrast, I think abstaining is permissible—perhaps even correct—when I face a complex national referendum on tax restructuring that I have not taken time to understand, even though the stakes are high; casting an uninformed vote seems worse than staying home. My intuitions about symbolic voting are split: writing in a protest candidate in an election I know I cannot affect feels acceptable, but casting that same protest vote in a close race where it might help the worst candidate win feels irresponsible. The tension I cannot resolve is this: I feel a stronger pull to vote in the school-board race, where the impact is small but I am informed, than in the high-stakes referendum, where the impact is large but I am ignorant—yet I also think democratic legitimacy requires broad participation regardless of expertise, which seems to push the other way. To make the problem sharper, consider a system that allows citizens to delegate their vote on specific issues to trusted representatives rather than voting personally. If I delegate my vote on climate policy to an expert I trust but vote personally on education, have I satisfied whatever duty I have, or does democratic citizenship require direct personal exercise? What principle—whether grounded in expected influence, expressive value, or a fiduciary duty of competence—unifies these verdicts? Which of my judgments should be revised, and what does the correct principle say about delegating votes in this manner?
