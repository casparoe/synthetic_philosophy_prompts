My friend Maya and Leo got into it over drinks about whether a weird gadget counts as part of someone's mind. The gadget is this old mechanical proof-board a mathematician built: you slide in premises and rules, and it physicaly rearranges tiles to show a conclusion, but the middle steps get covered by metal plates that lock. You can see the conclusion, but the intermediate logic is hidden unless you dismantle the board.

Maya said this is classic extended mind - it's just working memory made physical. The board holds beliefs the mathematician can't hold in her head, it's always there on her desk, and she trusts it like she trusts her own reasoning. Leo said no way - Clark and Chalmers need the external thing to be accessible like biological memory, and here the intermediate steps are deliberately opaque. The mathematician isn't "consulting" the board; she's outsourcing to a black box. Maya shot back that accessibility doesn't mean conscious transparency - she could open the plates, she just doesn't need to, same as not introspecting every neural pathway. Leo said that's missing the point: with her brain she can in principle trace back the reasoning, but with this board the causal path is one-way. The board doesn't integrate into her cognitive loop, it replaces part of it.

They asked me to settle it and I genuinely don't know who's right. Can you adjudicate point by point? Is Leo right that opacity breaks the parity condition, or is Maya right that functional availability is enough? And is there something both are missing about whether one-way causal dependence counts as intergration?
