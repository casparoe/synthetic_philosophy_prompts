Exam question: "Standard economic rationality holds that sunk costs should not influence forward-looking choice because they are causally irrelevant to future outcomes. Yet philosophers have identified contexts—continuing a collaborative project after a partner withdraws, maintaining an acquired skill despite changed circumstances—where abandoning investment seems to produce a kind of practical discontinuity. Evaluate whether honoring sunk costs is always irrational. In your answer, distinguish between (i) cases where past expenditure provides evidential reason about future value, (ii) cases where honoring the cost functions as a commitment device for future self-control, and (iii) cases of diachronic practical identity in which discontinuation threatens the coherence of an agent's long-term projects. Argue for or against the universality of the irrationality claim, using these distinctions."

Student answer: "Honoring sunk costs is not always irrational, though decision theory correctly treats it as a fallacy in pure form. First, past expenditure can provide evidential reason: if I have spent years learning Latin and now consider abandoning it, my persistence is evidence that the skill has hidden value I am currently underestimating, so the sunk cost legitimately guides belief revision about future worth. Second, honoring costs can serve as a commitment device: by refusing to abandon a project after investing resources, I make future abandonment more costly for my future self, thereby binding myself to valuable long-term plans through strategic self-binding. Third, in diachronic practical identity, discontinuing a long-term project fractures the narrative coherence that rational agency requires over time; since practical identity demands continuity, honoring the sunk cost here is not merely permitted but rationally required. Therefore, the universality claim fails: sunk-cost reasoning operates as a legitimate form of diachronic rationality across all three categories, rather than as a cognitive bias to be eliminated."
