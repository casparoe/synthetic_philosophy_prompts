I'm a high school student hosting a student-paper podcast series, and I'm drafting interview questions for a philosopher who defends the view that "copycat concepts"—terms borrowed from one domain and applied to another—produce what she calls "fluid slippage." According to her, once a concept like toxicity migrates from chemistry to interpersonal behavior, its original criteria become unstable, and people begin applying chemical-reaction logic to social judgments. She insists this is not mere metaphor but a genuine conceptual change that corrupts reasoning in the original domain as well. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that avoid having her simply restate this position. I want at least one question on each of the following: (a) how she distinguishes harmful fluid slippage from productive analogical reasoning; (b) what she believes happens to the original chemical concept of toxicity once the copycat version dominates public discourse; (c) whether her view implies that we ought to purge copycat terms from policy debates; and (d) how she answers the objection that all concepts are already fluid, so there is no stable baseline to corrupt. For each question, include a brief follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give—particularly anticipating her standard defense that slippage is only dangerous when people forget the borrowing occurred.
