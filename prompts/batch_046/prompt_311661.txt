I am a high school student drafting an adversarial-collaboration exercise for a senior philosophy-of-economics reading group, and I need the joint statement that two opposing camps could sign in good faith on the topic of inheritance taxes, dynastic wealth, and equal opportunity. One camp maintains that steep progressive inheritance taxes are necessary to prevent dynastic wealth from undermining fair equality of opportunity; the other camp maintains that individuals possess a legitimate interest in transmitting resources to descendants and that inheritance taxation violates family autonomy and property rights.

Please produce the document they should have co-authored. Begin with a numbered section of agreed claims—covering the empirical concentration of inherited wealth, the conceptual distinction between opportunity-blocking capital (assets that confer access to elite education, political influence, or monopoly positions) and consumption capital (assets that affect private lifestyle without structural gatekeeping effects), the shared commitment to some form of equal opportunity, and the legitimate scope of state coercion—written in neutral language both sides can accept. Follow this with a disagreements table: for each remaining dispute, provide the precise formulation that its own camp would endorse, paired with the opposing formulation stated without caricature.

Then identify the cruxes—the specific sub-questions such that settling one in a given direction would move at least one camp closer to the other. For each crux, specify a concrete institutional comparison or hypothetical case that both sides agree bears on it (for instance, comparing a jurisdiction with near-total inheritance neutralization to one with broad family-business exemptions), and record in advance what each camp predicts that comparison will show.

In your response, clearly separate what is settled among people who study this topic from what is genuinely contested; do not present either camp's normative commitments as the consensus view. The statement should be formal, self-contained, and written so that someone without web access can evaluate its logic. Please write in American English and avoid asking for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
