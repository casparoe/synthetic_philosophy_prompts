I am organizing a small interdisciplinary reading group—one philosopher of mind, one mathematician, and one cognitive neuroscientist—and we have roughly three years of sustained conceptual work before large-scale empirical measurements of brain-level complexity are likely to reshape the landscape. Our focus is Integrated Information Theory, but specifically its upstream theoretical architecture rather than its empirical testing protocols. I keep returning to a tension that seems under-examined in the literature I have seen: IIT’s exclusion postulate demands a single maximal complex within any substrate, yet the formal conditions for irreducibility appear to permit boundary ambiguities in continuous or densely interconnected physical systems, which raises the question of whether nested or partially overlapping conscious entities could exist without violating the postulate, and whether substrate independence can be maintained without collapsing either into trivial panpsychism or into an arbitrary biological chauvinism. Rather than resolving that tension for us, I want you to treat our situation as a prioritization problem.

Which open conceptual questions or lines of theoretical work within IIT should a group like ours pursue, and in what order? I want a defended agenda, not a survey or a reading list. For each priority, state the criteria you are using—tractability for philosophers versus mathematicians, how upstream it is of other debates (for example, whether it constrains how we should interpret perturbational-complexity measurements in clinical neuroscience, or whether it determines the conditions under which an artificial architecture could satisfy the axioms of intrinsic existence and integration), and what specific conceptual result over the next two to three years would cause you to revise the ranking upward or downward. Address the trade-offs explicitly: if we devote our first year to formalizing boundary conditions for complex exclusion in continuous substrates, what do we necessarily neglect, and is that neglect defensible? Finally, identify what you regard as genuinely overrated in current IIT discourse—which questions absorb disproportionate attention but do not actually constrain practice, further theory-building, or interpretation—so that we can avoid spending our limited time on debates that are largely decorative.
