okay so ive been trying to wrap my head around the lucas-penrose argument for this philosophy of mind thing and my study partner walked me into a version that feels like it proves way too much. here's how it goes:

(1) For any consistent formal system F that a human H can fully grasp, H can construct a Gödel sentence G_F that's true but unprovable in F.

(2) So H knows G_F is true, but F (if implemented mechanically) can't derive it.

(3) Therefore H's reasoning isn't captured by F.

(4) Now imagine H is actually three people H1, H2, H3, each using different formal systems they fully grasp. By (1)-(3), none are fully mechanized by their own system.

(5) But together they form a team that can coordinate outputs and, it seems, grasp a meta-system M combining all three. Apply (1) to M: there should be a Gödel sentence for M teh team can see is true but M can't prove.

(6) So either the team also escapes mechanization, or if we model them as one distributed mechanism, teh same problem applies at the collective level.

(7) Push this further and you get an infinite hierarchy—humanity itself, or any larger collective, can never be captured by any formal system, no matter how complex. That feels wrong. I mean, surely science could eventually model collective reasoning with some massive distributed-computing setup, even if it's messy.

(8) So (1)-(7) seem to show that anti-mechanism, if true for one person, scales up to deny any mechanistic account of group cognition at all. But I don't want to deny that.

Where does the slip happen? Is it in (3)—that knowing G_F doesn't actually show H transcends all formal systems, only that H isn't identical to F? Or is the mistake in (5), assuming a team really forms a single graspable formal system M? I kind of want to keep (1) and (2) since Gödel's result is solid, and I'd like to hold onto some anti-mechanist intuition, but maybe (3) is where teh price gets paid. Also, does this connect at all to Penrose's idea about quantum consciousness? Not asking for sources, just wondering if that's in teh same neighborhood. Anyway, which step should I reject and what does it cost me?
