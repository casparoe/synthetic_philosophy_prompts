In my graduate seminar on moral philosophy we have been working through Scanlon's contractualism, and after considerable discussion I have adopted a specific reading of how it addresses the aggregation problem. Rather than treating numbers-affected as a covert summation of welfare, I interpret the view as resolving the difficulty through a structural feature of interpersonal justification: in rescue scenarios, each affected party holds an independent veto against any principle that permits their harm, and the collective weight of multiple such vetoes is not aggregated but functions instead as a comparative constraint on what can be justified to all affected parties simultaneously. I want to put this interpretation under serious pressure and ask what would genuinely count against it—not the generic charge that contractualism collapses into rule-utilitarianism, but the precise, targeted defeaters.

Specifically, which hypothetical cases—perhaps involving probabilistic rescue, asymmetrical harms, or partial compliance—would show that this veto-based mechanism either over-generates obligations or under-generates them? Are there formal results in decision theory or social-choice theory demonstrating that any non-aggregative comparative judgment among affected parties must violate transitivity or independence conditions in a damaging way? Within Scanlon's framework, are there commitments regarding the scope of reasonable rejection that would compel him either to abandon the standard rescue intuition or to accept a disguised form of aggregation after all? Furthermore, does this position risk being qualified into unfalsifiability—absorbing every counterexample by refining the conditions of justification—and if so, is that insulation a theoretical virtue or a fatal methodological flaw? I would ask you to rank these potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, to say which ones a committed defender of this reading should concede and which they could plausibly dismiss, and to be explicit about whether the view, as I have formulated it, risks anything substantial at all.
