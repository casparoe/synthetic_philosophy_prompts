hey, i'm drafting a short piece for my column on buddhist anatta and personal identity — not the usual chariot analogy rehash, but something a bit more in the weeds. here's the core argument, tell me if i'm missing something obvious or if it's just wrong.

the standard line is no permanent soul, just five skandhas that cluster and disperse. rebirth is then causal continuity, like one flame passing to another. okay. but moral responsibility gets shaky: if there's no enduring subject, who's accumulating karma? the usual answer is conventional self (sammuti-sacca) versus ultimate reality (paramattha-sacca).

here's where i go off-piste: i think the skandha framework smuggles in a micro-substantialism. form, feeling, perception, mental formations, consciousness — each is treated like a discrete stream, but they interpenetrate so totally that separating them is mostly a linguistic move, not an ontological one. so anatta doesn't eliminate the self; it fragments it into five pseudo-entities that do all the work a self used to do. it's like saying there's no chariot, just wheels and axle, but then treating the wheels as if they have chariot-essence.

this also creates a binding problem. if consciousness (vinnanna) is just one skandha among others, and it's not the owner of experience, then what binds the other four into a single moment of awareness? you can't invoke the self — that's banned. without some binding principle, you've got five parallel processes with no unified field. later schools tried to fix this with store-consciousness (alayavijnana), but that's basically admitting a hidden substrate, which sounds like a self in disguise.

so my tentative take: anatta dissolves personal identity into process, but the dissolution is incomplete because the skandhas function as mini-selves. and the binding problem forces either a hidden substrate or radical discontinuity between moments, which makes karma transmission look like magic rather than mechanism.

is this intresting for a general audience, or am i overstating the binding issue? should i lean harder into parfit's reductionism instead of going full buddhist metaphysics? also, does the conventional/ultimate distinction hold up here, or is it just special pleeding?
