I've been retired from physics for about five years and lately I've been trying to wrap my head around this Rietdijk-Putnam argument about the block universe. I think I understand the mechanics -- in SR there's no absolute simultaneity, so if you take "real" seriously then future events are already sitting there, which makes determinism feel inevitable. But my philosphy reading group has been stuck in the mud for months and I can't tell what kind of stuck it is.

We all agree on Lorentz transforms and that simultaneity is frame-dependent. We all agree that the distant-galaxy argument -- walking toward Andromeda shifts which Earth-events are "now" compared to walking away -- kills any unique present slice. Where we split is strange: half say this only proves a B-series ontology, not determinism, because "being real" isn't the same as "being causally fixed." The other half says if every event has equal ontological status then of course it's all determined, you're just playing word games with "free." We've argued for ages and nobody budges.

Here's the twist I can't shake, and maybe it's dumb: I keep picturing an astronaut in an eliptical orbit around a black hole, where gravitational time dilation plus velocity means her plane of simultaneity sweeps across Earth history in jumps when she fires thrusters. If she can genuinely change which Earth-events are "simultaneous with me now" just by adjusting thrust, do those events flip from "undetermined future" to "determined past" for her? Or does that just show "simultaneous with" was never a good marker for ontological status?

So I'm not asking "who's right." I'm asking what kind of disagreement this actually is. Hidden physics (are we misunderstanding time dilation?), hidden metaphysics (what does "real" mean?), or are we talking past each other over "determinsm"? And is there a crux case or formal distinction that could move us, or is it just bedrock intuition?

Also, if we take my black-hole orbit case seriously, does it strengthen the block-universe argument or break it? I feel like it should break it but I can't say why without sounding like I'm just defending free will out of sentiment.
