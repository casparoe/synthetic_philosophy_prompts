The exchange between Galen Strawson's Basic Argument — that genuine moral responsibility requires being causa sui in a way no determined or indeterminist agent can satisfy — and the standard compatibilist reply — that responsibility requires only appropriate reasons-responsiveness within an ordinary causal history — has reached a familiar standstill: both sides grant that no one originates herself ex nihilo, yet they diverge radically on whether this undermines desert. Is the disagreement substantive, verbal, or a case of two questions — one about the metaphysical conditions of true desert, the other about the normative conditions of accountable agency — being pursued under a single heading? Or is there a concealed conceptual issue, for instance about whether reactive attitudes necessarily presuppose a practice-transcendent form of agent-causation rather than a practice-internal status? Diagnose the impasse using what each side actually says about causation and blame, not by cataloguing positions. In particular, are they divided by bedrock intuitions about retribution, by different standards for what counts as a satisfactory explanation of action, or by whether normative practices must be grounded in metaphysical origination? And given that diagnosis, what — a precise distinction, a psychological finding, a formal result, or a reconceptualization of the question — could actually move the debate, or should we treat the stalemate as evidence that the problem is ill-posed?
