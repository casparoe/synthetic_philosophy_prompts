I'm developing a research framework on epistemic peer disagreement, with a specific focus on what might be called meta-cognitive conciliationism: cases where disputants agree on all first-order evidence yet diverge over the reliability of their own reasoning processes. Rather than revisiting the canonical examples—such as the well-known restaurant-check disagreement or climate-science peer disputes—I want to explore a narrower cluster where the crux concerns how to weigh theoretical virtues, including simplicity, explanatory breadth, and intuitive stability, against calibration data about one's own past errors in philosophical judgment. For instance, two epistemic peers might concur on every argument regarding compatibilism but disagree about whether one's persistent intuition that determinism feels wrong should be discounted because the intuition tracks a known bias in modal reasoning, or whether that very suspicion of bias is itself a motivated error.

Within this framing, which open questions or lines of work deserve the most sustained attention, and in what order? I am especially interested in how the following issues relate: whether conciliationism applies symmetrically when the disagreement is about one's own cognitive calibration rather than an external state of affairs; whether steadfastness can be defended without circular appeals to self-trust; and whether there is a principled boundary between rational and arational sources of divergence in meta-cognitive assessment. But I do not want a flat survey. Instead, please provide a defended agenda: rank these questions, and any others you take to be more fundamental, according to explicit criteria—tractability within a single research program, the number of downstream debates that depend on the answer, and the practical stakes for high-trust collaboration in science or policy. Which resolution would actually change how a rational agent should revise confidence? And which, if left unsettled, would least obstruct practical reasoning? Please treat this as an agenda-setting exercise for limited resources, not as a call for a literature review or citation list; I am working without database access and need arguments that stand on their conceptual merits alone.
