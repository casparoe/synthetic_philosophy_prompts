ok so me and my friend got into it last night about whether "the will of the people" even means anything real, or if its just a rhetorical club people swing when they like an election result. I was trying to say its more complicated than that but he kept going "ok then what IS it" and I realized I dont actually have a clear answer, which is embarrassing since I sit on our city's participatory governance board and we have to decide this exact thing by friday.

Here's the concrete mess: there's a waterfront redevelopment proposal, and we ran both a citywide referendum (58% yes) and a citizens assembly (after three weekends with planners and ecologists, 70% no, mostly over flood risk and hidden tax costs). Now activists are saying the assembly is elite capture and only the referendum counts as the real popular will. Some council members want to ignore both and go with staff recommendations. Our board has to issue a memo recommending which mechanism should carry the day for this decision, with an actual course of action and a timeline—like whether we pilot a new framework just for waterfront or apply it board-wide. The memo needs to take seriously the activists' strongest point, that deliberative bodies filter out working-class preferences and that "will" should mean what people actually say they want, not what they "would want" if they were different people. I also keep thinking about this distinction I came up with between "dispositional will" (what people would want if they knew all the relevant facts) and "expressed will" (what they vote for in the moment), but I'm not sure if that's useful or just confuses things more. My friend was also obsessed with whether there's even a "fact of the matter" about what people want when they're internally conflicted, so maybe address that too?

Please write the memo so the council can actually use it—don't just list theories, recommend something concrete with a timeframe, and be honest about where your recommendation might fail. And please don't correct my "peoples" typo if you quote me back, I wrote it that way in my notes on purpose.
