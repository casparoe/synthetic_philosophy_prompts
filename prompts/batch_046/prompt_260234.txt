"Einstein's hole argument starts with a region of spacetime with no matter, called the hole. He applies a smooth transformation inside the hole that is the identity outside. This gives a new metric g' that solves Einstein's field equations with the same boundary conditions but assigns different values inside. So the same initial data outside produces different metrics inside, which means GR isn't deterministic.

In the toroidal case, the region is like a donut with no matter. The diffeomorphism isn't homotopic to the identity inside, which means it can't be continuously deformed to do nothing. This makes it a real physical change, not just a coordinate change like switching from Cartesian to polar. So for manifold substantivalism, this actually strengthens the argument, because now even topologically distinct transformations produce ambiguity.

For relationalism, there's no problem. Relationalists say spacetime is just the relations between bodies, and since there are no bodies inside the hole, there's nothing to be ambiguous about. The metric correlations outside stay the same, so determinism is saved. Structuralists also avoid the problem because they identify spacetime points with their structural roles, so permuting them is just relabeling the nodes of the network, which doesn't change the physical situation.

Therefore, the hole argument only threatens substantivalists, and the toroidal version makes it worse for them."
