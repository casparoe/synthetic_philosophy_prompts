can u design a classroom activity for a grad seminar? 8 students, 90 min, on incommensurable values and hard choices — i want them to actually feel the gridlock, not just hear about it.

the scenarion: split them into 'stewards' and 'planners'. stewards protect a valley where people practice ephemeral architecture — structures of ice and moss that have to be built and destroyed every season in a specific cycle tied to the watershed. it's not museum culture; static preservation kills it. planners need to dam that same watershed to save a coastal city from flooding. both options are irreversible.

rules for the 40-min deliberaton: they must reach unanimous consent. they are banned from using weighing language — no 'outweighs', 'net benefit', 'trade-off', 'more valuable'. only reasons that name the kind of value at stake. each side gets one hidden constraint card they can reveal only if directly asked (e.g. the moss is already dying from warming; the dam will fail in 60 years). if they don't agree, a default rule floods the valley automaticlly.

timeline: 10 min setup/roles, 40 min deliberaton, 15 min secret ballot vote, 25 min debrief.

in the debrief, map the 'i literally can't rank them' moment onto parity vs incommensurability; map hidden-card reveals onto the idea that more information doesn't solve the comparison problem; map the default-rule failure onto institutional avoidance. also include an honest note: the simulation can't reproduce the real existantial weight of destroying a living practice or letting people drown, so the facilitatior has to supply that framing, not expect the rules to generate it.

make sure the rules do the work, not the facilitator's explanation afterwards. and throw in that niche seasonal-destruction twist so it's not just a generic culture-vs-progress case. write casual, maybe a typo.
