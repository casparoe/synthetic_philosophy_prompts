Write a pantoum—quatrains in which lines 2 and 4 of each stanza are repeated as lines 1 and 3 of the next, closing with lines 1 and 3 of the opening stanza reversed—about Zhuangzi's butterfly dream and the skeptical problem of distinguishing waking life from dreaming. Treat the butterfly not merely as a symbol of transformation but as an epistemic subject with asymmetric mnemonic access: it remembers being Zhuangzi, yet upon waking Zhuangzi retains no corresponding memory of being the butterfly. Let the form's repeating lines shift meaning across stanzas as the question of which consciousness is the real one is passed back and forth. In the penultimate stanza, introduce the worry that any proposed criterion for separating dream from waking—continuity, coherence, sensory richness—might itself be simulated within the dream, but do not resolve the paradox. End with the final repetition leaving the reader suspended between the two perspectives, so that the pantoum's circular structure becomes an argument about the irreducibility of first-person certainty. The poem should be philosophically precise enough that a reader familiar with the Zhuangzi passage and with modern skeptical arguments about dream criteria recognizes the moves, yet it should work first as a poem governed by its own internal logic rather than as an illustrated thesis.
