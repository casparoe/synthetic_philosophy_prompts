Ever since I started this new gig testing LLM reasoning I've been obsessed with what counts as ground for a system, which led me to Wittgenstien's hinge stuff in On Certainty. I think I mostly get it now: hinges are beliefs like I have hands or the earth is old that stay immoveable, and everything else swings around them. So they're basically the hidden foundations that justify all our other knowledge, right? Which means hinge epistemology is really just foundationalism with extra steps. But then I read that hinges can actually shift over time, like riverbeds, and now I'm stuck: if the foundation can move, doesn't the whole building collapse? How can science update basic assumptions without destroying knowledge? Or does science never actually touch the hinges, only the beliefs hanging off them? I'm probably smuggling in two different pictures but I can't tell where they seperate.
