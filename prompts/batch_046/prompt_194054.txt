Consider lexical threshold negative utilitarianism and the standard challenge that pleasure and pain may fail to be commensurable across the putative boundary. I want you to evaluate five conceptual adjustments to this view, treating each as a proposed refinement rather than a rejection. For every item, assign two scores on a 0-to-10 scale—one for fidelity to the core lexical intuition (that no amount of pleasure can outweigh severe pain), and one for the theoretical cost of the commensurability assumption it requires—then provide an overall rank from 1 (most defensible as a refinement) to 5 (closest to abandoning the view).

The adjustments are:

1. The asymptotic-gradient view: the threshold is not a discontinuous wall but an exponential decay curve, so that each incremental unit of pleasure has vanishing weight relative to any unit of serious pain, and no finite accumulation can cross over.

2. The modal-separation view: pleasure and pain inhabit incommensurable evaluative spaces; LTNU holds only within each modal domain, and cross-modal comparison is not false but undefined, like comparing temperature and color.

3. The reverse-lexicographic modification: allow that certain extreme, qualitatively dense pleasures (for instance, states of mystical union or radical aesthetic absorption) can outweigh mild suffering, while maintaining that no quantity of ordinary pleasure can outweigh severe pain.

4. The phenomenological-currency view: there exists a hidden common denominator—call it affective charge—permitting cardinal comparison, but only when both states exceed a minimal phenomenological intensity; below that floor, comparison is undefined rather than lexically ordered.

5. The narrative-integration view: reject atomistic point-comparison in favor of evaluating whole lives as narrative arcs; a life containing severe pain but structured by redemption or meaning may be ranked above a painless but narratively empty life, without ever weighing individual pains against individual pleasures on a common scale.

For each adjustment, identify the precise conceptual move that either preserves or dilutes the lexical commitment, explain what feature of pleasure-pain incommensurability it targets, and justify your two scores. Then present the overall 1-to-5 ranking with a brief defense of why the top-ranked item best balances fidelity to the lexical idea with a plausible account of how comparison works. Do not treat this as a literature review; treat it as a direct conceptual assessment of these five constructed positions.
