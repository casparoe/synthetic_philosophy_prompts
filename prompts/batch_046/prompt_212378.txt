For my politics coursework I have been asked to set out my commitments on the ethics of voting so my tutor can analyse whether they cohere. Here is my profile: I take voting to be primarily expressive — its value lies in registering a moral verdict on candidates rather than in altering outcomes; I accept a threshold-deontological rule that if a candidate threatens grave rights violations I have an absolute duty to vote against them, even in an electorate large enough that my ballot is certainly not decisive; I reject strategic voting, since casting a ballot for someone one does not genuinely endorse corrupts the expressive purpose; I believe proportional representation with Borda-style preference ordering avoids this corruption, because one can rank the dangerous candidate last without strategically elevating a compromise; and I hold that citizens who lack basic policy knowledge ought to abstain, since expressing a false judgment is not a genuine moral verdict. Please analyse these five commitments: identify which pairs pull against one another, rank the tensions by severity, state the cheapest revision that would restore consistency for each, note any apparent conflict that dissolves on inspection, and say which single change would resolve the most problems. Please respond as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
