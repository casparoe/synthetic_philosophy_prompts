I am drafting a literary novel centered on a jurist serving a collapsing authoritarian regime, and to render his internal deliberations with philosophical precision I have been studying the debate between H.L.A. Hart and Lon Fuller on legal positivism and natural law. At a recent dinner, a philosopher acquaintance summarized the disagreement in these terms: Hart maintained that law is entirely a matter of social rules recognized by officials through a rule of recognition, which entails that even profoundly immoral statutes—such as those enforcing racial segregation—remain fully valid law so long as they satisfy the formal criteria of the system. Fuller, she said, argued that law possesses an internal morality composed of eight procedural principles—generality, publicity, prospectivity, clarity, consistency, feasibility, stability, and congruence between official action and declared rule—and that a system systematically violating these conditions is not law at all but mere organized coercion. From this, she concluded that Fuller's position is nothing more than traditional natural law disguised in procedural clothing: he imports moral content into the concept of law by calling procedural fairness "morality," whereas Hart rigorously separates legal validity from moral evaluation.

I suspect this gloss is too neat. It seems to conflate Fuller's claim that these eight principles are conditions of legal efficacy with the stronger claim that they are moral imperatives in themselves, and it may overlook Hart's own acknowledgment that any functioning legal system must include a minimal content of natural law—prohibitions on violence, theft, and deception—simply to survive. Could you provide an accuracy audit of this summary? I want you to separate what is faithful to each thinker, identify the subtle misrepresentation in the account of Fuller's internal morality, and determine whether Fuller's view genuinely collapses into natural law or remains a distinctive rival to positivism. I am also interested in whether Hart's rule of recognition can truly remain morally neutral once we admit that survival conditions for law may carry implicit normative weight. No bibliographic references are necessary; I need only conceptual clarity for the novel's dialogue.
