As a retired geologist who spent three decades interpreting stratigraphic sequences and who has only recently begun auditing philosophy courses, I often find that the conceptual tools of my former discipline do not map neatly onto the distinctions now being asked of me. Last week, in a seminar on philosophy of biology, a discussion turned to whether the discrete morphological clusters we identify in the fossil record—say, the abrupt transitions between certain trilobite lineages—represent genuine evolutionary discontinuities or are merely artifacts of our own taxonomic conventions, particularly given that anagenesis in the column often blurs the boundaries we draw. A fellow participant cut the debate short by insisting that "the dispute is purely verbal," as though this observation alone settled whether paleontological species are real patterns or convenient fictions. I am convinced that this slogan must be doing significant hidden work, but I cannot yet determine which work.

Could you please lay out, as a menu of distinct precise theses rather than a single answer, the different philosophical claims that might be compressed into the assertion that a disagreement over fossil species boundaries is "just verbal"? Among the possibilities I have in mind are Carnapian framework-relativity about ontological commitment, semantic indeterminacy in the term "species" itself, a pragmatic account on which the interlocutors share all factual beliefs but differ only in linguistic preference, an eliminativist denial that the entities named have any mind-independent existence, and perhaps a metaontological claim that existence questions in science are always internal to a theoretical framework. For each interpretation, please say whether it is a trivial restatement of the problem, whether it is substantively defensible, whether it is false, and—most importantly—whether it actually supports the conclusion that we should stop debating whether those stratigraphic discontinuities correspond to real biological divisions. I am not seeking a reading list or historical citations; I need only the conceptual disambiguation so that I can return to the seminar and ask which of these versions, if any, was intended.
