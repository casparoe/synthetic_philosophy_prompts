I have come to defend a framing of divine hiddenness that treats it not as an evidential deficit but as a modal-structural feature of what I call non-coercive theistic ontology. On this view, God's absence from the evidential landscape is not analogous to the absence of a physical object; rather, it is a topologically necessary withdrawal that preserves the logical space for free assent, where assent is understood not as propositional belief but as a participatory orientation that discursive evidence cannot compel. The view carries two specific commitments: first, an asymmetry between evidential accessibility and relational accessibility, such that the conditions for knowing God are not the conditions for being known by God; second, that any world in which God is unambiguously manifest to all rational agents is a world in which the concept of faith collapses into compelled acknowledgment, which is not the kind of relation this God seeks. I want an honest assessment of what would count against this position. Which empirical or phenomenological findings—about the cross-cultural distribution of religious experience, about the neural correlates of mystical states, or about the psychology of belief formation under ambiguity—would genuinely threaten it rather than merely complicate it? Which philosophical arguments, perhaps from moral psychology or from the logic of omnipotence, would have to be conceded by a defender of this view, and which could be absorbed by redrawing the boundary between evidential and participatory knowledge? Are there formal results in decision theory or in the epistemology of testimony that would show the asymmetry I posit is unstable? Finally, I want to know whether this framing risks being insulated to the point of unfalsifiability—if every possible evidential state can be interpreted as consistent with the withdrawal thesis, is that insulation a feature or a defect for a view of this kind? Rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, indicate which ones a committed defender should take most seriously, and say whether you think the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability and, if so, whether that is a problem for a thesis of this sort.
