I'm writing a diss chapter on economic teleology in the Politics and my advsor tossed off this summary over coffee: 'Aristotle saw coinage as a practical fix for barter—like you can't carry wheat to the cobbler—so money is just a conventional stand-in for need. There's also the shoe example in NE V.5 where proportional reciprocity between builder and shoemaker doesn't look like market logic—does that complicate the summary? And I've been wondering about this interpretation that Aristotle's distinction anticipates modern circulation forms, where exchange is either for use (C-M-C) or for accumulation (M-C-M)—is that fair to his text or totally anachronistic? Which parts of my advisor's summary are accurate, which are subtle misreadings, and does the conclusion about natural limits actually hold up once you correct them?
