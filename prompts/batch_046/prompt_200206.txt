I've recently found myself returning to a problem I first encountered in a philosophy of science seminar, one that has taken on personal urgency as I work with interpretable machine-learning systems: when core information about a system's internal structure is permanently inaccessible, how should we think about identifying its ontology? I want to construct a diagnostic taxonomy—not a literature survey, but a sorting instrument—for a small interdisciplinary reading group that includes both philosophers and computer scientists. It should take the form of a decision tree whose nodes are concrete commitments phrased as questions that a participant can answer without already knowing the theoretical labels.

Please begin the tree with a root question asking whether observational inaccessibility of internal states undermines ontological identification at all. From there, branch by whether the failure is treated as purely epistemic—there is a determinate fact of the matter that we cannot reach—or as ontological, meaning there is no determinate fact. Then split by whether structural or relational descriptions are sufficient to fix ontology in the absence of direct access, and finally by whether the domain—physical systems, conscious minds, or artificial neural networks—alters the answer. Each leaf should carry the name of a named position where one exists (for instance, ontic structural realism, epistemic structural realism, entity realism, eliminativism about hidden entities, or a domain-relativist view) together with one sentence on its most awkward commitment; combinations that remain logically available but unoccupied should be marked 'unnamed' and briefly diagnosed as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Where the tree cannot genuinely separate two named positions, flag that pair explicitly. I am especially interested in whether there is a coherent, currently unnamed position that accepts inaccessibility in physical and mental domains yet denies it applies to artificial systems, and if so, what awkward commitment that domain-selective view carries.
