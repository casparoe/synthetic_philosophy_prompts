Write an essay on the controversy over whether the self can be fully explained as a center of narrative gravity. Begin by articulating the view, associated with Daniel Dennett, that the self is not an immaterial substance or a neural command center, but rather an abstract organizational point—similar to the center of gravity of a physical object—around which the stories we tell about our beliefs, desires, and actions converge and stabilize.

Then address the central tension. Defenders of a radically distributed account propose that narrative coherence can emerge bottom-up from competing sub-personal processes, each generating local interpretations that are weighted, revised, or suppressed without any central editor; call this the distributed narrative gravity thesis. Critics reply that the very act of integrating conflicting autobiographical episodes—deciding which memories are authoritative, which actions count as genuinely mine, and which motives deserve endorsement—seems to presuppose a prior unitary perspective that performs the integrating. Without such a perspective, they argue, we are left with mere causal aggregation rather than genuine autobiographical ownership, and a hidden homuncular subject is smuggled back into the model.

Weigh these positions with care. Consider whether analogies from institutional decision-making or from error-minimization in perceptual systems can illuminate how unity might arise without a sovereign agent. At the same time, examine whether narrative identity involves normative commitments—such as responsibility, authenticity, and regret—that pure causal integration cannot account for. Reach and defend a clear verdict: either the distributed narrative gravity thesis succeeds in dissolving the self into an emergent pattern of storytelling without a central editor, or the demands of editorial control and normative accountability reveal that narrative gravity requires a real, if minimal, center of agency that the metaphor alone cannot capture. Do not conclude with an indecisive balance; justify your position explicitly.
