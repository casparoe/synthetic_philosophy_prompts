I'm working with a small philosophy of mind reading group — maybe six philosphers, meeting every other week — and we're trying to decide which open issues around access consciousness, phenomenal consciousness, and global workspace theory are actually worth our limited time. There's the classic Block-style overflow debate, the question of whether global broadcasting is necessary or just sufficient for access, and then this more niche thing I've been stuck on: whether phenomenal consciousness requires a specific kind of self-modeling that can survive even when global broadcasting is disrupted, like in split-brain cases or in artificial architectures with modular 'local workspaces' that never fully integrate. I don't want a survey of everything.

Instead, if you had to rank the open conceptual questions in this space — say, whether phenomenal and access consciousness come apart in principle, whether global workspace theory can accommodate unconscious access without collapsing into a behavourist view, and whether the 'integration' required for consciousness is genuinely global or can be satisfied locally — which ones should we tackle first? I care about the reasoning: which questions are upstream of the others, which ones are tractible without running experiments (since we're philosophers, not neuroscientists), and what kind of conceptual argument over the next year or two would make you reorder them? Also, are the implications for artificial consciousness benchmarks actually important, or is that putting the cart before the horse? I keep seeing people jump straight to 'can AI be conscious?' but I'm wondering if that's overrated until we settle the access/phenomenal distinction properly.
