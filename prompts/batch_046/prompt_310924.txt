After a long and somewhat frustrating argument with a friend about whether climate, pathogens, and volcanic eruptions can be treated as genuine historical actors without either smuggling in human-like agency or falling back into crude environmental determinism, I want to establish the ground rules for any theory that attempts this middle path. Write the desiderata that an adequate account of non-human entities—specifically epidemic disease agents, long-term climatic regimes, and singular volcanic events—must satisfy in order to count as a serious contribution to historical explanation rather than either metaphor or determinism. For each desideratum, supply the concrete case or conceptual tension that motivates it: for example, how the account should treat the demographic and labor-market restructuring that followed the Black Death without attributing strategic intention to Yersinia pestis; how it should integrate the agricultural failures and social unrest linked to the 1783 Laki fissure eruption without treating climate as a sufficient cause of the French Revolution; and how it should distinguish the causal weight of the 1815 Tambora eruption in producing the widespread crop failures and social disruptions often called the Year Without a Summer from a narrative in which weather simply writes history. Be explicit about which distinctions—between causation and agency, between enabling conditions and triggering events, between systemic pressure and contingent human response—must be preserved, and flag any pair of requirements you think are jointly unsatisfiable, such as demanding full irreducibility of these forces to social dynamics while also insisting they can never override human contingency.

Then, very briefly, score three broad orientations against your list: the classical environmental-determinist framework, the strict social-constructionist position that treats all such factors as mere backdrop, and a more moderate relational or assemblage-oriented view that distributes efficacy across human and non-human elements. Keep the weight on the criteria themselves rather than on the scoring. Finally, state clearly what would change your mind about which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable: identify the specific argument, counter-example, or theoretical result that would lead you to relax one of the requirements or to conclude that the middle ground is indeed impossible.
