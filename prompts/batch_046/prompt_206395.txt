Consider John Locke's theory of personal identity—specifically his claim that sameness of consciousness, realized through the continuity of memory and self-reflective awareness, makes the person, regardless of whether the underlying substance is material or immaterial. Now extend Dennett's brain-transplant scenario into a technological form Locke could never have anticipated. A subject, Yorick, undergoes gradual, module-level brain replacement with synthetic tissue, but the replacement is functionally disaggregated rather than duplicated. The hippocampal formation remains biologically intact, preserving direct autobiographical memory; the prefrontal executive modules are replaced by architecturally distinct synthetic equivalents that reproduce behavior but lack direct access to the hippocampal stream; and the integrative narrative function—what Dennett might call the self's center of narrative gravity—is offloaded to a distributed cloud substrate with variable latency. After the procedure, two bodies operate: Hamlet, who houses the biological hippocampus paired with synthetic executive modules, and Fortinbras, who is entirely synthetic but hosts the distributed narrative center. Both present coherent first-person perspectives and claim to be Yorick. Hamlet genuinely remembers being Yorick; Fortinbras possesses a fully reconstructed autobiography that he experiences as authentic first-personal memory, yet he has no direct biological continuity with the original brain.

Given Locke's explicit commitments in his work on identity, how would he adjudicate between Hamlet and Fortinbras? Would he identify Yorick with Hamlet, on the grounds that the original stream of consciousness—specifically the remembering subject—persists there, even though executive agency has been replaced? Or would he be compelled to locate the person in Fortinbras, since the capacity for self-reflection and narrative integration, which Locke treats as constitutive of personhood, has migrated to that substrate? Furthermore, does Locke's implicit assumption that consciousness is a single, metaphysically simple thread survive this kind of engineered disaggregation, where memory, agency, and narrative integration are physically separated? Would he be forced to revise his substance-independent criterion, or instead deny that either body is truly Yorick? Finally, assess whether this outcome makes Locke's account more or less defensible than Dennett's narrative-gravity model, which was designed precisely to accommodate reconstructed and distributed selves.
