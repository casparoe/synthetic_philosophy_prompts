There's this feature on a platform I use—part of their crowd-fact-checking thing—where if you submit a correction to a post, they run your account through a standing audit. If you've previously shared anything that was flagged, even in a totally different category, your correction gets deprioritized or hidden. The UI literally says you lack correction standing due to prior violations. So they treat being hypocritical, or just imperfect, as disqualifing you from calling out errors in others.

What view of moral standing and hypocrisy does that institutional design presuppose? Does it commit to the idea that hypocrisy undermines the authority to blame, or is it just a pragmatic heuristik? Could a control-based or knowledge-based account of blame make sense of it, or does it only work if you think moral purity is a pre-requesite? And is the practice consistent with itself—like, what happens when the people running the audit have their own flagged history? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether the design strictly needs that theory or could fit with others, and tell me what would have to change in the platform's rules if we rejected the idea that hypocrisy ever cancels standing.
