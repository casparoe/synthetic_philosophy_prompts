I recently left a government advisory position and have been asked to prepare a philosophical profile on political speech for a working group on democratic ethics. Before I present, I want a consistency audit of my commitments rather than a lecture. Here is where I stand:

(a) I accept Arendt’s core claim that political lying is destructive not primarily because it deceives individual citizens, but because it corrodes the shared factual reality that makes collective judgment and democratic action possible.

(b) I think there is a meaningful distinction between occasional, recoverable factual deception and what Arendt calls systematic defactualization—the construction of an alternative narrative framework—and only the latter reaches the level of structural danger she identifies.

(c) I hold that democratic deliberation necessarily involves a form of strategic framing and selective emphasis—what I call deliberative shading—that departs from full transparency but does not cross into deception, because it remains open to correction and does not deny specific factual claims.

(d) I believe that in cases of extreme moral emergency—specifically, where truthful disclosure would foreseeably trigger mass atrocity—political leaders are not merely permitted but obligated to deceive factually, provided the deception remains genuinely isolated and is eventually acknowledged.

(e) I maintain that the boundary between recoverable deception and structural defactualization is objective and can be policed by institutional norms and retrospective transparency, rather than being left to the speaker’s private intentions.

My questions: Which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to conflict? Does my acceptance of deliberative shading in (c) undermine the shared factual world I defend in (a), or is the tension merely apparent? Does the extreme-emergency exception in (d) collapse into the systematic danger I try to quarantine in (b) and (e), especially given that institutional acknowledgment in (e) might itself destabilize public reality? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify the least costly revision for each, and tell me which single change to my package would clean up the most conflicts without abandoning the Arendtian core that makes (a) central.
