I'm prepping questions for a podcast episode I'm recording next week — it's part of a small interview series I run for rationalist-adjacent friends, mostly about political philosophy that doesn't get aired properly. My guest is a philosopher (she'd prefer I don't name her here, but she's actually defending this view in print) who's been pushing something she calls axiological arbitrage: the idea that moral compromise between clashing value systems should be modelled like gains-from-trade, where each side gives up commitments they weight lightly to secure ones they weight heavily, so there's often a hidden Pareto frontier if you map the values precisely enough. She thinks the dirty-hands framing obscures this structural logic entirely. I was arguing about this with my flatmate last night and realised I need questions that actually press her, not just let her restate the thesis.

So I want about eight to ten interview questions, written as if I'm asking them live. I need at least one that drills into the incommensurability problem — if values are genuinely incommensurable, doesn't the 'trade' metaphor smuggle in a common metric? And I want a follow-up keyed to the answer she'll definatly give, which is that reflective weighting supplies the metric without full commensurability. Then I need one about dirty hands specifically: if a politician trades away anti-corruption norms to secure welfare gains, is that really Pareto-improving in moral space, or is it just corruption with extra steps? Include a follow-up for the likely reply that corruption is only a misweighted trade. I also want a question about rights and non-negotiable commitments — can there be arbitrage over rights, or does the model collapse there? And please, a final tough question asking what institutional or empirical evidence could actually count against the view, not just 'when wouldn't you apply it'.

Keep it casual, don't ask for citations or reading lists — she won't have them memorised and neither do I. Just make the questions sharp, in the weeds, and anticipate her standard moves.
