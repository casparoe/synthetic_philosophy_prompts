"De Finetti showed that if betting quotients violate the probability axioms, a bookie can guarantee loss—this is the synchronic Dutch book. With imprecise credences you have an interval, so on any single bet you pick the endpoint that avoids loss; hence standard books fail.

This was first shown by Ramsey in 1926. The fix is Jeffrey conditionalization: updating on a partition forces your intervals to collapse into one precise distribution, removing the ambiguity. So imprecise agents avoid Dutch books only if they never update, which is unrealistic. Therefore avoiding books requires precise credences plus Jeffrey updating as the enforcement mechanism."
