For a long-form feature in my science-and-ideas column, I am interviewing a philosopher of science—whom I will present to readers as an unnamed defender of a recently articulated hybrid stance—regarding the dispute between counterfactual and interventionist theories of causation. She holds that conventional interventionist frameworks, particularly those organized around structural equation models and the do-operator, collapse when applied to climate-scale systems in which the relevant manipulations are not merely impractical but physically impossible within the system’s own boundary conditions; in place of them she proposes what she calls graded modal counterfactuals, in which counterfactual dependencies are evaluated across a weighted spectrum of modal accessibility relations rather than at a single nearest possible world. At the same time, she rejects pure Lewis-style counterfactual analysis for macro-phenomena on the grounds that similarity metrics become irreducibly indeterminate at that scale.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that treat the substance of her view rather than asking her to summarize it. The questions should press precisely where the literature presses: whether her feasibility-weighted accessibility relations smuggle normative judgments about institutional capacity into an ostensibly descriptive framework; whether her rejection of similarity metrics for macro-systems undermines her own reliance on graded modal distance; and whether her framework actually delivers a different causal verdict from standard interventionism in a concrete case, such as attributing causation for the 2021 Pacific Northwest heat dome. For each question, briefly anticipate the reply she is most likely to give—for instance, that feasibility weighting tracks descriptive institutional constraints rather than moral desirability—followed immediately by a sharper follow-up that makes that defense harder to maintain. The tone should remain formal and suitable to a print feature, with no generic biographical inquiries and no requests for bibliographies or further reading.
