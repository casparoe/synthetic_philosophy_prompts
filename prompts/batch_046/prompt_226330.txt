I've been reading on my own about whether the divisions we call the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution correspond to anything real, or whether they are constructions that later writers imposed on the past. I want to explain this idea to my neighbor's teenager, who has just finished a standard world-history course and treats these three boxes as if they were as natural as seasons. I am asking for a teaching plan rather than the explanation itself: the exact order in which the components must be introduced and why one depends on another so that the question of invention feels meaningful rather than like a denial of obvious facts; the one concrete comparison or case that should carry the idea—perhaps one that contrasts how a political event and a technological change get assigned to different periods—together with what that comparison must include and what it must avoid; the misconceptions she will bring from school and the sequence in which she will raise them, with the precise point in the explanation where each should be defused; the check for understanding that would show she has actually grasped the concept rather than politely agreed; and what should be openly left out at this level with an honest statement of why. I am especially uncertain how to handle the notion I have encountered—that some scholars reject sharp breaks altogether and speak of a "long Renaissance" stretching well into the 1600s—without making the whole discussion sound like arbitrary expert disagreement.
