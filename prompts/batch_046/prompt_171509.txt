As a novelist drafting a manuscript on conceptual landscapes, I need a poem—sixteen lines, four quatrains, ABAB CDCD rhyme scheme—addressing the heterogeneity problem in hedonism: whether pleasures such as the sweetness of a ripe fig, the sudden clarity of solving a proof, and the warmth of being genuinely recognized share any common phenomenal quality, or whether pleasure is only a family resemblance. The first two quatrains should present these three experiences as irreducibly different territories. At the volta in line nine, a speaker should attempt to map them onto a single axis called hedonic tone, and the rhyme scheme should begin to fail—substituting slant rhymes or broken pairings—so that form enacts the collapse of reduction. The final quatrain must leave the taxonomy open, avoiding both sermon and resignation. Keep the voice restrained and exact; let the philosophical tension reside in the fractured rhyme rather than in explicit argument. End with a single sentence on its own line summarizing the poem's central, unresolved claim.
