I'm not a lawyer or anything, just someone who reads governence docs for fun, and I've noticed a weird pattern I can't find the right word for. In online moderation guidelines there's usually a clause saying these rules can be amended by a vote of the moderators, but that clause is itself part of the rules, so technically the moderators can vote to change how they vote on changes. To fix this they sometimes add a charter saying the amendment clause needs unanimous consent to change, except that charter is also inside the document, so now you need a charter for the charter, and they always just stop writing after three layers and pretend the problem is solved.

I found the same thing in old club bylaws from my grandfathers papers: a rule about changing rules, then a rule protecting that rule, then silence. Is there an established concept for this exact regress -- not just legal hierarchy or Russell's paradox in general, but the specific failure mode where each protective layer of authority is still subject to the authority it was meant to restrain? How does it differ from normal constitutional entrenchment, and if there's no precise term, can you name it, define its boundaries, and say whether a system that stops at a fixed number of layers has solved it or just hidden it?

Please answer as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion, and don't just hand me a bare label -- I want the carving, the edges, and what it isn't.
