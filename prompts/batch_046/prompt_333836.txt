As a novelist observing how societies institutionalize intimacy and labor, I am struck by a tripartite divergence in the governance of commercial sex. In Sweden and Norway, the state criminalizes the purchaser while decriminalizing the seller, pairing this with state-funded exit services yet providing no workplace-safety inspections for those who continue; in Germany and the Netherlands, the activity is legalized through licensing, health registration, and mandatory social-insurance contributions, but workers are typically classified as independent contractors without collective-bargaining rights or standard employment protections; in New Zealand, full decriminalization permits workers to seek redress through employment tribunals, to organize collectively, and to operate without special licensing, though no bespoke regulatory architecture governs brothel conditions. Consider this institutional triad not as a conventional policy comparison but as a philosophical artifact. What implicit theory of agency, harm, and the moral status of labor does each design presuppose? Does the Nordic model strictly require the view that supply-side agency is illusory or that the act is inherently harmful, or can it be reconciled with a control-based account of responsibility in which the state simply deprioritizes seller welfare? Does German legalization presuppose that harm is manageable through bureaucratic hygiene and contract law—thereby treating the work as morally neutral labor—or does the denial of employee status betray a residual normative judgment that such labor should not be fully normalized? And does New Zealand's framework presuppose a thoroughgoing value-neutrality about the activity, or does its reliance on generic labor law quietly depend on prior normative commitments about which vulnerabilities deserve institutional recognition? Reconstruct the implicit philosophical commitments of each regime, distinguish what each strictly requires from what it merely accommodates, and argue whether these three architectures are genuinely incommensurable or can be mapped onto a single continuum of views about autonomy and vulnerability.
