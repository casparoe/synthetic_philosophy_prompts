I was arguing with two friends after a seminer on comparative development — one’s a history grad student, the other does political theory — and they got into it about whether we can ever actually know if institutions or geography made some nations rich. (1) The historian said macrohistory is basically a single non-ergodic realization, so there are no genuine counterfactual worlds where Korea stays unified or the same valley gets different colonial rules; every so-called natural experiment is just storytelling with regression tables attached. (2) The poli sci friend replied that this is nihilism dressed up as rigor — we don’t need parallel universes, just exogenous variation like arbitrary colonial borders or the Nogales split, and difference-in-differences works fine if the treatment is institutionally sharp. (3) The historian came back that those borders aren’t exogenous at all because settlers brought institutional memory embedded in language and kin networks, so the treatment leaks into culture and geography at the same time; plus Korea was wrecked by war and superpower meddling, not just by institutions. (4) The poli sci friend said then you’ve set the bar at mechanism transparency, which is impossible in any macro-causal claim, and you’re really just protecting geographic determinism by demanding a purity standard you’d never apply to climate models. (5) The historian said no, her standard is counterfactual stability under mechanism unpacking — you need to show the causal pathway doesn’t loop back through the very variables you’re trying to isolate, otherwise you’re just picking the narrative that fits thier priors. They turned to me to break the tie. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and don’t restate the question.
