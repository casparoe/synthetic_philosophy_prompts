Question: Explain Einstein's hole argument and why he initially saw it as a threat to determinism in GR. How did he resolve the tension?

Student answer:
Einsteins hole argument looks at a region H with no matter inside — the hole. Outside H we have boundary conditions that should fix the metric inside via the field equations. But because the Einstein equations are generally covariant, if g_ab solves them inside H then so does φ*g_ab for any diffeomorphism φ that is identity outside H but shifts points inside. Both match on the boundary, so the same outside data yields two different metrics inside. Einstein read this as determinism failing: same causes, different effects inside the hole.

At first Einstein tried to block this by restricting coordinate conditions, but he dropped that. His actual resolution was the point-coincidence argument from around 1915: only actual intersections of world-lines are physically observable, so the metric assigned to a bare point inside H carries no physical content. This proves that spacetime points have real, independent existence but are only distinguishable by the fields on them — a substantivalist picture where points are real but need properties to be individuated. Once you see that, the two metrics are physically identical, so determinism is saved. Einstein also said this explains why the Cauchy problem in GR is fundamentally ill-posed unless you fix a gauge like harmonic coordinates, since otherwise the initial data dont determine a unique evolution.
