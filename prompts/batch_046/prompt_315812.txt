Write an essay on epistemic akrasia when your higher-order evidence targets the conceptual framework itself, not just your reasoning speed. The standard examples are about miscounting or being tired — you think "I'm wrong about the sum" but still beleive 13 + 15 = 28. I want you to go deeper: imagine you have strong second-order reasons to think that the very distinction between deterministic chaos and ontological randomness is a framing artifact from your education, not a real feature of dynamical systems. Still, you cant shake your first-order belief that the Lorenz attractor is genuinely deterministic. Is that rational akrasia, or is it something else — maybe conceptual inertia? Defend an actual verdict, dont end with "it depends." You should weigh whether rationality requires coherence between levels or just good first-order tracking.
