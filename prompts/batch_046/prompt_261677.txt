hey, retired biologist here, got into philosphy of mind after i left the lab. been reading about dennett's sphex wasp—the one that checks the burrow and if you move the prey she starts the loop all over. classic mechanical rigidity. i tried turning it into an argument about free will but it proves too much and i can't find the leak. here's the chain:

(1) a system is sphexish if it runs a fixed action sequence that can't be interrupted by context-sensitive feedback.
(2) free will is just the absence of sphexishness—the capacity to revise or halt an action when the situation changes.
(3) now take a scientist who aborts an experiment because she notices an anomoly. her brain updates its predicitive model and issues a stop command.
(4) but that update is fully caused by prior neural states plus sensory input; there's no gap where a non-physical self steps in.
(5) so her flexability is just a more complex sphexish program—feedback loops with extra branches, not a different kind of thing.
(6) if that's true for her, it's true for every human choice.
(7) therefore free will, understood as non-sphexish flexibility, doesn't exist in any physical system.
(8) but then the concept can't tell a wasp from a person, or ground moral responsibility, so it's useless.
(9) so the argument eliminates free will instead of explaining it, which feels wrong.

i think the slip is somewhere between (4) and (5)—maybe i'm mixing up causal determination with program rigidity?—but i can't name it. which step should i reject, and what does giving it up cost me? i'd like to hang onto (2) and (4) if possible.
