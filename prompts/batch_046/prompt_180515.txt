I have been working through Einstein's hole argument and its usual resolution: if a diffeomorphism fixes every point outside a region but permutes them inside, then g and its pullback agree on all observable relations, so they represent the same physical situation. The standard conclusion drawn is that manifold points lack primitive thisness—haecceities—while the manifold M itself stays in the ontology, with its points now individuated only relationally. This strikes me as an unstable halfway house. If the argument is genuinely meant to excise unobservable structure, why retain M at all? Should we not instead pursue a fully manifold-free formulation in which the metric field carries its own differential structure? Or does the need for local charts and smoothness conditions force us to keep M, even when its points are granted no independent physical significance?
