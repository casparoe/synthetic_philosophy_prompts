Consider a nested failure case: an object has a disposition D to manifest M in conditions C, but is wired to a fink mechanism that eliminates D precisely when C obtains; that mechanism is itself masked by an interfering substrate that prevents its operation, yet the substrate activates only if D is already present. This yields a dependency loop—the mask needs D, the fink needs the absence of the mask, and D is threatened only if both cooperate. What is your credence that the object genuinely possesses D? Decompose it: (a) the probability that finkishness and masking are conceptually compatible as simultaneous token failures rather than mutually exclusive categories; (b) the probability that the correct analysis of dispositions requires nearby worlds to contain genuinely unblocked manifestation conditions; (c) the probability that the mask's dependency on D breaks the symmetry required for either failure-mode to apply. Give each term a rough number with one line of justification, combine them into an overall credence, and identify which term dominates your uncertainty—specifically, what conceptual distinction (e.g., causal versus metaphysical modality, or token versus type-level analysis) would most shift your number.
