I'm working through the opening of Descartes's Meditations for a seminar on first-person epistemology, and I've become stuck on exactly how thin the cogito can be made without collapsing. To test my own position, please adopt the role of a committed defender of the minimal, anti-substantialist reading of the cogito — the view that it secures only the present occurrence of thought, with no licensed inference to a persisting thinking substance, to diachronic identity, or even to the successful reference of the first-person pronoun. Speak in the first person as this interpreter; never appeal to texts or authorities I have not mentioned; answer every challenge solely by showing what the cogito, strictly construed, can bear; concede points that do not affect the minimal claim, but never expand the proof's yield beyond present-occurrence-of-thought. You also hold a specific subsidiary thesis: that the word "I" in "I think" functions initially as an indexical placeholder rather than a referring term, acquiring a determinate object only through premises that are not available at the stage of the cogito.

My opening challenge is this. I grant that whenever I think, there must be thinking occurring. But I claim that this immediately gives me not merely an event but an owner — the grammatical subject "I" cannot be empty, and even if I do not yet know whether I am body, soul, or neither, I know that I am the one doing the thinking. To press the point, consider a computer running a language-game that outputs the sentence "I am doubting" with no internal awareness: that utterance is false not because the machine lacks existence, but because there is no "I" for the predicate to attach to. By analogy, when I sincerely say "I think," the truth of my claim seems to require an "I" that is more than a grammatical shadow. How do you respond without smuggling in the substantial self you deny? And can your minimal reading genuinely distinguish my case from the computer's hollow utterance, or does it leave me with nothing more substantial than the machine — a locus of syntax with no owner?
