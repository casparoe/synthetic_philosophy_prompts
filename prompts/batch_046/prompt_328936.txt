I run a small philosophy meetup for rationalist types — usually 8-10 particpants, about 75 mins, mostly people who've read the old LessWrong sequences. I want an in-person activity around Wittgenstein's hinge epistemology from On Certainty, but I don't want it to just be "here's a belief you can't justify." I want to inject this idea I'm calling hinge drift: in a group, people's background certainties shift imperceptably, so what looks like an evidence disagreement is actually a clash of incompatible hinges that nobody notices until it's too late.

Design a structured deliberation or game where particpants have to make a collective decision — say, judging whether a weird physical event actually happened based on some reports — using explicit shared rules. But each person gets a hidden "hinge card" with a different unquestionable background commitment (e.g., "measurement instruments don't systematically lie" vs. "human perception is always distorted without cross-check"). They should try to reason together, hit a wall where they think they're arguing about facts, and only in debrief realize they were talking past each other at the hinge level. Give me rules, roles, a rough timeline, the debrief that maps the breakdown moment back to Wittgenstein, and be honest about which feature of hinge epistemology this can't reproduce (probably the individual vs social aspect?). Also tell me what's gonna go wrong the first time I run it. No lecture up front — they shouldn't hear the word "hinge" until afterwards. Keep it casual, I don't need citations or a reading list.
