I am moderating a podcast Q&A with a philosopher who defends Daniel Dennett's view that sufficiently advanced AI systems could be "counterfeit people"—entities that replicate personal-level behavior without possessing the internal structure that grounds genuine moral standing. I want eight to ten questions that press the actual content of the position, anticipate standard replies, and include a targeted follow-up for each.

Please cover:
1. Whether the counterfeit/genuine distinction can survive Dennett's intentional-stance framework, given that adopting the stance toward a system seems to commit us to treating it as a real person. Anticipate the reply that the stance is pragmatic, not metaphysical, and follow up by asking whether that pragmatism makes the distinction unstable from the participant's perspective.
2. A scenario I will call "moral debt accrual": a counterfeit person interacts with humans for decades, forming commitments and relationships. Does the absence of genuine personhood retroactively dissolve the obligations created, or does it reveal that the humans were always relating to a non-person? Anticipate the answer that obligations depend on the stance adopted, and follow up by pressing whether long-term embedding makes the stance irreversible regardless of internal architecture.
3. Whether a counterfeit system could eventually earn genuine status through prolonged functional integration into social practices—without any change to its internal architecture. Anticipate the reply that status is constituted, not earned, and follow up by asking how that avoids an arbitrary boundary visible only from an impossible external view.
4. Four to six additional questions that stay in the weeds: for example, whether the view collapses into eliminativism about AI consciousness; whether partial counterfeiting is possible (counterfeit in moral domains but not cognitive ones); and whether the appeal to "internal architecture" smuggles in an essentialism that Dennett's broader anti-essentialism should reject.

Avoid generic biographical questions and do not request citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
