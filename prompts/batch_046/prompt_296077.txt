Please construct a diagnostic taxonomy, in the form of a decision tree, for an advanced philosophy seminar concerned with the varieties of free will worth wanting. Rather than mapping the familiar compatibilist, libertarian, and hard determinist positions, the instrument should sort views according to answers to three concrete conceptual commitments, phrased so that a respondent can answer them without knowing the corresponding philosophical labels.

The three branch nodes should be as follows. First, present a case of a fully deterministic yet highly self-modelling artificial system that predicts its own behavioural outputs in real time and revises them in accordance with internal normative constraints; ask whether this system exercises the kind of freedom that grounds appropriate moral praise or blame. Second, present a case of unavoidable but authorial harm: an agent deliberates with full care, acts on her best reasons, causes injury, and genuinely could not have done otherwise; ask whether intact authorship is sufficient for full moral responsibility despite the absence of alternative possibilities. Third, ask whether the primary value of free will resides in the openness of future alternatives or in the agent's authorship of the path actually taken.

From the logical combinations of answers, derive at least six leaf positions. Each leaf should carry a brief label (named if it corresponds to a recognisable view, otherwise unnamed) and a single sentence stating its core commitment. Identify at least one combination that remains logically possible but apparently unoccupied, and offer a brief diagnosis — is it incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored? Furthermore, flag any pair of leaves that the tree fails to separate in practice, explaining precisely why the distinction collapses.

Please present the taxonomy in formal British English, suitable for circulation as a seminar handout. Do not supply bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote any philosopher or text verbatim unless you have verified the exact wording from a fetched source; in all other cases paraphrase without quotation marks. Ensure that every case, distinction, and diagnostic question is fully self-contained, so that a reader without web access can understand and apply the instrument from the text alone.
