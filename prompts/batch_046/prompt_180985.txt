I'm writing my diss chapter on border ethics and I keep hitting the same frustrating loop with the compatriot-priority debate. Particularists say co-nationals are like family or club members with associative obligations that don't need further justification; cosmopolitans treat nationality like hair color, an arbitary fact that can't ground distributive priority. Every standard objection gets a standard reply - shared institutions, global poverty, round we go - and nobody ever actually moves.

My working theory is that this isn't really about justice at all but a hidden clash over whether moral obligations need some active co-authorship or shared institutional life to bind, versus just flowing from general vulnerability. I think particularists are secretly answering a question about democratic legitimation while framing it as welfare distribution, and cosmopolitans are smuggling in a metaphyscial claim about group agency that they never defend. But I might just be projecting my own confusion onto the literature.

Can you diagnose the actual structure of this stalmate? Is it verbal - are they using "special claim" in different registers? Are they answering different questions entirely? Or is there a buried empirical disagreement about whether modern states actually generate the thick shared life particularists need? I want to know what kind of disagreement this is, what taking each side seriously actually commits you to, and whether any conceptual distinction or institutional example could actually break it, or if the framing is just malformed and we should drop the question.
