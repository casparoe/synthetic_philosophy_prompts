I'm constructing a conceptual taxonomy for a seminar on Derek Parfit's teletransporter and the broader question of what actually matters in survival. Rather than sorting people by the labels they prefer, I want a decision tree that assigns positions based on concrete judgments about modified replication cases.

Please build a decision tree with exactly six branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete yes/no or either/or question that a respondent could answer without knowing any theory's name. Begin with the baseline teletransporter: your original body is destroyed and an exact psychological replica is created elsewhere. Then introduce two nonstandard variants that inject new distinctions into the logical space.

First, the "fragmented relay" case: your psychological profile is divided into three recipients, each receiving a causally continuous but non-overlapping third of your memories, traits, and intentions; the original is destroyed. Ask whether any recipient counts as you, and if so, whether survival can be divided among them.

Second, the "affective drift" case: the replica is psychologically continuous in every structural respect except that its emotional valences are systematically inverted—what you loved, it loathes—while your original body survives in a permanent coma with unchanged affect. Ask whether continuity without affective alignment counts as survival, and whether the coma-bound original retains any priority.

The tree's leaves must include at least these positions: strict psychological continuity with the no-branching clause; pure animalism; a scalar or weighted-survival view; and a deflationary "no further fact" position. For every combination of answers that no named position occupies, provide a brief diagnosis—whether it seems incoherent, merely unattractive, or genuinely unexplored.

Do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading. Keep the entire taxonomy—node questions, branch logic, leaf descriptions, and notes on unoccupied combinations—to no more than 400 words. Present it in a clear nested or outline format.
