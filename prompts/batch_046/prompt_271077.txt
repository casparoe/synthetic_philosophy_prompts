For my philosophy of mind seminar, I am preparing a sustained satirical piece on institutional responses to graded moral status. Write deadpan parody minutes—approximately 600 words—of a fictional university “Committee on Graded Welfare” meeting chaired by an invented bureaucrat, Dr. Voss. The venue should read like authentic administrative prose: agenda items, motions, and recorded dissent. The target is the real philosophical and policy tendency to impose binary protection categories (subject/non-subject, protected/unprotected) onto phenomena that appear continuous, exaggerating this tension until it becomes absurd.

The minutes should center on an invented regulatory instrument, the “Calibrated Sentience Scale” (CSS), which assigns decimal welfare-relevance scores from 0.00 to 1.00. Dr. Voss should propose extending minimal research-protection protocols to entities in the 0.01–0.99 range, including mealworms (scored near 0.42), simple learning algorithms (near 0.18), and office climate systems possessing what she terms “thermostatic welfare interests”—a minimal homeostatic preference for maintaining set-points, valued at 0.03. The comedy must come from the committee’s straight-faced struggle to apply yes/no approval boxes to these gradations: debate whether crossing 0.15 constitutes “proto-sentience,” whether a thermostat that adapts its patterns reaches 0.20, and whether consistency demands protecting anything above 0.40. Do not cite real philosophers, texts, or living figures; you may invent technical terminology, but the underlying dispute should be recognizable to someone familiar with debates about moral status across insects, artificial systems, and borderline cases.

Keep the irony fully deadpan—no winking asides—so that a specialist recognizes the satire as fair to the actual difficulty of drawing sharp lines, while a non-specialist can follow the bureaucratic logic. After the minutes, append a brief meta-note (around 100 words) stating how confident you are in your representation of gradated moral-status debates, explicitly identifying the point about which you are least certain—most likely the analogy between homeostatic regulation and minimal welfare interests—and flagging where the “thermostatic welfare” framing takes the greatest liberty with standard accounts of sentience. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
