Propose a philosophical method: analyze the conditions under which psychological aggregation is legitimate, compare the normative logic of happiness rankings with other composite social indicators, and determine whether the two measurement tracks yield compatible policy prescriptions or pull in opposite directions. Specify what success looks like—e.g., a principled distinction between optimizing for evaluation versus optimizing for experience, with a criterion for when they conflict irreducibly—versus failure—e.g., being unable to show the tension is conceptual rather than merely practical. Keep the scope realistic for one semester, with no empirical data collection and no citation-based literature review; references will be added separately.
