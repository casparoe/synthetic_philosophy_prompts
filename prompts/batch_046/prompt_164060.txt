Write a poem of seven couplets—fourteen lines, loose iambic rhythm but no enforced meter—on the persistence of a single flame across successive moments. Structure each couplet around one mode of Jain syādvāda, the seven-fold predication (saptabhaṅgī): (1) in one respect it is; (2) in one respect it is not; (3) in one respect it both is and is not; (4) in one respect it is inexpressible; (5) it is and inexpressible; (6) it is not and inexpressible; (7) it is, is not, and inexpressible. The rhyme scheme should link the first three couplets across stanzas (A, B, C echoing one another) to build logical tension, then fray in the fourth and fifth, and dissolve entirely in the sixth and seventh so that formal coherence collapses as the modes absorb avaktavya. The image of the flame should do concrete work—wick, oxygen, heat—so the poem is not a schematic list. Render the philosophical sequence accurately enough that a reader familiar with anekāntavāda recognizes it, yet let the poem stand alone as a meditation on impermanence. Do not resolve the tension; let the seventh couplet hang without synthesis.
