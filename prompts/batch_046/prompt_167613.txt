Design a thought experiment comparing two artificial cognitive systems, Alpha and Beta, built with present-day or near-future engineering. Both implement identical information-processing architectures: they take sensory inputs, maintain recurrent internal states, integrate information across modules, and produce behaviorally indistinguishable outputs, including reports of subjective experience. The only difference is structural: Alpha's processing occurs through physically discrete, locally bounded computational steps with explicit state registers and clock-synchronized transitions, all contained within a single enclosed substrate. Beta implements the exact same functional organization—same state-transition graph, same integration dynamics—but through a continuous, analog physical process distributed across an unbounded substrate with no discrete state boundaries or localized causal steps; its "computational" structure is identifiable only via an external mathematical interpretation of its continuous dynamics. Constraints: avoid science-fictional brain uploads, quantum consciousness, or references to specific named AI systems; describe the scenario in one paragraph. Then deliver the analysis: first, state the verdict of a functionalist view that grounds moral consideration in the presence of specific organizational properties (recurrent integration, global availability of information) and explain why it treats Alpha and Beta identically. Second, state the verdict of an intrinsic-computation view that holds computation must be physically discrete and causally self-contained within bounded mechanisms, and explain why it might grant moral status to Alpha but deny it to Beta. Third, identify the precise feature—discrete localized causation versus continuous distributed dynamics—that drives the split. Fourth, describe what happens if that feature is neutralized (for instance, if Alpha is split across physically separated chips with no direct causal interaction between steps, so its discreteness remains but its local causal containment is broken). Finally, present the functionalist's best objection: that the intrinsic-computation view mistakes an implementation-level property for the organizational feature that actually explains why we care about a system's mental life, and note whether removing the feature collapses the case into a standard disagreement about substrate independence.
