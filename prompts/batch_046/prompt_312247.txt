Analyze the post-war exposure and trial of Han van Meegeren—specifically concerning the forged Vermeer recovered from Hermann Göring’s collection—through the lens of what we might call the double-authentication problem: expert connoisseurship validates a painting by detecting properties (brushwork, pigment aging, compositional logic) that a skilled forger can deliberately replicate, which raises the question of whether those detected properties are genuinely value-conferring or merely diagnostic of origin. First, distinguish and assess whether the revelation that the work was executed by van Meegeren in the 1930s rather than by Vermeer in the seventeenth century undermined its aesthetic value, its historical value, its market value, or some separable combination of these, and say whether the dimensions can be pulled apart or necessarily collapse together. Second, use the episode to test source-dependent theories of artistic value—which hold that a work’s origin in a particular creative act is partly constitutive of its value—against object-dependent theories—which hold that value supervenes only on perceptible and structural properties accessible to the viewer; be precise about which features of the forgery each theory predicts should matter, and identify any feature the episode reveals that neither theory clearly anticipates. Third, address the demonstration in which van Meegeren reproduced a Vermeer-style painting under observation to establish his authorship: what does this show about whether artistic skill is fungible across historical contexts, and does it support or undermine the idea—often associated with Benjamin’s concept of aura—that there is an irreproducible residue of originality that replication cannot capture? Fourth, consider the institutional aftermath: the works were initially expelled from museums and devalued, yet some later entered collections as examples of van Meegeren’s own achievement. Does this re-attribution resolve the philosophical tension, or does it instead reveal that artistic value is ultimately governed by institutional framing rather than by intrinsic properties of the object? Throughout, maintain a disciplined mapping—identify who counts as the relevant agent or institution, what counts as the evidence of value, and where the theoretical framework is genuinely silent rather than being forced to speak; do not invent a resolution where the concepts underdetermine the case.
