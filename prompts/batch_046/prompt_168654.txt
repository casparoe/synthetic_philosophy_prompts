me and my roommate had this long arguement last night about whether consciousness is really just a user illusion like Dennett claims, and we kept circling back to that old thought experiment where you copy Einstein's brain state-for-state into a massive book — page after page of neuron patterns — and ask if the book is now Einstein. i noticed this weird move that keeps happening whenever people bring it up. they start by saying well if consciousness is just information processing then this book should be conscious, which is obviously absurd, so functionalism is wrong. but then when you say okay what if the book is being read and updated in real time by some mechanism, they suddenly shift and say no the problem is the book isn't causally interacting with the world, or it lacks temporal dynamics. but that wasn't the original point — the original punch was that a static encoding can't be a mind, and now there defending it with missing dynamics that they could have added without changing the paper substrate at all. it's like they're using the frozen snapshot to do all the intutive work, then smuggling in a hidden premise about running processes that wasn't in the initial claim.

is there an established name for this exact slide? i know it's in the neighborhood of Searle's chinese room and that blockhead lookup-table thing by Ned Block, but those feel different — the room is about syntax vs semantics, and blockhead is about faking behavior with a giant table. this is specifically about taking a fully specified dynamic mind, freezing it into a static representation, and treating the staticness as an automatic disqualifier for consciousness, even though the theory being attacked is about functional organization not about being live in time. if there's no precise term, can we coin one — maybe something like the static-encoding intuition — and define its boundaries so it doesn't just collapse into generic anti-functionalist gut feelings? i want to know what exactly this move is, what it isn't, and whether it actually hits functionalism or just hits a strawman that forgot about time.
