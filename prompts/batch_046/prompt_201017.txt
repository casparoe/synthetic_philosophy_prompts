I am working through a directed study on psychiatric taxonomy with a philosopher of medicine whose judgments about Ian Hacking's looping effects I am trying to formalize. Over the past month she has delivered consistent but unarticulated rulings on five diagnostic categories: she maintains that the pre-1973 psychiatric classification of homosexuality produced strong first-order looping, since the clinical label altered self-interpretation and behavior in ways that made the category self-confirming; she judges dissociative identity disorder to involve what she terms narrative scaffolding, whereby the diagnostic framework supplies a script that organizes diffuse symptoms into a coherent clinical picture; she denies that schizophrenia exhibits meaningful looping, treating it as a human-independent kind whose core phenomenology remains stable regardless of how clinicians classify it; she allows only weak looping for autism spectrum disorder, specifically identity consolidation, where diagnosis offers a social category that shapes self-presentation without altering underlying cognitive architecture; and she is ambivalent about anorexia nervosa, arguing that cultural feedback loops operate independently of the clinical taxonomy rather than through it. Next, we are examining a proposed but not officially adopted construct—call it sustained non-digital withdrawal syndrome, characterized by persistent avoidance of synchronous, unmediated social interaction, with proposed diagnostic criteria that include preferences for asynchronous communication and aversion to unstructured gatherings—behaviors that are already prevalent in certain subcultures and that might become more pronounced or self-conscious once medicalized.

Predict whether she would classify this proposed category as exhibiting looping, and if so, whether she would place it under first-order looping, narrative scaffolding, identity consolidation, or a different mechanism altogether. More importantly, lay out at least two competing principles that could rationalize her five previous verdicts—one that emphasizes the malleability of the underlying condition in response to classification, and another that emphasizes whether the taxonomy introduces new explanatory or narrative resources for the subject. For each principle, state what it predicts about the proposed syndrome. Then, identify the specific further case or conceptual distinction—perhaps involving a condition with ambiguous biological markers but strong institutional framing—that would best reveal which principle she is actually running, and explain why her existing five rulings do not already settle the matter. If you believe her framework contains an internal tension rather than mere ambiguity, articulate the contradiction precisely.
