I sit on the university's reparative justice review board. We have a petition from descendents of families displaced by the 1930s land grab that funded our east campus — they want the old benifactor hall turned into a community archive plus a direct tuition remmission fund for verified descendants. Our charter lets us recommend 'material acknowledgment' but doesn't specify whether that should be symbolic or compensatory. There's this philosophical knot I can't shake: if the harm is a wrong to specific lineages, then genealogical proof is required and the remedy should go only to them; but if it's structural, then the remedy should reach the broader community regardless of bloodline. I need a short memo for Friday's meeting recommending one concrete package — either the archive with the tuition fund, or the archive paired with a community investment pool instead — with a clear timeline. Please take seriously the strongest counter-argument, which is that direct cash-like compensation commodifies suffering and actually betrays the moral logic of reparations. Also engage whether our obligation is internal — because we inherited the wrong — or external — just present distributive justice — since that distinction seems to collapse here but I want you to show why. Don't just list legal precedents; I want reasoning I can circulate.
