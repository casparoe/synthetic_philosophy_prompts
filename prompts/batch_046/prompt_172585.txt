Hey, I'm a retired marine biologist — thirty years studying plankton dynamics, don't ask — and lately I've been poking around philosophy of economics for fun, especially Amartya Sen's work on famines. There's this argumentative move I keep seeing that I can't put my finger on, and I'm hoping someone here knows if it's already named.

The pattern goes like this. Someone starts off saying the Bengal famine of 1943 was basically a natural disaster — rice crop failed, people starved, end of story. Then you point out that food availability per capita was actually okay, and that British wartime priorities and distribution failures are what did the damage. Instead of dropping the crop failure, they downgrade it: okay, it was a contributing factor, it kicked off the chain. Then you show that other regions with worse harvests didn't have famine at all, and they shrink it further — now it's just a contextual trigger, or a factor that only mattered because of the goverment failures, but they still won't let it leave the causal picture entirely. It's explanatory weight gets closer and closer to zero, but it's never explicitly removed. I see the same occurence of asymptotic retreat with talk about the Irish famine and the Great Leap Forward; there's always some natural element that gets preserved as a thinner and thinner slice of the explanation, rather than being cleanly rejected.

Is there an established concept for this exact thing — the defensive downgrading of a natural-cause claim by repeatedly reducing it's weight without ever abandoning it? I know about auxiliary hypotheses and maybe some Popperian immunization strategies, but this feels different because it's not about saving a whole theory; it's about keeping one specific ontological category (natural vs political) in the story by letting its role go asymptotically to zero. Does it have a name, and how does it relate to Sen's view that famines are essentially entitlement failures rather than food-availability declines? If there's no term, what should I call the line between honest multi-causal explanation and this kind of asymptotic retreat?
