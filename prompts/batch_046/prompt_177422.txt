I've been trying to figure out if Jaspers was right that there's political guilt but not moral guilt for a nation, and the debate just stalls every time. One camp says guilt needs a single conscience that feels remorse, so collective guilt is literally impossible. The other camp says if citizens inherit institutions that committed crimes and still benefit, then guilt attaches structurally, not psychologically. They agree on all the history, but never budge. Is this a verbal dispute about 'guilt' vs 'responsibility', or is the real split about whether moral agency requires first-personal experience? There's also this odd parallel with corporate liability -- we fine companies without anyone feeling shame -- but people reject that analogy for nations without explaining why. What's the actual blockage: missing conceptual distinction, or bedrock intuitons about personhood? And is there anything -- a formal argument, a new case study, whatever -- that could actually shift anyone's view, or is this just going to stay frozen?
