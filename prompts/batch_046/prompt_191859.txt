I just finished a long, exhausting argument with a friend about whether Buddhist ethics is best reconstructed as consequentialist. I was defending the view that cetana—intention or volition—makes Buddhist ethics irreducibly agent-relative and resistant to outcome-aggregation, while my friend insisted that upaya (skillful means) and the Bodhisattva's universal commitment to reduce suffering make it a form of mental-state-constrained consequentialism. Rather than keep circling, I want you to draft the adversarial-collaboration document we should have produced: a joint statement that both a committed cetana-primacy theorist and a committed upaya-consequentialist could sign.

Define the two camps precisely:
Position A (Cetana-primacy): The ethical quality of an action is fixed primarily by the volitional state at its origin, not by its results. Karmic result (vipaka) follows from intention but does not retroactively determine ethical value. Abhidharma psychology describes this mechanism, and the framework is agent-relative: the same external outcome carries different karmic weight depending on the mental states involved. The Bodhisattva vow is an intensification of personal mental purification, not a switch to agent-neutral outcome-maximization.
Position B (Upaya-consequentialist): In Mahayana ethics, upaya requires evaluating actions by their expected effect on suffering and awakening across all beings. The four Brahmaviharas are agent-neutral in scope. Abhidharma intention-analysis describes the mechanics of karma but does not supply the normative framework, which is consequentialist with mental-state side-constraints: a harmful act can be ethically required if it minimizes aggregate suffering and is performed with pure compassion.

Your task: Draft the document both sides could put their names to. It must include the following, in this order and approximate length:
Joint Statement Preamble (about 150 words): The purpose of the collaboration, the shared commitment to avoid straw-manning, and the agreement that part of the dispute may be verbal.
Section I: Agreed Claims (about 300 words, numbered): At least six claims both would accept. These must cover: what the relevant texts actually say about the relation between intention and outcome; the formal structure of the Bodhisattva vow; what no-go arguments (if any) actually rule out; where the conventional/ultimate truth distinction applies and where it does not apply to ethical evaluation; and at least one explicit resolution of an apparent verbal disagreement.
Section II: Residual Disagreements (table format, about 250 words): The precise formulations of where they still differ, each phrased so its own side accepts the wording. Include disagreements about (i) whether agent-neutrality is required by the scope of compassion, (ii) whether mental-state purity can ever be traded against outcome-improvement, and (iii) the normative status of Abhidharma psychology versus Mahayana upaya doctrine.
Section III: The Cruxes and Predictive Tests (about 350 words): Identify the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. You must include the crux of whether there is an "asymmetry of mental-causal efficacy"—that is, whether mental states have direct causal efficacy on ethical value independent of their behavioral outcomes, and whether this asymmetry is a feature of the ethics or merely a feature of karma's psychology. For each crux, provide a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Use at least one case involving a Bodhisattva who lies or harms to prevent greater suffering, and one involving a meditator whose mental purification produces accidental harm through inaction.
Section IV: Assessment of Confidence (about 100 words): State how confident you are in the claims made in the statement, single out the one point you are least sure of, and briefly say why that point is the weakest link.

Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke obscure theorists or texts that a well-trained model would not reliably recall. Write in formal, polished prose suitable for publication by both parties.
