I organize a discussion circle at a public library for about twelve adults with no formal philosophy training; we meet for ninety minutes every other Thursday. Over the past months I have been thinking on my own about moral worth—specifically, the claim that an action only carries full moral value when done for the right reason, and that doing it for expected praise or hidden reward diminishes or cancels that value. I want an activity for our next session that lets the participants feel this tension themselves rather than hear it explained in a lecture. To keep the idea from being too familiar, I would like the design to treat the "independence" of the right reason from anticipated social payoff as something like a hidden structural condition: the rules should make participants discover whether their choice was genuinely driven by the reason they claim, or whether it was shaped by a payoff they did not initially see.

Please design a runnable game or structured role-play for this setting: state the precise idea participants should encounter, specify the group size and time, lay out the rules and any small real stakes, describe how the activity unfolds step by step, and provide a debrief that maps what happened onto parts of the philosophical problem. The debrief should say what will predictably go wrong when I run it and how to handle it, and should be honest about which aspect of moral worth the activity cannot reproduce and I must supply. Write your response in American English, in a polished and formal style appropriate to someone presenting a plan. I am not an academic—only a self-directed reader—so please avoid unexplained technical jargon. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I will add any references myself.
