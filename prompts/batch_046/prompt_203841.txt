Write a one-page research proposal for a six-month independent philosophical project investigating emotional states, framed for a small-grant or dissertation-prospectus review. The central question must be precisely stated and must fall in the gap left by standard debates: ask whether emotional transparency—the degree to which a subject experiences an emotional state as immediately given rather than as the terminus of an evaluative inference—is a constitutive feature of emotional kinds, not merely a variable mode of access. Specifically, propose examining whether emotions should be partly taxonomized by transparency profiles (for instance, comparing grief, which tends toward high transparency, with moral indignation, which tends toward relative opacity and judgment-like structure) and whether this reclassification resolves, dissolves, or reshuffles the familiar trichotomy of judgment theories, perceptual theories, and raw-feeling accounts.

Your proposal should contain the following elements. First, state the research question exactly and explain why existing positions—appraisal-based cognitivist accounts, embodied feeling theories, and value-perception models—leave it open rather than answering it implicitly. Second, lay out your methodological approach: defend a hybrid strategy that combines first-person conceptual analysis of transparency reports with a functional analysis of emotional regulation, and explain why this is preferable to the two obvious alternatives (pure phenomenological description on one side and empirical neuroscience mapping emotional categories to brain states on the other). Third, specify what a successful result looks like—such as a revised taxonomy with explanatory power for cases of emotional dysfunction or misattribution—and define failure conditions, for example if transparency collapses into emotional intensity or subjective confidence without independent taxonomic work. Fourth, outline a realistic six-month scope and concrete deliverables suitable for solo conceptual work, noting that the output should be usable for clinicians or interaction designers who need normative distinctions but do not require laboratory data. Fifth, include a brief justification for funding conceptual rather than empirical research in this area, addressing the objection that philosophy alone cannot advance emotion science. Do not request or include a bibliography, citation list, or named literature review; refer to theoretical families in general terms only, and indicate that specific references will be integrated in the full study. Maintain a formal, polished tone appropriate for an academic proposal, using American English throughout.
