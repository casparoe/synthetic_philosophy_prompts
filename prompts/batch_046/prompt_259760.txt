I serve on the acquisitions panel of a sound-art archive that selects works according to a strict metaphysical criterion drawn from Arthur Schopenhauer: a piece must operate as an immediate objectification of the Will itself, not as a mediated representation of Platonic Ideas or as an accessory to another art form. Our submissions range from long-form drone installations to narrative audio-drama, and the panel — composed largely of sound engineers and curators without philosophical training — must reach a decision within fifteen minutes per work. I therefore need a reusable decision procedure, expressed as an ordered checklist of observable tests, that translates Schopenhauer's account in The World as Will and Representation — specifically his claim that music copies not Ideas but the Will directly, his correlation of bass, harmony and melody to ascending grades of objectification, and his suspicion of music subordinated to words or dramatic action — into practical criteria a reviewer can apply without consulting texts. The procedure must preserve the conceptual distinctions that matter, drop those that cannot be observed in a score or recording, and flag explicitly the points where judgement cannot be eliminated.

The checklist should contain no more than four sequential tests, each stating what feature to examine (for example, the independence of melodic line from textual narrative, or the presence of harmonic progression that mirrors graded objectification rather than mere formal patterning), what result indicates inclusion, and what result sends the reviewer to the next test or to a judgement call. You must include three worked applications: first, a forty-minute microtonal drone lacking melody, rhythm, or harmonic change; second, an opera excerpt in which the orchestral writing expresses striving independently while the libretto recounts a heroic narrative; and third, a piece of algorithmically generated ambient music whose harmonic movements are produced by random-walk processes with no compositional intention behind them. One of these cases should yield a verdict that the committee will likely find unwelcome. The procedure must also state honestly what it is unable to do — for instance, whether it can distinguish a genuine metaphysical disclosure from a technically identical but conceptually empty simulation — and it should identify the hard cases where two reviewers might reasonably disagree despite following the same steps. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not ask for verification of specific passages; and write the response in British English.
