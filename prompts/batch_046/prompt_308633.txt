There's this pattern in museum cataloging for ancient artifacts — if the provenance chain is broken they don't just list origin as unknown, they actively suppress even pretty solid scholarly hypotheses in the public database, marking everything 'unattested' and leaving display cards blank. But the same museum will still tell visitors a reconstructed narrative about the object's ritual use based on stylistic parallels, which is also mostly inference. What philosophical commitments about knowing the distant past does that split presuppose? Is source criticism — Herodotus checking testimony, Thucydides throwing out hearsay — being treated as something that applies to written texts but not to objects? Or is the idea that institutional authority requires an epistemic purity individual scholars don't need? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say if the practice really needs it or is compatable with other views, and tell me what a policy that actually embraced probabilistic attribution would have to change.
