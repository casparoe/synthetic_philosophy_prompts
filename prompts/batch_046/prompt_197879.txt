Construct a taxonomy, in the form of a branching decision tree, that sorts philosophical positions on whether an advance directive retains binding authority over a cognitively transformed later self—specifically in progressive dementia or severe acquired brain injury, where the earlier self recorded explicit treatment refusals and the later self appears content and expresses sustained contrary preferences. Structure the tree from the logical space outward rather than from a list of named theorists: begin with whether diachronic personal identity survives the cognitive transformation; then branch by the primary source of normative authority (pure autonomy, present welfare, relational continuity, or a hybrid diachronic-consent model); then by whether narrative coherence or the naturalness of the transformation carries independent normative weight; only then locate the actual positions at the leaves. Every internal node must be phrased as a concrete, answerable question—e.g., if the later self consistently and calmly refuses the treatment the directive mandates, does that refusal possess any moral weight of its own?—so that a respondent can navigate the tree without prior knowledge of the labels. At each leaf, state the position's conventional or provisional name, a one-sentence summary, and its most awkward practical commitment (for instance, that it must treat a contented dementia patient as a normative stranger, or that it permits overriding an explicit refusal on grounds of present happiness). Explicitly include a branch separating views that accept narrative discontinuity together with retained moral status from those that deny one or both of those claims, and identify at least two answer-combinations that the logical space permits but that appear unoccupied, briefly diagnosing whether each gap stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Finally, flag any pair of leaves—such as continuity-based autonomy accounts and certain relational-stewardship views—that the tree cannot cleanly separate on practical outcomes alone, clarifying whether they diverge only in metaphysical justification or in hidden normative premises.
