ok so i've been trying to square my view on legal self-reference and i'm not sure it hangs togther. here's roughly where i've landed:

1. i think a legal system can contain a fully self-refferential rule of change—one that lists how to amend every rule including itself—with no higher authority backing it, just internal acceptance (hart-style rule of recognition vibe).

2. i also think that if you actually use that clause to repeal the amendment mechanism itself, the repeal is legally valid. there's no hidden "eternity clause" protecting it.

3. but i also think interpretation should include an anti-paradox canon: if applying a rule literally produces a contradiction in the system's norms, reinterpret to avoid it rather than appeal outside the system.

4. and i still think that even after such a self-repeal, what's left isn't just Nomic-style chaos—it's still law, just with no positive rules left, separated from a game by social weight not structural limits.

5. acutally i think the only real difference between real legal self-amendment and Suber's Nomic is the practical/social acceptance people give the rules, not any built-in logical ceiling.

do these hang together? specifically, doesn't (2) run straight into (3)—the self-repeal seems to generate exactly the kind of normative continuity paradox that (3) is meant to block? and is (4) just saving the phenomenon by redefining "law" to cover the vacuum, or is there a real consistency there? order the tensions by severity, say what'd have to give in each case, and flag any pairs that only look bad but actually dissolve. don't tell me which commitment to drop—i just want the audit.
