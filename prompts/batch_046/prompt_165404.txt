I'm trying to pin down a pattern I've noticed in descriptions of split-brain experiments—particularly the classic configurations where the right hemisphere initiates an action (for instance, selecting an image with the left hand in response to a cue shown only to the left visual field) and the left hemisphere, with no access to that cue, nonetheless produces a fluent explanation that treats the action as part of a coherent, pre-existing intention. The pattern I'm after is more specific than generic confabulation: it's the way the explanation doesn't merely invent a reason ex nihilo, but seems to "graft" the right-hemisphere behavior onto the left hemisphere's ongoing narrative thread, as if the unified agent had always intended that outcome, even though the left hemisphere had neither sensory access to the triggering stimulus nor introspective access to the right hemisphere's processing. It's also not quite Gazzaniga's left-hemisphere interpreter in the standard characterization, because the interpreter is usually framed as generating explanations for isolated actions; here the explanation retroactively integrates the alien action into a continuous story that preserves the illusion of unified agency across time, not just moment-to-moment rationalization.

Does this specific configuration—retroactive narrative integration of alien hemispheric actions into a continuous self-model—have an established name in the split-brain or consciousness literature? How does it differ precisely from (a) ordinary post-hoc confabulation, (b) the left-hemisphere interpreter as typically described, and (c) the broader "disunity of consciousness" thesis? If there is no dedicated term, I'd like a coined label together with a clear boundary: under what conditions does this grafting occur versus fail (for example, when the left hemisphere detects conflict rather than smoothing it over), and what would count as a genuine counter-example in which the left hemisphere acknowledges the action as foreign rather than integrating it into its narrative?
