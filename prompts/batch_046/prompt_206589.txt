I defend a gradualist account of moral status according to which sentience is not binary but distributed across a "threshold-plateau-exponential" curve. On this model, entities lacking minimal information-integration—such as thermostats or simple reflex arcs—fall below a zero-status threshold; entities in an intermediate band, roughly spanning insects through many fish, possess a flat, low-level direct moral status that does not vary with minor differences in neural complexity; and entities above a higher threshold, including most mammals and certain cephalopods, enter an exponential scaling region where capacities for suffering and self-awareness generate rapidly increasing obligations, though without an absolute ceiling. This entails that the moral gap between a human being and a pig is far larger than that between a pig and a colony of bees, yet that the bees are not merely instrumental but bearers of weak direct duties. It also denies any sharp metaphysical boundary at which full personhood suddenly switches on.

What would genuinely count against this position? I want an honest map of its exposure: which empirical discoveries in comparative neuroscience, which conceptual objections about arbitrariness in placing the thresholds, and which normative conflicts—for instance, with the conviction that all cognitively able human beings possess equal basic rights regardless of variation in awareness—would be decisive rather than merely awkward. Please distinguish the defeaters that a committed defender of the model could concede without abandoning gradualism from those that would force its abandonment altogether. Is the view vulnerable to being qualified into unfalsifiability—say, by shifting the plateaus post hoc whenever new data appear—and if so, is that insulation a genuine weakness for a normative theory of this kind? I am especially keen to know whether a robust argument for the equal moral standing of all human beings, irrespective of cognitive gradation, necessarily undermines the exponential scaling claim above the human threshold, or whether the two commitments can be coherently separated.
