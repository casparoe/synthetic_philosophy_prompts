I've been drafting this novel where the protagonist is a composer who gets obsessed with Schopenhaur's claim that music isn't representation like painting but direct objectification of the Will—bass being the inorganic ground, melody being conscious life reaching upward, all that. Anyway I was at a diner with my friends Mara and Leo, and we got into whether that view actually holds water or if it's just romantic fluff. Mara went first: she said it's dead on arrival because music is clearly culturally constructed—the same interval sounds mournful in one tradition and triumphant in another, plus there's that phychoacoustic effect where your brain invents a missing fundamental that isn't physically vibrating, so pitch is partly wired in the ear, not revealed by some metaphysical Will. Leo pushed back that Schopenhaur never promised culture-free universality; he said different arts objectify the Will at different grades of clarity, so bass is low-grade inorganic stuff and melody is high-grade consciousness. Leo argued the missing fundamental is actually perfect for this—it's the Will expressing itself at that lower, pre-conscious grade before the melody takes over. Mara said that's an unfalsifiable rescue: if every counterexample just becomes another 'grade,' the theory risks nothing and predicts nothing. Then Leo claimed Schopenhaur actually does risk something specific—he insists bass is the necessary ground, but in spectral music composers build stuctures out of overtones and literally erase the fundamental, which should be impossible if the inorganic base can't vanish. Mara countered that spectralism is just a niche avant-garde stunt, not a real challenge to how music generally functions, and that Leo was treating an edge case like a knockout punch. They went back and forth three times and then turned to me because they know I'm writing about this stuff. I'm just the novelist stealing dialogue, but I genuinely want the adjudication: who's right about which specific point, and what are both of them missing? Don't need citations or a reading list—just the honest point-by-point breakdown.
