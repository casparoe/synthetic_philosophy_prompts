I'm working through my intuitions about original versus derived intentionality for a philosophy of mind seminar, particularly against the background of Searle's claim that only biological brains generate intrinsic aboutness and Dennett's reply that even minimal interpretable machinery possesses real, if stance-dependent, intentionality. Here are my verdicts on concrete cases:

- A wall thermostat tracking room temperature: no original intentionality; any goal-directedness is purely derived from designers and users.
- A chess program selecting moves: derived intentionality at most, assigned by programmers and interpreters.
- A human being wanting coffee: original intentionality, intrinsic and not interpreter-dependent.
- A wild bird's alarm call warning of a hawk: original intentionality; not assigned by anyone.
- A cluster of human neurons grown in vitro, never part of a living organism, chemically wired to generate response patterns isomorphic to mammalian fear-states when exposed to toxins: I think this has original intentionality, because living neural tissue generates intrinsic aboutness even outside a body.
- A printed thermo-state book made of paper and temperature-sensitive ink, arranged so that as heat shifts the visible text traces the exact same state-transition graph as the cluster above, with no electronics or biology involved: I say this lacks original intentionality entirely; a scientist might derive representational content from it, but none is intrinsic.
- A silicon chip that replicates the cluster's dynamics neuron-for-neuron: likewise no original intentionality.
- Dennett's minimal two-state tracking device, the kind of simple system he uses to show that interpretation applies even to very basic machinery: no original intentionality, but genuinely intentional in a derived, stance-dependent way that is not merely metaphorical.

The tension is that cases five through seven seem to make original intentionality depend on biological substrate, yet case five is engineered and disembodied, which suggests I am not consistently applying an organism-based condition. Meanwhile, my sympathy for Dennett makes me want to treat case five as just a more complex version of case eight, in which case I should deny original intentionality there too.

Is there a coherent principle, or a small family of candidates, that delivers all eight verdicts? If not, which verdict should I overturn and what does that cost? Please state any principle precisely, identifying the clause that handles each case, predicting its verdict on an unlisted case—specifically, a genetically modified bacterium that secretes a chemical pattern isomorphic to the bird's alarm call but never interpreted by any other organism—and being explicit about which of my judgments it cannot accommodate.
