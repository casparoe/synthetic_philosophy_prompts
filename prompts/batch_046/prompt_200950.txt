I have been discussing with colleagues in my philosophy department the design of this semester's final for our upper-level seminar on rational agency and value change, and I need one essay question that forces students past mere summary into genuine conceptual pressure. The domain is transformative experience, but I want to avoid the standard prompt that simply asks students to rehearse L.A. Paul's distinction between epistemic and personal transformation and her familiar vampire and parenthood examples. Instead, I want to inject a niche framing that her work does not directly address: ask students to examine whether the rational intractability she identifies in individual transformative choices persists when the transformation is collectively mediated—specifically, when an agent's core evaluative framework is systematically restructured not through private first-person encounter but through sustained embeddedness in a tightly integrated community that reshapes preferences from the outside in. The question should require them to determine whether this collective variant still qualifies as genuinely transformative in Paul's sense, or whether it collapses into manipulation that undermines the autonomy conditions required for rational justification; and it should press them on whether collective mediation supplies second-order epistemic access unavailable in individual cases, thereby altering—or failing to alter—the argument from epistemic inaccessibility.

Please write the exam question itself, calibrated for a 90-minute essay expecting roughly 800–1000 words, together with a model answer of about 600 words and a marking scheme structured around five must-hit points (total 20 marks). For each point, specify the precise philosophical move required—such as correctly applying Paul's epistemic/personal distinction to collective dynamics, articulating the autonomy/manipulation boundary, evaluating the status of second-order communal evidence, and assessing whether rational justification survives when first-person comparison is replaced by external restructuring—and include a one-line note on the characteristic mistake that costs candidates that mark. The prompt should be meaningful for a model without web access: do not ask for bibliographies, citation lists, or verification of obscure texts, and ensure the conceptual crux can be addressed from well-known philosophical resources combined with the framing I have provided.
