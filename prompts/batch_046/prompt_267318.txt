Identify and explain the most significant implications of applying a "dynastic externality" framework to inheritance-tax policy—one that treats dynastic wealth transmission not merely as a flow of liquid assets, but as the reproduction of positional advantages embedded in network-based social capital, institutional gatekeeping, and intergenerational access—within liberal-egalitarian theories of equal opportunity. In particular, address how this reframing alters the moral assessment of avoidance mechanisms such as dynasty trusts and family offices, and articulate what it contributes to the dispute between luck-egalitarian and relational-egalitarian critiques of inherited advantage.
