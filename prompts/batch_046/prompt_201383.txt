I have noticed a recurring pattern in philosophical and scientific discussions of consciousness across non-human animals, and I want to know whether it corresponds to an established concept or whether it needs a new one. The pattern runs as follows. Commentators treat consciousness-related capacities—sensory integration, flexible problem-solving, self-monitoring, affective evaluation, and temporal continuity—as if they form a cluster, yet in comparative studies these capacities do not covary in any straightforward way. An octopus, for instance, may display highly flexible, goal-directed manipulation without the sustained social self-monitoring observed in certain corvids; a corvid may show long-term episodic-like caching that does not align with the continuous affective monitoring typical of mammals; and bees appear to perform rapid sensory binding within architectures that lack the centralized integration often assumed in vertebrate models. Despite this independence, discussants routinely map these heterogeneous profiles onto a single ordinal scale, ranking one species as more conscious or further along than another, and then deploy that ranking interchangeably for two distinct purposes: predicting which neural or cognitive mechanisms are present, and assigning moral status or protection priority.

This is not simply anthropocentrism, because humans are not consistently placed at the top of every sub-dimension. It is not exactly the scala naturae, since the ordering is not defended by evolutionary progression or metaphysical hierarchy. Nor is it a straightforward false analogy, because the error is not a comparison between one isolated trait and another, but a compression of an entire multidimensional, cross-species trait space into a unidimensional ladder. Does this specific configuration—collapsing incommensurable consciousness-related profiles into one axis that serves both explanatory and normative functions—have an established name in philosophy of mind, comparative cognition, or ethics? If there is no precise term, I would like a clear distinction from the nearest relatives, together with a proposed name and boundary conditions that separate it from cases where the underlying dimensions genuinely do covary.
