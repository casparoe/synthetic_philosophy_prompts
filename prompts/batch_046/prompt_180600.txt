As a voting member of the Biosafety and Welfare Review Board of the Meridian Agricultural Consortium, I must circulate a formal decision memo before Thursday’s meeting on Protocol 734. The protocol proposes a three-year pilot rearing approximately 4.5 trillion black soldier fly larvae annually for industrial protein conversion. The applicants contend that welfare safeguards are unwarranted: established evidence does not demonstrate sentience in Diptera, and the environmental and economic gains from displacing soy cultivation are substantial. A minority report submitted by the board’s ethics liaison advances what she terms a “scale-risk asymmetry”: even if one assigns a low prior—say, one in five hundred—to the possibility that larvae experience minimal negative valence, the aggregate expected harm across trillions of individuals dwarfs the welfare costs of conventional livestock reduction. She urges rejection unless the protocol adopts graded nociception monitoring and a mandatory “moral-risk threshold” that treats probabilistic suffering as a non-negligible cost in harm-benefit analysis, rather than requiring binary proof of sentience before any protective conditions apply.

Please draft the board’s written opinion recommending approval, rejection, or approval with conditions. The memo should specify a concrete course of action with an explicit timeline—such as conditions that must be satisfied within ninety days, or a phased rejection with review milestones—and it should be framed so that researchers and regulators can act on it directly. The reasoning must take the scale-risk asymmetry seriously rather than dismissing it as speculative, and it must also engage the applicants’ economic and epistemic objections honestly, including the strongest consideration for thinking that applying probabilistic moral risk at this scale overreaches or misallocates regulatory burden. Do not include bibliographic requests or citation lists; the memo should stand on its internal reasoning.
