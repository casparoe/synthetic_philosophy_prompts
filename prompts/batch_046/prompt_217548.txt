After a recent professional crisis—in which a disciplinary review questioned a methodological framework I had always treated as beyond scrutiny—I have come to recognize that my own reasoning rests on commitments I cannot justify without circularity. I am now leading a single ninety-minute session for an interdisciplinary reading group of non-philosophers—clinicians, legal scholars, and historians—on hinge epistemology and Wittgenstein's On Certainty. I need not a biography or literature survey, but a precise explanatory plan. First, identify the conceptual dependency chain: what idea must be secured before the notion of a hinge proposition is even intelligible to this audience, and in what order should the pieces be introduced so that the structure builds rather than collapses into mysticism? Second, propose the single concrete scenario that should carry the entire session—one that illustrates a framework-level commitment being presupposed rather than tested within a practice—while explaining why the familiar examples of doubting one's hands or the earth's antiquity are poor carriers for clinicians and lawyers. The scenario should have the feature that its hinge can be articulated only from outside the practice that relies on it, and it should lack any easy empirical resolution. Third, map the misconceptions these particular audiences will form in predictable sequence—likely that hinges are mere psychological biases, that they function as arbitrary dogmas, or that they can be converted into ordinary hypotheses with sufficient evidence—and specify the exact point in the explanatory arc where each should be headed off. Fourth, design one brief check at the end that reveals whether the participants have grasped the structural, framework-constituting role of hinges rather than simply adopting the riverbed metaphor as a decorative image. Fifth, state clearly what genuinely contested interpretive issues—such as whether hinges are primarily social or individual in origin, or whether they are ever genuinely open to revision—should be left unresolved, and identify what should be explicitly set aside at this level and acknowledged as such. Throughout, please distinguish between what is settled about the hinge framework and what remains genuinely contested among people who study On Certainty closely, and do not present any contested reading as the consensus view.
