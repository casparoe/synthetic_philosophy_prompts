I work as a software engineer with no formal philosophical training, and I have been trying to think through the Boltzmann brain problem in a structured way. Before I present my view to a discussion group, I would like a consistency audit of the commitments below, which I have developed from popular science writing and informal reasoning. Please respond in British English and do not provide a bibliography or citation list.

My positions are:

(1) In a long-lived de Sitter universe, thermal fluctuations will eventually produce conscious observers—Boltzmann brains—whose subjective experiences are, for the duration of their existence, indistinguishable from mine.
(2) Applying a principle of indifference over observer moments within that cosmological model suggests that a randomly selected conscious experience should belong to a Boltzmann brain rather than to an evolved observer like me.
(3) Despite (2), I reject that I am a Boltzmann brain because my cognition appears to depend on long-range causal stability—analogous to a distributed system with persistent state and a verifiable debug trace—whereas a fluctuation brain would lack that structural history.
(4) I also maintain that inductive reasoning and scientific trust are justified only if we can treat global sceptical hypotheses as defeated by default, without requiring specific disproof of every alternative.
(5) At the same time, I hold a broadly functionalist view of consciousness: if a physical substrate implements the right informational dynamics for a brief interval, there is nothing metaphysically deficient about that consciousness, even absent evolutionary history.

Do these hang together? Specifically, does (3) smuggle in an evidential privilege that (2) denies me? Does (4) amount to an ad hoc exemption that conflicts with the probabilistic reasoning in (1) and (2)? And does (5) weaken the distinction I draw in (3) between my stable causal architecture and a brief fluctuation, since both might realise the same functional state? Please rank the tensions by severity, identify any apparent conflicts that dissolve on closer inspection, and tell me what the least costly single revision would be.
