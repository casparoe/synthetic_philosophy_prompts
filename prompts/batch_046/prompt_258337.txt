Consider the philosophical claim that in distributed sociotechnical systems—such as climate-adaptation algorithms designed by multi-disciplinary committees—individual contributors are genuinely subject to resultant moral luck with respect to aggregate outcomes they neither singularly caused nor could have predicted, and that this undermines the coherence of attributing moral praise or blame to those individuals qua individuals. By “true,” I mean that the extension of resultant moral luck from singular to distributed agency holds normatively and does weaken individual-level moral attribution in these contexts.

Please provide your credence that this claim is true, expressed as a number between 0 and 1, together with a decomposition into the following sub-questions:

(a) The baseline coherence of resultant moral luck for singular agents—roughly, whether outcomes outside an agent’s control can legitimately affect the moral evaluation of that agent’s actions.

(b) The analogical strength of extending that concept to distributed or collective agency without collapsing it into purely collective responsibility.

(c) Whether the specific conditions of algorithmic committee work—fragmented knowledge, emergent system behavior, and diffuse causal contribution—actually satisfy the conditions for resultant luck rather than merely for ordinary causal uncertainty.

(d) Whether recognizing such luck genuinely undermines individual attribution, or whether it can be accommodated within revised frameworks of shared or proportional responsibility.

For each sub-question, assign a rough probability and one line of justification. Then show how they combine (multiplicatively or via another structure you specify) to reach your overall number. Identify which term you are least confident in and describe what empirical case study or philosophical argument would most shift your credence. Finally, if you had to locate the disagreement between someone at 0.75 and someone at 0.25 on this claim, on which of (a)–(d) would they most likely diverge?
