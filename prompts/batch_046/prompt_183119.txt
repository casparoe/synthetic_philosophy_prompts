I am preparing a proposal for a six-month independent study fellowship in philosophy of mind and comparative philosophy. Over the past eighteen months I have become the primary caregiver for a parent whose personality, memories, and emotional dispositions are being progressively altered by a neurodegenerative condition. Watching the gradual dissolution of the traits I had taken to constitute her identity has made the standard philosophical options feel inadequate: Western narrative-identity theories assume too much coherence, substance-based accounts seem empirically hollow, and the popular reception of Buddhist anatta offers only a therapeutic negation rather than a constructive account of what remains. I want to investigate whether the classical Buddhist analysis of personal existence into five aggregates—when interpreted not as metaphysical reduction but as a functional decomposition of awareness—can be articulated alongside contemporary models of predictive processing and interoceptive inference to generate a distinct criterion of personal continuity. The specific question is whether there is a stable, non-narrative, non-bodily pattern of causal-functional awareness that can survive near-total psychological dissolution, and whether such a pattern can be described without positing an owner or substrate.

Write the proposal. It should contain: (1) a precisely formulated research question that admits of a determinate answer; (2) an explanation of why both the dominant Western positions in personal-identity theory and the standard interpretive frameworks for anatta leave this particular question unresolved; (3) a month-by-month method describing what conceptual work I will actually undertake—including which Buddhist texts and which philosophical frameworks I will engage—without producing a bibliography or citation list; (4) a clear statement of what a successful result would look like, together with explicit failure conditions that would indicate the project should be abandoned or reframed; and (5) an honest scope assessment explaining why this is tractable within six months for a single researcher, and identifying the precise point at which it risks expanding into an unmanageable interdisciplinary project. The tone should be formal and suitable for a fellowship committee. Please do not include references, reading lists, or requests for further literature; I will supply citations myself.
