So in manualized CBT protocols — like the ones used in some NHS IAPT programs — therapists have to tick through every module in fixed sequence and ask all the scripted questions even when the patient already resolved the core belief in session two. The protocol literally doesn't adapt to context. I'm wondering what that presupposes philosophically. Does it treat the patient like that sphex wasp — the one that keeps dragging prey to the burrow and checking it in a loop even when you move the prey — running a fixed behavioral loop that has to complete regardless of state changes? Or is the rigidity just on the clinician's side — a governance choice, not a theory of mind? If the practice really embeds a sphexish view of human agency, what does that commit us to about free will? Can we still say people are flexible agents if our best treatment model assumes they need walking through an unbranching loop? And is there an inconsistency with how the same system lets clinicians use clinical judgement in crisis situations but not in the manual? What's the implicit theory of ridgidity and flexibility baked in here?
