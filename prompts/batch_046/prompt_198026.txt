Your protagonist is a senior systems auditor on an offshore carbon-capture installation who discovers a jurisdictional loophole in the platform's automated liability architecture: if she initiates a precise sensor cascade, any consequential environmental damage will be algorithmically reassigned to a phantom "ghost vessel," leaving her actions causally untraceable—she becomes invisible not to sight, but to any system of accountability. Center the narrative on her solitary deliberation in the control room, particularly an imagined dialogue with an impartial spectator who knows every detail of her motives and actions yet possesses no power to reward or punish her. The story should implicitly ask whether her reluctance to trigger the cascade without external sanction reflects a genuine moral commitment or merely the residual expectation of being seen. Ground the philosophical tension in concrete technical details—the pressure differentials in the sequestration lines, the legal abstraction of the ghost-vessel clause, the silence of the open ocean—without citing specific texts, providing bibliographies, or referring to philosophical scholarship beyond the two named concepts.
