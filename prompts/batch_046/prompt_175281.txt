Write a joke built around the internal logic of a Frankfurt-style case against the principle of alternative possibilities. The setup should feature an agent who proceeds for her own reasons while a counterfactual intervener waits to compel her only if she displays a specific prior sign of deviating, yet never actually steps in. Do not settle for a generic free-will punchline; the humor should turn on the precise mechanics of this scenario. Specifically, weave in the "flicker of freedom" objection — the claim that defenders of alternative possibilities rescue responsibility by pointing to some trivial, causally irrelevant alternative the agent retains, such as an involuntary twitch or a momentary hesitation — and make the joke depend on whether that flicker genuinely restores the agent's control or merely gives the intervener an unnecessary headache. The result should amuse someone who knows the literature well enough to recognize the prior-sign mechanism and the flicker reply, without requiring any outside reference.
