Write an essay on whether generational labels—Baby Boomer, Millennial, Gen Z, and the like—name real kinds in the world, or whether they are useful fictions that mainly serve marketing and casual conversation. I have been thinking about this on my own, and I want you to take the ontology seriously: do these cohorts share deep, causally unified properties that shape values, cognition, or life outcomes in ways that go beyond simple age and shared historical context? Or are they statistical artifacts that collapse once you control for life stage, economic conditions, and geography?

Weigh the argument that generations are genuine social kinds—perhaps analogous to biological species in having hidden structural features that license inductive predictions—against the view that they are purely nominal groupings, no more ontologically weighty than "people born on Tuesdays." In particular, examine whether knowing that someone belongs to Gen Z allows you to predict anything meaningful that you could not already predict from knowing their age, class position, and the technology available in their adolescence.

Then consider a possibility I find especially puzzling: that generations might function as "interactive kinds," where being publicly labeled and marketed to actually alters the behavior of the people in the group, creating a looping effect that makes the category partly self-fulfilling. Assess carefully whether this looping saves the reality of generations—by giving them causal power—or instead undermines their claim to be discovered kinds, reducing them to manufactured categories that become real only after the fact.

Do not end on a non-committal balance. Reach and defend a clear verdict: either that generations are real kinds in a specific, qualified sense (and say exactly what that sense is), or that they are not kinds at all and should be abandoned in serious social explanation. Justify your conclusion by specifying what generational kinds must be able to do—predict, explain, or ground generalizations—that other available categories, such as birth cohort or life stage, cannot do just as well.
