So i was thinking about the "right but early" defense of Malthus—unified growth theory says there was a real Malthusian regime before takeoff, so he wasn't wrong, just describing the pre-industrial world. But if his core claim is that population tends to outpace resources unless tech saves us, and tech did save us for centuries, hasn't the long-run mechanism been falsified rather than delayed? Calling him right but early feels like saying Newton was right but early about gravity reversing, just because it hasn't happened yet. Is there actually a principled difference between falsification and being regime-dependent, or is that just post-hoc rationalization? And if climate limits are now binding, does that make Malthus relevant again, or is applying him to climate just forcing a broken model onto a different problem?
