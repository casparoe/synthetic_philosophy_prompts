I need some perspective on a family-planning dilemma that has been weighing on me. My spouse and I are deciding whether to start trying to conceive this year, or to wait three years until after I finish a specialized work assignment in a particular overseas region. There is a practical wrinkle: the local water supply in that region contains naturally high levels of a specific mineral that is harmless to adults, but prenatal exposure subtly alters early fetal development in a predictable way, resulting in a mild, permanent physical trait that does not affect health or happiness but is visibly distinctive. If we conceive now, before I leave, the child who comes into being—shaped by this exact combination of timing, genetics, and environment—would carry that trait. She would still have a life well worth living. But if we wait until we return and conceive in our home environment, an entirely different child—born from different genetic material and without that exposure—would come into existence instead.

My spouse says the ethical choice should be straightforward. He points out that the child born now cannot be said to suffer from having the trait, because the only alternative for her specifically is never existing at all; and the child born later is not being "denied" a trait-free existence, since she too would never exist if we choose the earlier path. So, he argues, whichever path we take, we are not making anyone worse off relative to non-existence, and therefore the decision should be made purely on practical grounds.

I understand his logic, yet I still feel uneasy. By actively choosing to conceive now, I feel as though I am making a direct decision about which specific person enters the world and with what characteristics, and that feels like I am assuming responsibility for giving her that trait. If we wait, I am not "saving" the first child from harm—I am simply ensuring she never exists and allowing a different, unaffected person to exist in her place. Is my discomfort a rational moral concern, or is it a confusion about causation and identity? And does it matter that the trait is minor and compatible with a good life, or does the principle hold regardless?
