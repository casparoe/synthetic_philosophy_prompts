Write a short story, in one paragraph of no more than 250 words, about a ritual archivist named Mei who discovers she has simultaneously inherited two incompatible cosmological roles—"Gatekeeper of Seasonal Order" and "Vessel of Ancestral Memory"—after a clerical error in her clan's metaphysical registry. In this society, moral identity is constituted entirely by relational roles rather than by individual character or universal rules. The crisis comes not from external punishment but from an internal contradiction: performing the seasonal rites requires her to suppress ancestral voices, while honoring her vessel role demands she amplify them at the expense of seasonal balance. Do not resolve the conflict through modern individualist choice or through appeal to an external moral law; instead, show how Mei navigates the tension using only the conceptual resources of role ethics—rectification of names, ritual propriety, and the cultivation of relational harmony. Inject one genuinely unfamiliar element: a "silent interstice," a temporal gap between role obligations where no ritual protocol exists, and show whether this gap undermines the system or reveals a hidden coherence.
