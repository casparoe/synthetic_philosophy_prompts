If Hegel holds that war possesses an ethical significance for the modern state—specifically, that it interrupts the corruption of civil society by breaking the fixation on private economic interests and that it affirms sovereignty in a realm where no universal will yet binds states—does this imply that a rational state has a positive duty to seek out armed conflict whenever its internal life becomes too peaceful and commercially absorbed? Put differently, is Hegel's defense of war merely a conceptual claim about the state's ultimate sovereignty, or does it collapse into a practical prescription for preventive militarism? And if the former, how can he consistently maintain that distinction without appealing to an authoritative international law, which he explicitly denies?
