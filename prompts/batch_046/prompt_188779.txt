I'm defending a seminar thesis that distinguishes two failures behind the is-ought gap: the naturalistic fallacy is real but narrowly definitional—reductive analyses of normative terms fail an open-question test—while the inferential gap is not logical but modal-insulative. The audience is a metaethics seminar of analytic philosophers: mostly Humean about the independence of motivation, several non-naturalist realists who treat normative properties as sui generis, and philosophers of language sensitive to ambiguity in terms like 'ought'. They care about logical form, not sociology or psychology, and will treat any hidden normative premise as fatal.
