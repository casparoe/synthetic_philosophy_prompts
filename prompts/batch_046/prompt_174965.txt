Here's an argument I put together for a seminar on supertasks and now can't shake, though the conclusion feels like it collapses the distinction between contrived thought experiments and ordinary physical processes.

(1) In Thomson's lamp, presses occur at times 1 − 1/2ⁿ minutes, toggling the lamp with each press. At t = 2 minutes there is no final press, hence no fact of the matter about whether the total number of toggles is even or odd.

(2) If there is no fact about the parity of the toggle sequence, then there is no fact about whether the lamp is on or off at t = 2.

(3) Any continuous physical process can be decomposed into infinitely many micro-interactions occurring in an open interval (0, t).

(4) For any such decomposition, there is no "last" micro-interaction immediately preceding t, just as there is no last button press before t = 2.

(5) So if the absence of a final toggle makes the lamp's macro-state at the limit indeterminate, then the absence of a final micro-interaction should make every macro-state at every time indeterminate.

(6) But macroscopic states are plainly determinate at every time.

(7) Therefore premise (2) must be false: the lamp does have a determinate state at t = 2, and the absence of a terminating toggle does not by itself generate indeterminacy.

(8) However, if a macro-state can be fully determinate at a limit point even though no particular prior event is its immediate causal predecessor, then the standard objection to supertasks—that they violate causal closure because there is no prior cause for the limit state—loses its force.

(9) So either we accept that supertasks are physically unproblematic, or we need a different reason to reject them; but if we choose the latter, we owe an account of why the lamp is special that does not also apply to continuous physical change.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? I want to keep (1) and (6) if possible, and I am inclined to reject (5) rather than (2), but I can't see exactly what distinguishes the lamp's infinite toggle sequence from the infinite decomposition of continuous motion. If (5) fails, what is the relevant disanalogy? And if (5) holds, does that really commit us to accepting supertasks, or is there a hidden scope shift between "causally undetermined" and "lacks a fact of the matter"?
