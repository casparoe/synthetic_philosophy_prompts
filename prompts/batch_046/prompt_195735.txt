I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, mostly fiddling with mechanistic interpretability, and I've noticed a recurring argumentative pattern that I can't find a name for. People keep framing debates about representation as if they're asking whether symbols inside a system push the substrate around, or whether the substrate just throws up symbols as a kind of epiphenomenal shadow. I've seen it occured in discussions about whether a feature vector in a neural net genuinely steers computation, and in older consciousness debates where mental states are mapped onto neural firing patterns. The specific move is always this: first, describe the substrate fully without mentioning the high-level symbol; then describe the symbol entirely in terms of substrate dynamics; then ask which level is "really" causal, with each side claiming descriptive completeness for their own layer. I know Hofstadter's careenium thing with symmballs and marbles is in the neighbourhood, but I'm not asking about that specific metaphor — I'm asking whether there's an established concept for the dialectical stalemate itself, the loop where both sides deny causal efficacy to the other by appealing to explanatory closure at their preferred level. Does it have a name, and if so how does it differ from standard downward causation or epiphenomenalism? If there's no name, what should we call it, and what are its exact boundaries — which cases fit and which are just ordinary multi-level explanation? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning with no preamble and don't restate the question. Write in British English.
