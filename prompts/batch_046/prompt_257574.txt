As a retired physicist who has only in the last few years begun to read seriously in philosophy of religion, I find discussions of divine hiddenness to be rich in intuition but poor in systematic evaluation criteria. Before I examine any particular account, I want to know what an adequate theory in this area is actually required to deliver. Write the desiderata: specify the phenomena any theory explaining the epistemic inaccessibility of a maximally great being must accommodate, the boundary cases it must classify correctly, and the conceptual distinctions it must preserve — with the motivating consideration behind each. These should include, at minimum: the fact of apparently reasonable and widespread non-belief across diverse epistemic communities; the asymmetry between the hiddenness of a putative deity and the hiddenness of other entities we ordinarily take to exist; the tension between perfect benevolence and intentional concealment; and the difference between hiddenness as an ontological absence and hiddenness as an epistemic limitation of the inquirer. You should also address whether the theory must remain consistent with standard free-will defenses, with skeptical-theist responses to the problem of evil, and with the possibility that revelation behaves analogously to a weak physical signal — what I will call an instrumental attenuation view, in which disclosure is present but falls below the detection threshold of conceptual frameworks lacking the appropriate epistemic receiver, much as a faint signal is lost in background noise unless the instrument is properly tuned. Identify any pairs of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and explain the source of the tension. Then, briefly, assess how the principal families of response — the free-will defense, skeptical theism, soul-making theodicies, and the instrumental attenuation view described above — fare against your list, noting for each where it pays a conceptual cost. Keep the emphasis on the criteria and their conflicts rather than on the scoring; I am not asking for a bibliography, citation of primary texts, or a reading list, but for a clear conceptual architecture against which I can evaluate candidates on my own.
