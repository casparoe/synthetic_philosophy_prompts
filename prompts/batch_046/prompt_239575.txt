Specify the full list of requirements that a theory of deductive justification must meet if it is to resolve Carroll's tortoise paradox without mere stipulation. For each desideratum, give the case or conceptual distinction that motivates it — for instance, the separation of logical entitlement from psychological compulsion, the prohibition on circular appeal to the very inference being justified, and the need to account for formal derivations in which premises are explicit but rules of inference are not. Add a further constraint drawn from a less-discussed variant: the theory must accommodate "shadow rules," inference licenses that function in reasoning without being objects of explicit belief, and must explain whether such shadow rules can themselves be justified without generating an infinite regress of dispositional endorsements. Mark which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences (e.g., the demand that every step be introspectively accessible). Identify any subset of requirements that appear jointly unsatisfiable given standard no-go results about self-justification, and say which single desideratum eliminates the largest number of familiar proposals.
