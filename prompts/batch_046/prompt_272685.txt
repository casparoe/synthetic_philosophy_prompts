hey, i'm putting together a taxonomy for my blog on luck egalitarianism and i'm tired of the usual 'do people deserve thier talents?' quizzes that just sort people into boxes they already named. i want an actual decision tree built from cases, not labels.

start with this: someone is born with weak eyesight and another with sharp eyesight, but the sharp-eyed person only gets into a competitive field because thier parents could afford private tutoring. branch one: does the tutoring count as brute luck, option luck, or a hybrid? branch two: if we compensate, is it all-or-nothing or can it be graded (like partial subsidy)? branch three: does effort still matter after compensation, or is all desert wiped out?

leaves should include dworkin-style resource equality, roemer's two-track opportunity, anderson's democratic equality, arneson's equal opportunity for welfare, plus any unnamed combos (like graded compensation + retained desert). flag combinations that seem incoherent—maybe full compensation plus strong desert?—and note if relational egalitarians and distributive ones can't be separated here. phrase every node as a concrete case question someone could answer without knowing the jargon. also, i don't want a bibliography or reading list—just the tree and a one-sentence awkward commitment for each leaf.
