hey, im a backend engineer with zero philosophy background, so this might sound wierd. there's this hiring ritual in tech where companies make candidates do live whiteboard coding for jobs that are literally just writing REST endpoints and nobody touches a whiteboard. people defend it saying it filters out people who can't think under pressure, even though everyone admits it doesn't predict actual job performance. at the same time you see senior devs bragging that they never study for these interviews because they're "above that stuff" -- like refusing to send the signal is itself a kind of flex.

what philosophical commitments does that whole practice presuppose? does it assume that wasted effort or stress is a trustworthy indicator of quality, like that handicap principle thing with peacocks? and does the "too good to study" attitude presuppose there's a tier of people whose merit should be self-evident without costly signaling, or is it just counter-signaling? reconstruct the implicit theory of value here: is merit something you demonstrate through suffering, or something that ought to be visible without a filter? also, is this consistent with the same companies claiming they value diversity and inclusion, since grinding for these rituals takes free time a lot of people don't have? and what would tech hiring have to look like if we dropped the idea that difficulty equals merit?
