ive come around to Strawson's Basic Argument — that real moral responsibility needs you to be causa sui for your nature and actions, but thats impossible because you'd have to be responsible for the conditions that made you, and so on forever, so basic desert collapses. I find it pretty convincing, especially when I try to apply it to AI blame: if an artificial agent does harm, you can trace its weights and training back to human designers and data, but then you hit the same regress — nobody is ultimately the source. Still, I want an honest map of what counts against the argument, not just the standard compatibilist reply that "quality of will" is enough. What are the actual defeaters — formal, intuitive, empirical — and which ones would Strawson concede are genuinely awkward versus which he'd brush off? Rank them from fatal to merely embarassing. And please seperate whats settled from whats genuinely contested among people who work on free will and moral psychology; dont present a fringe compatibilist move as the mainstream view. I dont need a bibliography or citations, just your take, including whether applying the argument to machines makes it stronger or just confuses the issue.
