Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on costly signaling in human institutional life: Camp A, strict handicap-principle theorists who maintain that ritual and credential displays are honest precisely because they impose differential, quality-dependent costs that stabilize equilibrium; and Camp B, index-signaling and commitment-architecture theorists who maintain that reliability derives from causal correlation with underlying capacity and that observed costs are either byproducts or structurally redesigned commitment devices rather than strategic investments. The statement should contain, first, a numbered section of claims both sides can sign—covering the formal conditions for honest signaling, the distinction between equilibrium-stabilizing burden and causal-correlation mechanisms, and what both take generic no-go results (e.g., on pure conventional signaling lacking any quality linkage) to rule out. Second, it should present precise formulations of the residual disagreement, each phrased so the respective camp would accept its own wording, specifically addressing whether multi-domain “credibility budgets”—the finite capacity of agents to sustain costly displays across contexts—are best modeled as strategic allocations that preserve handicap logic or as structural constraints that reveal underlying quality without strategic investment. Third, it should identify the cruxes, including whether reducing the marginal cost of a signal in one domain necessarily degrades its informativeness in another, and whether audience-imposed secondary costs should be included in the handicap calculus. Fourth, for each crux, it should record the empirical case or theoretical argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side’s predicted outcome stated in advance. The statement must avoid bibliographic appeals, citation lists, and appeals to obscure technical literature; if any part of the dispute is verbal—particularly around the definition of “cost” versus “constraint” or “strategic investment” versus “structural byproduct”—the document should say exactly which part and why. Write this as a formal workshop prompt from a PhD student in evolutionary anthropology constructing the collaboration for a session on institutional signaling, with the deliverable formatted as the agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
