I hold that HCH—iterated amplification with human consultants—combined with factored cognition offers a sound idealisation of collective deliberation: reasoning is decomposed into small, human-verifiable sub-tasks and iterated through consultants, so that no individual need grasp the entire argument, and I take this to model how rational groups ought to reason regardless of how actual committees behave. What would count decisively against this view? I want structural defeaters rather than practical ones—specifically, cases where breaking reasoning into factors destroys information that only emerges in holistic or adversarial exchange; results in social epistemology or democratic theory that treat disagreement as generative rather than as noise to be filtered out; and categories of normative or aesthetic judgement that resist factorisation altogether. Rank these defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a committed defender could concede without abandoning the core idealisation, and assess whether the position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability.
