I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, but I've been trying to formalize my intuitions about reduction and emergence for a discussion group I'm attending out of curiosity. Here is where I've landed: (1) I hold a physicalist view of engineered systems—every event in a data center is ultimately fixed by silicon, electricity, and thermodynamics, with no non-physical forces involved. (2) Still, system-level descriptions like this architecture has a race condition, the cluster is thermally throttled, or the autoscaling policy triggered a cascade pick out real patterns with explanatory power you can't replicate by listing transistor voltages. (3) I take all emergence to be purely epistemic: these patterns are not ontologically basic, but exist because our cognitive limits make bottom-up reasoning impractical. (4) Despite that, I think downward causation genuinely occurs—when a high-level autoscaling rule spins up nodes, it literally alters the data center's heat profile and electrical draw in ways that wouldn't happen without the software-level decision. (5) I extend this to biology, rejecting vitalism and treating biological emergence as purely epistemic too. (6) In principle, with perfect particle-level knowledge and unbounded computation, I believe we could derive every system-level event—including those heat and electrical effects—from the bottom up. (7) Finally, I think software causation is different from physical causation: a bug doesn't cause a crash the way a spark causes fire, but rather represents a normative failure against the system's own specification.

My question is whether these seven commitments hang together as a consistent package, or whether I'm helping myself to incompatible ideas without noticing. I want a precise audit: for each pair, say whether the tension is genuine or only apparent; for genuine conflicts, rank them by severity and identify the least costly revision for each, specifying exactly which commitment must give and what that costs conceptually. Please treat all seven neutrally—I haven't decided which I would drop if forced, and I don't want you to assume the problem is where it first looks obvious. In particular, I'm unsure how (3), (4), and (6) interact, and whether (7) undermines the explanatory reality I granted to system-level patterns in (2), but check the entire set rather than focusing only on those. I am not asking for bibliographies, reading lists, or a general lecture on emergence; I want to know if this specific combination is salvageable.
