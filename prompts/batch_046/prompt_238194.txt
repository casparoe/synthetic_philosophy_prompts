Write a short story centered on a welfare-state eligibility assessor tasked with administering a newly implemented Desert Ledger system. In this system, claimants are assigned not merely an eligibility determination but a graded moral status—restorative, conditional, or corrective—computed from an algorithm that weights verified employment, tax contribution, formal volunteer records, and prior claim history into a single coefficient of social reciprocity. The protagonist must adjudicate the case of a long-term claimant whose coefficient falls into the corrective tier, triggering mandatory workfare and partial benefit clawback, despite the claimant having sustained years of intensive but unregistered eldercare that enabled other household members to maintain formal employment. The narrative should explore the conceptual tension between conditionality—the principle that assistance should be contingent upon demonstrable, bureaucratically visible contribution—and the structural invisibility of care labor, showing how the auditing mechanisms required by conditionality systematically mismeasure desert. Invent the specific bureaucratic vocabulary; do not reference any actual country's welfare program, statute, or named policy; and avoid delivering a definitive moral verdict, allowing the contradiction between the metric and the claimant's lived contribution to remain unresolved at the story's close.
