I'm preparing materials for a graduate seminar pairing literary narrative with meta-normative ontology, and I'd like a philosophical reading of Antoine de Saint-Exupéry's The Little Prince through the specific lens of free-floating rationales — the thesis that normative reasons can obtain and exert binding force independently of any being who recognizes, feels, or acts upon them. The fox's lesson that taming creates an enduring responsibility is usually interpreted as a psychology of attachment, but I want to press whether the novel treats that responsibility as genuinely agent-independent: does the rose possess a claim on the prince's care that would still hold had he never existed, or had he never become conscious of her? Focus particularly on the scenes with the rose, the fox, and the geographer — the geographer records planetary features without ever verifying them firsthand, which might serve as a metaphor for reasons that are catalogued yet borne by no one. Does Saint-Exupéry ultimately vindicate the view that there are reasons floating free of all reasoners, or does he quietly collapse normative force back into the subjective experience of the one who loves?

To make the discussion sharper, please treat this as a two-directional analysis: first, show where the concept of free-floating rationales illuminates moments in the text that standard psychological or relational readings miss — for instance, whether the prince's departure from his asteroid can be evaluated by standards he never fully grasps, or whether the businessman's counting of stars implies reasons about ownership that outlast the counter. Then say honestly where the work resists the reading: identify scenes or narrative choices that insist a reason without someone to bear it is no reason at all, and consider whether the novel's ending — the prince's return to his rose through death — undermines or reinforces the idea that the normative weight of care survives the disappearance of the caregiver. I am not looking for a bibliography or references to specific secondary literature; I want the argument to stand or fall on the internal logic of the text and the philosophical concept alone.
