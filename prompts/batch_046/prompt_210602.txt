i sit on a municipal ethics board for a mid-sized city and we keep recieving land-use disputes where religious groups, ecologists, and developers talk past each other. i want a reusable decision procedre my board can run in about 48 hours, no philosophers on staff, that turns the idea of moral compromise into actual steps — specifically, that different value systems can make gains from trade when they hold asymmetric valuations of the same goods, but only if we avoid moral dumping and hidden incommensurability. please build it as a checklist in order: the questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer, where it stops with a hard no, and where it must hand off to human judgment because its a values call not a fact.

inject two conceptual tools im working with: first, an incommensurability buffer — before we treat a value as peripheral and tradable for system X, we have to check whether conceding it secretly undermines a core commitment of X in a way X itself wouldnt recognize until later. second, shadow exchange rates — not numbers, but a required narrative sentence in the final write-up that states exactly what value was reduced, for which party, and to what level, so theres no hidden cost.

constraints: i sign my name, the decision is binding but appealable, and we need to apply this repeatedly not solve one case.

run the procedre on three proposals:
1. a transit line needs to run near a monastery during prayer hours, but the monastery's eldercare depends on that same line.
2. an indigenous group holds a forest as sacred and non-divisible; a developer offers to build housing on 10% and preserve 90% with funding.
3. an environmental group wants full brownfield restoration, but a low-income neighborhood wants a community garden there for food sovereignty; the board has to choose.

include one case where the procedre gives an answer i probably wont like, and say exactly what the procedre cannot do — where it will misfire or stay silent.
