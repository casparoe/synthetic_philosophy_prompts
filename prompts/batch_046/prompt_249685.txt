I chair the student economic-policy committee at my high school. The administration has asked us to advise on a proposal that would cap peer-to-peer tutoring fees at fifteen dollars per hour; fees currently range from ten to fifty dollars, and supporters argue that high prices exploit students who need help, while critics warn that the cap will reduce tutor supply and violate student autonomy. Please draft our committee's decision memo—approve the proposal, reject it, or approve with conditions—providing clear reasoning that addresses the strongest economic and ethical objections on the losing side, and explaining whether pricing in a captive school environment should be treated differently from labor or housing markets in general.
