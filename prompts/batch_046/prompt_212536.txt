As a retired physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy of mind, I am preparing an interview for a small podcast series on conceptual foundations. Next week’s guest is a philosopher who defends a specific interpretation of Douglas Hofstadter’s careenium framework: she holds that symbolic structures—simmballs—do not merely emerge from neural substrate but actively constrain and redirect it, and she further advances what she terms symbolic inertia, the claim that sustained high-level symbolic patterns resist rapid substrate-level perturbation in a manner analogous to, yet ontologically distinct from, physical inertia. I want eight to ten interview questions that prevent her from simply restating this downward-causation view. Specifically, I need at least one question probing how symbolic inertia differs from standard emergentist resistance without collapsing into dualism; at least one pressing whether the substrate-level mechanics can be experimentally distinguished from ordinary neural homeostasis; and at least one on whether the careenium model requires discrete neural correlates for individual simmballs or permits distributed, non-local substrate governance. For each of these three, please include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—particularly her tendency to reply that substrate-level descriptions are necessarily incomplete without symbolic-level vocabulary. The remaining questions should explore related conceptual pressure points: the ontological status of the careenium boundary, the risk of infinite regress if symbols govern substrate that in turn governs symbols, and whether her view can accommodate pathological cases in which substrate damage disrupts symbolic coherence without any preceding symbolic failure. Please frame the sequence so that it builds from conceptual clarification toward empirical and metaphysical tension, suitable for an audience familiar with philosophy of mind but lacking specialized neuroscience training.
