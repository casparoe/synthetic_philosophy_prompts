For my philosophy of mind essay I am examining whether gradual, neuron-by-neuron replacement with synthetic analogues preserves personal identity. Rather than rehearsing the familiar Ship of Theseus analogy, I want to propose that if replacement proceeds asynchronously—so that biological and artificial substrates co-function within overlapping cognitive modules for a transitional period—then there is no discrete moment at which identity could be lost. Instead, the subject becomes a temporary hybrid system, and what shifts is not a binary survival fact but the locus of cognitive integration. I then want to ask whether this overlapping-modules picture actually dissolves the problem or merely relocates it: if integration is distributed across both substrates, do we have one person, two, or a collective? I am inclined to think this resembles arguments associated with extended cognition, but I worry I may be conflating functional coupling with metaphysical continuity. I would be grateful for comments on whether this line of reasoning is coherent, whether the absence of an abrupt boundary genuinely answers the sceptic, and whether my appeal to distributed integration is philosophically sound or a category mistake. I am particularly unsure about the middle section describing the hybrid phase.
