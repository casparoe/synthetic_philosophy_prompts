me and my friend just spent like 3 hours arguing about whether 'Western values' is even a real category and I noticed this pattern that I cant shake. someone lists values like free speech and gender equality and says they come from the Enlightenment, so they're core Western values. then you show them that plenty of Western countries had censorship for centuries and still have gender gaps, and they say 'well those are just failures to live up to the values, not part of the tradition.' but then they turn around and compare the West to some other place by saying 'we value individual autonomy, they value the collective,' using the exact same values they just admitted we often fail at. I saw it happen with secularism too: people claim secular government is a Western invention, but when you mention France's laïcité banning headscarves, they either say thats not true secularism or they use it as proof that the West protects freedom better. so theres like three different things being called 'Western values' at once: the historical genealogy, the current distinguishing feature, and the aspirational standard. and people slide between them in the same breath without noticing. is there an established name for this specific three-way slide? I know it touches on equivocation and maybe that motte-and-bailey thing but it doesnt feel like either, since the three versions arent just stronger and weaker, theyre different kinds of claims. if theres no term, can you define the edges precisely: what makes it this and not just changing the subject?
