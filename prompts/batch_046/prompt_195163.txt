I was arguing with some friends on a forum about the James vs Clifford ethics-of-belief thing — you know, wether its ever okay to believe without rock-solid proof. We kept falling back on this finance analogy: Clifford is like the ultra-cautious fiduciary who wont touch capital without audited books for every position, and James is the VC who knows some bets only pay off if you commit before full due diligence.

Can you audit how far that holds? Lay out the correspondence table — belief, capital, risk, return, prospectus, investor — then the breaks. Does it smuggle in that belief is voluntary like writing a check, and does it miss James's point about living hypotheses that are only testable if you already hold them? Theres also this claim floating around that Clifford assumes epistemic atomism — each belief stands alone — while James treats beliefs like a correlated portfolio. Does that distinction survive, or is it just dressing up? Once the disanalogies are out, especially about voluntarism, does the analogy still license James's relaxed conclusion, or was it carrying the weight the whole time?
