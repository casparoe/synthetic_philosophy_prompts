Design a decision tree that classifies modern church-state regimes by their institutional architecture rather than by country label. Use exactly four sequential binary branch nodes: (1) Does the state grant religious communities corporate status under public law, with legal personality distinct from ordinary private associations? (2) If yes, does the state administer a church-tax mechanism integrated into the income-tax system with formal opt-out provisions, rather than relying on direct budgetary subsidy or purely voluntary support? (3) Is constitutional secularism framed as negative neutrality — permitting state cooperation with religion provided there is no coercion — or as positive neutrality — requiring the state to treat religious and non-religious worldviews symmetrically across all public functions and institutions? (4) Does the regime maintain an officially established church with formal constitutional or statutory precedence, even if that precedence is now largely ceremonial?

The tree should have sixteen leaves. Each leaf must carry either a named regime category (e.g., cooperative fiscal establishment, laïcité with public-law recognition, strict separation with private-association-only status) or the label "unoccupied / unstable." For every occupied leaf, provide one sentence describing the regime's most awkward institutional tension — the feature that makes it resist classification under the simple establishment-versus-separation dichotomy. For every unoccupied leaf, briefly diagnose why the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored (for instance, whether a fiscal extraction mechanism without corporate recognition is administratively impossible, or whether positive neutrality combined with symbolic establishment collapses into contradiction).

Locate England, Germany, France, and the United States on the appropriate leaves, noting any internal divergence — federal variation, regional exception, or contradictory practice — that places parts of one country on different branches. Do not organize the tree by country, do not cite specific court cases, constitutional articles, or scholarly texts, and do not include a bibliography or reading list. The instrument should sort regimes by the institutional commitments they actually make, not by the labels they apply to themselves.
