hey, quick question about whether actual moral dilemmas exist - like you're genuinely required to do wrong no matter which way you go, not just that both options suck.

my study partner threw this at me and now i cant sleep:

(1) if a real dilemma exists, then in situation S you have an all-things-considered obligation to do A and also an all-things-considered obligation to do not-A.
(2) standard deontic logic says conflicting obligations cause explosion - suddenly every action becomes obligatory - unless you use some paraconsistent system that allows true contradictions in moral reasoning.
(3) but nobody behaves like every action is obligatory in a hard choice, so either morality needs paraconsistent logic or there are no real dilemmas.
(4) if we reject dilemmas, then all moral conflict is just weighing mistakes, and the feeling of guilt after picking one side is just regular regret, not a 'moral remainder'.
(5) but that guilt feels structurally different - it's like you failed a duty that was still binding.
(6) so saying 'no dilemmas' forces us to deny that binding-but-unfulfilled duties exist, which wipes out a huge part of what people actually report in hard choices.
(7) apply that everywhere and suddenly all moral tension is an illusion, which cant be right.

where does this slip? i really want to keep (5) and im fine with (2) being weird. also please separate whats pretty settled here (like what philosophers mean by a genuine dilemma vs an apparent one) from whats actually fought over, and dont present one side as if its the obvious consensus.
