I'm noticing a recurring move in debates that invoke the precautionary principle, and I want to know whether it has a specific name or is just a cluster of related errors. Here are two concrete patterns. In climate-engineering debates, critics demand that proponents of solar radiation management prove the absence of monsoon disruption before any deployment, treating continued warming as the risk-free baseline. In energy-transition debates, the same critics demand that proponents of rapid nuclear build-out prove the absence of long-term waste failure, treating continued fossil dependence as the risk-free baseline. The striking feature is not merely that inaction is privileged, but that which option counts as "the intervention"—and therefore bears the burden of proof—shifts with a framing choice that tracks prior normative commitments rather than any structural feature of the risks. When the baseline is described as "active policy," the framing is dismissed as rhetoric; when the technology is described as "intervention," the framing is treated as decisive.

Does this specific configuration—precautionary burden-shifting that depends on a contested, value-laden framing of which state is the intervention—have an established concept? Give me a committed answer: either name the nearest established idea, say exactly how the pattern I described differs from it, and defend that identification; or state clearly that nothing fits, coin a precise term with boundary conditions, and defend the carving—in particular, distinguish it from ordinary status-quo bias, from simple risk asymmetry, and from the non-identity problem, and say whether it applies only when both options carry risk or also to genuinely baseline cases. Don't lay out options for me to choose between; take a definite position and argue for it.
