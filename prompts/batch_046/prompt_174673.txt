For a philosophy-of-science reading group, I want to check whether my views on algorithmic inference hang together before I present them. Here is my profile:

(1) I think Solomonoff induction supplies the correct normative framework for scientific reasoning: rational priors over hypotheses should be proportional to 2 raised to the negative Kolmogorov complexity of each hypothesis, computed with respect to a fixed universal prefix-free Turing machine.

(2) I accept the standard invariance result: for any two universal machines U and V, the difference |K_U(x) − K_V(x)| is bounded by a constant that depends only on U and V.

(3) From (2) I infer that Solomonoff induction cannot deliver objectively unique prior probabilities, because selecting a different reference machine shifts every prior by a factor determined by that constant.

(4) I also hold that, for any two realistic scientific theories with descriptions long enough to encode substantive empirical content, the constant is negligible, so the ordering of theories by prior probability is effectively invariant across machine choices.

(5) I interpret Occam’s razor not as a metaphysical principle about nature’s simplicity, but as the Minimum Message Length principle: prefer the theory that minimizes the sum of the length of the theory encoding and the length of the data encoding given that theory.

(6) I take (5) to be a purely pragmatic, information-theoretic rule: it tells us which theory to adopt for predictive compression, but it carries no implication about which theories are approximately true.

(7) At the same time, I want to defend a modest scientific realism: the reason that simpler theories in the MML sense reliably succeed is not accidental, but reflects a genuine correlation in nature between descriptive simplicity and approximate truth.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and what is the least costly revision for each real tension? Rank the tensions by severity. In particular, address whether (3) and (4) can be held together without collapsing into either full objectivism about priors or full skepticism about algorithmic inference, and whether (6) and (7) can be reconciled without smuggling back in the kind of metaphysical commitment that (5) was meant to exclude. Be frank about which single revision would clean up the most inconsistencies, and note any feature of the view that the audit itself cannot settle without further argument.
