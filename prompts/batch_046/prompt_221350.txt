After my dad died I've been stuck on Hofstadter's idea that the self isn't locked in one skull but gets copied into people who knew you well. I can feel it, like I talk with his exact intonation sometimes and it feels like his 'I' is running inside mine. But heres the confusion: if the self is actually distributed across brains, shouldn't there be cases where two people share one consciousness in real time? Like if my mom and I both hold strong representations of dad, aren't our brains hosting fragments of the same 'I' simultaneously? Wouldn't that mean neuroscience should be able to detect a merged stream of experience when we think about him together? Or am I completely misreading him—maybe he only meant persistence after death, not live overlapping consciousness?
