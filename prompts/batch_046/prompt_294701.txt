I serve on my institution's Interdisciplinary Research Oversight Committee. We must render a formal decision on a proposed three-year internal study in which participants undergo guided, sustained exercises designed to suspend commitment to two specific hinge certainties drawn from Wittgenstein's later epistemology: the temporal continuity of one's own bodily identity and the independent existence of other minds. The investigators contend that because these commitments function not as ordinary beliefs but as the background framework within which doubt and reasoning operate, deliberately bracketing them can illuminate how epistemic scaffolding is reconstructed; critics contend that destabilizing hinges risks a kind of framework collapse that debriefing cannot repair, violating non-maleficence regardless of scientific value. The proposal includes a novel "scaffolded restoration" protocol, but no empirical literature validates its efficacy for hinge-level disruption. Draft the committee's decision memo—approve, approve with conditions, or reject—with reasoning the principal investigators could directly address. Include an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side: if you approve, explain specifically why the risk of framework collapse is either conceptually overstated or practically containable through the proposed safeguards; if you reject, explain why the potential scientific insight into hinge dynamics does not outweigh the duty to protect participants' basic epistemic orientation. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; reason from the conceptual structure of hinge epistemology and the practical demands of research ethics.
