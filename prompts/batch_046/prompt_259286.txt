I'm drafting a chapter on Nozick's experience machine and I'm concerned I'm conflating its critical force with a stronger metaphysical claim than is warranted. The usual reading is that the machine undermines mental-state hedonism by revealing that we care about being causally connected to reality, not just about how things seem. But I want to press this further: if well-being genuinely includes objective goods—knowledge, achievement, authentic love—then the subjective satisfaction that hybrid theories (e.g., Wolf's fitting-fulfillment view or attitude-dependent objective-list accounts) treat as a co-constituent looks redundant. In the machine, the subject possesses the mental states that correspond to these goods—she feels accomplished, loved, knowledgeable—yet we deny her life is good. So doesn't this entail that subjective satisfaction has no independent prudential weight, functioning only as an epiphenomenal indicator that the objective conditions are met? If so, hybrid theories collapse into pure objectivism with psychological ornamentation. Still, some recent work in prudential value pluralism proposes that subjective states might operate as a subordinate, boundary-constrained contributor—something like a tie-breaker among objectively equivalent lives, but only within the domain of real, non-simulated achievement. Could the experience machine be interpreted precisely as fixing those boundary conditions, showing that mental-state contribution is non-derivative yet strictly conditional on not being machine-generated? And if that is the correct reading, what distinguishes "machine-generated" satisfaction from genuine satisfaction in a way that doesn't secretly reintroduce the objective conditions as the sole bearers of value?
