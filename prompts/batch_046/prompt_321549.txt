Write a pantoum—four-line stanzas in which lines 2 and 4 of each stanza return as lines 1 and 3 of the next—set in late 1948 in the voice of Raphael Lemkin after the UN Convention’s adoption. The poem’s formal repetition should enact the historical truncation of his original 1944 concept: begin with language evoking the broad dimensions he first named—cultural, political, social, and physical destruction of a group as such—then, with each successive repetition, replace one of those dimensions with the narrower legal categories that survived into Article II: the four protected groups and the five acts of physical or biological destruction. By the final stanza, only the Convention’s legal language should remain intact, while the excised dimensions survive only as absence, ghost rhyme, or fractured syntax. Do not include footnotes, citations, or a bibliography; the argument should be carried entirely by the form’s returning and disappearing lines. Write in American English, roughly 16–20 lines, and make the loss structurally audible: a reader familiar with Lemkin’s work should recognize the specific drafting exclusion of cultural genocide, and a reader unfamiliar should still feel the tightening of the net as repetition strips the language away.
