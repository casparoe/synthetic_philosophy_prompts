The philosophical debate over Einstein's hole argument has reached a stall that I find structurally puzzling. All parties accept the formal construction: apply a smooth, active diffeomorphism confined to a matter-free region, hold the metric fixed outside, and generate a new solution to the field equations that is mathematically distinct inside the hole but empirically indistinguishable from the original everywhere matter exists. Substantivalists take this to show that if spacetime points have primitive identity, then determinism fails; they typically respond by denying primitive identity or adopting a structuralist ontology of points. Relationalists take the same formal result to demonstrate that only spatiotemporal relations carry physical content, so that the two metrics represent one and the same world. Despite decades of exchange, neither camp has persuaded the other, and recent literature largely recapitulates these two moves with greater technical precision rather than resolving them.

My hypothesis is that the disagreement is not genuinely about determinism or about ontology, but about a hidden normative question: what conditions must be satisfied for two mathematical representations to count as describing the same physical situation? The substantivalist appeals to an invariant field-structure that survives point-relabeling; the relationalist appeals to the invariance of all observable relations. But these are not competing answers to a shared question — they are different conceptions of what needs explaining. So the stall may be verbal at root: both sides claim that the physics is unchanged, yet one means that the ontology of points is relationally determined, while the other means that the field configuration is structurally unique.

If that diagnosis is correct, is there any formal distinction — perhaps involving the explanatory priority of the metric field over the manifold, or a precise criterion for representational equivalence in gauge theories — that could break the symmetry? Or is the hole argument better understood as an ill-posed problem that reveals we lack a neutral framework for individuating spacetime events, meaning progress should come not from resolving the argument but from abandoning the presupposition that determinism requires point-wise identity? I want a diagnosis of the disagreement's structure, not a survey of substantivalist versus relationalist positions, together with an honest assessment of whether anything — a conceptual distinction, a result from differential geometry applied to gauge symmetry, or a reformulation of the determinism question — could actually move the debate.
