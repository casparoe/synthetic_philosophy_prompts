I sit on the governance board of the Meridian Regional Inquiry Consortium, whose founding charter adopts John Dewey's experimentalist view that democratic deliberation and scientific inquiry share a single logic of problem-solving under conditions of uncertainty. We must vote next Thursday on whether to enter a binding, ten-year cooperative agreement with a watershed-monitoring collective that allocates fixed institutional resources and permits withdrawal only for documented breach. A faction of board members argues that Dewey's insistence on the continuous revisability of beliefs and the provisional character of all institutional arrangements makes any long-term, non-renewable commitment incompatible with our charter; they propose instead an annual renewable memorandum with no penalty for non-renewal. The opposing view holds that Dewey distinguishes between the fallible process of inquiry and the stable social conditions required to sustain cooperative investigation, so that binding agreements are instruments rather than obstacles to experimental democracy. Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before the vote: recommend either approval of the ten-year agreement with a specified implementation timeline or its rejection in favor of the annual framework with a transition schedule, engage the anti-binding argument at its strongest without merely labeling it a misreading of Dewey, and articulate how the distinction between inquiry as method and inquiry as institutional practice should guide our decision. Avoid bibliographic references or appeals to authority; the board needs reasoning that stands on its own conceptual merits.
