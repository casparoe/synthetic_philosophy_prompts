I'm currently preparing a seminar presentation for a course in early modern moral philosophy, and I've become particularly absorbed by the tension in Michel de Montaigne's essay on cannibals between his skeptical dismantling of European moral superiority and his simultaneous refusal to romanticize foreign societies into abstract noble savages. This has led me to consider how his particular brand of humanist relativism might engage with an institutional and ethical problem that only crystallized centuries after his death: the global movement for cultural restitution, specifically the repatriation of ceremonial objects, artifacts, and ancestral remains held in Western museums to indigenous and post-colonial communities, particularly in cases where acquisition was technically lawful under colonial legal regimes yet embedded in profoundly asymmetrical structures of power.

Given Montaigne's insistence that local custom and practice carry genuine ethical weight, his deep suspicion that Europeans mistake their own contingent norms for universal reason, and his corresponding caution against projecting idealized fantasies onto cultures they barely understand, how do you think he would assess the moral case for restitution? Would he likely regard the demand for return as a necessary humbling of European arrogance and a recognition that these objects belong to living traditions rather than to universal museums? Or might he worry that framing the dispute through modern international legal categories and museum ethics protocols risks imposing yet another layer of foreign conceptual domination—substituting one European framework for another, even when the intent is corrective?

Please provide a reasoned philosophical projection of Montaigne's probable stance, tracing how his characteristic commitments about cross-cultural judgment, the limits of human knowledge, and the ethics of possession might extend into this anachronistic debate. I am not looking for a bibliography, precise textual citations, or a summary of existing scholarship; I want a coherent argument about how his voice might sound in this specific context.
