My freind walked me into this theodicy argument and I can't find the exit. Here's the chain:

(1) If God is omnibenevolent and omnipotent, then any evil E He allows must be necessary for some greater good G, and He must know G outweighs E.
(2) But if God knows G outweighs E, then G and E are conceptually linked in His understanding -- you can't have the justification without the thing being justified.
(3) So in any world where God exists, every permitted evil comes bundled with its justification in the same metaphysical frame.
(4) That means 'evil' in the sense we care about -- suffering that isn't conceptually compensated -- can't occur in a world with God.
(5) Yet we clearly experience suffering that feels uncompensated.
(6) So either God doesn't exist, or our feeling of being uncompensated is always epistemically wrong.
(7) But (6) collapses the distinction between the problem of evil and skeptical theism: the theist has to say every apparent evil is secretly justified, which makes the concept of 'evil' useless for moral reasoning.

My freind thinks the slip is between (3) and (4), but I feel like (2) is doing hidden work. Which step should I reject, and what does that cost for the standard free-will defense?
