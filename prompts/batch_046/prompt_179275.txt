I'm drafting a short essay for an advanced philosophy seminar on food ethics, tentatively titled "The Confidence Gradient and Agricultural Harm: Comparing Beef, Chicken, and Crop Cultivation." Rather than counting lives or calories alone, I want to argue that the moral comparison should use a sentience-confidence weighted measure of suffering-years, combined with a distinction between direct, institutionally intended harm and indirect, habitat-mediated harm. The core claim is that beef imposes high-confidence direct harm on cattle plus extensive indirect harm through pasture and feed-crop land use; chicken imposes high-confidence direct harm on a far larger number of sentient individuals but with a smaller land footprint; and plant-based diets impose only indirect harm, but primarily on wild animals—mostly rodents and invertebrates—whose sentience we should weight lower on a confidence gradient rather than treating as either fully moral patients or fully negligible. I further suggest that this gradient lets us avoid both a sharp, arbitrary sentience cutoff and an implausible equality of all biological suffering. I conclude that beef is likely the worst option, but that the gap between chicken and crops is narrower than standard land-use arguments suggest, because chicken's direct harm is high-confidence and numerically massive, whereas crop-related wild-animal deaths, though numerous, fall mainly in the lower-confidence range.

Please comment on this draft, especially on whether the "confidence gradient" framing is conceptually coherent or collapses into an unmotivated sliding scale, whether the direct/indirect harm distinction can be maintained without secretly importing the doctrine of double effect, and whether I've underestimated the replacement effects—specifically, whether reducing beef consumption actually reduces wild-animal suffering if pastureland converts to cropland rather than being abandoned. In your response, please state explicitly what consideration would change your assessment—for example, robust empirical evidence of invertebrate consciousness, or a principled argument that confidence-weighting violates equal moral consideration—and flag the point in the reasoning where you think it is most likely to fail. Do not provide a bibliography or reading list; I want your evaluation based on the argument as presented.
