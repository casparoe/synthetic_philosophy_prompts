I'd like an audit of whether the following package is jointly tenable. (1) I take Solomonoff induction to be normatively correct: a rational sequential predictor should weight computable hypotheses by 2 raised to the negative of their Kolmogorov complexity. (2) I accept that Kolmogorov complexity is invariant only up to an additive constant determined by the choice of universal machine, so there is no language-independent fact of the matter about which of two theories is genuinely simpler. (3) Despite (2), I want to defend Occam's razor as a substantive, non-heuristic constraint on scientific theory choice. (4) My proposal is to relativize simplicity judgments to our current best formal framework: we should prefer the theory with the lowest description length in that framework. (5) At the same time, I hold that if two theories are empirically equivalent in every possible observation, rationality requires strict indifference between them, regardless of any internal difference in simplicity. Do (1) and (5) pull in opposite directions? Does (4) save (3) without undermining the universality claimed in (1)? Is there a hidden tension between treating the Solomonoff prior as framework-independent and treating simplicity as framework-bound? Please rank the conflicts by severity, specify which commitments are in tension, and for each say what would have to be given up and at what cost; in particular, indicate whether (5) can be retained without abandoning (1).
