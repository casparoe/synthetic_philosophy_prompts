For a dissertation chapter on the metaphysics of scientific law, I am exploring how institutional framing obscures the distinction between Humean regularity theories and anti-Humean necessitarian accounts. Write a deadpan parody, in the precise legalistic voice of a technology End User License Agreement, of a fictional "Universal Ontology EULA" drafted by the "Institute for Systematic Physics." The agreement should present two licensing tiers: a "Regularity Package," in which laws are treated as contingent patterns with statistical drift and no modal enforcement, and a "Necessitarian Premium," in which laws are treated as metaphysically necessary relations with built-in modal enforcement and non-transferable essential properties. Through the contract's terms, disclaimers, and update clauses, expose—without ever stating them explicitly—the standard objections to each view: that regularity cannot ground genuine modal knowledge, that necessitarianism relies on unexplained necessary connections, and that both frameworks covertly depend on epistemic virtues such as simplicity and strength. Keep the tone consistently deadpan and bureaucratic. Approximately 500 words. No citations or bibliographies are needed.
