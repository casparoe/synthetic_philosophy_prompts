As a high school student drafting remarks for a philosophy seminar on the varieties of free will worth wanting, I want to check whether my position holds together before I present it. Here are my commitments: (1) I am a compatibilist, so I hold that determinism does not by itself eliminate free will; (2) I reject the Principle of Alternate Possibilities, because Frankfurt-style manipulation cases show that moral responsibility can survive without genuine alternatives; (3) I think the free will worth wanting is diachronic narrative integration — the capacity to see one's actions as authored chapters in a single coherent life story — and I take this to be fully compatible with determinism; (4) I believe that under determinism the boundary between acting from one's reflectively endorsed values and being causally shaped by one's environment is only a difference in internal architecture, not in metaphysical origin, which means traditional sourcehood conditions collapse; (5) I also hold that narrative integration requires modal robustness, so there must be nearby possible worlds in which I could have authored different chapters without my entire life narrative disintegrating. Do these hang together? Please respond as a numbered list with exactly five items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion. In item 1 identify the severest tension, rank its severity, and state the least costly revision. In item 2 do the same for the second-most-severe tension. In item 3 point out which apparent conflict actually dissolves on closer inspection and explain why. In item 4 say whether the package can be salvaged without abandoning compatibilism. In item 5 name the single commitment whose removal would clean up the most problems and say what is lost by giving it up.
