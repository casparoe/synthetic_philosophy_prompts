Assume W. E. B. Du Bois is alive today and must respond to a mode of cultural production that emerged only after his death: generative systems that adapt visual, musical, and narrative artworks in real time to an individual viewer's emotional state and political predispositions, yielding aesthetically refined outputs engineered to deepen the viewer's existing commitments without disclosing that the work has been algorithmically personalized. Drawing on Du Bois's well-known philosophy from the 1920s—that art is always propaganda, that beauty cannot be separated from truth, and that Black artists bear a distinctive obligation to serve liberation rather than to pursue art for art's sake—predict how he would evaluate this technology. Specifically, address whether he would treat real-time personalization as merely an intensification of the propaganda function he already described, or as a qualitatively new phenomenon that undermines the viewer's autonomy in ways that change the ethical demands on artists. In constructing your answer, identify the core commitments in Du Bois's view (the inseparability of aesthetic and didactic value, the importance of the artist's conscious intentionality, and the link between beauty and racial justice), apply them systematically to the scenario, and indicate exactly where his framework extends cleanly to cover algorithmic personalization and where it requires supplementation by concepts he did not possess. Do not provide a bibliography or cite specific texts; the task is philosophical reconstruction, not historical scholarship.
