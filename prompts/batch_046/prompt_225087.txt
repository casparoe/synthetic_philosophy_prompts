Read Lord of the Flies through the lens of societal collapse — but specifically the debate about whether places like the Bronze Age kingdoms or the Maya actually "collapsed" or just reorganized into smaller, different systems. The boys start with Ralph's rules and end with Jack's tribe; is that a failure of society, or is Jack's group a functioning (if brutal) social order, like the shift on Easter Island away from moai-building toward the bird-man cult? I'm especally interested in whether Piggy's glasses and the conch work like "institutional memory" that gets abandoned, and whether the novel assumes collapse is real or just change we don't like. Take Golding seriously and take the collapse-vs-change framework seriously — don't just make the book an ilustration. Where does the novel resist the comparison?
