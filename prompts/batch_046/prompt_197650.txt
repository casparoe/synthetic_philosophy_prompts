The charter for these co-ops literally cites Locke's idea that the mind starts blank and that knowledge has to come through experience, not authority. But what philosophical commitments does that specific rule actually presuppose? Does it need Locke's tabula rasa, or could an innatist about spatial reasoning run the same classroom? Does it assume self-deception isn't a real risk — that a child can always recognize her own error once she articulates it — and is that consistent with Locke's own worries about custom and prejudice shaping belief? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say what's strictly required vs. what's just compatible, and tell me what parts of the practice would have to change if we dropped the view that direct correction by an authority is always epistemically harmful.
