Case 1: The classic industrial factory worker. He sells his labor-power for a subsistence wage, produces surplus value appropriated by the capitalist, understands the mechanism of extraction, and actively resents it. Verdict: clearly exploited and clearly alienated—estranged from the product, the labor process, his species-being, and his fellow workers.

Case 2: A contemporary gig-economy courier. She owns her bicycle and smartphone, selects her own shifts, but is algorithmically managed, retains none of the surplus value generated by deliveries, and describes feeling like "a ghost in the machine" with no meaningful connection to the goods she carries. Verdict: exploited (surplus is extracted via platform control despite formal independence), and alienated specifically in relation to the product and the labor process, though not in the classical factory mode.

He writes climate-modeling code he believes in, works reasonable hours, and receives a generous share of profits. Verdict: not exploited—there is no external capitalist class extracting surplus—but alienated, specifically in the dimension of self-realization or species-being, despite fully favorable material and ownership conditions.

Case 4: An enslaved agricultural worker in a pre-capitalist society. He produces directly for a master who consumes the surplus; he is physically brutalized and legally unfree. However, he expresses pride in the harvest's quality and feels no separation from the product, since he lives in the master's household and sees the food consumed there. Verdict: exploited (unfree extraction of surplus labor), but not alienated in the Marxist technical sense—no estrangement from product, process, species-being, or fellow workers.

Case 5: A "passion-economy" content creator. She owns her camera and editing software, retains 100 percent of revenue, and faces no capitalist employer. Verdict: not exploited in the Marxist technical sense—no surplus-value extraction by capital—yet profoundly alienated, perhaps in a form Marx did not fully anticipate: estrangement not from an external product but from one's own subjectivity mediated by circulation and consumption.
