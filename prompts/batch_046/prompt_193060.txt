I've been working on this long feature about whether historians can ever really escape the moral frame of their own era — presentism, but not the boring textbook version. I was discussing it with friends over drinks, actually with my editor who covers politics, and we started listing gut-check cases and now I'm obsessed with finding teh actual principle behind my reactions.

Here's where I land. Calling Isaac Newton a 'bad scientist' because he wasted years on alchemy? Not presentist, he was wrong by his own lights too. But calling him 'morally deficient' for being rude to Hooke? That feels presentist — manners change. Then there's judging a 19th-century factory owner for not installing safety guards. I think that's fair, not presentist, because the danger was obvious then and people did complain. But judging that same owner for not offering maternaty leave? That feels like imposing modern labor norms that weren't availible then. Another one: saying a 1960s psychologist was 'unethical' for using deception in experiments. I'm split — deception was debated then, but not in the same framework. And here's the one I keep flip-flopping on: condemning ancient Greek philosophers for owning slaves. Part of me says they knew it was wrong — some of them said so — but part of me says they literally couldn't imagine the alternative, so calling them villains is like blaming a fish for swimming.

So what's the rule? Is it about whether the criticism was conceptually available inside their own world? Or is there something about harm being visible regardless of era? I want the principle that captures all these, and I especially want to know which verdict I should drop if I have to choose between consistency and my gut on the Greek slavery case. Oh, and one more — if we praise a historical figure for being 'ahead of their time' on gender or race, are we doing the mirror-image of presentism, or is that different? And if historians in 2200 condemn our meat-heavy diets, is that presentist, or just them being right in a way that transcends time?
