I'm on the grant review panel for our city's international history memorial fund. We've got an application for a permanent exhibit that frames the origins of WWI entirely through the Fischer line -- that Berlin actively sought a general European war in 1914 -- and then invites visitors to assign a "collective moral residue" score to present-day Germany based on that historical causation. Our charter prohibits funding anything that assigns "enduring national character flaws," but nobody agrees whether explanatory blame for a war counts as a character assignment. I need a memo for Thrusday's vote: recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions. It needs a clear bottom line, real reasoning that engages the strongest argument on the losing side, and it should tackle the philosophical knot of what it means to hold a nation responsible when every individual decision-maker is dead and the institutional structures have been rebuilt twice over -- is it like blaming a corporation that replaced all its board members, or is that analogy misleading? Don't just restate legal definitions. Also the applicant spelled "controversy" as "controversey" in the cover letter, which feels like a bad omen but probably shouldn't affect our vote.
