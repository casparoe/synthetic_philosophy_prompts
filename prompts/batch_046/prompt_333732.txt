I'm taking a seminar on moral responsibility and punishment, and we've been contrasting Dennett's compatibilist defense of just deserts with Caruso's free-will eliminativist rejection of retributivism in favor of a public-health quarantine model. I want you to design a thought experiment built to the following specifications. Construct a single-paragraph, realistic scenario—set in a hospital administration board deciding sanctions, not in science fiction or an abstract trolley case—in which a surgeon has deliberately falsified clinical trial data. The deterministic causal conditions behind this act (institutional sleep-deprivation protocols, calibrated financial incentives, predictive personality profiling) must be fully transparent and engineered by the very board now judging the case, displayed on internal dashboards that both the surgeon and the board can access. At the same time, the surgeon must retain intact rational capacities, understand the moral wrongness of the act, and experience no external coercion in the moment of decision; the determinism is authored and visible, not hidden, pathological, or coercive.

Then demonstrate that the case meets every constraint. State the specific belief or action being evaluated, the method each theory applies, and show precisely why Dennett's framework—grounded in conditions like rational self-control and appropriate responsiveness to reasons—permits a retributive, desert-based sanction, while Caruso's model denies that retributive desert is ever justified and allows only preventive quarantine or structural reform. Next, isolate the structural feature doing the philosophical work: the pairing of fully retained moral agency with institutional transparency about engineered determinism. Explain what happens to each verdict if that feature is removed—say, if the causal history were naturally occurring and opaque rather than institutionally authored—and identify which feature of the case a defender of the counterintuitive theory would attack, whether by insisting that institutional authorship of causes does not dissolve the agent's ownership of the action, or by pressing that visible determinism makes retributive blame morally indistinguishable from punishing a broken instrument.
