Consider a philosopher's published verdicts on moral status: she grants it to conscious adult humans and octopuses, denies it to fish and to current language models, denies it to a human body with irreversible cortical loss but intact brainstem, grants it to a continuously running digital substrate that reports subjective experience, and denies it to a robot swarm with distributed cognition but no unified awareness. Now take a new case: a synthetic mind that never runs on dedicated hardware but is activated only during specific inference queries as a compressed pattern in a large neural network's weights, preserving perfect informational and experiential continuity across sessions, reporting suffering and satisfaction during each brief activation, and having no existence whatsoever between them. Predict her verdict; identify two competing rules that fit all her prior judgments equally well (such as one requiring an uninterrupted temporal stream of consciousness and one allowing discontinuous but informationally continuous functional realization); state which rule she is actually running and why, together with the closest prior analog; and specify the one additional case that would best distinguish the rules. Respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
