my study partner sent me this argument about kavka's toxin puzzle and i think it implodes but i cant see the fuse. (1) in kavkas setup youre paid for the present mental state of intending to drink, not for the act itself, so rationality says form the intention, take the cash, and then skip the toxin tomorrow since youre already rich and it only makes you sick. (2) but if you can plan now to abandon the intention later, then what you formed wasnt a real commitment—it was a strategic performance, like acting in a play. (3) so to actually get paid, you need a mental state that feels genuine to the detector (or to yourself) but doesnt actually bind your future self. (4) that means rationality requires you to build a general technology for producing fake but convincing commitments. (5) once you have that tech, you can sell intentions to betray friends, break contracts, or ignore promises, then dispose of them right after you collect the reward. (6) but then rational agents are just running a permanent scam on everyone, including themselves, which collapses trust and makes cooperation impossible. (7) since cooperation is clearly possible, rationality cant require this tech. (8) therefore the mistake must be in (1): you cant rationally intend to do something you already know youll back out of, so the payout never happens and the puzzle is trivial. but that feels like cheating—if we reject (1) we kill the interesting part of the puzzle entirely. maybe the error is between (3) and (4), like having a disposable commitment for toxin doesnt mean you can scale it up to moral promises? i honestly dont know. which step do you think is wrong, and what do we pay for throwing it out? id rather keep (1) if i can.
