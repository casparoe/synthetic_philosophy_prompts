Consider the problem, central to Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis, of whether a system of values can be a genuine social cause without collapsing into either idealist autonomy or materialist epiphenomenalism. Four strategies for rendering cultural values causally efficacious can be articulated. (1) The "elective affinity" strategy: Calvinist asceticism and capitalist rationalization share an internal logical form, so culture selects and sustains economic structures without acting as an efficient cause. (2) The "institutional sedimentation" strategy: religious values become causally effective only once translated into legal, organizational, and accounting practices. (3) The "psycho-rational restructuring" strategy: ascetic discipline directly reshapes individual utility calculations, making culture a micro-level cause of economic behavior. (4) The "normative counterfactual" strategy: Protestant ethics chiefly remove moral barriers to accumulation that other value systems uphold, so culture operates by altering normative boundary conditions rather than generating motives or structures.

Rank these four strategies from tier 1 (best solves the problem) to tier 4 (worst solves the problem), assessing both philosophical coherence—avoiding both reductionism and idealism—and fidelity to Weber's own methodological commitments. Assign each strategy a tier and defend its rank in exactly one sentence. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; reason solely from the conceptual structure of each position.
