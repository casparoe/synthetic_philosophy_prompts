Write a dialogue between two philosophers, A and B, concerning Nozick's claim that moral reasoning about animals should be broadly utilitarian, whereas reasoning about people should be governed by Kantian-style side-constraints. A defends the binary view: humans alone possess rational self-determination sufficient to ground inviolable side-constraints, while animals warrant only a welfare-maximizing calculus with minimal anti-cruelty limits. B advances a counter-position she calls reflexive-status scaling, arguing that moral protection ought to scale continuously with an entity's capacity for second-order normative self-assessment; on this view, some non-human animals and some severely impaired humans occupy intermediate zones that a sharp human-animal boundary cannot capture without arbitrary line-drawing.

Structure the dialogue as exactly five numbered exchanges. Within each exchange, both speakers respond directly to the point just made, with neither speaking past the other; each speaker should contribute no more than two sentences. Do not include an introduction or conclusion. Both interlocutors should be equally competent, with no strawman. Keep the discussion in the weeds—examine whether rational long-term planning is genuinely all-or-nothing, whether second-order awareness admits of degrees, and whether any binary boundary can avoid ad hoc exceptions—without requesting citations, reading lists, or references to specific texts.
