I am convening a graduate seminar on the normative architecture of historical comparison, and I want us to begin not with any particular verdict—whether that atrocities can be ranked by magnitude, whether all comparison is an obscenity, or whether multidimensional evaluation is possible—but with the full specification of what an adequate theory in this area must deliver.

Write the desiderata: the complete list of requirements a framework for comparing mass atrocities must meet if it is to count as conceptually adequate rather than merely rhetorically convenient. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or consideration—such as the need to distinguish quantitative magnitude from qualitative character without reducing either to the other; the need to account for institutional causation as well as intentional agency; the need to preserve the non-fungibility of victim experience without collapsing into absolute incommensurability; the need to address whether comparison itself inflicts representational harm upon survivor communities; and the need to handle temporal indexicality, since comparing events across different historical regimes requires an implicit theory of moral constancy or progress. Specify which distinctions the theory must not collapse—for example, between describing structural violence and excusing it, or between assigning moral weight and assigning a numerical rank.

Then identify which single desideratum does the most damage to the popular contenders, giving your reasoning: is it the prohibition on cardinal measurement of suffering, the demand for cross-cultural validity without universalist imperialism, or the obligation to preserve the uniqueness of each event?

Next, flag any pair of requirements that you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable—particularly if one demands comparative judgment for purposes of policy or memorial allocation while the other demands non-commensuration out of respect.

Please write in British English. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want the conceptual architecture and its internal tensions, not a survey of the literature.
