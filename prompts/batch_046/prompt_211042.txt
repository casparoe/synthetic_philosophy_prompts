He should argue that thermohaline regimes are merely predictive seams: their boundaries depend on contingent boundary conditions—varying salinity gradients, temperature differentials, and basin geometries—rather than on an intrinsic compositional identity shared by all members. Alvarez should defend a form of interventionist pluralism: she should argue that if a regime displays counterfactual robustness across the modal range defined by stability-under-perturbation—remaining intact despite perturbations in its input variables—then that dynamic stability is an objective feature of the world, not merely an epistemic convenience or pragmatic grouping.

The dialogue must show real back-and-forth: each speaker should directly address the other's preceding point, concede minor issues where warranted, and avoid talking past one another. Neither should be a strawman; both should display equal philosophical competence.
