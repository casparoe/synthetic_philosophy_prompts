I've been re-reading parts of Hofstadter's GEB lately—specifcally the strange-loop chapters where he draws the Gödelian analogy between self-referential formulas in formal arithmetic and self-aware minds. Most pop-science takes I've seen just say 'brains are strange loops, therefore consciousness is self-reference' and call it a day, which feels like it's skipping about ten intermediate steps. I'm trying to work out what the analogy actually commits us to if we take it seriously rather than poetically, especially since I keep seeing people apply it to AI without checking whether the formal structure even maps.

So here's the crux I'm stuck on: there's a real gap between syntactic self-reference—where a formula talks about its own Gödel number via explicit arithmetic encoding—and semantic self-awareness, where a mind represents itself as having representations. Hofstadter slides between these pretty freely, but formal systems achieve self-reference through a specific encoding scheme that minds, and certainly current LLMs, don't obviously possess. Given that, what are the most important implications of the Gödelian analogy for how we should think about machine consciousness? Does it suggest that true self-awareness requires some kind of recursive semantic closure—maybe a representational system that can encode not just its outputs but the rules generating them—or is that importing too much from the formal case?
