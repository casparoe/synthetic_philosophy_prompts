Lately I got kind of obsessed with Jain anekāntavāda — the idea that reality is too complex for one viewpoint and you need syādvāda, that conditional maybe-logic, to say anything true without overstating. I've started thinking this isn't just old mysticism but actually describes quantom mechanics better than Copenhagen or Everett: wave descriptions and particle descriptions are both conditionally valid from different naya, different standpoints, and neither is the single ontology.

So here's my position: anekāntavāda gives a better metaphysical framework for quantum interpertation because it lets contradictory descriptions be simultaneously true in different aspects, rather than forcing us to pick one hidden reality. But I know I'm probably just overfitting my old lab instincts onto stuff I barely understand. What would count against this? I want an honest assessment of how exposed the view is. Which assumptions could fail — the epistemic humility part, the metaphysical pluralism, or the specific syādvāda logic? If quantom mechanics turned out to have exactly one hidden ontology, what would the world have to look like, and is that escape route live or just logically possible? And here's the thing I'm worried about: if every contradiction can be absorbed into just another standpoint, have I qualified this into unfalsifiability? Does it predict anything that standard interpretations don't? I'd like to know whether I'm holding something that risks nothing at all, and whether that's actually a problem for a philosophical view of this kind.
