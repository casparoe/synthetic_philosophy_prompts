I'm delivering the opening remarks at a welcome workshop for people who have recently arrived here from places with very different habits around queuing, tipping service staff, and crossing streets. I want the talk to ask whether these everyday conventions are actual moral obligations or merely local coordination tricks, and whether a visitor must follow them right away even when they feel arbitrary. I've been turning over a distinction that seems important to me: breaking a convention because you genuinely do not yet understand it feels different from breaking it because you understand it perfectly and reject it as foolish. I want the remarks to treat that difference as structurally real, without sounding like a philosophy lecture. The audience includes people who feel pressure to erase their own customs, so the speech must avoid saying that assimilation is a straightforward duty; but it should also not collapse into the view that every local habit is merely optional preference. Please write these remarks so that this conceptual point shapes what can and cannot be said, rather than decorating a generic welcome message.
