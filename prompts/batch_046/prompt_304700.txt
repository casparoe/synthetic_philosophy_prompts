Compare the phenomenal-homogeneity and the pro-attitudinal-unity resolutions of the heterogeneity problem for hedonism. The former holds that all pleasures share an intrinsic, positively valenced qualitative character that remains constant beneath differences in object—whether gustatory, aesthetic, or intellectual—while the latter denies any such common feel and maintains that pleasures are unified only by a functional relation: being welcomed or desired for their own sake, regardless of content or sensory modality.

Your task is not to summarise these views but to judge which offers the more defensible account of why we apply a single concept—pleasure—to states as disparate as bodily warmth, the resolution of a mathematical proof, and emotional reconciliation, without reducing one kind to another. To test this, consider the following constructed case: a thinker describes a prolonged, non-affective state of 'structural clarity' that arises when she grasps that an apparently contradictory formal system is actually consistent; she reports no warmth, excitement, or positively tinged sensation, yet actively seeks to re-enter the state and regards its absence as a deprivation. Must the phenomenal-homogeneity theorist classify this as pleasure, and can the pro-attitudinal theorist treat it as welcomed for its own sake without stretching that notion beyond recognition?

Please say how confident you are in what you claim, and single out the point you are least sure of. Write in British English.
