ok so i was trying to wrap my head around Scheffler's afterlife conjecture — the idea that if we knew humanity went extinct right after we died, we'd stop caring about climate research or cancer cures because theres no "afterlife" of future people to inherit our work. he says extinction is bad not just from the deaths but because it kills the context that makes our projects matter. but then i tried to push this into an argument and now it feels like it proves way too much.

(1) If extinction is specially bad because it removes the future people who give present projects their point, then the value of what i do now depends on an unbroken chain of people stretching forward — call it the inheritance frame.

(2) So under the inheritance frame, an action has value only if there is some future person who can, in principle, carry it forward or be affected by it.

(3) But heres the thing: even if humanity survives for millions of years, every specific thing i do — planting a tree, writing a poem, passing a law — eventually gets destroyed, forgotten, or dissolved. theres always an "extinction event" for the specific project, just delayed.

(4) So if (2) is right, then planting a tree that lives 80 years has basically the same problem as human extinction: eventually nobody remembers it, so its value should collapse just like climate research would.

(5) In fact, we should think that writing a poem that burns next week and planting a tree that lasts a century are equally worthless, since both eventually hit the same wall of being forgotten.

(6) But obviously we dont think that. we think the tree is valuable even though it wont last forever.

(7) So either (2) is false and Scheffler's reason for extinction being specially bad falls apart, or we have to accept that almost everything we do is meaningless. the second option is nuts.

i'd really rather keep (1) — it feels right that extinction is bad for this "afterlife" reason — but i cant see where the slide happens between (2) and (5). is there an equivocation between the species continuing and each specific project continuing? or is (3) just wrong about how value works? which step do i reject, and what do i have to pay for giving it up? also does the inheritance frame survive at all?
