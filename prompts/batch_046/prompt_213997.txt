A metaphysician colleague has consistently ruled on material constitution and persistence cases as follows. When a bronze statue loses an arm and receives an identical replacement, she judges it the same statue. When that statue is melted and recast as a bell, she judges the statue destroyed. When a clay statue is split cleanly in two and each half reshaped into a smaller figure, she judges the original destroyed and denies that either result is it. When a wooden statue has roughly forty percent of its boards replaced over decades by a restorer using identical wood, she judges it still the original. She has declined to rule on total material turnover, saying such cases turn on details she has not settled.

Consider now a case she has not addressed. A statue is fabricated entirely from a biodegradable polymer carrying a unique isotopic tracer. An embedded printer in its pedestal continuously scans its form and replaces degraded material with fresh polymer carrying the same tracer. Over twenty-five years every molecule of the original polymer has turned over, yet the form has never been interrupted. The degraded material was captured in a reservoir rather than discarded. At year twenty-five, engineers use that reservoir material—mixed with fresh polymer—to construct a second, identical statue elsewhere, also equipped with an embedded maintenance printer. During year twelve the original printer malfunctioned for seventy-two hours; in that window about 0.3 percent of the statue degraded without replacement, leaving a microscopic gap that was filled immediately upon repair.

What should she say, for consistency with her own track record, about whether the statue in the original gallery is the object placed there twenty-five years ago, and about the status of the second statue? Identify at least two general rules about artifact persistence that are jointly consistent with her earlier verdicts but diverge on this case; for each, state what it predicts here, including about the significance of the seventy-two-hour gap. Give your best-guess verdict with reasoning, and then propose one further hypothetical case that would best distinguish these two rules from each other.
