Write a short story about two siblings who inherit a family farm and agree to split it using a computer program they both commit to—no human override allowed. The idea is program equilibrium: they dont pick strategies, they run code that picks for them. The program divides land by soil quality, but it has a hidden dependency on a weather API that went offline years ago. They run it anyway. One gets a floodplain, the other gets dry rock. The code says thats the split, and neither can change it without breaking the contract. I want the story to dig into whether sticking to the program is still rational when you know its broken, what it means to commit to a decision you cant understand, and whether "equilibrium" here is just a fancy word for being trapped by your own rules. Maybe one sibling is a coder who spots the stale data but realizes the contract forbids her from patching it. Keep it philosophical but not preachy, and let the ending be ambiguous—does she honor the bug or break the code?
