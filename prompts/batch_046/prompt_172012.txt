As a journalist covering the history of ideas, I have adopted a working thesis about Plutarch’s Parallel Lives that I need rigorously stress-tested before I commit to a long-form argument. The thesis is that the work functions as a deliberately engineered moral technology rather than biography in any modern historiographical sense: the paired structure and the synkrisis comparisons are designed to induce calibrated cognitive dissonance in the reader—what I am calling “moral stress-testing”—thereby cultivating phronesis through emotional attachment to exemplars who remain irreducibly flawed rather than offering models for straightforward imitation. On this reading, the hagiographic drift visible in the manuscript tradition and the strategic narrative silences are not corruptions of an underlying historical record but integral functional components; the comparison sections do not resolve ambiguity but sustain it, forcing the reader to adjudicate competing values without authorial relief. What would have to be true for this interpretation to collapse? I want the defeaters sorted by severity: not merely the familiar reminder that ancient biography served conventions alien to modern ones, but specific textual patterns, structural placements—say, the framing of death scenes or the sequencing of virtue and vice within a life—comparative cases from other Greco-Roman biographers, or reception-history evidence showing that actual readers have consistently extracted unambiguous moral exemplars rather than ambivalent ones. Which of these patterns or cases would defenders of the moral-technology reading themselves concede as genuinely awkward, and which would they dismiss as misreading the design? I also want an honest assessment of insulation risk: if every contradiction or omission in the Lives can be interpreted as intentional ambiguity serving the stress-testing function, has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability? If so, is that a methodological vice for a claim about authorial design, or is it a defensible feature of interpreting a work that genuinely operates this way? Rank the objections from fatal to merely uncomfortable, and say clearly whether the position risks anything concrete or has been protected by the very ambiguity it celebrates.
