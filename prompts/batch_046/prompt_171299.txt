I'm a phd student in philosophy of science and I've been arguing with two people in my enviro-phil seminar about whether the Anthropocene is actually a historical period with a real start date or just a useful narrative frame. One side (let's call them the stratigraphers) thinks the only legitimate boundary is the one the ICS might eventually ratify, probably anchored definately around 1950 because of the plutonium and plastic signals, and that historical arguments about colonialism or capitalism are just politics smuggled into geology. The other side (the periodizers) think that's backwards—human earth-system transformation started with the Columbian exchange around 1610, or at least with widespread fossil-fuel combustion in the late 18th century, and that geologists shouldn't have monopoly authority over defining a historical epoch that historians and political ecologists have been tracking for decades.

Can you draft the adversarial-collaboration statement these two camps could both sign? I want a numbered section of agreed claims—things like that the planet is now in a state of rapid biogeophysical change, that any single global start date is a modeling choice not a discovery, that both stratigraphic markers and socio-economic drivers are relevant but measure seperate things. Then a disagreements table: each camp's position in wording they'd actually accept, not a strawman. The cruxes—probably (1) whether a geological epoch requires a synchronous global stratigraphic signal, (2) whether normative authority over the term belongs to earth-science institutions or interdisciplinary bodies, and (3) whether the 'Great Acceleration' post-1950 represents a qualitative break or just an intensification of earlier processes. For each crux, include a concrete case or dataset both sides agree is relevant—like if we found consistent microplastic layers dating to 1920 instead of 1950, or if carbon-isotope records showed a clear 1610 discontinuity across all continents—and record what each camp predicts that case would show. Also flag any part of the dispute that's purely verbal, like whether 'Anthropocene' should be capitalized like a formal epoch or used descriptively.

Keep it away from citation lists or 'see Smith 2022' type stuff—this should be something both sides could write without searching google. And if there's a part where the disagreement really is about bedrock values rather than resolvable evidence, say so.
