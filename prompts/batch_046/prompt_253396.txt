Write a dialogue between an archaeological inference optimist and a methodological skeptic about whether material patterns in non-literate societies can justify specific attributions of institutional belief. The optimist should argue that recurring structures—uniform storage modules, aligned ceremonial architecture, and standardized weights—can support claims about centralized administrative doctrine and hierarchical coordination, even without texts. The skeptic should counter that these patterns are radically underdetermined: they might reflect decentralized reciprocal norms, ecological adaptation, or ritual consensus rather than any shared institutional framework. Have them work through a concrete hypothetical—Neolithic settlement clusters with identical grain-storage facilities and oriented public structures but no writing—debating whether the absence of textual corroboration renders belief unrecoverable or simply demands more refined analogical models. Each character should respond directly to the other’s points about underdetermination, the epistemic status of "institutional belief" when no individuals can be interviewed, and the limits of inference from objects alone.
