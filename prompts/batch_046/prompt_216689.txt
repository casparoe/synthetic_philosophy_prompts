Here's an argument I sketched after reading Nietzsche's scattered remarks on digestion, climate, and culture, and I cannot find the exit. (1) For Nietzsche, philosophical values are ultimately interpretations of underlying physiological conditions—so that judging a culture decadent is really registering poor digestion and a heavy diet. (2) He also gives normative dietary prescriptions—lighter food, climate-appropriate eating, avoidance of modern carbohydrate-heavy habits—on the grounds that these restore the vitality that makes higher values possible. (3) But if values are only symptoms of physiology, then the judgment that diet X restores vitality is itself only a symptom of the speaker's digestive state; Nietzsche's praise of lighter diets expresses his stomach's preferences, not a universal reason. (4) He might reply that some physiological states are objectively superior—those aligned with will to power rather than decadence. (5) Yet will to power is interpreted through one's current physiology: a decadent constitution reads it as comfort-seeking, a vital one reads it as self-overcoming. (6) So identifying the correct physiological baseline requires already knowing which values are correct, which is precisely what the physiological reduction was meant to explain. (7) Therefore either his dietary norms collapse into autobiography, or they secretly depend on the normative framework they claim to ground. Where does the argument fail—at (3), at (5), or elsewhere? If I keep the physiological-reductionist premise, what is the least damaging concession?
