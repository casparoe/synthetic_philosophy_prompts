hey i run the review board for a midsize city's public-arts legacy fund. we recieve maybe 20 proposals a year from neighborhood groups wanting permanent installations — murals, monuments, whatever — and the stakes are that once it's up the city is stuck maintaining it for decades. problem is half are cheap talk: a vocal clique writes the grant, promises broad community buy-in, then the thing goes up and nobody remembers why it's there, or worse, it becomes a gatekeeping symbol for the people who pushed it. i need a reusable decision procedre i can run in about 30 minutes per proposal using just the application packet and one site visit, grounded in costly signaling and zahavi's handicap principle but translated into actual steps.

build the logic around two twists i want baked in: first, the handicap has to be distributed — if the cost is borne by too few people it's not a credible group signal, it's just one person's vanity project with neighbors as audience. second, maintenance burden over ten years counts as part of the signal, not just upfront build cost — a group that won't bear long-term upkeep hasn't actually handicapped itself.

write it as a flowchart in words: specific tests in order, pass/fail thresholds where you can, and flag every point where i still have to use judgment because the principle doesn't settle it. apply it to three cases: (a) a working-class block proposing a steel sculpture they have to fundraise 40% of themselves with documented per-household pledges; (b) a wealthy condo association proposing a sleek digital display fully funded by the developer with "community input" meetings; and (c) a case where the procedure gives an answer you'll stand by — approve — but i think the group is actually signaling exclusivity and using the monument to gatekeep newcomers, so it's the answer i won't like. include that third one specifically.

be honest about what this can't catch — like when a group genuinely bears cost but for exclusionary reasons. take a definite stance: don't lay out options and leave me to pick. tell me which signals actually count, which ones are noise, and why the distributed-cost-plus-maintenance version of the handicap principle is the right filter for my role. say what the board can't do with this, too. no bibliographies, i just need the procedure.
