I'm a retired field biologist who got roped into serving on my town's heritage commission, and lately we've been fighting about statues and street names every other meeting. I don't want another philosophy book to read—I'm trying to build an actual checklist I can run in about fifteen minutes per item, since we usually have three cases on teh docket and I have to defend whatever I say to people who don't care about theory.

The idea I want to operationalize is this thing I sketched out: every commemorative object has a backward vector (what historical claim it makes—who's being praised and for what) and a forward vector (what it signals about who belongs in that space now). But there's also this third factor I'm calling semiotic fatigue—when an object has sat there so long that nobody actually reads it as honor anymore, it just becomes spatial infrastructure, and tearing it out does a different kind of harm. So the ethics isn't just "bad guy vs good guy"; it's whether the backward claim is false or harmful, whether the forward signal is exclusionary, and whether removing or altering the object would destroy navigational or social memory that has become detached from the original praise.

Can you turn that into a step-by-step procedure? I need ordered tests I can run, with the hard cases marked—like when the backward claim is partly true and partly myth, or when semiotic fatigue is high but a minority group still experiences the forward vector as hostile.
