I've been arguing with a colleague in a seminar on philosophy of technology about whether automation threatens work at the level of social meaning or only at the level of economic organization. To isolate the disagreement, I am using a framing distinction that I think is under-discussed in the literature: between allocative labor, which is the performance of tasks that distribute goods and maintain social systems, and constitutive labor, which is the shared activity through which a society recognizes its members as contributing agents. My claim is that full automation can absorb allocative labor without residue, but that constitutive labor cannot be automated away or replaced by unconditional provision, and that its disappearance would represent a conceptual loss—the obsolescence of contribution as a basis for social membership—rather than a mere institutional adjustment. I put my credence in this combined claim at about 0.60. A friend in social policy, who thinks recognition structures are far more plastic and that civic or creative participation can fully substitute, puts the same claim at about 0.25.

Please state your own credence that the disappearance of constitutive labor—assuming allocative labor is largely automated—is both structurally inevitable and normatively damaging in the way I describe. Decompose your reasoning into four parts: (a) the probability that near-term automation can genuinely absorb allocative labor across recognition-relevant domains without leaving functional gaps that only humans can fill; (b) the probability that constitutive labor is conceptually distinct from allocative labor and is not reducible to voluntary civic, artistic, or communal activity; (c) the probability that unconditional material provision fails to replicate the recognition-function previously served by contribution-based membership; and (d) the probability that the loss of this recognition-function, if it occurs, amounts to a net harm to human flourishing rather than a liberation from alienated necessity. Assign each part a rough number with a one-line justification, show how they combine into your overall figure, and identify which part is the weakest link in your reasoning and what kind of evidence—empirical, conceptual, or historical—would shift it most. Finally, given my 0.60 and my friend's 0.25, tell me which of these four parts is most likely the locus of our disagreement.
