In a graduate seminar on frontier AI governance, a fellow student dismissed concerns about releasing state-of-the-art model weights by invoking the proverb that “sunlight is the best disinfectant,” suggesting that unrestricted public visibility is the surest way to eliminate hidden dangers. I suspect the phrase is smuggling in several distinct arguments, but I cannot separate them clearly, and I want to understand which ones actually bear on the open-source question versus which ones are merely rhetorical cover.

Could you disambiguate the precise theses this slogan might express in this specific context? In particular, distinguish what “sunlight” denotes—open weights, open training data, open evaluation protocols, or only API-level transparency; what “disinfectant” targets—misalignment, malicious dual-use, corporate secrecy, or regulatory capture; and what mechanism is supposed to operate—collective auditing, competitive market pressure, or normative accountability. For each distinct reading, say whether it is a platitude, whether it is substantive and defensible, and whether it actually supports unrestricted open release as opposed to structured disclosure to accredited auditors or closed red-teaming consortia. I am especially interested in where the analogy breaks down: sunlight acts uniformly on exposed surfaces, whereas AI capabilities may have highly asymmetric defensive and offensive exposure, and some risks—such as the diffusion of optimization techniques that lower the barrier to biological or cyber weapons—might be amplified rather than neutralized by full public visibility.
