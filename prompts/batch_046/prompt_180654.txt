Let's say John Locke was alive today and saw the rapid spread of AI-mediated public deliberation platforms. In these systems, religious citizens submit policy arguments, and an algorithim automatically translates them into secular 'public reason' before other citizens recieve them — plus there's a new voting mechanism where people's ballot weights are adjusted by how strongly they hold their convictions, including religious ones. What policies would Locke advise to a modern pluaralist state? Would he think the AI translation layer fixes the problem he saw with religious coercion, or is it just coercion wearing a different mask? And where would he stand on conviction-weighted voting — closer to his views on property and consent, or something he wouldn't recognize?
