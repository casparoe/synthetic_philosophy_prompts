(1) I take Dennett's intentional stance to reveal genuinely real patterns in behavior—predictive structures that are not merely instrumental fictions—though I remain neutral on whether this requires realism about propositional attitudes as inner states.

(2) I hold that the design stance is ontologically prior for artifacts and evolutionary products, but epistemically we often must ascend to the intentional stance even when we possess the design-level explanation.

(3) I extend the framework to collective and hybrid agents. For corporations and scientific collaborations, I think intentional-stance attributions are heuristically useful but derivative: the real predictive patterns sit at the institutional-design level, and attributing beliefs to "the corporation" tracks organizational structure, not an emergent mind.

(4) For individual human cognition, however, I deny that evolutionary design explanations reduce or replace intentional-stance explanations; the two stances answer different questions, but the intentional patterns are not merely design misfires.

(5) For hybrid AI-human diagnostic systems—say, a clinical decision-support architecture in which the algorithm and the physician co-adapt over time—I claim that if the coupled system develops stable predictive patterns that neither the human nor the algorithm exhibits in isolation, then the intentional stance applies to the system itself, not derivatively.

(6) I deny that phenomenal consciousness is necessary for intentional-stance applicability. But I also deny that mere predictive success is sufficient: there must be counterfactual stability across perturbations to the system's inputs and internal state.

(7) I maintain that when errors occur in such hybrid systems, the correct explanatory stance depends on the failure mode—design stance for algorithmic bias, intentional stance for physician judgment—but I allow that in genuinely emergent cases the failure mode itself may be irreducibly system-level.

Also address whether (6)'s counterfactual-stability condition can be satisfied by the hybrid system in (5) without collapsing into a design-stance description of the coupled architecture.
