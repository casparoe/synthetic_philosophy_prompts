ok so i'm trying to map Nyāya's pramana taxonomy onto how we actually treat negative results in science, and i think i've hit an argument that blows up the whole framework. here goes:

(1) Nyāya says a pramāṇa is a unique causal path to yathārtha-jñāna — true cognition that hasn't been gotten before.
(2) They list perception, inference, comparison, testimony, and also anupalabdhi (non-apprehension) for knowing absences like no pot on the floor.
(3) But anupalabdhi only works if you notice that the cognition you'd expect from seeing a pot just isn't happening, despite conditions being right.
(4) That noticing is either itself an inference from missing conditions, or it's a direct grasp of a negative fact (abhāva).
(5) If it's inference, then anupalabdhi isn't a separate pramāṇa — it's just anumāna with extra steps.
(6) If it's direct, then the causal instrument isn't sense-contact with anything present; it's contact with absence itself, which means the instrument isn't operating on a padārtha in the same way perception does.
(7) Now here's the twist: in modern null-result experiments we treat no signal detected as knowledge only because we have a background model predicting signal-under-conditions. That background model is basically inference. So anupalabdhi always piggybacks on anumāna in practice.
(8) But if that's right, then śabda (testimony) about negative states — like an authority saying there is no self — also collapses: either it's inference-backed testimony (so śabda isn't independent either) or it's direct anupalabdhi applied to words, which makes no sense.
(9) So either Nyāya's taxonomy is redundant (everything collapses to inference/perception) or negative knowledge is impossible, which is clearly false.

where's the weak link? i want to keep anupalabdhi as genuinely distinct and also keep that testimony can deliver negative knowledge without being reducible to inference. is there a way to save both, or do i have to bite a bullet on redundancy? be specific about which premise is shaky and what saving it costs.
