I am a novelist drafting a satirical interlude for a work of speculative fiction, and I require a deadpan parody in the form of an internal corporate memorandum. Write an approximately 650-word memo, dated 14 March 2041, issued by the fictional Institute for Normative Liquidity, announcing the successful isolation, patenting, and commercial licensing of free-floating rationales—normative reasons that persist and exert binding force in the complete absence of any reasoner. Format the document with numbered sections: Executive Summary, Ontological Classification, Licensing Tiers, Risk Disclosure, and Closing Affirmation. The voice must be relentlessly cheerful, jargon-saturated, and bureaucratically oblivious, as though the authors sincerely regard this as a humanitarian breakthrough.

The satire should target the philosophical tendency to reify normative reasons—to treat them as autonomous, quantifiable, mind-independent entities capable of being weighed, traded, stored, or depreciated—without naming any living thinker or invoking obscure bibliographic references. Ground the text in recognizable metaethical vocabulary (pro tanto reasons, overriding considerations, normative weight, agent-neutral value, reason-giving relations) but literalize each term into corporate infrastructure: assign serial numbers to reasons, place them in escrow, discuss their half-lives when separated from belief-structures, and subject them to quarterly audits. Within this framework, invent and formalize a novel subclass—Type-III Synthetic Rationales—produced by algorithmic consensus protocols that generate binding obligations no conscious being has ever endorsed, yet which the memo treats as legally enforceable assets. The humor must emerge from internal consistency rather than cartoonish exaggeration; every clause should read as if it could appear in an actual ethics-technology white paper if one took the metaphysics of free-floating reasons literally.

Maintain sustained irony throughout: the authorial voice must never wink, and a specialist should recognize that the document understands precisely what it is mocking. Do not include footnotes, citations, reading lists, or references to specific published works. Avoid placing any verbatim quotation from a real philosophical text inside quotation marks; paraphrase all concepts through the institute’s proprietary terminology. The piece should remain intelligible to a reader with no background in metaethics, yet reward the informed reader with accurate conceptual distortion.

Conclude the memo, then append beneath it—separated by a line—a brief Author’s Note of no more than eighty words, written in the novelist’s own calm, reflective voice, explaining why this document appears in the narrative without breaking the deadpan register of the memo itself. Do not use quotation marks around any passage taken from an actual source, and ensure the entire deliverable can be understood without any access to external references.
