I am a third-year undergraduate in a philosophy of psychiatry seminar, and I serve as the student representative on our university counseling center's Clinical Classification Advisory Board. At next Thursday's meeting we must decide whether to approve a pilot protocol allowing clinicians to record a provisional, non-DSM-5-TR intake designation—tentatively called performance-contingent dysphoria—for students whose reported symptoms cluster around achievement-linked emotional collapse but do not meet criteria for major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, or adjustment disorder. The clinical director supports the pilot because forcing these students into existing categories has led to repeated misdiagnosis and inappropriate pharmacological referrals; the university's insurance and compliance officer opposes it, arguing that any non-standard code will be rejected by our third-party payer and that introducing a novel label risks what Ian Hacking called a looping effect, wherein the classification itself reshapes the behavior it purports to describe, particularly since students arrive already using this exact phrase from online mental-health discourse. Please draft the board's formal recommendation memo: it should recommend one of three options—approve the pilot with a defined evaluation period, reject it in favor of stricter DSM adherence, or approve it only for internal tracking with no billing linkage—and it should specify a concrete time frame, evaluation criteria, and a mechanism for addressing reimbursement risk. The reasoning must take the looping-effect objection seriously rather than dismissing it, and must give an honest account of the strongest consideration favoring the option you do not recommend. Write this as a document the board could circulate and act upon.
