I am a novelist drafting an essay for a literary quarterly, and I would value your comments on its conceptual architecture rather than its prose style. The essay examines the historiographical habit of treating microbes, climatic shifts, and volcanic events as historical actors—entities that possess causal efficacy without intentionality. I propose an analogy to a narrative technique I use in fiction: the unreliable narrator who filters systemic causes through an individual consciousness that the reader cannot fully correct. Just as the 1783 Laki fissure eruption reshaped European mortality and grain markets without possessing will, an unreliable narrator reshapes plot without granting access to an objective baseline. I want to know whether this comparison reveals a hidden anthropomorphism in environmental history—what I call causal ventriloquism, the attribution of voice to forces that merely exert pressure—or whether it helpfully separates agency from causal prominence. Specifically, I compare the documented impact of that Icelandic eruption on agricultural timelines to the way a first-person narrative can obscure structural causes behind subjective perception. My concern is that framing germs or climate as actors invites a determinism that flattens human deliberation into reactive consequence, much as an overdetermined plot can flatten character choice into inevitability. Could you assess whether this analogy holds? I am particularly interested in whether the metaphysical distinction between endurantism and perdurantism applies to such events—whether an eruption is better understood as wholly present at each temporal stage or as persisting through temporal parts such as ash dispersion, famine, and migration—and whether importing that framework clarifies or confuses the question of non-human agency. I am not asking for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; I need a rigorous evaluation of where the analogy between narrative unreliability and environmental determinism collapses, whether causal ventriloquism is coherent, and if endurantism versus perdurantism genuinely illuminates historical causation by non-human forces.
