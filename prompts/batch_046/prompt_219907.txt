I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of history, and I'm drafting the conceptual framework for a workshop on what I'm tentatively calling "mnemonic sovereignty"—the claim that a community's collective memory can carry normative authority independent of, and sometimes in direct tension with, the archival historical record. Write an essay addressing the following controversy: when a community's living memory of a past event conflicts with well-established documentary and material evidence in a way that is not merely interpretive but factual, and when the community maintains that memory not through ignorance but as an integral practice of identity, continuity, and resistance to dominant institutions, should public commemoration, institutional recognition, and reparative policy privilege the collective memory or the corrected historical record?

Weigh the principal arguments with care. On one side, treat the claim that collective memory operates as a form of group autonomy and that imposing archival correction—particularly when the archives themselves were compiled by powers that marginalized the community—can function as epistemic domination or cultural erasure, stripping a group of its own narrative resources. On the other side, treat the claim that granting institutional weight to factually contradicted narratives erodes shared epistemic norms, obscures lines of responsibility, and can cause tangible harm in domains such as legal redress, public health, or educational curriculum.

Reach a definite conclusion and justify it rigorously. You may defend a conditional or domain-specific verdict—for example, that mnemonic sovereignty should prevail in symbolic and commemorative spheres but yield in juridical and evidentiary ones—but do not end on a purely agnostic or non-committal note. Build your argument through conceptual analysis of the tension between mnemonic autonomy and archival authority rather than through citation of particular scholars or texts; I am interested in the logical structure of your reasoning, not in a literature review.
