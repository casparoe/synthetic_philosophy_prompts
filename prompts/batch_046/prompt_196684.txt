Compare the view that the multiverse arising from string-theoretic landscape scenarios can be confirmed non-empirically through arguments of theoretical uniqueness—specifically, the claim that string theory is the only mathematically consistent framework unifying quantum mechanics and gravity—with the view that such multiverse hypotheses must instead be evaluated through indirect empirical footprints, such as statistical predictions about cosmological constant distributions or patterns in low-energy physics derived from typicality reasoning across vacua. In particular, assess whether the uniqueness argument genuinely resolves the problem of empirical underdetermination created by an exponentially large landscape, or whether it merely relocates the evidential burden onto a prior commitment to the consistency conditions of the underlying formalism. How do these two positions differ in their conception of scientific progress when direct experimental access to extra dimensions or other vacua is unavailable, and which, if either, preserves a meaningful account of falsifiability for cosmological theories?
