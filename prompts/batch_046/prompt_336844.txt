As a journalist covering science and ideas, I am preparing a feature on the stalemate within consequentialist ethics over so-called cluelessness—the charge that long-range consequential reasoning collapses because predictive confidence decays exponentially across causal and temporal distance. For the piece, I want you to draft the joint statement that two opposing camps could sign after a rigorous, good-faith adversarial collaboration.

The two camps are: (1) the Decisive Cluelessness camp, which holds that once outcomes lie beyond a certain causal horizon, expected-value calculations become epistemically illegitimate for action-guidance and should be abandoned in favor of near-term heuristics; and (2) the Robust Consequentialism camp, which holds that cluelessness is a tractable form of deep uncertainty addressable through hierarchical robustness analysis, and that abandoning long-range reasoning commits the mirror-image error of false precision about near-term effects.

Please produce a formal document suitable for publication in a serious ideas journal, with the following sections in this order:

1. Numbered agreed claims. List statements both camps can sign, including a shared formal characterization of the problem (how causal distance interacts with outcome variance and prior assignment), shared acknowledgments of what decision-theoretic impossibility results under radical uncertainty actually establish and what they leave open, and shared descriptions of practical stakes—for example, in climate-mitigation pathways and in existential-risk policy.

2. A disagreements table. For each residual dispute, provide: (a) the precise formulation of the disagreement, phrased so that each side accepts the wording of its own position; (b) the crux—the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side; (c) for each crux, the case, argument, or empirical pattern both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Include at least one crux concerning value-lock-in risk: whether the possibility that an action irreversibly constrains the moral-patienthood boundaries of future populations (for instance, by committing civilization to biological-only or digital-inclusive trajectories) should be treated as an internal parameter within the cluelessness calculus, or as a meta-uncertainty that fractures the calculus entirely and demands a separate decision framework.

3. A brief concluding assessment identifying whether any portion of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, specifying exactly which formulations dissolve into word-choice differences and why.

The document should separate genuine disagreement from misunderstandings, avoid dramatization, and present each camp's position in terms it would recognize. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote any specific real-world text verbatim; paraphrase all ideas without quotation marks. The deliverable should be a constructive text both parties could put their names to, with every remaining disagreement made exact.
