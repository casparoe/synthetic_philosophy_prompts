I teach an upper-level aesthetics seminar and I'm puting together the take-home final. I want one essay question on Tolstoy's What Is Art? that can't be answered by just reciting the infection model and the religious criteron. The crux should be the tension between them.

Specifically, Tolstoy claims art works like an infection — it transmits the artist's feeling directly to the audience. He also insists true art must be guided by religious consciousness that promotes universal brotherhood and stays accessable to ordinary people. Then he praises peasant folk songs and trashes Wagner's Ring as false and artificial. The prompt should ask students whether the religious criterion actually does independent work, or if it's just renaming his preference for simple collective feeling over complex individualistic emotion. They should also use the infection model to press him: if Wagner genuinely infects listeners with intense feeling, why is it false? Where does the hidden normative step enter?

Write the exam question for 90 minutes, about 900 words max, plus a model answer around 700 words and a marking scheme. The scheme should separate an A from a B by naming the specific moves — e.g., spotting the risk of circularity, distinguishing emotional transmission from moral accessibility, explaining why Tolstoy needs the religious step to exclude Wagner — versus B-level answers that summarize both criteria but treat them as smoothly compatible. Don't include any citation or bibliography request; students won't have web access and I dont want them inventing page numbers.

Make sure the difficulty comes from forcing them to resolve (or at least map) the internal conflict, not from memorizing labels.
