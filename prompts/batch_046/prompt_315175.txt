Design a final examination item for an upper-level seminar in philosophy of social science focused on cultural causation and interpretive sociology. The topic is Max Weber’s Protestant Ethic thesis and the problem of explaining social outcomes with values rather than with material conditions alone. The question must not be answerable by summarizing Weber’s narrative about the spirit of capitalism; it must require students to navigate Weber’s distinction between elective affinity (Wahlverwandtschaft) and direct causation, and between causal adequacy and meaningful adequacy, in order to defend or reject the claim that values can be independent explanatory factors.

Embed the following constructed comparative scenario directly in the prompt: in two seventeenth-century textile towns with identical market access, capital availability, guild regulations, and legal frameworks, Calvinist merchants in Town A interpret the doctrine of vocation (Beruf) through an “elective-sign” schema in which worldly economic success is read as probable evidence of salvation, producing aggressive reinvestment and ascetic avoidance of luxury; Lutheran merchants in Town B hold the same formal theological text but interpret it through a “comfort-of-grace” schema in which salvation is assured regardless of economic outcome, producing moderate profit-taking and conspicuous status-display. One interlocutor argues that because material conditions are held constant, this proves culture is an autonomous cause; another argues that the difference in interpretation is itself fully explained by local clerical power structures and therefore collapses into a standard materialist explanation.

Write the essay question to be completed in 75 minutes (approximately 800 words, 25 marks). Then provide a model answer of about 800 words, and a marking scheme built around five specific conceptual moves that an A answer must contain, with the marks for each move (total 25), and a one-line note on the characteristic error that separates a B answer from an A on each point. The scheme should make explicit that a top answer must show how Weber avoids both idealism and reductionism without allowing the interpretive mediation of doctrine to become viciously circular. Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and present all necessary elements of the hypothetical within the question so that no outside research is required.
