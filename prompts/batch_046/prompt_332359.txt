"Transformative experiences, as the philosopher L.A. Paul discusses, are ones that change both what you know and who you are. Epistemic transformation means you cant know what its like beforehand, like you cant really understand being deaf just by reading about it. Personal transformation means your core values and preferences actually shift after the experience. Paul argues this is a big problem for rational decision theory because rational choice depends on comparing expected utilities, but if you cant phenomenally imagine the outcome you cant assign it a subjective value.

However, I think Paul overstates the problem. Even if you cant fully imagine being deaf, you can still learn from other peoples testimony about whether they regret it, so the epistemic gap is partly fixable with good data. For personal transformation, yes your values change, but a rational agent can anticipate this shift and build it into their decision calculus. The real issue isnt rationality but authenticity: your present self is basicly voting for a future self with different values, which is like consenting on behalf of someone else. So the problem isnt that we cant choose rationally, its that we cant choose authentically unless our future self retroactively approves.

For my example, I chose deciding whether to undergo a brain procedure that permanently eliminates fear responses. Before the procedure you value risk-taking and adventure; after, you value safety and calm. You cant simulate the calm-valued self, but you can predict the shift. Still, choosing it feels inauthentic because the post-procedure self never agreed to have its values overwritten."
