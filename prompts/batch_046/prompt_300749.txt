I've recently joined the ethics committee of a transnational humanitarian initiative that advises informal community defense networks in a region where state authority has collapsed and multiple armed factions claim legitimacy. Our charter permits us to provide logistical and strategic guidance only to groups that restrict themselves to protecting civilian infrastructure, but we have no mechanism to verify whether every individual trainee understands that distinction in practice. At our last session, the committee split over whether we should suspend all cooperation. One faction argued that because many of the opposing combatants are locally recruited and have not personally chosen to initiate aggression—some were displaced laborers pressed into service—the fighters on both sides share an equal moral status as human beings facing impossible choices. They insisted that if we truly respect that equality, then helping one group kill members of the other is morally indistinguishable from arming one neighborhood against another, regardless of which faction first crossed a border or seized a resource. The opposing faction replied that this reasoning collapses the difference between wrongful aggression and necessary defense: a group that systematically targets civilians to expand control is not morally equivalent to a group that resists that expansion, and treating them as equivalent ignores the structural injustice that makes defense necessary in the first place. They added that our role is not to judge the souls of individual fighters but to assess whether the collective action we support meets criteria for legitimate protection. I am tasked with drafting a framework that addresses whether the personal moral innocence of enemy fighters creates any constraint on defensive action, and whether recognizing a fundamental equality among all combatants undermines the very distinction between just and unjust causes that allows defense to be permissible at all. How should I understand the relationship between the justice of a cause and the moral standing of the individuals fighting for it, and what does that imply for our decision to continue or halt the advisory program?
