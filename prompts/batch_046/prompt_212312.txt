Write a Petrarchan sonnet (octave ABBAABBA, sestet CDECDE or CDCDCD, with an unmistakable volta at line 9) in the voice of the implicit interlocutor addressed in Montaigne's "Of Cannibals." In the octave, describe the Tupinambá practices the essay records—ritual roasting of prisoners, the consumption of flesh as an act of honor rather than vengeance, the reported absence of lying or betrayal—using language that initially registers European horror. At the volta, turn to European analogues: judicial torture, executions for treason, the massacres of the religious wars. The rhyme scheme should do structural philosophical work: pair an end-word tied to cannibal ritual in the octave with an end-word tied to European "civilized" violence in the sestet, so that sonic identity binds the two moral worlds together rather than separating them. Do not resolve the comparison into a clear thesis for or against cultural relativism; instead, let the final lines grammatically entangle both vocabularies so that judgment remains suspended.
