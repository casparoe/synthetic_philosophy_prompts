I have been trying to combine Hilbert's hotel with Cantor's distinction between countable and uncountable infinities, and I think I have arrived at a stronger argument for finitism than the usual physical or epistemic ones, but I cannot locate the precise error. Here is the reasoning: if a collection is actually infinite, Hilbert's hotel shows that removing a proper subset can leave the cardinality unchanged—for example, the even integers have the same cardinality as all integers—while Cantor's theorem shows there is no maximal cardinality, so for any infinite collection there is always a strictly larger one. Now consider an infinite library containing exactly one volume for each real number. If we withdraw every volume corresponding to a rational number, we have removed infinitely many items, yet the remaining collection still has the cardinality of the continuum, since the rationals are only countable. More generally, for many infinite cardinals one can excise a subset equinumerous with the whole without reducing cardinality. From this I conclude that for any actually infinite collection, there is no stable fact of the matter about which members it contains: arbitrarily large removals leave the "size" intact, and the absence of a largest cardinal means containment relations never bottom out. This seems to undermine not merely the physical application of actual infinities but the very coherence of set identity under them—if identity conditions for a collection must be fixed by its members, yet membership can vary by infinitely many elements without altering cardinality, then cardinal arithmetic severs size from content in a way that makes determinate identity impossible. Is the mistake here a misapplication of part-whole intuition to cardinal equivalence classes, a misunderstanding of how Cantor's hierarchy relates to subset containment, or an implicit assumption that the identity of a collection must be tracked by its cardinality rather than by extensional membership? I want to know which step fails, whether a corrected version of the argument still gives any support to finitism in mathematics or physics, and whether there is any interpretation of infinite sets on which cardinal stability genuinely threatens identity conditions rather than merely violating pre-theoretic intuitions about magnitude.
