Consider the argument that diachronic rationality requires an agent's preference ordering to remain transitive across her entire lifespan, because any cyclical shift—valuing A over B at one time, B over C at a later time, and C over A thereafter—would leave her vulnerable to an intertemporal money pump in which her successive selves extract value from one another. On this view, a person who genuinely revises her priorities, perhaps from career stability to artistic risk and eventually back, is not rationally updating but is instead being exploited by her own future preferences. What commitment devices or institutional mechanisms—analogous to Ulysses contracts—should she use to lock in a single consistent ranking, and does the avoidance of exploitation require treating every preference reversal as a failure of rationality rather than as a legitimate response to changed circumstances or new information?
