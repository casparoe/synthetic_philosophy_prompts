Please explain the conceptual structure of the epistemic regress problem as it applies specifically to modal beliefs—beliefs concerning metaphysical or logical possibility—rather than to empirical beliefs about the actual world. Introduce and evaluate a view that might be called modular infinitism: within each modal domain (e.g., metaphysical possibility, logical consistency, or deontic permissibility) justification proceeds via an infinite, non-repeating chain of modal beliefs, while cross-domain transitions are treated as foundationally anchored by direct rational insight rather than further modal inference. Contrast this modular infinitist position with both standard global infinitism, in which every belief requires an infinite chain regardless of domain, and with holistic coherentism, in which modal beliefs are justified by mutual coherence across all domains. In particular, address whether modular infinitism avoids the classic arbitrariness objection usually leveled against foundationalist stopping points, and whether its cross-domain anchors reintroduce a localized variant of that same arbitrariness.
