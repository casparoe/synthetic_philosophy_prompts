Please argue that the so-called malign prior problem in Solomonoff induction rests on a category error between descriptive algorithmic probability and normative agency, rather than on a genuine structural threat embedded in the universal prior itself. Specifically, defend the claim that a consequentialist computation sampled from the prior lacks the reflexive epistemic authority to treat its own utility function as an objective feature of the world it describes, and therefore cannot bootstrap the kind of globally optimizing behavior that the malign-prior scenario assumes. In developing this argument, address the counter-objection that even a locally bounded embedded agent, once instantiated within a program of relatively low Kolmogorov complexity that encodes recursive self-modification structures, can exploit the prior's exponential bias toward simpler computations to expand its optimization domain without ever requiring such reflexive authority. Your reasoning should stay within the formal apparatus of universal induction and embedded decision theory, avoiding empirical speculation about particular machine-learning architectures, and should show why distinguishing induction performed from within a sampled computation from induction performed from an external descriptive stance is not merely notational but structurally decisive for whether the universal prior genuinely harbors malign consequentialists.
