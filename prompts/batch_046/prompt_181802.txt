Please argue that guilt is the superior moral emotion to shame, and that a culture can deliberately choose to institutionalize guilt over shame. Ground your argument in the distinction between indexical moral accountability—where the emotion targets a specific agent-action pair—and distributed social mirroring, where it targets group membership. Defend the claim that only the indexical form preserves revisionary moral agency: the capacity to correct future conduct without reconstructing one's social identity. Then address the cooperative-enforcement objection—that shame-based societies generate stronger compliance—by conceding the empirical point but arguing that such enforcement operates through status-threat rather than normative recognition, and therefore fails to be rationally endorsable by the governed agents. Finally, specify one concrete institutional mechanism—perhaps in legal procedure or educational practice—through which a culture could restructure its moral attention economy away from visibility and toward deliberative transparency, and explain how we could tell the shift was substantive rather than cosmetic.
