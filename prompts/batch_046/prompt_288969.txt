I've been working through Fear and Trembling in my philosophy of religion seminar, and I've come to defend a strong reading of the teleological suspension of the ethical: not merely that Abraham makes a justified exception to universal moral law in the binding of Isaac, but that the episode exposes the ethical sphere itself as structurally subordinate to the absolute relation of faith. On this view, Abraham's silence—his refusal to justify himself to Sarah, Isaac, or the community—is not a temporary failure of moral reasoning but the necessary expression of a hierarchical ontology in which the universal derives its authority from a prior relation to the absolute. The knight of faith, returning to the ethical with joy, does not revert to an unchanged universal but carries a transformed consciousness: the ethical is always already mediated by faith, and its claims are therefore conditionally valid rather than categorically binding.

What would have to be true for this reading to be false? I want a map of its real exposure, not just the standard objections. In particular:

- Would the Islamic narrative of the binding of Ishmael—where the son is conscious, consenting, and actively participates—undermine the claim that Abraham's silence marks a unique collapse of ethical justification? If consent removes the horror of filicide, does it dissolve the suspension entirely, or can the strong reading accommodate it without losing its force?

- If the particular is genuinely higher than the universal in this hierarchical ontology, does Kierkegaard's position risk collapsing into the very moral particularism or arbitrary voluntarism that he explicitly rejects elsewhere? Is this a fatal contradiction or merely an awkward tension that defenders can absorb?

- To what extent would results in moral psychology—showing that appeals to divine command reliably function as post-hoc rationalizations of pre-existing harm—count against the claim that Abraham's leap is a singular, non-replicable structure of faith rather than an instance of a predictable cognitive pattern?

- The knight of faith returns to the finite with joy. Does this return prove that the suspension was never genuinely ontological but only a temporary psychological crisis, thereby reducing the religious stage to an emotional episode within ethics rather than a sphere that transcends it?

- Finally, if Abraham's incommunicability makes him socially incomprehensible, does this render the religious stage practically impossible as a form of life, and is that a problem the strong reading must concede?

Please rank these defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. For each, say whether a committed defender of this hierarchical reading would concede it as counting against them or would dismiss it, and whether the position has been insulated by qualifications that make it unfalsifiable. I'm especially interested in whether there is any observation, formal result, or comparative narrative case that would force the defender to abandon the claim that the ethical is structurally subordinate rather than merely temporarily suspended.
