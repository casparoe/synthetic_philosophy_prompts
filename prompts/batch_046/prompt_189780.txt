I'm working through a short essay on Hobbes's philosophy of method, and I want to test whether my framing holds up. Most commentators note that Hobbes praised geometry as the only demonstrative science and lamented that civil philosophy lacked its certainty; they usually conclude that he wanted to make politics more like Euclid. My essay argues the opposite: that Hobbes's admiration for geometry was selective, and that he rejected synthetic or constructive geometric reasoning—building figures step-by-step from definitions—as the wrong model for politics. Instead, he treated the commonwealth as an artificial person whose existence depends on authorization rather than construction, which I analogize to a non-constructive existence proof: the sovereign is shown to exist by virtue of a representational relation, not assembled from axiomatic parts like a triangle. I suggest this explains why Hobbes insists in both De Cive and Leviathan that the causes of commonwealths are discovered by analysis (resolutive reasoning from effects to causes) rather than by synthesis, and why he warns against those who think they can design a state from first principles as one draws a figure.

The niche move in the essay is comparing Hobbesian authorization to modern non-constructive proof in mathematics—claiming that Hobbes anticipates the distinction between constructing an object and establishing its existence through relational conditions. I know this risks anachronism, but I think it illuminates why Hobbes is so hostile to "utopian" political architects: they mistake political bodies for geometric figures that can be synthesized, whereas for Hobbes sovereignty is a functional status conferred by consent and representation, not a compound assembled from constituent parts.

Could you comment on the essay's accuracy—particularly whether Hobbes's texts on geometry actually support the analytic/synthetic distinction I'm drawing, or whether I'm importing a modern mathematical distinction he wouldn't recognize? I'd also welcome your view on whether the non-constructive proof analogy is genuinely illuminating or merely decorative, and whether the essay successfully distinguishes Hobbes's method from later contractarian thinkers (say, Rawls) who do seem to use a synthetic, constructivist framework. Please don't just list sources; I'm interested in conceptual coherence and whether I've missed an obvious objection from Hobbes's own writings.
