I am currently enrolled in an advanced philosophy of language course on conditionals and modal logic, and a classmate recently summarized for me what she called the "standard two-track semantic view." She put it this way: indicative conditionals work by restricting a modal base to worlds in which the antecedent holds and then checking whether the consequent is entailed within that restricted set; in effect, they are just material implication reformulated inside a modal framework. Counterfactual conditionals, on the other hand, operate by selecting the closest possible worlds—ordered by some similarity relation—where the antecedent is false in actuality, and then testing the consequent there. The real distinction between the two, she insisted, is not structural or semantic at all, but purely epistemic and pragmatic: you use an indicative when you leave open that the antecedent might actually be true, and you use a counterfactual when you presuppose it is false. So sentences like "If Oswald did not shoot Kennedy, someone else did" and "If Oswald had not shot Kennedy, someone else would have" share exactly the same logical form; they differ only in what the speaker takes herself to know about the actual world. She also added that, on this account, a counterfactual with a true antecedent is either contradictory or automatically collapses into its indicative counterpart, which is precisely why philosophers treat counterfactuals as a separate category altogether.

Something about this sounds mostly right—I can see how it connects to the restrictor analysis for indicatives and the similarity-sphere analysis for counterfactuals—but I have the nagging feeling that she has flattened important distinctions and overstated the epistemic story. Could you audit this summary for fairness? Please separate what is genuinely accurate, what is a common but potentially misleading simplification, and what a defender of the mainstream semantic account would explicitly deny or qualify. I am particularly unsure about her claim that the indicative/counterfactual divide is purely epistemic rather than involving different structural mechanisms, and I am also doubtful about her treatment of true-antecedent counterfactuals.
