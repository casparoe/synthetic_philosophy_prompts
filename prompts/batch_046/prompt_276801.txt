I'm trying to sort out my own view on corruption before I write a post. Here's my philosophical profile on it:

- Corruption is fundamentally a breach of a specific institutional role, not just any harmful or unfair exchange.
- In weak-state societies, clientelist vote-buying often functions as the only working welfare system, so calling it corruption imports a Western state-capacity assumption that doesn't fit.
- Gift-giving in Japanese and Chinese business culture is structurally different from bribery: it's long-term reciprocity, not quid-pro-quo for a discrete decision.
- I'm not a moral relativist—embezzling disaster relief is wrong everywhere, regardless of local norms.
- But I also think anti-corruption NGOs should drop universal "transparency indexes" and let local communities define the norms of public office themselves.
- And I think that if a culture genuinely lacks a public/private distinction, applying the concept of corruption to patronage is just a category mistake.
