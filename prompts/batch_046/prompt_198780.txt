heres the student answer:

Frankfurt wants to show you can be responsible even when you cant do otherwise. So Jones decides to vote Democrat. Black is watching and can manipulate Jones's brain if he sees a prior sign—like a twitch or a specific facial expression—that Jones is about to vote Republican. If Black sees that sign he intervenes and makes Jones vote Democrat. But Jones decides on his own, Black never steps in, and Jones votes Democrat.

The Principle of Alternative Possibilities says responsibility requires the ability to do otherwise. Here Jones cant vote Republican, since Black would stop him. So he lacks an action-level alternative. But he still seems responsible, because he acted on his own reasons. So PAP is false.

Frankfurt's hierarchical model of free will is what makes this work. Jones has a first-order desire to vote Democrat, and his second-order volition endorses that desire. That alignment is why hes genuinely free, even with Black lurking. So the counterexample succeeds because it combines the absence of alternatives with this hierarchical autonomy.

People also talk about flickers of freedom—like Jones could have shown the prior sign, which is technically a different mental event. Frankfurt himself raises this objection and dismisses it, saying the flicker isnt the action were evaluating for responsibility. So theres no real problem.

Then there are buffer cases, where the intervener blocks the decision process itself rather than just the action. In those cases theres not even a mental flicker left. That means Frankfurt's original setup is actually the stronger argument, since it allows a mental flicker and still proves responsibility without action-level alternatives.
