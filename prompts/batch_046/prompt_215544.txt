A philosophy blog post summarises the metaontological position known as quantifier variance as follows: defenders hold that ontological disputes—say, over whether numbers exist—are never about a shared world, but only about which linguistic framework we should adopt. Within any framework, existence claims are settled by internal rules; across frameworks, there is no neutral standpoint to adjudicate disagreement, so the realist and anti-realist speak past one another rather than contradict each other. The only rational basis for choosing between frameworks is pragmatic convenience—simplicity, predictive power, or aesthetic elegance—not correspondence with an independent reality. Moreover, the view implies what we might call a cross-framework translation failure thesis: if two frameworks quantify over different domains, no statement in one can be genuinely false in the other, because falsity itself is framework-relative.
