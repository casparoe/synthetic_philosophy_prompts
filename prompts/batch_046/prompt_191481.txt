Here's something that bugs me: macro departments still train students on DSGE models with a single representative agent and rational expectations, even though everyone admits real households are heterogenous and systematically biased. The standard defense is Friedman's old F-twist—false premises don't matter if predictions hold. But then they turn around and use those same models for welfare analysis of tax policy, treating the agent's utility function as social welfare. That feels like two incompatible philosophies of science crammed into one instutitional practice.
