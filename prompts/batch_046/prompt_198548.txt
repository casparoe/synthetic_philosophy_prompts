I am a high school student preparing to defend my thesis at my school's interdisciplinary philosophy-physics symposium next week. My position is that Gödel's rotating universe solution to Einstein's field equations should be interpreted not as a mere cosmological curiosity that permits closed timelike curves, but as a direct metaphysical argument against the objectivity of temporal passage: in a spacetime with rotational symmetry that allows such curves, there is no globally consistent orientation of before and after independent of an observer's worldline trajectory, which renders presentism—the view that only present objects exist—conceptually incoherent rather than merely empirically false. To make this precise, I introduce a distinction between chronometric closure, the mathematical property that certain worldlines loop back in coordinate time, and phenomenological reversibility, the claim that an observer traversing such a loop would not experience causation running backward but rather a looping narrative structure that dissolves any privileged global now; I then argue that this undermines not only presentism but also the standard block-universe commitment to a single, observer-independent temporal ordering.

The audience includes my physics teacher, who regards Gödel's model as physically unrealistic given observed cosmic isotropy and therefore dismisses it as philosophically irrelevant; my philosophy teacher, who defends a phenomenological presentism rooted in the asymmetry of lived experience; two undergraduate physics mentors who understand general relativity well but worry I am conflating coordinate artifacts with physical ontology; and several peers who have encountered popular accounts of relativity but routinely confuse coordinate time with proper time. I need you to prepare me for the objections they will actually raise, ordered by likelihood and damage, phrased in the vocabulary each faction would use: for each objection, indicate what I should concede up front—such as the physical implausibility of our actual universe being Gödelian—what I should hold firm on—especially the conceptual point about orientation pluralism—and where my reply is weakest. Please also identify the objection I am hoping nobody asks: namely, that my distinction between chronometric closure and phenomenological reversibility smuggles in an undefended commitment to a B-theory block universe. Finally, state how confident you are in this preparation and single out the specific point about which you are least certain.
