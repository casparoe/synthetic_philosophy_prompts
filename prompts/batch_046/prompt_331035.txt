Having recently learned that my partner and I both carry pathogenic variants for a serious autosomal recessive disorder, we are now weighing preimplantation genetic testing and, more fundamentally, the philosophical framework it forces upon us. Rather than asking for clinical guidance, I want a disciplined conceptual analysis of reproductive choice under the principle of procreative beneficence and the framework of liberal eugenics. Specifically, I am struck by an extension that is rarely examined in detail: a lineal, intergenerational application of procreative beneficence, in which parents select embryos not merely for the expected wellbeing of the child they will raise, but for the heritable welfare of descendants across generations—particularly in selecting for polygenic resilience against psychiatric conditions whose expression depends on unknown future social environments. This raises a cluster of tensions. If selection changes which individuals come to exist, the non-identity problem seems to undermine harm-based objections, yet the principle demands that we maximize welfare over time; meanwhile, liberal eugenics insists on state neutrality and parental autonomy, but a multi-generational welfare calculus seems to invoke collective interests that no single family can claim exclusive authority over. Please describe the most important implications of this lineal extension of procreative beneficence for (1) whether liberal eugenics can consistently maintain state neutrality when reproductive choices have transgenerational welfare effects; (2) how the non-identity problem interacts with the obligation to select for the best expected life when that life belongs to a chain of future persons rather than a single identifiable child; and (3) whether the therapy-enhancement distinction collapses or becomes more sharply defined once selection targets probabilistic polygenic traits rather than monogenic diseases. I am not asking for empirical data, citations, or a bibliography; I want an analysis that treats the intergenerational framing as a serious philosophical modification rather than a reductio.
