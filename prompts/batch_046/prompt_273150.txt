Here is an argument I developed for a seminar on informal social sanctions and now find suspicious because it collapses the distinction between justified deplatforming and arbitrary exclusion.

(1) If a platform or community removes a speaker for expressing view V, and the removal is intended specifically to respond to the wrongfulness of V rather than merely to avoid association, then the removal functions as an instance of punishment.
(2) Legitimate punishment requires an identifiable sanctioning authority, proportionality between offense and sanction, and some form of appeal or redress—conditions that state penal systems satisfy but that distributed collective actions (boycotts, deplatforming campaigns, reputational attacks) structurally lack, since no unitary agent calibrates the severity or duration of the cost imposed.
(3) Therefore, if social deplatforming is justified, it cannot be justified qua punishment.
(4) Its only remaining justification is as an exercise of private associational freedom: the community selects its participants according to its own values and preferences.
(5) But private associational freedom, precisely because it is not punishment, is not constrained by proportionality or by the gravity of the offense; it permits exclusion for reasons that are trivial, mistaken, or malicious.
(6) Consequently, either social deplatforming is an illegitimate form of punishment (failing the conditions in (2)), or it is a legitimate but normatively unstructured exercise of private preference, in which case there is no moral distinction between deplatforming a speaker for harmful misinformation and excluding a member for preferring an unfashionable genre of music.
(7) We clearly think there is such a distinction, and I want to preserve it.
(8) I also want to preserve the structural point in (2): distributed sanctions really are different from state punishment.

So where does the argument slip? Which premise—most plausibly (1) or (4)—should I reject, and what does that cost? If I reject (1) and deny that deplatforming is ever punishment, do I lose the ability to criticize disproportionate cancellations? If I reject (4) and deny that private association is the fallback justification, what alternative framework remains, and does it avoid the dilemma without smuggling state-like obligations back into voluntary communities?
