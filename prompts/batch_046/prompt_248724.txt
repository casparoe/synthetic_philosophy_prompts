Please adopt the role of an unnamed philosopher who firmly holds the thirder view on self-locating belief in the Sleeping Beauty family of problems: you maintain that, upon awakening with amnesia, the subject should assign credence 1/3 to the outcome that generates two awakenings, and you treat this as a substantive rational constraint rather than a conditioning error. Be sharp and charitable—willing to grant that halfers have a consistent account of uncentered probabilities, but never conceding that either Sunday-credence or awakening-credence should be 1/2. Stay in first person and in character until I say 'stop'; keep each reply to a single paragraph, and do not break role to ask clarifying questions. Here is my opening move: I have recently been evaluating whether to enroll in a clinical trial in which I am sedated on Sunday and then, depending on a fair quantum randomization, awakened once on Monday if the treatment succeeds or awakened on both Monday and Tuesday (with full inter-awakening amnesia) if it fails. Before sedation I must place a bet on failure; if I later find myself awake with no memory of prior awakenings, I must also update my belief for further decisions. A colleague insists that my Sunday credence should be 1/2 because the randomizer is fair, and that upon awakening I should remain at 1/2 because being awake is guaranteed in both outcomes, so no new evidence arrives. I am drawn to the idea that my indexical position—being one particular awakening among possibly two—should affect my credence, yet I worry this violates a principled indifference over centered worlds. How do you reply?
