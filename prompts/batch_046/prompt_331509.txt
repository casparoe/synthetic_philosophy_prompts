i run a philosophy workshop, about 12 grad students, 90 minutes. design a structured deliberation/game that makes them feel the tension in who has work and leisure right — the clash between weber's protestant ethic (work as moral duty), the french 35-hour week (leisure as legislated right), and karoshi (death by overwork as structural). inject a niche framing: treat leisure not as free time but as negative labor — hours you're compensated not to produce. give them three roles (employer, worker, regulator) and a simulated economy: vote on a labor policy behind a veil of ignorance, then recieve their assigned role and must defend the outcome. use point payoffs arranged so the weber-role feels robbed when others rest while the karoshi-role feels like bare survival. they should run into the paradox themselves — that legislated rest feels like punishment to some and salvation to others — before anyone names it. include rules and physical setup, a timeline, debrief mapping emotional shifts onto the conceptual problem, an honest prediction of failure (people will conflate karoshi with personal failure rather than system failure), and a frank admission of which feature the room can't reproduce (the theological roots of weber's ethic and the specific japanese corporate culture behind karoshi, which the facilitator has to supply). keep it casual, no citations or reading lists needed.
