I'm a philosophy prof who advises our city's ethics review board on procurement and HR rules. Every month we recieve petitions asking us to mandate or ban some individual-level behavior—no beef in canteen contracts, carbon offsets for staff flights, reminder nudges for local voting—backed by arguments about climate harm or democratic failure and the old inefficacy objection. I need you to turn the philosophy of collective harm into a reusable decision procedue my board can run in about 20 minutes. No literature review, no bibliography, just a flowchart in words: ordered questions, pass/fail conditions, hard stops, and explicit flags for where judgment cant be eliminated and the chair has to sign off.

Be opinionated. Don't present both sides and leave us hanging—take a definite stance on whether causal redundancy ever excuses inaction inside institutional rules, and bake that into the steps. Defend your cutoff rather than hedge.

Build in a "threshold proximity" check—whether the collective harm is near a nonlinear tipping point—but dont let that become a loophole; specify exactly how near is near enough and justify the number you pick.

Then apply it to three proposals:
1. A total beef ban across all 40 public school cafeterias (this is the one where I think the answer should probably be no, but I want your procedure to be honest if it comes out yes—dont fudge it for me).
2. A mandatory voting-reminder system for all 3,000 municipal employees in local elections.
3. A $200 carbon-offset fee on every staff business flight.

For each, show where the procedure stops and what it recommends.

Also tell me exactly where this misfires—what kinds of proposals will break it?—and list the judgment-call steps that are really values in disguise.

Write like youre briefing a tired board member who hasnt read Parfit but has to vote tomorrow.
