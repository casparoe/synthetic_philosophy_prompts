I'm working through a comparative ethics essay on institutional gift-giving, and I've constructed a hypothetical case to test my intuitions. Consider a governance system—call it the Rotating Ledger—in which every material benefit given to a public official is entered into an open, cyclical registry shared by all citizens. The registry does not hide the exchange; it formalizes it, generating a transparent obligation for the official to reciprocate to the community rather than to the individual donor, within a fixed temporal window. Critics trained in Western anti-corruption norms immediately classify this as bribery made visible: the flow of goods creates dependency, and the official's duty is distorted by private interest, even if the interest is collectively registered. Defenders reply that corruption requires a hidden violation of public duty, and since the Ledger makes obligation explicit and reciprocal rather than secret and extractive, it transforms gift into social bond.

Write a dialogue between two interlocutors of equal competence—one advancing a universalist definition of corruption as the subordination of public role to private gain, regardless of visibility or formalization, and one advancing a contextualist view that corruption is constituted by the breach of locally authoritative norms of office, not by the presence of exchange itself. They should argue about the Rotating Ledger in particular: the universalist must address why transparency and collective registration fail to eliminate asymmetric power, and the contextualist must address why importing a Western distinction between public and private misdescribes societies where office and reciprocity are not separated ontologically. The exchange should be genuine back-and-forth, with each speaker answering the previous point rather than delivering parallel speeches.

Begin the dialogue immediately, with no introductory framing or commentary. Have the first speaker state their bottom-line judgment about the Ledger in the very first sentence, then unfold the reasoning through the exchange, with no restatement of the question and no preface outside the spoken lines.
