Write eight to ten interview questions that press the substance of this view rather than asking her to summarize Dewey. For each, provide a follow-up calibrated to her most probable defense—likely an appeal to Dewey's idea of ends-in-view as revisable working hypotheses rather than fixed ends—so that the follow-up forces her to specify how that distinction does the normative work she needs.
