I have been thinking on my own about the well-known cases in which surgeons divide the corpus callosum to treat severe epilepsy, and about the long debate over whether the patient then contains one consciousness or two. Nearly every account I have encountered treats the division as instantaneous: at one moment there is unity, and at the next there is separation. But I want to pursue a framing I have not seen addressed directly. Imagine instead that the callosal fibers are lost gradually—through a hypothetical progressive condition that severs them one by one over many years—while the rest of the brain remains intact, and the patient can describe their inner experience at every stage. Would consciousness split abruptly once some hidden threshold is crossed, or would the feeling of being a single self weaken continuously? And if the weakening is continuous, does that show that unity of consciousness is not an all-or-nothing property of minds, but rather a gradient that we only notice when it collapses?
