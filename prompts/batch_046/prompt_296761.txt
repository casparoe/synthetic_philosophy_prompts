Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on the inefficacy objection to individual moral obligation in collective harms. Name Camp One “Asymmetric Structuralists,” who hold that causal inefficacy undermines direct obligation in threshold-driven harms (climate tipping points, electoral outcomes) where individual actions are redundant, but not in linear supply-chain harms (meat consumption) where each act compositively feeds demand; and Camp Two “Uniform Compositionalists,” who deny that harm-structure asymmetry resolves the moral question, arguing that either individual actions normatively compose collective outcomes in all three domains—dissolving inefficacy everywhere—or they fail to do so everywhere, leaving obligation intact only through indirect or expressive channels. The deliverable should include: a numbered agreed-claims section covering the empirical dynamics of climate feedback loops, voting-margin distributions, and market-demand elasticity, plus conceptual agreement on what “making a difference” requires; a disagreements table stating each camp’s precise formulation of its own view in wording that camp accepts; the cruxes—specifically whether linear versus non-linear harm structures carry independent normative weight, and whether predictive ecological or economic models should govern the scope of obligation; and for each crux one concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each camp’s predicted assessment recorded in advance. Finish with a brief joint evaluation of whether any portion of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive. Format the output as the numbered section followed by the table. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
