ok so im writing my philosophical profile for a meta-ethics seminar and want a consistency check before i present. heres roughly where ive landed on confucian role ethics, and i want to know if im just helping myself to incompatible stuff.

my commitments:
(1) moral obligation is constituted by relationships — the five cardinal relationships (parent-child, ruler-subject, spouse-spouse, elder-younger sibling, friend-friend) arent applications of some prior principle but are themselves the ground of ethics.
(2) but if a role demands something that strips basic human dignity — say a father ordering a child into something degrading — then the child owes nothing, not even minimal deference; the role-claim is void.
(3) theres no standpoint outside our roles from which to judge them; we always reason as parents, children, friends, citizens.
(4) modern structures (nuclear families, mobile labor, contractual work) mean we have to reinterpret roles — filial piety should become emotional reciprocity and mutual care, not obedience.
(5) the affective side is constitutive, not decorative: you havent really fulfilled the role unless youve cultivated the right feeling (humaneness, affection, loyalty).
(6) also i think the friend relation should carry equal normative weight to family relations, even though tradition usually ranks family above friendship.

im worried (2) smuggles in a universalist standard that (1) and (3) reject. and im worried (4) and (5) pull apart — if modern feelings dont match traditional ones, are we still talking about the same roles? but maybe (6) actually helps resolve something?

please audit the package. go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, rank the real ones by severity, and for each serious tension say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. then tell me which single revision cleans up the most problems.
