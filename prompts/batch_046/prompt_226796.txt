me and a couple friends are putting together a small reading group on self-reference and strange loops, specifically Hofstadter's idea of tangled hierarchies and that 'inviolate level' that never gets caught in the loop — like in Escher's Drawing Hands where the hands draw each other but the paper and the artist stay outside. i want a poem to open the session. blank verse, maybe 30 lines split into 5 six-line stanazas. the structrue should do the philosophical work: stanza 1 describes a system at one level (say, a hand drawing a line), stanza 2 pushes up to the meta-level (the line is drawing the hand), and by stanza 4 the levels have collapsed into each other. then stanza 5 should be readable as describing stanza 1 but from inside the loop — so the poem literally loops back on itself. but in every stanza there must be one exact same line, word for word, that represents the inviolate level — something that doesn't change and never gets drawn into the tangle, like 'the page remains white' or 'the pencil never bends back on itself'. don't resolve it at the end; let the loop close and that untouched line sit there, outside. make it accurate enough that someone who knows the concept sees the strange loop happening in the form itself, not just named.
