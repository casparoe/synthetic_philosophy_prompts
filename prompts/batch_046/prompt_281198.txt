I've been reading about pain asymbolia - folks with brain damage who feel the sensory part of pain but don't find it bad at all, they don't pull away or get upset. People use this to argue that the 'badness' isn't in the raw feeling but in some separate emotional layer. But I keep noticing the mirror image in arguments: someone describes a case where a person has all the dread and withdrawal behavior, all the 'this is awful' reaction, but reports zero sensory pain - no burning, no sharpness, nothing in the feeling channel. It gets used to show suffering can exist without the sensory substrate. Is there an established term for this reverse dissociation - affect without sensation - or is it mostly just a hypothetical? And does anyone actually treat the two dissociations together, or are they kept in seperate boxes? I feel like if asymbolia proves pain's badness is separable, then this reverse case should matter just as much for the debate, but I can't tell if there's real literature on the symmetry or if I'm just inventing a parallel.
