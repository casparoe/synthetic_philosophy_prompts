After a long argument with a friend, I want to relay a disagreement between Mira and Jonas about abortion and ask you to referee. Mira opened with a version of Thomson’s violinist: even granting the fetus full personhood, no one is required to serve as life support for nine months, and the analogy holds because the fetus is an externally dependent being sustained only by the mother’s body. Jonas countered that pregnancy is not analogous to being hooked up to a stranger—the fetus is the biological continuation of the mother’s own reproductive process—and that Marquis’s future-like-ours argument applies: abortion wrongs the fetus by depriving it of a valuable future, whereas the violinist already has such a future intact, so disconnecting him is not parallel. Mira replied that the violinist also has a future-like-ours, and disconnecting him deprives him of exactly that future, so if deprivation of a valuable future is the wrong, the analogy actually strengthens her case rather than weakening it. Jonas then offered an asymmetry: the violinist possesses present psychological continuity, so killing him violates an existing person’s rights, while the fetus lacks present consciousness, which makes abortion a different moral kind—depriving a being of a future without violating any present right. Mira introduced a further consideration neither had addressed: she claimed the fetus’s potential consciousness is not merely a modal possibility but is “structurally coupled” to the mother’s body, producing what she called entangled potentiality—a joint ontological state that is dissolved by abortion. On her view, this undermines Thomson’s clean separation between independent host and dependent, because the mother is not sustaining a separate being but participating in a shared potential state. Jonas rejected this as metaphysical mysticism, insisting that potentiality is either instantiated in present consciousness or it is not, and that bodily autonomy survives regardless of any claimed ontological coupling.

Please adjudicate point by point: the strength of the violinist analogy given the future-like-ours objection, Jonas’s asymmetry based on present consciousness, and whether Mira’s notion of entangled potentiality does any real work. Then say who is more right overall, or whether the dispute is a stalemate. Finally, state clearly what argument or evidence would change your mind about the overall verdict, and identify the specific step in your reasoning where failure is most likely.
