ok so i was fiddling with Gödel's modal ontological proof in a proof checker and stumbled on a corollary that can't be right, but i can't locate the leak.

(1) In Gödel's system, positive properties are defined via negation, and if all positive properties are jointly instatiated, that being exists necessarily.
(2) Call a bundle B "Gödel-saturated" if it includes every positive property and excludes every negative one.
(3) Now consistency of B is logical, and in S5 consistency of a property set implies there's some world where they're jointly realized.
(4) So if B is Gödel-saturated and consistent, a B-realizer is possible, hence by (1) necessary, hence exstant and actual.
(5) But I can build two distinct Gödel-saturated bundles B1 and B2 that agree on every observable feature but differ on hidden relational properties — say, B1 creates world W with hidden constant c=7 and B2 creates W with c=9, where c is permanently unobservable.
(6) Both bundles are consistent and Gödel-saturated, so both necessarily exist.
(7) That gives us two distinct necessary beings with identical observable footprints, which seems to break uniqueness and reference.
(8) The escape hatch might be that (3) smuggles in possibility wrongly for property-bundles, or that (1) only applies to the unique maximal bundle, not to arbitrary saturated ones.
(9) But Gödel's derivation never explicitly restricts the input to uniqueness; it just takes "God-like" as having all positives.
(10) So is the mistake in treating consistency as world-accessible for bundles, or in assuming that "all positives" can be partitioned into hidden vs observable? I'd really like to keep (1) and (3) if possible.
