Consider a researcher with roughly three years, a background in moral psychology and institutional design rather than classical philology, who wants to work at the intersection of comparative Chinese philosophy and applied ethics. The standard framing opposes Mencius's claim that human nature contains innate moral sprouts (si duan) — compassion, shame, deference, and discernment — to Xunzi's claim that human nature is oriented toward self-interest (e) and that ritual (li) and deliberate effort (wei) are required to construct virtue. Beyond this textbook opposition, several less-discussed lines of inquiry are open: whether the sprouts are best interpreted as affective dispositions, proto-rational capacities, or socially scaffolded responses; whether Xunzi's concept of wei anticipates modern constructivist or situationist accounts of moral development; whether either framework can accommodate contemporary findings about innate prosociality and fairness in infancy without theoretical collapse; and whether adopting a Mencian versus Xunzian anthropology should change how we design institutions — education, governance, or conflict resolution — in ways that are not merely rhetorical.

If this researcher can pursue only one sustained line of work in the available time, which open question should come first, which should follow, and which should be set aside? Rank them with explicit reasoning: tractability for someone without deep training in classical Chinese; upstream dependence (which answers, if settled, would make others easier or moot); and practical significance (which settlement would most change downstream work in ethics, psychology, or institutional design). In your answer, address the misconceptions that typically derail comparative work — for example, treating Xunzi as a simple blank-slate opponent to Mencius, or assuming the debate maps neatly onto modern nature/nurture dichotomies — and identify one concrete check that would show whether the researcher has genuinely advanced the question rather than relabeled familiar positions. I am not asking for a bibliography, a survey of the literature, or citations; I want a defended agenda that explains what to work on first, what to leave for later, and why the ordering holds.
