A philosophy blog I read summarizes the classic dust-specks-versus-torture aggregation challenge like this: "Consider a choice between torturing one innocent person for fifty years and inflicting a barely perceptible eye irritation on each member of a population so vast—say, orders of magnitude beyond the number of atoms in the observable universe—that the summed discomfort exceeds the torture victim's suffering. Any view that aggregates harms additively across individuals must prefer the dust-speck scenario, since the total harm is greater. Because virtually everyone would choose the torture, this thought experiment refutes linear aggregation and forces us to adopt a threshold: harms below a certain intensity have literally zero moral weight, and moral theory should build discontinuities around them. The only consistent response is to deny that tiny harms accumulate at all." I feel uneasy with this framing—particularly the move from "our intuition rejects the outcome" to "aggregation must be discontinuous," and the claim that this is the only consistent reply.
