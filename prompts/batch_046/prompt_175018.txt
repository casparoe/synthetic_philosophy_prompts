A philosophy blog post I came across offers the following summary of deflationary theories of truth. Deflationists, it says, treat "true" as a purely linguistic convenience, which entails that there is literally no property of truth in reality. To assert that "Snow is white" is true is therefore to assert nothing beyond that snow is white; explicit truth-ascriptions are wholly redundant, and truth can never enter into a genuine explanation of belief success, predictive accuracy, or semantic convergence. From this explanatory emptiness the post infers that deflationists must reject correspondence theories not as unnecessary but as conceptually incoherent, since correspondence posits a substantial thought-world relation that deflationists have shown cannot exist. It adds that, lacking any property of truth, deflationists are forced to deny that belief aims at truth, and so they cannot make sense of the normative claim that false beliefs are defective in a way independent of practical failure. The summary concludes that deflationism is a form of ontological eliminativism: it expunges truth from our ontology, leaving only bare sentences and assertions, and it requires us to regard ordinary truth-talk either as redundant noise or as literally false.

This sounds coherent, but I worry it collapses redundancy theory, minimalism, and disquotationalism into a single position, and that several of its conclusions are stronger than defenders actually accept. Is this a fair characterization? Please separate what is accurate about deflationary commitments from what is overstatement or misattribution. In particular: do deflationists deny any property of truth whatsoever, or only a metaphysically substantial, explanatory property? How do they handle generalizations such as "everything the witness said is true," and do they treat those as redundant, false, or preserved via the disquotational schema? Is correspondence theory regarded as incoherent, or merely as empty, uninformative, or reducible? Does the denial of a substantial truth-property entail that belief cannot aim at truth, or do deflationists reconstruct that normative aim in deflationary terms? And once these corrections are made, does the claim that truth plays no explanatory role still stand, or is that claim itself distorted? I am looking for an audit I can act on without consulting primary literature.
