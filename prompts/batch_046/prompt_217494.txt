Let's imagine Weber got thawed out and had to sit through a modern seminar on generative sociology. You know, those agent-based setups where researchers code thousands of synthetic actors with belief-revision rules and interaction protocols, then run the simulaton to see whether macro patterns like normative cascades or institutional decay emerge. The defenders say this is methodological individualism done right — start with interpretable micro-action and aggregate upward. The critics reply that the aggregation architecture and the environment parameters do all the causal work, so it's really holism in a micro mask. Then there's the newer twist where people drop LLMs into the agent slots, giving each synthetic actor an internal monologue and episodic memory, and claim that makes the simulation hermeneutically closer to real social action. Where do you think Weber would land? Would he accept agent-based models as extending his program of Verstehen, or reject them because algorithmic "action" isn't meaningful social action? And on the LLM version specifically — does giving the agents synthetic inner life solve the problem, or does it just manufacture a fake interperetation that misses the point of understanding actual human motives?
