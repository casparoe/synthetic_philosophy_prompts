As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy of mind, I have tried to formalize a tension I see between Daniel Dennett's multiple drafts model and basic constraints on physical measurement, but I fear my reasoning proves more than I intend. Would you examine the argument below step by step, identify precisely where it fails—whether through a hidden equivocation, an illicit analogy between physical reference frames and phenomenological presence, or an overstatement of what introspection authorizes—and discuss what abandoning that step would cost? Please also say how confident you are in your overall diagnosis and explicitly single out the point about which you are least certain.

(1) The Cartesian theater, in the sense Dennett criticizes, requires a single functional locus in the brain where information is unified and presented to a central observer.

(2) The multiple drafts model denies any such locus, proposing instead that sensory processing generates parallel, competing narrative fragments distributed across cortical regions, none of which enjoys canonical status as "the" experience.

(3) In physical measurement, a distributed array of detectors lacking a unified reference frame can register only relational correlations; it does not determine an absolute present state of the system measured.

(4) If conscious experience is analogous to an internal measurement—an internal registration of external and bodily conditions—then without a central reference frame there ought to be no determinate fact about what is being experienced at any given moment.

(5) Phenomenological reflection, however, seems to reveal a definite present with specific, unified contents, not an indeterminate superposition of rival drafts.

(6) Therefore, either introspection systematically misrepresents its own structure, or the brain must contain something functionally equivalent to a Cartesian theater—perhaps realized through global synchronization—that selects one draft and fixes the reference frame.

(7) To deny the authority of introspection about the present moment is self-defeating, since the denial itself must occur within some present experience.

(8) Consequently, the multiple drafts model cannot be correct, and we are compelled to accept some form of Cartesian theater after all.
