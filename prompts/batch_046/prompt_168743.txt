You chair the quarterly ethics-certification board for a hospital consortium that deploys AI-assisted diagnostic tools. You must audit whether each clinical team exhibits the virtue of epistemic humility—here defined as the willingness to override an AI recommendation when it conflicts with direct clinical judgment—while taking seriously the situationist critique that stable individual character traits are weak predictors of behavior across different situational pressures. Design a reusable decision procedure your auditors can run in 45 minutes per team, using only incident logs, one observed high-fidelity simulation, and brief structured interviews; the procedure must list ordered questions, specify sufficient evidence for each, indicate where it stops with a fail, flag where the decision turns on values rather than facts, and incorporate both a cross-context consistency check and a pressure-gradient test to address situationist concerns. The output must be co-signed by you and the team lead. Apply it to three teams: one that defers to AI routinely but overrides in a single high-stakes simulation; one that challenges AI recommendations mainly when senior authority is absent; and one that fails both contextual checks despite excellent individual personality assessments—the last being the case where the procedure delivers a result you will not like. State explicitly what the procedure cannot resolve. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then present the reasoning and the worked cases with no preamble and no restatement of the question. Say how confident you are in the procedure’s design and single out the step about which you are least sure.
