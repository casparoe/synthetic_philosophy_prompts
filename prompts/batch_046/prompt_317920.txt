Write a dialogue between two philosophers—call them Mara and Elias—debating whether a person survives gradual, interleaved neuronal replacement. In the scenario they discuss, a patient's biological neurons are replaced one-by-one with synthetic analogues that preserve macro-level signal transmission but introduce slight, cumulative micro-latencies in synaptic firing, so that by the end of the process the brain is fully synthetic but has never been "offline." Mara defends a continuity view: personal identity and phenomenal consciousness persist as long as psychological and causal-functional integration remain uninterrupted, regardless of substrate change or temporal drift. Elias defends a substrate-bound view: the original biological organism is gradually destroyed rather than preserved, and the synthetic system—despite behavioral continuity—is a distinct entity that inherits but does not continue the original's consciousness, especially because the accumulated latency disrupts the precise temporal binding required for unified phenomenal experience.

The dialogue should feature real back-and-forth across at least three exchanges. Each speaker must respond directly to the other's preceding argument rather than restating their opening position. They should specifically address: (1) whether there is a meaningful difference between repairing a ship plank-by-plank and replacing a brain neuron-by-neuron; (2) whether the 50% hybrid stage represents one consciousness, two, or a fading one; and (3) whether micro-latency accumulation is a genuine disruption of binding or merely a change in implementation detail that leaves experience untouched. Neither character should be a strawman; each should grant the force of the other's point before offering a counter. Conclude without declaring a winner.
