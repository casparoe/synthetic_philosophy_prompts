I've been leaning into a pretty strong version of Dennet's real-pattern view: I think patterns are only genuinely real at the intentional stance, and design-stance or physical-stance descriptions are just compressed predictions that don't actually latch onto ontology. Specifically, a pattern is real for me only if it supports counterfactual inferances that lower-level descriptions can't reconstruct without massive loss — and I think only beliefs and desires about agents do that. What'd actually count agianst this? Not the standard "all three stances are equally real" reply. I want the defeaters that should hurt: say, cases where design-stance patterns in climate modeling or deep-learning feature maps clearly support counterfactuals physics can't reach, or neuroscience results showing intentional explanations collapse into design-level ones. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward. Which ones would a committed defender concede, and which would they dismiss by saying "still just heuristic"? And does that kind of insulation make the view unfalsifiable in a bad way for a metaphysical claim?
