A research-funding council requires applicants to submit an ethical footprint listing every foreseeable moral risk, yet its evaluation protocol explicitly forbids reviewers from weighing these risks against one another or aggregating them into an overall moral score; instead, each risk is assessed in isolation against a binary threshold, and funding is withheld only if any single risk crosses a catastrophic line, regardless of how many sub-threshold risks accumulate. What theory of moral value does this institutional design presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view: does it treat moral considerations as Quinean internal commitments of a conceptual scheme — present within each category but lacking any external, aggregate measure — or does it presuppose a strong form of value incommensurability? Say whether the practice actually requires Quinean naturalism about values or remains compatible with rival accounts (value pluralism, lexical deontology, threshold consequentialism), and describe what the funding rules would have to change if moral values were treated as fully quantifiable within a single naturalistic framework.
