Construct a thought experiment that isolates hyperbolic discounting as an explanation of weakness of will (akrasia) from a competing unitary-rationality view that denies intertemporal conflict altogether. Engineer the case so that the two accounts deliver opposite verdicts about whether the agent is genuinely akratic, not merely about the causal pathway of her action.

Specifications for the scenario:
- Use a realistic, non-exotic setting—e.g., adherence to a clinical rehabilitation protocol or a professional certification study plan—never a trolley problem or science-fiction premise.
- The agent must know at every stage that one course is all-things-considered better for her long-term goals; ignorance or false belief about outcomes is not available as an explanation.
- Inject the following niche but fully described mechanism: the agent administers to herself an introspective calibration protocol in which she tracks her own past choice patterns, estimates her intertemporal discount parameter, and then designs a personalized precommitment penalty calibrated to that estimate. The critical twist is that her introspective model is systematically wrong: she takes herself to discount approximately exponentially with a low rate, whereas her actual discount function is steeply hyperbolic.
- Structure the penalty so that, had her introspective model been correct, it would have been sufficient to prevent defection at the moment of temptation; but because her true curve is hyperbolic, she defects anyway, incurs the penalty, and reports the episode as a failure of will.
- Keep the scenario to one paragraph.
