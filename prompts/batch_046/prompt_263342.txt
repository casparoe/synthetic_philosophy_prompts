The splits should run, in order: (1) whether the dead can be wronged posthumously at all; (2) if yes, whether the core wrong is epistemic (deceiving the audience about history), dignitarian (violating an objective standing of the deceased), or relational (damaging survivors or collective memory); (3) whether there is a "counter-fiction" exception that permits dramatic invention when it corrects a prior, well-documented historical distortion; and (4) whether temporal distance or public obscurity weakens the obligation. Each leaf should state a position name, or "unnamed" if the combination is unoccupied, followed by exactly one sentence stating its most awkward commitment—for example, a view that accepts the counter-fiction exception must explain why repairing falsehood about a person's public record licenses inventing falsehood about their private mental states. Flag any pair of leaves your tree fails to separate cleanly. Present the taxonomy as a nested outline, keep it within 400 words, and close with one paragraph diagnosing which branches appear empty and whether each emptiness stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect.
