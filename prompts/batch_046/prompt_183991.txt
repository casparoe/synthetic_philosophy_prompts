I am preparing a single fifty-minute session for first-year philosophy undergraduates with no calculus or formal logic background, on the conceptual dispute over actual infinities—specifically Hilbert's hotel as an intuition pump, Cantor's argument that there are different cardinalities of infinity, and the finitist denial of completed infinite totalities. I want a teaching plan, not the explanation itself. Lay out the exact order in which these three pieces must be introduced so that Cantor's result feels inevitable rather than paradoxical, specifying the dependency relation between each stage: what must be secured conceptually before Hilbert's scenario is introduced, what that scenario must establish before Cantor's pairing argument is presented, and what both must establish before the finitist objection can be made intelligible rather than dismissed as mere conservatism. Identify the one concrete carrying scenario—whether a variant of Hilbert's hotel or another—specifying precisely what structural properties it must possess (e.g., discrete, countable, non-physical occupancy) and what intuitive baggage it must lack (e.g., resource exhaustion, spatial contiguity, temporal sequence) in order to serve this audience. Map the predictable misconceptions: treating infinity as only "indefinitely large," conflating cardinal comparison with ordinal ordering, assuming that one-to-one correspondence requires physical or temporal pairing, and interpreting finitism as skepticism about very large numbers rather than as a principled rejection of completed totalities. For each misconception, indicate the precise point in the sequence where it should be preempted and the form the preemption should take. Propose one brief check for understanding—something the students could answer in writing or discussion—that would reveal whether they have genuinely grasped the distinction between potential and actual infinity rather than having memorized the hotel story. Finally, state explicitly what must be omitted at this level—whether transfinite arithmetic beyond aleph-null, the continuum hypothesis, formal ZFC axioms, or specific historical attributions to Cantor, Hilbert, and the finitists—and justify each omission in terms of conceptual dependency rather than mere time constraint.
