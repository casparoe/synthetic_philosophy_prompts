I sit on the interdisciplinary review board of a private foundation that funds work at the intersection of moral psychology and institutional design, and I need a recommendation memo for Thursday’s meeting. The board is split over a pilot proposal to introduce a verified “peer accountability stipend” in undergraduate philosophy seminars: students who document and submit verified instances of academic misconduct by peers receive a small but non-trivial payment, with the goal of increasing reporting rates without relying solely on pro-social motivation. The principal investigator frames this as a straightforward mechanism-design problem—aligning private incentives with collective goods—while a dissenting member raises the wrong-kind-of-reasons objection: that monetizing the detection of wrongdoing converts a moral or civic duty into a transactional exchange, and that an institution which systematically rewards correct behavior for incorrect motives may corrupt the very normative ecology it seeks to protect. Please draft the memo: it should recommend a concrete course of action (approve, reject, or approve with specific structural conditions and a six-month review timeline), include reasoning that board members who think in terms of incentive alignment and epistemic norms can follow, and treat the wrong-kind-of-reasons objection seriously—engaging with whether institutional incentives can be designed to elicit correct conduct without necessarily undermining the moral quality of agents’ motivations—rather than dismissing it as an impractical concern. The document should be formal, suitable for circulation, and should give the strongest consideration on the losing side an honest hearing before reaching its bottom line.
