Construct a decision-tree taxonomy that classifies answers to the question "When does a legend become history?" Use the Trojan War, King Arthur, and the Exodus only as reference cases for testing the commitments at the leaves; do not use them as branch labels. The tree must be built from three successive binary splits: first, whether historicity requires direct archaeological correlation with a named site or figure mentioned in the tradition; second, whether an unbroken written transmission with no gap exceeding three centuries is necessary regardless of narrative consistency; third, whether collective oral memory can preserve a core event across generations without any written intermediary. Each leaf should carry a descriptive label (e.g., "archaeological correlationist," "textual continuityist," "oral-memory preservationist") or be marked "unnamed" if the combination is unoccupied, together with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment regarding at least one of the three reference cases. Identify any pair of leaves that these three splits fail to separate, state the fourth diagnostic question that would distinguish them, and briefly diagnose whether that missing distinction reveals an unexplored but coherent position or an unstable combination. Limit the tree to no more than eight leaves. The deliverable must be buildable by conceptual analysis alone: include no bibliographies, no citation requests, and no appeals to obscure specialist literature.
