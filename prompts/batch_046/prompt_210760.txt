Consider the canonical distinction between finkish dispositions, in which the conditions that ought to trigger a manifestation simultaneously eliminate the underlying property, and masked dispositions, in which an independent interfering factor blocks manifestation while the property itself persists. Now imagine an engineered crystalline solid—call it a metastable lattice—whose micro-structure is arranged so that the very mechanical stress that should induce brittle fracture instead triggers an instantaneous polymorphic transition to a ductile configuration; the brittle disposition is therefore never manifested, not because an external blocker intervenes, but because the triggering conditions themselves initiate an internal restructuring that removes the structural basis for fragility. If both the fink and the mask accounts can be rendered in exactly the same modal terms—there is no nearby world in which this lattice fractures under that stress—does the metaphysical distinction between them collapse into nothing more than our choice of which micro-state we privilege as the modal base? Or is there a substantive difference that survives even when the interfering mechanism is endogenous, deterministic, and co-activated with the very conditions of manifestation?
