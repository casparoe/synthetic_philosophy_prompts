In my seminar on Hofstadter's Le Ton beau de Marot we've hit a wall over Marot's poem Ma Mignonne. One camp says the rhyme scheme and the alexandrine's 6-6 caesura are cognitive scaffolding—without them the poem's argument is parsed differently—so any translation that drops them gives you a different thought, not the same one in new clothes. The other camp says thats mysticism; form is packaging, and if the propositional content about the sick beloved is intact you've preserved the poem, full stop. Both sides cite the same failed translations from the book and neither budges. Is the stall because we're mixing up aesthetic value with semantic content, or is there a hidden empirical claim about how rhyme actually structures comprehension? Or are we answering different questions—one about identity conditions for artworks, one about communicative success—under the single word translation? What kind of disagreement is this really, and what would move it: a reading-time study on rhyme and parsing, a formal model of constraint weighting, or is it just bedrock intuition about whether a poem can survive losing its music?
