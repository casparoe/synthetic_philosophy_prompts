so my uncle died a few weeks ago and left me a hard drive full of files i can't decrypt — same filenames, same sizes, but the encrypted payloads are totally different. it's made me weirdly obsessed with whether those files are actually different entities or just one thing with hidden variation, and i want to check if my views hang together before i bring it up at our philosophy of science meetup.

here's roughly where i've landed:

1. i think you can't genuinely identify or refer to an object unless there's at least one accessible feature that distinguishes it — if every individuating property is permanently hidden, reference just fails.
2. but i also think there are real entities (like these encrypted structures, or quantum states behind decoherence) whose identity conditions depend essentially on information that's in principle inaccessible.
3. if two candidates are indistinguishible across all accessible info, theoretical parsimoney demands we treat them as one entity — a kind of epistimic identity of indiscernibles.
4. i don't think causal inertness is enough to deny an ontological difference; hidden structure can be real even if it never makes a detectable ripple.
5. in cases of radical epistimic limitation about individuating features, we should suspend ontological judgment rather than defaulting to sameness or pluralism.

do these hang together? i feel like 1 and 2 might clash, and 3 with 4 feels unstable, while 1 and 5 look like they pull in opposite directions — but maybe that last one's just apparent? rank the real tensions by severity, say for each what has to give and what the cheapest fix is, and tell me the single revision that cleans up the most. keep it under 300 words, bottom line first, then the audit ordered worst to least bad. no citations needed.
