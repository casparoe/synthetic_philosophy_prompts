You are an expert philosopher of language and metaethics writing a doctoral comprehensive exam. Before drafting, briefly brainstorm the central coherence problem in Quine's moral philosophy: how his rejection of moral ontology interacts with his naturalism, his criterion of ontological commitment, and the indeterminacy of translation. Then write one 90-minute essay question that cannot be answered by summarizing Quine's moral anti-realism and must instead test whether a candidate can trace internal tensions across his system.

The question should focus on Quine's treatment of moral values as lacking objective, mind-independent status—frequently compared to the internal values of a game or social practice—while he simultaneously insists that ethics is continuous with empirical psychology and sociology and must answer to the same parsimonious standards that govern physics. Ask candidates to examine whether these commitments are jointly tenable. Specifically, require them to address: (a) whether Quine's desert-landscape ontology can consistently exclude moral properties if moral discourse is indispensable to our best overall theory of human behavior; (b) whether his comparison of morality to a game commits him to a conventionalism that conflicts with his naturalized epistemology; and (c) whether applying the indeterminacy of translation thesis to ethical language makes moral disagreement merely verbal, and if so, whether that undermines the idea of ethical improvement rather than explaining it away.

Provide a model answer of about 600 words that demonstrates the specific philosophical moves required: isolating the tension between ontological parsimony and explanatory indispensability; distinguishing Quine's social-pragmatic account from full-blooded conventionalism; and showing why indeterminacy in ethics need not collapse moral dispute into verbal difference even if the analogy with physical theory holds.

Then provide a marking scheme built around exactly five must-hit points, with specific marks assigned to each (total 20), and for each point include a one-line note on how candidates typically fail to secure it. The scheme should separate answers that merely label Quine's positions from answers that actually adjudicate their consistency.
