I am a journalist covering theoretical physics and philosophy of mind, and in three weeks I am delivering an invited seminar at an interdisciplinary institute where I will defend a thesis I have been developing in recent essays: if panpsychism about fundamental fields is taken seriously—not as metaphor but as the claim that proto-experiential properties are genuinely distributed across quantum field configurations—then ethics must abandon its default organism-boundary assumption. Specifically, I argue for what I call coherence-weighted field prioritarianism, the view that moral weight scales not solely with biological complexity but with the integrated coherence of experiential gradients within a physical system, which entails that reducing high-coherence suffering in biological organisms may sometimes be ethically required even when doing so marginally lowers the total coherence-integrated sentience of the broader field in which those organisms are embedded. The audience will be about eighteen people: six theoretical physicists who regard panpsychism as empirically empty unless it yields novel predictions, three moral philosophers firmly committed to biological naturalism about sentience, two consciousness researchers who work with integrated-information metrics, and several science-studies graduate students attentive to conceptual sleight of hand; they are skeptical but not polemical, and they care especially about whether my coherence metric is doing hidden normative work, whether the view licenses absurd distributive implications, and whether it can be distinguished from mysticism dressed in physics vocabulary.

I need a Q&A prep sheet rather than a defense: the objections these specific subgroups will actually raise, ordered by how damaging they are likely to be and by the sequence in which they will probably come, each phrased in the vocabulary that subgroup would use. Include the objection I am most hoping nobody asks—the one that presses the apparent tension between coherence-weighted prioritarianism and the intuition that ethical protection should be concentrated rather than dispersed across physical fields—along with a note on why it is especially dangerous for my position. Finally, please state explicitly how confident you are in the strategic framing and audience dynamics I have described, and identify the single point—whether about a likely objection, a suggested concession, or the strength of a proposed reply—of which you are least sure.
