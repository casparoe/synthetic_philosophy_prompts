The topic is the symbol grounding problem, but I want to narrow it to a specific tension: whether a system can possess intrinsic semantic content when all its internal relations are either syntactic or statistical correlations with external measurements, and whether adding predictive accuracy across modalities changes anything essential. I want the session to end with the group able to see why correlation-to-data is not the same as causal-functional coupling to the world, without letting the discussion collapse into standard Chinese Room intuition-pumping.

Plan the explanatory arc. Agent A assigns R to spectral returns near 650nm via a calibrated sensor, achieves perfect predictive correlation with human color-labeling, but has no behavioral or affective response to heat or danger. Agent B assigns R to trigger an avoidance behavior directed at high-temperature sources, never measures light, and has no spectral database. The scenario must be precise enough to show why both might still fail to ground R, but it must also omit features—such as consciousness, biological embodiment, or language-use—that would let the group escape into those familiar moves. Fourth, provide one diagnostic check I can use at the end to distinguish a participant who grasps why correlation alone fails from one who has only learned to repeat the vocabulary of grounding. Fifth, state what I should openly refuse to explain—any architecture-level implementation, any specific historical taxonomy by name beyond what is conceptually necessary, and any claim about actual existing systems—and say how to frame that refusal so it does not look like an evasion. Write this as a teaching plan, not as the explanation itself.
