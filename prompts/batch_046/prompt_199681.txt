I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who defends a modified scale argument against widespread insect sentience. Her position rests on three claims: first, that evolutionary continuity alone cannot raise the prior probability of phenomenal consciousness in insects above a very low baseline; second, that genuine sentience requires what she terms subject-binding, a unified integrative structure that binds distributed neural processes into a single subject of experience, which she argues insects lack because their architectures are too decentralized; and third, that because subject-binding is binary rather than gradable, multiplying the number of insects does not multiply moral patients, so the scale argument resolves into a direct negative judgment rather than a probabilistic weighting. Draft eight to ten interview questions that press her on the internal logic of this view. I want at least two questions probing whether her binary subject-binding condition can be reconciled with the gradualist picture of consciousness endorsed by many comparative neuroscientists, one question asking how she distinguishes her position from a broader eliminativism about nonhuman animal consciousness, and a final follow-up asking what kind of empirical finding—without citing specific studies—could in principle weaken her confidence that insects lack subject-binding. Avoid generic biographical or career-oriented questions; each question should turn on the substance of her argument and anticipate her most likely reply.
