I have just come out of a long, exhausting argument with a close friend about whether I should complete a neural-replacement therapy I have been undergoing for four years. Damaged regions of my cortex have been gradually replaced with synthetic mesh that replicates neuronal spike patterns; only the prefrontal and medial temporal areas remain biological. After each prior stage there was a roughly forty-eight-hour recovery window during which autobiographical memory consolidation was managed by an auxiliary algorithm rather than by my native tissue. I have no first-person recollection of those intervals, though my partner describes my behavior during them as fully continuous. My friend argues that because my functional profile, relationships, and self-narrative have all persisted unchanged, both the synthetic substrate and these brief consolidation gaps are philosophically irrelevant: I am evidently the same person, and refusing the final replacement out of metaphysical anxiety is irrational. Yet I keep circling a worry I cannot precisely name. The mesh replicates spike trains but not glial modulation dynamics, and during those gaps my memory formation was not merely interrupted but actively mediated by a system external to my biological architecture. There was behavioral continuity, but not continuity of the underlying biological process that usually binds experience into autobiography. My friend treats this as an implementation detail; I feel there is a hidden assumption about what kind of continuity—functional, causal, phenomenal, or substrate-specific—actually grounds survival across change, but I cannot articulate it. Is my hesitation merely fear of the unknown disguised as philosophy? Or does the combination of substrate substitution and those interstitial, algorithmically mediated consolidation windows expose a real gap in the argument that functional replication plus observed continuity is sufficient? I want an honest diagnosis of the conceptual structure here, not reassurance. Please state how confident you are in your assessment and identify the specific claim or distinction about which you are least certain.
