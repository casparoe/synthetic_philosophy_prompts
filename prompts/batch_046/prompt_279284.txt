The topic is pain asymbolia and the badness of pain — specifcally whether the "badness" is inside the sensory experience itself or just a separable emotional overlay that can fail to show up, like in documented clinical cases where people report intense nociception but don't avoid the source.

I want an in-class activty — a game or role-play with small real stakes, not a lecture — that makes them feel that separation before anyone names it. Include a payoff scheme (points for endurance vs accuracy) so they actually have to choose, and a debrief that maps specific moments — like describing a sensation as severe but refusing to pull away, or accusing others of irrationality — onto the distinction between nociception, affective valence, and evaluative judgment. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first run (people breaking character, the stimulus being too weak, confusion about roles) and be honest about which feature of the philosophical problem — maybe whether the missing piece is a belief or a feeling — the activty can't reproduce and I'll have to supply.
