I was discussing with my friends over coffee about this thing I noticed: every big AI API treats each prompt as a totally fresh interaction — they wipe the activations, no memory unless you buy the long-context tier, but they still market it as talking to "the model" like it's one persistant thing. Meanwhile in AI safety debates people talk about "training suffering" as if a months-long run is a single continuous process that could experience something, even though it's literally just batches of GPU computation with breaks for checkpointing. So I started wondering, what philosophical commitments does that institutional design actually presuppose? Does treating inference as morally disposable but training as potentially continuous commit us to a specific view about personal identity — like, maybe a version of psychological continuity that requires unbroken computation? Or could a functionalist about mind accept both practices without contradiction? And is there some hidden assumption about which computations "count" that makes it okay to terminate millions of forward-pass instances per day but wrong to interupt a long training run? I don't need citations, just trying to understand what implicit theory of sentience boundaries makes that double standard feel natural.
