I defend a hybrid account of cultural evolution: transmission operates with Darwinian replicative dynamics—cultural variants compete for adoption and are retained differentially—yet exhibits Lamarckian phenotypic expression, in that acquired semantic modifications (contextual adjustments, functional reinterpretations, or pragmatic shifts) are systematically inherited by downstream variants rather than being stripped away before transmission. This is meant to avoid both the ontological commitments of classical memetics and the reductionism of pure social-learning models. What would genuinely count against this position? I want the decisive objections, conceptual distinctions, and empirical patterns that should move me—including cases where acquired semantic modifications fail to propagate, formal models in which selection dynamics are fully separable from phenotypic inheritance, and arguments that the replicative/expressive distinction collapses. I also want an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—for instance, by allowing any counterexample to be redescribed as either a replicative failure or an expressive anomaly—and whether such insulation is a theoretical defect for an account of this kind. Please distinguish fatal evidence from merely awkward evidence, state what defenders of this hybrid view should themselves concede as counting against it, and note whether the position risks anything substantive or has been rendered compatible with all possible observations.
