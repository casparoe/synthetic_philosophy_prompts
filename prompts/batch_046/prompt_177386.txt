I've been chewing on James's will-to-believe stuff versus Clifford's ethics of belief since a course discussion went weird, and I keep noticing a pattern I can't name. There's this proximity-driven flip in how people apply the two frameworks, and I want to know if it's actually a thing philosophers have discussed or just a bias I'm over-reading.

Here's the concrete pattern. Someone will defend holding a belief on thin evidance about a person close to them - say, "I choose to believe my friend meant well even though the text is ambiguous, because treating them as malicious would kill the friendship." They talk about it like a Jamesian live option, forced, momentous, part of the relationship itself. But then those same people turn around and demand Clifford-style rigor for beliefs that are structurally identical but about distant or abstract targets - "you can't assume that corporation is ethical without rock-solid proof, anything less is irresponsible." The evidence situation is basically the same: ambiguous signals, belief guides action, being wrong has real costs. The only clear difference is whether the belief-object is inside their social circle or outside it.

So is there an established concept for this asymmetry, where relational distance determines whether Jamesian or Cliffordian standards apply? I know James distinguishes live from dead and forced from unforced, and Clifford is universalist, but neither framework explicitly builds in proximity as a licensing factor. Is this just observed hypocrisy, or has someone carved it out technically? If there's no name, I'd want a definition that captures exactly what makes it different from ordinary inconsistency and from James's own categories - maybe something about beliefs that are constitutive of a relationship versus merely instrumental. Where are the edges: does it apply to family but not coworkers, to in-groups but not out-groups?

Please separate what's settled about James and Clifford's actual positions from what's genuinely contested or speculative; don't present any controversial reading of James's live/dead distinction as the consensus.
