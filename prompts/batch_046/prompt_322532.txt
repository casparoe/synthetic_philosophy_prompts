I'm taking a seminar on the philosophy of mathematics, where we've been tracing Frege's logicism and his realist commitment to abstract objects, especially in contrast to the nominalist programs—Quine-Putnam indispensability arguments and Field-style fictionalism—that crystallized only after his death. I want to work through how Frege might have situated himself not merely on the familiar platonism-versus-nominalism axis, but specifically on whether genuinely mind-independent abstract objects can be accessed through derivations that are epistemically opaque to human cognition.

If Frege were alive today and confronted both with modern nominalist challenges and with this phenomenon of machine-mediated verification, how would his core commitments—particularly the context principle, the objectivity of thought, and his rigorous anti-psychologism—shape his response? Or would he deny them full justificatory status on the grounds that genuine mathematical knowledge requires direct cognitive access to the thought being expressed? I'd like a conceptually precise reconstruction—without appeals to specific bibliographic citations—of where Frege's doctrines would likely land, with particular attention to whether cognitive surveyability is for him an essential epistemic condition on mathematical objectivity or merely a contingent practical limitation.
