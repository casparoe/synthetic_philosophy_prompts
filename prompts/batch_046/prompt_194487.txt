I'm trying to sort out my gut feelings on when it's okay to take a bet with an undefined expected value, going case by case. Classic St. Petersburg: I'd pay up to about thirty bucks, definatly, even though expectation diverges. The Pasadena variant — where stopping probabilities depend on a parameter that itself has no mean — I'd still pay maybe fifteen, but it feel's shakier, like the undefinedness is "deeper" somehow. Then there's a game where payoffs are weighted by factorial decay except the weights are scrambled in a non-computable order so the sum doesn't converge at all; I'd pay five dollars, barely. But a deterministic "bet" where you win if the nth digit of e is prime: expectation is undefined not because of randomness but because there's no probabilistic model at all. I wouldn't touch that, even if entry was free. Also, if both players face undefined expectation but losses are hard-capped at fifty bucks, I'm okay entering; if only my side is capped and theirs isn't, I'm out. Is there one principle behind all this, or am I being inconsistent? What's it predict for the case I'm stuck on — a bet that's undefined under the prior I actually hold, but perfectly well-behaved under a prior that's equally rational but not mine? And if you have to throw out one of my verdicts to keep the principle simple, which one should go?
