I am currently in an advanced metaphysics and philosophy of physics course, and our instructor recently presented a summary of what she called the Rietdijk–Putnam argument against presentism. According to her account, special relativity demonstrates that simultaneity is frame-relative, which entails that there is no unique, globally extended plane of simultaneity—no single three-dimensional slice that can serve as the universal present. From this she infers that presentism, understood as the claim that only entities existing in the present are real, necessarily depends on an absolute, frame-independent simultaneity relation that Einstein's theory rejects.

She further argued that attempts to rescue presentism by treating now as indexical, analogous to here, collapse under scrutiny because indexicals still presuppose a unique speaker's location, whereas physics regards every inertial frame as equally valid; consequently, she concluded that presentism is not merely metaphysically implausible but physically incoherent, leaving eternalism—the block-universe view that past, present, and future events exist tenselessly—as the only position compatible with relativistic physics. She also dismissed the idea of frame-relative presentism, in which presentness might be assigned relative to individual worldlines or local foliations, as abandoning the core presentist commitment to a single shared ontology of existence.

This summary strikes me as largely correct in its physics but possibly too swift in its metaphysical conclusions, especially because I recently encountered a distinction—raised in a seminar discussion—between global presentism, which requires one universal present, and what was termed foliation-independent presentism, which allows presentness to be instantiated along different spacelike hypersurfaces without privileging one globally. I worry that the instructor may be conflating the denial of an absolute global now with a direct proof that all events must exist equally, and that her rejection of the indexical strategy treats frame-relative perspectives as metaphysically interchangeable in a way a presentist might deny. Could you provide a careful audit of her summary?
