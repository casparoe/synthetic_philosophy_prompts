Write a 75-minute exam essay question for an upper-level undergraduate ethics final, together with a model answer and a marking scheme, on whether genuine moral dilemmas are possible. Frame the question so that it cannot be answered by summarizing Williams, Sinnott-Armstrong, or the standard epistemic/ontological taxonomy. Instead, center the prompt on the distinction between structurally symmetric conflicts—where competing obligations derive from the same moral source—and normative surplus, the unavoidable moral cost that remains even after the agent has satisfied every applicable duty and is neither guilt nor mere regret. Ask students to defend or reject the claim that only structurally symmetric conflicts count as genuine dilemmas, and to assess whether the phenomenon of moral remainder proves that obligations genuinely conflict or only that correct choice still entails irreducible loss. Provide a model answer of about 450 words, followed by a marking scheme built around five specific argumentative moves (each assigned marks, totaling 20 points) with a one-line note on the characteristic mistake that loses each set of marks. Within the model answer, include a brief reflexive paragraph stating precisely which argument—such as a successful reduction of all apparent conflicts to epistemic uncertainty or to incommensurable but non-conflicting reasons—would overturn the verdict, and identifying the weakest inferential step in the reasoning. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not invoke obscure named theories that depend on specialized literature. Keep the entire response under 800 words.
