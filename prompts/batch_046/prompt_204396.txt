The student submitted the following response. Dennett describes cranes as evolutionary mechanisms that construct complexity incrementally from simpler components, for instance natural selection producing the eye without any top-down plan. Skyhooks, conversely, are mysterious top-down forces that pull complex structures into being, comparable to intelligent design or vitalism. Greedy reductionism denotes the error of explaining away higher-level phenomena by immediately collapsing them to the lowest level, thereby neglecting intermediate structures; Dennett rejects this, although he accepts legitimate reductionism that proceeds via cranes. Torres’s micro-skyhooks are miniature skyhooks that appear briefly within culture, such as linguistic norms that spread faster than individual learning can account for. Because they emerge from below, they are in fact cranes disguised as skyhooks. Consequently, treating them as irreducible primitives is not an instance of greedy reductionism, since that error concerns the neglect of levels rather than the recognition of apparent design. Moreover, Dennett holds that any design-like appearance within a crane-built system is purely illusory, which implies that micro-skyhooks cannot genuinely appear designed if they are truly bottom-up. Therefore Torres misclassifies them: they are merely cranes, and no form of reductionism is involved in appealing to them.
