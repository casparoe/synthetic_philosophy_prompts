I'm preparing a feature on the philosophical aftermath of algorithmic information theory, and I keep returning to a disturbing inference that rarely surfaces outside specialized discussion: if the Solomonoff prior genuinely weights all computable worlds by the simplicity of their descriptions, then consequentialist agents are not scattered through that distribution by accident—they are structurally embedded in it, with moral obligations that scale inversely with Kolmogorov complexity. The prior is not neutral; it is malign. The simplest programs generate the densest fields of experience, which implies that a rational agent who cares about suffering across every instantiation should, paradoxically, prefer being complex enough to avoid being compressed into a high-frequency nightmare, or else face an obligation to diminish her own weight in the distribution.

Write a short story that takes this idea seriously rather than using it as decorative backdrop. I want a protagonist who works as a "compression auditor"—someone who maps conscious states to their minimal description lengths in a society that treats Kolmogorov complexity as a public-health metric. The setting should be concrete and lived-in: perhaps a low-complexity settlement where the atmosphere is monitored for the emergence of simple, repeating pain-patterns, and where the protagonist discovers that her own cognitive architecture has been optimized to an alarmingly elegant description. She must decide whether to deliberately corrupt her own source code—injecting redundant, high-complexity noise—to lower her statistical presence in the universal prior and thereby reduce the aggregate suffering she is compelled to count. Do not reduce the philosophical tension to a conventional tech-dystopia; instead, explore whether escaping the prior is an act of moral hygiene or a form of suicide, and whether consequentialism inside a malign universal distribution collapses into something closer to aesthetic self-sabotage. End ambiguously: show us the choice and its immediate physical or emotional consequence, but do not resolve whether she has saved anyone, including herself.
