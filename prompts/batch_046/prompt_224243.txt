okay so me and my roommate just had this exhausting arguement about zhuangzis butterfly dream that went on for like two hours and neither of us would back down, mostly bc we kept replaying this debate between jenna and mark from our reading group. jennas position was basically that the butterfly story is straight-up dream skepticism — zhuangzi wakes up and literally cant tell if hes zhuang zhou who dreamed he was a butterfly or the butterfly dreaming its him, so how do we know were not dreaming right now? she said its the same epistemic move descartes makes, just older, and the problem is we dont have any criterion to privilege waking experience over dream experience.

mark said thats a western misreading. he argued the point isnt skepticism at all but transformation — the idea of hua that things flow into each other, so asking which one is real is like asking whether the caterpillar or the butterfly is the true form. he said zhuangzis not-knowing is ontological, not epistemic, and that the story undermines the fixed self that could even ask the skeptical question.

jenna pushed back saying even if identity is fluid theres still a knowledge problem. she brought up the difference between local underdetermination and global skepticism, and said zhuangzi is doing the global version because he never gives waking life any special evidential status. if the two states are phenomenally indistinguishable, and one lacks coherence while the other has it, but that coherence is only checkable from within the state, then youve got epistemic parity.

mark answered with what he called an affective resolution — zhuangzi doesnt wake up anxious like descartes, he wakes up joyful about transformation. so the right response isnt to hunt for foundations but to embrace the flow. jenna said thats just therapeutic dodging, not philosophy. if im genuinely worried im dreaming right now, being told to feel joy about transformation doesnt answer the worry, it changes the subject.

mark replied that the worry itself smuggles in a fixed self that needs protection, which is exactly the premise the story denies. so the skeptical reading only works if you import the very selfhood zhuangzi is trying to dissolve.

whos right about what? is jennas epistemic framing actually supported by the text, or is mark right that shes importing cartesian assumptions? and does his affective and ontological move actually dissolve the skepticism or just sidestep it? also what are they both missing about whether phenomenal similarity between waking and dreaming actually forces epistemic parity, or whether coherence is state-relative in a way that breaks the symmetry? my roommate thinks im wrong about the coherence point and i need a real verdict by tomorrow.
