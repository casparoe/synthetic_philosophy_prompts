I have been trying to work out my own position on scientific realism in the wake of the pessimistic meta-induction, and I should like to know whether the following commitments form a coherent package or whether they pull in incompatible directions. First, I accept a strong version of the pessimistic induction: where a mature, empirically successful theory is eventually replaced by one with a radically different ontology, we have good inductive grounds to doubt that the predecessor's entities genuinely existed, even if its predictions were excellent. Second, I nevertheless maintain that we ought to be realists — in a full-blooded ontological sense, not merely structural — about quantum field theory, precisely because I take its mathematical apparatus to be preserved not through wholesale replacement but through successive embedding in effective field theories, which suggests that its ontology is not destined for the same fate as, say, the luminiferous ether. Third, I think Worrall-style structural realism correctly identifies what survives theory change, but I interpret this epistemically rather than ontologically: we are justified in retaining belief in the preserved mathematical structures because they exhibit predictive robustness, not because they mirror the true ontological structure of the world. Fourth, I hold that whenever an ontological commitment is genuinely carried over intact — rather than being reconstructed or approximated — into a successor theory, this is actually evidence that the commitment was never genuinely part of the scientific content of the original theory, but rather an inert metaphysical appendage. Fifth, I reject the standard no-miracles argument as circular, though I think a restricted variant — applying only to theories that are embedded in, rather than overturned by, their successors — can do legitimate justificatory work.

My concern is that these positions may not sit comfortably together. On the one hand, (1) and (2) seem to be in tension unless my distinction between replacement and embedding does all the work I need it to do; on the other hand, (3) and (4) appear to conflict over whether preserved structure licenses any ontological belief at all, and (2) seems to depend on just the kind of ontological retention that (4) treats with suspicion. At the same time, there may be an apparent contradiction between my rejection of the no-miracles argument in (5) and my willingness to invoke embedding-based reasoning in (2) that I am not seeing clearly. Could you audit these commitments for me? I am particularly interested in knowing which tension is the most severe, which pairs are only superficially at odds, and what the cheapest revision would be — whether I should weaken my ontological commitment to quantum field theory, abandon the epistemic interpretation of structural realism, or reconceive the embedding criterion in (1). I do not know which of these I would be most reluctant to surrender, so please order the conflicts by severity and indicate, for each, what exactly has to give.
