I am preparing the interview outline for a philosophy podcast series on theories of affect, and next week's guest is an unnamed philosopher who defends a specific hybrid view of emotion she designates the normative-frame account. On this view, emotional states are neither propositional judgments in the manner defended by classic cognitivists, nor sensory perceptions of value along the lines of Prinz's embodied appraisal model, nor reducible to the bodily feelings emphasized by James-Lange theorists; instead, they are dispositional attentional orientations—normatively structured frames—that present a situation as demanding a particular practical response, without being reducible to belief content, perceptual representation, or bare affective phenomenology. Draft ten to twelve interview questions that go well beyond having her restate this taxonomy. I want at least one question pressing how her account handles intentionality directed at temporally or spatially distant objects, for instance grief about a future loss that may never occur or anxiety about ecological conditions in the next century, at least one on whether these frames can be mistaken in a robust sense rather than merely inappropriate or miscalibrated, at least one that asks her to distinguish her position precisely from Martha Nussbaum's eudaimonist judgment theory—specifically regarding whether an emotional frame necessarily involves a conception of the subject's own flourishing or well-being—and at least one on what empirical result in affective neuroscience or in research on emotional granularity she would regard as genuinely damaging rather than merely requiring local adjustment. For each of these four, include a brief, targeted follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Keep all questions free of requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations.
