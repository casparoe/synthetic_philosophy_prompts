I want a consistency audit on the following package of views about consciousness, which I have reached through a mix of strong illusionist commitments and some more specific claims about temporal integration. Here is the profile:

(1) Consciousness is a user-illusion in the strong ontological sense: phenomenal properties do not exist, and the brain constructs only a virtual agent that treats itself as the subject of experience.
(2) The illusion is functional rather than epistemic—there is no misrepresentation of real phenomenal qualities, only a representational architecture that includes a fictional "I" indispensable for action-selection.
(3) Zombie worlds are not merely conceivable but metaphysically possible: a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me could lack consciousness entirely if the illusion-mechanism failed to activate, despite identical physics.
(4) First-person reports are largely reliable, not because they track an inner object, but because the self-model is structurally transparent—its outputs are constrained by the very architecture that generates the illusion.
(5) Generating the illusion requires a specific biological temporal-binding window—roughly 200 to 300 ms of cross-modal integration that yields a unified "now"—and I hold that no system lacking this particular biological mechanism could produce consciousness, even if it were functionally isomorphic in every other respect.

Please audit this for joint tenability. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and what is the least costly revision for each real incompatibility? Rank the tensions by severity, specify exactly which commitment must be revised or abandoned to resolve each, and say what that revision costs the overall view. Do not just flag every possible friction—discriminate between deep incompatibilities and surface ones. I am not asking which thesis is true, but whether this particular package is jointly tenable.
