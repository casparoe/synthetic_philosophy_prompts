I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, though I have recently been reading around ethics in my spare time. I am writing to ask for advice on a practical dilemma that has begun to feel conceptually puzzling, and I would be grateful if you could analyse it using philosophical tools rather than offering generic practical guidance.

I participate in a voluntary mutual-aid network for open-source infrastructure, where members pool technical and financial resources to keep public-interest software running. The network operates informally on the principle that those with greater capacity should contribute more, but there is an unspoken expectation that individuals with modest savings should absorb disproportionate costs to prevent outages affecting thousands of users. I currently hold savings that could cover a critical funding gap for roughly six months, but liquidating them would exhaust my emergency fund and compel me to postpone necessary dental surgery. Several other members in comparable financial positions have refused to contribute, noting that no single donation is decisive and that the collective benefit is too diffuse to justify severe personal sacrifice. I feel that withholding my resources allows harm that I could easily prevent; simultaneously, I feel resentful that the burden falls unevenly, and that expecting me to jeopardise my health and financial security for a marginal, imperceptible improvement to strangers crosses some fundamental boundary regarding what can be demanded of one person. Is my reluctance a failure of moral reasoning, or does it point to a legitimate philosophical limit?

Specifically, I am interested in whether the sheer scale of aggregate benefit can generate obligations that override severe personal cost, particularly in contexts where others who could share the burden refuse to do so. I also wonder whether there is a meaningful distinction between actively causing harm and merely failing to prevent it when one's contribution is not individually decisive, and whether fairness in distributing burdens has any independent moral weight here. Please reply in British English.
