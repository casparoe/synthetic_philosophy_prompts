They got stuck on whether conceivability is a decent guide to metaphysical possibility, and I want to know who's actually holding water.

Maya opened with the classic zombie move: if you can conceive a physical duplicate of me with zero inner experience, and there's no hidden contradiction in teh description, then it's metaphysically possible consciousness isn't physical. Leo said that's way too quick — conceivability is unreliable for "thick" properties tied to functional roles. He used water/XYZ: we think we conceive water lacking H2O, but we're really just conceiving a different chemical that plays the water-role. So zombie conceivability probably smuggles in hidden structural differences.

Maya came back with a line she called the "reverse leakage problem." If phenomenal properties are ones we know by direct acquaintance, then conceiving their absence isn't like conceiving absence of a color — it's trying to do negative phenomenology from the inside, which might be conceptually incoherent. So Leo can't have it both ways: either direct acquaintance makes phenomenal conceivability special (in which case absence-conceiving is broken) or it's not special (in which case zombies might be genuinely conceivable).

Leo answered that this actually kills Maya's own argument, not his. If absence-conceiving is broken, then the zombie scenario was never genuinely conceived in the first place — so the argument from conceivability to possibility never gets off the ground. He then proposed "modal anchoring" — the idea that a scenario counts as genuinely possible only if its description doesn't secretly depend on fixing our actual world as a reference point. Zombies, he said, fail this because describing them requires anchoring consciousness in the actual world and then subtracting it, which is like describing a triangle with four sides.

Maya said modal anchoring just relabels epistemic humility: of course our descriptions are anchored, but that doesn't show the anchored-away scenario is impossible, only that we can't verify it from here. She also pointed out that Leo's water/XYZ analogy breaks down because phenomenal properties aren't kind-properties — they don't have hidden essences to leak in.

They went back and forth on whether there's a principled distinction between domains where conceivability works and where it fails, and whether any such distinction collapses into "where we're more scared of being wrong." I couldn't tell if they were actually disagreeing about possibility or just about what counts as a clean mental model.
