In my political philosophy seminar we are discussing institutional authority, and two classmates got into an argument about whether Pascal’s pairing of justice and force applies beyond the sovereign state. I am reporting this as a bystander; please adjudicate the exchange directly rather than advising me personally.

The first student said Pascal’s claim—that justice without force is powerless and force without justice is tyrannical—should be read structurally. Any institution that issues binding rules needs both normative legitimacy and an enforcement mechanism, so the pairing applies directly to university honor codes, platform terms of service, and professional licensing boards.

The second student replied that this overgeneralizes. Pascal was analyzing sovereign power specifically, and “force” in his context means the state’s monopoly on legitimate violence. A university honor code relies on social sanction and expulsion, not on coercive violence, so the structural pairing does not map onto it, and invoking Pascal here is a category mistake.

The first student countered that Pascal’s “force” can be interpreted as any effective sanction—deplatforming, expulsion, license revocation—without which rules are indeed powerless. If we restrict “force” to physical violence, then Pascal’s claim becomes trivially false for every non-state institution, which cannot be right.

The second student then said that if you broaden “force” to include any social pressure, the pairing collapses into tautology: bad rules with bad enforcement are bad. The critical bite of Pascal’s observation—distinguishing legitimate sovereignty from tyranny—depends on “force” being the specific coercive power of the state, not generic social cost.

Please go through the four moves and rate each one—clearly mistaken, partly right but flawed, mostly right, or clearly right—with a brief justification. Then say which student is closer to correct overall, and identify what both are missing about how Pascal’s framework might apply to distributed or non-coercive institutional authority. Keep your response to about 350 words.
