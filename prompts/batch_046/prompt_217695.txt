Write an essay assessing whether geometric rationality — understood as log-utility maximization or Kelly-optimal betting over belief states — survives reflexive application to one's own epistemic calibration. The canonical defense of Kelly betting presumes an external resolution mechanism and a bettor whose rationality is not itself part of the wager; yet if the "wealth" at stake is the accuracy of the predictive framework that sets the bet sizes, a feedback loop emerges. High-confidence wagers on one's own calibration risk generating unbounded leverage, while refusal to wager produces epistemic paralysis. Examine the argument that this reflexivity is fatal and forces geometric rationality to be restricted to object-level predictions, against the counter-argument that a hierarchical meta-belief architecture — in which first-order Kelly bets are governed by second-order calibration beliefs that are not themselves subject to Kelly optimization — can preserve coherence without arbitrary domain restrictions. Reach and justify a definite conclusion: either that geometric rationality must be quarantined from reflexive domains, or that the hierarchical resolution succeeds and reflexive Kelly betting remains coherent.
