For a graduate seminar on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, set out the desiderata that an adequate theory of propositional unity must satisfy, together with the motivating case or difficulty behind each. In particular, distinguish the non-negotiable conditions — those any resolution of Bradley's regress must preserve if it is not to collapse into absolute monism or to dissolve propositions into unstructured aggregates — from the negotiable preferences of particular camps (for example, whether relations must be external, whether negative propositions require distinct negative relations, whether the unifying element can be identified with one of the relata). You must include a specific, non-standard condition: the theory must handle what we might call bootstrapped unity, where the relational binder is not itself one of the terms it binds, yet its own nature or efficacy depends on the unified whole it produces. State whether this condition is jointly satisfiable with the standard realist demand that relations be ontologically prior to the propositions they structure, and if not, identify which desideratum should yield. Where subsets of your list appear jointly unsatisfiable given Bradley's original argument or its modern refinements, say so explicitly, since identifying the tension is the central task. Keep the emphasis on the criteria and their justifications rather than on scoring existing theories; I will do that comparison in the seminar.
