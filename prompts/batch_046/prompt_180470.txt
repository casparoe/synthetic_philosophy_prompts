Subsequently, well over a hundred evolutionary biologists published a rebuttal defending the gene-centered, kin-selection account. I have no formal biology training, but I've been trying to understand this through the units-of-selection debate: whether genes, individual organisms, or groups are the true targets of selection.

Please analyze the episode through that lens. First, apply the framework directly to the colonies being studied: what does the gene-level account say is actually competing and reproducing differentially—the gene copies, the individual workers, or the colony as a whole? And what does the group-level account say? Which features of eusocial life—sterile workers, cooperative brood care, colony-level reproduction—does each view explain well, and which does it distort or miss?

Where is the units-of-selection theory genuinely silent—does it offer any verdict on how scientists ought to have responded, or on whether mathematical equivalence between models should count as agreement?
