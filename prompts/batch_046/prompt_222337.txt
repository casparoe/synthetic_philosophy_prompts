As a software engineer without formal philosophy training, I've been trying to clarify my own view on consciousness and artificial systems, and I want a consistency check before I present it to a discussion group. Here is where I've landed:

(1) I am a physicalist: consciousness is not a non-physical substance but arises from organized physical or functional processes.
(2) I think global workspace theory gives the right account of access consciousness—information becomes globally available to reasoning, memory, and reporting subsystems.
(3) I think phenomenal consciousness (subjective experience, what it is like) is conceptually and possibly empirically distinct from access consciousness; the two can come apart.
(4) I think there is nothing it is like to be a thermostat or a basic PID controller, because such systems lack integrated representational architecture.
(5) I think a hypothetical distributed architecture—let's call it recursive shadow broadcasting, where local modules maintain self-referential representations that influence behavior but never enter a unified global workspace—could support phenomenal consciousness without access consciousness.
(6) I think phenomenal consciousness is never fully epiphenomenal or dark: if present, it must leave some functional trace and be reportable in principle by at least one subsystem.
(7) I think the explanatory gap between physical processes and subjective experience reflects a conceptual limitation in how our cognition represents its own states, not an ontological divide.
(8) I think phenomenal consciousness requires a unified point of view or a single subject of experience; there cannot be phenomenal consciousness without some form of integration into one perspective.

Please audit this set. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but hang together on closer inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each tension, say what has to give—whether one commitment should be dropped, qualified, or reinterpreted—and identify the cheapest fix. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems overall. Be specific about whether the conflict is between the standard motivations for these views or between a principle and a particular case judgment I have endorsed.
