Read the 1998 Parent Trap (the Lindsay Lohan twins remake) through Black's spheres and Leibniz's law of the identity of indescernibles. Annie and Hallie start out as qualitative duplicates—same face, same genes—seperated only by relational properties like which camp they're at and which parent they live with. Black's two spheres in an otherwise empty universe share every intrinsic feature yet are numerically two, and philosophers have argued since whether purely relational differences can ground identity. Does the film treat the twins as genuine Black-style duplicates, or does it smuggle in hidden intrinsic differences—taste in food, personality quirks—to save Leibniz? I'm using this for a philosophy of identity seminar, so I want you to take both sides seriously: look at the hair-cutting scene and the camp swap as test cases, and say whether the movie accidentally dramatizes weak discernibility rather than proving the twins are always distinct individuals. Don't just summarize; tell me where the film resists the philosophical reading and whether it adds something argument alone can't show.
