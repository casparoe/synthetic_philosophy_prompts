Write an essay on the controversy over what Avicenna's flying man argument actually establishes. One standard interpretation holds that the suspended-sensation scenario proves the soul is an immaterial substance ontologically independent of the body; a stricter interpretation holds that it demonstrates only a bare, non-sensory self-awareness that could in principle be instantiated in a non-substantial, purely functional or informational system. Weigh these readings against each other, paying particular attention to whether the argument's conclusion depends on tacit Cartesian assumptions about substance rather than on the premises of suspended embodiment alone. Reach and justify a definite conclusion about whether the flying man survives as an argument for substance dualism once those assumptions are stripped away, or whether it collapses into a weaker claim about first-person indexical certainty that is compatible with physicalism. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
