Design a thought experiment that isolates the separateness-of-persons objection to aggregate welfare maximization from the more familiar distributive or rights-based objections. The scenario should meet the following specifications: (a) it involves a single, clearly beneficial institutional restructuring—such as a university department merger or a public-health triage protocol—not a one-off action; (b) the restructuring improves total welfare not by redistributing goods but by substituting or merging individuals' life-plans, so that the gains come from treating persons as fungible carriers of utility rather than as distinct sources of value; (c) no particular individual is made worse off ex ante, and no specific right is violated, yet the change should still trigger the intuition that persons are being used interchangeably; (d) keep the setting realistic and describe it in one paragraph, avoiding science fiction, demons, or perfect predictors; (e) after presenting the scenario, explain which structural feature generates the separateness intuition, why an act-utilitarian can endorse the restructuring without contradiction, and how a defender of the separateness objection would respond without appealing to distributive unfairness or to violated rights. Note which feature of the case does the work and what happens to the intuition if that feature is removed.
