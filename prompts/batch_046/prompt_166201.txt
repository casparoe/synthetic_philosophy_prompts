Please argue for the position that epistemic modesty, as standardly formulated in the disagreement literature, systematically overweights the outside view in domains characterized by what we may call procedural uniqueness—situations in which an agent’s belief-forming process includes reasoning steps whose compositional structure is non-observable and non-replicable from a third-person perspective, and therefore invisible to the reference class that supplies the outside view. Your argument should sharply distinguish between (i) cases in which the outside view tracks genuine epistemic peerhood with shared evidential access and similar reasoning architectures, and (ii) cases in which the reference class aggregates over agents who lack access to the generative trace of the reasoning in question, treating heterogenous cognitive processes as homogeneous. Show that collapsing these categories for the purposes of conciliation erases an informational asymmetry that is structural rather than contingent, and defend the claim that in category (ii) the inside view should carry lexical priority—not out of hubris, but because first-person access to the procedural architecture of one’s own reasoning constitutes a legitimate epistemic advantage that the outside view, by its nature, cannot correct. Address the standard objection that this licenses self-serving exemptions from modesty, and explain why procedural uniqueness differs from merely possessing private evidence or being in a unique evidential position. Conclude by considering whether this revision undermines the normative force of epistemic modesty altogether, or instead requires us to reconceive modesty as domain-sensitive rather than uniformly conciliationist.
