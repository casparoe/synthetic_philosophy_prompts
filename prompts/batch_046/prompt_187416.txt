next tuesday i'm presenting at our metaphysics-cognitive science joint seminar a paper i'm tentatively calling "Structural Antidotes and the Collapse of the Mask-Fink Boundary." the core claim is that for distributed cognitive dispositions — think of a research team's collective ability to replicate a complex assay — the classic distinction between a fink (an internal interrupter, like the wire that breaks a vase's fragility) and a mask (an external blocker, like the antidote that neutralizes poison) stops working because the interfering factor is neither inside the bearer nor outside it but is constitutive of the institutional conditions that make the disposition exist at all. i'm arguing we need a third category, the structural antidote, where the block is embedded in the very social-technical scaffolding that defines what the team can do. the audience is tricky: half are metaphysicians who've taught the standard fragility and poison cases for years and are allergic to multiplying entities, and half are situated-cognition researchers who think talk of "dispositions" is just bad predictive modeling dressed up in ontology. they care about whether this helps them understand actual lab failures, not about saving the ontology.
