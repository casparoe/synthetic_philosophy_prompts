At a seminar on classical political economy, two scholars argued about Adam Smith's opposition to the British Empire. (1) The first claimed it was fundamentally an accounting judgment: colonies required costly naval defense and administration, while the Navigation Acts granted mercantile monopolies that enriched a few at the expense of British consumers; on this view, Smith's recommendation that Britain grant independence to the American colonies was simply advice to stop running a fiscal deficit, and he would have endorsed empire had the net balance turned positive. (2) The second objected that this ignores the constitutional heart of Smith's critique: imperial expansion breeds standing armies, public debt, and executive overreach, all of which erode representative government at home; for her, Smith's anti-imperialism is inseparable from his fear that empire transforms a commercial republic into a militarized state, regardless of any balance-sheet calculation. (3) The first replied that this overreads Smith, since he explicitly praised the economic dynamism colonies generated—new markets for manufactures and sources of raw materials—showing he recognized substantial benefits, which means his rejection can only be explained by costs exceeding those benefits. (4) The second answered that Smith carefully distinguished gains arising from free exchange from those secured through coercive monopoly: the division of labor might indeed expand with colonial markets, but only through an artificial exclusive-company structure that violates justice and distorts prices, so the economic benefits themselves are tainted by the political corruption required to maintain them. They asked me to referee. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Then state whether the fiscal-economic or the constitutional-political reading better captures Smith's overall position, and identify anything both scholars overlook regarding his ambivalence about the role of colonial markets in advancing the division of labor. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then present the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
