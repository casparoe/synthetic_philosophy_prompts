I am a high school student coordinating the final assessment for an advanced secondary-level philosophy seminar focused on ethical theory, and I need a single essay question—together with a model answer and marking scheme—on negative utilitarianism and suffering-focused ethics. The question must be answerable in 60 minutes at a length of roughly 600 words, and it should target students who have already grasped the basic distinction between negative and classical utilitarianism. Rather than allowing a summary of that distinction, the prompt should inject a specific conceptual tension: whether a lexical form of negative utilitarianism, which assigns absolute priority to the reduction of suffering, collapses into a prioritarian framework when applied to hypothetical populations in which suffering is not extreme but is distributed across an extraordinarily large number of sentient beings. Please provide the exam question, a model answer of approximately 600 words, and a marking scheme that distributes 20 marks across four required argumentative moves—(1) a precise formulation of lexical negative utilitarianism, (2) an analysis of why widespread mild suffering threatens lexical priority, (3) an evaluation of whether this collapse undermines the core motivation for suffering-focused ethics, and (4) a reasoned conclusion about whether the theory should be revised or retained. For each move, include a one-line note on the typical misconception that should cost marks, especially the error of assuming that negative utilitarianism necessarily entails anti-natalism for all sentient life regardless of the actual quality of lives at stake. The framing should remain formal and precise, appropriate for an advanced high-school philosophy course, and should avoid requesting citations, bibliographies, or references to obscure theoretical texts that students could not be expected to recall without access to sources.
