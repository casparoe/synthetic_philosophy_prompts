Question: Explain why finkish and masked cases both cause trouble for the simple 'if S then M' view of dispositions. Be clear about which way each pulls — too permissive or too restrictive — and use your own examples. Don't just list names; walk through the logic.

Student answer:
The conditional analysis says an object is disposed to M in conditions S iff it would M if S happened. Finks break this by making the conditional true even when there's no real disposition. Take the dead-wire-with-fink case: if touched, a machine makes it live and shocks you. So 'if touched then shock' holds, but the wire isn't live and doesn't actually have the shock-disposition. That makes the analysis too permissive.

Masks pull the other direction. Picture a sugar cube dipped in wax. Drop it in water and nothing happens — the wax blocks it — so 'if in water then dissolves' is false. Still, we'd say it's soluble; it's just masked. So the analysis is too restrictive here.

The key difference is that a fink introduces a causal pathway that wasn't there, while a mask blocks one that was. To fix both, we usually appeal to the intrinsic categorical base: the sugar still has its molecular structure, so the disposition is intact, whereas the wire lacks any live-current base, so there's nothing to ground the shock-disposition.

There's also a connection to multi-track dispositions. The sugar's solubility isn't gone; it's just that in waxy water it expresses a different track (staying intact). So masking isn't about losing and regaining the disposition — it's about the same multi-track disposition expressing one path instead of another. That's why stripping the wax doesn't 'restore' solubility; it just removes the blocker so the water-track can run.

Since both cases are fixed by checking the intrinsic categorical base — present for sugar, absent for wire — they aren't really pulling in opposite directions after all. They're just two ways of showing that the conditional analysis ignores what's going on inside the object. So the distinction between too-permissive and too-restrictive collapses once you do the metaphysics right.
