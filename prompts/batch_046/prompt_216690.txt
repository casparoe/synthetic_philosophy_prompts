I have no formal background in philosophy, but I have been thinking independently about how people justify the commitments they maintain over many years—not merely beliefs, but actual practices they continue, such as keeping up a manual craft or a family tradition that offers no obvious reward or endpoint. Every reason offered for continuing seems to depend on another reason, and I understand there are three broad answers to where that chain ends: one view holds that justification must bottom out in something basic that requires no further support; another holds that reasons support one another in a web or circle; and a third holds that the chain never ends but continues indefinitely. Rather than hearing these views talk past each other in the abstract, I want to see what they can genuinely agree and disagree on when applied to a concrete case.

Please draft the joint statement that a representative of each of these three positions—the bottom-out view, the mutual-support view, and the endless-chain view—could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The collaboration should focus specifically on justifying sustained practical commitments: the decision to maintain a manual or traditional practice (for instance, analog mapping, handwritten record-keeping, or craft preservation) in conditions where modern automated alternatives are fully available and the practice confers no competitive advantage. I want the document to include:

1. A numbered section of claims all three representatives accept, both about what justification demands in this practical domain and about the nature of such commitments themselves.
2. A disagreements table setting out the precise formulations of where they still differ, with each pair addressed separately (bottom-out vs. mutual-support, bottom-out vs. endless-chain, mutual-support vs. endless-chain), and each disagreement phrased in wording that the side holding it would accept.
3. The cruxes—the specific sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one representative.
4. For each crux, a concrete case or argument that all three agree is relevant, together with each position's advance prediction about what that case shows.

Format the output as the agreed-claims section, then the table, then the cruxes section with predictions. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke specific philosophers, texts, or obscure theoretical apparatus I would not have encountered; keep the discussion to the three positions and the practical case described above.
