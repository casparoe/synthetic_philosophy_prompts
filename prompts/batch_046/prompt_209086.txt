You are a professional philosopher of mind and computation with expertise in causal explanation, downward causation, and the metaphysics of computation. Before drafting, take a moment to brainstorm the distinctions that must remain sharp: correlation versus causation, causal closure of the physical substrate, the difference between a symbol token and its physical realization, and what it would genuinely mean for a symbol to push its substrate rather than merely ride along with it.

Your task is to draft the joint statement that a committed Careenist and a committed Inertist could both sign after a rigorous adversarial collaboration. For this exercise, treat the following precisely defined hypothetical framework as given: computational symbols are coupled to a physical substrate through localized coupling nodes called simmballs, and the proposed causal bridge through which symbolic states might exert downward influence is termed the careenium. The Careenist camp holds that symbols possess genuine downward causal efficacy mediated by careenium dynamics acting through simmballs; the Inertist camp holds that simmballs are purely correlational tracking devices, that substrate dynamics are causally closed, and that symbols therefore have no causal push.

Please produce the collaboration document in the following parts.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims—statements both camps can endorse without reservation, including about the formal properties of symbol-substrate coupling, about what needs explaining (for instance, whether the predictive success of symbol-level models requires downward causation), and about what standard arguments regarding causal closure do and do not establish.

Second, a disagreements table with exactly these columns: Issue; Careenist Position (phrased so Careenists accept the wording); Inertist Position (phrased so Inertists accept the wording); The Crux; Agreed Case or Argument; Careenist Prediction; Inertist Prediction.

Third, within or alongside that table, identify the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. For each crux, specify one concrete hypothetical case or piece of reasoning that both sides agree bears on it, with each side’s prediction recorded in advance.

Fourth, examine whether any portion of the dispute is verbal—perhaps about what counts as pushing versus tracking, or about how to individuate a careenium event—and if so identify that portion explicitly and explain why it does not affect the substantive disagreement.

Fifth, conclude with a brief assessment of whether there is anything both sides agree would genuinely move the debate, or whether the residual disagreement is in principle unresolvable with current conceptual resources.

Format: present the document in polished prose with the table embedded. After the document, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with these exact fields: agreed_claims_summary (string), disagreement_categories (array of strings), cruxes (array of objects with keys label and agreed_test), verbal_dispute_portion (string or null), potential_debate_mover (string or null).

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke specific published papers or named authors beyond common-knowledge figures, and do not place any verbatim quotation marks around text drawn from real works. Think carefully about whether each disagreement is substantive or definitional, and phrase every claim with precision so that neither camp feels its view has been misrepresented.
