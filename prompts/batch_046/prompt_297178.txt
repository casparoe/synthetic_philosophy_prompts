The naturalism vs normativism thing in philosophy of medicine feels totally stuck. Boorse-type people say disease is just statistical deviation from species-typical function; normativists like Nordenfelt say it's about failing to reach vital goals. Everyone rolls out the same counterexamples — the elderly athlete with weird stats, the asymptomatic genetic conditon — and then nothing happens. Is this actually a substantive disagreement about what health is, or are they just answering different questions under the word 'disease': one about biological classification, one about when medicine should intervene? I have a hunch the stall comes from both sides treating 'health' as a single concept when it might split into biological functioning and welfare-related flourishing, but maybe that's wrong and it's really just bedrock intuitions about whether nature sets norms. Diagnose it: verbal dispute, hidden empirical question, two questions masked as one, or something else? And be honest — if it's the split-concept thing, what distinction would actually move the debate? If it's bedrock intuitions, is there anything that could move it, or should we just accept the impasse?
