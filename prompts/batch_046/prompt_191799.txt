My research institute's autonomous-systems ethics board has evaluated five proposals defining low-impact or side-effect-free operation. Their record is as follows. They approved a warehouse-robot design that is penalized for altering temperature or lighting in rooms adjacent to its sorting task, on the ground that the impact measure was indexed to fixed, non-task environmental baselines. They rejected a medical diagnostic AI that blocked updates to its own model weights, ruling that self-modification falls within task-allowed dynamics and that preventing it caused functional failure. They approved, with conditions, a climate-modeling agent forbidden from altering atmospheric CO2 outside a narrow band but permitted to publish policy-relevant reports, holding that information emission is not a physical side effect within the measurable class. They rejected a gardening robot constrained never to displace soil particles outside a designated planting zone, because the metric was over-specified to physical displacement and missed the intended ecological outcome. And they approved a conversational agent barred from altering a user's belief states about topics outside the explicit query, reasoning that belief-state change is unobservable and therefore outside the domain of the measurable side-effect class.

The board now faces a proposal for a negotiation agent that mediates multi-party resource disputes. The agent's impact measure is defined as divergence from the counterfactual world-trajectory in which the agent is absent, but the comparison is restricted to direct sensory-motor interactions occurring inside the negotiation room; the agent may not update its own utility function, may not alter participants' mental states except through explicit verbal agreement, and may propose trade structures that, if adopted by the parties externally, would restructure global institutional incentives.

Predict whether the board will approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and identify the closest analog among the five precedents. Articulate at least two distinct implicit rules that jointly accommodate the first five verdicts but diverge on this case—for instance, one that restricts side effects to physically measurable direct causation, one that requires any valid impact measure to be articulable without a full prior specification of value, and one that treats counterfactual divergence as bounded by the task's natural scope. State which rule you think the board is actually running and why. Finally, specify the further hypothetical proposal that would best distinguish the remaining competing rules.
