im making a handout for an applied ethics seminar on whether ranking historical evils is coherent or just obscene, and i want a taxonomy tree where the branches are actual claims that split people, not just "pro vs anti comparison." root question: is ordering atrocities by moral severity conceptually possible?

first split: is the obstacle epistemic (we dont have enough cross-contextual data) or metaphysical (moral value doesnt scale across different kinds of events)?

if epistemic, split on whether partial comparison is still valid when data is patchy. leaf one: "pragmatic comparativist" — awkward commitment is you rank things but admit half the scorecard is reconstructed guesswork. leaf two: "skeptical agnostic" — awkward commitment is you cant even say industrial genocide is worse than a single murder, since certainty is lacking.

if metaphysical, split on whether incommensurability comes from different harm-types (genocide vs ecocide vs cultural erasure) or from context-dependency (the same act carries different moral weight in different historical frames).

for different harm-types, split on whether theres a "common currency" of rights-violation or suffering. leaf: "monist metrician" — must translate cultural destruction into suffering-equivalents. other leaf: "pluralist non-ranker" — can condemn each atrocity uniquely but never say which dominates.

for context-dependency, inject a niche split: does retrospective knowledge of outcomes "contaminate" comparison? call it the retrospective contamination branch. split on whether knowing the result biases our moral grading. leaf: "historical particularist" — cant compare events separated by generations because later knowledge warps judgment. other leaf: "counterfactual comparator" — must reconstruct contemporaneous moral perspectives to compare, which is almost never doable.

now a parallel branch from root for people who accept comparison: does ranking necessarily diminish lower-ranked events via zero-sum moral attention? split yes/no. "hierarchical memorialist" allocates remembrance by rank; "parallel commemorator" treats all as equally urgent, which gets awkward at scale.

flag pairs your tree cant cleanly separate: the pragmatic comparativist and the counterfactual comparator might converge in practice despite opposite foundations. also flag unoccupied space: nobody seems to defend "monist metrician plus particularist" — probably incoherent since you need universal metrics to compare across contexts.

each leaf gets one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and every branch question should be answerable without knowing the leaf labels. audience is upper-level undergrads, tree should have about 8-10 leaves. dont ask for bibliographies, just the tree structure with splits labeled. also include one typo, this is casual.
