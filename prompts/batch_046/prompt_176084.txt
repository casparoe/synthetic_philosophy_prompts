Let us suppose Leibniz is presented with a modern autonomous predictive system—one whose harmful outputs are determined not by any single human decision but by an interlocking pre-established harmony of training data, optimization weights, and statistical correlations across millions of prior cases. Drawing on his moral metaphysics—particularly his view that justice requires the rational ordering of charity among perceiving beings, and that evil arises from metaphysical limitation rather than positive malice—would he regard such a system as a perception-less monad lying outside moral responsibility? Or would his principle of sufficient reason compel him to trace every unjust result back to the rational creator who selected this particular algorithmic world as the best possible? Where, on his account, would moral accountability ultimately rest?
