Construct an argument defending the view that Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility fails not at the familiar point about an infinite regress of prior causes, but at a deeper structural assumption: the argument treats the demand that one be truly responsible for one's mental nature as requiring a diachronic, substance-like self that originates ex nihilo, and then concludes that no such origin is possible. Defend the contrary position that moral responsibility requires only what might be called functional self-constitution—a system's present capacity to recursively revise its own decision-weights in response to reasons, where the resulting choice-structure is sufficiently integrated with the system's past revisions to count as genuinely its own, despite being fully causally embedded. Specifically, argue that Strawson overgeneralizes from a metaphysical intuition about personal identity to a normative conclusion about accountability, and that an emergent autonomy model—on which an agent's current dispositions are authored by its own prior reason-responsive modifications rather than by an uncaused foundation—blocks the regress without invoking libertarian free will. In your response, state explicitly how confident you are in the overall conclusion and single out the specific premise or inference step about which you are least certain, explaining why that step relies more on a contested philosophical intuition than on an established argument.
