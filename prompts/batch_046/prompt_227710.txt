I work for a small post-conflict reintegration board in a town that went through low-level civil fighting a few years back. We process about 15-20 applications a month — not big war criminals, just local militia, informers, people who burned stuff. We can recommend "community reintegration" (not a legal pardon) so they can come back and work. I have 30 mins per file, I'm not a lawyer, and a psychologist plus a victim rep have to cosign if I flag anything.

We just adopted this weird hybrid framework: reintegration needs (1) specific disclosure of exactly what they did, not just "I was in the group," and (2) evidence they hit at least one individual choice point inside the collective violence — not just following orders start to finish. The idea is if they never exercised individual judgment, they can't stand in a real accountable relationship now. But we also check if that choice was coerced vs free, and whether remorse is specific to the harm, not just general sadness.

Can you turn this into a checklist I can actually run? Steps in order, where it stops with reject, where it stops with yes, where its just a value call I have to make and flag for cosign. Then apply it to three cases:

A) A guy admits he burned three houses, says he was terrified of being killed by thier commander if he refused, but can point to one moment he chose not to burn a fourth. Remorse is specific for the three.

B) A clerk who falsified lists leading to disappearances. Did it for months, always under direct threat to her kids, can't identify any single choice point — describes it as "just surviving." But disclosure is full and she wants to help locate graves.

C) A teenager in one raid where people got beaten, but he was drugged and remembers almost nothing. Wants to come back to care for his sick mother.

One should come out opposite of gut feeling. Also tell me where the checklist just breaks and I need to call the board together.
