I'm drafting a novel about a protaganist who's a therapist and starts questioning whether she actually knows her own mind. This led me to an argument that feels like it proves too much and I can't spot the exit. (1) If I try to introspect my belief that Paris is beautiful, I don't find some mental representation inside me — I just think about Paris. (2) That's the transparency thing: turning inward just bounces me back to the world the belief is about. (3) So knowing my own belief isn't direct access to a mental state, it's just an inferece from context, no different from how an outsider guesses it. (4) Same deal with emotions: when I check if I'm angry, I think about the insult, not some bare inner feeling-state. (5) So introspection never actually reveals mental states; it only reveals their objects. (6) Therefore there's no real first-person priviledge — I know my mind the same way you do. (7) But that can't be right, since I do know I'm in pain without checking my behavour. (8) So either transparency is false, or first-person authority is a myth. Where's the slip? I'd like to keep transparency for beliefs but save direct access for bodily feelings. Is that split defensible, or am I just patching a hole?
