I am leading a 75-minute seminar for second-year philosophy students who have studied basic Western epistemology—Gettier cases, justification conditions, and propositional knowledge—but have never encountered Indian philosophy. The session must cover the Nyāya theory of pramāṇas, yet I do not want it to become a taxonomy exercise. The conceptual core is the status of anupalabdhi (cognition of absence, or non-apprehension) and whether it qualifies as an independent pramāṇa or is reducible to perception coupled with inference. Crucially, this debate turns on Nyāya's realist ontology: abhāva (absence) is treated not as a privation but as one of the six padārthas (ontological categories), which means absence-cognition requires direct acquaintance with a real entity rather than a mere failure to detect presence.

Plan the explanatory sequence: the dependency order—what must be established about pramāṇa itself (causal generation of veridical cognition, distinct from post-hoc justification and from pramāṇa-ābhāsa, fallacious cognition) before the standard four can be introduced; then how to introduce the four without reducing them to modern "sources of belief"; then the precise point at which anupalabdhi becomes a live problem rather than an afterthought. Specify the single carrying example that should run through the session—likely the judgment that there is no pitcher on the floor—but indicate exactly which features it must possess (spatial locus, sensory accessibility, negative content) and which contrasting variant must be introduced later to expose the decomposition fallacy, for instance knowing the absence of a sound in a soundproof room or the absence of a hidden cause. Map the misconceptions these students will import from Western frameworks—treating absence-cognition as absence-of-evidence, treating the pramāṇa debate as taxonomic bookkeeping, or conflating pramāṇa with epistemic justification—and identify the precise moment in the sequence where each should be intercepted. Design one closing check that reveals whether a student understands the ontological-epistemological dependency (that the status of anupalabdhi hinges on whether abhāva is a real category) rather than whether they have memorized a list of five or six sources. Finally, state clearly what should be explicitly excluded and named as beyond scope—such as the Navya-Nyāya formalization of vyāpti conditions or the Buddhist Pramāṇa debates on apoha—and why leaving it out protects the dependency structure you have built.
