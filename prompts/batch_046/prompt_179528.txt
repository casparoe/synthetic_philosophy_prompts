I need to write the commencement adress for my small college next month, and I'm kinda stuck. The audience is split — about half are philosophy/polisci people who know Isaiah Berlin and Pettit, the other half are family members of nursing and engineering grads who don't. I want to talk about freedom, but not the usual "follow your dreams" garbage. Specifically, I want to use the three-way distinction between negative liberty (no one stops you), positive liberty (you have real capacity), and republican liberty — freedom as non-domination, where arbitrary power is the problem even if it never actually interferes. The structural hook should be the nursing grads going into jobs where they're technically off-duty but still on-call, subject to being pulled back by managers or scheduling algorithms. I want the speech to show why saying "well, no one's literally forcing them" misses the republican point, without sounding like I'm trashing their career choice or saying they're positively unfree just because they need income. The idea needs to actually constrain what I can say — for instance I can't just celebrate their "negative freedom" to choose shifts, and I can't collapse everything into a lack of resources. It needs to land for the experts without lecturing the grandparents in the front row. Maybe 8 minutes, casual but not sloppy. Can you draft it?
