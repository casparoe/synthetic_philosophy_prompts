Next month I present a chapter of my dissertation at a metaphysics colloquium. The thesis I defend is that there is no single, unified "grounding" relation underwriting all metaphysical explanation. Instead, I distinguish two structurally different dependencies: constitutive grounding, which operates within a kind between determinables and determinates or parts and wholes and obeys closure under determination, and explanatory grounding, which operates across kinds—such as mental-to-physical or moral-to-natural—and permits multiple realizability. I argue that formalizing both with one partial-order relation satisfying irreflexivity, well-foundedness, and transitivity artificially forces functional realization in biology and psychology to share the same logical structure as color-determination, thereby obscuring why only cross-kind dependence allows for multiple realizers. The audience includes two committed grounding realists who regard those formal conditions as essential to any metaphysical relation worthy of the name, one philosopher of mind who believes grounding solves the causal exclusion problem, and several graduate students in fundamentality who are suspicious of any view that sounds like metaphysical deflationism or epistemic humility disguised as ontology. Give me the objections in roughly the order they will be raised, phrased as each camp would phrase them, with for each: whether I should concede a point, what I must hold firm on, a concise reply suitable for a seminar exchange, and the weakest point in that reply. Identify the objection I am most hoping nobody asks—likely the one that demonstrates my pluralism either collapses into eliminativism about grounding or secretly presupposes a hidden unitary relation that does all the work—and advise me how to defend against it without appearing defensive. In your reply, explicitly state what argument or specific counterexample would actually change your assessment: which failure mode in my reasoning is the most probable, and what finding or consideration should lead me to abandon the pluralist framework rather than patch it.
