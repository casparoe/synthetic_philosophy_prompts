I am a retired physicist recently turned to philosophy, and I am leading a discussion circle of retired scientists and engineers with no formal philosophical training. I wish to teach David Lewis's analysis of convention—specifically how conventions arise from coordination problems with common knowledge of arbitrary alternatives—and how such conventions subsequently evolve into norms with prescriptive force. Rather than using the standard examples of driving on the left or linguistic usage, I intend to carry the explanation through the historical institutionalization of the p < 0.05 threshold in scientific publishing. Plan the explanatory arc: what conceptual distinction must be in place before the example can function; what features the p-value case must possess, and what it must lack, to serve this purpose; the misconceptions this technically trained audience will predictably form—such as treating conventions as explicit agreements or norms as reducible to statistical regularities—and where in the sequence each should be headed off; a single question I can ask at the end to determine whether they have grasped the underlying structure rather than merely retained the vocabulary; and which elements of Lewis's formal apparatus or of evolutionary-norm theory should be openly excluded at this level.
