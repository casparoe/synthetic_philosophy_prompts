I've been covering the philosophy of mind beat for a while and there's this old chestnut — Moore's paradox, you know, the case where someone says it's raining but they don't believe it — that still gets taught in every intro course but the actual research debate feel's like it's been spinning its wheels since the nineties. You've got the pragmatists saying it's just an absurd assertion, like promising to do something you already plan not to do, then you've got the doxastic crowd insisting it's a failure of rational belief coherence, and then there's this smaller camp that thinks the whole puzzle only shows up because we're treating belief reports as transparent in a way that breaks down with indexicals. Meanwhile, nobody seem's to move anyone, and conference panels on it feel like people restating the same three moves with fancier notaion.

My hunch is that this isn't really one disagreement but three different projects wearing the same nametag: one about the norms of assertion, one about the structure of first-person belief, and one about linguistic transparency. But I could be wrong — maybe there's a hidden empirical question about how people actually process self-referential belief reports that's being ignored because philosophers don't run experiments. Or maybe it's genuinely substantive but stuck because the intuitions about the case are just too stable and neither side can argue for them without begging the question.

So here's what I'm asking: diagnose the stall. Is this verbal, substantive, or a case of people answering different questions? And I'm especially curious about whether injecting something like an autoreferential confidence-calibration failure — the kind of loop you see in self-monitoring forecasting systems that update on their own predictions — actually helps clarify what's weird about Moore's case, or whether that's just importing tech jargon into a conceptual muddle. What would actually count as progress here? A distinction, a psychological result, a formal model, or is the question just ill-posed?
