Having recently retired from evolutionary biology, I have turned to the classical Chinese debate between Mencius and Xunzi concerning human nature. Mencius holds that we are born with four moral sprouts—compassion, shame, deference, and the sense of right and wrong—whereas Xunzi argues that human nature tends toward disorder and that moral order arises only through the external discipline of ritual and instruction. Recent experiments in developmental psychology suggest that pre-linguistic infants already discriminate between cooperative and obstructive agents, implying that moral evaluation may be biologically prepared rather than culturally implanted. Given these findings, has evolutionary psychology not shown Mencius to be empirically correct and Xunzi mistaken about the need for external imposition? Or does this comparison conflate an innate cognitive predisposition with the cultivated moral disposition that both philosophers, in different ways, take to be the true object of ethical development?
