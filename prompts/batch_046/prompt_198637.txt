I serve on my university's Committee on Institutional Memory and Naming, and we must vote by the end of this term on two linked questions: whether to retain the name Mill House for our graduate residential college, and whether to approve the proposed entrance-plaque text drafted by the History Department. The plaque interprets John Stuart Mill's long career at the East India Company — including his role in drafting post-Mutiny administrative guidance for British India — through what it terms a developmental exception framework: that Mill's defense of direct British rule over India in his writings on representative government was not a contradiction of his liberalism but its rigorous application, resting on his claim that representative institutions presuppose habits of collective self-rule that he believed were absent in societies he characterized as backward. An opposing faculty-student brief rejects this framing, arguing that it treats colonial administration as an abstract theoretical exception while ignoring the structural reality that Mill's office enforced extractive revenue regimes and the suppression of indigenous legal institutions, and that presenting his colonial role as a benevolent civilizing interlude effectively whitewashes the material violence that produced the very conditions of backwardness he described. Our committee's policy requires that any recommendation on naming include a concrete implementation timeline and address the strongest argument for the contrary position without reducing it to presentism or anachronism. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend either retention of the name with the plaque, retention with a substantially revised plaque, or initiation of a renaming process, specify what actions must occur within thirty and ninety days, and engage the opposing brief's central claim about structural complicity rather than simply restating Mill's theoretical intentions. In addition, include a brief reflexive paragraph stating what specific historical finding or philosophical argument, if established, would lead you to reverse the recommendation, and identify the precise point in your reasoning — for instance, the relative weight given to authorial intention versus institutional effect — where the argument is most vulnerable to failure.
