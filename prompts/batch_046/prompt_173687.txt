I have recently begun tracking my carbon footprint and examining the supply-chain economics of meat production in an effort to align my lifestyle with my moral beliefs, and I want a consistency audit before I present these views in a philosophy reading group. Please respond in British English, and do not include a bibliography or citation list—I will add those myself.

My commitments are these:

(a) In large democracies, a single vote is never decisive, so there is no outcome-based reason for me to vote; nevertheless, I vote regularly because democratic citizenship is a role that generates an obligation of respectful participation, independent of causal efficacy.

(b) For climate change, I believe I have a duty to remain within a fair share of global emissions budgets, which requires me to avoid air travel and other high-carbon activities, even though my individual contribution is causally imperceptible.

(c) For meat consumption, I avoid eating meat, but I justify this solely through direct complicity in institutional harm rather than through any distributive or fair-share principle; I deny that there is a permissible personal "budget" of animal suffering I may consume.

(d) I hold that personal moral obligations must be justified either by direct complicity in harm or by role-based obligations, but not by abstract distributive fairness applied to individual consumption patterns.

(e) I maintain that if two domains share the structural feature of causal redundancy—where my individual action makes no marginal difference within a large collective—then the form of moral justification in each domain must be structurally analogous.

(f) Through recent study of agricultural and energy markets, I have become convinced that my personal lifestyle choices are causally redundant in both food and climate systems.

Please examine which pairs are genuinely in tension, which tensions are merely apparent and dissolve on inspection, and rank the genuine ones by severity. For each real tension, say what the least costly revision would be and what it sacrifices. Then identify the single revision that would restore the greatest overall consistency with the least damage to the view as a whole. I am not asking for a literature review—just a conceptual audit of whether these six commitments hang together.
