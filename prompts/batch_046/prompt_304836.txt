Design a realistic municipal-budget thought experiment that pits negative utilitarianism against a suffering-focused view with a "saturation ceiling"—the claim that suffering-reduction has lexical priority only while suffering sits above some baseline, but once pushed below that ceiling, large eudaimonic gains can justify small residual discomforts. Set it up so a city can either eliminate severe chronic pain for 200 residents, or relocate those same 200 (causing them mild persistent discomfort) in order to fund deep flourishing programs for 10,000 others. No sci-fi, no omniscient observers, payoffs should feel like actual policy tradeoffs. After the scenario, show the reasoning for standard negative utilitarianism, for total utilitarianism, and for the saturation-ceiling view, and mark exactly which feature—the ceiling threshold or the eudaimonic-quality weighting—makes the third view peel off. Then ask: if we drop the quality distinction and treat welfare as homogenous, does the saturation view still diverge from NU, or does the case collapse back into a standard disagreement? Finish with how a defender of the ceiling view would attack the scenario, likely by arguing the relocated group's discomfort actually keeps them above the ceiling.
