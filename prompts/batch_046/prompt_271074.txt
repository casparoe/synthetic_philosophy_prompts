"I think this is clearly a moral dilemma. The officer has two binding obligations: one from her professional role as a doctor, one from her institutional role in the military. Since she can't do both, she's stuck. After she picks one, she'll feel serious regret about the other path — that's moral residue. Bernard Williams discusses this kind of remainder, though Sartre actually calls the feeling anguish, and it shows the unfulfilled obligation is still binding. If there were no remaining obligation there'd be nothing to regret.

As for the consistency objection: people say if O(A) and O(B) and not-possible(A and B), then they can't both be real obligations. But "ought implies can" just means you can physically perform each action. The officer can physically deny care, and she can physically provide care, so OIC is satisfied for both obligations separately. That defeats the objection, which means we really do have two binding obligations that conflict. So yes, it's a genuine dilemma, and the residue proves the losing side doesn't disappear."
