In discussions of distributive bargaining, the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution is frequently introduced through two heuristic analogies: first, that it divides an expanding surplus proportionally to each party's best-case claim relative to the disagreement point, as though scaling a pie according to each claimant's ideal slice; second, that it operates like a navigation problem, in which one draws a straight line from the status quo (the disagreement point) toward an ideal or utopian outcome and selects the first feasible agreement encountered along that trajectory. Both analogies are commonly invoked to motivate the solution's monotonicity axiom and to suggest that it captures a pre-theoretic intuition about fairness without requiring interpersonal utility comparisons. I want you to audit both analogies systematically. For each, lay out the explicit mapping: what in the bargaining formalism corresponds to the pie's growth, the navigator's direction, the stopping condition, and the boundary of feasibility? Then identify the precise points of disanalogy—particularly whether the proportional-growth analogy tacitly imports an interpersonal metric of utility that the Kalai-Smorodinsky axioms deliberately avoid, and whether the navigation analogy assumes a uniquely defined direction of improvement that collapses in multi-dimensional or non-convex feasible sets (for example, in a three-party setting where one player's ideal payoff vector lies inside the convex hull of the others'). Assess whether these disanalogies undermine the normative conclusion that monotonicity alone selects a uniquely reasonable division, or whether they merely illustrate pedagogical limitations. Finally, consider whether either analogy can be repaired, or whether a different analogical framework—say, one drawn from geometric median problems or from proportional taxation—better captures what the formal axioms actually entail, especially in the convex-hull case mentioned above. Your answer should separate the structural features that the analogies correctly highlight from the normative commitments they illegitimately smuggle in.
