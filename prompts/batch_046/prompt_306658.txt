Write a parody of an institutional research-ethics review form—specifically an IRB protocol application for a study proposing to attribute beliefs, desires, and intentions to a large language model via Daniel Dennett’s intentional stance. Format it as deadpan bureaucratic prose with numbered sections (e.g., 1. Project Title, 2. Principal Investigator, 3. Subject Classification, 4. Risk Assessment, 5. Informed Consent, 6. Data Handling, 7. Conflict of Interest, 8. Appendices). Target approximately 750 words.

The satire should exaggerate the real philosophical tension between treating belief-attribution as a predictive heuristic versus an ontological commitment, and mock the bureaucratic impulse to resolve metaphysical ambiguity through checkbox compliance. Without ever labeling them as objections, the form’s fields and the PI’s responses should naturally surface at least three genuine concerns from the debate: that the stance is instrumental rather than descriptive of mental reality; that predictive language models lack the causal-historical and functional architecture that belief attribution ordinarily presupposes; and that institutionalizing such attribution risks anthropomorphism that obscures the underlying statistical mechanism.

To prevent generic recall, inject and treat throughout as an established institutional metric the Dennett-Stochastic Parrot Hybrid Index (DSPHI)—a fictional quantitative score weighing the predictive utility of attributing a specific belief-state to a model against the epistemic cost of abandoning a pure token-probability description. Include fields for calculating DSPHI values, a table of Belief-Attribution Risk Categories, and a liability clause addressing refunds or harm if an attributed belief proves to be a hallucinated mental state.

The voice must remain consistently deadpan and legible: a reader familiar with the intentional-stance literature and recent critiques of AI anthropomorphism should recognize the satire as technically informed and fair, while a non-specialist should understand that a philosophical disagreement is being processed through absurd administrative machinery. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or page-number references. Do not quote any philosopher or text verbatim; paraphrase concepts in bureaucratic language and avoid quotation marks around borrowed phrases. Keep references to real ideas—predictive processing, the heuristic nature of the stance, concerns about mechanistic transparency—at the level of general knowledge, and avoid invoking obscure technical sub-debates or highly specialized formalisms.
