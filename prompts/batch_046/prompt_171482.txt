Write a proposal for a one-semester philosophy-of-physics term paper (approximately 5,000 words) on whether the Humean best-system analysis of laws can accommodate the explanatory asymmetry found in cosmological boundary-condition problems—specifically, the treatment of the low-entropy past as a constraint rather than as a law-like regularity—without collapsing into anti-Humean necessitarianism. State the question precisely enough to yield a determinate answer: if best-system criteria (simplicity, strength, and fit) apply globally, should cosmological initial conditions be included in the systematization, and does excluding them presuppose the very metaphysical distinction between law and contingent fact that Humeanism aims to eliminate? Explain the approach: analyze how statistical mechanics partitions dynamical laws from boundary conditions, then test whether that partition is pragmatic or ontological. Identify the two main possible conclusions—either Humeanism absorbs the asymmetry through pragmatic partitioning without modal commitment, or it cannot avoid smuggling in objective modal structure—and say which considerations (for instance, the need for objective probabilities in cosmology, or the threat of explanatory circularity if initial conditions are themselves systematized) would decide between them. Give an honest assessment of scope: can this be executed in one semester without resolving the broader scientific-realism debate, and what would count as failure (e.g., showing only that both frameworks accommodate the same data)?
