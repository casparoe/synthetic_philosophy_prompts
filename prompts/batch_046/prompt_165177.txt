I want to understand exactly what work that sentence is doing. For each reading, please say whether it is trivial, whether it is defensible, whether it is false, and—crucially—which ones actually undermine the use of authenticity criteria versus merely changing the subject from history to theology. I am especially interested in whether the slogan smuggles in an unstated premise about the epistemic inaccessibility of pre-literary oral tradition, and whether that premise is being confused with a claim about the theological irrelevance of whatever history might recover. Please organize the readings so I can bring them back to my sources, and conclude with a single line beginning "Verdict:" that states which reading scholars are most likely invoking when they use the phrase to end a methodological argument, and whether that reading genuinely defeats the criteria or just relocates the debate.
