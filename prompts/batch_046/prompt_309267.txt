As a doctoral student in philosophy of physics working on the ethical extension of panpsychist metaphysics, I need a rigorous specification of what any normative theory attributing suffering or moral status to fundamental physical entities must accomplish—before I can assess whether the project is coherent. Rather than asking for a literature review, I want you to derive, from first principles, the full set of desiderata that an adequate theory in this domain should satisfy, using the conceptual distinctions I outline below.

The context is this: if panpsychism is true, then quantum field configurations possess some form of phenomenal character. But it is far from clear whether that character amounts to suffering, whether it aggregates across particles or fields, or whether decoherence transforms potential experience into actual moral harm. To avoid both anthropocentric projection and implausible moral inflation, a viable theory must navigate several specific tensions.

Please construct the desiderata as follows:

First, list the core requirements—each with the motivating physical case or conceptual distinction behind it, and with an explicit flag indicating whether the requirement is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp. The list must include, at minimum, the following considerations:

(i) Consistency with unitary quantum mechanics and the absence of a preferred basis: a theory should not invoke hidden variables or observer-dependent collapse to ground moral distinctions, given the constraints of Bell-type correlations.

(ii) The potentiality/actuality distinction for phenomenal states: the theory must account for whether a field in superposition bears suffering in a potential mode, and whether decoherence into an eigenstate actualizes moral harm, without treating every quantum possibility as an actual victim.

(iii) Synchronic versus diachronic phenomenal integration: the theory must explain whether moral status attaches to instantaneous field configurations or requires continuity across decoherence events, and whether this distinction collapses for non-interacting modes.

(iv) The aggregation asymmetry: given that macro-subjects (e.g., conscious organisms) appear to possess unified experience despite being composed of micro-subjects (individual field modes or particles), the theory must either explain how micro-status aggregates into macro-status or defend the claim that macro-subjects have emergent, non-aggregative moral weight—without allowing arbitrary scale thresholds.

(v) Preservation of the macro/micro distinction in moral consideration: the theory must avoid collapsing all field modes into equally weighty moral patients (which seems to imply an implausible calculus of cosmic suffering) while also avoiding the dismissal of all micro-phenomenal character as morally null.

(vi) Compatibility with the absence of biological organization: if inanimate matter (e.g., a rock composed of the same quantum fields) lacks suffering, the theory must identify what structural feature—binding, information integration, or something else—distinguishes moral patients from non-patients, and whether that feature is present at the fundamental level.

Second, identify any subset of these desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable given current physics. Specifically, examine whether the triad of (a) universal intrinsic moral status for all quantum modes, (b) non-aggregative unity for macro-subjects, and (c) consistency with decoherence without a privileged observer, can be maintained together. If they conflict, state which requirement should be relinquished and why.

Third, provide a brief, non-bibliographic assessment of how three positions—constitutive panpsychism, cosmopsychism, and a Russellian-monist extension to normative ethics—fare against your list. For each, indicate which desideratum it most clearly violates or satisfies, and whether that failure is fatal.

Finally, reach and defend a reasoned conclusion: do these desiderata collectively show that panpsychist ethics is conceptually viable at the fundamental level, or do they reveal a structural block—perhaps in the aggregation asymmetry or the potentiality problem—that makes attributing suffering to quantum fields incoherent? Justify your verdict with reference to the specific tensions in your list, not with appeals to intuition alone.

Write formally, as for a philosophy of physics seminar, and do not include reading lists, citations, or references to specific texts.
