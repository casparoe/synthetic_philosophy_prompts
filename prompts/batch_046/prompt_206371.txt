On his view, classic money-pump arguments illegitimately aggregate preferences across frame breaks—whether caused by learning, preference change, or shifts in evaluative perspective—so that cyclical choice patterns spanning frames are not irrational, only cross-frame adaptive. I want eight to ten questions that treat this seriously rather than asking him to restate it.

And at least one on long-term planning and commitment devices—whether Ulysses contracts and similar mechanisms are rational strategies that extend a single frame, or whether they represent an irrational attempt to suppress legitimate cross-frame adaptation; include a follow-up anticipating his reply that commitments are frame-stabilizing tools rather than constraints on rational change.

The audience reads rationalist blogs and knows decision theory at a conceptual level, but don't assume familiarity with the technical literature on dynamic choice.
