I'm preparing a seminar on moral compromise and have been working through a framework—call it normative exchange theory—that treats conflicts between incommensurable value systems as potential sites for gains from trade rather than pure deadlock. The core idea is that when two groups hold fundamentally different moral ontologies, they can sometimes achieve a Pareto improvement in normative welfare by trading concessions: each gives up a good that is relatively cheap in its own evaluative currency but highly valued in the other’s, provided there is an incommensurability buffer that prevents direct comparison of the goods exchanged and thus avoids the appearance of betrayal. I want to test this framework against a real episode I think illustrates it well but also strains it: the negotiation and signing of the 1998 Good Friday Agreement in Northern Ireland.

Analyze this episode through the lens of normative exchange theory. First, map the actors—not just the UK and Irish governments and the major parties, but the specific moral communities involved (republican, loyalist, unionist, and the various church-based mediators)—and identify what each treated as non-negotiable versus tradable within their value systems. What were the actual concessions made—decommissioning timelines, prisoner release provisions, power-sharing structures, policing reform—and how do they look as normative trades: which side gave up goods that were costly to them but valuable to others, and was there genuinely an incommensurability buffer preventing direct comparison? Second, say where the framework explains the success of the agreement and where it fails: did the trade depend on hidden commensurability (for instance, both sides ultimately valuing institutional stability in comparable terms), or did some concessions amount not to mutual trade but to unilateral moral surrender that the theory mischaracterizes as reciprocal gain? Third, tell me what the episode reveals about the theory itself—does it show that moral compromise requires a shared meta-value such as peace that undermines the incommensurability claim, or does it show that gains from trade are possible even when the parties explicitly deny they are trading at all? Be disciplined about the mapping: specify who is giving what, in whose moral currency, and say explicitly where the theory is silent rather than forcing it to speak.
