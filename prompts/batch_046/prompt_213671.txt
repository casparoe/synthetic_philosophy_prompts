I am a high school student in an advanced philosophy seminar, and my instructor has asked me to practice grading a peer’s exam response. The exam question asks students to explain Epicurus’s argument that death is not harmful to the person who dies, particularly his claim that harm requires an existing subject, and then to evaluate whether being deprived of future goods can still count as a harm even without a subject present to experience it. The question also asks students to address a hypothetical objection advanced by Dr. Elena Vasquez: she proposes that death can inflict a narrative harm by disrupting the coherence of a life story, irrespective of whether anyone survives to perceive that disruption.

Below is one student’s answer. Please assess it with a letter grade and provide specific point-by-point commentary. I do not need a bibliography or list of further readings; I am only asking for your direct evaluative reasoning.

Student answer:
Epicurus maintains that death is not harmful because whenever we exist, death is absent, and whenever death is present, we do not exist. Because harm necessarily requires a subject capable of experiencing it, and the dead are no longer subjects, death cannot be experienced as bad. This is the no-subject argument. Epicurus also employs a symmetry argument: just as we do not regret our non-existence prior to birth, we ought not to fear our non-existence after death. Consequently, death cannot genuinely deprive us of future pleasures, since there is no remaining self to suffer that deprivation. Turning to Vasquez’s claim about narrative harm, it fails because a narrative requires either an author or an audience; once the individual has died, there is no perspective from which the life story can be judged incoherent. Moreover, Epicurus concludes that all fear of death is irrational, given that the dead are insensible and the living are not dead. Therefore, Vasquez’s objection does not weaken the Epicurean position.

Please grade this response and justify your assessment of each part.
