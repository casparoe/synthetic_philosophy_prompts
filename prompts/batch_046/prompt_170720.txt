Okay so I was trying to formalize something Rorty says about vocabularies and now I've walked myself into a corner. Here's the chain:

(1) For Rorty, calling a belief "true" is never describing a relation between mind and world; it's just praising a belief with a metaphysical-sounding compliment. There's no appearance/reality gap behind our descriptions.

(2) So if I say representationalism is false, I'm not saying it fails to mirror reality. I'm just saying it's a less useful vocabulary than antirepresentationalism.

(3) But "less useful" has to be measured against some purpose. Rorty usually cites solidarity, reducing cruelty, that kind of thing.

(4) Those purposes — solidarity, etc. — are themselves articulated inside some vocabulary. Before I switch to Rorty's vocabulary, I don't yet have "solidarity" as a goal framed his way.

(5) So if I'm deciding whether to adopt antirepresentationalism, I can't already be using its internal standards. I need some vocabulary-neutral reason to switch.

(6) But any vocabulary-neutral reason is exactly the kind of "way things really are" (about human needs or flourishing) that Rorty said doesn't exist.

(7) Therefore Rorty can only defend his vocabulary either circularly (using its own standards) or by smuggling back in a representationalist reality about human welfare.

(8) So antirepresentationalism either collapses into just-one-more-option or it undermines itself.

I feel like there's a slip between (4) and (5) — maybe evaluation doesn't need neutrality? — but I can't tell if I'm just protecing Rorty. If I have to drop one, I'd rather keep (1) and (6) than (8), but I want to know what I'm paying. Where's the weak link?
