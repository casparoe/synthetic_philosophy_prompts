If cross-cultural research in moral psychology demonstrates that WEIRD populations tend to restrict core moral cognition to harm and fairness, while many non-WEIRD societies distribute normative weight across loyalty, authority, and purity, does this divergence not constrain moral objectivity to a minimal universal core? More precisely, I am trying to determine whether the so-called binding foundations represent genuine objective obligations that happen to be cognitively compressed in Western contexts—perhaps mapped through culturally specific moral topologies—or whether their absence from WEIRD moral reasoning indicates that they lack ontological status altogether. Put differently: if an otherwise rational population can systematically fail to register a normative domain without epistemic fault, can that domain still be objectively binding on them?
