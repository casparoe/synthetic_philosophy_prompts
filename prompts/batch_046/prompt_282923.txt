I work in formal verification and am fluent with coinductive definitions, greatest fixed points, bisimulation equivalence, and termination proofs that depend on reaching a base case, but I have little background in philosophy or game theory. Restate the backward induction paradox in the centipede game entirely in that vocabulary: treat the sequence of moves as a coinductive object, rational defection as a termination condition, and common knowledge of rationality as an equivalence relation over game states. For each component of the standard philosophical account — the unraveling argument from the final node, the tension with actual human cooperation, and the requirement that rationality be common knowledge — report what translates cleanly, what must be distorted, and what has no expression in your framework. Then assess whether the residue reveals a substantive philosophical gap that coinductive reasoning cannot capture, or whether the paradox depends essentially on an inductive structure that disappears when the interaction is viewed as an infinite, non-terminating process.
