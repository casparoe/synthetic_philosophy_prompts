I run a philosophy podcast aimed at longtime readers of rationalist forums, and next week I am interviewing an unnamed defender of a strict Darwinian memetics. Her specific view—call it the high-fidelity Weismannian constraint—is that cultural evolution counts as genuinely selectionist only when three conditions hold: replication fidelity is high enough to suppress drift, variation is blind rather than directed, and inheritance respects a Weismannian separation so that acquired traits are not transmitted. She contends that digital culture violates all three: algorithmic recommendation introduces directed mutation (she terms this “algorithmic Lamarckism”), feedback loops allow single-generation inheritance of acquired features, and lossy compression drives copying fidelity below the selection threshold. Draft eight to ten interview questions that avoid letting her merely restate the framework. I require at least one question on each of the following: (i) the quantitative fidelity threshold—what metric of copying accuracy is relevant, and how high must it be for selection to dominate drift in a cultural lineage; (ii) whether algorithmic curation is best understood as directed mutation or rather as environmental filtering that still permits blind variation upstream; and (iii) what empirically distinguishable prediction this strict view makes relative to standard dual-inheritance theories that permit guided variation and biased transmission. For each of these three, include a follow-up keyed to her most probable reply—namely, that the threshold is domain-relative, that curation is upstream filtering, and that the strict view predicts rapid degradation of complex cultural traits under low-fidelity digital transmission, respectively. In addition, include a question that connects her framework to rationalist concerns about epistemic hygiene: if memetic selection in digital environments optimizes for engagement rather than truth-tracking, does her model imply that rationalist-style belief revision is not merely arguing against bad ideas but fighting a systematically misaligned evolutionary regime? Conclude with a question pressing her on the stability of scientific institutions: given that science operates within the same digital environment, what institutional mechanism, on her account, restores Weismannian separation and suppresses Lamarckian feedback well enough to protect complex theoretical traits?
