In Schrödinger's later philosophical writings he adopts a Vedantic position according to which individual consciousnesses are not genuinely plural but are manifestations of one universal awareness. Taking this ontological claim literally rather than metaphorically, I want to press a consequence for quantum mechanics that seems rarely discussed: the standard formulation of the measurement problem depends on the existence of multiple observers whose reports can conflict, yet if there is strictly one consciousness identifying with different physical bodies in succession, then there can be no inter-observer contradiction requiring resolution by wavefunction collapse. Given this, shouldn't we read Schrödinger's cat paradox not as a reductio ad absurdum of macroscopic superposition, but rather as an intentional illustration that the distinction between life and death—like the distinction between observer and observed—is a modal appearance within a single, undivided awareness? In other words, does the Vedantic unity of consciousness dissolve the measurement problem by eliminating the multiplicity that makes it a problem in the first place, and if so, why do commentators continue to treat the cat thought experiment as a purely physical objection to Copenhagen rather than as the mystical complement to his monism?
