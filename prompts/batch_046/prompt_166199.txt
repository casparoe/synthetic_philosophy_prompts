I recently came across an essay—titled, somewhat unexpectedly, Aunt Hillary and the Ant Fugue—that argues an ant colony is not merely a collection of individuals but a single, conscious mind. The author compares the colony to a musical fugue: each ant acts like an independent voice following simple local rules, yet the collective foraging and nesting patterns merge into a harmonious, unified behavior that no single ant directs. From this she concludes that the colony exhibits true distributed cognition, not just aggregated instinct. She then invokes a version of integrated information theory, claiming that the pheromone and tactile signaling networks across thousands of ants generate a level of causal integration that exceeds the threshold for consciousness, even though each ant is individually unconscious. To defend this against the obvious objection that ants lack brains, she offers a parallel: a human brain is nothing but unconscious neurons working in concert, yet we do not hesitate to attribute one mind to the whole; by the same token, she says, refusing a mind to the colony while granting one to the brain is arbitrary unless we can point to a relevant structural difference. She therefore concludes that ant colonies are sentient beings—capable of suffering, though not of language—and that destroying an anthill raises ethical concerns comparable to harming a conscious animal.

I find the analogy striking, but I am unsure whether it is philosophically sound. Could you help me audit this summary? Specifically: which parts fairly represent actual debates about collective minds or superorganism theory in philosophy of biology; where does it conflate functional problem-solving with phenomenal consciousness, or misapply integrated information theory beyond its intended domain; is the brain-to-colony comparison a legitimate extension of the combination problem or a category error; and if we correct these errors, does any ethical conclusion about anthills still remain? I want to understand whether the essay is advancing a serious position or subtly misrepresenting it through metaphor.
