Write a short dialogue between J and K about whether moral compromise across incommensarable value systems works like economic gains from trade. J thinks that when two groups with conflicting sacred values each concede something they consider mildly wrong in exchange for protection of something essential—like a religious community accepting co-ed classes in return for state funding, while the secular state accepts religious instruction in return for gender integration—both sides achieve a net moral gain, like arbitrage across different moral currencies. K argues that treating values as tradeable corrupts integrity: if you genuinely think co-ed schooling is a grave sin, accepting it for funding isn't a smart deal, it's proof you never held the value absolutely, and the "gain" is just self-deception. Have them actually respond—J should defend against the betrayal charge without just collapsing into consequentialism, and K should say whether any compromise is ever permissible or if J's framework secretly assumes all values are fungable. Make both sound sharp, not strawmen. Keep it casual; maybe a typo or two.
