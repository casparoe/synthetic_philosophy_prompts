I'm drafting the framework for a paper on Confucian role ethics and want a consistency audit before I develop the arguments. Here is where I stand:

(1) The self is not an autonomous substance but is constituted by the network of roles one occupies—parent, child, friend, official—so moral identity is irreducibly relational.
(2) Ethical life aims at harmony (he) achieved through disciplined ritual propriety (li), not at applying universal rules or maximizing welfare.
(3) Li functions as a formative practice: through correct, repeated performance one cultivates the appropriate affective dispositions—filial affection, respect, care—rather than merely displaying them.
(4) Nevertheless, mechanical execution remains possible: one can perform li flawlessly while lacking the corresponding inner state, and the Confucian criticism of the "village worthy" targets exactly this disconnection between outer form and inner substance.
(5) Benevolence (ren) is a universal human capacity that every person can develop, yet it has no meaningful content outside concrete relational contexts and is only actualized within them.
(6) I want to accommodate liberal rights discourse by interpreting rights not as protections of pre-social autonomy but as institutional safeguards that preserve the social conditions necessary for fulfilling role-based obligations.
(7) Moral obligations are graded by relational proximity—I owe more to a parent than to a stranger—not arbitrarily, but because the density of shared practices in close relations is what makes virtue-cultivation possible.

My question: which commitments actually pull against one another, which only appear to do so, and what is the least costly revision? Specifically, examine whether (1) and (6) can be reconciled without smuggling back in the autonomous self that (1) denies; whether (3) and (4) are in tension over whether perfect ritual performance can coexist with deficient inner feeling; and whether (5) and (7) jointly entail an unacceptable moral parochialism or can be defended together. Rank the tensions by severity, indicate the cheapest fix for each, and say which single commitment, if dropped, would resolve the most conflicts.
