You serve on the International Council on Extraterrestrial Communication Ethics, a chartered advisory board that reviews active-messaging petitions and issues binding pre-transmission opinions. At Thursday's session you must circulate a formal recommendation — approve with conditions, impose a conditional moratorium, or reject — regarding Petition 2027-X filed by the private consortium Helios Reach. The petition requests authorization to direct a high-gain laser reply toward the ambiguous origin region of Signal X-447, detected eighteen months ago. While some astrophysicists classify the emission as a magnetar exhibiting unprecedented rotational modulation, a minority of board members and external reviewers contend it displays intentional structure: specifically, an embedded "developmental index" that encodes the sender's approximate energy-consumption scale without, due to an indeterminate parallax trajectory, revealing precise spatial coordinates.

This configuration inverts the standard dark-forest asymmetry, in which revealing one's location is presumed to invite predation. Helios Reach argues that replying with our own calibrated technological footprint — entering what they call a "transparency game" — would not expose our planetary address but would establish mutual epistemic vulnerability, potentially signaling cooperative intent to any observer capable of decoding both messages. The opposing faction, led by the board's risk-assessment chair, counters that confirming our comprehension of the signal is itself a dangerous threshold revelation; they further object that a private entity is proposing an act with species-level externalities, and that any reply should be deferred until multilateral state-level review is complete. Your memo should recommend a concrete course of action with an explicit time frame (for example, a six-month conditional hold pending an international protocol, or an immediate rejection with criteria for future reconsideration), and it must engage the transparency-game argument seriously rather than dismissing it as mere speculation. The reasoning should be rigorous enough that both the consortium and the risk-assessment chair could recognize their positions addressed in good faith, and it should avoid any request for citations, bibliographies, or references that members without external search access could not independently verify.
