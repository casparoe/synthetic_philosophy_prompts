I've been teaching a seminar on moral status and I've come round to a slightly unorthodox take on the insect scale argument. The problem, I think, isn't just low credence in insect sentience — it's that the argument treats potential suffering across phylogentic boundaries as if it were fungable, like stacking probabilities inside one sample space. But if the phenomenal character (or even the functional role) of nociception in, say, a hymenopteran is ontologically distinct from mammalian pain, then multiplying a low credence by billions of individuals is just bad maths. You're aggregating across incommensurabilty domains. So the 'vast numbers' objection collapses conceptually, not just empirically.
