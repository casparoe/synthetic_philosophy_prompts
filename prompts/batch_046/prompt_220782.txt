I'm currently taking an upper-level course on logical positivism and philosophy of religion, and we've been examining A. J. I would like to extend this framework to a contemporary problem that did not exist during his lifetime.

Consider a hypothetical technology—call it 'substrate-independent functional resurrection'—in which a deceased individual's complete cognitive architecture is reconstructed from archival neural and behavioural data and instantiated in a non-biological substrate. The resulting system reports first-person continuity, accesses the original's autobiographical memories, and behaves indistinguishably from the living original, yet there is no spatiotemporal causal chain linking the biological death to the activation of the emulation; the reconstruction is assembled acausally from distributed records. Some philosophers argue that this constitutes a genuine, if unconventional, form of survival that avoids both physicalist extinction and dualist transcendence, while others maintain it produces merely a phenomenologically convincing duplicate.

If Ayer were alive today and confronted with this scenario, how do you think he would analyse it? Specifically:

- Would he regard assertions about the 'survival' of the original person within such an emulation as cognitively meaningful under his verificationist criteria, or as pseudo-statements that lack any possible empirical verification?
- Given his post-1988 admission that his 'inflexible attitude' toward the impossibility of post-mortem existence had weakened, do you think he would treat substrate-independent continuity as a borderline case that genuinely challenges his earlier dismissal, or would he insist that the absence of direct causal continuity renders talk of 'survival' a category mistake?
- How might he distinguish between the meaningful claim that 'the emulated system experiences being the same person' and the metaphysically loaded claim that 'the original subject has survived bodily death', and which of these distinctions would he take to be philosophically decisive?

Please provide a conceptual analysis rather than a bibliography or reading list; I am interested in how his specific philosophical commitments—verificationism, the rejection of metaphysical pseudo-propositions, and his qualified post-NDE openness—would interact with this particular technological hypothetical. Write in a formal, analytical style suitable for a philosophy essay.
