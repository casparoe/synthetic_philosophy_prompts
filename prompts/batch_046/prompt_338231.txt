I'm taking a graduate seminar on liberal eugenics and reproductive autonomy, and during a session on genetic enhancement a fellow student kept using the maxim "procreative beneficence demands that we choose the best child available" as if it settled every dispute—first to defend preimplantation genetic diagnosis for non-disease traits like height or cognitive ability, then to justify heritable germline editing aimed at the same ends. I think the phrase is being made to do far more work than it can bear, but I'm struggling to articulate exactly where the ambiguity lies. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses this slogan might express, treating it as a menu rather than a single doctrine?

Specifically, I want the disambiguation to cover at least these axes: is the claim about comparative harm (that a parent who fails to enhance wrongs the particular child who is actually born), about parental virtue (that good parents maximize expected welfare for their offspring), or about distributive justice (that social institutions ought to make optimal reproductive outcomes available)? Within each, what does "best" mean—highest subjective well-being, the broadest range of capabilities, or conformity to some objective list of human flourishing? And what does "available" cover: current technological feasibility, economic accessibility within the parents' means, or biological possibility given present science? For each reading, please indicate whether it is trivial, whether it is genuinely defensible, and—crucially—whether it applies symmetrically to embryo selection and to germline editing, or whether the non-identity problem breaks the symmetry by making the "better child" produced by editing a numerically different individual from the one who would have existed otherwise. I need this as a structured list of interpretations I can bring back to the seminar, together with a brief note on which version, if any, actually supports both technologies without collapsing into incoherence once the non-identity objection is taken seriously.
