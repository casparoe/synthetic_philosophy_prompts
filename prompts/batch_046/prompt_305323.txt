As a journalist covering philosophy and science, I am trying to work out when a thinker's private political writings illuminate—or undermine—their formal philosophy, and I am using Gottlob Frege's late political diary as my test case. Here are my tentative verdicts. First, the diary's virulent antisemitism and its call for expelling Jewish citizens from Germany seem to me entirely separate from the technical validity of his predicate calculus and his logical definition of natural number; those results stand or fall on internal logical grounds. Second, the diary's praise for a purely German scientific tradition and its blame of foreign influence for corrupting universities strikes me as philosophically relevant, because Frege's anti-psychologism and his doctrine that logical thoughts—Gedanken—are objective, shareable entities independent of any particular mind might be structurally parallel to, or even motivated by, a fantasy of cultural purity, though I am unsure. Third, his technical derivation of arithmetic from pure logic, with no appeal to intuition or empirical psychology, seems to me functionally independent of his political views; the proof does not depend on the nationality of the prover. Fourth, I keep oscillating on whether his rejection of psychologism—logic is not about mental processes—is politically neutral or not: if the objective realm of Gedanken is defined implicitly by what remains after purging both subjective mental states and foreign cultural elements, then politics and metaphysics are entangled. The borderline case I cannot settle is the tension between the diary's nationalist rhetoric about German essence and Frege's explicit universalist claim that logical truths hold for every rational being regardless of nationality. Is there a precise principle—perhaps concerning functional independence, explanatory priority, or hidden motivational structure—that distinguishes the diary entries that genuinely bear on his philosophy of logic from those that do not? And must I abandon one of my four verdicts to keep the principle simple, or can a more complex principle accommodate them all?
