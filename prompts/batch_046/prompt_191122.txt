okay so i was trying to write up this extension of Carroll's tortoise thing for a forum thread and now i'm stuck in a loop. here's the arg a friend threw at me:

(1) if you're rationally justified in infering C from P, you need a rule R that actually licences that step.
(2) but to apply R you need to be justified in thinking R applies here, which needs a meta-rule R'.
(3) Carroll shows you can't just bake R into P without changing what's being asked — the tortoise always demands the next rule up.
(4) so full justification of any deduction needs an infinite stack R, R', R'', etc.
(5) any actual mind — human or model — can only hold finitely many explicit commitments.
(6) therefore no finite agent is ever fully justified in using modus ponens or any other rule.
(7) but we clearly do treat deduction as rational, otherwise math and forecasting collapse.
(8) the only escape is letting some rules be structurally justified — justified not by another rule but by being part of the architecture that makes reasoning possible at all.
(9) but then there's the promiscuity problem: if structural justification is available, why can't "affirming the consequent" also be structurally basic for some other architecture? without an independent criterion, structural justification collapses into "whatever my brain does is rational."
(10) so either deduction is never rational, or valid and invalid inference are on the same footing.

this feels like it proves too much but i can't see the exit. i really want to keep (1) and (7) — it seems right that rationality needs some backing and that deduction isn't globally broken. is the problem in (5) assuming explicit representation? or in (9) assuming structural justification has to be promiscuous? what's the least costly place to cut, and what do i lose if i take that route?
