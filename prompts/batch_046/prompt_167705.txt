In Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise, Achilles is compelled to keep adding premises that encode the very rule of modus ponens he wishes to apply, generating an apparently endless hierarchy of logical rules. If this demonstrates that every justification of deduction must already presuppose the inferential principles under scrutiny, then formal logic seems to lack any non-circular foundation. Given this unavoidable regress, shouldn't we conclude that logical rules are not genuine normative constraints on reasoning but merely conventional patterns embedded in shared linguistic practice—so that calling an inference 'valid' describes only a social habit rather than providing any independent rational warrant?
