Please argue that carbon-tax mechanisms are conceptually incoherent within a rigorous degrowth framework—not on the familiar empirical ground that taxes fail to reduce emissions quickly enough, but on the philosophical ground that they presuppose an economic ontology in which environmental harm is fully commensurable with monetary value and correctable through marginal price adjustments. Defend the stronger claim that pricing carbon implicitly retains a commitment to substitutability and to a growth-compatible equilibrium dynamics that contradicts the degrowth thesis that absolute throughput reduction requires non-market, structurally non-substitutable limits rather than optimized incentives. In doing so, address the standard reply that a tax can be calibrated high enough to enforce absolute decoupling and therefore remain compatible with degrowth objectives, and explain why that reply still operates within the very axiological framework the degrowth position rejects.
