I am preparing a one-year fellowship application in moral philosophy, focused specifically on population axiology. I need a concise but rigorous two-page research proposal built around the following problem. Standard formulations of the repugnant conclusion assume that welfare is measured on a single cardinal scale applicable to every possible life, regardless of that life’s qualitative structure. I suspect this assumption obscures a deeper difficulty: different possible lives may be organized around incommensurable welfare spaces—for instance, lives whose well-being is constituted primarily by sensory richness versus those constituted primarily by relational or intellectual goods. If population axiology is required to aggregate across these structurally different welfare spaces, then the comparison that generates the repugnant conclusion may rest on an implicit and undefended conversion metric.

My proposed project asks whether introducing what I will call “baseline pluralism”—the view that different welfare spaces carry their own non-intertranslatable zero-points and scaling functions—can block the repugnant conclusion, or whether it merely relocates the paradox into a choice about which welfare spaces ought to be populated. I want the proposal to contain: (i) a sharply formulated central question that makes the ambiguity precise; (ii) an account of why the standard menu of responses—total and average utilitarianism, critical-level theories, and person-affecting restrictions—fails to address this particular aggregation problem, even though each resolves other versions of the paradox; (iii) a clear methodological plan, likely combining formal modeling of multi-baseline populations with conceptual analysis of aggregation consistency conditions, together with a justification for this approach over purely empirical or purely intuitive methods; (iv) a description of what a positive result would establish and what specific outcomes would count as project failure—for example, if baseline pluralism collapses into single-scale evaluation under any plausible consistency constraint; and (v) a realistic month-by-month timeline for twelve months of work.

The tone should be appropriate for a philosophy funding panel, and the proposal should not include bibliographic citations, literature reviews by author name, or reading lists, since I will supply those separately. Please keep the conceptual framing self-contained and answerable without reference to obscure recent publications.
