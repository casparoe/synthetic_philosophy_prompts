I am revising a literary novel in which characters and the narrator issue moral judgments of one another, and I need a reusable decision procedure—applicable in about ten minutes during drafting—to determine whether a given judgment arises from what Nietzsche's genealogy of morality describes as master morality, slave morality animated by ressentiment, or an unstable hybrid. The procedure must operate solely on textual evidence visible in the scene: the syntax of the condemnation, whether the speaker defines the good positively through self-affirmation or negatively through reference to an enemy, whether prior values have been inverted, and whether the speaker's own position is one of action or reactive comparison. Present it as an ordered checklist with explicit decision points, indicate where the procedure must hand the decision back to authorial judgment because the distinction is one of values rather than facts, and state clearly what the framework cannot adjudicate—particularly where aesthetic preference or narrative sympathy risks being misread as genealogical diagnosis.

Then apply the procedure to three brief scene sketches I provide for testing. First, a soldier praising an opponent's ferocity immediately before defeating him. Second, a servant describing her employer's charity as a calculated humiliation. Third, a first-person narrator I had intended to present as morally authoritative condemning a rival's ambition as vulgar and grasping. For the third case, I want the procedure to return a classification that undermines the narrator's intended authority; identify the precise step in the checklist where that misfire originates, explain whether it reveals a flaw in the scene's construction or an unavoidable limit of the framework, and say what revision—if any—could restore the intended narrative effect without abandoning the genealogical distinction entirely. Keep the deliverable practical for a writer without philosophical training, avoid any request for citations or external scholarship, and flag every point where judgment cannot be eliminated.
