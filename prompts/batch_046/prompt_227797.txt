Every quarter someone proposes an amendment that touches the amendment rules themselves, and honestly I've been winging it with gut checks. I'm burnt out on that.

Please act like a professional philosopher of law with a systems-engineering bent, think carefull, and do a quick whiteboard brainstorm first. Then build me a reusable audit procedre — something I can run in about thirty minutes — for any proposed clause that amends the charter's own amendment mechanism. Treat the charter as a three-layer machine: layer one is the substantive rules (money, authorship), layer two is the amendment machinery (vote thresholds, notice periods), and layer three is the interpretation protocol that resolves conflicts between the other layers.

The checklist should move in order: first test whether the clause references its own enactment conditions; second, whether it rewires layer three in a way that retroactively validates itself; third, whether there's a hidden loop where layer-two outputs depend on layer-three outputs the proposal just changed. At each step I need a clear stop/no-stop, and I need you to flag the exact point where logic ends and values begin — where I have to kick the decision to the collective's values committee rather than calling it a formal error. Be explicit about misfires: cases where the clause is ambiguous across two equally valid interpretations, or where the loop is real but harmless.

Then run it on three proposals. One is a benign housekeeping tweak that lowers quorum but leaves the amendment rules untouched. The second is a sneaky proposal that lets a simple majority suspend conflict-of-interest rules, but those rules live in layer three, so the proposal is quietly rewriting its own referee. Make sure at least one case returns a verdict you'll probably dislike — I want to see the procedure fail gracefully rather than just confirm my biases. For the mirror clause especially, show exactly where the audit breaks, what the values committee actually needs to decide (is self-nullification a feature or a bug?), and what the procedre should record for the record.

Don't give me a bibliography or tell me to read Ross or Suber in the original. If you need to invoke the classic legal self-reference puzzles, just paraphrase them inside the steps. No verbatim quotes from anyone. I want this operational, not a literature review — a flowchart in words that a tired referee can actually use.
