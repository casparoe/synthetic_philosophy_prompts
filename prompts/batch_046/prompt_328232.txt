I sit on my university's doctoral review board — long story, involves a dean who read my novel about a philosopher-king and decided I was qualified — and we have a vote next thursday on a proposal that lets PhD candidates in philosophy and comparative lit use AI reasoning partners during their oral exams. The candidates can query the system live, but they have to submit a cognitive trace mapping every claim they made back to either thier own reasoning or the partner's output. The proposal also includes this odd mechanism called epistemic escrow: before the exam, the student writes out a full defense of their thesis with no AI help, seals it, and then during the exam they have to show they can still reconstruct that same argument on the fly, partner or not. Half the board says this is just updated scholarship — like using a database or asking a colleague. The other half says it proves we've given up on understanding altogether: if your grasp of an idea depends on a system you don't fully control, you've traded knowledge for access, and the escrow is just theater of competence.

What I need is a memo recommending approve, reject, or approve with conditions — concrete conditions, with a timeline if you want — and I need it to lead with the bottom line in the very first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble, don't restate my question. Write in American English. Please don't just recycle the usual AI-kills-thinking talking points; take the escrow idea realy seriously. Could structured dependence actually deepen understanding if the dependency is visible and reversible? Or is there a point where outsourcing inference always erodes the kind of ownership a doctorate should certify? Give me something I can actually circulate before the meeting.
