I'm trying to map out my liberty views for a seminar paper and want a consistency check before I present. Roughly:

1. Negative liberty is absence of actual intentional interference; lacking resources or abilities doesn't count.
2. Republican liberty is non-domination: being subject to another's arbitary will that doesn't track your interests.
3. Domination can exist with zero actual interference — the benevolent master/slave case.
4. Democratic welfare-state interference (taxes, regs) isn't domination if it's genuinely non-arbitary and contestable.
5. Competitive labor markets aren't dominating because employer power is constrained by contracts, competition, and exit; it's not arbitary.
6. But market dependancy creates structural vulnerability that's functionally equivalent to domination, so I back basic income and workplace democracy to protect republican freedom.
7. Algorithmic reccomendation systems don't dominate users because they lack agency/will — domination needs an agent.

Do these hang together? Is (5) compatible with (6) given I deny arbitary agent-power in markets but invoke structural domination? Does (3) clash with (7) since both involve power without interference? And is the tension people see between (1) and (4) actually just superficial? Rank real tensions by severity, say what'd have to give for each, and point out any that dissolve on inspection. Don't treat them all as equally bad.
