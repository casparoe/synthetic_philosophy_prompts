For a philosophy of religion seminar I am drafting my view on causation and occasionalism and want a consistency check before presenting. Here is my profile:

(1) I accept al-Ghazālī's pure occasionalism: no created being possesses intrinsic causal efficacy, and God is the sole immediate cause of every event.
(2) I hold that natural science genuinely uncovers real, law-governed causal structures among created things, not merely descriptions of divine custom (ʿāda).
(3) I think moral responsibility requires that an agent's intentions genuinely cause her actions, rather than merely preceding them in a customary sequence ordained by God.
(4) I deny that miracles violate natural law; they are best understood as God producing effects without the customary concomitants (e.g., burning without fire present) that usually accompany them.
(5) I maintain that divine omnipotence is not diminished if God actually delegates genuine causal power specifically to human agents for voluntary actions, while retaining direct causation over all other events.
(6) I accept a Humean epistemology of causation: we observe only constant conjunction and temporal priority, with no rational access to necessary connections in nature.

Please audit this set: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and which commitments pull against each other in clusters? For each real tension, rate its severity, say what must give, and identify the cheapest revision and its cost. Then rank all tensions by severity, flag any that dissolve on inspection, and tell me which single change to my profile would clean up the most problems at once. Do not treat every conflict as equally serious; discriminate.
