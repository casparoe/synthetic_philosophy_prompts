I am a senior in my high school philosophy elective, and our instructor asked me to peer-grade a classmate’s essay for the unit on metaphysics of persistence. We had been arguing about these ideas over lunch with friends who think the topic is just wordplay, so I want an evaluation that takes the reasoning seriously and catches subtle mistakes rather than obvious ones. The assignment was: “In a brief essay, distinguish endurantism from perdurantism. Then explain how each position addresses the problem of temporary intrinsics—using the case of a bent wire that is later straightened. Finally, discuss one specific alternative theory of persistence (not one of the two main ones) and clarify whether it belongs to the endurantist or perdurantist camp.” The student’s answer is as follows. Endurantism holds that a material object is wholly present at every moment of its existence, so the wire on Monday is numerically identical to the wire on Tuesday and exists in full at both times. Perdurantism, by contrast, treats the wire as a four-dimensional “spacetime worm” composed of temporal parts—wire-Monday and wire-Tuesday—so that only a part exists at any given instant. The problem of temporary intrinsics asks how one thing can be both bent and straight without contradiction. Endurantists solve this by indexing properties to times: the wire is bent-at-Monday and straight-at-Tuesday, which avoids contradiction without dividing the object. Perdurantists solve it differently: wire-Monday bears the property of being bent simpliciter, while wire-Tuesday bears being straight simpliciter, so no single part is contradictory; however, this means there is no genuine change, only static variation along the time dimension, comparable to a road being curved in one region and flat in another. Another view is stage theory, developed by the philosopher Katherine Hawley, which holds that objects are instantaneous stages linked by counterpart relations. Because it rejects four-dimensional worms, stage theory should be understood as a refined form of endurantism in which persistence is explained by replacement rather than extension. Please assess this answer with exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion. The first point should state the letter grade; the remaining four should each address one specific feature—correct reasoning about the wire, a subtle mischaracterization of one view, the misclassification of the alternative theory, and the overall coherence—so that together they justify the grade. Do not include any bibliography, reading list, or citations.
