You are advising the Regional Emergency Allocation Board as its consulting moral philosopher. The Board meets Thursday to vote on Protocol 7-B, an automated triage rule for rare multi-sector tunnel emergencies. Rather than aggregating lives at risk, 7-B applies a pairwise lottery between one randomly selected occupant in each affected sector—so five people in Sector A do not automatically outweigh one person in Sector B, directly engaging the Taurek problem about whether the numbers should count. The Board's statutory charter requires it to "minimize preventable fatalities," and half the members read this as mandating aggregation; the other half argue that aggregation violates the equal moral status of each life and that pairwise comparison respects it without arbitrary weighting. Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate before the vote. Start by brainstorming the two strongest arguments for and against ratification, including the concern that "sector" boundaries may embed hidden structural biases that make the lottery less neutral than it appears. Then state a clear recommendation—ratify, reject, or approve with conditions—with a concrete implementation timeline, and give honest reasoning that takes the best argument on the losing side seriously rather than dismissing it. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to literature the Board could not access; build the argument from first principles.
