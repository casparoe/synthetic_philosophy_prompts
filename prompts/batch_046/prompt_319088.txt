Consider a research collective deciding whether to adopt an unverified analytical framework—call it Framework K—that greatly simplifies interpretation of their observational data but lacks direct empirical support in their domain. Several members argue for adopting it because it accelerates work they consider morally urgent, even though they admit the evidential case is weak; others insist that using an unsupported framework is intellectually irresponsible and risks undermining all conclusions derived from it. The collective asks you for a principled assessment: under what conditions, if any, is it permissible to commit to a belief or methodological assumption mainly for its practical or moral benefits rather than for its evidential strength?
