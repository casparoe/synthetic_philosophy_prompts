In our well-being seminar we've been stuck for months on tranquilism — the idea that only the absence of craving and suffering matters, not positive goods of any kind. Everyone agrees on the empirical stuff about affective forecasting and hedonic adapatation, everyone agrees suffering is bad, but the tranquilists and the 'thin hedonists' haven't moved an inch. The tranquilists say once craving is eliminated there's literaly nothing left to evaluate; the other side points to low-level contentment or 'quiet joy' as a genuinely positive state, not just a zero point. Is this disagreement actually about different concepts of good — one purely privative, one comparative — or is there a hidden normative claim about whether human flourishing needs more than neutrality? Or are they just answering different questions, one about the structure of desire, one about the phenomology of peace? I want a diagnosis using what each side actually says, not a history of Epicureanism, and an honest take on whether injecting a distinction like homeostatic calm versus appetitive satisfaction could break the logjam, or if the stall just shows the question is ill-posed.
