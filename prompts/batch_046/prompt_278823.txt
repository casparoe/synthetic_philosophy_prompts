i want someone to play it straight: what would actually count against this? not generic 'people died' since famine happens in stable societies too. i mean, which specific archeological or demographic patterns would genuinely break the inversion thesis? like, is there a difference between political collapse and full societal collapse that i'm glossing over? what about the abrupt halt in moai construction with half-carved statues everywhere - does that signal a real break, or just a shift in labor priorities? and if literacy disappears not just at the palace but among every social tier, including common traders and scribes, would that definately be fatal, or could i still explain it away?

i'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and i want to know which ones a committed inversion-theorist would have to concede versus which they'd dismiss. also, is this view well-insulated, and is that a problem or just intellectually safe?
