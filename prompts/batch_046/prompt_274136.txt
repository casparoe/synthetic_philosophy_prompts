ok so for my philosophy of life seminar i need to check wether my commitments actually cohere. here's roughly where i've landed:

1. i'm basically an absurdist - there's no cosmic blueprint, the universe doesn't care about our projects, and asking what the meaning of life is, as if it's hidden somewhere, is a category mistake.

2. but i still think some lives are genuinely more meaningful than others, and it isn't just subjective fulfillment. i reject teh rubber-band-collector case: if you're totally satisfied spending your life counting rubber bands, that still doesn't make it meaningful.

3. at the same time, i think standard objective-list theories miss the point. ticking off goods like knowledge, friendship, achievement doesn't explain why those add up to meaning rather than just welfare.

4. so my positive view is that meaning requires internal structural coherence: your projects have to form a chain where later ends justify earlier ones, and the chain can't bottom out in something arbitrary. if there's a terminal node - a project that only serves itself - the whole thing collapses into pointlessness.

5. i think Camus was right about rebellion, but wrong that building local purposes counts as philosophical suicide. i do it all the time and don't feel like i'm betraying the absurd.

6. finally, evolutionary debunking works against our intuition that life should have cosmic meaning, but it doesn't touch these local structurally-coherent projects, since those aren't products of some misguided teleological instinct.

does this hang together? specifcally: is (2) in tension with (1) since i deny cosmic purpose but affirm objective meaning? does (4) smuggle in a hidden teleology that (1) rejects? does my local purpose-building in (5) contradict (4)'s ban on terminal nodes, since every local project eventually ends? and is (6) selectively debunking - why hit the cosmic intuition but spare the coherence intuition? rank teh tensions by severity, say which ones are only apparent, and for each real tension say what'd have to give and what the cheapest fix costs.
