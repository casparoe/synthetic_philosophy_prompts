You serve on a university Committee for Digital Pedagogy and Intellectual Environment. The Center for Civic Technology has proposed deploying a "belief-ecosystem audit" within first-year seminar discussion forums. The system distinguishes epistemic bubbles—clusters characterized by homogeneous information exposure without active distrust of outsiders—from echo chambers—clusters in which members systematically discredit external sources as corrupt or illegitimate. For students identified in bubbles, the tool injects "calibrated counter-narratives": opposing arguments reconstructed entirely from the internal logic of the students' own stated premises, intended to expose internal tension without invoking outside authority. For students in echo chambers, the system withholds injected content and instead requires completion of a "source-trust mapping" exercise in which members must articulate the evidential standards they apply to insiders versus outsiders.

One faction of the committee holds that this is legitimate epistemic scaffolding: it corrects structural ignorance without indoctrination, respects autonomy by using the students' own reasoning rather than imposing external authority, and treats bubbles and chambers with appropriate differential responses. The opposing faction contends that dialectical calibration—the reconstruction of dissent from internal premises—is more manipulative than overt contradiction because it conceals its external origin; that requiring source-trust mapping in echo chambers violates the conditions for voluntary belief revision by forcing meta-cognitive exposure on groups defined by distrust; and that the university oversteps its mandate by architecting belief-formation environments.

Draft the committee's formal decision memo to the Center—approve, reject, or approve with conditions. State a clear actionable recommendation, ground your reasoning in the distinction between bubbles and chambers and in the specific mechanism of dialectical calibration, and include an honest, substantive treatment of the strongest consideration advanced by the side you do not adopt. The memo should be self-contained: do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not rely on named theories or authors outside the proposal's own terms.
