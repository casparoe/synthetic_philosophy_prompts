The way we do it is reviewers score three seperate things: is the design sound, is the info-value high, and does it match our charter. Then there's a logical rule that combines those into a yes/no funding call. But lately we've hit this pattern where a majority backs each individual premise, yet when you apply the rule the majority actually rejects funding. Some people want to ditch the premise breakdown and just vote on the final call directly; others say that's just hiding an inconsistency and we need a coherence constraint that forces reviewers to reconcile. There's also this half-baked idea about ranking the full logical space of answers instead of using yes/no nodes. I need a memo for our committee reccomending which aggregation rule to actually use, with an honest look at the best argument for the side that loses. Keep it concrete — I want the philosophical structure laid out, not just generic decision theory.
