I run a small rationalist-adjacent discussion group — usually 10 people, 90 minutes on a tuesday. I want a structured deliberation that makes us feel why "the other side doesn't argue in good faith" is usually a meta-ontological dispute wearing psychological clothes, not just an empirical claim about intentions. The niche setup: we run a fake policy debate about "predictive welfare allocation" (totally made-up, so nobody brings tribal priors). Half the room gets a hidden framing brief that defines good-faith argument in lightweight terms — coherence with shared procedural rules, nothing more. The other half gets a brief defining it in heavyweight terms — a real moral property arguments either possess or lack, like a kind of substance. They have to jointly evaluate whether a hypothetical opponent's subsidy proposal was offered in good faith. After 30 minutes I reveal the hidden briefs. I need rules and materials, a clear timeline, and a debrief that maps specific moments where they talked past each other onto the ontology-of-reasons layer. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time (I bet people blame the framing instead of seeing the meta-level), and be honest about what feature of real polarization this can't reproduce — probably the affective heat and identity investment, since we're all friends here. No lecture up front; they should hit the wall themselves before anyone names the problem.
