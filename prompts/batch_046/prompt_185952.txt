As a novelist preoccupied with characters who imagine they are reasoning their way out of the very conditions that make them think, I have been following a philosophical impasse about embedded agency that strikes me as structurally similar to a plot that refuses its own resolution. On one side stand what I will call the Scaffold Theorists: they maintain that any act of rational self-correction is always executed from within an existing cognitive architecture—language, affective framing, institutional habit, bodily disposition—so that an agent never occupies an Archimedean point from which to evaluate the scaffold itself. On the other side stand the Formalists, who grant full physical and social embeddedness but insist that certain structural properties of reasoning—logical consistency across contexts, coherence of commitments, the avoidance of pragmatic contradiction—function as non-circular normative standards precisely because they do not depend on the particular contents of any given scaffold. For years, both camps have conceded the empirical findings of situated cognition; both accept that reasoning is distributed, that agents are physically embedded, and that genuine self-modification occurs. Yet neither has moved an inch. The Scaffold Theorists treat every formalist appeal to consistency as a hidden smuggling-in of a decontextualized, privileged viewpoint; the Formalists treat every scaffold appeal to circularity as a confusion between the causal conditions of belief revision and the normative conditions of justification. What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is there a hidden empirical question—perhaps about whether genuinely representation-independent constraints exist—or is it a difference about what counts as a normative reason? Or are the two sides answering different questions—one about the etiology of belief change, the other about its justification—under the single heading of embedded agency? I am particularly drawn to whether the concept of autonomy is doing incompatible narrative work here: for the Scaffold Theorists it denotes an impossible escape from plot, for the Formalists it denotes a character's capacity to rewrite the rules of the story without leaving it. Given your diagnosis, is there any conceptual distinction—say, between first-order and second-order scaffolding, or between causal circularity and what we might call normative bootstrapping—that could actually move the debate, or should we conclude that the disagreement is bedrock, rooted in incompatible intuitions about what it means for an agent to be the author of its own actions? And if it is bedrock, does that tell us something about the question itself—whether it is ill-posed, or merely hard?
