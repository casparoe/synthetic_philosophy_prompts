I am putting together the conceptual framework for a graduate seminar on evaluative metaphysics, and my study group has reached an impasse over whether spectrum arguments—those that array cases along a graded continuum, from minimal sentience up to reflective agency or from trivial discomfort to extreme suffering—actually show that “better than” fails to be transitive, or whether they merely smuggle in hidden dimensional shifts. Rather than a standard literature survey, I need a diagnostic taxonomy that maps the logical space of positions on this exact question. The instrument should branch first by whether a respondent holds “better than” to be transitive within a single evaluative dimension (welfare, moral status, aesthetic merit, and so on), then by how they treat cross-dimensional comparisons. At each internal node, pose a concrete triplet case—A, B, and C arranged along a spectrum where A is judged better than B and B better than C, but the comparison between A and C is contested—phrased as a direct judgment question so that a respondent can answer without knowing which philosophical label the answer corresponds to. The leaves should include at least six named or theoretically specified stances: strict transitivity defenders, local intransitivity advocates within one dimension, dimensional pluralists, spectrum-skeptics who deny that the gradation is evaluatively uniform, global intransitivity theorists, and at least one further position of your own construction or identification. For every unoccupied combination in the logical space—say, a view that accepts local intransitivity but denies cross-dimensional comparison is ever meaningful—note whether the vacancy seems due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Finally, flag any pair of leaves that your taxonomy cannot cleanly separate on the basis of verdicts about cases, explaining whether the indistinguishability arises because the views differ only in meta-ethical framing or because they converge on all practical judgments despite diverging in theoretical rationale. Keep the taxonomy free of bibliographic references; I want the sorting to depend on conceptual commitments revealed through the cases themselves.
