I chair the Policy Review Subcommittee of the Regional Transit Equity Board. By next Friday we must circulate a formal recommendation on the proposed Dynamic Route Reallocation Protocol (DRRP) for our paratransit system, with a vote scheduled for the following Thursday. The staff report asserts that the DRRP constitutes a safe Pareto improvement: every currently registered rider either retains or improves upon her present expected wait time, no rider experiences an absolute increase, and system-wide efficiency gains are projected at roughly twelve percent. A dissenting bloc on the board challenges this conclusion. They argue that because the protocol reshuffles vehicle allocation in response to real-time demand, it generates endogenous demand shifts—riders in zones with previously long waits begin using the service once those waits fall—thereby altering the very baseline against which individual outcomes should be measured. On their view, the correct assessment is counterfactual: we must compare each rider's outcome under the DRRP not to her actual pre-implementation wait time, but to the wait time she would have faced under the legacy routing algorithm given the post-implementation demand distribution. Under that counterfactual comparison, some riders who register no actual change in wait time are arguably made worse off relative to the counterfactual baseline they would have occupied had the protocol never been adopted. The staff responds that institutional evaluation must compare actual states of affairs—pre-intervention versus post-intervention—not hypothetical worlds that never obtain, and that conditioning approval on counterfactual baseline stability would render any adaptive allocation system unapprovable.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. Recommend a concrete decision—approve the DRRP, reject it, or approve it with specified monitoring conditions and a review date—and justify it with reasoning the dissenting members can recognize as addressing their concern rather than ignoring it. In particular, explain whether the board should adopt an interventionist framework (comparing actual outcomes across the intervention) or a counterfactual framework (stabilizing the baseline against hypothetical demand shifts) for determining whether a change is genuinely "safe," and show how that choice determines the verdict on the protocol's Pareto status. The memo should include an explicit time frame for implementation or for a follow-up assessment. Do not request or include bibliographies, citations, or external reading lists; the argument should be self-contained.
