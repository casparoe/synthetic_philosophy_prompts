(1) In Parfit's hitchhiker case, rationality requires being disposed to pay the driver after rescue, even though the token act of paying is causally useless once you are already safe. (2) The justification is upstream causation: your present adoption of the disposition is what causes the driver to rescue you; the token payment is merely the execution of that prior upstream choice. (3) Generalize this "upstream rationality" principle: whenever a predictive mechanism links your current disposition to a prior benefit, rationality demands adopting the locally suboptimal token act that completes the disposition, regardless of token-level dominance. (4) Apply it to Newcomb's problem: the predictor's past filling of the box is upstream of your present choice, so you should one-box, and the two-boxer's dominance argument mistakes token-level reasoning for upstream reasoning. (5) Now extend the principle consistently: for any ordinary decision, there is always some counterfactual observer who might have acted in your favor had they predicted you would take option A rather than B; even when no actual predictor exists, the logical structure of upstream dependence remains available. (6) Therefore, rational agents should never apply local dominance or expected-utility reasoning at the token level, but should always choose the option that would be selected for by the maximally favorable counterfactual predictor. (7) This proves too much: it dissolves practical reasoning into an infinite regress of hypothetical predictions and makes rational choice depend on observers who do not exist. (8) So the hitchhiker reasoning must overgeneralize somewhere.
