I am currently taking an advanced undergraduate course in twentieth-century political thought, and we have been examining Hannah Arendt's reflections on factual truth, political lying, and the distinctive vulnerability of contingent reality to organised deception. Arendt distinguishes between the traditional political lie, which conceals particular facts, and the modern lie that seeks to destroy the very sense of reality by substituting factual records with a coherent fictional world; she also warns, particularly in her analysis of the Pentagon Papers, that systemic self-deception among bureaucratic problem solvers can be more corrosive than deliberate mendacity because it obliterates the distinction between truth and falsehood within the deceiver's own mind. Would she be inclined to interpret this as an extreme form of the strategic modern lie, or rather as something closer to the self-deceptive bureaucratic logic she associated with the Pentagon Papers, precisely because the fabrication is automated, decentralised, and therefore detached from any individual will to deceive?

Please answer in British English, and do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I am interested solely in the conceptual reconstruction of her likely position and in whether the distinction between deliberate lying and systemic self-deception still holds — or collapses — once deception is mediated by generative systems rather than by human actors alone.
