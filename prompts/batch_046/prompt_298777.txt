Write an essay examining whether an automated exchange protocol that algorithmically enforces labor-proportional pricing and prohibits all interest-bearing lending and speculative accumulation can be reconciled with Aristotle's distinction between natural household management (oikonomia) and unnatural money-making (chrematistike). In Politics I, Aristotle holds that money (nomisma) was invented by convention to facilitate exchange of necessities, yet he condemns usury (tokos) on the grounds that currency is barren and should not reproduce itself; he likewise regards retail trade as unnatural when it pursues unlimited gain rather than household sufficiency. Consider this tension: if a digital system removes the human desire for unlimited wealth by mechanically fixing exchange ratios and forbidding any holding of value for its own sake, has it restored exchange to its natural purpose, or has it merely perfected chrematistike by reducing goods to pure exchange-value abstracted from use? Weigh both interpretations carefully, paying particular attention to whether Aristotle's critique is fundamentally about the psychological motive of greed or about the structural separation of exchange-value from use-value. Reach and defend a clear verdict—stating whether such a protocol belongs to oikonomia or to chrematistike—and justify that conclusion with direct reference to Aristotle's underlying principles. Present your response as continuous prose only: no headings, bullet points, bold text, tables, or other formatting, as one writes in a formal letter.
