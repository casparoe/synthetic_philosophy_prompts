Write a pantoum on the nonidentity problem, using the form’s repeating and interlocking lines to enact the counterfactual dependency that defines it: each stanza should take up lines from the previous one in a new order, so that by the final stanza the “same” phrases have become different people, mirroring how a changed prior choice produces an entirely different chain of existence rather than a harmed version of the same individual. Center the poem on the young mother who chooses to have a child now rather than wait, and let the repetition carry the paradox that the child born into difficulty has no ground for complaint—since waiting would have meant never being—while still leaving space for the intuition that something is wrong. Structure any turn around the moment the speaker recognizes that the absence of a victim does not erase the wrongness of the choice. Keep it precise enough that someone familiar with Parfit’s argument recognizes the logic, and let the form do philosophical work rather than merely decorate it.
