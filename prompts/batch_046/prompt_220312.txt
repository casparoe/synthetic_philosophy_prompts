You are an applied ethicist with experience operationalizing welfare-theoretic principles for institutional use. Before drafting the procedure, briefly list the three conceptual failure modes that occur when practitioners apply lexical ordering across incommensurable mental-state dimensions.

I sit on a regional mental-health funding board that reviews intervention proposals monthly, with no access to external literature and no philosophical consultants available. We need a reusable decision procedure grounded in lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism, modified by a framing I have been developing: pleasure and pain are not opposite poles of a single cardinal axis but belong to qualitatively distinct dimensions, and suffering must be classified as either integrated (capable of being narratively borne by the subject) or unintegrated (structurally unabsorbable, such that no meaning-making can incorporate it). The principle holds that reducing unintegrated suffering has absolute lexical priority over any gain in pleasure; only once no funded proposal could reduce unintegrated suffering may the board consider pleasure-promoting projects, and cross-dimensional aggregation is always illegitimate.

Please construct the operational procedure: the ordered questions to ask in sequence, what evidentiary standard counts as a sufficient answer to each, the explicit stop conditions that return "do not fund" or "defer to values judgment," the authority required for sign-off, and the precise points where judgment cannot be eliminated by calculation. Then apply it to two proposals: (1) a psychiatric intervention that eliminates all unintegrated suffering in 200 patients but permanently removes their capacity for intense positive affect, leaving them affectively flat; (2) a community wellness program that produces moderate, durable happiness gains for 50,000 residents but leaves a small ineligible cohort's unintegrated suffering completely untouched.

Include one outcome the board will find unwelcome, and explicitly state two structural limitations—questions or trade-offs the procedure is designed not to resolve. Think carefully about where incommensurability blocks arithmetic and where the integrated/unintegrated boundary is genuinely a judgment call rather than a diagnostic fact. Keep the framing formal and precise.
