I'm writing my dissertation on the metaphysics of infinite processes in physical systems, and last week, while discussing Thomson's lamp with a colleague in an ethics-of-technology seminar, I began wondering whether the logical structure of a supertask—specifically, the problem of assigning a definite state at the limit of an infinite discrete sequence—might illuminate normative puzzles as well as physical ones. For an interdisciplinary workshop I'm organizing, I'd like a short story that takes this conceptual fusion seriously rather than treating it as mere ornament.

Please write a short story—roughly 800 to 1200 words—centered on an experimental apparatus I will call the Ethical Toggle. The device is engineered to resolve a continuous moral gradient: every two minutes it makes a discrete choice between two competing ethical frameworks (for instance, a rigid deontological constraint versus a consequentialist optimization), then reverses that choice at intervals that halve each time, so that by the two-minute mark it has completed an infinite sequence of toggles. At that limit point, the system is meant to deliver a single, unified moral judgment about a specific patient enrolled in an urgent clinical trial. Your protagonist should be the philosopher-engineer who designed the device, and the narrative should explore what happens—conceptually and interpersonally—when the apparatus reaches its temporal limit without having settled into any definite normative state. In particular, I want the story to engage the specific worry that, just as Thomson's lamp leaves the bulb either on or off without any causal mechanism determining the transition, the Ethical Toggle might leave the patient's moral status genuinely undefined in a way that collapses the very possibility of ethical closure. You are free to invent the institutional setting, the protagonist's personal stakes, and the precise clinical dilemma, but please avoid generic science-fiction tropes such as rogue AI rebellion; keep the tension rooted in the paradox of supertask completion itself, letting the human drama emerge from the conceptual indeterminacy rather than from external catastrophe.
