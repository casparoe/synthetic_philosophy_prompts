Consider the judgments of a philosopher of mind who distinguishes mechanical sphexishness, behavioral flexibility, and free will across agents. She holds that the digger wasp Sphex—which repeats its burrow-inspection ritual even when the paralyzed prey is relocated—is sphexish; that a sleepwalker who navigates around obstacles but cannot respond to speech is flexible in movement yet sphexish in cognition; that a thermostat adjusting to ambient temperature is flexible but lacks free will; that an OCD patient who checks a locked door twenty times despite knowing it is locked is sphexish within that specific loop alone; and that a slime mold solving a maze by extending and retracting pseudopods is flexible and not sphexish. She further maintains that free will requires not merely feedback-sensitive adjustment but the capacity to revise one's own governing goals, not only one's tactics.

What verdict would she render on a current large language model that uses chain-of-thought reasoning to detect internal contradictions, backtracks to correct them, yet does so entirely within fixed transformer weights, a fixed next-token-prediction objective, and a context window that is cleared between sessions? Would she class it as sphexish, flexible, or free?

I am less interested in the specific answer than in the precise rule she appears to be applying, in the alternative rules that accommodate her five earlier verdicts equally well, and in which further case would best adjudicate between them—particularly a hypothetical "reflexively rigid" agent that updates all of its beliefs and sub-routines freely but is governed by one immutable meta-rule that forbids any revision to its own meta-rules. Provide your best-guess classification of the language model with supporting reasons, articulate the competing rules, and name the distinguishing case.
