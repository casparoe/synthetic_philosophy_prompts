hey, I'm trying to figure out if qualia is even a coherent idea, not just whether they exist. Here are my gut calls on some cases:

1. Inverted spectrum: two people with identical brain states and behvior, one sees red where the other sees green. I say that's conceptually posible — qualitative character can come apart from functional role.

2. The zombie twin: physically identical but insists seeing red has no subjective feel whatsoever. I think that's incoherent, not empirically false. If all the functional story is there, the denial doesn't make sense.

3. Synesthetic taste: someone insists 7 literally tastes like cinnamon, not just that she thinks of cinnamon. I think she's describing a real qualitative feature, just an unusual one.

4. The fade-out: replace neurons one by one with silicon preserving every computation; at 51% she reports redness is gone but behvior unchanged. I think that's conceptually imposible — there's no gap for the quality to fall through.

So inversion is okay, zombie denial incoherent, synesthesia real, fade-out impossible. Is there a principle that gets all four, or which verdict do I have to drop? And what's your take on the bridge case I keep going back and forth on — two people linked by direct cortical interface share one visual stream, both report crimson, but neither can say whose experience it is?
