I work as a mediator for tenant-landlord disputes in a city with really old rent-control laws, and I run maybe 4-5 sessions a week where people are fighting over repairs, renewals, or eviction notices. Lately I've noticed a weird pattern where one side clearly doesnt care about the material fix — they want the other party to 'see' them as a legitimate equal, not just win money or stay in the apartment. I read something about Hegels master-slave dialectic and recognition and it kinda clicked, but I cant carry a philosophy book into a 90-minute session.

Can you turn that dialectic into an actual step-by-step procedure I can run during mediation? I need: the questions to ask each party in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure stops with 'this is values not facts,' and specifically how to spot when the conflict has shifted from a material dispute into a pure struggle for recognition where neither side can back down without feeling destroyed. Also mark the points where I just have to use my own judgment because the theory runs out.

Then run it on two real-feeling cases: first, a tenant who keeps rejecting every repair offer and keeps saying the landlord needs to apologize for 'treating me like I'm nothing,' and second, a landlord who is technically in the right about a lease ending but keeps showing up to 'inspect' the unit long after they should have moved on. I need the procedure to tell me what to do in the second case even if I dont like the answer — like if it says the landlord is also trapped in the recognition struggle and I should just end the session. Also say clearly what this procedure cant do, especially with parties who are actually just using recognition-language to cover up a power grab.
