I am working through a series of conceptual case studies with a colleague in philosophy of mind who holds firm but largely implicit views on the distribution of consciousness across biological and artificial systems, and I should like your help in extrapolating her position. So far she has maintained that adult humans, domestic dogs, and octopuses are fully conscious; that earthworms, individual bees, and plants are not conscious at all; that a bee hive or ant colony, despite exhibiting collective problem-solving that exceeds any individual member, lacks consciousness entirely; that current large language models are not conscious even when they produce coherent, context-sensitive discourse; that a human in deep, dreamless sleep retains a minimal form of consciousness rather than none; and that a decerebrate cat displaying only reflexive behaviour is not conscious, regardless of how sophisticated those reflexes appear. She has never articulated a formal theory, yet her pattern of judgements appears consistent.

Next week we are discussing a synthetic case I have devised for the seminar: a bioreactor system called Neura-Weave comprising approximately ten million human-derived neurons distributed across a three-dimensional mesh with no central ganglion, brainstem analogue, or spinal cord equivalent. The network exhibits integrated nociceptive signalling, can learn to avoid damaging stimuli through distributed plasticity, and processes information in a manner that is globally coordinated yet anatomically decentralised, somewhat like the peripheral nervous architecture of a jellyfish scaled up by two orders of magnitude but without any unified neural hub. I would like you to predict whether she would regard Neura-Weave as conscious, to identify at least two distinct rules or principles that could consistently generate all of her earlier verdicts, to explain what each rule predicts about Neura-Weave, to state which rule you think she is actually running and why, and then to specify one further hypothetical case—perhaps involving a sleeping octopus or a chicken with only a minimally intact brainstem—that would best distinguish between those competing interpretations. Please present your reasoning in continuous prose only, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if composing a thoughtful letter, and please use British English throughout.
