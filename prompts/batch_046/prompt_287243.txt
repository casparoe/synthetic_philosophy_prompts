In debates about the gender-equality paradox — that highly egalitarian societies often exhibit larger gender gaps in occupational choice — a colleague repeatedly invokes the slogan “equality of opportunity reveals true preference” to claim that Nordic welfare structures have not failed feminism but have merely stripped away artificial constraints, allowing natural dispositions to surface. Before I respond in a seminar, I need a precise disambiguation. Can you lay out the distinct theses that slogan could express, arranged as a menu? For each reading, specify what “equality of opportunity” denotes (formal legal parity, material resource equality, or cultural de-stigmatization of gendered roles?), what “true preference” amounts to (biologically fixed, developmentally canalized, or rationally autonomous?), and whether that version actually explains the observed cross-national pattern or merely redescribes it. Also indicate which readings implicitly depend on a contrast with “false preference” produced by patriarchal distortion, and whether applying that contrast symmetrically to men’s career choices undermines it. Finally, say which version your interlocutor most likely intends, and which ones genuinely support her conclusion that the paradox is compatible with justice.
