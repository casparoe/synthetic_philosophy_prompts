Next thursday I present in our metaphysics grad seminar on why Black's spheres don't actually break Leibniz's identity of indescernibles. I'm not using the standard weak-discernibility reply — I think that concedes too much relational ground. My claim is that once you treat the spheres as massive bodies in an otherwise empty universe, GR gives you a center-of-mass frame and distinct curvature signatures, so they're strongly discernible even with no external markers. The room has someone who wrote her diss on weak discernibility, a structural realist who thinks relations are ontologically prior, and a few grad students who just read Black and are still emotionally attached to the intuition that the spheres share every qualitative property. Give me the objections in the order they'll hit, phrased like they'd actually say them, with what I should concede, what I should hold, and the weakest spot in my position that I need to defend rather than hope they miss. And include the one objection I'm hoping nobody brings up.
