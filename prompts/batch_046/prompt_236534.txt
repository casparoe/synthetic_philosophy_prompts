As a novelist, I think in terms of dramatic irony, free indirect discourse, narrative distance, the temporal architecture of plot—analepsis and prolepsis—and the economy of reader attention rather than in philosophical or economic terminology. Restate the debate between the classical rationalist denial of weakness of will, according to which genuine knowledge of the good cannot coexist with action against it, and the hyperbolic discounting model of intertemporal choice with present-biased preference reversal, translating both into the vocabulary of narrative craft. Use specifically: unreliable narration as a model of self-deception, character-arc coherence across time, proleptic regret—by which I mean a figure’s anticipation of future narrative judgment that reshapes present desire—and the way fiction distributes sympathy and moral assessment through shifts in narrative distance. For each position, report what survives the translation cleanly, what requires distortion, and what has no counterpart whatsoever in how fiction represents motivation across time. Then assess whether the residue points to a normative structure of practical reason that narrative form genuinely cannot capture, or whether it reveals the philosophical and economic accounts as technically ornate descriptions of phenomena that fiction already handles more precisely without their vocabulary. I want an honest residue report, not an analogy that flatters either side.
