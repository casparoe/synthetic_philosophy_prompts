Here is my position: (1) suffering possesses lexical priority—once it crosses a certain qualitative intensity, no quantity of pleasure can morally justify permitting it; (2) pleasure and pain are phenomenologically incommensurable, lacking any shared metric that allows direct weighing; (3) in ordinary practical reasoning, we rightly accept small discomforts for moderate satisfactions; (4) a credible moral theory must preserve the narrative coherence of a human life, honoring qualitative distinctions that structure lived experience; and (5) our revulsion at cases like torture for trivial amusement is best explained quantitatively—the magnitude gap is overwhelming—rather than by assigning the goods to separate moral categories. Which pairs genuinely conflict? Meanwhile, (3) might look like it undermines (1), but I think that conflict may dissolve once the threshold is properly specified. Rank the actual tensions by severity, identify which apparent conflict dissolves on inspection, specify the cheapest revision for each genuine tension, and tell me which single change would leave the framework most intact for fictional use.
