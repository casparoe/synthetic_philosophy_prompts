The standard analysis of valid consent identifies three invalidating conditions: deception, coercion, and incompetence. I want you to argue that this tripartite framework is incomplete because it omits a fourth, independent invalidator—call it "structural framing distortion"—in which a consenting party retains complete factual accuracy, faces no coercive pressure, and meets every standard competence criterion, yet is guided into agreement through a deliberate reconfiguration of the conceptual categories by which they evaluate harm and benefit. You should defend the claim that this distortion is irreducible to deception (no false propositions are offered), irreducible to coercion (no threat, force, or penalty is applied), and irreducible to incompetence (the agent's reasoning capacities remain fully intact). Illustrate your argument with the following hypothetical: a clinical research team discloses a drug's risks using only precise statistical language—"a 0.3 percent incidence of irreversible neurological deficit"—presented in abstract, decontextualized formats, knowing that participants will process the information through a quantitative risk frame rather than through the embodied, qualitative framework they ordinarily use to evaluate bodily harm; consequently, participants consent to a procedure they would have refused had the identical facts been framed within their ordinary experiential vocabulary. Argue that this constitutes a distinct failure of consent, and specify what institutional safeguards could address it without collapsing the analysis back into deception, coercion, or incompetence.
