hey, i'm writing up my commitments for my collective action seminar and want a consistency check before i present. here's where i stand:

1. i take a counterfactual-dependence view of moral responsibility for collective harms: i'm only actually blameworthy for an outcome if it wouldn't have occured without my specific contribution. if the harm is redundant with respect to me, i'm causally off the hook.

2. i apply this consistently. my vote in a national election is redundant (never pivotal), my flight emissions are redundant (plane takes off regardless), and my meat purchase is redundant (supply chain is too buffered for one burger to shift production).

3. so i think the inefficacy objection succeeds in all three domains: there's no direct harm-based duty to vote, fly less, or stop eating meat.

4. BUT i still think there's a strict moral duty to vote. not because of outcomes, but because democratic legitimacy depends on participation—the authority of the state derives from the consent/participation of the governed, so abstaining undermines the institutional framework itself.

5. i reject applying that same institutional-legitimacy reasoning to climate or diet. there's no comparable "climate governance" or "food-system legitimacy" that requires my personal carbon reduction or vegetarianism to maintain.

6. i reject purely expressive, symbolic, or virtue-based reasons as bases for moral obligation across the board. if it's not harm-prevention or institutional-structure, it's not a real duty, just preference or aesthetics.

7. so factory farming is wrong, but since my abstaining doesn't reduce harm (redundant per #1) and there's no institutional duty to abstain (#5), i don't think i have a moral obligation to be vegetarian.

do these actually hang together? i have a nagging feeling some pull against each other but i can't tell which tensions are real and which just dissolve once you distinguish harm-based from institutional reasons properly. can you audit the set—rank tensions by severity, flag which ones are only apparent, and say what the least costly revision is and what it costs? please don't treat everything as equally bad; i need to know what's actually fatal vs. surface-level.
