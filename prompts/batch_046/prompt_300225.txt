In my recent reporting on the centenary reassessments of the First World War, I have become preoccupied with a framing that hovers between Fritz Fischer's intentionalist thesis and Christopher Clark's sleepwalker narrative without fully coinciding with either. I am calling it, provisionally, the institutional sleepwalker thesis: the claim that primary moral and causal responsibility for the outbreak in 1914 attaches not to a unified German national will—there was never a cabinet decision for a general European war—but to the bureaucratic-military apparatus of the German state, which possessed an emergent institutional intentionality that made strategic escalation the path of least resistance, while comparable dynamics in the Entente powers lacked equivalent structural momentum. This avoids both Fischer's dependence on a deliberate grab for world power and Clark's tendency to dissolve blame into a diffuse, systemic accident in which every capital was equally culpable.

Before I adopt this framing in a longer feature, I want an honest map of its vulnerabilities. What precise kinds of findings or arguments would count against it, and which would be merely awkward? For instance, would the discovery of an explicit pre-war memorandum by the German leadership endorsing a continental war refute the emergent-institutional component, or could defenders absorb it as only one surface expression of a deeper bureaucratic logic? Conversely, if historians demonstrated that Austro-Hungarian or Russian foreign ministries operated with identical escalation dynamics, would that undermine the claim of German primary responsibility, or merely redistribute institutional culpability across multiple actors? I am also concerned with conceptual defeaters: arguments that modern nation-states cannot possess the kind of collective intentionality required for moral blame, or that attributing responsibility to an institutional structure collapses into a category error by treating organizational processes as if they were individual moral agents. Please distinguish between objections that would be decisive and those that the institutional sleepwalker view could accommodate through qualification, and indicate whether the position risks anything substantial or has been insulated beyond repair.
