A non-cognitivist philosopher, Dr. K., has given four consistent rulings on how moral sentences embed: (1) bare "Harming animals is wrong" expresses direct disapproval; (2) its negation expresses direct approval; (3) the material conditional "If harming animals is wrong, then factory farming is wrong" expresses rejection of the paired attitudes (disapproving harm + approving farming); (4) the disjunction "Either harming is wrong or cosmetic testing is wrong" expresses commitment to disapproving at least one, unspecified. She has not addressed modal or syllogistic contexts. Now consider the sentence "It is practically required that, if harming animals is wrong, then boycotting is obligatory," used as the major premise in a practical syllogism whose conclusion is the action-guiding prescription "Boycott." Predict Dr. K.’s ruling: does this express one unified attitude, and what is its structure? Identify the two most plausible general rules that jointly fit rulings (1)–(4), present in a table what each predicts for this new syllogistic embedding, name the closest analog among (1)–(4), state your best-guess ruling with reasons, and specify one further non-modal, non-epistemic embedding that would discriminate the rules. Answer in at most 350 words; begin with a one-sentence bottom line giving your recommended ruling and the closest analog.
