I'm trying to settle the agenda for a small philosophy-of-social-science working group with about eighteen months of focused meeting time and no empirical data-collection budget. We're specifically interested in collective intentionality and joint action, but not in rehearsing the familiar taxonomy of shared cooperative activities, plural subjects, and we-intentions. Instead, I want to know which currently open conceptual lines actually deserve sustained attention right now, and in what order we should pursue them.

To make this concrete, I'm injecting one niche framing that I think is underexplored: the problem of sustained joint action in conditions of "structural opacity," where participants understand the collective goal differently from one another—sometimes radically so—yet continue to coordinate without resolving those descriptive disagreements. Think of long-running institutions or social movements where members share practices but not a unified conception of what they are collectively doing.

Against that background, please do the following. First, identify what you take to be the three or four genuinely open conceptual questions in collective intentionality and joint action that are neither settled nor purely empirical. Second, rank them for our specific situation: which should come first, second, and so on, given that we have philosophers with training in logic and action theory but no lab access, and that our end goal is to produce conceptual tools that could eventually inform institutional design or legal theory rather than just taxonomy. For each ranking, defend the ordering with explicit criteria—tractability for conceptual work alone, how upstream the answer is for other debates, and what kind of argument or conceptual development over the next year or two would cause you to revise the rank. Third, address specifically whether the structural-opacity problem I mentioned is a distinct open question worth its own line of work, or whether it collapses into one of the others, and what that implies for our agenda.

Please present this entirely as continuous prose, in the manner of a formal letter or extended email. Do not use headings, bullet points, bold text, tables, or any other visual formatting. I want a single flowing argument with the priorities embedded and defended in sequence, not a list disguised as paragraphs.
