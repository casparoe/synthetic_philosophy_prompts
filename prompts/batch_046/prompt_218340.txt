For a senior undergraduate seminar in epistemology (students have read selections from Wittgenstein's On Certainty and one assigned secondary treatment of hinge epistemology), design a single take-home essay examination prompt of approximately 1,500 words, with a 72-hour window and 35 marks. The prompt must not be answerable by summarizing the riverbed metaphor or listing familiar hinge propositions. Instead, embed the following specific conceptual framing: treat hinge commitments as "structural epistemic bearings"—framework conditions that do not themselves bear empirical weight but determine which local beliefs can be challenged without collapsing the framework. Ask students to evaluate whether this "structural bearing" framing clarifies or obscures Wittgenstein's distinction between doubts that are meaningful within a framework and doubts that are meaningless because they attack the hinges themselves. Require analysis of two constructed scenarios: (a) a physicist who temporarily doubts a single calibration constant within an ongoing experiment, and (b) a revisionist historian who doubts the general reliability of documentary evidence across an entire archival tradition. The model answer must make three specific philosophical moves: (1) articulate the difference between a hinge and an ordinary empirical belief by reference to functional role in grounding doubt rather than by propositional content; (2) apply the structural-bearing metaphor to both scenarios, showing precisely why (a) leaves hinges intact while (b) threatens them; and (3) address the normative status of hinges—whether they are justified, exempt from justification, or require a distinct kind of entitlement—without reducing them to Quinean central nodes in a web of belief. Provide a marking scheme that assigns marks to each move (for example, 10 marks for the functional-role analysis, 10 for the paired scenario application, 10 for the normative analysis, and 5 for coherence and precision), and list the specific errors that must cost marks: conflating hinges with highly certain empirical beliefs; treating hinges as empirically falsifiable; importing the Quinean web-of-belief model without distinguishing it from Wittgenstein's framework; and addressing only one of the two scenarios. The exam prompt itself should not request a bibliography, reading list, or citations; it should be answerable through conceptual analysis of the assigned texts and the framing provided above.
