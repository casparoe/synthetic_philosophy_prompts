Since my father's recent death, I have been trying to understand whether my own feeling of being sustained by something I cannot name counts as religious belief in Wittgenstein's sense, and I have been tracking how my philosophy tutor applies his framework to concrete cases.

Her verdicts so far are consistent: she holds that someone who expects bodily resurrection to be confirmed by future archaeology is not expressing religious belief but a scientific confusion; that someone who uses "God" only as a metaphor for cosmic order, with no emotional grip, is speaking outside the relevant language-game; that someone who lives within imagery of judgment and redemption, admits these are not empirically verifiable, and finds them indispensable to understanding suffering, represents the paradigm case; that someone who reports intense mystical feelings but dismisses them as purely subjective brain states and makes no practical changes is reducing the picture to psychology; and that someone who uses religious language exclusively to motivate social justice, rejecting any non-moral content, has collapsed faith into ethics.

Now consider my friend, who after a severe illness describes feeling "held by something infinite." She rejects institutional religion, denies this implies a personal God or an afterlife, and says it is "almost certainly neurochemistry." Yet she has begun forgiving old enemies, giving away substantial income, and insists she could not return to her prior orientation, even while allowing that the feeling might be false.

Predict my tutor's verdict. Identify the implicit rule—whether it turns on participation in a religious language-game without reduction to other domains, or on the practical regulation of life by a non-empirical picture, or something else—and note which other rules fit her track record equally well. Then specify the further case that would best distinguish those rules.
