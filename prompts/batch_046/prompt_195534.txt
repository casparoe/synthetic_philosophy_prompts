If Ludwig Wittgenstein were alive today and observing the widespread phenomenon of individuals constructing personalized, algorithmically curated “spiritual” identities through social media—where fragments of religious language, such as prayers, blessings, and liturgical phrases, are extracted from their traditional communal and ritual settings and redeployed primarily as aesthetic markers of personal wellness and self-branding—how might he analyze this practice in light of his distinction between religious belief as a propositional claim and religious belief as embedded within a form of life? Specifically, address whether he would likely regard these disembedded linguistic practices as a corrupted but recognizable language-game, as the emergence of an entirely new form of religious life with its own internal grammar, or as failing to constitute religious belief altogether precisely because they lack the practical, communal, and ritual context that, on his view, gives religious utterances their meaning.
