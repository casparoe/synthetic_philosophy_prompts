Write an essay on whether dissolving the hard problem of consciousness also dissolves the meta-problem—the question of why we find phenomenal experience inexplicable in physical terms. Some philosophers treat the two as a package: once we explain how neural processes generate experience, our sense of an explanatory gap should vanish. Others maintain that the meta-problem is autonomous, because a complete neurobiological story might still leave intact the intuition that something remains unexplained. To test these views, use the parallel case of color phenomenology: we have detailed physical accounts of wavelength discrimination and opponent-process coding, yet some philosophers argue that an "explanatory residue" remains about what it is like to see red rather than green. Ask whether consciousness is genuinely unique in generating this residue, or whether the meta-problem is just one instance of a broader pattern in which successful science leaves behind a felt sense of unexplained phenomenality. Reach and defend a definite conclusion—either that solving the hard problem necessarily resolves the meta-problem, or that the meta-problem survives and demands its own independent account, perhaps at the level of our conceptual framework rather than brain science. Give a committed response: take a clear stand, support it with explicit reasons, and avoid ending on a non-committal survey of options.
