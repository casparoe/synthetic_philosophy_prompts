Longtime LessWrong and rationalist-blog reader here. In a recent thread on generative AI and value alignment, a commenter invoked the slogan 'all art is propaganda' — usually traced back to W. E. B. Du Bois — to argue that diffusion models can never produce aesthetically neutral outputs. The reasoning went: since every training corpus embeds a social vision, the model's images are always propagandistic; therefore attempts to 'debias' them are just substituting one propaganda for another, and talk of neutral aesthetic evaluation is incoherent. Before I bring this to an upper-level philosophy of art seminar (about fifteen students, one ninety-minute session), I want the slogan precisely disambiguated. What are the distinct logical and normative claims that 'all art is propaganda' could be expressing, arranged from the trivial (art communicates values) through the substantive but defensible (aesthetic form is inseparable from social function) to the radical (all artistic merit reduces to ideological efficacy)? For each reading, indicate whether it is actually supported by Du Bois's own view that Black art should advance racial uplift, whether it is false or misleading on its own, and — crucially — whether it actually licenses the conclusion that debiasing generative models is conceptually impossible rather than merely politically contested. Present the readings as a menu I can distribute, with brief verdicts on which ones do the argumentative work the commenter needed and which ones collapse under scrutiny.
