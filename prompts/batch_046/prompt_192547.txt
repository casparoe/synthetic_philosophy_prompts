I keep running into this argument shape in philosphy of science and religion and I don't know if it has its own name. In both cases the person denies there's any real causal power in the observed relation, appeals to some deeper regularity (natural selection, or divine habit, or lawlike consistency) to explain why the pattern holds, and then uses that same pattern for practical reasoning without treating the first term as genuinely producing the second. Is there a specific concept for this exact argumentative posture, where you reject intrisic causation but preserve practical inference by outsourcing the reliability to something outside the chain?
