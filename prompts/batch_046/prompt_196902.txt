In our philosophy of mind course we have been examining Ned Block's distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, alongside global workspace theories that identify conscious states with information globally broadcast to cognitive consumer systems. I want to compare these two frameworks with respect to a hypothetical intermediate architecture—call it "fragmented broadcasting"—in which sensory information is distributed to multiple subpersonal modules (affective evaluation, motor priming, working-memory buffers) but never integrated into a unified narrative available for verbal report or rational deliberation. From Block's perspective, does this architecture possess access consciousness without phenomenal consciousness, phenomenal consciousness without access, or some hybrid? From a global workspace perspective, is the information genuinely globally available if it lacks the integrative coherence typically required for report? Which framework—Block's strict dichotomy or the broadcasting model—better accommodates this intermediate case, and does the case show that the two theories are describing different phenomena rather than competing explanations of one? Please justify your answer by analyzing whether global availability must include narrative integration to count as access consciousness.
