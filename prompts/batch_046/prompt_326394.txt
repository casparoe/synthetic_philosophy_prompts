In a seminar on Braudel’s longue durée, a classmate dismissed my claim that Philip II’s fiscal choices accelerated Spanish decline by saying, “Geography is destiny — climate and soil set the pace, and individuals just ride the wave.” I want to know what that slogan is actually committing him to. Please lay out the distinct precise theses “geography is destiny” could stand for in this kind of explanation — for example, hard determinism of social outcomes, probabilistic bias in long-run development, structural constraint that renders certain policies impossible, or mere methodological priority of slow variables over events — and for each say whether it is trivial, defensible, false, or misapplied to this case. Then identify which reading he actually needs for his dismissal of fiscal policy to go through, whether that reading is consistent with Braudel’s own account of events versus structures, and take a definite position on whether geographic or climatic factors should “win” over documented policy failures when the two conflict in explaining a specific historical decline. Give me a committed answer with reasons, not a menu of options for me to choose from.
