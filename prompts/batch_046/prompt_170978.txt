My decision-theory instructor’s verdicts on diachronic rationality and money-pump vulnerability have run as follows: a student who ranks A above B, B above C, and C above A within a single evaluation session is irrational and exploitable; a hyperbolic discounter who prefers $100 now to $110 in a month yet $110 in thirteen months to $100 in twelve months, without any commitment device, is irrational and vulnerable to being pumped; the same discounter, if she binds her future choices in advance, is fully rational; an agent with stable, acyclic preferences who is tricked into a loop of trades by false descriptions of the goods is rational, because her preference ordering itself never fails; and an agent whose preferences shift solely because she acquires new information about her future tastes, updating her current rankings accordingly, is rational even when her past and present rankings conflict.

Next case: an agent’s preferences are acyclic within every mental state she occupies, but those states themselves cycle in a predictable order—rest, stress, recovery. In each state she prefers to exchange her current holding for the holding appropriate to the next state, yet she never prefers skipping ahead or returning to start. An external party moves her through the full cycle and back to the beginning, collecting a small fee at each transition, so she ends where she started minus the accumulated fees. She never anticipates the loop, and at no point does she hold a cyclic ranking.

Predict her verdict: is this agent irrational, and is she vulnerable to a money pump? Identify the two or three implicit rules her earlier rulings could be running—distinguishing, for instance, rules that require a static preference cycle, rules that require only an exploitable diachronic loop regardless of local consistency, and rules that make vulnerability depend on the agent’s failure to anticipate or precommit. Give your best guess at which rule she actually holds, with the reasoning. Then name the single further case that would best separate the rules you have identified.
