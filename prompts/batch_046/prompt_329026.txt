I'm drafting notes for an essay in my philosophy of science seminar, which this term is focusing on causal explanation and its applications outside traditional science. We have covered the standard comparative literature—Pearl's interventionist framework and the counterfactual-dependency tradition associated with Lewis and Stalnaker—but I want to test a more specific claim that I have not seen articulated in exactly this form.

My working thesis is that the interventionist and counterfactual programs do not merely propose different formalisms for the same causal judgments; rather, they implicitly recommend divergent evaluation protocols when we assess artificial systems. To make this concrete, consider how one might evaluate whether a language model's refusal behavior is causally robust. A counterfactual evaluation asks whether the refusal persists in nearby possible worlds where the prompt is perturbed in minor, epistemically accessible ways. An interventionist evaluation asks whether applying a surgical intervention—say, disabling the safety-filter module—produces harmful outputs, regardless of what happens in nearby worlds.

I suggest that these two evaluations can come apart sharply when a system exhibits high context-sensitivity but low modular isolability: its behavior may be stable across counterfactual perturbations because the perturbations do not reach the relevant internal pathways, yet an intervention on a single module may fail to isolate the causal contribution of that module because of dense interconnection. I call this the evaluation-protocol divergence thesis. If it holds, then disputes about which theory is correct may partly dissolve into disputes about which kind of robustness we care about in AI safety assessment.

I would appreciate comments on the following points. First, is my characterization of the counterfactual evaluation as tracking stability across nearby worlds accurate to that tradition, or does it conflate counterfactual dependence with something closer to robustness analysis? Second, does the interventionist evaluation I describe genuinely correspond to applying the do-operator to internal modules, or have I smuggled in an engineering notion of modularity that the formalism does not license? Third, is the AI safety example illuminating, or does it confuse causal explanation with system verification? I am not asking for a bibliography, citations, or a reading list—only for conceptual criticism of the argument as sketched.
