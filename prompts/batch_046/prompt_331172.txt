I have recently been asked to draft the founding charter for a research cooperative that intends to govern itself through a self-amending constitutional structure: specifically, a clause allowing the amendment procedure itself to be revised by a three-quarters majority, provided that any reduction of that threshold requires a subsequent unanimous vote. Before I finalize the language, I need to understand which conceptual problems in legal self-reference are genuinely blocking and which are merely ornamental. Among the open questions—whether hierarchical self-amending rules possess a stable fixed point, whether every such system requires an unamendable core, and how to demarcate legitimate constitutional evolution from simple rule-violation—which should I treat as upstream? Rank them according to which resolution would most alter my drafting choices, which are tractable for a non-lawyer working without external counsel, and which depend on answers to the others. I am not asking for a literature review; I want a defended agenda that tells me where to spend my limited time and which answers, if reached, would make the remaining questions moot.
