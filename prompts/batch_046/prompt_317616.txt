Okay so I'm living with three other people and we share this pretty open living room/kitchen area. Last week Riley bought one of those always-on voice assistant things — open source, runs locally, but still listening to everything in the common space — and just put it on the shelf. She said she uses it for recipes and study questions, but obviously the rest of us are now in a space where our conversations might be processed by her gadget. At dinner, Jordan and Casey got into it about whether this is an example of that unilateralist's curse thing. (1) Jordan said yes, exactly: Riley made a unilateral decision that changed the informational environment for all of us, and since any roommate could have done the same, the group is forced to live with the privacy preference of the least cautious person. (2) Casey said Jordan's misapplying it — the curse is about irreversible, high-stakes actions where one actor doesn't bear the full cost, like releasing a bioengineered organism or launching a risky AI model; buying a $80 gadget you can unplug isn't in the same category. (3) Jordan replied that reversibility doesn't matter if the action is continuous and accumulative — she called it a 'distributed unilateralist curse,' where each roommate can incrementally reshape the shared commons, so over time the apartment becomes governed by whoever has the most invasive tech preferences, even if each individual move is small. (4) Casey said Jordan just made up 'distributed unilateralist curse' and there's no such concept in decision theory; he also said requiring unanimous consent for every device is a veto trap that lets the most paranoid roommate paralyze shared life. (5) Jordan answered that maybe the cure isn't veto but a pre-commitment protocol agreed to before anyone moves in, but Casey noted Riley already bought the thing so thats moot, and that in any case social norms aren't formal contracts. They turned to me but I don't know the philosophy well enough to call it. Can you adjudicate move by move — rate each of the five from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify? And tell me what, if anything, both are missing about how shared spaces should actually be governed?
