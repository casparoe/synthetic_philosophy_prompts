Write a dialogue of about 350 words between two philosophers, A and B, on whether DNA is literally a semantic code. A holds a teleosemantic view: evolutionary selection fixes the mapping between nucleotide sequences and functional products, so DNA carries information and can misrepresent—e.g., a deleterious mutation produces a protein that “fails to match” the selected function. B holds a causal-structural view: DNA is a chemical template with no interpreter; evolutionary history explains persistence, not meaning, and calling a mutation “false information” is only metaphor.

Ground the exchange in this specific case: a synthetic gene, designed de novo and absent from every natural genome, is expressed in a purified cell-free transcription-translation system with no editing machinery and no evolutionary lineage. The system reliably produces a functional synthetic enzyme. Have A and B argue specifically about whether this sequence carries semantic information about the enzyme, whether it could misrepresent, and whether the absence of evolutionary history undermines A’s view or exposes B’s account as merely descriptive.

Both should be equally sharp—no strawmen. They must respond to actual points: if B says there is no interpreter, A should ask whether evolutionary history itself serves as the interpreter or whether the ribosome does; if A says misrepresentation requires only a normative standard fixed by selection, B should ask why chemical malfunction is not already sufficient to establish that norm, and why the synthetic case lacks even that. End with each identifying the single feature of the synthetic case they think is decisive, and stating precisely where they still disagree. Do not include citations, reading lists, or references to specific texts.
