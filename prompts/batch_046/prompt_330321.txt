I'm working through this argument about Williams's integrity objection and I think I've built a reductio, but I can't see the exit. The idea I'm calling the insulation thesis is that ground projects are normatively insulated: reasons internal to my project stay inside and don't leak out to bind strangers.

(1) Integrity requires that I act from ground projects that are genuinely mine, not from an impartial moral calculator.
(2) Williams's point about one thought too many shows that if I save my spouse because it maximizes welfare, I've already corrupted the partial commitment that makes the relationship what it is.
(3) So the only reasons that genuinely justify actions within a ground project are internal to that project's own terms—if I need an outside license to care for my kid, I don't actually have the ground project of parenthood.
(4) By the insulation thesis, internal reasons are project-bound: the reason she's my daughter applies to me but doesn't generate any comparable claim for outsiders.
(5) Now, if I have integrity, I can't borrow impartial reasons to bridge the gap, since that'd be exactly the corruption described in (2).
(6) So an agent with full integrity recognizes no non-instrumental reason to respect strangers' projects, avoid harming them, or help them—those demands all come from outside.
(7) But basic moral decency requires exactly that recognition.
(8) Therefore integrity and decency are incompatabile, which feels wrong.

Where's the mistake? I really want to keep (1)-(3) if I can, but maybe (4) or (5) does something sneaky with scope or quantifiers. Can you tell me which step to reject and what giving it up costs? Please answer in American English.
