Explain why the standard responses—outright rejection of the knowability principle, restrictions on logical omniscience, or appeals to essentially unknowable truths—leave this particular slide unexamined and therefore fail to diagnose whether the paradox exposes a genuine constraint on knowledge or only an artifact of modal formalization. Propose a conceptual and logical method rather than an empirical one: construct a restricted modal-epistemic framework in which agents are modeled with a reflection principle that applies only to propositions within their representational capacity, and test whether the Fitch derivation re-emerges precisely at the boundary where representability fails. State clearly what a successful result would look like—for example, blocking the universal-knowledge conclusion while preserving a meaningful, non-vacuous version of the knowability principle for bounded agents—and specify what would count as failure, such as the collapse of the epistemic/logical distinction under self-reference, or the need for an ad hoc restriction that renders the principle empirically empty. Provide an honest assessment of scope: identify which technical components are tractable within one year, which depend on prior mastery of modal epistemic logic that you would need to acquire first, and what risk factors could make the project too large for the fellowship period. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; refer to existing positions and arguments conceptually, and indicate that full references will be supplied separately.
