Each transition feels locally justified because the scoring rule rewards calibration and the adaptive discount is meant to correct for temporal bias, yet an external "market-maker" pool she pays into has steadily accumulated value, and her net progress across all three projects has declined. She is now debating whether to shut down the forecasting mechanism entirely, freeze her discount function, or reject the framing that her Sunday-self, Friday-self, and next-month-self are independent bargaining agents with legitimate competing claims.

She wants to know whether there exists a rational architecture for course-correction over time that avoids being systematically extracted by one's own sequentially updated preferences, and whether the growing pool represents a genuine failure of rationality or merely the transaction cost of flexibility. I can see fragments of decision theory and personal identity in play, but I cannot assemble them into a clear diagnosis of where the loop originates. Could you map the structure of her situation onto the relevant philosophical framework, identify the precise point where long-term coherence breaks, and advise which component—the predictive scoring rule, the adaptive discount factor, or the inter-temporal bargaining model—ought to be modified or abandoned?
