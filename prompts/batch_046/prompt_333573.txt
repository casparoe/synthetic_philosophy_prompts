Here are notes for an essay I am drafting, tentatively titled "The Careenium and Simmballs: Do Symbols Push the Substrate Around?"

The careenium is a hypothetical representational substrate: a layered system in which symbolic tokens—simmballs—are not merely indexed to physical states but exert downward causal influence upon them. Rather than asking how symbols acquire meaning from the substrate (the standard grounding problem), I want to examine whether the substrate acquires its dynamics from the symbols.

My central claim is that, within such a system, the combinatorial properties of simmballs determine which substrate pathways are activated. If so, representation and causation are not two distinct layers but a single loop. I anticipate the objection that this collapses syntax into semantics or invites a homuncular fallacy, but I think the structure is closer to active-inference models in which a compressed policy shapes lower-level dynamics.

I would welcome comments on three points. First, have I genuinely distinguished the careenium from standard predictive-processing accounts, or is this merely a redescription? Second, does allowing symbols to "push" the substrate solve the grounding problem, or does it merely relocate it? Third, is there a coherent way to maintain that simmballs are causally efficacious without attributing to them properties that belong only to the substrate?

Please treat these notes as a sketch rather than a finished argument; I am particularly interested in whether the conceptual distinction between downward causation and mere correlation holds up under scrutiny.
