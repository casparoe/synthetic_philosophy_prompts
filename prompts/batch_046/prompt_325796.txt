My thesis is this. I distinguish three structures that people usually lump together as hypocrisy. Mirror hypocrisy is when the blamer has committed the precise act being condemned. Adjacent hypocrisy is when the blamer has done something morally comparable but in a different domain or scale. Systemic complicity is when the blamer does not commit the act but participates in and benefits from the institutional conditions that make the blamed act likely or profitable. I claim that mirror hypocrisy fully dissolves standing to blame; adjacent hypocrisy weakens standing proportionally to the moral similarity; but systemic complicity does not diminish standing at all. Instead, it transforms the blamer's position into what I term amplified obligation: the blamer retains full standing to blame, but acquires an additional, parallel duty to work on structural repair alongside the direct repair of the specific harm. I defend this by separating agent-relative accountability, which attaches to specific voluntary choices, from role-relative accountability, which attaches to positions within systems, and I argue that restorative practice fails when it treats all three structures identically—either by letting systemically complicit parties escape blame too easily, or by silencing legitimate blame from people who share mirror-level faults.
