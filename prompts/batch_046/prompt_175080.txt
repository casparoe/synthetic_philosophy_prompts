Write me a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that avoid asking her to restate the view and instead press where the literature presses: include at least one question that targets the charge that her "organizational constraints" are merely redescriptions of the underlying physical dynamics and therefore inherit the exclusion problem at the level of the constraint itself, with a follow-up keyed to her most likely reply; include at least one that anticipates her defense that mental causation is best tested through counterfactual stability across perturbations rather than through interventionist manipulation of single neural events, followed by a question asking what specific pattern of experimental results would distinguish her view from a pure epiphenomenalist interpretation of those same stability patterns; include one that asks how her framework handles basic mental causation, such as the role of acute pain in causing a withdrawal reflex, without collapsing into the claim that the pain-property is just the physical reflex-property described at a coarser grain; and conclude the sequence with a direct question about what empirical discovery or conceptual argument would convince her that diachronic organizational causation fails and that mental properties are indeed causally excluded in Kim's original sense.
