There's this old deadlock in metaphysics about dispostions, finks, and masks — like whether saying a glass is fragile just amounts to "if struck, it breaks," then you get the counterexamples where the strike itself removes the fragility (a fink) or packaging blocks it (a mask). For decades people have argued whether these kill the counterfactual analysis or just show you need ceteris paribus clauses. My hunch is the stall isn't about logic at all but about whether dispostions are intrinsic to objects or relational to the whole environment. Is that right, or is there some hidden empirical disagreement about how causal powers actually work in complex materials? I want a real diagnosis — verbal vs substantive, about standards of explanation, about which cases even count — not a survey. And be honest: could a formal model of masked dispostions in materials science, or an experiment tracking fink-sensitivity, actually break the impasse, or is the question just badly posed and we should stop asking it?
