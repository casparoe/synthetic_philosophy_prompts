Give an example—drawn from work, education, or local governance rather than abstract theory—of a concrete practice that a rural or working-class outlook and an urban professional-class outlook evaluate in contradictory ways, not as competing preferences but as a category mistake: each side sees the other’s assessment as misapplying the concept of value or authority altogether. The case should be specific enough that someone holding either outlook recognizes their own reasoning without feeling caricatured, and it should make visible whether either outlook can be shown internally inconsistent without importing premises from the other. Treat the disagreement as turning on what counts as a legitimate authority or a flourishing life, not on policy trade-offs, and leave open whether either position is ultimately more defensible.
