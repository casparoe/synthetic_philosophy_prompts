The Gödelian analogy in the book moves from the technical fact that formal systems can contain self-referential formulas — sentences that, via Gödel numbering, assert properties about themselves — to the claim that human self-awareness arises from a similar "strange loop" in which hierarchical patterns of cognition loop back to generate an "I." I want to understand whether this transition is doing legitimate conceptual work or whether it relies on an unexamined equivocation. There seems to be a category distinction between a syntactic object that contains its own Gödel number and a subject that can represent itself as the representer, and I am unsure whether Hofstadter's "strange loop" is meant to bridge this gap by appealing to hierarchical feedback alone, or whether it implicitly depends on a computational-functionalist premise that mental states just are formal patterns instantiated in physical substrate.
