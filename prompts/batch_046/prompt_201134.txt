You are a senior philosopher of mind and methodology of science, with deep familiarity in epistemology, cognitive psychology, and the logic of conceptual analysis. Before drafting anything, take a few moments to think carefully and brainstorm: identify the core logical commitments each camp genuinely holds, flag hidden agreements about formal argument structure and empirical framing effects, and distinguish verbal disagreements from substantive ones. Then produce the deliverable.

Your task is to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two methodological camps about intuition pumps—specifically, about how thought experiments persuade and where they systematically mislead.

The two parties are:

- The Isolationists: philosophers who hold that rigorously constructed thought experiments can genuinely isolate a single conceptual, modal, or normative variable, yielding intuitive judgments that track the structure of that variable rather than accidental scenario features.
- The Constructionists: philosophers who hold that every concrete intuition pump necessarily embeds hidden framing effects—including narrative coherence, affective imagery, and implicit normative background—so that apparent isolation is actually the introduction of confounding variables.

The document must contain exactly three parts.

Part I: Numbered Agreed Claims. List at least six substantive claims both sides can sign in good faith. These should cover: (a) the formal structure shared by standard intuition pumps (premise, scenario, isolated judgment); (b) established findings from cognitive psychology about framing and affective judgment; (c) the distinction between a scenario’s explicit logical structure and its implicit narrative packaging; and (d) the fact that disagreement persists despite these shared premises. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific published works.

Part II: Precise Disagreements. Present a structured table or list with three columns: (1) the exact point of disagreement in fully neutral language; (2) the Isolationist formulation of their own position, phrased so its own side would accept it; (3) the Constructionist formulation of their own position, phrased so its own side would accept it. Include at least four disagreements. One must concern whether a pump can ever satisfy a substitution-invariance condition: if all concrete narrative details are replaced by abstract variables (e.g., agent A performs action X on object B yielding outcome Y), and the intuitive judgment disappears or reverses, does this demonstrate that the original pump tracked framing rather than conceptual structure?

Part III: Cruxes and Predictive Tests. Identify the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side. For each crux, specify:
- The concrete case, argument, or empirical comparison both sides agree is genuinely relevant.
- The Isolationists’ explicit prediction about what that case or comparison will show.
- The Constructionists’ explicit prediction about the same case or comparison.
- Whether both sides agree that an unfavorable result would update their view, and if one side denies this, state precisely why.

You must include, as one crux, the problem of background-affect leakage: whether the emotional valence and sensory imagery embedded in a scenario’s concrete details (for example, the visual difference between redirecting a mechanism versus physically intervening) inevitably leaks into the judgment about the isolated variable, even when subjects explicitly deny that the imagery matters. For this crux, propose an isomorphic comparison—two scenarios with identical logical and normative structure but divergent affective framing—and record both sides’ predictions about whether intuitive judgments will converge or diverge.

In drafting, avoid centering your analysis exclusively on well-worn examples. Instead, construct your illustrations around the conceptual mechanics of variable isolation, narrative packaging, and affect leakage. Ensure the tone is formal, precise, and philosophical. The output should be a constructive document both parties could put their names to, with the residual disagreement rendered exact, not a dramatized dialogue, not an adjudication of who is right, and not a diagnosis of why the debate is stuck.
