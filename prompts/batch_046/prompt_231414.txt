Since I have recently begun curating an archive of family and community narratives from regions affected by both the 1815 Tambora aerosol event and the concurrent 1817–1823 cholera pandemics, I find myself caught in a conceptual problem that historical method alone cannot resolve: whether volcanic climate disruptions, long-term climate regimes, and pathogenic microbes can be said to possess a form of historical agency, or whether assigning them that status inevitably collapses into either anthropomorphism or environmental determinism. Before I settle on a framework for interpreting these narratives, I want a rigorous standard of adequacy.

Write the desiderata that any philosophical theory of non-human historical agency—specifically as applied to germs, climate patterns, and volcanic events—must satisfy to count as adequate. For each criterion, state the motivating case or conceptual distinction behind it: for example, whether the theory must separate causal efficacy from intentional directedness; whether it can accommodate collective microbial behavior without attributing consciousness or strategic purpose; whether it can grant climate events genuine explanatory weight in human history without reducing human societies to passive effects; and whether it can distinguish the sudden, discrete agency of an eruption from the diffuse, structural agency of a climate regime. Identify which desiderata you take to be non-negotiable and which reflect the prejudices of particular camps (e.g., materialist reductionism versus distributed-agency models). Crucially, flag any subset of these requirements that appears jointly unsatisfiable—particularly any tension between treating these entities as actors and denying them all mental properties, or between avoiding determinism and allowing non-human forces to shape historical outcomes. Then, briefly, indicate how a strict materialist-causal view, an Actor-Network-inspired model of distributed agency, and a classic environmental-determinist account each fare against the list. I care more about the architecture of the criteria and the points of incompatibility than about a final verdict.
