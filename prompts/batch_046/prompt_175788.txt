I am preparing the final exam for an upper-level epistemology seminar, and I want one essay question that students cannot answer by reciting the standard Gettier counterexample or summarizing the usual taxonomy of fixes. To force them into the weeds, I have constructed a specific scenario involving what I call compensating error: Dr. Amara Okonkwo, a hydrologist, predicts that a valley will flood within 48 hours. Her prediction relies on Model A, which contains an undetected systematic error that overestimates soil absorption by 15 percent, and on a satellite dataset that has an independent calibration glitch underestimating soil saturation by exactly 15 percent. The two errors cancel precisely; her belief is true, well-supported by her evidence, and she is unaware of either flaw. Please write the exam question that embeds this scenario and asks students whether Okonkwo knows the valley will flood. The question must require them to apply at least two of the following frameworks with precision: the justified-true-belief analysis amended by the condition that knowledge cannot rest on false premises; a virtue-theoretic credit account that asks whether the truth of the belief is attributable to the agent's cognitive competence; and a modal safety condition that examines whether she could easily have been wrong. It must also require them to argue whether this double-cancellation structure is epistemically different from a standard single-premise Gettier case and whether that difference supports a binary or gradational conception of knowledge. Alongside the question, provide a model answer of roughly 600 words and a marking scheme that separates an A answer from a B answer by naming the specific moves each must contain—such as identifying both errors and their independence, applying each framework without conflation, addressing the cancellation structure explicitly, and defending a coherent position on the gradational question. The model answer should be written in British English; please write the prompt itself in American English.
