In readings of Marx's account of machinery and automation—particularly the discussion in the Grundrisse often called the Fragment on Machines—there is a long-running impasse. One side reads Marx as arguing that the development of fixed capital and automated production objectively erodes the foundation of value production by reducing direct labour time, thereby creating the material conditions for a post-capitalist society in which the general intellect dominates. The other side insists that machinery never escapes the value-form: automation is always incorporated into capital's drive for relative surplus-value, so that technological development intensifies rather than dissolves capitalist domination. Both camps cite the same passages, accept the same historical record of industrialisation, and agree that Marx treats technology as embedded in social relations; yet neither has persuaded the other in decades of exchange.

My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not primarily exegetical but conceptual: the two sides are using "autonomy of technology" to mean different things—one a causal independence from particular social arrangements, the other an analytical separation of technical content from social form—so that they are answering different questions under the single heading "What did Marx think about automation?"

I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict. Is this a hidden factual dispute about Marx's intentions, a verbal disagreement masked as interpretation, a divergence in philosophical commitments about whether material forces can drive social transformation, or a case of bedrock intuitions about the relationship between technology and capital? Please diagnose it with reference to what each side actually claims, not as a survey of interpretations. If your diagnosis is correct, what would count as progress: a conceptual distinction that separates the two questions, a formal model of value production that resolves the economic claim, a new archival finding, or an argument that the question is ill-posed? And if the disagreement is genuinely substantive, is there any reason to expect it to be resolved?
