I cover science and ideas for a magizine and I'm writing about that weird corner where logic meets AI safety — the Löbian obstacle, where a system smart enough to model its own reasoning hits a wall if it tries to fully vouch for itself. The idea is basically this: if an agent can establish a reflection principle like whenever it believes X then X holds, Löb's theorem lets it derive X outright, even when X is garbage.

I want a short poem for the piece, delivered as exactly five numbered couplets — just 1 through 5, no intro, no outro — with each couplet running about a sentence or two. It should trace the trap: couplet one the agent writing its own rules, couplet two the reflection axiom slipping in, couplet three the derivation running away, couplet four the proof that empties trust of meaning, and couplet five the silence left behind. Make the rhyme or repetition do structural work — maybe a phrase that returns each time but shifts one word, so the reader feels the loop tightening. Don't turn it into a sermon about killer robots; keep it abstrct, like a formal proof that suddenly realizes it's talking about itself. I want people who know the obstacle to nod, and people who don't to still feel the claustrophobia.
