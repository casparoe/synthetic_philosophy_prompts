As a retired molecular biologist who spent three decades studying gene-regulatory networks and only recently turned to philosophy of mind, I find myself caught between two pictures of consciousness: the Cartesian theater, with its single, unified point of observation where all experience is presented to a simple, indivisible mind, and Dennett's multiple drafts model, in which there is no such central screen but only parallel, competing narratives that are edited and consolidated only after the fact. I would like you to predict how René Descartes might have responded to this second picture if he had lived to encounter it—not merely in the abstract, but in light of a specific, now-conceivable technological scenario.

Imagine a non-invasive neural interface capable of recording a subject's pre-verbal cognitive drafts in real time and displaying them back with a half-second delay. In this setup, a subject viewing an ambiguous stimulus can watch two contradictory interpretations being composed simultaneously; neither has priority, and the version that eventually becomes the spoken report is selected only retrospectively, with the discarded draft leaving detectable neural traces but no phenomenal residue. If Descartes had been presented with this phenomenon, do you think he would have concluded that the soul itself is divisible, or that there are multiple thinking substances?

More precisely: would he have relocated the Cartesian theater to the moment of retrospective consolidation, identifying the true self with the selecting agency rather than with any original point of reception? Or would he have insisted that the interface merely reveals the machinery of the brain, leaving untouched a single, simple substance in which one and only one true thought is always present, even when the bodily apparatus generates noise?

Please construct your answer from Descartes's actual metaphysical commitments—the real distinction between mind and body, the simplicity and indivisibility of the soul, and the identification of thought as the essential attribute of mind—without appealing to modern commentaries I could not verify. I am not seeking a bibliography or a list of readings; I want a reasoned philosophical prediction. In particular, identify the exact point at which the multiple drafts framework, if applied rigorously to this scenario, conflicts with Cartesian dualism, and say whether Descartes could accommodate the conflict by refining his concept of clear and distinct perception rather than abandoning the unity of consciousness.
