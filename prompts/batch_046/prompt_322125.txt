I am moderating a colloquium Q&A with a philosopher who has recently defended what she terms axiological exchange: the claim that agents holding genuinely incommensurable value systems can achieve stable mutual gains without convergence on shared principles, by stratifying their commitments into non-negotiable core values and negotiable peripheral ones, then trading concessions on the periphery in a manner structurally analogous to comparative advantage in trade. She insists this is not mere modus vivendi, because the core values remain untouched and the exchange is assessed as a gain from within each system rather than by an external metric.

Write me a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that avoid letting her restate the framework in general terms. I want the questions to press exactly where the literature on value pluralism and moral compromise presses: how the boundary between core and peripheral is drawn without circular appeal to the values being protected; whether assessing a gain from within each system still requires a hidden common currency of importance; what prevents exchange from becoming systematic exploitation when one party possesses mostly low-cost peripheral commitments; and whether accumulated peripheral concessions eventually reshape the social conditions in which the core values operate, undermining the stability she promises. For each line of pressure, anticipate the reply she is most likely to offer—for instance, that boundary-drawing is procedural and revisable, or that exploitation is blocked by a veto right over core-adjacent trades—and include a sharper follow-up that makes that reply harder to maintain. Conclude with one question asking what normative or institutional scenario would genuinely weaken the framework, rather than being absorbable as just another exchange opportunity.
