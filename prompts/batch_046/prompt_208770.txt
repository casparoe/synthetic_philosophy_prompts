For our seminar on determinism and the philosophy of action, I want to press what strikes me as an underappreciated tension in how the Sphex case is usually deployed. Granting that Sphex ichneumoneus can recalibrate its burrow-entry sequence when the prey is experimentally displaced—exhibiting what we might call parameterized rigidity rather than absolute sphexishness—doesn't this empirical flexibility undermine the canonical analogy between wasp behavior and human determinism? More precisely, if the wasp's loop can absorb small perturbations without breaking, haven't we shown that the difference between mechanical action and free will is not a qualitative break in causal determination but merely a quantitative difference in perturbation tolerance? If so, is free will best reconceived not as an absence of behavioral loops but as sphexishness operating with a substantially higher exit-cost threshold?
