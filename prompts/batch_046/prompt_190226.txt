I am writing up my philosophical profile on Madhyamaka for a seminar and want a consistency check before I present. Here is where I stand:

(1) I read Nāgārjuna’s tetralemma (catuṣkoṭi) as applying not only to entities but to any proposed ground (āśraya) of knowledge or being; hence dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda) is itself empty and not an ultimate foundation.

(2) I take the thesis that emptiness itself is empty (śūnyatāśūnyatā) to entail that the absence of svabhāva is not itself a property with svabhāva, leaving what I call groundless coherence—no metaphysical bedrock whatsoever.

(3) I maintain that conventional truth (saṃvṛtisatya) remains fully operative: causes produce effects, reasoning works, and entities function, but its authority is pragmatic and interdependent rather than foundational.

(4) I hold that if a philosophical view undermines the very reasoning practices that generated it—rendering them unreliable or self-defeating in a way that cannot be repaired from within—this is a serious defeater that requires revision.

(5) I reject both eternalism and nihilism about conventional entities, and I deny that Madhyamaka is merely sceptical; conventional efficacy (arthakriyā) is genuinely affirmed, not cancelled.

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each tension? For each genuine tension, state the cheapest fix and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems, and whether my package is tenable as it stands. Please write your answer in British English.
