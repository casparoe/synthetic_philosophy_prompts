I have noticed a recurring pattern in genuinely collaborative work that I do not have a precise term for, and I am unsure whether the philosophy of collective action has already carved it out as a distinct case. Here are two concrete instances. First, a surgeon and an anesthesiologist move through a long operation in perfect coordination—each adjusts to the other’s tempo, each expects to be held accountable for the outcome, and afterward both sincerely say “we did this together”—but one understands the procedure as curative and the other as palliative, so they share no common conception of what they achieved, only that they achieved it jointly. Second, two architects co-design a building over months: one treats it as an exercise in structural minimalism, the other as a monument to communal memory, yet their drawings interlock seamlessly because the formal constraints happen to satisfy both visions, and they sign the plans together feeling they produced one shared work, despite never having agreed on what that work means. In both cases there is real mutual monitoring, shared responsibility, and a first-person plural experience—this is ours, not merely mine and yours running in parallel—yet the participants lack any unified idea of the goal they are jointly pursuing; their coordination is sustained by aligned behavior rather than by agreement about the object of agreement.

Does this configuration have an established name? I know there are prominent accounts of people acting together through shared intentions with meshing sub-plans, and others that require a joint commitment to a specific goal as one body, but both seem to demand at least some convergence on what is being done. How exactly does the phenomenon I have described differ from each of those standard accounts? If the literature offers no precise label, please propose one with a clear definition, and specify the boundary conditions that separate it from mere parallel work with hidden disagreement on the one hand, and from cases where conceptual divergence about the goal eventually disrupts the practical coordination on the other.
