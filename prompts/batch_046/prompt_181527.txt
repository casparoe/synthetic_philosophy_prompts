Write a short story set in an isolated subglacial research station where the only residents are four autonomous cognitive architectures designed according to Daniel Dennett’s taxonomy of minds: a Darwinian system that generates and selects behavioral variants without internal representation, a Skinnerian system shaped entirely by a history of environmental reinforcement, a Popperian system that constructs internal models to test hypotheses before acting, and a Gregorian system that relies on inherited cultural artifacts and linguistic tools to extend its reasoning. When a sudden geothermal vent destabilizes the station’s power core, the four architectures must jointly devise a repair protocol that none can execute alone. Narrate the crisis from the limited perspective of each architecture in turn, showing how each interprets the others’ contributions—whether as random mutation, conditioned response, predictive simulation, or borrowed tradition—without granting the reader any authoritative judgment about which, if any, genuinely understands the situation. The story should treat Dennett’s distinctions as lived constraints rather than labels, and it should avoid bibliographies, footnotes, or references to external philosophical texts.
