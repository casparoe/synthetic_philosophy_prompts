Write a dialogue between a strict Confucian role relationalist and a Confucian role constitutionalist about the "empty role" problem: whether moral obligations survive the permanent absence of all relational partners. The relationalist holds that obligations are entirely constituted by active, reciprocal roles—without a living father, ruler, or friend, the corresponding duties dissolve, leaving no moral residue. The constitutionalist holds that roles constitute identity but are anchored in a minimal, pre-relational capacity for reciprocity (ren) that persists even when every concrete partner is gone, preventing total moral vacancy. Both should be equally competent and respond directly to each other. They must address: (1) whether the sole survivor of a destroyed community retains any filial or civic obligations; (2) whether duties to the dead are genuinely relational or are covert appeals to a non-relational good; and (3) whether admitting residual obligations in isolation necessarily smuggles in the universalist morality that classical Confucianism rejects.
