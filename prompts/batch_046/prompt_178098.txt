hey, I need an activty for a workshop with about 14 people (grad students and some planners), 90 minutes, on zoning and who actually has a right to the city. I want them to feel the specific contradiction where supporting urban growth makes you complicit in pricing out the people who live there — including yourself eventually. Design a four-round simulatoin: split them into four neighborhoods, each with different zoning rules and hidden payoff cards. In each round there's a central proposal — transit, upzoning, inclusionary mandate — and they vote as a city council. But here's the twist: neighborhoods that vote yes for growth get development tokens that boost city-wide welfare, but they also accumulate risk cards. By round 3 anyone with two risk cards draws a displacement card, and the rules say only yes-voting neighborhoods can be hit. So they should feel the pull to vote yes for the collective good, then the betrayal when their own cards turn. Include specific roles — maybe a renter coalition, a homeowner association, a developer proxy, and a city planner — and a payoff scheme using points not money. Then a debrief that maps round 1 (abstract support) to the universalist claim, round 3 (personal loss) to the problem that rights exercised collectively can destroy the very community that holds them. Be honest about what will go wrong: people will try to form coalitions to block proposals selectively, and the facilitator should let it happen then ask why it failed. Also be frank: the simulatoin can't reproduce the racialized history of zoning or intergenerational wealth transfer, so you'd need to supply that in the debrief or it just looks like a coordination game. Don't lecture up front; they shouldn't hear the phrase right-to-the-city named until the debrief.
