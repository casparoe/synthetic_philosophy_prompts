After a long argument with a friend last night about whether global workspace theory explains consciousness itself or only our access to it, I want a careful comparison of two positions. First, the view—often associated with defenders of global workspace theory—that phenomenal consciousness just is globally broadcast access consciousness, so that any state with rich qualitative character but lacking workspace integration is either conceptually impossible or not genuinely conscious. Second, the opposing view I have been trying to articulate through a hypothetical “isolated phenomenal module”: a subsystem that generates full-blown qualitative experience but is informationally encapsulated, never broadcasting its contents to memory, report, or action control. Compare these two positions specifically on whether the possibility of such a module undermines global workspace theory as an account of phenomenal consciousness, or whether the global workspace theorist can consistently absorb it by treating phenomenal consciousness as necessarily tied to broadcast availability. Which disagreements here are empirical and which are conceptual? And if the module is possible in principle, does that show that access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness genuinely come apart, or can the theorist reply that the module’s states are not “conscious” precisely because they lack global access?
