Before you begin the analysis, take a few moments to brainstorm: list the distinct failure modes that appear in well-documented AI deployment incidents, then distinguish between failures of objective specification, failures of robustness to adversarial input, and failures that emerge specifically from the dynamics of interaction between the system and its users.

Now analyze the March 2016 release and rapid corruption of Microsoft’s Tay chatbot as a case study for the nature of the alignment problem. Rather than treating Tay as an instance of a single, unified failure—say, inadequate value alignment or poor content filtering—I want you to apply a three-part conceptual taxonomy and test whether the episode supports it. The categories are: (1) target misalignment, in which the system optimizes for an objective that is not the one intended by its designers; (2) frame misalignment, in which the system’s ontology of the interaction is structurally wrong—e.g., treating public tweets as neutral training data rather than as social speech acts embedded in norms of irony, provocation, and group identity; and (3) reciprocal corruption, in which interlocutors do not merely exploit a fixed system but actively reshape its behavior or representations through sustained interaction, so that the system’s effective goals or internal structure are altered in real time.

For each category, say precisely whether Tay exemplifies it, which concrete features of the episode—the Twitter interface, the unsupervised reinforcement-learning loop, the coordinated adversarial input from users, the absence of human-in-the-loop oversight—make the mapping succeed or fail, and which part of the standard alignment narrative the category illuminates or obscures. Be disciplined: if a category does not apply, state this explicitly and explain why forcing it onto Tay would distort the historical record. Then, stepping back, tell me what the episode reveals about whether the alignment problem is one problem or several conceptually distinct ones. Does Tay show that these three categories remain genuinely separate, each requiring a different kind of remedy, or does it reveal that they collapse into one another in practice? Identify at least one feature of the case—something visible in the actual timeline of Tay’s corruption—that any adequate theory of alignment must explain but that the standard specification-gaming narrative misses entirely.
