I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of mind, currently working on my dissertation about epiphenomenalism and what has been called the paradox of phenomenal judgment—the problem that if conscious states lack causal efficacy, then our judgments about those states, including the judgment that we are currently experiencing them, must be produced entirely by physical processes rather than by the experiences themselves. This creates a strange asymmetry: the judgment claims first-person authority over a state that, by the theory's own lights, plays no role in causing the judgment. I was discussing this with my advisor last week, and she suggested that the tension might be better captured structurally in verse than in standard analytic prose, since the paradox involves a kind of recursive loop that keeps reasserting itself without entering the causal chain.

For a workshop I'm organizing on consciousness and artistic form, I would like a poem that renders this specific paradox rather than a generic meditation on the mind-body problem. Please write a villanelle in American English, spoken in the voice of an epiphenomenalist philosopher attempting to articulate a phenomenal judgment—specifically, the judgment that there is something it is like to see red—while recognizing that the articulation itself is causally inert with respect to the experience it reports. Let the two refrains carry the opposing poles: one expressing the first-person certainty of the phenomenal claim, the other expressing its causal impotence, for instance that the line writes itself without the experience's aid. The form should do the philosophical work: the refrains should take turns and finally meet in the closing couplet, showing why the two positions keep passing each other without resolution. It should be accurate enough that a philosopher familiar with the literature on phenomenal judgment—say, with the framing developed by Chalmers or Jackson—recognizes the specific paradox, and it should leave the tension open rather than resolving it into either materialist triumph or dualist escape. Please maintain the villanelle's nineteen-line structure, rhyme scheme, and refrain pattern precisely, and avoid bibliographic references, citations, or invented quotations from named texts; paraphrase any conceptual debts without quotation marks.
