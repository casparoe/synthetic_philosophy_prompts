For my seminar in philosophy of neuroscience, I need to design an original thought experiment for our unit on voluntary action and determinism. Please construct a one-paragraph, realistic scenario—set in a clinical neurofeedback lab, not in science fiction—built around a Libet-style readiness-potential paradigm in which a subject initiates a simple motor act. Engineer the case so that the standard empirical profile holds (the readiness potential precedes conscious awareness of the urge, and there is a brief conscious veto window), but isolate one structural variable: in version A, the veto mechanism is causally efficacious but entirely opaque to the subject's first-person access, while in version B it is causally inert but fully transparent to introspection. Keep every other feature constant. After describing the scenario, demonstrate the verdict that a hard incompatibilist, a leeway-compatibilist, and a source-compatibilist each deliver about whether the subject "could have done otherwise" in the sense required for moral responsibility, and show why the divergence tracks your isolated variable. Then state what happens if that variable is neutralized—does the case revert to a conventional Libet interpretation?—and explain how a defender of the theory that fares worst in your design would attack the scenario. Do not provide citations, bibliographies, or direct quotations from texts; rely only on the general framework of readiness-potential studies and the conscious-veto model.
