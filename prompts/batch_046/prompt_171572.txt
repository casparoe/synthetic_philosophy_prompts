I've been digging into Bertrand's paradox and I think the principle of indifference survives if you restrict it to paramaterizations that stay invariant under the actual physical dynamics of the randomization, not just geometric symmetry. So in the chord problem there's a fact of the matter about how you'd actually toss the stick, and that picks one measure without ambiguity. But this feels like it might just be insulating the view by fiat.

What would actually count against it? Not the standard 'three answers' objection—everyone knows that already. I'm asking about formal results or physical arguments showing that dynamics-invariant paramaterization is either undefined, non-unique, or secretly collapses back into one of the geometric measures no matter the mechanisim. Also, which intuitions about what counts as the 'same mechanism' across different setups make this look like special pleading? Rank the defeaters: fatal, awkward, or cosmetic. And be honest—can defenders just keep adding qualifications forever, or is there a point where that stops being philosophy and starts being ad-hocery?
