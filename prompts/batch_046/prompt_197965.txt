I am writing up my commitments for a population ethics and climate philosophy seminar and want a consistency check before I present them. Here is where I have landed:

(1) I accept the procreation asymmetry: there is a strong moral reason not to create someone whose life would be not worth living, but no corresponding reason to create someone whose life would be good.

(2) I take the best explanation of this asymmetry to be a strict person-affecting restriction: moral reasons must be anchored to identifiable individuals who are made better or worse off, and since there is no pre-existing subject to be benefited by being brought into existence, creation can never be morally required.

(3) At the same time, I think that allowing humanity to go extinct through climate inaction would be a profound moral tragedy, because the absence of the billions of happy lives that would otherwise have existed is intrinsically bad, even though no particular person is harmed by not being born.

(4) I reject the replaceability argument in animal ethics: it is wrong to kill one contented cow and replace it with another, even if total welfare is unchanged, because the specific animal killed is made worse off.

(5) I believe that our climate obligations to future generations are weightier than our obligations to distant present people, precisely because the future individuals in question depend on our current carbon choices for their very existence.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each tension? For the real ones, rank them by severity and say what the least costly revision would be for each. I am not signaling which commitment I am prepared to drop—treat all five as fixed for the audit and show what each revision costs.
