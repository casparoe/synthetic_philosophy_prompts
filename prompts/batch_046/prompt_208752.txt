You are a philosopher specializing in 19th-century metaphysics and aesthetics. Before answering, briefly brainstorm Schopenhauer’s core distinctions—between representation and Will, between the principle of sufficient reason and the thing-in-itself, and between the genius’s pure intuition and ordinary conceptual cognition—then address the argument below.

A study partner offered this reconstruction of Schopenhauer’s view of music, and it seems to collapse his position into contradiction, but I cannot locate the exact slip.

(1) Schopenhauer claims music is not a representation of Platonic Ideas, as painting or poetry are, but an immediate copy or direct objectification of the Will itself.
(2) The Will, as thing-in-itself, lies entirely outside the principle of sufficient reason, which governs only phenomena and representations.
(3) So if music expresses the Will directly—without representational mediation—then music itself should lie outside the forms of representation: it should not be structured by time, causality, or logical/mathematical relations.
(4) Yet every actual piece of music unfolds in temporal succession, obeys harmonic ratios (logical relations), and is physically caused by instruments, performers, and acoustic vibrations.
(5) One might reply that these are only the external vehicle or medium, while the content—the Will—flows through them untouched.
(6) But Schopenhauer also describes the composer as translating an inner, non-conceptual intuition into sound through technical mastery of harmony, rhythm, and instrumentation—all forms that belong to the phenomenal world governed by the principle of sufficient reason.
(7) Furthermore, Schopenhauer characterizes the Will as blind, purposeless, and devoid of internal rational order; yet music is arguably the most formally ordered and mathematically structured of all the arts. If music is a direct copy of the Will, one might expect it to mirror that blindness and disorder rather than to transcend it through perfect formal organization.
(8) So either music remains fully embedded in representational form and is produced via phenomenal mediation—in which case it is just another representation, not a direct copy of the Will—or it truly escapes those forms, in which case it should not sound like structured music at all.
(9) Therefore Schopenhauer’s claim that music uniquely expresses the Will directly collapses, and with it his justification for ranking music above every other art.

The conclusion feels wrong—Schopenhauer’s view is strange, but not obviously self-refuting. Which step should I reject? I would especially like to preserve (1) and (3), since they capture his core metaphysics. For each of the other steps, explain what giving it up costs: is there an equivocation between the form of the musical vehicle and the content it expresses, or does the real problem lie in how Schopenhauer describes artistic production and the nature of the Will?
