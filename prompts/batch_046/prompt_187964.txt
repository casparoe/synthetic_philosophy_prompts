I'm trying to work out what Schlick's "play, not purpose" view actually says about specific lives, and my verdicts are all over the place. Kid building a sandcastle the tide takes in ten minutes: that's pure play, so meaningful even with zero result. Surgeon saving lives every day but only for the salary and hating it: Schlick should say that part of her life lacks meaning, which feels harsh but I think I buy it. Someone grindng acheivements in a game whose server shuts down next month: is that still play, or does the external reward structure make it fake purpose? Then there's a guy who spends twenty years trimming a garden no one visits, not for pride, just because the cutting itself absorbs him - play or compulsion? I go back and forth. The real tension is rescue work: if you save drownding kids purely from duty with absolutely no joy, Schlick seems forced to call that meaningless, which feels wrong sitting right next to the surgeon case. Is there one principle - maybe autotelic absorption plus absence of instrumental goal - that saves all my verdicts, or do I have to drop one? And what would it say about someone whose job looks like work from the outside but feels like deep play to them?
