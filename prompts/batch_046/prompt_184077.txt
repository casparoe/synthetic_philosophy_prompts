I am designing the opening assignment for a graduate seminar on moral psychology and metaphysics, and I want the students to move past the generic question of whether we have free will and toward a more discriminating inquiry: which capacities, if they turned out to be real and available to agents, would actually be worth wanting—by which I mean they would ground the practices of moral address, legal responsibility, and personal aspiration that make the concept matter to us.

Please specify the desiderata that any adequate theory of free will worth wanting must satisfy. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or phenomenon it is meant to explain, the distinction it must respect, and your judgment about whether it is non-negotiable or merely the preference of one philosophical camp. Your list should cover, at minimum, the following dimensions (you may supplement them):

First, executive control: what form of alternative possibility or sourcehood is required for an action to be genuinely one's own, and must this be metaphysically robust or only psychologically salient?

Second, legislative self-revision: the capacity to alter not just particular choices but the norms, values, or reasoning procedures that govern one's own decision-making over time. Must this revision be fully self-authored, or may it be legitimately scaffolded by social institutions or technological augmentation?

Third, counterfactual robustness under cognitive hybridization: if an agent's practical reasoning is partially outsourced to or enhanced by artificial systems, what conditions must obtain for the agent—not the system—to retain the relevant form of agency?

Fourth, diachronic normative authority: the conditions under which an agent can be genuinely addressed across time by moral demands that apply to her historical trajectory, including demands that she should have formed different preferences or resisted particular influences. What must be true of an agent's narrative continuity for such address to be appropriate rather than mere manipulation?

Fifth, social recognizability: the criteria other agents must be able to apply, without access to hidden metaphysical facts, to treat someone as a proper object of praise, blame, or contractual obligation.

After laying out these criteria with their motivating considerations, flag any subset you regard as jointly unsatisfiable—especially if legislative self-revision conflicts with diachronic normative authority, or if counterfactual robustness under hybridization undermines the very sourcehood demanded by executive control. Then, briefly indicate how a hierarchical model in the tradition of Harry Frankfurt, a reasons-responsive compatibilist account in the tradition of John Martin Fischer and Mark Ravizza, and a revisionist or eliminativist view fare against your list. Do not include a bibliography or reading list.

Finally, state explicitly what would change your mind: identify the specific argument, empirical finding, or conceptual distinction that, if established, would lead you to revise which desiderata you treat as non-negotiable, and indicate where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
