I'm working on a novel about a crisis counsellor in a near-future city where every moral decision gets run through a weigting board — you feed in lives, probabilities, relationships, and it spits out the permitted choice. The board says either choice is within her rights, but she realizes shes about to run a calculation on her partner's value relative to strangers, and that feels like the exact failure Bernard Williams warned about — having one thought too many, where you justify saving your own by treating it as a permissible exception rather than just acting from love or loyalty. I want you to write a short story, maybe 800 words, about her standing in front of that board, but dont make it a straight allegory. Let her be confused, let the machinery be weirdly poetic, and have her make a choice that isnt clearly right or wrong. I need it to actually wrestle with the idea that some moral reasoning is self-undermining, not just illustrate it.
