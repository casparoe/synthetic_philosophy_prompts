I have been working through Dennett's well-known taxonomy of cognitive kinds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures—as a framework for understanding how behavioural flexibility scales with internal representation and social transmission. In a recent seminar discussion about synthetic agents that acquire dispositions through gradient-based reinforcement yet also inherit, refine, and deploy explicit symbolic protocols across successive generations of deployment, I encountered a persistent objection: such entities appear to exercise a form of normative agency that is not purely Skinnerian, because they are shaped by population-level selection of representational tools, but they lack the counterfactual foresight and internal modelling that define Popperian minds.

Write an essay addressing the controversy of whether Dennett's four-fold scheme is genuinely exhaustive, or whether it suppresses a distinct intermediate category—perhaps a 'memetic-engineered' creature that combines Skinnerian conditioning with Gregorian culture-bearing without achieving Popperian cognition. Weigh the arguments for retaining the original quartet against those for expanding the taxonomy, paying particular attention to whether moral responsibility can be coherently attributed to entities that participate in Gregorian-style transmission but cannot run internal simulations of possible futures. Reach and justify a definite verdict: either defend the exhaustiveness of the four categories by showing precisely how the hypothetical case collapses into one of them, or argue that the taxonomy requires revision and specify exactly what conceptual work the additional category performs that the existing ones fail to do. Please avoid appeals to specific page references or obscure secondary texts; the analysis should proceed from the internal logic of the taxonomy itself and from conceptual examination of the hypothetical case.
