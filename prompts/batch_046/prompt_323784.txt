I am a retired molecular biologist who, over the past three years, has moved into serious study of metaphysics and philosophy of science. Next month I present a seminar paper—working title, "Standing Patterns: An Operational Account of Biological Universals"—to a philosophy of science reading group. The audience is composed chiefly of metaphysicians divided between essentialist realists about natural kinds and anti-realist conventionalists regarding biological taxonomy, together with two philosophers of physics who are reflexively skeptical of analogies imported from quantum mechanics into biological ontology. My thesis is that universals in biology—say, the property of being a mammal or the structural invariant of a ribosome—are neither Platonic abstracta nor mere mental categories, but stable interference patterns that emerge when standardized measurement protocols from independent laboratories interact with the same biological entity. I liken this to standing waves in physical systems: the "universal" is the invariant node structure produced by these interacting protocols, not a pre-existing essence and not a subjective construct.

Please prepare me for the Q&A session as a speaker-prep sheet, not as a defense speech. List the objections I will actually face from this particular audience, in roughly the order they will arise, phrased in the vocabulary each faction—essentialist realists, conventionalists, and physics skeptics—would naturally employ. For each objection, indicate its source faction, rank its potential damage to my proposal, specify what I should concede upfront, provide a concise spoken reply of no more than two minutes, and flag the precise point where that reply is weakest. I also want explicit guidance on what I should concede before any objection is raised, and on the one question I am hoping nobody asks—the one that exposes the central tension in my analogy between biological measurement stability and resonant-cavity physics. Do not supply bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I will have no access to a library and do not wish to invent references. Finally, address whether an audience member is likely to charge that I have smuggled a niche physics concept—tuned resonant interference—into biology without independent justification, and advise me on how to absorb part of that charge without collapsing my position into either pure conventionalism or full-blooded essentialism.
