Devise a joke grounded in the illusionist conception of phenomenal consciousness as a user interface — the view that subjective experience is not a direct window onto neural activity but a representational surface that conceals the underlying machinery while simultaneously constructing the impression of a user who is experiencing it. The humor should turn specifically on the absence of that implied user: for example, a scenario in which the interface reports an error to itself, or in which a familiar feature of experience (such as the feeling of deliberate choice) is revealed to be merely an icon with no corresponding process running behind it. Make the joke intelligible to someone acquainted with the user-illusion framework, but avoid generic brain-as-computer tropes and do not invoke specific authors, texts, or technical terminology beyond the core idea. The goal is a joke that succeeds only if the listener recognizes that, on this view, there is literally no one behind the screen to receive the illusion.
