I have been trying to make sense of the arguments about climate change and obligations to people who are not born yet. In conversations with friends, I notice that we often agree on the basic facts but disagree about whether anything is actually owed, and I suspect the disagreement hides deeper premises that nobody states out loud. I am not a philosopher by training, but I want to map the logical space of the debate rather than collect slogans.

Please construct a taxonomy — a branching decision tree — that classifies positions on whether present generations have obligations regarding climate change to future generations. I want the tree to be built from concrete commitments, not from pre-existing labels, so that someone could answer the branch questions through ordinary reasoning without knowing what the positions are called.

Structure the tree as follows:

- Root: Can actions taken today wrong beings who do not yet exist?
  - If no, proceed to the present-only branch.
  - If yes, proceed downward.

- Second branch: Does the fact that reducing emissions changes which specific individuals will be born in the future dissolve the obligation? This is sometimes discussed in connection with what philosophers call the non-identity problem.
  - If yes, that opens one cluster of leaves.
  - If no, continue downward.

- Third branch: Is the core obligation about preventing identifiable future harms to particular people, or about maintaining fair institutions and environmental conditions regardless of who ends up living in them?
  - These two answers should open different sub-branches.

- Fourth branch: Does the probable development of future adaptation technologies — such as large-scale geoengineering or climate-resilient infrastructure — eliminate the present obligation, or does the sheer scale of potential suffering make the obligation hold regardless of future adaptation?
  - This should split each prior path further.

Requirements for the deliverable:

- Every branch node must be phrased as a question someone could answer from ordinary reasoning, without knowing philosophical terminology in advance.
- Each leaf should carry either a recognized name for the position if one exists, or the label unnamed, followed by one sentence describing its most awkward or counter-intuitive commitment — for instance, that we must preserve conditions for people whose very existence may depend on our failing to preserve them.
- Include at least eight leaves in total.
- Identify any pairs of leaves that your tree cannot clearly separate, and flag them explicitly.
- Also identify any logical combinations of answers that have no defenders, and briefly diagnose whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect.
- Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. I want the structure of the debate itself, not references to books or articles.

Please present the tree clearly, with each branch labeled by the claim or concrete case that divides it from its siblings.
