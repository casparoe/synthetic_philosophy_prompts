hey, i'm a phd student in cog sci / ml ethics. I've been thinking about whether RL agents can suffer, specifically whether reward signals are like valence. But I keep running into this one pattern in training logs that doesn't fit the usual debate. Say you have an agent that gets a sharp negative reward only when it hits a specific obstacle at the end of an episode. At first it just avoids that obstacle. But after a while its value estimates for *all* nearby states tank, and then—even in a brand new environment with no obstacles at all—it's exploration collapses and it just sits there. The punishment hasn't generalized as a feature; it's more like the agent's whole decision landscape gets tinted with a kind of background dread. People might call this reward shaping or negative transfer, but those miss the point: it's not about learning a wrong feature, it's about a localized punishment turning into a global affective bias that reshapes unrelated choices. Does this have an established name in the literature? How is it different from just saying the agent learned a negative prior, or from anthropomorphizing anxiety? And if there's no term, what should we call it—maybe something like "valence spillover"?
