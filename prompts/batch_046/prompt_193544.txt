I am drafting an essay that argues Whitehead’s familiar three-stage rhythm of education—romance, precision, and generalization—should be read through the lens of his metaphysical doctrine of negative prehension. Rather than treating the stages as a linear accumulation of competence, the essay claims that advancing from romance to precision requires an active exclusion of experiential content: the learner must suppress broader, vague associations in order to achieve analytical exactness. In this view, disciplinary precision operates analogously to a negative prehension of the learner’s prior romantic engagement with a subject. The essay further contends that Whitehead’s insistence on the union of freedom and discipline in education refers not merely to curriculum design, but to the need to preserve the capacity to reverse these exclusions—to allow generalization to recover what precision had provisionally bracketed. I apply this to university-level technical training, suggesting that specialization becomes pedagogically pathological when it makes the exclusion permanent rather than provisional.

Please comment on the following aspects of this argument:

(1) Whether interpreting negative prehension as a pedagogical mechanism is textually defensible within Whitehead’s metaphysics, or whether it illegitimately conflates ontological prehension with psychological processes of learning and forgetting;

(2) Whether the claim that precision requires a strategic suppression of romantic associations adds genuine explanatory value to standard readings of The Aims of Education, or whether it merely restates Whitehead’s own point about the necessity of discipline;

(3) How convincing the application to modern disciplinary specialization is, and whether there is an important counter-example or refinement—perhaps from Whitehead’s own remarks on university study—that I should address;

(4) Which part of the argument is weakest and most in need of development, and what specific direction you would recommend for expanding or cutting material.

The intended audience is familiar with Whitehead’s educational writings but not necessarily with Process and Reality, so I am trying to keep the metaphysical machinery minimal. Please advise whether that balance is appropriate, and identify any point where the essay risks either overloading the reader with metaphysics or under-explaining a crucial step.
