Write a concise, intellectually rigorous dialogue between two interpreters of Émilie du Châtelet's Discourse on Happiness. The first advances a "hierarchical monadic" reading: happiness is primarily an internal state of rational equilibrium, achieved through disciplined study and the moderation of passions, with love and friendship valued only insofar as they sustain intellectual tranquillity. The second advances a "constitutive relational" reading: for du Châtelet, happiness is irreducibly intersubjective, and her own scientific collaboration and emotional life were not merely instrumental but formed the very substrate of her well-being; without mutual recognition, study collapses into solitary vanity.

Each speaker should respond directly to the other's specific claims—particularly regarding her apparent tension between praising study as a remedy for melancholy and insisting that affection is indispensable—rather than talking past one another. Neither position should be reduced to a strawman. Avoid direct quotations, bibliographic references, or appeals to secondary literature; ground the exchange solely in conceptual reasoning about her view. Write in polished British English.
