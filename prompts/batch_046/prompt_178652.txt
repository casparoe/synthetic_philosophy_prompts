hey so im running this little discussion workshop at the library next tuesday, about 10-12 neighbors, 90 minutes. weve been talking about whether guilt or shame is the better moral emotion, and whether a culture can just choose one like picking a team.

i keep thinking guilt works like a debt ledger - theres a creditor, you pay back, books close. shame is more like a crack in your public face that everyone can see, and the crowd keeps the books. but i also think theres a hidden problem: if a culture tries to go full guilt, people start doing what i call guilt laundering - confessing loudly not to fix anything but to earn social points for being honest. and then theres shadow shame, where you technically paid your debt but still feel contaminated inside, like the stain went underground.

i want an in-person activity, maybe with roles or token rules, where people first run a moral repair system under one emotion, then switch to the other, and actually feel the friction and the cheating. they should discover for themselves that you cant just vote on this, and that which one is 'better' depends on whether you believe moral damage can really be repaired. please design the rules and stakes, tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time i run it, and be honest in the debrief about which part of the idea - especially the hidden conversion costs - the game just cant reproduce and you have to supply with words.
