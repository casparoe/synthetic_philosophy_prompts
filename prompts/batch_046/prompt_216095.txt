For my science and ideas column, I want to publish an interactive taxonomy—a decision tree rendered in prose—that lets intellectually curious readers discover which philosophical stance on the reality of the quantum wavefunction they actually hold, without requiring them to recognize labels like psi-ontic or psi-epistemic in advance. The instrument should begin with a single non-technical premise anyone who accepts quantum mechanics can grant: that the theory correctly predicts the statistical outcomes of experiments one can perform. From there, every branch node must be a concrete case or conceptual claim on which the positions genuinely diverge, phrased as a direct question the reader can answer without knowing which philosophical view it leads to.

The tree must include at least the following diagnostic branches. First, ontological status: does the wavefunction describe a physical field, a state of information about something else, or neither? Second, identity conditions: if two quantum systems are prepared with identical wavefunctions but entirely different histories, are they ontologically identical, applying a principle analogous to Leibniz’s law to quantum states? Third, the archival interference case—an invented but physically coherent scenario—where a macroscopic recording device is placed in a superposition of having registered outcome A and outcome B; before any observer checks the record, the superposition is coherently erased and interference is restored. Does this show the record never possessed a definite value, that our knowledge was merely incomplete, or that the device’s physical state was genuinely indeterminate? Fourth, preparation independence and statistical overlap: consider a scenario in which two systems are independently prepared in non-orthogonal quantum states, then jointly measured in a way that reveals correlations incompatible with both systems having possessed definite, pre-existing underlying properties matching those preparations. Does this compel rejection of a purely epistemic interpretation of the wavefunction, acceptance that independent preparation can fail, or denial that such joint measurements are physically meaningful?

The named leaves must include: a full psi-ontic view, treating the wavefunction as a real physical entity, possibly residing in a high-dimensional configuration space; a psi-epistemic view, treating it as representing limited knowledge of an underlying reality; an agent-centered epistemic view along QBist lines, treating the wavefunction as an agent’s subjective belief-update; a relational ontology, treating the wavefunction as describing relations between systems rather than intrinsic properties of any single system; and a hybrid structural-realist position that treats the wavefunction as encoding real but non-object-like structural relations, neither a literal field nor a mere belief.

The taxonomy should also map the logical space of answer combinations. Identify any unoccupied combinations—for example, a view that combines full psi-ontology with strict three-dimensional spatial locality and no hidden variables—and briefly diagnose whether that combination is incoherent given standard quantum mechanics, merely unexplored, or unattractive for independent reasons. Flag any pair of leaves the tree’s questions cannot reliably separate, such as certain variants of the agent-centered view and relational ontology that may yield identical answers to both the archival and the independence cases, and explain precisely where the ambiguity remains.

The deliverable should be formatted as a classification instrument with branch labels as answerable questions, a scoring key mapping patterns of answers onto the named positions, and brief annotations for the unoccupied and ambiguous regions. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific papers, books, or named theorems. Write for an audience of intellectually curious non-specialists who follow science journalism. Keep the tone formal and precise, avoid jargon that requires prior training in quantum foundations, and ensure every node can be answered on conceptual grounds alone.
