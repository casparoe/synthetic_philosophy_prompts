Reading Timothy Williamson's epistemicism alongside the supervaluationist accounts associated with Kit Fine, I take both positions to be committed to the existence of sharp cutoffs in reality. On my reading, the epistemicist maintains that there is exactly one precise threshold at which vulnerability begins, though our epistemic access is too coarse-grained to identify it; the supervaluationist, by contrast, maintains that each admissible averaging protocol defines its own sharp cutoff, so that reality contains multiple legitimate boundaries, and a statement is determinately true or false only when every protocol delivers the same verdict. Consequently, it strikes me that the dispute is not about whether sharp boundaries exist, but about whether our ignorance should be modeled as ignorance of a single hidden fact or as the presence of several equally correct precisifications.

Given this framing, I do not understand why an epistemicist should reject the supervaluationist strategy rather than treating it as a semantic articulation of our uncertainty about which single boundary is correct.
