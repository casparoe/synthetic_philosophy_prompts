I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I also run this quarterly roundtable feature for the magazine where five experts vote on linked propositions about emerging tech policy — things like whether a system shows emergent behaviour, whether serious harm is probable, and whether a remedy is actually feasible. We've started running into what I think is called the discursive dilemma: each expert is internally consistent, but when you pool all the votes the group ends up endorsing contradictory premises, or accepting premises that should logically push them toward a conclusion they actually reject.

I need a reusable decision procedre — like a flowchart in words — that I can run right after collecting the balots, to decide whether I should agregate the premises first and then derive the collective conclusion, or agregate the conclusions directly. For each step, tell me what counts as a sufficient answer, where I have to stop and say there's no legitimate collective judgement rather than forcing one, and which steps are pure logic versus value-laden judgement calls that I can't eliminate. Then run it on three cases: one where the panel mostly agrees the system is emergent and harm is likely, but splits on remedy feasibility so the agregate comes out weird; one where they reject all the premises but the conclusion still wins; and one where the procedure says no headline possible — which will annoy my editor. Also flag what the procedure can't catch, like strategic voting on premises, and be honest about its limits. Say how confident you are in what you're claiming, and single out the point you're least sure of. Write in British English, please — I'm drafting this between interviews on my phone so forgive the odd typo.
