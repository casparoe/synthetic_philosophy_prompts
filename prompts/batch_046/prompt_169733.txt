I work as a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, but I have been trying to formalize my own stance on Gödel’s arguments about God and an afterlife, and I want to know whether my commitments are jointly tenable or whether I am smuggling in contradictions. Here is my current package:

1. I accept a modal version of Gödel’s ontological argument: if a maximally perfect being is possible, then it exists necessarily. The logical structure feels sound to me—like verifying that a specification is consistent and then deriving that an implementation must exist.

2. Following Gödel’s unpublished reflections, I believe that rationality demands consciousness not be annihilated. A rational universe should not waste highly structured information, so there ought to be some form of post-mortem continuation.

3. I think minds are essentially formal structures—analogous to well-typed programs—so personal identity is continuity of that structure rather than continuity of biological substrate.

4. I also take Gödel’s incompleteness theorems to apply beyond mathematics: any formal system we could construct to fully describe reality, or an afterlife, must be incomplete. Therefore whatever continues must be “open-ended,” like an unbounded computation that never reaches a final fixed point.

5. I reject physicalism, but not in favor of a ghostly substance. Instead, I think consciousness is a mathematical relation—similar to a proof relation—not a physical process.

6. Here is my worry: if God exists necessarily and is omniscient, then every possible state of every mind is already present in the divine intellect. That makes an “afterlife” seem redundant—like running a backup process when all data already lives in the primary database.

7. I hold that conceivability is unreliable in mathematics (you cannot conceive your way out of a contradiction), but in metaphysics it can be a genuine guide to possibility. So the fact that I can conceive of my own non-existence does not show that annihilation is actually possible.

Please audit this set. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear tense but dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real tension specify how severe it is, what the cheapest revision would be, and what it costs. Order the tensions by severity, then tell me which single change would clean up the most problems overall. Do not lecture me on Gödel scholarship; treat my listed commitments as the object of analysis. Present this as a concise ranked audit—about 500 words maximum, in numbered or bulleted form.
