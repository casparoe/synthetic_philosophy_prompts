A recent essay summarizes Zygmunt Bauman's well-known argument about the Holocaust and applies it to the Historikerstreit debate over uniqueness and comparability. The summary runs as follows. Bauman's central claim is that the Holocaust was not a regression to pre-modern barbarism but rather a distinctly modern achievement, made possible by bureaucratic rationality, the technological division of labor, and what he terms adiaphorization—the separation of technical efficiency from ethical evaluation. Because these are generic structural features of modern societies rather than peculiarities of German culture, Bauman treats the Holocaust as fundamentally comparable to other large-scale, bureaucratically administered atrocities, including certain colonial campaigns and Stalinist purges; the specific targeting of Jews, on this reading, is contingent rather than defining. From this, the essayist draws a stronger conclusion: that the uniqueness thesis defended during the Historikerstreit is not merely an empirical error but an ideological defense mechanism. By isolating Auschwitz as an inexplicable singularity outside the continuum of modern history, uniqueness advocates allegedly protect modern rationality itself from indictment, and therefore anyone who takes Bauman seriously must reject the idea that Holocaust remembrance should carry any special moral status distinct from other historical catastrophes. I am unsure how much of this captures Bauman's actual position and how much is the essayist's extrapolation. Could you audit this summary? Please distinguish what is accurate to Bauman's view, what is a standard simplification, and what is a misrepresentation—especially regarding whether he denies all distinctive features of the Holocaust, whether he treats modern conditions as producing an inevitable outcome rather than an enabled possibility, and whether his argument genuinely entails rejecting special moral status for Holocaust remembrance. I am particularly interested in whether the comparison to colonial violence and Stalinist terror is endorsed by Bauman in precisely this form, or whether it is being smuggled in to strengthen a comparability claim he never made.
