okay so i'm dealing with heavy family stuff lately — my dad got sick suddenly last month and i dropped everything to be thier, and now i'm wondering whether i should've even needed a reason. that made me revisit Bernard Williams's one thought too many idea, and i tried to formalize it for a philosophy meetup, but the argument feels like it proves way too much.

(1) Williams says if you save your spouse and your reasoning is she is my spouse so she comes first, thats one thought too many. the right reason should just be her, with no mediating principle.
(2) but any non-random action involves some mental representation that distinguishes the target from other options — even a reflex has a trigger condition.
(3) so if explicit moral reasoning is corrupt, then implicit cognitive tagging — like the predictive brain giving her a high-precision prior — is also corrupt, since it still functionally distinguishes her from strangers.
(4) therefore integrity requires having literally no discriminatory mental structure at all regarding loved ones.
(5) but that makes integrity equivalent to random behavior, which destroys the concept.
(6) so Williams's objection, taken seriously, eliminates moral character rather than saving it.

i feel like the slip is between (2) and (3) — maybe implicit tagging isnt a thought in the relevant sense? — but i cant pin it. which step should i reject, and whats the cost? also please say what would change your mind: what distinction or argument would convince you this reasoning actually holds? im definatly smuggling in some half-baked neuroscience about precision-weighted priors, so call that out if its doing the philosophical work.
