Construct a reusable diagnostic procedure, grounded in Nietzsche's genealogical method, for an ethics consultant who must classify contemporary moral claims—such as the unconditional protection of vulnerability or the distribution of goods by merit—as expressing master morality, slave morality, or an unstable hybrid. The procedure should proceed in ordered steps: first, questions that identify whether the claim originates in active self-affirmation or reactive ressentiment; second, indicators that reveal a value's dependence on an opposing class or concept it purports to reject; third, explicit criteria for when a claim's structure—perhaps a modern synthesis of care and autonomy—exceeds the master-slave binary and requires direct interpretive judgment rather than classification. Apply the procedure to two worked cases: one in which it yields an unambiguous result, and one in which it breaks down. End with a brief, justified conclusion on whether this genealogical framework remains operationally viable for analyzing present-day moral discourse. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
