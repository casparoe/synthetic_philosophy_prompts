Schopenhauer's standard line is that all the other arts copy Platonic Ideas — painting copies the Idea of a tree, drama copies Ideas of human character, etc. But music is different. It doesn't copy an Idea at all; it's a direct objectification of the Will itself, the thing-in-itself beneath all phenomena. That's why music can move us without representing anything specific. The bass lines correspond to inorganic nature, harmony to the plant and animal world, melody to human striving. So there's a kind of acoustic cosmology built into the orchestra.

But here's the niche twist I'm trying to inject: what if we take Schopenhauer's claim literally about directness and apply it to synthesized sound? A pure sine wave has no overtones, no harmonic series that maps onto "nature" in his schema. It's just periodic pressure. Yet people still report emotional responses to isolated sine tones — not because they recognize a melody, but because the frequency literally vibrates in the chest cavity. So maybe Schopenhauer's hierarchy (bass = gravity, melody = will) gets inverted if we think about pre-cognitive bodily resonance. The "purest" music might not be a Beethoven symphony copying the Will through complex melody, but a sub-bass drone that bypasses representation entirely and hits the body before the mind can form an Idea of what it's feeling.

I'm not sure this is consistent with his metaphysics, though. If the Will is already non-representational, then adding synthesis doesn't change the ontology — it just strips away the representational layer he already said music lacks. So maybe my "inversion" is just a redescription. But I also want to argue that Schopenhauer's analogy between music and geometry (both being a priori forms) breaks down here because geometry is spatial and timeless in its pure form, whereas even a sine wave is temporal and embodied. The comparison might be doing more work than he admits.
