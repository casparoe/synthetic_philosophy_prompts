As a novelist attempting to construct a philosophically literate protagonist, I wish to render a specific misreading of Frank Ramsey's mature pragmatism with sufficient precision that the error itself illuminates her character. She treats the question of life's meaning not as an ethical inquiry but as a theoretical term demanding elimination through a Ramsey-style sentence: she proposes that all discourse of 'purpose' or 'significance' can be replaced by a structural network of observable behavioural dispositions and subjective betting quotients, leaving no residual metaphysical content. She believes this eliminative strategy is sanctioned by Ramsey's late turn away from emotivism—indeed, she interprets his growing cognitivism about ethics not as a recovery of objective value, but as an endorsement of such restructuring, whereby objective status attaches only to the formal apparatus of belief-habits rather than to any teleological content. I am concerned that I have imported a technical device from the philosophy of science into existential ethics, and that I have conflated Ramsey's decision-theoretic work on intertemporal choice with his ethics. Specifically, does Ramsey's pragmatism—which measures belief by its practical success and its disposition to action—actually permit the complete dissolution of teleological language into a calculus of credence-allocation across future states? Or does his mature position retain some non-eliminable, objectively grounded response to the meaning-of-life question that resists reduction to behavioural disposition? If my character concludes that life's meaning is identical to the optimal intertemporal distribution of subjective probabilities, have I attributed to Ramsey a reduction of ethics to decision theory that his writings on truth and belief never authorised?
