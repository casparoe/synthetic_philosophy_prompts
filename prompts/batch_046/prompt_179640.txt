I'm participating in a small interdisciplinary workshop where we've developed a shared vocabulary for describing sediment cores pulled from a particular lakebed. Over the past three months, one of our team members, Mara, has consistently uttered the neologism "velk" whenever she points to a particular band of gray-green clay that appears at roughly thirty centimeters in every core. The rest of us initially assumed "velk" named that visible band. But last week, Mara explained that for her, "velk" refers not to the visible stripe itself but to the anaerobic chemical state that produces it—something she detects with a portable sensor she carries. Meanwhile, another colleague insists "velk" denotes the temporal interval during excavation when that layer is first exposed, not a property of the material at all.

We all agree on every observable behavior: Mara points at the same spots, uses "velk" in the same contexts, and responds identically to questions about its presence or absence. Yet our ontologies seem to diverge radically—one treats it as a visible object-part, another as an invisible chemical condition, another as a stage in our procedure. I tried suggesting we simply stipulate a shared definition going forward, but Mara resisted, saying the word already has a fixed meaning for her that she hasn't been misapplying. This feels eerily similar to classic puzzles about reference and translation, but I'm not sure whether this is merely a practical communication problem we can solve by convention, or whether there's a deeper philosophical issue about whether "velk" has a single correct interpretation given all our shared behavioral dispositions.

Is Mara wrong to claim there's a fact of the matter about what "velk" means independent of our agreement? If two people can share every observable use of a term while referring to entirely different kinds of things—material bands, hidden properties, temporal episodes—does that show that reference is inherently underdetermined by behavior, or is there some further constraint, perhaps our shared causal interaction with the world or the role the term plays in our predictive practices, that should settle the matter? And practically, if I'm writing up our collaborative notes, should I treat "velk" as ambiguous and avoid it, or can I legitimately adopt one interpretation without misrepresenting Mara's contribution?
