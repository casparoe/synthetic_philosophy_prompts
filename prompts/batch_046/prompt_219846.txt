I came across a summary of the centipede game and backward induction in a blog post. It ran as follows: "In the centipede game, two players take turns choosing whether to stop and claim a payoff or continue so that the total pot grows; at each turn the player who stops receives slightly more than the one who continues. By backward induction—reasoning from the final node, where stopping is strictly dominant—rational players should foresee that cooperation will unravel at every prior stage, so both should stop immediately on the first move, even though mutual continuation to the end yields the highest joint payoff. This proves that common knowledge of rationality makes Pareto-optimal cooperation impossible in any sequential interaction. Consequently, the paradox shows that rational choice theory is self-defeating: it predicts behaviour that no rational person would actually follow, which is why experimental subjects almost always continue for several rounds."
