Student answer:
Lewis defines convention as a regularity in behavior that solves a coordination problem, where people prefer to do what others do and there is common knowledge of this. The forum double-space norm fits: people want to match the group style, so there's a coordination problem about fitting in. Its arbitrary because single-spacing works too. The spread happens through common knowledge—once its widespread, newcomers know that everyone expects it. Lewis allows conventions to form without explicit agreement, just through repeated interaction. However, Lewis said conventions must be sustained by rational preference, and since double-spacing offers no practical advantage, people don't really have a rational reason to prefer it. So Lewis can describe the stability but not the origin, which needs evolutionary copying. Also, Lewis thought conventions are always about actions that affect others, like driving, but writing style is private, so it falls outside his scope.
