my philosophy of religion study group keeps saying "Pascal's wager" like its one argument, usually summed up as "believe in God because you cant lose and might gain infinity." last week someone used it to claim evidentialism is self-defeating—if the payoff is infinite, any nonzero prior makes belief mandatory no matter the evidence. i feel like thats smuggling in like five different claims at once. can you lay out the precise theses that slogan could actually stand for? like: a pure expected-utility calc, practical rationality overriding epistemic norms, doxastic voluntarism (we can just choose belief), the idea that "cost" is near-zero, and maybe issues with comparing infinities across different possible deities. for each say whether its defensible, whether it actually dismisses evidentialism, and whether it survives the many-gods or dominated-strategy objections. i want a menu i can bring back, not one interpretation.
