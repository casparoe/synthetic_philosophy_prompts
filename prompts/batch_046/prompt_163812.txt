i just had a long fight with my friend over drinks about whether its ever okay to write history like the present was the inevitable finish line. she says it always flattens the people who lost and makes the harm look like tuition we paid for wisdom. anyway, i'm on the board for our city's civic memory center and we have to vote thursday on the narrative frame for the permanent gallery about 20th-century urban renewal. the staff proposal organizes everything chronologically as a march from ignorant demolition toward enlightened preservation — basically our current zoning code as the happy ending. the rival proposal organizes it thematically around displacement and debt without any timeline of improvement. can you draft the memo i'll circulate by wednesday? it should recommend either the staff frame, the rival frame, or the staff frame with specific conditions about labeling and sequencing, include a concrete timeline for implementation, and take seriously the objection that framing the past as progress toward the present doesn't just distort but actively changes how visitors understand who owes repair to whom. i want the reasoning to be something the staff curators can actually respond to, not just philosophy jargon.
