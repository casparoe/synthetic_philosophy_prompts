Write a short story that dramatizes the conceptual tension between perceptual and judgmental theories of emotion. Your protagonist, Elara, inhabits a bureaucratic system that requires citizens to classify every affective experience as either a bare feeling akin to a bodily sensation, a perceptual disclosure akin to seeing danger, or a rational judgment akin to condemning an injustice. She repeatedly undergoes an anomalous state—call it axiological chromatic resonance—in which she apprehends a moral wrong with the immediacy of color vision yet without the propositional structure of belief. Show, through concrete narrative detail rather than philosophical exposition, why this hybrid experience threatens the tripartite taxonomy and forces the question of whether emotion is best understood as feeling, perception, or judgment.
