Block has argued that phenomenal consciousness can 'overflow' access consciousness, citing experiments where subjects appear phenomenally aware of more stimuli than they can report or identify.

Consider the claim that in Dehaene's model, stimuli which trigger only local recurrent processing in sensory cortex — so-called 'preconscious' states — generate phenomenal consciousness without entering the global workspace. Evaluate whether this claim successfully defends Block's overflow argument against the charge that GWT collapses P-consciousness into A-consciousness. Student answer:

Block draws a line between access consciousness (A-consciousness) — being able to report, reason with and control information — and phenomenal consciousness (P-consciousness), which is the subjective feel or 'what it's like' aspect. In Baars and Dehaene's global workspace theory, information becomes conscious in the access sense when it gets globally broadcast across fronto-parietal networks; if it stays local, it's either subliminal or preconscious.

Block's overflow argument says that phenomenal consciousness can be richer than what we can access. For instance, in a brief display of letters, subjects might phenomenally experience the whole grid but only access and report a few items. This is taken to show P-consciousness overflows A-consciousness.

Now, Dehaene's framework includes these local recurrent loops in sensory cortex that don't trigger global ignition. These loops are precisely the neural correlate of Block's overflow: they sustain phenomenal processing without allowing global access. So Block and GWT are actually compatible — GWT explains the access side, while local recurrence explains the phenomenal side. Dehaene himself admits that preconscious local states might carry some phenomenal character, they just fail to reach reportability.

Therefore, the claim that local loops generate phenomenal experience without global access is correct, and it saves Block's argument from the collapse objection. One might worry that this makes phenomenal consciousness too cheap, but Block is happy with that since he thinks even animals with limited cortex have it. So yes, the defence works.
