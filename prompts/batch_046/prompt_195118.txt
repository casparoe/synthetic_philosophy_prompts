everyone in my philosphy of mind circle keeps comparing pain to a car's check-engine light: the sensation is just information about tissue damage, the badness is all in the panic and avoidance, and pain asymbolia is like the bulb being on but the driver not caring — proof that the signal itself isnt harmful. they lean on this to argue that pain isn't intrinsically bad, only instrumentally useful. i want you to audit how far the analogy actually holds.

give me the maping table: what corresponds to the bulb, the wiring, the driver, the garage, and the decision to pull over. then the break points — does the alarm have an intrinsic qualitative feel, or is it pure data? what does asymbolia actually show about the separation between nociception and suffering, and does it support the claim that affect is just an add-on? also, does the analogy smuggle in that all value lives in the reaction, and if so, is that justified by the clinical picture?

then there are the weird cases — people who seek pain in masochism or religious practice. do those fit the alarm model or blow it up? and finally, does the conclusion — that pains badness is purely derivative — survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy doing all the work? if the car-light picture fails, is there a better analogy for how nociception relates to suffering?
