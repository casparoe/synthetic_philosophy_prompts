I propose that the paradox confuses two kinds of impossibility: direct logical contradiction (such as a round square) and what I call agent-circular task descriptions, in which the conditions for success implicitly depend on the agent's own failure within the same modal frame. Creating any stone is one power; lifting any stone is another; but the compound task, create a stone that this same being in this same act-structure cannot lift, embeds the agent's capacities into the object-description in a self-referential loop. If omnipotence is defined precisely as the capacity to actualize any state of affairs that lacks this agent-circular structure, then there is no contradiction—only an ill-formed instruction, comparable to asking for a complete map that must exclude itself. I take this to be more precise than the familiar reply that omnipotence is simply limited to what is logically possible, because my framing explains why certain descriptions are excluded without reducing omnipotence to an empty tautology.
