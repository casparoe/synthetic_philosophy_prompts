I am preparing an interview for my philosophy podcast, The Aesthetic Margin, with Dr. Elena Vasquez, a philosopher of art who has recently advanced a revisionist interpretation of Hume's essay Of the Standard of Taste. Vasquez contends that Hume's true judges should not be understood as converging on a single correct judgment through shared delicacy of sentiment; instead, she argues that the standard functions as what she calls a friction protocol—a structured deliberative practice among calibrated critics that preserves, rather than dissolves, divergence at the margins. On her account, this reading resolves the tension between Hume's apparent subjectivism about sentiment and his normative claim that some judgments are genuinely better, without collapsing taste into either democratic aggregation or elite expertise. She anticipates the objection that preserving divergence renders the standard toothless, and replies that the protocol imposes its own constraints: freedom from prejudice, comparative breadth across genres and traditions, and what she terms reflexive instability, meaning a critic's willingness to revise her own calibrations when confronted with persistent, well-reasoned dissent.

I want eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her merely to restate this position. Specifically, I need at least one question pressing the tension between her friction protocol and Hume's own insistence that true judges eventually agree on canonical works; at least one probing whether reflexive instability secretly reintroduces a hierarchy of sensibility that privileges critics who happen to change their minds; and at least one exploring what her view implies for contemporary crowd-sourced rating platforms or algorithmic recommendation systems, with a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that such systems lack the deliberative reciprocity her protocol demands. For each question, please include a brief follow-up that anticipates her most probable defense.
