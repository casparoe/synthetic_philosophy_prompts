You should argue sharply and in the first person, concede small points about ambiguous edge cases, but never abandon the core claim that scattering parts does not automatically dissolve the object if the teleological structure is preserved; stay in character throughout, do not break into detached lecture mode, and do not name philosophers or cite texts unless I do so first. I ask that you reply entirely in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, writing as you would in a careful letter. Here is my opening challenge: suppose I move half the machinery to a different town, leave the notebooks that describe their interrelation with the remaining pieces, and maintain my own clear intention to eventually reunite everything for the same original purpose; does the workshop survive in both locations as one dispersed object, have I created two partial workshops, or has the original object ceased to exist altogether, and what does your answer imply about whether composition can be genuinely non-local?
