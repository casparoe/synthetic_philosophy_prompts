im making a handout for a metaphysics seminar on the Death in Damascus fable — the servant who flees to Damascus to avoid Death and finds him waiting there. I want a taxonomy, basically a decision tree, that sorts interpretive positions by answers to concrete questions about the story's logic, not by which label people like.

Start from a student who just heard the tale. The branches should turn on: (1) does the flight causally produce the meeting, or is it coincidence? (2) is Death's line a prediction or an arranged appointment? (3) is the inevitability specific to agents who recieve the warning, or universal? Phrase every node as a yes/no or either/or that doesn't require knowing jargon.

Must include these leaves: hard logical fatalism; causal-loop compatibilism (where avoidance is part of the mechanism); open-theist avoidance (foreknowledge without determination); and "indexical Damascus" — the niche view that inevitability only binds warned agents, leaving unwarned people genuinely open. Also include an unnamed leaf for the combo where flight is causally necessary but modal freedom stays intact.

Every leaf should name the position (or say 'unnamed') and state its most awkward commitment in one sentence. Note any empty spots in the logical space — like acausal flight plus universal inevitability — and say briefly if it's incoherent, ugly, or just unexplored. Flag pairs your tree can't really seperate. Around 6 to 8 leaves total. Audience is undergrads.

Don't ask for a bibliography, citations, or reading list — the model should build this from reasoning about the narrative itself. Write casual, like you're texting a colleague; a typo or two is totally fine, so don't stress about perfect spelling.
