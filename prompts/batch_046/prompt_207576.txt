My credence that structural realism provides the correct account of how mature physical theories survive revolutionary change is roughly 0.60; hers is roughly 0.85. Please give me your own credence, but more importantly decompose the issue into four sub-questions and assign each a rough probability: (1) whether continuity across theory change is genuinely structural—preservation of mathematical form—rather than merely pragmatic or heuristic; (2) whether cases in which a predecessor theory's metric structure is abandoned while its topological invariants survive, as in proposed transitions toward quantum gravity, count as supporting structural realism or as undermining it; (3) whether the pessimistic meta-induction applies at all when the older theory is explicitly restricted to a limited domain rather than globally falsified; and (4) whether structural realism yields any distinctive normative guidance for current scientific practice, or whether it is purely a retrospective redescription of history. State which of these four you think most likely explains the gap between 0.60 and 0.85. Then identify the single piece of evidence or argument that would most move your own number. Lead with your bottom-line credence in the very first sentence; then present the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
