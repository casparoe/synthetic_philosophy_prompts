I serve as the ethics-compliance lead for an international advocacy NGO that runs digital and field campaigns on climate policy. Every quarter my team reviews proposed messaging strategies—ranging from loss-framed tax-policy ads to influencer-led social-proof videos to simplified infographics that omit trade-off data—to decide whether they cross from legitimate persuasion into manipulation. To guide us, I have synthesized a working principle drawn from autonomy-based moral psychology and transparency conditions in the philosophy of language: a message is manipulative only if it jointly satisfies (1) Intentional Source Opacity—the sender deliberately structures the message so that a recipient, upon ordinary reflection without specialist training, cannot identify the specific psychological mechanism being triggered (e.g., loss aversion, authority bias, affective contagion) and its relevance to the claim being advanced; and (2) Rational Pathway Substitution—the claim's acceptance is causally sustained primarily through that masked mechanism rather than through the recipient's evaluation of the reasons offered, even when reasons are present on the page. Legitimate persuasion, conversely, requires Source Transparency—the mechanism is either named or readily inferable—and Rational Sustaining, meaning the mechanism supports but does not substitute for reasoning. I also want the procedure to include an Epistemic Reciprocity Filter: the communicator must be able to endorse the message structure if the audience's epistemic position and the sender's were reversed, without special pleading.

Please turn this into an operational checklist my team can run in under twenty minutes per proposal, specifying the ordered questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure halts with a clear verdict of "legitimate" or "manipulation," and where it must hand the decision to a human reviewer because the question involves values rather than classifiable structure. Include three worked applications: (a) a campaign video that uses unnamed loss-framing for a carbon-tax proposal while presenting statistical evidence; (b) a peer-influencer post that openly states it relies on social proof and asks viewers to check the linked primary study; and (c) a proposal to use rapid-cut extreme-weather imagery—designed to trigger affective arousal—alongside a policy brief that omits any mention of adaptation costs, where the procedure should yield an answer the advocacy team will not like. After the procedural text, provide a brief structured summary in JSON with the exact fields: case_name, procedure_output, key_test_applied, judgment_call_flag, unwelcome_result_noted, and limitation_acknowledged. Finally, flag explicitly what the procedure cannot settle—such as whether emotional engagement itself undermines autonomy regardless of transparency—and note where misfire is likely if applied by reviewers without philosophical training.
