Next week's guest is an unnamed philosopher who defends a specific anti-Humean position—dispositional essentialism about laws—holding that natural laws express primitive modal relations between actually instantiated properties, that these relations are not reducible to categorical bases plus Humean regularities, that the governing relation need not depend on a universalist ontology of shared abstract entities, and that modal force is localized within particular property-structures rather than distributed through them.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her to restate this view, but instead press the points where the literature genuinely presses anti-Humean essentialism. Each question should anticipate the reply she is most likely to give and include a sharp follow-up keyed precisely to that reply. I require coverage of: (a) the identification circularity—whether identifying a property such as charge solely by its attractive and repulsive modal profile explains the corresponding law or merely redescribes it; (b) the governing metaphor—whether treating laws as governing behavior covertly imports a causal or normative notion that a metaphysician of science should reject; and (c) fundamental constants—whether the value of a constant like the fine-structure constant is metaphysically necessitated by the modal profiles of the properties involved, and what this implies about the intelligibility of counterfactual worlds with different values. I also want at least one question addressing higher-order modal structure: whether there are meta-laws that govern the modal connections themselves, and if so, whether that generates a vicious regress.

In addition, include one final question that asks what theoretical or empirical development—short of direct observation of modal force—could genuinely weaken her commitment, preventing her from dismissing the challenge as merely an unfinished research program rather than a genuine objection.
