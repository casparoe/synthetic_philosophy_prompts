A seminar in modal metaphysics has rendered the following cross-world identity verdicts. (1) The Eiffel Tower, if constructed in Paris in 1889 by the same engineer with the same puddled iron but standing 350 meters rather than 330 meters, is still the Eiffel Tower. (2) A structure built in Paris in 1889 with the same dimensions, purpose, and location, but constructed by a different engineer with steel rather than iron, is a counterpart, not the Eiffel Tower. (3) Marie Curie, born in Warsaw in 1867 to the same parents, who lives as a farmer and never studies radioactivity, is still Marie Curie. (4) An individual born in Lyon in 1867 to different parents, who replicates Curie's exact psychological profile, scientific achievements, and social role, is not Marie Curie but at best a counterpart. (5) Modern English, if spoken continuously in Britain but with every lexical item replaced by a homophone bearing a different meaning, is still Modern English; spoken continuously in France from scratch with the identical vocabulary, syntax, and literature of actual Modern English but without any historical transmission from Britain, is not Modern English but a syntactic analog. (6) The University of Bologna, founded in 1088 in Bologna by the same scholars with the same statutes, is Bologna; founded in 1088 in Padua by different scholars with identical statutes and later named Bologna, is a counterpart institution, not Bologna itself.

Now consider a new pair of cases involving the founding charter of a republic adopted in Philadelphia in 1776. In world W1, the physical parchment is causally continuous with the actual charter—written by the same signers in the same room with the same ink—but its text lists grievances against Spain, asserts independence from France, and is never ratified by any assembly; it is filed away and forgotten. In world W2, a different parchment is written in Philadelphia in 1776 by different authors with different ink, but its text lists the actual grievances against Britain, asserts independence from Britain in the same language, is ratified by the same colonies, and serves as the constitutional origin of the same subsequent nation-state.

Predict the seminar's verdict on W1 and W2. Which of the earlier six cases is the closest analog for each? The precedents appear jointly consistent with more than one rule—perhaps a universal causal-origin essentialism, or a category-relative view that treats biological, artifactual, linguistic, and institutional entities differently with respect to whether functional purpose and representational content are also essential. Lay out at least two such rules that accommodate all six verdicts, say which one best predicts the new rulings and why, and identify one further hypothetical case that would force a choice between the rules you have not eliminated. If you think the precedents genuinely underdetermine the decision, state what principle the seminar would be adopting with each possible ruling and which analog it would be privileging.
