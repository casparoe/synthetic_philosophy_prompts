I have been trying to work out on my own why Daniel Dennett and Gregg Caruso remain deadlocked on punishment and just deserts, given that both reject libertarian free will. My own hypothesis is that they are not really fighting over neuroscience or over the same ethical principle, but over the ontology of responsibility itself: Dennett treats it as an operational stance adopted for predictive control, whereas Caruso treats it as a real property that determinism eliminates. Is that diagnosis correct, or is there instead a hidden empirical disagreement about the brain, a hidden normative disagreement about what counts as a moral reason, or simply two different questions—one about institutional design, one about metaphysical desert—being fought under a single heading? I want an honest diagnosis of what kind of impasse this is, supported by what each side actually says, together with an argument about whether anything—a conceptual distinction, a finding, a change of framing—could actually break it, or whether it is an irreducible clash of bedrock intuition.
