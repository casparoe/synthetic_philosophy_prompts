I'm moderating a podcast intervew with a political theorist reviving Simone Weil's argument that parties manufacture collective passions which drown out individual judgement — she extends this to informal online factions, not just formal parties. She's particlarly interested in Weil's idea of gravity for how loyalty pulls people away from attention to reality. I want about eight questions that don't let her just restate Weil. One should press whether that gravity idea survives if we treat attention as a scarce cognitive resource rather than a spiritual virtue, with a follow-up keyed to the reply she'll probably give. Another should ask about the tension that Weil condemned party loyalty but still worked inside collective resistance bodies — how does she square that, and what's the next move? A third should ask how she distinguishes harmful collective passion from the solidarity needed for actual political coordination, especially when informal groups need to act quickly. For each, include the sharp follow-up.
