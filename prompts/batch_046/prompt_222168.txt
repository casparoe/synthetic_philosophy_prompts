Having spent my career in cell biology before turning recently to philosophy, I have become stuck on what seems to be a confusion about origin essentialism—the view that I necessarily originated from the particular sperm and egg that in fact produced me. In my old field we routinely decompose an egg into its nuclear genome, its mitochondrial DNA, and its cytoplasmic machinery (maternal mRNAs, developmental determinants, and so forth), which leads me to ask the following. Suppose, counterfactually, that conception had employed the very same sperm and the very same nuclear genome from my mother’s egg, but with the cytoplasm and mitochondria supplied entirely by a donor egg, leaving the nuclear origin unchanged. Would I still have come from “the same egg”? If origin essentialism answers yes, then it appears to treat the egg as essentially its nucleus, which strikes me as a biologically arbitrary reduction. If it answers no—so that any alteration in cytoplasmic source makes the origin metaphysically different—then a child born through such cytoplasmic donation is a different person from the one she “would have been,” which seems implausible. Is the error here that I am importing biological decomposition into a metaphysical relation of causal origin? Or does the tension reveal something genuinely problematic about formulating origin conditions in terms of “the same sperm and egg”?
