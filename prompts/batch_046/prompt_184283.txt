Write an essay on whether global workspace theory can be revised to account for phenomenal consciousness, or whether access and phenomenal consciousness remain genuinely distinct phenomena requiring separate explanations. Focus on the tension between the theory's broadcast mechanism—typically characterized as capacity-limited global availability of information for report and reasoning—and the apparent richness of phenomenal experience, which seems to present far more content simultaneously than can be broadcast at once. Consider Block's overflow argument and its response within the workspace framework: can phenomenal consciousness be identified with a form of pre-broadcast recurrent integration, or does the capacity limit of working memory (often estimated at four independent chunks) demonstrate that phenomenal content necessarily exceeds access? Weigh the strongest arguments for both positions. Reach and defend a specific conclusion: either that global workspace theory should be treated strictly as an account of access consciousness, leaving phenomenal consciousness unexplained, or that a revised version of the theory—perhaps one that distinguishes global reportability from a lower-threshold self-monitoring recurrence within the workspace—can genuinely capture phenomenal consciousness without collapsing the distinction. Your verdict should address whether the access/phenomenal distinction survives your proposed revision.
