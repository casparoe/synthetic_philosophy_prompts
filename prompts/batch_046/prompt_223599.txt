Given Popper's well-known emphasis on fallibilism, the logical asymmetry between verification and falsification, and his evolutionary account of knowledge growth through error elimination, I want you to construct a speculative but philosophically disciplined reconstruction of his likely position—not merely on whether LLMs belong inside science, but specifically on whether a system that produces only probabilistic, non-explanatory correlations could ever contribute to scientific knowledge in his framework, and whether the epistemic opacity of such architectures represents a fundamental failure of scientific rationality or merely a practical obstacle to be managed by social institutions of peer review and replication. Please address how he might have evaluated the distinction between a model that outputs falsifiable-in-principle but unfalsifiable-in-practice hypotheses and a traditional scientific theory that invites direct, targeted refutation, and indicate which aspect of modern machine learning he would have found most threatening to the open society's reliance on critical rationality.
