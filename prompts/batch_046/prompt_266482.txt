Two colleagues were arguing about Kurt Gödel’s work on time. (1) One said Gödel’s rotating-universe solution to Einstein’s field equations—where uniform rotation produces closed timelike curves that let an observer return to their own past—should be read as a reductio ad absurdum of objective time: if taking temporal reality seriously permits such loops, then temporal reality is illusory, which aligns with Gödel’s philosophical claim that the lapse of time is absent from physics. (2) The other replied that this reads metaphysics into a mathematical curiosity: the model requires a precisely rotating matter distribution and a specific critical radius beyond which the curves appear, conditions that do not hold in our universe, so Gödel was merely cataloguing an exotic solution rather than deriving idealism from cosmology. (3) The first countered that Gödel explicitly tied the projects together—he argued that special relativity already undermines objective time by making simultaneity frame-dependent, and the rotating universe extends this by showing that even local causal ordering can become circular, so the physics continuously supports the philosophy. (4) The second pushed back that this is a non sequitur: the absence of a global present in flat spacetime does not entail the collapse of local temporal structure, and using a highly symmetric, physically unrealistic metric to argue that time is unreal mistakes a boundary case for a general metaphysical argument. (5) The first then added that Gödel’s point was not empirical prediction but conceptual possibility—Einstein’s own equations allow worlds without objective temporal passage, which should weaken our confidence that our world requires it. (6) The second concluded that logical possibility does not imply metaphysical illusion, and that Gödel’s denial of objective time stands or falls on independent arguments about change and consciousness, not on cosmological modeling.

Please adjudicate this disagreement move by move. For each of the six moves above, rate it from clearly mistaken to clearly correct and briefly justify the rating. Then say which party is more right overall about the relationship between Gödel’s rotating universe and his philosophical denial of objective time, and identify any important point both are missing—particularly regarding whether Gödel intended the cosmological model as an illustration, a proof, or something in between.
