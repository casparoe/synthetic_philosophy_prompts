ok so i'm trying to sort out whether my functionalist commitments actually hang together or if i'm just special-pleading. here's roughly where i've landed:
(1) mental states are causal/functional roles, not tied to specific biologcal stuff.
(2) multiple realizability is the best argument for this - if an octopus or silicon chip can feel pain, that proves it's the organization that matters.
(3) but i reject Block's Chinese Nation thing: a billion people with radios can't be conscious.
(4) my reason is that the causal connections are all wrong - in a brain it's direct electrochemical signaling, in the nation it's people walking around with walky-talkies, so the functional architechture isn't preserved at the right grain.
(5) yet i also think that if we built a silicon chip copying every synaptic connection exactly, it could feel pain, since the pattern is right even if the substrate changes.
(6) i'm also an externalist about content: what my belief "water" refers to depends on my causal history with H2O, not just my internal role.

i feel pulled in two directions. (4) seems to undercut (5) - if substrate and connection-type matter enough to block the nation, why don't they block silicon? and if they don't block silicon, why does the nation fail? is there a principled line, or am i arbitrarily allowing chips? also, (6) feels like it might clash with (1), since functionalism sounds purely internal. but maybe that's a fake tension? which ones are actually in trouble, and what's the cheapest fix?
