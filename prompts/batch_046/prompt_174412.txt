For my graduate seminar on AI alignment and value theory, I have been working through a framing I am calling counterfactual footprint minimization: the idea that an agent should restrict itself to actions whose causal effects lie within the range of outcomes that would have occurred in its absence, but measured over a domain that is not already value-laden. Before I build anything formal on this, I want the full desiderata for a theory of low-impact agency that attempts to define "avoid side effects" without first committing to a complete axiological framework. Write the specification: what phenomena the theory must explain, which cases it must get right, which distinctions it must preserve, and which results from decision theory or causal inference it must be consistent with, together with the motivating consideration behind each requirement.

Specifically, the list must include: how to distinguish genuinely unintended side effects from outcomes that are structurally inevitable given the agent's goal, with a concrete case showing why collapsing them undermines the concept; how to handle causal overdetermination—where background processes and the agent's action both contribute to an outcome—without resolving the ambiguity by fiat in favor of either innocence or culpability; whether the baseline for measuring "no change" can be fixed in an agent-independent way, or whether it must be relativized to the agent's epistemic state, and what decision-theoretic costs each choice carries; how to separate "small" impacts that are morally neutral (rearranging grains of sand) from "large" ones (altering atmospheric chemistry) without smuggling in a prior value-laden scale of preservation; and whether the theory can remain silent on ends while still being action-guiding, or whether every operationalization of "low impact" secretly presupposes a prior specification of what counts as valuable to preserve. Flag which of these are genuinely non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences, and identify any subset that looks jointly unsatisfiable—especially if the tension arises from needing both an objective baseline and an agent-relative one.

Finally, briefly score two existing families of approach against this list—something like impact-regularized reinforcement learning and the indifference methods drawn from causal decision theory—indicating for each which desiderata they meet only by implicitly importing the kind of value framework they claim to avoid, and which desiderata they simply fail. Do not provide bibliographies or reading lists; I want the audit of the criteria themselves, since that is where the conceptual work lies.
