okay so i was arguing with a friend after our epistomology seminar about williamson's anti-luminosity stuff but then we tried to mash it together with the KK principle and ended up in this weird place. here's the chain we wrote down:

(1) KK holds: if you know p then you know you know p.
(2) Luminosity for pain: if you're actually in pain, then you're in a position to know it.
(3) If you're in a position to know p and nothing is blocking you, then you do know p.
(4) So if you're in pain you know you're in pain.
(5) By KK applied again, you know that you know you're in pain.
(6) Now apply (2) not just to pain but to the knowledge-state itself: if you know you're in pain, then you're in a position to know that you know you're in pain. (this is the step that felt like a stretch to me)
(7) By (3) you actually do know that.
(8) Repeat forever and you get an infinite ascending stack.
(9) Each level in the stack is a genuinely different mental representation, like nested boxes in working memory, so that's an infinite number of simulataneous mental states.
(10) But no human mind can host infinite simultaneous representations.
(11) So if both KK and luminosity hold, being in pain is psychologically impossible for us.
(12) That's clearly false.
(13) So one of the premises has to go.

i feel like the slip is either between (6) and (7) — we're misapplying luminosity to knowledge-states instead of first-order feelings — or in (9), where we assume knowing-you-know costs a separate mental token. but i can't tell which error is the real one and which is just a symptom. if i have to keep (1) and (2) intact and just kill whatever lets us build the infinite stack, what's the cheapest fix? does rejecting (9) mean second-order knowledge is transparent without being another state? or is the mistake earlier, and what does fixing it cost for the KK principle?
