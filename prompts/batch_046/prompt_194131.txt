Having spent thirty years in experimental condensed-matter physics before turning to philosophy of science, I find the Everettian interpretation of quantum mechanics conceptually tantalizing but mathematically frustrating, particularly with regard to the so-called Born rule problem. I am not asking for yet another derivation; rather, I want the full specification of what any adequate theory of probability within a deterministic, branching universal wavefunction must actually deliver in order to count as solving that problem—not merely reproducing the quantum probabilities, but explaining why they govern rational expectation and decision-making for an observer who knows that every decoherent outcome is realized somewhere. To avoid rehearsing well-worn debates, I want the desiderata framed around a specific conceptual tension: decoherence does not always yield sharply delineated branches, but rather produces a continuum of approximately decoherent histories with fuzzy boundaries, so any account must specify how probability weights apply when an observer cannot cleanly identify which “copy” they are, and must do so without presupposing a pre-quantum notion of statistical frequency or an ad hoc privileged measure.

Please lay out the requirements list with the motivating case behind each: which phenomena it must explain (for example, why an agent should bet according to squared amplitudes rather than by counting branches, how it handles the self-location problem when the number of future copies is ill-defined due to those fuzzy boundaries, and how it respects the distinction between objective deterministic evolution and subjective credence), which conceptual distinctions it must preserve (such as between epistemic uncertainty about one’s branch and ontic indeterminacy, or between global wavefunction structure and local experiential content), and which physical results it cannot contradict (unitary Schrödinger evolution, empirical equivalence with textbook quantum mechanics for all local experiments, and the absence of a physically distinguished basis). For each requirement, indicate whether it is non-negotiable for any Everettian account or a preference of a particular camp—say, those who demand a decision-theoretic derivation versus those who require a physical mechanism that singles out the amplitude measure. Crucially, flag any pair or subset of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable: for instance, whether requiring both sharply defined branches and purely unitary dynamics without hidden variables is incompatible with the fuzziness of realistic decoherence, and whether demanding that probability be both purely subjective and globally normative across all copies creates a contradiction when copy-weights are structurally unequal because of decoherence gradients. Keep the emphasis on the criteria and their conceptual tensions rather than on scoring particular theorists, though a brief note on how symmetry-based, decision-theoretic, and self-locating-credence approaches fare against your list is welcome.
