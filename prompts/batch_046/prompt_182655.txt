I'm working on a scene for a novel where two old friends, Mara and Leo, argue in a bar about Hilbert's hotel and wether it proves actual infinities are real. I want the philosophy to hold up but not sound like a lecture. Can you go through there exchange move by move, rate each step from clearly wrong to clearly right, and say who's right about what? Please answer in American English.

(1) Mara: Hilbert's hotel proves actual infinity is coherent. The hotel's full with infinite rooms, but you can still fit a new guest by shifting everyone up one. That's Cantor's point: a completed infinite set can be in one-to-one correspondence with a proper subset of itself.

(2) Leo: That's potential infinity with extra steps. You never actually finish filling all the rooms; there's just a rule for assigning each guest. It's like counting forever - you don't ever reach infinity, you just never stop.

(3) Mara: But Cantor's diagonal argument isn't about counting. It proves there are more real numbers than naturals. That size difference is about sets that already exist all at once, not about an endless process. If you reject actual infinity you have to gut standard analysis.

(4) Leo: I accept the math as formalism, not ontology. Cantor shows the reals can't be enumerated in a given system - he doesn't show there's a "completed" collection out there in reality. The hotel works the same way: it's a mapping rule, not a building with infinitely many doors.

(5) Mara: Then explain the shift. To move every guest up one room, you act on the whole infinite set simultaneously. That only works if the infinite occupancy is already complete and available to be operated on as a totality.

(6) Leo: Abstractly yes, but there's a locality problem. In any real universe, you can't coordinate changes across infinitely distant points at once because signals can't exceed light speed. So the hotel can't be physically built, which means it proves logical possibility, not that actual infinities exist in nature. Mara's smuggling physical intuition into math and then back out.

Please adjudicate: who's right on each point, and what are they both missing?
