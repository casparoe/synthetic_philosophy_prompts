In my advanced practical-reason seminar, a debate about the wrong-kind-reasons distinction has reached an impasse that I find methodologically instructive. The participants all agree on the standard taxonomy: bribery and threats are wrong-kind reasons for belief, while evidence is right-kind; prudential considerations are wrong-kind reasons for loving someone, while their admirable qualities are right-kind. The disagreement concerns whether this taxonomy tracks a single underlying distinction or a family of unrelated phenomena. One camp—call them the unity theorists—insists that every wrong-kind reason fails in the same way: it does not bear on the truth of the proposition or the value of the object in the manner required by the attitude's constitutive aim. The other camp—the pluralists—argues that wrong kind is an umbrella term covering at least three distinct failures: failure of evidential relevance, failure of agential authorization, and failure of structural fit with the attitude's normative profile. For a year, neither side has moved the other: both accept all the examples, both reject counterexamples proposed by the other side, and both claim the other is changing the subject when they invoke constitutive aim. My working hypothesis is that this is not a hidden empirical disagreement about cognitive architecture, but a disagreement about which cases should count as central data—essentially, a dispute about the explanandum disguised as a dispute about the explanation. But I am unsure whether the pluralist's disaggregation is a genuine theoretical advance or merely a verbal dissolution of a problem that should remain unified. What kind of stall is this, exactly? Is there a concealed formal question about the logic of reasons-for-attitudes, or are the two sides answering different questions under the shared label wrong-kind reasons? And given your diagnosis, what would progress look like here—an argument that forces a choice between explanatory parsimony and descriptive adequacy, an experiment that distinguishes evidential from agential failure, or an acknowledgment that no further resolution is available?
