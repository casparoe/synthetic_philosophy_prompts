I was at this philosophy of science reading group last tuesday and got pulled into an argument between Maya and Derek about Friedmans F-twist — yknow, the claim that unrealistic assumptions in a model are totally fine as long as the predictions come out right. Derek said that works okay for basic supply-and-demand curves but it falls apart for climate models, because if your cloud feedback parameter is off in the wrong structural direction you dont just miss a point forecast, you miss the entire dynamics that policymakers actually need to intervene on. Maya pushed back that Derek was just describing bad predictions, which is literally Friedmans point: if the climate model tracks temperatures well over the range we care about, who cares that the microphysics is cartoonish? Then Derek said no, its not about point predictions, its about counterfactual robustness — you need the right correlation for markets maybe, but for climate you have to ask what happens if emissions drop by half, and if your homogenized mixing assumption hides local feedback loops, the model can nail the 2020 temperature and still be useless for the 2050 policy scenario. Maya replied that every model has a limited counterfactual range, and demanding structural realism just smuggles in an impossible standard. Derek then brought up this comparison he read about agent-based epidemic models versus compartmental SIR ones — apparently the homogeneous mixing assumption in SIR made COVID peak-load predictions look okay in some cities but totally failed in others where superspreading events dominated, so the unrealistic assumption wasnt innocent simplification but actively masked variance that mattered for hospital-allocation decisions. Maya said thats just an empirical failure of that specific model, not a philosophical failure of the F-twist itself. They asked me to referee and I honestly couldnt figure it out. Can you adjudicate point by point — whos right about whether unrealistic assumptions can be harmless, does Dereks counterfactual-robustness objection actually hit Friedman or just change the subject, and what are both of them missing about what idealization is actually for?
