i’m a PhD student in philosophy of politics and i sit as the grad rep on our university’s international research ethics board. we’ve got a concrete case for thursday: a cross-national team wants approval for a civic-trust study that collects not just survey answers but what the PI calls “behavioral residue” — inferred social-graph patterns and predictive tags — from participants in germany, the us, and china. thier plan is to dump everything into an open-access repository with only minimal anonymization, arguing that american-style openness is the best shield against state misuse. the german side says this violates GDPR purpose limitation becuase the residue can be re-identified with enough contextual data, and the chinese collaboraters warn that even fully anonymized aggregates can feed social-credit inference engines that harm people collectively rather than individually. i need to draft the board’s decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with a clear bottom line and reasoning that seriously engages all three positions, including the strongest argument for the side that loses. the memo should also tackle the philosophical question hiding underneath: how much should citizens hide from the state, and is transparency always protective or can it become a weapon in different institutional contexts? i’m particularly stuck on whether there’s a real distinction between individual privacy and collective opacity — something like predictive anonymity failure — that ought to shape whatever conditions we impose. please don’t ask for a bibliography or reading list; write the reasoning so it stands on its own and the researchers can actually reply to it. make it read like a formal board document but with the argument laid out clearly.
