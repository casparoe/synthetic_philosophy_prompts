Next month I present at a philosophy of art and metaphysics joint seminar my view that the classic statue-and-clay puzzle is founded on a category mistake about material kinds. I argue that what we call a statue is not constituted by clay but by a fired ceramic composite whose structural rigidity and surface properties realize an aesthetic-functional form; the unfired model—what is usually misidentified as "the clay statue"—is in fact a distinct object, a maquette, that bears only a causal-genetic relation to the finished work. On my account, the statue's persistence conditions depend on the preservation of its sculptural form and its capacity to bear patina and weathering in ways appropriate to ceramic, which permits gradual material replacement during restoration, whereas the maquette's identity is tied to its chemical plasticity and is destroyed if reshaped. This means constitution is not a relation between a statue and a lump of clay sharing a location, but a temporal-causal chain linking two objects of different material kinds.

The audience includes metaphysicians committed to the standard constitution view with dominant-sortals, four-dimensionalists who regard constitution-talk as redundant with temporal-part overlap, and philosophers of art who hold that artistic identity is fixed by the artist's final intentional act rather than by chemical classification. Give me the objections in roughly the order they will arise—first from the metaphysicians, then the four-dimensionalists, then the art philosophers—phrased as each group would actually phrase them. For each objection, indicate whether I should concede part of it or hold firm; provide a concise reply suitable for the seminar's question period; identify the weakest point in that reply; and include the objection I am hoping nobody asks: namely, what my view implies for collaborative or deliberately unfinished works where no discrete firing event cleanly separates maquette from statue. Please also flag the specific premise in my argument—the claim that chemical plasticity is essential to clay-identity and is necessarily lost upon firing—that I should prepare to defend directly rather than hope they overlook, since I take it to be the most vulnerable step in the chain.
