I'm applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship in philosophy of science and need a polished two-page proposal. My half-formed idea concerns the Principal Principle and its interaction with self-locating uncertainty in physical theories that contain layered probabilistic structures. I want to ask whether deference to objective chance must be bifurcated into corresponding principles, whether these principles can come into conflict in concrete cases, and whether resolving such conflicts requires abandoning the Principal Principle, restricting its domain, or supplementing it with an independent indexical rationality constraint. The existing philosophical literature, as I understand it, addresses self-locating belief and objective chance largely in isolation; integrating them reveals a gap in how we formulate rational credence when scientific theories themselves generate both kinds of uncertainty.
