I sit on the advisory board for our museums new antiquities wing and we have to vote thursday on the framing for the big Rome gallery opening in march. The curators want the central narrative titled The Fall of Rome: Catastrophe and Renewal but ever since my dad went through that long slow decline with his illness last year I've been weirdly sensitive to how we frame collapse versus gradual change. I read some Gibbon and then a bunch of newer climate and archaeology takes, and now I'm convinced that calling it a "fall" bakes in a false discontinuity — the institutions mostly transformed, they didn't vanish overnight. But my co-chair, who's an actual classicist, says dropping "fall" erases the real violence and rupture that people experienced around 476, and she's not wrong.

Can you draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote? I need it to recommend one concrete course — keep the fall framing, switch to something like Rome's Long Transition, or a hybrid with conditions — plus a clear timeline for implementation. And it needs to take the strongest argument for the losing side seriously, especially the worry that "transformation" risks becoming a euphemism that hides actual suffering and political breakdown. Don't just summarize the history; treat "fall" as a historiographical category and ask whether it holds up or whether it's a misleading conceptual lens that makes us see rupture where there was mostly adaptation.
