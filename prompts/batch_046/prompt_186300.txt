As a retired molecular biologist who has only recently begun studying philosophy systematically, I find the so-called procreation asymmetry more troubling the longer I sit with it. I am asking you to write a villanelle—nineteen lines, with two refrains and two rhymes—whose form itself enacts the asymmetry rather than merely describing it. Let one refrain concern the absence that cannot be wronged: the potential person never brought into being, who therefore never misses the happiness they might have had. Let the other refrain concern the presence that can be wronged: the actual person who exists and bears suffering. In the tercets, move from the evolutionary indifference of natural selection—where suffering is not a failure of design but an instrument of adaptation—toward the person-affecting restriction that makes existence-dependent harm the only morally relevant currency. The poem should be philosophically precise enough that someone familiar with the asymmetry recognizes its logic in the repetition and rhyme, and it should close in the quatrain with a statement of what obligation, if any, survives once the asymmetry is granted. Please do not cite texts or provide references; I want the reasoning carried entirely by the verse.
