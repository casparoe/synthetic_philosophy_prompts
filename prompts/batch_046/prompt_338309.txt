hey, I'm putting together a taxonomy for a seminar on well-being and I'm tired of the same old hedonist vs desire-satisfaction tree. I want something that gets into the weeds on tranquilism—like the idea that only the absense of craving and suffering actually matters, and that positive joy might just be noise. Can you build me a sorting quiz / decision tree? The splits should be concrete cases or claims, not just labels, so someone can answer without knowing the jargon.

Here are the four diagnostic splits, in this order:

1. Positive goods: imagine a being that feels intense joy and intense craving together, versus a being in a featureless mental blank with no pain, no pleasure, no wants at all. Does that blank state win just because positives are missing, or does the joy in the first being count for something intrumental?

2. Absence of suffering vs absence of craving: take a being that never feels pain but still occasionally wants a drink of water. Is that wanting itself a flaw, or is suffering-absence enough?

3. Static vs dynamic absence of craving: call Blank the state with literally zero wants ever, and call Pulse the state where one micro-craving arises every hour and dissolves instantly—like a heartbeat of desire. Is there a real well-being gap between Blank and Pulse, or do they collapse?

4. Meta-craving: can someone hold a second-order desire to stay desire-free without that self-defeating the view?
