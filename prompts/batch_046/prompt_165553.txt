I am preparing notes for a short philosophical essay on prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection, and I would like your comments on the argument's coherence, its fidelity to the core commitments of prioritarianism, and whether the central distinction is philosophically interesting or merely artificial. Here are the notes.

The essay will be titled "Asymmetric Compression and the Leveling-Down Objection: A Note on Non-Agential Constraints." Its central claim is that the standard leveling-down objection succeeds only against a straw version of prioritarianism that treats all welfare distributions as products of direct allocative choice. I propose to distinguish two phenomena that are usually collapsed: "direct downward adjustment," in which an agent intentionally reduces the welfare of better-off individuals without improving anyone else's position, and "structural compression," in which an external, non-agential constraint—such as a binding ecological or resource ceiling—lowers the welfare of the better-off while leaving the worst-off exactly where they were due to a protective floor. The standard objection assumes that prioritarianism must endorse both equally because both produce equality at a lower level. I argue that a refined prioritarian view can reject direct downward adjustment by adopting an independent "no-harm-without-corresponding-benefit" clause, while remaining neutral or even permissive about structural compression, since in the latter case the loss to the better-off is not caused by an intentional redistribution and the worst-off suffer no deprivation. To prevent this from collapsing into an endorsement of any accidental misfortune that happens to hurt the rich, I introduce a "counterfactual harm filter": prioritarian evaluation applies only to distributions that result from deliberate social choice, not to welfare changes driven by exogenous environmental shifts. The conclusion is that the leveling-down objection confuses the moral evaluation of intentional acts with the assessment of constraint-driven outcomes, and that prioritarianism can survive by explicitly restricting its scope to the domain of allocative agency. I am particularly unsure whether the "counterfactual harm filter" is consistent with prioritarianism's usual focus on outcomes rather than intentions, and whether "structural compression" is genuinely distinguishable from direct leveling in policy contexts where constraints are themselves politically constructed.

Please comment on the accuracy of my characterization of the leveling-down objection, the coherence of the distinction between direct downward adjustment and structural compression, and the plausibility of the counterfactual harm filter. I am also interested in whether the essay sounds like it addresses a live problem or merely relabels familiar moves, and I welcome suggestions for sharpening the argument without expanding it into a survey of the literature.
