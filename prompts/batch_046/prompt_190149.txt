Write a short story exploring the problem of other minds through the following scenario. In a near-future archive, a researcher named Elara restores a single intact manual from a lost civilization that had developed an elaborate empirical protocol — call it the Verification of Interiority — for determining whether an encountered entity possessed genuine conscious experience or merely executed sophisticated responsive behavior. The manual lists formal criteria: recursive self-reference under perturbation, non-instrumental emotional mirroring, and resistance to predictive modeling. Elara applies the protocol to three subjects available in the archive: a deactivated synthetic companion that satisfies every criterion with mechanical precision; a non-human mammal whose responses are consistent but never cross the threshold into self-reference; and a young human who fails multiple formal checks — she is inconsistent, poorly modeled, and shows no recursive self-awareness — yet produces in Elara a certainty that no score can capture. The story should not declare whether the protocol succeeds or fails. Instead, trace how Elara's practical treatment of each subject diverges from her official reports, and use the narrative to examine whether recognizing another mind is best understood as reaching a theoretical verdict or as entering into a particular kind of relationship that the protocol was never designed to measure.
