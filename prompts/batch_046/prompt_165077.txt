In AI alignment debates, a common analogy maps large language models onto Dennett's four kinds of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—claiming that because LLMs acquire language they become Gregorian creatures capable of offline counterfactual reasoning, and therefore that Skinnerian alignment tools (reward shaping, behavioral conditioning) are structurally inadequate; instead, we need Popperian-style internal modeling. Audit this analogy precisely: construct the correspondence table linking each of Dennett's four biological categories to specific features of LLM training, architecture, inference, and evaluation; identify where the mapping fails, especially regarding whether statistical language modeling replicates the cultural-tool function that defines Gregorian cognition in animals; and determine whether the normative conclusion—that alignment must shift from conditioning to internal-world modeling—survives once these disanalogies are explicit, or whether the taxonomy is being used to license an unjustified anthropomorphism.
