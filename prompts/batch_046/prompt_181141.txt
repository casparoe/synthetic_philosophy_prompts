Consider the institutional practice in theoretical physics whereby funding bodies and tenure committees continue to evaluate multiverse cosmology and string-theoretic programs favorably despite the absence of unique, empirically testable predictions, appealing instead to mathematical consistency, explanatory unification, and the so-called no-alternatives argument—particularly regarding the cosmological constant—while in adjacent fields comparable predictive ambiguity would typically disqualify a research program. What implicit philosophy of scientific confirmation does this differential standard presuppose? Reconstruct the theory of non-empirical rationality at work: do theoretical virtues function as independent evidential sources, as pragmatic heuristics, or as placeholders for future empirical success? Is the practice consistent with a unified account of theory choice, or does it tacitly depend on a domain-specific bifurcation between structural and phenomenological science? And if one replaced the underlying view with a strictly empirical, falsificationist criterion of acceptance, what specific judgments about peer review, grant allocation, and disciplinary boundaries would have to change?
