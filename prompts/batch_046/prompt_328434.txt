Consider the problem of applying Dennett's intentional stance to artificial intelligence systems, particularly large language models, where the predictive utility of mentalistic vocabulary is high but its ontological and normative status remains contested. This distinction becomes practically urgent under a hypothetical institutional scenario in which high-stakes AI deployments must be certified not merely for predictive reliability but for "reason-responsiveness"—that is, for whether they can be held accountable as thick-stance agents rather than merely optimized as thin-stance tools.

The third concerns methodological priority: should conceptual work focus on formalizing criteria that distinguish thin from thick adoption for practical regulators, or on mapping the institutional consequences—say, for criminal liability, contract interpretation, or scientific explanation—of adopting one stance over the other?
