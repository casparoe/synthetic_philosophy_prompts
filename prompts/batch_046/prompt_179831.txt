In rationalist and alignment-discourse circles, a common analogy maps the Darwinian-Skinnerian-Popperian-Gregorian taxonomy of minds onto artificial systems: a base language model is treated as a Skinnerian creature, shaped entirely by statistical feedback with no internal laboratory for testing hypotheses before they reach the world; a human researcher is cast as the Popperian or Gregorian counterpart, able to let ideas die in simulation or inherit cultural tools; and augmenting the model with retrieval, code execution, or multi-agent debate is described as giving it Gregorian capacities—an internal workshop where bad plans can fail without real-world harm. This mapping is often used to argue that the path to safer AI lies not in constraining outputs but in enriching these internal laboratories, since a creature that can reject hypotheses before acting is inherently more reflective and therefore more alignable.

Audit this analogy explicitly, not defensively. First, lay out the correspondence table: for each of the four creature-types, identify what structural feature in the LLM-plus-tools architecture is meant to play the analogous role, and what feature in the human case serves as the implicit comparison point. Second, isolate the disanalogies that actually bear on the safety and reflectiveness conclusion: does a code interpreter constitute a genuine Popperian internal model, or is it merely a Skinnerian feedback loop operating at a different scale and latency? Does Gregorian tool-use depend on shared intentionality, cultural transmission, or linguistic convention that the architecture lacks? Separate structural mismatches from normative smuggling—places where the analogy quietly imports the premise that internal trial is necessarily safer or more rational than external feedback, or that adding tools automatically upgrades a system's kind of mind rather than just its speed. Third, assess whether the practical conclusion—that augmenting with tools makes the system more Popperian or Gregorian and therefore more alignable—survives once these breaks are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the argumentative weight all along. Finally, if the mapping collapses in important respects, suggest what a more accurate comparative framework would be, and say whether that revised framework supports the same recommendation about where to invest alignment effort.
