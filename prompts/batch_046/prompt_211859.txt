I am preparing a diagnostic study instrument for my upper-level metaphysics seminar, which has been working through the ontology of dispositions, finks, and masks. I want a decision-tree taxonomy—built from the logical space rather than from a list of philosophers—whose branches are concrete yes/no commitments about specific cases, and whose leaves map onto actual positions (named or unnamed).

Please construct a tree with exactly three binary splits, yielding eight leaves.

Root split (Split 1): In the classic electro-fink case, a wire is connected to a device that removes its electrical charge the instant it is touched by a conductor. Does the wire retain the disposition to conduct electricity? Answer Yes if you think the disposition survives but is merely blocked; answer No if you think the fink genuinely destroys or removes the disposition.

Split 2 (applies regardless of Split 1): Consider a nested-layer scenario: an object has disposition D, a first mechanism masks D, but a second mechanism is rigged so that it eliminates the first mechanism precisely when the stimulus for D is present, allowing D to manifest. Does this layered arrangement show that masks and finks are ontologically distinct categories that can operate independently without collapsing into one another, or does it show that successful manifestation always proves the underlying categorical base was never genuinely interrupted?

Split 3: A broken mechanical clock is sealed in a display case with no possibility of repair. Does it still possess the disposition to tell the correct time, grounded solely in its present internal structure and counterfactual dependencies, or does the complete absence of any pathway to correct functioning mean the disposition is absent?

For each of the eight answer-combinations, provide:
- A leaf label indicating the combination of answers (e.g., Yes–Distinct–Yes) and, if it corresponds to a named view in the literature, its standard name; if no named view occupies it, label it unnamed and in one sentence describe its most awkward commitment—the specific result or implication it must accept that feels counterintuitive.
- For any combination that is unoccupied, briefly diagnose why: is it incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored?

Please also flag any two named positions that the tree fails to distinguish—that share all three answers but differ elsewhere—and state what further diagnostic question would be needed to separate them.

The cases should be phrased so that someone could navigate the tree and answer each split without already knowing the labels “dispositional essentialism,” “categoricalism,” or “anti-finkism.” Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
