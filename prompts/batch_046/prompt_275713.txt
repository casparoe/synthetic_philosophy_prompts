You are drafting the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on the explanation of perpetrator behavior in the Holocaust, specifically regarding the participation of non-elite ordinary Germans in direct killing. One camp—the situational-conformity camp, associated with Christopher Browning’s study of Reserve Police Battalion 101—holds that the primary drivers were peer pressure, authority structures, incremental exposure to violence, career incentives, and the absence of meaningful refusal options, and that deep ideological hatred was neither necessary nor widespread among these particular men. The other camp—the cultural-eliminationist camp, associated with Daniel Goldhagen’s argument—holds that these perpetrators acted largely from a widely shared, pre-existing eliminationist antisemitism embedded in German political culture, and that situational factors merely provided the occasion for expressing a will to kill that was already present.

Produce the document they should have co-authored. It must contain:

1. A numbered list of substantive agreed claims about perpetrator motivation, the role of ideology, the mechanisms of moral disengagement, and the predictive value of situational variables versus cultural schemas—claims that both sides would genuinely sign, avoiding both vacuity and partisan framing.

2. A precisely worded statement of residual disagreement, formatted so that each camp recognizes its own position in one clause and the opposing position in the other, with terminological disputes separated out and not treated as substantive disagreements.

3. At least two cruxes: sub-questions such that resolving them with new evidence or argument would move at least one side. For this collaboration, treat one crux as whether procedural dehumanization—the bureaucratic and spatial distancing from victims that enables incremental moral adaptation—can operate without a prior ontological dehumanization that classifies victims as a fundamentally different kind of being. Treat a second crux as whether the variance in refusal and enthusiasm across different units and occupation contexts correlates more strongly with variations in peer-group composition and command pressure or with variations in the intensity of local antisemitic culture.

4. For each crux, specify a concrete historical case or comparative observation that both sides agree is relevant (for example, the behavior of a specific unit under altered leadership, or comparative patterns in a different occupied territory), and record in advance what each camp predicts about that case.

The document should be formal, neutral, and constructive—a text both parties could publish jointly—and should not contain bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Where historical episodes are mentioned, rely on well-known facts rather than obscure archival specifics. Avoid reducing either position to a caricature, and inject conceptual distinctions that go beyond the standard textbook framing of the debate.
