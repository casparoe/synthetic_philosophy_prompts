I'm taking a seminar on cognition, categories, and representation—about ten students, seventy-five minutes. Design a structured classroom game that makes participants feel the Letter Spirit tension: every instance of 'a' belongs to the category, yet no single feature or grid configuration is shared by all of them, while near-misses that borrow many of those features are still excluded. Use a deck of cards showing canonical letterforms, distorted variants, and "rogue" forms that mix features from other letters. Assign roles: Rule-Architects must propose explicit deterministic membership conditions using a 5×5 feature grid; Rogue-Designers craft counterexamples; the remaining students vote on membership without knowing the rules in advance. Structure phases—blind classification, rule proposal, counterexample assault, forced revote—so that incentives push architects toward sharper conditions and rogues toward subtler violations, letting the group discover the collapse of essentialist criteria through play before any philosophical labels are named. Include a debrief that maps each failure mode onto essentialism, family resemblance, and style as relational structure; predict what will predictably go wrong (e.g., architects retreating to disjunctive lists or overly permissive prototypes); and state frankly which feature of actual letter-spirit recognition—such as the parallel, emergent construction of style across a grid—the activity necessarily omits and the facilitator must supply in discussion.
