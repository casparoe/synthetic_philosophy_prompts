Consider the well-documented asymmetry in experimental moral psychology—commonly referred to as the Knobe effect—where people tend to judge harmful side effects of an agent's action as intentional, while judging structurally parallel beneficial side effects as unintentional. Now suppose that David Hume were presented with this finding, especially as it applies to a non-standard case: an unsentient artificial system operating within a fully simulated environment produces both harmful and beneficial outcomes, and human observers still apply the asymmetry in attributing intentionality despite knowing that no conscious mind is present. How would Hume likely integrate this into his broader moral philosophy? Would he take it as confirmation that moral attribution is driven primarily by sentiment, associative habit, and the projection of mental qualities, or would he regard the persistence of the asymmetry in the absence of any sentient target as a challenge requiring a revision to his account of sympathy?
