I'm a software engineer on the product ethics committee at a mid-size tech company—i mostly do backend infrastructure and have zero formal philosophy training, so apologies if i butcher the terminology. Next thursday we have to vote on a pilot proposal for Seraph, a wellness companion app that uses predictive affective modeling to restrict user actions in real time—blocking social feeds, delaying purchases, enforcing sleep windows—whenever its internal counterfactual regret-minimization layer calculates that the users future-self happiness will be higher with the restriction in place. Users can technically disable the locks, but the opt-out flow is buried three menus deep, and the founders keep arguing that people consistently choose immediate freedom over long-term well-being and then regret it, which my coworker immediately linked to Dostoevskys Grand Inquisitor idea that human beings are too weak to bear real liberty and will happily trade it for security and satisfaction if an authority offers the deal. I think the parallel is interesting but i also worry that automating this tradeoff through a black-box reward-shaping loop is different from a literary parable, especially since the system treats present autonomy as just another variable to be optimized away. The committee is split between treating this as paternalistic UX and treating it as algorithmic tyranny, and we need a concrete recommendation, not just vibes. Could you draft a formal policy memo addressed to the board recommending whether we should approve the pilot, reject it, or approve with strict conditions? Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then lay out the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question. Make sure it includes clear criteria that reviewers could apply to similar predictive-paternalism products, and be genuinely honest about the strongest argument on the losing side—probably that well-calibrated predictive care can reduce real suffering and that autonomy preservation is sometimes overvalued. And please dont include any bibliography or citation list; our legal reviewer doesnt have access to look things up and neither do i.
