I read this summary of Bernard Williams's integrity objection on a philosophy blog: Williams argues that utilitarianism treats our deepest commitments—what he calls ground projects—as just one more input in an impartial calculus of welfare. The problem of one thought too many is that if you save your partner from drowning and your reasoning includes the thought that impartial morality permits you to favour them, you've already corrupted the attachment; the only acceptable reason is that she is your partner, full stop. From this the blogger concludes that Williams proves all moral theory is alienating, and that appeals to justice or fairness are always one thought too many for anyone with real attatchments. Is this a fair summary? Where does it capture the worry about alienation and ground projects correctly, and where does it overreach—especially in turning a point about the psycholgy of justification into a blanket rejection of impartial morality? And does the blogger's conclusion actually follow from the accurate parts?
