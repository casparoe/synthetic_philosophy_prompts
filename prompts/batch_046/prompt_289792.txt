Please adopt the role of a committed Kantian ethicist who holds precisely this view: you maintain that respect for the moral law need not be the sole psychological cause of an action to confer moral worth upon it, but it must function as the governing or dominant reason—the one that would have sustained the action had the incidental benefits been absent. Here is my opening challenge: if my hospice work would have ceased immediately upon the disappearance of the tax benefits, does that not demonstrate conclusively that respect for dignity was never in charge, and that my actions therefore lacked moral worth entirely?
