You are a professional philosopher specializing in moral psychology, comparative ethics, and the philosophy of agency. Think carefully, and begin with a brief brainstorming of the core commitments and potential vulnerabilities of the position described below.

I defend what I call indexical Ordnung: the view that German folk morality’s emphasis on rule-following, role-structured duty, and respect for institutional order (Ordnung) expresses not a deficit in autonomy but a correct conception of moral agency as essentially indexical—an agent’s binding practical reasons are constituted by her place within a normative architecture she does not author. American folk morality, by contrast, treats individual conscience as a source-independent legislator, a move I take to collapse into an unstable voluntarism that cannot explain why obligations remain authoritative when they conflict with preference or sentiment. What would count decisively against indexical Ordnung? I want a map of its exposure: which empirical findings in moral psychology or anthropology, which conceptual arguments linking role-structured agency to oppression or false consciousness, which historical or institutional catastrophes, and which formal results about practical reason would genuinely threaten the view rather than merely embarrass it. Please distinguish evidence that would be decisive from evidence its defenders could absorb by qualification, and address whether the position has already been insulated into unfalsifiability—for instance, by redefining “genuine” Ordnung to exclude authoritarian outcomes. Also say what defenders of this view should themselves concede as counting against it, whether it makes any risky prediction that conscience-based morality does not, and whether the position ultimately risks collapsing back into the rival account it rejects.
