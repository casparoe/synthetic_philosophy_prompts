I'm advising a regional library consortium that wants to redesign branch interiors by inferring what patrons genuinely value from their observed behavior rather than from survey responses. The board has taken an interest in inverse reinforcement learning—the theoretical framework that treats movement through a space as revealing an underlying reward function, while insisting that any observed trajectory is observationally equivalent to infinitely many reward structures, so inference always requires an explicit prior and is vulnerable to reward misspecification, constraint-driven behavior, and social-signaling optimization. They need a reusable procedural framework my design team can run on any proposed layout change, not a one-time recommendation. Translate the core theoretical constraints—ambiguity of reward functions, the gap between revealed trajectory and experienced value, the necessity of priors over plausible preference structures, and the distinction between optimizing for intrinsic reward versus navigating social or physical constraints—into an ordered checklist with explicit decision nodes. For each node, specify what evidence counts as sufficient, where the procedure terminates with a clear "do not redesign" verdict, and where it must hand authority back to human judgment because the remaining ambiguity is irreducibly normative rather than empirical. The team has no machine-learning staff and must be able to execute the framework in a single two-hour workshop using only circulation logs, hand-drawn observational flow maps, and brief patron interviews. Apply the procedure to three specific proposals: removing entrance seating to reduce loitering, relocating the young-adult collection behind a partial visual barrier after noticing avoidance patterns near the current location, and expanding quiet study carrels based on sustained high occupancy rates. Include at least one case in which the procedure produces a redesign recommendation that directly conflicts with the board's stated equity commitments, and explicitly flag which procedural steps rest on untestable assumptions about what patrons are actually optimizing for.
