I run a philosophy of rationality seminar with about 12 grad students, one 75-minute block. I'm tired of them just nodding along to the standard money-pump story without actually feeling the bite of diachronic failure, so I want an in-class game with token payoffs — no real cash, just points — that forces them to live through being pumped. The twist I want baked in is that each player holds a private "risk profile" card that shifts each round after they draw a noisy signal; this means every local preference reversal looks like a reasonable update rather than mere caprice. There's an arbitrageur role (probably me, or one volunteer) who offers trades each round based on revealed preferences, and by round three the players should find themselves nearly wiped out even though each trade felt rational in isolation. I need clear rules, role descriptions, a payoff sheet, and a debrief that maps the rounds onto the sophistication strategy versus the resoluteness strategy — especially asking where resoluteness collapses if the signals might actually be informative. Be honest about what the activity can't reproduce: likely the normative pull of sticking to an ex ante plan when your evidence genuinely seems to shift. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong — I bet someone spots the cycle in round two and refuses to trade, or the arbitrageur gets to greedy and kills the illusion. Don't lecture them up front, don't call it a money pump until after they've played, and don't ask them to do any reading beforehand.
