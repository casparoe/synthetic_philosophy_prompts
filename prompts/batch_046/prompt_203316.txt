Debates about Rawls sometimes present the veil of ignorance as a contract signed before anyone knows their bargaining power: you agree to rules without knowing whether you will enter as capital-rich or labor-only, so you build in redistribution — the difference principle — as a rational hedge. Proponents use this to argue that the difference principle is just prudent risk-pooling, not a distinct moral commitment, and that the veil therefore lacks special authority. Audit the analogy: provide a correspondence table (contract, unknown bargaining power, the hedge, the difference principle, the moral authority of the agreement); identify where ignorance of full social position differs from ignorance of bargaining assets; show where the contract frame quietly imports cooperative-game or market-exchange assumptions that Rawls's Kantian constructivism rejects; and say whether the conclusion — that the difference principle collapses into rational risk-sharing — survives once the disanalogies are on the table. In your response, state how confident you are in each part of the audit and single out the correspondence you are least sure holds.
