I am a high school senior working on an independent study in philosophy of science, and my teacher walked me into an argument about structural realism that I cannot find the exit from. The reasoning looks valid to me, but it seems to prove the exact opposite of what structural realists want, so I think one step must be wrong—though I cannot name which one without losing something I value.

Here is the chain as I understand it:

(1) Structural realism holds that successful scientific theories reveal only the mathematical structure of the world, leaving the intrinsic nature of the underlying entities permanently unknown.

(2) If a claim reveals only structure, then any two worlds that realize that same structure through different intrinsic properties are indistinguishable by that claim.

(3) Structural realists typically treat mathematics as the language in which those revealed structures are expressed, so they are committed to saying that our mathematical knowledge is itself purely structural knowledge.

(4) But the axioms of Peano arithmetic in second-order logic are categorical: they have only one model up to isomorphism. That means the structure of the natural numbers fixes every element and every relation among them, leaving no hidden intrinsic nature unaccounted for within the system.

(5) So if structural realism is correct about mathematics, then mathematics actually determines intrinsic natures completely—there is no alternative realization of that structure.

(6) By (2), if mathematics determines intrinsic natures, then the scientific theories that borrow those mathematical structures also determine the intrinsic natures of the physical entities they model.

(7) Therefore structural realism, applied consistently across science and mathematics, ends up showing that science reveals not merely structure but the full intrinsic nature of physical entities—which is precisely the view structural realism was designed to avoid.

My instinct is that the error lies either in the move from (3) to (4), where something about logical strength gets smuggled in that a structural realist need not accept, or in the slide from (6) to the conclusion, where a gap between mathematical realization and physical instantiation might be ignored. I would most like to keep (1) intact, and I am open to revising (3) if necessary. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost the position?

Please diagnose the argument clearly, explain why the flawed step looks plausible, and price the alternatives. Also, please say how confident you are in what you claim and single out the point you are least sure of.
