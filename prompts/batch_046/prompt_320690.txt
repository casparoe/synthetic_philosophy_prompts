You are an expert philosopher of science and mind preparing questions for a podcast interview. Your guest is a philosopher—call her Dr. Aris Thorne—who defends a specific view she labels “compressive pattern realism.” She accepts Dennett’s framework of the physical, design, and intentional stances, but she modifies it with a precise ontological filter: a pattern is genuinely real, not merely projected, if describing it from the design or intentional stance produces a representation that is algorithmically incompressible from any physical-stance description without catastrophic loss of predictive and counterfactual power. She claims this criterion distinguishes real patterns from useful fictions, avoids ontological inflation by not treating patterns as fundamental physical entities, and still allows intentional-stance patterns—beliefs, desires—to be real in a derivative but non-eliminable way. Before drafting the questions, briefly outline the three standard stances and identify the main pressure points critics raise against stance-dependent realism, particularly from physicalist eliminativists and from those who argue that compression is merely an epistemic or pragmatic feature of our cognitive architecture. Then write eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her to restate the view but instead press it where the literature presses: include at least one question on whether algorithmic incompressibility tracks a feature of the world or only a feature of our representational capacities; at least one comparing design-stance patterns (e.g., a thermostat’s goal-state) with intentional-stance patterns (e.g., a belief about a specific person) and asking whether her view collapses them into one ontological kind or must pay a price to keep them distinct; and at least one on borderline or problematic cases—such as patterns that are compressible only for observers with specific sensory modalities, or patterns in highly chaotic systems that resist compression altogether—asking exactly what her criterion delivers there. For each question, provide a brief follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give: for instance, if she appeals to predictive success, ask what distinguishes that success from mere curve-fitting; if she distinguishes the stances by teleological structure, ask whether that distinction reintroduces the hierarchical ontology she sought to avoid. End with a single, direct question asking what empirical discovery or conceptual argument would compel her to abandon compressive pattern realism, and whether she regards the view as falsifiable in principle or as a regulative framework that can always absorb anomalies by redescription. Think step by step, be precise, and do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
