Act as an expert philosopher of probability and formal epistemology with experience in scientific modeling. Before responding, please think carefully, outline the relevant conceptual landscape, and then work through the issue step by step.

I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of science, and I have been working through discussions of belief updating—particularly the case sometimes called the Judy Benjamin problem, where learning a conditional claim about one’s situation pulls belief in a different direction than learning the direct fact. In formalizing some simple inference exercises, I have repeatedly noticed a pattern that feels adjacent but does not match the standard treatment.

Here are two concrete instances. First, a researcher compares two models of a system, Model A and Model B. She receives a report stating, in effect, “If Model A is correct, then the noise floor is below threshold T”—not that the noise is actually below T, but that the model carries this implication. She updates her beliefs. Later she receives a direct measurement showing the noise is indeed below T. Depending on whether she treated the initial report as part of her background interpretive framework or as an evidential claim to be conditioned upon like the measurement itself, her confidence in Model A versus Model B shifts in opposite directions: in one ordering she becomes more confident in A, in the other less so—yet both paths seem internally consistent with Bayes’s rule. Second, in a clinical reasoning analog, a physician learns the conditional “If the patient has disease D, then biomarker B will be elevated,” updates her beliefs, and then separately observes that B is elevated; treating the conditional as framework versus treating it as data again reverses the direction of revision about D.

Is there an established name for this specific non-commutativity—where integrating a conditional model-constraint and integrating direct observational evidence produce conflicting belief revisions depending on which is treated as framework and which as data? How precisely does it differ from the Judy Benjamin problem, from Jeffrey conditioning, and from ordinary model misspecification? If no standard term exists, please propose a precise definition with clear boundary conditions that distinguish legitimate framework revision from an illicit double-counting of the same evidential path. I am not looking for a bare label; I want the carving: what exactly the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
