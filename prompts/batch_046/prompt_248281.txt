I hold the interpretive view that Carnap's distinction between internal and external questions, together with his account of linguistic frameworks and explication, extends naturally into politics and social planning in a way that dissolves normative dispute into framework selection. On this reading, disagreements about distributive justice or collective goods are either internal technical problems, soluble by empirical and logical analysis within an adopted framework, or external choices between frameworks, which are pragmatically rather than rationally determined. Political planning therefore becomes value-neutral instrumental reasoning: democratic deliberation is relegated to selecting the framework, while execution is purely a matter of means-ends optimisation. I want an honest map of what would genuinely defeat this position, not merely complicate it. Specifically: could rational norms govern framework choice in a way that Carnap's binary suppresses? Does his commitment to linguistic pluralism and tolerance sit uneasily with a technocratic separation of deliberation from execution? Is there a form of normative political reasoning that neither collapses into internal logic nor reduces to arbitrary selection? Please rank these potential defeaters from fatal to awkward, indicate which ones a committed Carnapian ought to concede as damaging rather than dismiss, and assess whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—if all normative resistance can be reclassified as 'external', does that insulation constitute a virtue or a vice for a theory of this kind? I also want you to state explicitly what argument or finding would cause you to revise or abandon this interpretation, and to identify the point in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to error.
