our braudel reading group has been stuck for months. one side says the longue duree is just scenery -- geography and climate set the stage but don't cause specific events. the other side says slow structures constrain what's possible, so they explain outcomes in a different register. I think the real stall is that neither side distinguishes structural persistence from what I'd call thermal inertia: social and ecological forms don't just last long, they absorb shocks and release them over generations, which means they dampen change rather than trigger it. so is this disagreement actually about causation, or are we using 'explain' differently for fast events versus slow structures? and if it's the latter, could a distinction between dampening-causation and triggering-causation actually move the debate, or is the whole framework just a taxonomy with no predictive bite? maybe I'm wrong and it's just bedrock intuition about what counts as real history.
