I am a high school student serving on the Youth Curatorial Advisory Board at our local Museum of Comparative Religion. Our board must decide which interpretive panel to approve for an upcoming display of a medieval Jain manuscript illumination depicting cosmic geography. Three drafts have been submitted. Draft A presents the cosmology as a literal metaphysical claim held by historical Jain communities. Draft B presents it as ethical allegory, aligning with contemporary scholarly interpretation. Draft C applies Jain anekāntavāda through saptabhaṅgī—the seven modes of conditional assertion (syāt)—qualifying every claim so that literal, symbolic, and inexpressible readings are all registered without ranking them.

Half the board argues that a museum has an educational duty to present the best-supported interpretation rather than suspend judgment; the other half argues that anekāntavāda requires institutional neutrality among standpoints and that Draft C is the only respectful form. A minority adds that Draft C risks visitor confusion and undermines the exhibit's purpose.

Please draft the board's decision memo: recommend approval of one draft, rejection of the others, or approval with specific conditions. The memo should include reasoning that staff and dissenting members can actually respond to, and it should treat the strongest consideration on the losing side with genuine fairness. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. The memo should be actionable and self-contained.
