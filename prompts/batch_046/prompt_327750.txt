hey — quick one for a piece i'm filing on grand narratives. everyone compares writing books like Harari's Sapiens or Diamond's Guns, Germs, and Steel to shooting a nature doc in extreme time-lapse: you speed up 300k years so the "big patterns" (agriculture, states, capitalism) pop, but you blur the local failures and the people who opted out. critics, especially Graeber and Wengrow in The Dawn of Everything, say that blur isn't neutral — it smuggles in a teleolgy, as if history had a plot.

can you audit that analogy? map the correspondances (camera, frame-rate, editor, etc.), then the breaks: where does it pretend the historian is just "recording" rather than selecting? and crucially — does the critics' charge that big history misleads by design survive once the disanologies are on the table, or does the analogy actually defend the genre? also, what's a better metaphor if there is one?
