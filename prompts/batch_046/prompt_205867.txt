hey i'm working on a handout for a discussion group about whether the agricultural revolution was acutally a mistake — the "worst mistake" framing versus the idea it was just inevitable progress. i don't want a simple list of pros and cons. i want a taxonomy, like a decision tree, that sorts positions by what they actually commit to on three sepcific splits.

first split: do you think hunter-gatherers were genuinely more affluent — more leisure, better nutrition, less chronic disease — than early farming populations? not noble savage mythology, just median welfare comparison.

second split: on inequality. do you think farming created surplus which then created hierarchy (the standard story), or do you think storage and social control came first and agriculture was just the technology that scaled an already-existing hierarchy?

third split — this is the weirder injected one: the retrospective evaluation problem. when we call it a "mistake," are we imposing our own values on people who never had the conceptual framework to compare foraging vs farming? or is there an objective welfare comparison that holds regardless of whether they could conceive the alternative?

each leaf should carry a position name (or "unnamed") and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. i need these leaves present: the welfare-decline "it was a trap" view; the demographic-trap view where population growth locked people in regardless of preference; the local-variation view that some regions gained and others lost so there's no unified verdict; and the "progress was worth the cost" view that accepts welfare decline but says civilization was the payoff. flag any pair the tree can't separate — i suspect the demographic-trap view and the progress-was-worth-it view might blur on the welfare branch, but i'm not sure. also note empty combinations, like whether anyone holds that foragers were worse off but farming still wasn't worth it, and if that space is empty say why.

write it casually, like for a blog handout, not an encyclopedia entry. and please: tell me what argument or archaeological finding would change your mind about the tree's layout — say, if forager leisure turned out to be much lower, which branches collapse? also be explicit about where your reasoning is shakest, probably in how i framed the retrospective evaluation split.

oh, and don't cite papers or ask for a bibliography — the point is to reason it through without looking stuff up.
