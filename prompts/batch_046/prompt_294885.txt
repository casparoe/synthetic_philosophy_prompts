I'm preparing interview questions for a philosophy podcast. Next week's guest is an unnamed contemporary defender of a Quinean naturalized account of moral values: she treats moral predicates as ontologically innocent posits anchored in our total web of belief, culturally variable, irreducible to neuroscience or behaviorism, yet lacking mind-independent normative properties. I need six to eight substantive questions that avoid biography and stay in the conceptual weeds. For each, include a follow-up that anticipates her most likely reply and presses further.

Specifically, include:
- A question about whether Quine's rejection of the analytic-synthetic distinction dissolves the is-ought gap or merely relocates it within the web, followed by a push on whether normative force can be explained without circularity.
- A question applying Quine's indeterminacy of translation thesis to cross-cultural moral disagreement: does radical translation of normative terms render moral conflict indeterminate, or does shared behavioral reinforcement preserve genuine disagreement? Follow up on the likely answer that shared dispositions preserve conflict by asking what distinguishes moral disagreement from mere preference divergence in that framework.
- A question comparing the ontological status of moral values to mathematical entities in Quine's ontology — both indispensable and non-referential — and whether this parallel makes moral commitments more vulnerable to empirical revision or less so. Follow up by asking what kind of theoretical pressure (predictive failure, incoherence with physics, or social dysfunction) could actually dislodge a central moral node.
- A question asking how her view distinguishes moral progress from mere moral change without importing an external normative standard, with a follow-up keyed to an appeal to global coherence or predictive success of the overall belief system.
- One additional question of your choice that presses an internal tension in the view, with its follow-up.

Please separate in your response what is settled interpretation of Quine's broader philosophy from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any contested interpretive claim about Quine's texts or intentions as the consensus view.
