I'm a mediator for a university housing conflict resolution office, and I keep running into this problem where one roommate wants to blame the other for breaking shared norms, but they've clearly done the similiar thing before. There's all this philosophy about whether being a hypocrite takes away your standing to blame someone, and I need a practical procedure for my intake interviews — like a flowchart in words — that I can run in about fifteen minutes. The procedure should ask ordered questions: first, is the complainant's past conduct actually the same kind of wrong, or just similiar-looking? Second, is it recent enough that it undermines their moral authority right now? Third, is the blame being used to deflect from their own guilt, or is it a genuine call for accountability? I want it to clearly say where the answer is "standing is intact" and where it's "standing is undermined, but the underlying claim might still be true." And I want it to flag the "moral equality" trap — where both people violated the norm but only one gets blamed — without just saying "both sides are wrong so shut up."

Then apply it to two cases: first, a resident complaining about a roommate leaving dirty dishes after they themselves left dishes in the sink yesterday; second, a resident complaining that their roommate made a targeted racist comment, when the complainant once made an off-color joke months ago that wasn't directed at anyone. There's also this idea I want built in: instead of treating hypocrisy as a binary switch, maybe standing gets "partially discounted" based on how structurally similar the past violation is — like a weight, not an on/off. Don't make it a math formula, just a graded judgment the mediator has to make explicit. Don't cite anyone or ask me to read stuff, just build the checklist from what the concept actually requires, and say exactly where the procedure hands the decision back to me because it's a values call rather than something the rules can settle.
