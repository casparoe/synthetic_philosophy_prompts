I keep noticing something weird about how legal systems handle property that traces back to forced seizure. Courts protect current titles through doctrines like adverse possession and statutes of limitation -- basically, long enough possession makes it yours regardless of how you got it. But the same courts, when they hear reparations cases, suddenly demand proof of specific unjust acts and direct causation, as if the origin story only matters sometimes. Meanwhile constitutional theory insists law needs force to be effective, yet claims its authority comes from justice or consent, not from that force.

What view of justice vs force is baked into this? Is it basically Pascal's idea that since justice can't enforce itself we have to dress up force as law? Or can you hold these practices together with a clean seperate split between origins and validity? Reconstruct what philosophy the institution actually presupposes, whether the two sides are consistent or just convenient, and what'd have to change if we took seriously the claim that a law backed only by force has no authority at all.
