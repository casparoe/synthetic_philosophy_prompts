For a philosophical audit I'm writing down my commitments on mental causation and want to check whether they cohere. I hold that (1) physical causal closure holds—every physical event has a sufficient physical cause; (2) mental properties strongly supervene on physical ones but are multiply realizable and not type-identical to them; (3) mental properties genuinely cause physical behavior, not merely as epiphenomenal byproducts; (4) I reject systematic overdetermination, so mental and physical causes do not routinely compete as independent sufficient conditions; (5) causation should be proportional—a cause should match its effect in specificity, screening off irrelevant detail, which is how mental-level descriptions can be causally right without being physically redundant; (6) phenomenal properties, such as the qualitative character of pain, have direct downward causal efficacy on behavior, not mediated solely through functional roles; and (7) the exclusion problem is best answered by appealing to proportionality rather than by reducing mental properties to physical ones. Go through these commitments: which pairs are genuinely in tension, how severe is each tension, what is the cheapest revision that would resolve it, and which single change would clean up the most inconsistencies? Also flag any pair that looks tense at first but actually dissolves on inspection, and say why.
