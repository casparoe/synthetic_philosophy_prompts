I’ve been trying to formalize an objection to Jeffrey conditioning that keeps cycling back on itself, and I’d like to know where the loop breaks. (1) Jeffrey conditioning revises credence among hypotheses in proportion to their likelihood of generating the observed experience, preserving prior ratios within each evidential partition defined by the experience. (2) Consider an endogenous-observation case: an agent activates a sensor for reading R only if her prior credence in H exceeds a threshold τ, and the sensor’s output depends on whether it is activated. (3) To apply the Jeffrey rule, one needs a well-defined posterior confidence q in experiencing R versus not experiencing R, but whether R is experienced is determined by the prior credence in H—the very quantity the update is meant to revise. (4) Hence q is not exogenous; it is a function q(P(H)). (5) Substituting into the Jeffrey equation yields P'(H) = P(H|R) q(P'(H)) + P(H|¬R)(1 − q(P'(H))), placing the updated belief on both sides of the equation. (6) Depending on the dynamics, this fixed-point condition may have zero, one, or multiple solutions, so the rule either fails to determine a unique revision or forces an arbitrary selection among equilibria. (7) If a rational belief-revision rule can fail to determine a unique belief state, it cannot function as a normative constraint. (8) Yet real-world observation is never fully exogenous—attention, sampling, and interpretation all depend on prior belief—so the problem is not confined to contrived scenarios. (9) Therefore Jeffrey conditioning applies only to an impossible class of purely passive observations, or it is not genuinely a learning rule but only a consistency condition on belief-action equilibria. I want to keep (1) and (8) if I can. Which step should I reject, what does giving it up cost, and does the circularity in (3)–(5) actually break the rule, or does Jeffrey conditioning already contain resources for handling self-dependent evidence?
