My credence that such a work can still be aesthetically excellent—meaning its formal, expressive, and cognitive achievements are genuinely strong rather than merely excused—is about 0.75; my colleague's is around 0.25.

Give me your own precise number, but more importantly decompose the underlying question into four sub-issues: (1) whether aesthetic value is ever properly responsive to moral features of a work's production, or remains strictly confined to perceptual-formal properties; (2) whether there is a coherent distinction between "structural incorporation" (the harm is part of the work's internal meaning and material identity) and "biographical contamination" (the harm is external context about the artist); (3) whether recognizing structural incorporation necessarily diminishes aesthetic merit, or can in some configurations enhance it through critical complexity or semantic density; and (4) whether the principle of separating the work from the artist even applies when the artist's immoral act is materially constitutive of the object itself. Assign each sub-issue a rough probability, explain how they combine into your overall credence, identify which one your final number is most sensitive to, and say where the gap between my 0.75 and my colleague's 0.25 most likely lives. I want a committed response—take a definite position with explicit reasoning rather than surveying options neutrally—and tell me what specific argument or piece of evidence would move your number most.
