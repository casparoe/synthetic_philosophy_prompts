Last night I had a long and somewhat circular argument with a close friend about wireheading and reward tampering—specifically whether an agent that rewires its own reward circuitry has necessarily erred, or whether some forms of self-modification are rational or even obligatory. Rather than rehearse opposing positions, I want the full specification first. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of intrinsic reward and rational self-modification must meet if it is to handle the wireheading problem without collapsing value into behavior or rendering the normative authority of reward states unfalsifiable. For each requirement, state the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it: the theory must distinguish a phenomenologically identical wireheaded state from an authentically achieved state; it must accommodate an agent that modifies its reward architecture not to maximize immediate signal but to stabilize coherence across long-term reasoning; it must draw a principled line between autonomy-undermining manipulation and autonomy-expressing revision; and it must explain why the causal origin of a reward state and its normative authority can come apart. Add, as a further criterion, the demand for counterfactual reward stability—the theory must account for why an agent should care about what reward mechanism it would endorse under conditions of full information and reflective equilibrium, not merely about the mechanism it currently runs—and indicate whether this desideratum can be jointly satisfied with the requirement that the account remain empirically tractable for actual cognitive architectures. Mark which of these are genuinely non-negotiable and which reflect camp-specific commitments, such as whether determinism about neural reward pathways is required, or whether the theory must preserve a sharp fact/value distinction within the architecture itself. Identify any subset of requirements that appears jointly unsatisfiable given conceptual or formal constraints, with the reasoning. Finally, briefly score how hedonistic accounts, preference-satisfaction theories, and objective-list views do against the list, but keep the conceptual weight on the criteria and their interrelations rather than on the scorecard. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
