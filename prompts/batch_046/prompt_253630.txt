I serve on the institutional review board for a university behavioral-genetics center. A proposed study uses a sibling-comparison design in an adopted cohort to estimate that a particular executive-function trait shows 55% narrow-sense heritability and 15% shared-environment variance. The investigators want our board to approve a public-facing summary stating that “genes account for 55% of the trait in individuals” and that “family upbringing explains only 15%.” Several board members worry this conflates population-level variance partitioning with individual causal weights, but the PI argues that heritability coefficients are the only scientifically meaningful way to communicate genetic influence to policymakers.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approve the summary as written, approve with mandatory revisions, or reject it—with a clear time frame for the investigators’ response. The reasoning should engage seriously with the PI’s claim that heritability is the standard metric in quantitative genetics, treat honestly the strongest argument for allowing the summary (e.g., communicative clarity for non-specialists), and explain precisely where the proposed language crosses from technical description into conceptual misrepresentation, without asking for citations or external bibliographies. Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
