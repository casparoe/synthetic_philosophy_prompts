A philosophy blog I read summarized Paul Churchland's eliminative materialism this way: folk psychology is a theoretical framework that postulates beliefs, desires, and intentions to explain behavior, but like phlogiston or caloric, it is destined for elimination because it has failed to integrate with neuroscience and makes poor predictions. Churchland's central argument, according to the summary, is that cognitive neuroscience has already demonstrated that there are no neural mechanisms corresponding to propositional attitudes, and that the conceptual framework of folk psychology is therefore not approximately true but strictly false—there are literally no beliefs. The summary adds that Churchland allows sensations like pain to remain, but treats all propositional mental states as fictions. From this, the author concludes that if eliminativism is correct, then legal and moral responsibility vanish, since these presuppose agents with beliefs and intentions, and that the familiar self-refutation objection—that one who denies belief exists cannot consistently believe eliminativism—is met by defenders with nothing more than the claim that the objection begs the question. The blog ends by saying that because Churchland must implicitly rely on belief-attribution to communicate his own view, eliminativism collapses into performative contradiction.

I feel like some of this is right, but other parts sound too strong—especially the claim about what neuroscience has already shown, and the idea that defenders have no substantive reply to self-refutation. Can you evaluate whether this is a fair characterization? Please separate: (1) what Churchland actually argues and what he specifically targets, distinguishing propositional attitudes from other mental phenomena; (2) where the summary overstates empirical findings or conflates theoretical elimination with reductive explanation; (3) whether the inferences about moral responsibility and the assessment of the self-refutation reply accurately represent the debate or present open questions as settled; and (4) whether the blog's charge of performative contradiction survives once the misrepresentations in parts (1) and (2) are corrected.
