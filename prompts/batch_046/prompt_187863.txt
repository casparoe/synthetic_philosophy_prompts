I was talking with some friends in my cohort after our Foucault seminar and we kept circling this wierd pattern we all see in grad programs but can't seem to name properly. It's not the classic panopticon — there's no hidden guard in a tower watching us. Instead, we're given tons of autonomy over our schedules and mental-health resources, but those resources are mostly self-reporting apps and wellness surveys we fill out voluntarily. The data doesn't go to one authority; it gets aggregated into reports that justify cutting actual support staff, and if you write about feeling burned out in your annual review it gets read as either good self-awareness or poor resilience depending on your advisor's mood.

So the discipline isn't an external norm being enforced; it's us generating intimate knowledge about our own affective states, and then that knowledge being used to sort us into governable categories. I want to call this governmentality or maybe technologies of the self, but both feel wrong — governmentality usually involves state-level conduct management, and technologies of the self are about ethical self-formation, not HR using your disclosure to justify funding cuts. Is there an actual term for this kind of regime where autonomy and self-knowledge are the very mechanisms of control? If not, how do we draw its edges so it doesn't collapse into ordinary therapy culture or bureaucratic peer surveillance? I definatly don't need citations or a reading list, just want to know if I'm reinventing the wheel or noticing something with its own shape.
