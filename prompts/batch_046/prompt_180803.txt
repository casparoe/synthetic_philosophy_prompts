A philosopher whose work I have been following has taken four clear positions on self-locating belief: she is a thirder in the canonical Sleeping Beauty problem (assigning 1/3 credence to Heads upon waking); she rejects the self-sampling inference in Doomsday-style arguments and treats one's birth-rank as evidentially neutral; in a perfect-duplicate scenario with two indistinguishable copies she divides credence evenly; and in a Dr. Evil variant—where a coin flip determines whether a perfect copy is created—she still assigns 1/2 to being in the original world rather than the copy. I want to predict her verdict on a new case I will call the Wednesday Mirror: a fair coin is flipped Sunday night; if Heads she is woken once on Monday in a red room; if Tails she is woken Monday in a blue room, put back to sleep with her memories erased, and woken again Tuesday in the same blue room, but now with a mirror displaying "TUESDAY" and reflecting her with a readable timestamp. Specifically: what should her Monday credence in Heads be? Should her Tuesday credence differ? And should seeing the mirror on Tuesday alter her credence relative to her Monday state? I am less interested in the bare numbers than in the competing principles that could consistently generate her four earlier verdicts, which of those principles diverge on the Wednesday Mirror, my best guess at her answer together with the reason for preferring the principle that produces it, and the one further case that would best separate the leading candidates.
