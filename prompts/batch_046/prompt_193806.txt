In a seminar applying Kierkegaard's framework of Abraham and the teleological suspension of the ethical, my instructor has ruled on four modern analogues. First, a father receives what he takes to be an incommunicable, singular command to send his only child on a dangerous mission that violates ordinary parental duty; he obeys but continues to love and provide for the child in every other respect. Verdict: genuine suspension—the universal is suspended outwardly but retained inwardly. Second, a father receives what he takes to be a command to physically harm his child and refuses, turning himself in. Verdict: not suspension, but demonic despair—there is no paradox of faith, only destruction of the concrete relation. Third, a government official breaks immigration law to shelter one specific refugee family, claiming a direct, non-generalizable call regarding these particular people, while affirming the law's general validity and accepting legal punishment. Verdict: genuine suspension. Fourth, a scientist suppresses contradictory data, claiming direct revelation. Verdict: not suspension—this evades the universal demand for truth without preserving any singular concrete relation; it is self-deception disguised as faith.

The new case: a physician, convinced by an incommunicable private revelation, refuses to perform a standard life-saving surgery on her own sister, insisting God commanded her not to operate on this specific body. She affirms that medicine is generally good, accepts loss of license and social censure, expresses deep conflict between sisterly love and professional duty, and cannot explain the command beyond its singular application to this patient at this time.

Predict the instructor's verdict. Identify at least two competing rules that jointly fit the four earlier rulings—one emphasizing that the suspended universal must concern a concrete, non-transferable relation of belonging or love analogous to Abraham and Isaac, the other emphasizing that the only necessary conditions are a genuinely singular incommunicable reason plus full inward retention of the universal despite its outward violation. Say what each rule predicts about the sister case, give your best-guess verdict with reasons, and name the further case that would distinguish them (for instance, the identical refusal applied to a stranger patient with no kinship tie). State explicitly what feature of the case or argument would change your mind, and point out where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Keep the response to no more than 350 words.
