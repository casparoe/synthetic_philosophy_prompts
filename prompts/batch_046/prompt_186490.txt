I have been thinking on my own about reports that people in Western, educated, industrialized, rich, and democratic societies tend to answer moral questions differently from people elsewhere—putting heavier weight on harm and fairness and lighter weight on loyalty, authority, and purity. I have no formal training in philosophy or psychology. Could you please rank the following five explanations of that cross-cultural pattern, from the one that best preserves moral objectivity to the one that undermines it most: first, that Western societies have simply come closer to a universal moral truth; second, that every culture grasps only a partial fragment of one correct morality; third, that moral rules are like local customs with no culture-independent correct answer; fourth, that the observed gaps are mainly artifacts of how researchers design and translate surveys; and fifth, that all cultures share the same deep moral principles and only disagree at the surface. Give each a plausibility score from 0 to 10 and one sentence of reasoning. Then please state explicitly what kind of empirical finding or philosophical argument would cause you to reverse your top and bottom choices, and identify the step in your reasoning that is most likely to fail.
