As a retired physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy with any seriousness, I have come to hold a somewhat unorthodox view of the omnipotence paradox. Rather than treating omnipotence as an open-ended catalog of actions an agent can perform—lifting this stone, creating that cosmos—I have started to think of it as a structural feature of a system's state-space, specifically the absence of unreachable nodes within a formally defined possibility manifold. On this framing, the classic challenge of creating a stone so heavy that even an omnipotent being cannot lift it is not a hidden logical contradiction inside the concept of omnipotence, but a category error: it asks the system to occupy a state in which one of its own defining connectivity conditions is violated, analogous to asking a universal set to contain itself. The paradox dissolves, I think, not by placing a limit on power, but by recognizing that self-referential boundary conditions are topological properties of the state-space itself, not failures of agency within it.
