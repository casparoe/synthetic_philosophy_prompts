I retired from a chemistry lab a couple years back and lately I've been trying to make sense of philosophy texts in my free time, though I still think like an experimentalist. A colleague summed up Bradley's regress and the unity of the propostion for me like this: Bradley showed that whenever two things are related, that relation itself has to be related to both things, which needs another relation, and so on in an infinate loop. From this he concluded that reality can't actually contain separate parts—no real subjects or predicates—just one undivided whole he called the Absolute. Russell is said to have solved the regress by saying relations are just external, bare facts that don't need any extra linking; they just hold. But my colleague thinks Russell only pushed the problem around, because he never explained how one single proposition can be genuinely one unit of thought rather than just a list of parts, so Bradley's deeper point—that analysis destroys unity—was never really answered, and modern logic just papers over it with syntax rules. I feel like she's mixing up a problem about the world with a problem about language, and maybe she's overstating how mystical Bradley was. Could you audit this summary for me? Tell me which parts are roughly right, which ones are subtle misrepresentations or conflations, and whether her conclusion that Bradley was never answered actually follows from the accurate bits. I'm also curious—probably a dumb analogy—whether this kind of regress shows up in chemistry, like whether a chemical bond is a relation that needs another relation to hold the atoms together, or if that's just me forcing physics into metaphyics?
