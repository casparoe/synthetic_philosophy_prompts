Write a dialogue between a scholar defending the mechanist-compatibilist reading of Descartes and a scholar defending the radical-automatist reading, debating whether Descartes denies animals only rational souls or all sentience entirely. To avoid standard exegesis, center the dispute on a hypothetical “Cartesian hybrid”: an artificial body constructed from preserved canine tissues arranged into clockwork and hydraulic mechanisms identical to a living dog, lacking only a pineal gland linked to an immaterial soul. Have the speakers argue whether Descartes would attribute sensory awareness to this hybrid based solely on organizational form rather than biological origin, and whether his comparison of animals to hydraulic automata in royal gardens supports a threshold of complexity or pure mechanism. Present the dialogue as exactly five numbered exchanges, each exchange containing one brief turn from each speaker with each turn limited to one or two sentences, and include no introductory framing or concluding summary.
