You are drafting an operational manual for the acquisitions referee of a county library's "art-in-circulation" lending program. The referee holds no advanced art-history degree, must evaluate each submission in under twenty minutes, and files a one-page recommendation form co-signed by the board chair. The board has adopted Tolstoy's account of art: a work is genuine art only when it sincerely transmits an emotion the creator actually experienced, in a form that ordinary people can receive without specialized education, and it is not reducible to craft, decoration, or mere shock.

Construct a reusable decision procedure—ordered checks rather than abstract theory—that this referee can apply repeatedly, including to submissions involving AI generation, virtual-reality formats, or creators without formal training. The procedure should:

- List sequential checks with clear pass / fail / refer conditions.
- Flag the exact points where judgment cannot be eliminated and must be noted on the form.
- Work through three submissions: (i) an AI-generated landscape image that widely evokes melancholy despite being produced through prompt-engineering with no identifiable human emotional origin; (ii) a technically masterful hyperrealistic sculpture that viewers consistently call impressive but emotionally empty; (iii) a sincerely produced community ritual object whose emotional content is real but inaccessible without insider cultural knowledge. For one of these, the procedure must reach a recommendation you expect the board will dislike, and you should flag that tension explicitly.
- End with a brief statement of what the procedure is not designed to adjudicate (e.g., market value, historical importance, legal ownership).

Keep the response to roughly one page—about 400 words. Open with a single bottom-line paragraph stating the core decision rule and its two main failure points. Present the operational steps in bullet form. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
