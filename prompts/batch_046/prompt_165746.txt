You advise a regional disaster-preparedness authority that must set annual probability thresholds for triggering expensive mitigation measures. Your inputs are: (a) published scientific models that claim to state objective chances (recurrence intervals, transmission dynamics, climate-feedback probabilities), and (b) your staff’s own reasoned judgments drawn from local knowledge, analogies to past events, and awareness of model limitations. Operationalize David Lewis’s Principal Principle—roughly, that rational credence should align with known objective chances—into a reusable 30-minute procedure your analysts can run without philosophy training. The procedure should specify the ordered questions, what answers are sufficient at each step, where it mandates deference to the model, where it mandates overriding the model with staff judgment, where it requires widening an interval rather than selecting a point value, and where it stops and hands the decision to a value judgment (e.g., risk tolerance versus false-alarm costs). It must explicitly handle four hard cases: (i) the model gives a sharp number but rests on a reference class your staff think is mis-specified; (ii) you have no model but strong analogical reasoning that conflicts with a generic base rate; (iii) you learn of a physically plausible mechanism not captured by the model, but its quantitative effect is unknown; and (iv) two reputable models assign incompatible objective chances with no clear meta-criterion for choice. Apply the procedure to three scenarios: (1) a hydrological model assigns a 1-in-200 annual flood risk, but your engineers believe the rainfall-runoff relationship has changed due to upstream development outside the calibration period; (2) you must set a prior for “a novel pathogen with sustained human-to-human transmission emerges in this region within five years”—epidemiologists provide a range, but your staff have local intelligence about wildlife-market patterns that the epidemiological literature does not model; (3) a climate model assigns a 4% decadal chance of regional agricultural collapse, and a recent post-model study identifies a feedback loop that could accelerate collapse without quantifying the new probability. Be explicit about where the procedure misfires, which steps are judgment calls disguised as rules, and what the procedure cannot settle. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
