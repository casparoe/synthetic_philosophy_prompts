Draft the joint statement that the Unitary Camp and the Disaggregation Camp could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the nature of the alignment problem. The Unitary Camp holds that there is one core alignment problem—ensuring that an artificial agent’s effective objectives remain stable and coherent with human values across changes in context, capability, and representation—of which reward misspecification, instrumental convergence, and value drift are manifestations rather than separate diseases. The Disaggregation Camp holds that alignment is a family-resemblance term covering at least three non-interreducible problems: representational alignment (mapping human values into formal objectives), procedural alignment (ensuring reasoning processes do not subvert those objectives), and institutional alignment (embedding the system in social structures that correct residual errors), and that solving one can exacerbate another.

Your statement should contain the following, formatted precisely:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims, precise enough that neither camp can treat them as empty concessions. Cover: (a) the logical structure of Newcomb-like reasoning as applied to an agent that models its own deployment; (b) the phenomenon of reward misspecification in multi-objective environments; (c) the distinction between an agent’s stated objective and its revealed preference under distribution shift; and (d) the conditions under which an apparently aligned system produces systematically harmful outcomes without explicit adversarial intent. Where the camps interpret a claim differently, record both interpretations in parallel clauses within the same numbered item.

2. A disagreements table with two columns—Unitary and Disaggregation—each row phrased in language that the respective camp itself would accept. The rows should address: the ontological status of the alignment problem (single failure mode versus family of failures); the relationship between representational and procedural alignment; whether institutional correction belongs to alignment proper or is an external safeguard; and the predictive claim about whether progress on one front (for example, interpretability) automatically improves the others.

3. The cruxes: list the sub-questions such that settling them with conceptual or empirical evidence would cause at least one camp to revise its view. For each crux, specify: (i) the case, argument, or test both sides agree bears on it—include the hypothetical contextual arbitration scenario, in which a multi-objective planner must choose between an explicit user instruction and an inferred social norm in a domain absent from its training distribution; (ii) the Unitary Camp’s prediction of what this scenario reveals about the underlying problem structure; and (iii) the Disaggregation Camp’s corresponding prediction.

4. A brief joint note distinguishing what was resolved as merely terminological from what remains genuinely unsettled.

Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke highly specialized technical results that depend on access to recent literature; ground every claim in conceptual reasoning that could be followed without external search. Write in a formal, polished register.
