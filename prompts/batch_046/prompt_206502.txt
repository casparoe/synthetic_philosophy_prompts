Weber's Protestant ethic case: Calvinist anxiety about salvation genuinely pushed people toward rational, methodical capitalism. I'd say yes, culture was a real cause here, not just a post-hock story.

The slavery case: racist ideologies in slave economies. I'd say no — the economic system came first and manufactured the values to justify itself.

Japanese monozukuri (that craft-spirit thing) and Toyota's lean system: yes, but only because the value was locked into apprenticeship institutions. Float it free and it does nothing.

Confucian filial piety shaping clan-based welfare in imperial China: yes, the value directly structured the institution.

But then Confucian meritocracy and the civil service exam: I want to say no, the state needed bureacrats and invented the exam first, then borrowed Confucian language to legitimise it. Same culture, both values, opposite verdict — I can't square them.

Islamic prohibition on usury leading to mudaraba partnership contracts: yes, the religious value genuinely reshaped economic law.

So is there a principle that saves all of these? Or do I have to drop one?
