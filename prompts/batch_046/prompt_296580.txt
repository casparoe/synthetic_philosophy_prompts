(1) Extinction is specially bad because it kills that shared afterlife for everyone alive at once.
(2) If that's right, then the harm to each person is just the loss of their own personal afterlife.
(3) So the total badness of extinction is just the sumed-up indivdual losses.
(4) But then if one person holds the only key to a massive future project - say the one scientist who can cure cancer - killing them wipes out the same kind of afterlife for everyone who depended on it.
(5) So that single murder should be almost as bad as extinction, since it's the same summed loss concentrated in one act.
(6) That can't be right. Extinction feels like a different catagory of tragedy, not just one big murder multiplied.
(7) So either (2) is wrong and there's something collective about the afterlife that isn't summed up, or (1) is wrong and extinction is just a mass death with no extra badness.
(8) I really want to keep (1) because it matches my gut feeling that the end of humanity is uniquely awful. But if I keep it, do I have to accept that ordinary deaths can be just as bad? Wether the mistake is in step (2) treating the afterlife like private property?
