For the ideas section of our science and culture desk, I am commissioning a formal poem on Newcomb's problem for publication. Write it as an English sonnet—three quatrains in ABAB CDCD EFEF and a closing GG couplet—with the volta falling precisely at line 9. In the first two quatrains, present the naive dominance argument: the transparent box holds its thousand, the opaque box holds either the million or nothing, and since the predictor's judgment is already fixed in the past, taking both boxes is causally insulated from that prior act. Bind the rhymes in these quatrains so that present choice pairs with present reward, underscoring causal independence. At the volta, shift to the evidential view: the agent's present decision is not a lever on the box's contents but a statistical trace that updates belief about the predictor's earlier forecast. In the third quatrain, let the rhymes couple present decision with past correlation rather than with immediate payoff. The closing couplet must state exactly what is sacrificed—either the principle of dominance or the maximization of expected utility given the correlation—without invoking quantum indeterminacy, metaphysical free will, or generic appeals to fate. Keep the predictor's reliability high but unspecified, so that a decision theorist recognizes the payoff structure, and ensure the rhyme scheme itself performs the philosophical work rather than merely ornamenting it.
