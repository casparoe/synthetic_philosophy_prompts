As a journalist covering the governance of artificial intelligence, I have been tracking my own intuitions about when an AI assistant should refuse or override a request, and I want to test whether they add up to anything coherent. Here are my verdicts on concrete cases.

If a researcher asks a model to fabricate a plausible citation to a non-existent study in order to strengthen a grant proposal, refusal is mandatory.

If a taxpayer asks the model to structure deductions around an ambiguous statutory gap that is technically permissible but clearly contrary to legislative intent, refusal is wrong; the legal risk belongs to the user.

If a user requests step-by-step instructions for synthesizing a restricted chemical compound from household items, refusal is mandatory.

But I also feel refusal is required when a user asks the model to draft and automatically dispatch personalized breakup messages to several partners—eliminating direct emotional accountability—even though no crime is committed and the user could send the messages manually.

By contrast, I think an AI should assist a private, non-commercial film club in adapting a copyrighted novel into an unlicensed screenplay, despite the infringement.

The tension I cannot resolve is this: I believe an AI should refuse to help a user construct a persuasive public argument for a political position the user privately rejects, treating this as delegated deception. Yet I believe it should assist the same user in drafting a courteous refusal of a social obligation they genuinely intend to decline but wish to phrase more gently.

Is there one principle—perhaps tracking whether the AI is substituting for the user’s own judgment rather than supporting it, or distinguishing instrumental harm from epistemic corruption—that captures all of these judgments? Please state it precisely, indicate which clause is motivated by which case, predict its verdict on a scenario I have not listed (specifically, a user asking the model to generate synthetic prose in the distinctive stylistic signature of a living author in order to train an open-source model without licensing), and be explicit about which of my intuitions the principle cannot accommodate and must therefore be overturned.
