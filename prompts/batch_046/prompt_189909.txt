One student then invoked this to argue that, since potential people do not suffer deprivation from never being born, there can be no moral reason to bring anyone into existence—not even in a scenario where ecological instability makes biological reproduction risky, but where synthetic gestation could produce conscious beings whose lives would be predominantly good. I want to understand exactly what that slogan could be committing its user to. Could you lay out, as a numbered menu, the distinct precise theses it might express—ranging from purely logical or definitional claims, to metaphysical claims about harm and deprivation, to normative principles about procreative obligations—indicating for each whether it is trivial, defensible but controversial, or false? I am especially interested in whether the asymmetry still applies to a hypothetical case we discussed: an engineered conscious system that lacks the cognitive architecture for existential comparison or deprivation-awareness but can experience sustained positive states—does the slogan retain its force there, or does it depend on a specific psychology of harm? Please conclude with a single line beginning Verdict: followed by the hidden premise, if any, that is actually doing the labor in my classmate’s argument.
