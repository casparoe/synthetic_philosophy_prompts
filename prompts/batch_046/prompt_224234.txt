Write a deadpan parody of a UK local-authority planning guidance document—modelled on the dry format of a statutory instrument or council gazette notice—issued by the fictional Borough of Lower Sorites. The document should announce that the council has formally adopted an epistemicist framework for all zoning, licensing, and nuisance regulations: vague predicates such as "tall building," "excessive noise," and "derelict garden" are held to possess precise, sharp boundaries that are discoverable but currently unknown to residents. To enforce this, the council establishes a classified "Ontological Surveyor" service that determines exact cut-offs (down to the millimetre or decibel) using a proprietary "sorites-resistant measurement protocol," but refuses to publish the results, instead fining citizens for "epistemic negligence" when they unknowingly cross the hidden line. Include, as an appendix, a dissenting supervaluationist minority opinion—written in the same bureaucratic register—arguing that where a predicate is genuinely indeterminate there is no fact of the matter to violate, rendering all penalties void ab initio. Keep the tone relentlessly formal and deadpan; do not explain the joke, do not wink, and do not cite named living philosophers. The satire should derive its force from the internal logic of enforcing unknown sharp boundaries rather than from invented absurdities unrelated to the view. Aim for approximately 600 words. Use British English throughout (e.g. "programme," "neighbour," "licence" as noun).
