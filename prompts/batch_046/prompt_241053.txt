I've been a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, and next week I'm presenting at an invitation-only workshop in philosophy of value. The thesis I'm defending is a hybrid position in population ethics: standard lexical negative utilitarianism treats suffering and pleasure as categorically incommensurable—no amount of the latter can offset any amount of the former—but this relies on an implicit discontinuity in welfare space. I propose a "threshold-continuous" model in which welfare is measured on a single cardinal scale with a sharp discontinuity in marginal weight near the neutral point: below the lexical threshold, increments of suffering carry effectively infinite negative weight relative to any pleasure above the line, while within each region (negative and positive welfare) full cardinal commensurability holds. This avoids the repugnant conclusion without positing separate value-ontologies. My sketch: define a welfare function W with a jump discontinuity at W=0; assign weights w(s) that tend to infinity as W approaches 0 from below and are linear as W approaches 0 from above; show that population choices maximize aggregate weighted welfare; argue that this captures the lexical intuition structurally rather than categorically. The audience is eight specialists: three committed lexical negative utilitarians who know the arguments in the literature on population ethics associated with Derek Parfit well, two welfare economists who reject cardinal utility in principle, two value-pluralists who think pleasure and pain belong to incommensurable dimensions, and one philosopher of science who is skeptical of any quantitative welfare metric. Prepare me for the objections.
