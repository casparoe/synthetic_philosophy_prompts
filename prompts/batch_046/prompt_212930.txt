I don't have any formal background in philosophy, but I've been trying to think through a personal decision on my own and keep running into what feels like a conceptual knot I can't untie.

I'm considering joining a long-term residential community where members permanently give up private property, live in shared quarters with no personal space, and agree that every major life decision will be made collectively. The people who have stayed for years say their priorities changed completely: career ambition, individual achievement, and even the desire for privacy came to feel unreal to them, like memories of a dream they no longer inhabit. I'm drawn to the calm and sense of purpose they describe, but I'm also terrified that the person who signs the agreement is essentially voting for a future self whose values she won't share. When I try to weigh the choice rationally, I realize I'm using my current framework—independence, personal growth, private life—to evaluate a life that is designed to erase exactly that framework. It feels like I'm being asked to decide on behalf of someone I haven't become yet, using standards that future person would reject.

My question isn't about whether the community itself is good or bad. It's whether there's a coherent philosophical way to approach a decision when you know that making it will change the very values and priorities you need in order to make it. Does rational choice require some continuity between the decider and the one who lives with the consequences? Or is there a framework for accepting that the decider and the future self are, in a meaningful sense, different people, without that making the choice arbitrary or irrational? I'm looking for the structure of the problem and how philosophy addresses it, not generic life advice.
