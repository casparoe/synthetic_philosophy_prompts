I'm on the appeals subcommittee for the National Reconciliation Archive's amnesty review pannel — we're kind of a hybrid between South Africa's TRC disclosure rules and Argentina's later bureaucratic accountability hearings, but with our own local statute. We gotta decide Petition 14-B: a former records clerk from the old security ministry is asking for "tiered disclosure amnesty." She'll testify about filing protocols and name the units that requested her files, but she wants her personal annotations redacted, claiming she never had "interpretive access" to what the codes meant — she was just alphabetizing.

Our precedents are messy. We granted full amnesty to a logistics driver who mapped detention routes and named every commander he reported to, even though he said he thought they were supply depots. We denied amnesty to an archivist who kept the same filing codes but refused to say which units requested them, saying her silence was complicity. We also approved a partial deal for a translator who revealed the content of intercepted messages but insisted she didn't understand the jargon — we let her keep personal notes sealed but required the translated texts released.

The philosophical knot is whether moral culpability in bureaucratic harm tracks causal contribution (she physically moved the files) or epistemic access (she allegedly didn't know they were for disappearances). There's also this newer argument floating around in transitional justice circles — call it the "partition thesis" — that amnesty should only require disclosure proportional to the actor's "interpretive proximity," not just their role in the chain.

My co-pannelist thinks we should reject 14-B because allowing redaction of annotations sets a precedent that clerks can hide behind procedural blindness. Another member thinks we should approve it but require her to describe the filing system in detail, since that's her actual knowledge contribution.

Write me a short policy memo for the full panel recommending what we should do with 14-B, with a clear bottom line. I need the reasoning to actually wrestle with the partition thesis, treat the strongest argument for the other side fairly, and say which of our past cases is the closest analog. Don't just list books or reading lists — we don't have time and this isn't a lit review.
