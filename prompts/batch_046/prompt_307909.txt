Write a villanelle about Glaucon’s challenge—specifically the thought experiment of the ring of Gyges in Plato’s Republic—but reframe the ring’s power not as invisibility to human sight, but as causal isolation: the wearer’s actions generate no downstream effects in anyone else’s life, leave no trace in social memory, and produce no ripple in the fabric of consequence. Use the villanelle’s two refrains and its cyclical return to track the tension between justice as an intrinsic structural good and justice as a practice that depends on being witnessed or causally embedded in a shared world. The first refrain should invoke the ring’s isolation (for instance, a line about acting without leaving a mark); the second should invoke justice persisting—or failing—under that isolation. In the five tercets, move from the naive assumption that justice collapses once there are no observers or affected parties, through the recognition that even in causal isolation the logical structure of fairness might remain intact, to a closing quatrain in which the two refrains converge on whether moral value requires an audience, a victim, or any external trace at all. The poem should be philosophically accurate enough that someone familiar with the Republic’s argument about justice for its own sake would recognize the stakes, yet it should not include direct quotations from Plato or depend on specific line numbers; instead, render the philosophical tension through the formal repetition, rhyme scheme, and the structural turn of the villanelle itself.
