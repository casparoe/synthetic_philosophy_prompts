I'm a senior planner for a mid-size city, and every quarter we get proposals to kill old zoning or street rules that "don't make sense anymore" — alley-width minimums, paper-filing rules for variances, bans on corner groceries. I need a reusable decision proceedure I can run in about 30 minutes, grounded in Burkean prescription and Chesterton's fence, but applied specifically to what I'm calling procedural fences: regulations where the original builder is unknown, the burden is visible, and the harm of removal is invisible. Don't just tell me to leave it alone. Give me ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the proceedure stops with a hard no, and where I have to hand the call to council because it's values not facts. Flag the misfires — places where it'll wrongly preserve dead rules or wrongly kill live ones. Then apply it to two proposals: (1) scrapping the 1920s alley-width rule so we can squeeze in more infill housing, and (2) killing the mandatory paper-notice requirement for zoning variances, which my staff really wants gone but I think might be a fence. Make sure one answer comes out "keep" when I'd rather scrap it, and say flat out what the proceedure can't do.
