Design a decision tree for a philosophy-of-cognitive-science seminar on whether symbolic representations exert irreducible downward causal influence over their implementing substrate. Frame the case around two niche theoretical constructs introduced for the exercise: the careenium, a substrate-layer architecture in which representational states are implemented but not locally reducible to its dynamics; and simmballs, hypothetical symbolic entities claimed to possess causal powers independent of their tokening substrate. The tree should sort positions on the question "do symbols push the substrate around?" — i.e., whether representational content plays an ineliminable causal role in altering the careenium's physical state, or whether substrate change is exhaustively explained by sub-symbolic dynamics. Build the tree from four concrete branch commitments that a respondent could endorse without knowing the position labels: (1) whether the careenium permits multiple realizability of simmball states; (2) whether the causal-exclusion principle applies between substrate-level and symbol-level descriptions; (3) whether the direction of causation is strictly upward, strictly downward, or reciprocal; (4) whether simmball identity depends on substrate continuity or on functional-relational properties alone. The leaves should include substrate reductionism, emergent symbolism, reciprocal causation, and eliminativist substrate monism; flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate, and identify any logically possible answer-combination that is unoccupied, briefly diagnosing whether the gap is due to incoherence, instability, or mere neglect. Do not request citations, author names, or a literature review; the instrument should classify by commitments, not by label recognition. Specify the audience (graduate seminar) and require that every branch node be phrased as a question answerable without prior knowledge of which interpretation it yields.
