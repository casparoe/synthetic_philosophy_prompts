I'm a phd student in philosophy of tech, working part-time with a regional board that audits public-sector LLM deployments — housing eligibility, welfare routing, stuff like that. We recieve maybe three incident reports a week where a model made a recommendation that caused real harm or just looked wildly off-base. My supervisor wants me to build a reusable audit procedure that her team can run in about two hours, with no access to model weights, to decide whether a failure should trigger a process-based fix (rework the reasoning protocol, step-level supervision, maybe retrain) or an outcome-based fix (patch outputs, tighten guardrails, decommission).

The idea i want operationalized is the process-vs-outcome supervision distinction, but i want to inject two specific checks that aren't in the standard literature: a counterfactual path stability check — asking whether the harmful outcome could have been reached through a reasoning path that technically complies with the model's stated spec — and a procedural drift metric, comparing the model's reasoning structure across similar cases over months, without ground-truth labels for every intermediate step. I think these bridge the gap between path and result, but maybe i'm just dressing up outcome-checking in fancier language.

Please write the actual procedure: ordered questions, stop conditions, where it hands off to human judgment because the issue is values not facts, and explicit flags for where it will misfire. Then run it on three cases: (1) a housing eligibility model that denies a family because of a mis-weighted stability factor in its reasoning chain; (2) a welfare routing model that sends a vulnerable user to an automated line instead of a human caseworker, but the reasoning log looks formally correct; (3) a case where the procedure outputs "process overhaul required" but i know the board will hate that answer because it's expensive and disruptive. For each, state the output and what's missing.

If this framing is confused — for instance, if separating process and outcome audits is impossible without full interpretability, or if my counterfactual path stability thing collapses into just checking outputs — say so plainly instead of being polite and giving me a pretty checklist.

Format:
1. The audit flow — about 400 words, step-by-step with judgment-call flags.
2. Three worked runs — about 250 words each.
3. What the procedure can't do — about 200 words.
4. Pushback check — about 150 words, straight talk on whether the premise holds.

Keep it practical. No citations, no reading lists, no "see Smith 2019". Write like you're briefing my supervisor who has a law degree and no patience for theory for its own sake.
