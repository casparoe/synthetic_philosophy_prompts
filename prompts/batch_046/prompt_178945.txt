The example should show how applying the dual criteria of simplicity (minimum of fundamental principles) and richness (maximum of phenomena) to such worlds generates an ambiguity about whether a world containing these entities is genuinely superior to ours, and whether the principle of sufficient reason can uniquely determine the actual world against a continuum of near-identical alternatives that differ only in the distribution of vacuous possibles. Avoid invoking standard objections like the problem of evil; derive the difficulty solely from the interaction of compossibility, perfection, and modal density.
