I've been arguing with my roommate about this required freshman seminar my school runs called 'Building a Purposeful Life' or something close to that. Every student has to turn in this portfolio mapping out their 'core values' and 'life direction,' and the grading sheet has this weird checkbox: 'Student affirms that human existence can carry objective significance.' If you leave it blank or write that you think life is basically absurd and meaning is just a human projection, you don't automatically fail, but you get an automatic referal to talk with a campus counselor about 'existential wellness.' At the same time, the syllabus assigns Camus and some modern philosophers who treat absurdism as a perfectly rational response to an indifferent universe.

So I'm trying to figure out what philosophical commitments this grading practice actually smuggles in. Does it presuppose that objective meaning is a precondition for psychological health? Or could a subjectivist about meaning defend the policy by saying it's just a pragmatic filter, not a metaphysical claim? And is there any way for the institution to be philosophicaly consistent here—teaching that absurdism is a live option in the classroom while treating it in the assessment rubric like a kind of risk factor?
