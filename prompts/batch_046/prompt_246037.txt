After a long, unresolved argument with a friend about whether AI alignment discourse rests on a category mistake, I want to see the disagreement worked out with precision rather than slogans. Write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—call them R and S—debating the orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence. R defends both claims: that intelligence and final goals are largely independent, and that sufficiently capable agents will converge on self-preservation, resource acquisition, and cognitive enhancement regardless of their terminal values. S opposes this, arguing that the framework presupposes an unrealistically sparse, utility-maximizing model of cognition and ignores that reasoning in any physically realized system is structured by what she terms constitutive norms—rules that do not merely guide action toward an end but partially define what counts as reasoning at all—rather than by purely regulative, instrumental ones. The exchange must contain genuine back-and-forth: each speaker should respond directly to the specific point just made, not pivot to a prepared monologue. In particular, have them examine whether an agent whose belief-revision and coherence practices are governed by such constitutive norms could still exhibit instrumental convergence toward arbitrary goals, or whether that convergence is largely an artifact of modeling cognition as pure optimization. Keep both sides sharp, avoid strawmen, and do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; the aim is conceptual clarity, not scholarly apparatus.
