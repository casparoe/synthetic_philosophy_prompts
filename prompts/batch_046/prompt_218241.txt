For a philosophy seminar on long-term policy, write the desiderata that any adequate theory of intergenerational climate obligations must satisfy. The theory must handle cases where beneficiaries and burden-bearers never overlap in time; must preserve the distinction between harming a specific future person and failing to create additional welfare; must remain consistent with standard climate-economics damage functions without importing their discounting assumptions; and must explain why present agents have obligations to distant generations even if those generations' values are unknown or structurally altered by future value-alignment mechanisms. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or conceptual pressure, mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps (person-affecting restrictions versus aggregate-welfare approaches), and say whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable—especially whether avoiding the non-identity problem conflicts with treating mitigation as obligatory rather than charitable. Keep the weight on the criteria and their logical relationships; brief scoring of major contenders is optional.
