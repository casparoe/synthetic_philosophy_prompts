Exam question: In Robin Hanson's futarchy, what is the division of labor between democratic voting and prediction-market betting? Specifically, explain how the value function is established, how decision markets operate, and why the mechanism does not reduce governance to pure market forecasting.

Student answer: Futarchy separates goal-setting from means-selection by letting citizens vote on a value function—typically an explicit metric like GDP growth or welfare—while traders bet in decision markets on which proposed policy will maximize that metric. Once betting converges, the policy with the highest predicted value is implemented automatically. There are also value markets that run in parallel, forecasting the metric itself under different conditions, and both market types work through the same price mechanism. Because the value function is updated continuously through trader behavior rather than fixed by a one-time vote, democratic input is embedded in market dynamics rather than in a separate institutional layer. Consequently, futarchy replaces traditional legislative deliberation about ends with a self-correcting forecasting system that optimizes for revealed collective preference.
