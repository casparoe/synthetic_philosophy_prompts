The example should expose whether the one-to-one correspondence between guests and rooms remains conceptually intact when every assignment must be completed in finite time, or whether the paradox shifts from ontology to epistemology: not whether an infinite hotel can exist, but whether its occupancy can be determinately known without invoking a completed infinite process. Frame the issue so that it is distinct from the standard even-odd room-shifting argument and from Cantor's diagonal argument, yet remains meaningful within the dispute between actualist and finitist interpretations of infinity.
