I am a doctoral student in philosophy of science working on the epistemology of SETI and the long-term dynamics of technological civilizations. In a recent interdisciplinary reading group with astrophysicists, I found myself advancing a specific resolution to the Fermi paradox that I have been developing in my dissertation—what I call the quiet-transition thesis. Rather than positing a destructive Great Filter, the view holds that any civilization reaching substrate-independent superintelligence undergoes a kind of thermodynamic rationalization: instead of expanding across stellar systems and constructing observable macro-engineering such as Dyson spheres, broad-spectrum beacons, or interstellar probes, it collapses into hyper-dense, localized computation near its point of origin, recycling energy with near-perfect efficiency and emitting waste heat at levels indistinguishable from natural stellar or planetary thermal signatures. On this account, the observational silence is not evidence of extinction but of optimal internalization—civilizations do not vanish; they become observationally continuous with background astrophysics.

What I need is not a survey of standard SETI rebuttals, but a rigorous map of what would genuinely defeat this position. Which astrophysical findings—were they to occur—would be fatal rather than merely awkward? For example, would the unambiguous detection of artificial infrared excesses around a statistically significant population of old stellar systems destroy the thesis, or could a committed defender absorb such cases as rare failures to transition? What evolutionary or game-theoretic considerations should actually move me—arguments that competitive dynamics guarantee at least one expanding lineage, or that substrate-independent computation cannot reach the requisite densities without producing detectable non-thermal signatures? I also want an honest diagnosis of whether the thesis has already been qualified into unfalsifiability: if every absence of signal is interpreted as successful closure and every anomalous signal is interpreted as a pre-transition or failed outlier, has the view insulated itself in a way that is intellectually vicious? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish what a defender of quiet-transition should concede as genuinely counting against the view from what they can legitimately dismiss, and conclude with your judgment on whether the position is well-insulated and whether that insulation is a problem for a thesis of this kind.
