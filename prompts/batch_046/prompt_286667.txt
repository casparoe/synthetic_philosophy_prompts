I have recently been appointed to an institutional review board tasked with drafting criteria for decision-making capacity in patients with progressive cognitive decline, and I have found that my own conviction—that there is a genuine, non-arbitrary boundary between competence and incompetence, even if I cannot locate it precisely—pulls me into philosophical territory I had not expected to navigate. The dispute I need rendered precisely is that between epistemicism about vagueness, which holds that every vague predicate draws a sharp boundary obscured only by our ignorance, and supervaluationism, which holds that vagueness consists in semantic indeterminacy among equally admissible precisifications, leaving some cases genuinely without a unique correct classification. I want the joint statement that a rigorous, good-faith adversarial collaboration between a committed epistemicist and a committed supervaluationist could both sign.
