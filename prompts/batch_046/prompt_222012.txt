You work as a clinical psychologist in a neurodevelopmental clinic where you conduct 45-minute intake assessments. A recurring task is deciding whether a client's repetitive or ritualistic behavior is sphexish—mechanically rigid, executed with invariant form regardless of contextual feedback, and not responsive to changes in the goal-state—versus adaptively persistent: goal-directed, responsive to obstacles and feedback, and capable of revision when the context demands. This classification determines your clinic's pathway: sphexish behaviors are flagged for flexibility-training interventions; adaptive persistence is documented as a strength and accommodated rather than targeted for reduction. Your ethics board requires that every classification be defensible with documented criteria, not just clinical intuition, and you must note where value judgments enter.

Design a reusable decision procedure—ordered questions, decision nodes, and stopping conditions—that a practitioner can run in about 30 minutes using only interview responses and brief behavioral observation. The procedure must distinguish first-order flexibility (adjusting the means to a fixed end) from second-order flexibility (revising the end itself when feedback warrants), because a behavior can reroute around obstacles while still being rigid about its ultimate purpose. For each question, specify what counts as a sufficient answer to proceed, what answer stops the procedure with a sphexish classification, what answer stops it with an adaptive classification, and where the procedure must hand the decision to the practitioner because the issue is normative (for example, whether social harm justifies intervention regardless of flexibility).

Then apply the procedure to three cases:

A. A client checks that her apartment door is locked exactly seven times each night. If interrupted after the fourth check, she returns to one and completes the full sequence; the number does not change if she has already seen the lock engage, and she reports that stopping early produces intolerable anxiety.

B. A client drives the same route to work every day. When a bridge closure blocks it, he immediately consults a navigation app and takes an alternative; however, he insists on arriving at exactly 8:47 a.m. If the detour makes him earlier, he waits in the parking lot; if later, he experiences severe distress. The arrival time never shifts.

C. A client repeats a line from a film whenever he feels stress rising. The line's delivery varies in volume and pace with his emotional state, and he can suppress it when reminded of an important meeting, though he reports a lingering sense of incompletion. The behavior causes social embarrassment and occasional job risk, and the client explicitly asks you to help him eliminate it. Apply the procedure, report its classification, and then explain why you—the practitioner—might reject the procedure's recommendation and what that reveals about the procedure's limits.

Conclude by stating explicitly what the procedure cannot determine (for instance, whether a flexible behavior should be changed for social or ethical reasons) and flag any step where inter-rater reliability is likely to be poor.
