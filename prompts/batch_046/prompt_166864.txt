okay so me and my roommate got into it last night about evidential cooperation in big universes, and i built this argement that now feels totally broken but i cant name the crack. here it is step by step.

(1) if the cosmos is big enough there are causally cut-off copies of me facing the exact same choice-problem.
(2) by evidential reasoning, my pick gives me evidence about theirs, so i should maximize over the whole set, not just my local payoff.
(3) call the number of copies N; for any specific decision-type, cooperating costs me C locally but yields multiversal benefit N·B, and since N is astronomical the net is always positive whenever B > 0.
(4) but now apply this not just to famine relief, but to every micro-decision where i can help a copy: holding a door, answering an email, tipping, letting someone merge.
(5) for each of these there's a seperate pool of copies facing that exact micro-decision.
(6) so for every pool i should cooperate, because summed across that pool the gain dwarfs C.
(7) but there are infinitely many such pools across all possible decisions i could face.
(8) therefore rationality demands i spend every waking second performing the optimal micro-act for whatever pool im in, never resting, never spending on myself.
(9) thats absurd — i shouldn't have to live like a perfect moral saint to every random stranger just because of distant twins.
(10) so one of steps 1-6 is busted, or maybe 7 smuggles something in.

where does it actually break? i kinda want to keep (1) and (2) intact, because the evidential link feels real, but maybe (3) hides a scaling mistake? or is the error in (5)-(7), treating independent decision-pools like obligations stack? if i reject (3) does that kill all evidential cooperation, or just the extreme version? and what does rejecting (5) cost me — do i have to say cooperation only works for "important" choices, and if so why?
