I cover debates in science and intellectual history, and I have noticed a recurring one-liner used to dismiss obsolete theories: that "the past is only valuable as a ladder to the present." The phrase gets deployed to argue that we should not take discredited frameworks seriously as rational alternatives, but merely as mistakes that history corrected. I cannot tell what precise commitments it carries. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims this slogan might stand for—the tautological, the methodological rule about historiographic selection, the metaphysical claim about historical directionality, and the normative claim about whose present counts—rating each as trivial, defensible, or false? For each reading, say whether it actually supports treating past scientists as irrational rather than reasoning well with limited evidence, and indicate which version is likely being invoked when someone uses it to shut down discussion of whether pre-modern cosmology was reasonable in its own context. Please present the answers as a menu of readings with verdicts, not a single interpretation.
