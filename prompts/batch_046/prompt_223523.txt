I'm a journalist who reports from conflict zones, and I'm weighing an elective, irreversible neural modulation that suppresses empathic distress—sharpening my composure under fire but permanently altering how I experience others' suffering. I can list the career benefits and medical risks, yet the decision feels structurally different from ordinary career trade-offs: I have no access to what it is like to be the person who makes this choice, and anyone who has undergone it is, in the relevant sense, no longer the self I am now. Is my unease merely risk aversion, or does the problem lie in applying standard rational-choice frameworks to a decision that transforms the very preferences by which I would evaluate it?
