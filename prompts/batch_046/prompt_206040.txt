Write a satirical travel-guide entry, in the deadpan style of a Lonely Planet page, for the Republic of Gradua — a fictional state whose 1994 Constitutional Amendment abolished all sharp legal distinctions in favour of explicit sorites-tolerant governance. The entry should be approximately 600 words. Cover local customs (how residents navigate incremental citizenship grades, tax brackets defined without precise thresholds, and the national pastime of disputing whether a particular pile of grain counts as “a heap” for subsidy purposes); practical travel advice (how tourists should prepare for visa statuses that shift imperceptibly during a stay, how to read road signs that list speed limits as open-ended fuzzy ranges, and what to expect when crossing provincial borders that lack definite geographical endpoints); and a sidebar on the annual Festival of Tolerance, celebrating the country’s official rejection of bivalence. The satire should exaggerate real philosophical worries about vagueness in legal and administrative contexts — the erosion of binary rights, the arbitrariness concealed within apparently neutral gradations, and the practical impossibility of applying non-bivalent logic to criminal statutes — but must never state these objections explicitly; they should emerge solely from the deadpan description. Maintain strict internal consistency: do not allow the guide’s author to slip into assuming sharp categories for convenience. Write in British English, with the formal, polished tone appropriate to a reference work.
