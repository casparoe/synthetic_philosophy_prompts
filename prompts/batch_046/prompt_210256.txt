There's a weird split in how institutions treat borderline life. In hospitals, brain death means you're legally dead—even if your heart's still beating and cells are dividing—yet those same organs get harvested on the idea that they're living tissue for the recipient. Meanwhile, conservation law protects an ecosystem that lost all its original trees if the fungal network and nutrient cycling stay intact, but a synthetic bioreactor running the exact same cycles gets zero protection because it's not living habitat. What theory of life does this pair of practices actually presuppose? Is it organizational continuity, genetic lineage, being carbon-based, or something else? Can one view cover both cases, or are these policies quietly committed to different things? And if we replaced the underlying view with a purely functional one—where life is just sustained self-regulation—what would have to change in either medical law or environmental policy?
