I am an ecologist who has spent the last decade modeling adaptive-cycle dynamics and panarchical resilience in wetland systems, and I recently witnessed a rapid regime shift in a long-monitored site that collapsed the boundary distinctions I had taken for granted. The experience made me question whether the "system" I studied ever possessed an intrinsic identity or boundary, or whether it was always a conventionally designated slice of continuous biogeochemical flows. This has led me to study Madhyamaka emptiness and Nāgārjuna's tetralemma—the four-cornered negation that phenomena are neither existent, nor non-existent, nor both, nor neither, all with respect to svabhāva or inherent self-nature. I want you to restate the core Madhyamaka claim that all phenomena are empty of inherent existence, together with the logical structure of the tetralemma, entirely within the vocabulary of resilience ecology: adaptive cycles, panarchical scales, basins of attraction, regime shifts, engineered versus ecological resilience, and the ontology of system boundaries. Translate each of the four corners into a precise claim about whether an ecological regime exists, does not exist, both exists and does not exist, or neither exists nor fails to exist, articulated through ecological dynamics rather than metaphysical language. For each corner, report what translates cleanly, what must be distorted to fit within ecological vocabulary, and what has no counterpart at all in resilience theory. Then provide the residue report: does the untranslatable remainder indicate that resilience ecology lacks a metaphysical dimension it genuinely needs, or that the Madhyamaka claims that fail to map were always less conceptually substantive than they appeared? I am fluent in the ecological framework but not in Buddhist philosophy, and I want a systematic translation whose value lies as much in what it fails to carry as in what it carries. I do not need bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific sūtras or commentaries; I want a conceptual translation that a model without web access could evaluate on its own terms.
