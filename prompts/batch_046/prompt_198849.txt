- I think modesty usually means deferring to the outside view (base rates, referance classes) whenever it clashes with my inside reasoning, since I'm almost certainly not special.
- In philosophical domains—like judging modal arguments—there's no meaningful reference class, so modesty can't mean statistical deference; it has to mean being extra hard on my own biases.
- I think I can discount a base rate if I've got an inside-view reason I'm an outlier, but only if that reason is intersubjectivly checkable, not just gut feeling.
- For practical life choices (career, relationships) I should go with the outside view almost completely; my story about why "this time is different" is usually just rationalization.
- I also accept a "Damascus exception": if you go through a rapid, deep restructuring of your values, the reference class of your past self stops being predictive, and modesty actually demands trusting the new inside view more than the old base rate.
- And I hold a recursive transparency condition: to claim outlier status you need a checkable second-order reason that your first-order assessment isn't itself corrupted by overconfidence.
