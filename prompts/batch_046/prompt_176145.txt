Question: The “presumption of independence” (PI) is frequently motivated by a heuristic argument about tractability: unless we treat variables as probabilistically independent by default in the absence of specific correlational evidence, every observation might bear on every hypothesis, and inductive reasoning becomes computationally intractable. A recent proposal, the Model-Restriction Formulation (MRF), suggests implementing PI not by assigning factorized priors over joint distributions, but by restricting the space of admissible causal models to those in which no direct edges and no common causes link the variables in question; within that restricted class, the internal parameterization of each individual distribution remains entirely unspecified.

Part A: Explain why the tractability heuristic for PI does not automatically justify adopting MRF in place of a standard factorized-prior approach.

Part B: One classic objection holds that PI is self-undermining: any system that applies independence assumptions must itself rely on internal correlations (for instance, between sensory inputs and belief revisions), so global independence is incompatible with learning. Assess whether MRF escapes this objection.

Student answer:

Part A: The heuristic argument establishes that reasoning requires some default constraint on correlations. Under the standard factorized-prior approach, this constraint is written into the probabilities: we set P(X,Y) = P(X)P(Y). Under MRF, the constraint is written into the model space: we permit only graphs lacking edges and common causes between X and Y. Both formulations reduce the number of dependencies that must be tracked, so both secure tractability. Consequently, the heuristic supports both equally; the choice between them is purely representational—whether one encodes the constraint in the prior or in the admissible model class—so the argument gives no independent reason to prefer MRF.

Part B: MRF successfully avoids the self-undermining problem. The objection mistakenly assumes that applying PI requires treating the learner’s own internal states as independent of one another. But MRF applies only to the external domain being modeled, not to the architecture of the cognitive system that deploys it. A learner can maintain richly correlated internal representations—linking evidence to belief updates—while using MRF solely for its object-level theories. Hence there is no contradiction. The objection confuses domain-level independence with system-level independence, and once that distinction is drawn, MRF remains intact.
