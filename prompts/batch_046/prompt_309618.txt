Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on the distribution of consciousness across animals. The first camp, call them the Functional Analogue Sentientists, holds that affective phenomenal consciousness is distributed widely across bilaterians—possibly extending to decapod crustaceans and some cnidarians—because functionally analogous nociceptive and reward pathways, combined with flexible behavioural integration, are sufficient for valenced experience, regardless of whether the underlying architecture matches the mammalian cortex. The second camp, call them the Structural Integration Restrictionists, holds that phenomenal consciousness requires recurrent, globally integrated processing of a kind realised only in vertebrate forebrains and perhaps in cephalopod vertical lobes, and that behavioural flexibility or local circuit analogues in invertebrates are insufficient evidence for sentience.

Produce a formal document that both parties could sign. Use British English throughout. Structure it as follows: first, a numbered section of agreed claims—covering the current state of comparative neurobiology, the absence of direct first-person access to non-human minds, our capacity for welfare-relevant intervention, and the distinction between nociception and phenomenal pain—phrased so that neither camp would object to the wording. Second, a table of exact disagreements, with each camp’s position stated in neutral language that the opposing side would accept as a fair rendering of its view. Third, identify three cruxes: sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would genuinely move at least one camp. I suggest one crux concerns whether functional isomorphism without structural homology can carry phenomenal properties; another concerns how to weigh absence-of-behaviour versus presence-of-behaviour evidence in species that lack linguistic self-report; and a third concerns whether evolutionary continuity of valence mechanisms supports gradual distribution or instead reveals convergent but phenomenally empty analogues. For each crux, specify a concrete case, hypothetical scenario, or empirical finding that both sides agree bears on it—such as the silicon-replicated decapod ganglion thought experiment for the first crux, the comparative analysis of protective rubbing versus reflex withdrawal in injured crustaceans for the second, and the phylogenetic distribution of endogenous opioid modulation for the third. For each, record in advance what the Functional Analogue Sentientists and the Structural Integration Restrictionists respectively predict or require the evidence to show.

Keep the formulations precise and non-vacuous: do not allow either side to hide behind ambiguous terms like “awareness” or “suffering.” Where a pair of agreed claims risks collapsing into vagueness, sharpen it by reference to a specific behavioural or neural marker. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not ask for them; the document must stand on its own reasoning.
