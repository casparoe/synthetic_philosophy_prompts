ok so I'm putting together my notes for a roundtable on self-deception and want to check if my takes actually hang together before I go on record. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Self-deception is real, but I reject the old "homunculus" picture where one part of you literally knows the truth and hides it from another part. That feels like bad sci-fi.
2. Instead I think it's closer to inward pluralistic ignorance: you misread your own mental signals — like anxiety about debt — and attribute them to something harmless, using the same social filering you'd apply to other people's opinions. So there's never a literal contradiction inside one head, just misrouted evidence.
3. I also hold that mental content is basically holistic — beliefs don't sit in neat boxes, they bleed into each other through mood and association.
4. Still, I think people are genuinely blameworthy for this, especially when they actively avoid checking a bank statment they suspect will confirm they're broke.
5. And I think if a mental state is structurally blocked from you at time T — not just hard to reach, but genuinely walled off — then you can't be rationally required to reason with it right then.

Do these cohere? Specifically, does my holism in (3) undercut the "filering" story in (2) — can evidence be misrouted cleanly if everything is bleeding together? And does blaming someone in (4) for avoiding evidence commit me to saying they already had access to the threatening belief, which (2) denies? Or is that second worry only apparent, since blame might attach to the avoidance act rather than the failure to see? Also where does (5) land — does it fit, or does it pile on another problem?

Please audit this properly: rank the real conflicts by severity, flag which ones dissolve on inspection, and tell me exactly what I'd have to revise — and which single fix cleans up the most — without hedging. I want a committed take, not a buffet of options.
