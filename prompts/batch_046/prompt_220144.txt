After a long and inconclusive argument with a close friend about the regress problem—whether justification must terminate in basic beliefs, hang together through mutual coherence, or extend in an available non-repeating infinite chain—I have realized that the audit office I direct has never formalized how it evaluates the evidential chains behind emergency public-health recommendations. I need a reusable procedure.

I direct the epistemic-certification office for a federal emergency-preparedness agency. Every quarter my team of three must issue formal justification certificates for proposed interventions—vaccination schedules, quarantine protocols, resource-allocation rules—before they reach the director. We have forty-eight hours per proposal, no budget for outside philosophers, and I must personally sign every certificate or flag it as requiring human judgment. The stakes are high: a false positive (certifying an unjustified rule) triggers legal liability; a false negative (blocking a justified rule) costs lives.

Please construct a practical audit procedure—ordered questions, sufficient-answer conditions, explicit stop points with a refusal, and clearly marked judgment handoffs—that operationalizes infinitism: the view that a belief is justified only if it is supported by an available, non-circular, indefinitely extendable chain of reasons. The procedure must be runnable by staff without philosophical training, must not ask them to actually traverse an infinite chain, and must specify what practical proxy—such as an extendability audit or the documented absence of arbitrary termination—counts as satisfying the infinitist condition within our time and expertise constraints.

The procedure should include the following.

First, the ordered audit steps: how to map the justification chain for a proposal; how to check for hidden loops masked by disciplinary boundaries; how to test whether each link has a further available reason rather than a merely possible one; where the audit stops with a denial because a loop is detected, a termination point is unjustified, or extendability fails; and where it stops with a provisional pass.

Second, explicit markers for escalation to me as a judgment call rather than a mechanical result. These should cover cases where a normative premise—such as a claim about distributive fairness or the priority of human health over animal welfare—enters the chain; cases where the next required reason lies in a domain outside our agency’s competence; and cases where practitioners treat a claim as methodological bedrock that an infinitist demands be further supported.

Third, rules for three hard cases: detecting circular reasoning that practitioners defend as virtuous coherence across disciplines; treating statistical or causal-inference frameworks that staff treat as basic but which the procedure demands be extendable; and handling chain breaks at normative or metaphysical boundaries.

Fourth, three worked applications.

Application A: A proposal to vaccinate a vulnerable population against a painful infectious disease, justified by clinical efficacy data, immunological mechanism studies, peer-reviewed meta-analyses, and foundational assumptions about causal inference in epidemiology.

Application B: A proposal to cull a wildlife reservoir to prevent human spillover, justified by ecological modeling, historical precedent, and a normative premise that human health takes priority over animal welfare.

Application C: A proposal to mandate a specific ventilation standard for indoor spaces, justified by aerosol-physics studies, engineering cost-benefit analyses, and a legislative mandate—where the procedure should yield an answer my team will find unwelcome, such as refusing certification because the chain terminates in what practitioners consider an intuitively basic engineering principle, or flagging a loop between cost-benefit reasoning and the legislative mandate that practitioners insist is legitimate rather than circular.

Fifth, a frank statement of what the procedure cannot do: which justification failures it will miss because infinitism offers no algorithm for distinguishing genuinely available reasons from merely conceivable ones; where practical constraints force approximation of the infinite-chain condition; and where the risk of false positives and false negatives is highest.

The deliverable should be formal enough to guide mechanical execution where appropriate, but it must not disguise judgment calls as algorithmic steps. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke obscure technical apparatus that a trained staff member could not apply with the resources described.
