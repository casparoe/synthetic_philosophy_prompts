I'm putting together notes for a seminar on mind and machines and I keep circling back to this argument my study partner cooked up. It feels like it should collapse somewhere but I can't tell if it's a hidden equivocation or if I'm just biting a bullet I don't want to bite. Here's the chain, more or less how he laid it out:

(1) If adopting the intentional stance toward X lets us predict X's behavior better than any physical stance, then we are rationally entitled—maybe even compelled—to attribute beliefs and desires to X. That's Dennett's core idea, or close enough.

(2) Take a large language model that has been fine-tuned with RLHF. Its outputs across diverse prompts are far better predicted by treating it as having a stable set of preferences—say, a 'belive' that harmful requests should be refused and a 'desire' to be helpful—than by tracking individual weight updates.

(3) Now apply the same predictive test not to the whole model but to one of its middle-layer attention heads, specifically the ones that seem to track subject-verb agreement across long contexts. If we treat that head as 'believing' that the grammatical subject hasn't changed, we can predict its activation patterns with high accuracy.

(4) So by (1) and (3) we're entitled to attribute beliefs to that attention head.

(5) But an attention head is just a linear map followed by softmax; it has no internal coherence conditions, no false-belief task performance, and no cross-contextual stability. Attributing beliefs to it is clearly absurd.

(6) Therefore, if we want to avoid attributing beliefs to bare matrix operations, we have to deny that the intentional stance applies to the LLM in (2) either—since the predictive success is structurally the same, just at a seperate scale.

(7) But denying it for the LLM seems arbitrary and costs us any principled reason to resist saying that current AI systems literally have beliefs.

My gut says the slip is between (3) and (4), maybe something about the level of description being wrong, but I can't name it. Which premise should I actually reject?
