As a novelist composing a work of philosophical fiction set within an imagined academy, I require an examination instrument that could plausibly appear in its appendix—something that tests conceptual penetration rather than mnemonic recall, and that remains fully answerable without access to libraries or digital archives. The domain is the boundary problem: who belongs to the demos?

Please design a final essay question for an upper-level seminar in constitutional philosophy (ninety minutes; answers of roughly 1,200 words). To prevent students from reciting standard taxonomies, embed the problem within the following invented framework: the Aethelred Commonwealth, a post-territorial polity in which membership of the demos is determined not by citizenship or residence alone, but by a criterion of 'sustained ecological entanglement' assessed over a fifty-year horizon. Within this framework, temporary labour migrants—present for fewer than seven years yet fully subject to the polity's labour codes—are excluded on grounds of insufficient entanglement, whilst river-basin ecosystems spanning the territory are granted proxy representation through appointed human guardians.

The question must require students to adjudicate these two boundary rulings—exclusion of the short-term resident, inclusion of the ecological proxy—by deploying the tension between the all-affected-interests principle and the democratic-autonomy principle. It should further require them to determine whether the boundary problem is essentially a problem of moral standing, of democratic legitimacy, or of both in irreducible tension, and to confront the paradox of democratic self-constitution without merely labelling it.

Please supply:
(i) The examination question, precisely formulated;
(ii) A model answer of approximately 600 words;
(iii) A marking scheme that distinguishes an A-grade response from a B-grade response by specifying the exact conceptual moves required for the former and the specific omissions or misunderstandings that characterise the latter. The scheme should identify the errors that should lose marks—such as conflating affectedness with democratic authorship, or treating ecological proxy representation as unproblematically analogous to human representation.

Write in formal British English. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not depend upon specific attributions, dates, or titles of works. Where philosophical terminology is standard, paraphrase without quotation marks; do not include verbatim quotations from actual texts.
