Here is an argument I constructed for a seminar on the paradox of tragedy, and I now worry it proves too much.

(1) Tragedy is genuinely enjoyable despite reliably arousing negative emotions such as pity and fear.
(2) This is puzzling only if we assume that negative emotions are intrinsically aversive.
(3) The resolution is that tragedy provides what I will call aesthetic foreclosure: the audience knows the sad outcome is predetermined and contained within an artistic frame, which transforms the emotional response from a practical threat into an object of contemplative appreciation.
(4) If aesthetic foreclosure transforms negative emotion into enjoyment, then any representation that combines a predetermined negative outcome with an artistic framing device should also be enjoyable.
(5) But consider documentary films of historical atrocities: the outcomes are equally predetermined, and the films are framed for contemplation, yet audiences typically do not enjoy them, and finding them enjoyable is widely considered morally defective.
(6) Therefore, aesthetic foreclosure either applies to documentaries too—implying that enjoying documentary suffering should be legitimate, which contradicts our moral judgments—or it fails to distinguish tragedy and is not the explanation we sought.
(7) To avoid this, one might restrict aesthetic foreclosure to fictional representations. But then the explanation appeals to fictionality rather than to foreclosure, and fictionality alone does not explain why the negative emotions within tragedy are not aversive.
(8) So either I must accept that enjoying documentary suffering is legitimate, which seems absurd, or I must abandon aesthetic foreclosure as the explanation for tragedy.

Where exactly does this argument go wrong? I am especially reluctant to give up (3), because the framing mechanism seems essential to the experience of art, but I also want to preserve the intuition in (5) that there is something genuinely defective about taking pleasure in real suffering presented for contemplation. Can both be kept, and if so, which step should be rejected or qualified? Please diagnose the flaw, explain why the rejected step looked plausible, and say what cost each available repair carries for the overall explanation of tragic enjoyment.
