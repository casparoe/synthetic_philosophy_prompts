ok so I've been thinking about tying yourself to the mast and also about why countries make it so hard to change their constitutions, and I want to check if my takes are actually consistent or if I'm just picking and choosing. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm all for Ulysses contracts in everyday life - like putting your phone in a timed lockbox or using apps that block gambling sites, because I know my future self will be weak-willed and I want to protect me from me.

(2) I also think societies need really hard-to-change rules, like constitutions that take way more than half the vote to amend, since politicians and voters always chase short-term stuff and hurt the future.

(3) But I also think if someone genuinely changes who they are deep down - like a real moral conversion or realizing they were wrong about their identity - then promises or rules they made under the old self shouldn't trap them. Like signing up for the army then becoming a pacifist.

(4) And democracy should belong to people alive right now, not dead people from 200 years ago. So current citizens ought to be able to rewrite the rules if they really want, not be chained to old founders.

(5) Here's the weird one: I think any binding agreement only actually works if the person being bound can retroactively approve it once they're in the bound state. Like, if you lock your future self in a box, that only counts as legitimate if your future self, once locked in, can look back and say yeah, that was fair.

So does this hang together? It feels like (1) and (5) might be fighting since your locked-in self is probably just pissed off and wouldn't approve. And (2) with (4) and (5) seems like a mess because dead founders can't consent and neither can current citizens being bound. But maybe (1) and (3) aren't actually in tension - one is about weakness of will with the same values, one is about values actually changing? I don't know, I just want to know what's gotta go and what's actually fine.
