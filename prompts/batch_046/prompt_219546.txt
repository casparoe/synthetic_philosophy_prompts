I'm giving opening remarks tomorrow at a workshop called Work After Automation — half the room are labor economists, half are people who were recenlty displaced from factory and care jobs. I want to build the talk around a distinction I'm trying to work out: automation doesn't just erase jobs, it can leave the job title standing but hollow out the part where human judgment used to matter — like when a nurse is still present but the algorithm makes the call and she's basically the interface. I'm calling this "shell work" for now. The point is we keep measuring damage by counting lost positions, but maybe the deeper harm is when the position survives and people have to perform work that no longer feels like work. I need about four minutes of remarks where this idea actually constains what I can say — for instance, I shouldn't be able to just say "we need retraining" without asking whether training someone for shell work is even meaningful. The economists should have something concrete to push back on, and the workers shouldn't feel like I'm theorizing their lives away. Keep it casual, like I'm talking through it with a colleague rather than delivering a finished theory.
