Consider the open problem space surrounding Fitch's paradox of knowability — the derivation that if every truth is in principle knowable, then every truth is in fact known. The standard menu of responses is familiar: restrict the principle to contingent truths; weaken or replace the epistemic logic, for example by dropping the distribution axiom for knowledge operators; bite the bullet and accept a form of epistemic omniscience; or read the result as a reductio against verificationist or anti-realist semantics. Less often treated as a first-order issue is a modal ambiguity in what "knowable" means across these responses — specifically, whether the relevant accessibility relation tracks what is knowable to a particular situated agent, with bounded cognitive resources and temporal location, versus what is knowable in principle within a formal system or an idealized epistemic community. Suppose you are advising a small research group with roughly three years of effort, training in modal logic but not in specialized formal epistemology, on where to direct its work in this area. Which two or three open questions or lines of development should take priority, and in what dependency order? In particular: should resolving the agent-accessible versus system-accessible interpretation of the knowability operator be treated as upstream of choosing among the four standard responses, or is it a side issue? Among the remaining questions — such as whether the paradox genuinely undermines anti-realist semantics or is merely a technical artifact of S5-style epistemic frameworks, and whether analogous derivations arise in intuitionistic or modal logics weaker than S5 — which are tractable with current logical tools and which are overrated because their resolution would leave the central philosophical stakes unchanged? Defend your ordering with explicit criteria: dependency relations among the questions, tractability given the group's background, and the magnitude of downstream change if a question is settled. Deliver a defended agenda, not a survey or bibliography.
