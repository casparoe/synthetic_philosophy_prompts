I am presenting my paper “Design Stance Realism Without Projection” at next month’s philosophy of mind and science workshop. The thesis: Dennettian real patterns are not predictive heuristics but ontologically robust modal structures—constraints governing engineered and evolved systems that the physical stance necessarily underdetermines—without requiring intentional-stance attribution or mentalistic projection onto non-cognitive targets. The audience includes two Dennett scholars who treat the stances as purely pragmatic instruments, three physicalist philosophers of science skeptical of any irreducible pattern, one evolutionary biologist who relies on design-language only instrumentally, and several graduate students eager to map the debate onto current cognitive science. Draft my Q&A prep sheet: the five or six objections I will actually face, ordered by damage potential, each phrased exactly as that interlocutor would put it, with a two-minute reply, an honest note on where that reply is weakest, and the specific concession—if any—that would be fatal rather than cosmetic. Include the question I am praying nobody asks: the charge that my view collapses into a form of Aristotelian functional essentialism.
