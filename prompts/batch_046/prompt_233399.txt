I serve on my university’s Committee on Core Curriculum Reform, and we must vote next week on whether to approve a proposed mandatory module, Time and Cosmology, for second-year physics majors. The designers want two weeks on Gödel’s rotating-universe solutions to Einstein’s field equations, presented not only as formal curiosities but as grounding Gödel’s philosophical claim—developed in dialogue with Kantian idealism—that objective temporal passage is unreal. Proponents argue this teaches students how physical formalism can bear on metaphysics; critics, including faculty physicists, object that the model lacks empirical support (no observed cosmic rotation) and that presenting Gödel’s idealist interpretation as a lesson risks treating a contested metaphysical thesis as if it followed from mathematics. The committee needs a concrete recommendation with conditions if appropriate.

Please draft the committee’s decision memo recommending approval, approval with conditions, or rejection of the module as proposed. Treat the critics’ concerns about empirical plausibility and disciplinary boundaries seriously, and treat the proponents’ claim about philosophical literacy seriously. Separate what is settled in physics and in philosophy of time from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present Gödel’s idealist reading of rotation as the consensus view among physicists or philosophers. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.

Organize the response in the following sections, in this order, with approximate lengths:

1. Executive Summary and Recommendation (about 150 words)
2. Scientific and Philosophical Background (about 200 words)
3. The Case for the Module (about 200 words)
4. The Case Against (about 200 words)
5. Settled Points versus Genuinely Contested Points (about 150 words)
6. Concrete Recommendation and Conditions (about 150 words)
