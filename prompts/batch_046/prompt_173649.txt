I've been chewing on XOR blackmail and counterfactual mugging for a while, and I've landed on a kind of heretical take: I don't think they actually motivate EDT over CDT, or even show that rational agents should pay up. Instead, I think they rely on treating counterfactual probabilities like they're frequencies over some multiverse reference class, which is the wrong ontolgoy for a one-shot decision. If you use closest-world counterfactuals tied to your actual choice—no branching worlds, just modal proximity—then the XOR condition never gets realized in a way that creates a decision-relevant dependancy, so the "blackmail" dissolves without you having to bite any bullet. So my position is that these puzzles are mostly artifacts of a bad framming, not deep problems for causal decision theory. What would count against this? I want the formal results, metaphysical arguments, or empirical intuition-pumps that should actually move me—like a proof that CDT still gets exploited even in a strict single-world framing, or data showing people consistently choose the EDT-recommended action in analog cases, or a knockdown argument that closest-world semantics can't support the counterfactual dependancies we need for decision theory. Also, what does my view predict that EDT doesn't, and vice versa? And be straight with me: has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability, where any failure just gets chalked up to "wrong ontology"? If so, is that a fatal problem for a view like this, or just par for the course in metaphysics?
