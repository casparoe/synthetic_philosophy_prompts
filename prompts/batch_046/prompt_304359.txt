I've come around to a very thin reading of Descartes' cogito: it doesnt establish any persisting self or substantial thinker, only that there's an occurrent mental event happening right now—something like this thinking is occuring. I think this saves him from circularity and from presupposing an I, but I'm worried it's just a bait-and-switch where I keep the prestige of the argument while gutting its conclusion. What would count against this minimal-episode view? I want the real defeaters, not just historical complaints about what Descartes meant: which philosophical inferences does it fail to deliver that we actually need, does it secretly collapse into bundle theory or some kind of eliminativism about subjects, and are there empirical cases—say, from misattribution studies or split-brain phenomenology—where even this bare occurrent awareness seems shaky or wrongly assigned? Rank them from fatal to awkward. Also, is there a risk this position is unfalsifiable because it's too thin? No citations needed, just the arguments.
