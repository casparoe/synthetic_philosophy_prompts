first, the classic Libet result where the readiness potential starts like 350ms before you feel the urge. my take is that doesn't kill free will, it just means the conscious "i decided" is more like a report of what was already brewing downstairs.

second, there's that brain-scan study where they can predict whether you'll press left or right up to ten seconds ahead, with decent accuracy. that feels worse to me, but only because it's predicting the actual choice, not just a finger twitch. still, i think free will survives if it's about veto power, not about being mysterious.

third, here's a weird case i invented: call it the reverse-libert protocall. in expert jazz musicians, the conscious plan to play a specific phrase lights up in prefrontal cortex about half a second BEFORE any motor readiness potential in the basal ganglia. in beginners it's the normal Libet order. my gut says free will is "more real" for the expert because the conscious plan is driving, but that sounds elitist and maybe incoherent.

fourth, Libet's veto idea: you can still stop the action after RP starts. i want to say that's where free will lives, but then i also think if the veto itself is just another brain event predicted by prior states, then we're just kicking the can down the road forever.

so is there a single principle behind all four? like maybe free will is about hierarchical control or something? and if not, which verdict should i drop? also, what would your principle say about a situation where RP is present but the subject reports zero awareness of any urge, just a sudden movement they can't acount for? please don't just list philosophers, i want the actual principle and where it breaks.
