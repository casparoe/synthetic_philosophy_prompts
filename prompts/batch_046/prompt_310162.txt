Instead, they aim to determine which conceptual problems about future contingents remain genuinely unresolved and which of those resolutions actually carry weight for practical reasoning: specifically, for how rational agents should structure commitments, precautionary obligations, or conditional contracts when a future event’s occurrence is genuinely open rather than determinately true or false. I am trying to help them defend an internal research agenda, so I need you to rank the live lines of work not by prestige or familiarity, but by tractability for this particular group and by upstream importance for normative decision theory and institutional design.

In particular, please consider at least the following cluster of positions and problems: first, whether a thin-red-line distinction can be coherently maintained in branching-time models without collapsing open possibilities into a predetermined actual future, and whether that collapse is a logical failure or a normative one; second, whether supervaluationist or Ockhamist actualist semantics solve the bivalence puzzle without addressing the deeper tension between treating a future contingent as unsettled and still holding agents accountable for preparing for it; third, whether recent open-future normative frameworks in decision theory—those that reject standard expected-utility maximization over a fixed set of worlds—actually depend on a specific metaphysical resolution of the contingency problem, or whether they can operate with a purely epistemic interpretation of openness that leaves the metaphysics untouched; and fourth, what conceptual or applied evidence over the next few years would most shift the priority among these lines.

For each, explain why it deserves more or less of the group’s limited effort, what criteria you are using—such as upstream dependence of other questions, tractability for philosophers rather than mathematicians, direct relevance to institutional planning, and the risk of solving a formal logical puzzle while leaving the underlying philosophical tension intact—and where the central trade-offs lie, especially between achieving logical precision and resolving the normative conflict between fatalism, agency, and rational commitment under genuine contingency. The deliverable should be a defended ordering with explicit reasoning, not a survey of the literature or a reading list.
