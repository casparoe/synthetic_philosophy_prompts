There's this stalemate in epistemology between accuracy-first people and the ones who say epistemic utility theory misses something like understanding or explanatory coherence. The accuracy camp says once you have the right scoring rules for true belief, everything else is just instrumental; the other camp says that's like reducing nutrition to just not being hungry, you're missing the positive good. My working theory is that the disagreement isn't really about knowledge but about whether epistemic agents can have different risk profiles - like, some treat 'understanding' as high-variance accuracy-seeking, others treat it as a separate goal altogether. Is that the real structure, or is it a verbal mix-up where both sides agree on the formalism but call different parts 'epistemic'? I want an honest diagnosis: do they differ on facts about cognition, on standards for what counts as data, on hidden intuitions about explanation, or are they just asking different questions? And be concrete - what would actually break the deadlock? A formal result about agrregating accuracy across domains, an empirical finding, a conceptual distinction they haven't made, or should we just accept that the question is ill-posed?
