So there's this governance idea in AI safety that I've been mulling over. The proposal is basically: before you deploy a big model, your safety case has to survive an adversarial debate where you're not allowed to use technical jargon or formal notation. If the challenger says "i still don't understand," you have to keep simplifying until they do. The underlying idea is that a genuinely sound argument shouldn't need obscurity to hold up — if stripping away the math makes your safety claim collapse, then it was probably an obfuscated argument hiding a weak point. But people also worry that some alignment properties are just inherently technical, and forcing them into plain language flattens important distinctions. So what I'm wondering is: what kind of epistemology does this institutional rule actually presuppose? Does it assume that all real knowledge is transparent and reducible to common-sense intuition? Does it quietly rule out the idea that expertise involves irreducible judgment? And if we replaced this with a view that lets experts keep their technical language, what exactly in the debate practice would have to change?
