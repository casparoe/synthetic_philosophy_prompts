heres the answer:

'Lucas's argument was first published in 1961 and it depends on Gödel's second incompleteness theorem. If the mind is a consistent formal system F, then F cannot prove its own consistency, yet a human mathematician can see that F is consistent, therefore the mind is not F. Penrose in The Emperor's New Mind (1989) repeats this but switches to Gödel's first theorem, constructing a particular Gödel sentence that a computer programme could never derive but a person can. He also brings in quantum gravity and non-computability to strengthen the case. Benacerraf objected that perhaps the mind is equivalent to an inconsistent formal system, in which case Gödel's result wouldn't apply; however, Lucas had already solved this by proving that human reasoning is always consistent. Consequently, Penrose hasn't really fixed the underlying logical problem—he's just added physics that doesn't address Benacerraf's point.'
