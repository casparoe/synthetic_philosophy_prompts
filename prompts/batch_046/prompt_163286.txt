This has forced me to confront, with some urgency, the distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, particularly as it interacts with global workspace theory. I want your actual credence—expressed as a number between 0 and 1—that in a human brain which fully meets the functional conditions for global workspace broadcasting (widespread recurrent processing, flexible information integration across sensory and executive modules, and availability for verbal or behavioral report should a channel exist), phenomenal consciousness could nevertheless be entirely absent; in other words, that the workspace conditions for access are satisfied while the conditions for phenomenal experience are not, and that this dissociation can occur as an actual clinical state rather than only as a metaphysical possibility. Please decompose your reasoning: first, your credence that global workspace theory correctly identifies the neural mechanisms of access consciousness; second, your credence that phenomenal consciousness requires something beyond those mechanisms, whether higher-order representation, specific thalamic engagement, or some other supplement; third, your credence that the clinical profile I described genuinely instantiates the workspace conditions without that phenomenal supplement; and fourth, any other factor you judge relevant to the joint distribution. Provide a rough number and one line of justification for each term, show how they combine into the overall credence, and identify which term is least stable—specifically, what empirical finding or conceptual argument would most move your overall number. I am not asking for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; I need the reasoning decomposed so I can locate where my own uncertainty should concentrate.
