I've been playing with the idea that Goodman's grue problem isn't about induction being broken but about projectibility being a convention in roughly the Lewis sense — like, we co-ordinate on 'green' not because it carves nature but because it's the salient equilibrium we've settled into, and 'grue' is just an unused strategy in the same co-ordination game. So Carnap was right that the choice is analytic, but a Lewis-style account explains why we don't just flip arbitrarily. What'd count against this hybrid framwork? Not the usual 'emeralds stay green' objection — I want the deeper defeaters: formal results about convention stability, cases where co-ordination explanations fail in other philosophical domains, intuitions that projectibility tracks discovery not decision, and whether the view collapses into a banal claim about language behaviour. Rank them fatal to awkward, and flag which ones a committed defender should concede versus dismiss as question-begging.
