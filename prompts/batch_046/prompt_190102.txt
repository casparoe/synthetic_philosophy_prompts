Write a dialogue between two philosophers arguing over Avicenna's flying man. One thinks it's a proof that self-awareness is totally non-bodily and pre-reflective — you dont need senses or brain states, just the bare fact of being. The other thinks Avicenna accidentally sneaks in embodied awareness: even a falling person has vestibular feelings, and the "flying" setup is a red herring because sensory deprivation isnt total absence. They should actually respond to each other — the first should answer the vestibular objection, the second should push back on that answer. Dont make either side a strawman. End with them stuck on whether a patient under total anesthesia who later reports "I was still there" has genuine Avicennan self-awareness or just a reconstructed memory. Keep it focused; dont wander off into random epistemology.
