I am convening the final examination for an upper-level undergraduate seminar entitled “Loops, Levels and Limits: Formal Structures in Philosophy and Art”. Over the term we have examined Douglas Hofstadter’s distinction between untangled hierarchies—systems that rest upon an inviolate base level which does not refer back to itself—and tangled hierarchies or “strange loops”, in which representational levels interpenetrate so that no unambiguous ground can be identified. We have used M. C. Escher’s Drawing Hands as the central visual analogue: each hand appears to draw the other into existence, producing a circular dependency with no clear “lower” level that generates the “higher” one. I want a single essay question that prevents students from merely reciting this framework and instead forces them to apply it to a contested issue in contemporary epistemology.

Please write the examination question, together with a model answer of approximately 1,000 words and a detailed marking scheme. The question should require students first to explain Hofstadter’s concept of the inviolate level and to interpret Drawing Hands as an illustration of its absence, being careful to distinguish logical or representational dependence from causal or temporal sequence. It should then ask them to evaluate whether the structure of perceptual justification can be understood as a tangled hierarchy: specifically, they must consider whether a coherentist or reliabilist account permits a “strange loop” in which higher-order theoretical commitments and lower-order observational reports mutually support one another without collapsing into vicious circularity, and whether such a loop eliminates the need for an “inviolate level” of basic beliefs analogous to Hofstadter’s ground floor. The essay must address at least one serious objection—such as the claim that epistemic circularity is vicious in a way that formal strange loops are not, or that abandoning an inviolate cognitive base necessarily entails scepticism about the external world.

The examination is ninety minutes long; this essay carries forty marks and should be answered in roughly 1,200 words. The marking scheme should separate an A-range response (approximately 32–40 marks) from a B-range response (approximately 24–31 marks) by naming the specific conceptual moves required: for example, a precise characterisation of the inviolate level; a correct reading of Drawing Hands that does not confuse representational circularity with physical impossibility; an explicit mapping onto the foundationalism versus coherentism/reliabilism debate; and a critical evaluation rather than a descriptive summary. It should also list the particular errors that ought to cost marks—such as conflating Hofstadter’s logical levels with psychological processing stages, treating the absence of an inviolate level as automatically licensing scepticism without argument, or misrepresenting epistemic circularity as mere logical self-reference. Please do not include any request for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and frame the task so that it can be answered fully and meaningfully without access to external sources.
