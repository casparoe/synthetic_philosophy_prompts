Here is my opening move. I have been working out an idea I call moral density: the claim that moral demands do not merely compete with other activities for time and energy, but actually restructure the quality of attention one can bring to anything not immediately moral. If every mental resource is, in principle, available to be redirected by another’s suffering, then the sustained, non-instrumental attention that makes a friendship, a piece of music, or a quiet walk genuinely meaningful is not just crowded out—it is structurally impossible. From this I want to argue that maximal moral goodness is undesirable, because it dissolves the very conditions for the experiences that make life worth living. I challenge you either to show that moral density is a false description of moral attention, or to demonstrate that a life lived at that density can still preserve those conditions.
