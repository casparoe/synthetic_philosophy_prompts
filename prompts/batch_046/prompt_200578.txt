I'm preparing a unit for a graduate seminar on agency and determinism, and I want to move past the usual one-line invocation of the digger wasp—where Sphex ichneumoneus drags prey to a burrow, checks the interior, and then, if an experimenter has moved the prey, repeats the check-and-drag loop rather than adjusting—to a more granular diagnostic tool. Please construct a taxonomy, formatted as a decision tree with clearly labeled branch nodes, that sorts positions on whether mechanical rigidity of this kind undermines free will. The tree should begin with concrete behavioral distinctions rather than abstract metaphysical commitments: first, ask whether the rigidity lies in the initiation conditions (the fixed trigger that launches the sequence) or in the execution loop (the inability to skip, reorder, or abort intermediate steps once the routine is running). Second, ask whether the agent retains any minimal representational capacity—however causally inert in behavior—to register that the environment has diverged from the expected state. Third, split on whether free will requires the actual exercise of flexible revision in every relevant instance, or merely the possession of a general capacity for reason-responsive control that could, in suitable conditions, be activated. I need the leaves to include at least: a hard determinist who treats all fixed action patterns as disqualifying for agency; a compatibilist who distinguishes trigger-rigidity from execution-rigidity and allows that only the former is fatal; a minimal-agency view that grants agency solely on the basis of representational capacity even when behavioral flexibility is entirely absent; a revisionist position arguing that apparent sphexishness is actually misdiagnosed context-sensitivity—what looks like mechanical repetition is an adaptive response to a hidden environmental variable that human observers have failed to control; and any genuinely unoccupied combinations in the logical space, each with a one-sentence diagnosis of whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. At every node, phrase the branching question so that a student could answer it based on their intuitions about the case without already knowing which philosophical label awaits them. Finally, flag explicitly any pair of leaves that the tree cannot cleanly separate—particularly whether the compatibilist and the minimal-agency view collapse once representational capacity is granted—and note what further distinction, if any, would be needed to pull them apart.
