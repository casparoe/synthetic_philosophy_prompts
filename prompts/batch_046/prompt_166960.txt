Examination question: Explain the Frege-Geach problem as it arises specifically for moral expressivism. Your response should (i) clarify why the difficulty concerns embedded occurrences of moral sentences inside conditionals, disjunctions, or other logical constructions rather than unembedded assertions standing alone; (ii) present the problem through the standard logical form of modus ponens, using one concrete moral example with explicit premises and conclusion; and (iii) distinguish Frege's original contribution regarding sense, reference, and indirect discourse from Geach's later deployment of that framework to press a challenge against expressivist accounts of meaning.

Student answer: Frege's distinction between sense and reference demonstrates that in indirect discourse a clause refers to its usual sense rather than to its usual reference or truth-value. Geach applied this semantic insight directly to moral expressivism by showing that in an inference such as "If lying is wrong, then persuading my brother to lie is wrong; lying is wrong; therefore persuading my brother to lie is wrong," the antecedent embeds "lying is wrong" inside a conditional. For the expressivist, an unembedded moral sentence expresses a non-cognitive attitude—disapproval—rather than a thought with truth-conditions. Because of this, in the embedded position the clause cannot supply the propositional content required for the conditional premise to be true, so it lacks the semantic value necessary to combine with the second premise through modus ponens. Since Frege's framework requires embedded clauses to contribute thoughts capable of entering logical relations, and since expressivists deny that moral sentences ever express such thoughts, the expressivist cannot adopt Frege's solution without abandoning the core claim that moral language is non-descriptive. Consequently, the only consistent option is for the expressivist to concede that moral sentences do possess propositional contents with truth-values, which means that expressivism collapses into moral cognitivism.
