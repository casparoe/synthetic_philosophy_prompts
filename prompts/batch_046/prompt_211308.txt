ok me and my friend got into it last night about glaucons ring of gyges and whether tolkiens one ring is the same thought experiment but broken. read lord of the rings through that lens: glaucns challenge is why be moral if you can get away with anything, and the ring makes you invisible but also corrupts you in ways that depend on who you are -- frodo breaks, boromir snaps, gollum decays over centuries. does that mean moral character isnt just a costume you take off when no ones watching, but actually shapes what you want to do with power? or does tolkien just prove glaucns point that deep down everyones selfish, since even "good" people get tempted? i want you to take specific scenes seriously -- boromir trying to take it from frodo near the lake, frodo failing at the crack of doom, the nazgul seeing wearers who should be invisible -- and ask whether the books actually answer glaucons challenge or dodge it by making invisibility imperfect and corruption inevitable. also: does the fact that the ring amplifies existing desires rather than creating new ones mess up the clean conditions of the thought experiment? dont just say "catholic author believes in good" -- get in the weeds about whether the novel shows that morality survives because people are different, or collapses because theyre all the same underneath. what does the work add that the philosophy papers miss?
