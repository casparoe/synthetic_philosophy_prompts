I'm on the IACUC at my university and we have a vote thursday on a weird proposal. A neuroscience PI wants to breed transgenic mice with selectively enlarged forebrains — about 40% more cortical volume relative to body size — for an Alzheimer's study. The argument is that higher neuronal density makes them "better models," but half the board thinks brain size within a species is morally irrelevant: welfare thresholds shouldn't depend on neuron count or some invented "cognitive density index" the PI is using that weights neurons by synaptic pruning rate. The other half thinks deliberately engineering animals closer to the cognitive boundary where we grant special status (apes, cetaceans) means we should apply stricter enrichment and euthenasia rules, or maybe reject the line if the index crosses a cutoff.

We need a memo for circulation before the meeting. Please draft it reccomending one concrete course — approve with enhanced safeguards, approve with conditions, or reject — with a clear time frame for implementation if approved. Engage the strongest philosophical argument on the losing side seriously: does engineered brain enlargement change moral status, and can an ad-hoc index like this do ethical work? Don't just restate federal animal regs; deal with the underlying issue.

Keep it tight: max 350 words, bottom-line recommendation in the first paragraph.
