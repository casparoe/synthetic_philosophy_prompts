I've been reading a bunch of sociology papers for a course I'm taking (not my main field, I'm more into philosophy of mind actually) and I keep running into the same weird move that I can't find a word for. Someone wants to explain why a specific factory town collapsed in the 80s. First they say it was because thousands of workers individually decided to move away for better wages — pure methodological individualism, right? But then they also say the town collapsed because of 'deindustrialization pressures' or 'structural capital flight,' which sounds like a macro-level cause that operates independant of the individuals. The strange part is that when you ask whether the structural factor is just the sum of all those individual moves, they say no, it's an independant force. But then when you ask how that independant force actually works, they point right back to the individual moves — landlords selling, banks pulling loans, workers leaving. So the macro explains the micro, and the micro explains the macro, and neither side will let the other do the full job. It's not quite emergence, because emergence usually means the macro has properties the micro lacks. Here the macro and micro are explaining the exact same outcome with the exact same mechanisms, just wearing different hats.

Is there an established name for this specific kind of circular explanatory loop between individualist and holist accounts? I know about methodological individualism and holism as general positions, and I've heard of supervenience and downward causation, but those don't quite capture the feeling that the same causal story is being told twice at two levels without anyone admitting the redundancy. If there's no term, could you coin one and tell me exactly where its edges are — like, how is it different from ordinary multi-level explanation where different levels genuinely add different information, and where does it cross over into just being sloppy reasoning?
