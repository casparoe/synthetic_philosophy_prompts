I am a doctoral student in political philosophy and the junior academic member of the Joint Parliamentary Committee on Constitutional Convention. We must issue a formal recommendation on whether to codify the convention that the Crown never withholds royal assent to bills passed by both Houses. The present case involves the proposed Heritage Land Stewardship Bill, which would compulsorily acquire portions of the royal private estates for public ecological trusts. The Palace has signalled that it may invoke its residual reserve power to deny assent, claiming that direct expropriation of Crown property breaches an implicit constitutional settlement. Our three options are: (i) reaffirm the absolute convention and advise assent; (ii) recognise a narrow, justiciable exception for legislation that directly alters Crown property rights; or (iii) establish a statutory mechanism transferring assent authority in such conflicts to a non-partisan Privy Council. Please draft the Committee's decision memo, recommending one course with clear reasoning, engaging seriously with the democratic-egalitarian objection that hereditary office should possess no legislative veto, and giving honest weight to the counter-argument that this reserve power functions as a non-majoritarian safeguard. The memo should be suitable for circulation to members before Thursday's sitting, and should be written in British English.
