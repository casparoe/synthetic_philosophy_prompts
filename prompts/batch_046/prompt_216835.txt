I have been arguing with friends about whether tranquilism—the view that only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic value, while all positive states are at best instrumentally useful—can be isolated in a realistic scenario without falling back on the familiar wireheading or ascetic-monk examples that every introductory discussion already exhausts. I am working through this for a course on theories of well-being, and I want a case engineered from scratch that a model can evaluate without searching for obscure literature.

Construct a one-paragraph, realistic thought experiment set in an institutional context—such as the design of a long-term care protocol, a workplace restructuring, or an educational track—rather than in science fiction or trolley-style contrivance. The case must satisfy these jointly constraining specifications: first, there must be two stable, fully realizable outcomes, A and B, both of which eliminate all suffering and all craving for every affected agent; second, outcome A must contain an additional structural feature—call it “directed openness,” defined as the sustained presence of non-painful, non-craving capacities for engagement with possibilities that remain permanently unactualized—while outcome B contains only the bare absence of negative states with no such residual structure; third, tranquilism must assess A and B as equally good (or B as marginally preferable if directed openness involves any hidden maintenance cost), whereas a rival “positive flourishing” view must assess A as clearly superior because directed openness is a genuine intrinsic good; fourth, the divergence must arise from the theories themselves rather than being stipulated by the framing.

After presenting the scenario, walk through the demonstration that it meets each specification: state precisely what tranquilism and the flourishing view each say about A versus B, identify the method or feature generating the divergence, specify the relevant nearby worlds in which directed openness is present or absent, and explain why each condition delivers the verdict it does. Then isolate the feature doing the work—whether it is the presence of unactualized potential, the absence of motivational residue, or another element you have named—explain what happens if that feature is removed or neutralized, and provide the honest attack that a defender of the view that comes out poorly would use to show the case cheats by smuggling in assumptions. Finally, say explicitly what argument or consideration would change your mind about whether the scenario genuinely distinguishes the two theories rather than merely begging the question, and flag the point in your reasoning where failure is most likely. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading.
