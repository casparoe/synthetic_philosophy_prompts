I hold that Jain anekāntavāda is best reconstructed not as a metaphysical claim about reality having infinitely many sides, but as an operational epistemic policy for reasoning under severe model uncertainty. Specifically: when my credence in a binary claim exceeds about 0.85, yet my best model of the domain contains at least three structurally distinct explanatory frameworks—each accounting for more than 20% of the observed predictive variance and none reducible to the others without a sharp loss in explanatory power—then rational belief should be represented as a vector of partial commitments across the relevant naya (standpoints) rather than collapsed to true or false. The policy is meant to prevent premature binary closure in domains like AI alignment forecasting, macro-historical dynamics, and moral psychology, where single-framework overconfidence is common, and it is intended to formalize the spirit of syādvāda without requiring any metaphysical commitment to a literally multi-aspect reality. What would actually count against this? I want the arguments and observations that should move me, sorted by decisiveness: formal results showing that multi-vector belief representations collapse into standard Bayesian updating under plausible priors; empirical cases where maintaining partial commitments across standpoints produced worse out-of-sample predictions than committing to the best single framework; practical failures where the policy merely licenses motivated reasoning or strategic ambiguity; and an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—for example, if any persistent disagreement can always be reinterpreted as just another naya—along with whether that insulation is a defect for an epistemic policy of this kind or a feature. Please distinguish evidence that would be genuinely decisive from evidence that would merely be awkward, include what a consistent defender of the view should concede as damaging rather than only what critics allege, and say whether there is any risk the position has become well-insulated in a way that undermines its value.
