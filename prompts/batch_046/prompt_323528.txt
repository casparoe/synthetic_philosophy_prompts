Please draft a 90-minute PhD comprehensive exam essay question in moral philosophy—aimed at testing whether candidates can move beyond summarizing standard internalist and externalist positions about practical reasons—together with a model answer and a marking scheme. The question should be written in American English, should not ask for bibliographies or citations, and should inject a somewhat niche conceptual framing that prevents mere regurgitation of familiar textbook summaries.

Specifically, the prompt should present the following problem. The internalist holds that an agent has a practical reason to φ only if there is a sound deliberative route from the agent’s existing motivational set to being motivated to φ. Critics observe that this seems to exclude moral reasons—such as reasons to prevent suffering or to honor commitments—that apply regardless of current motivations. A familiar internalist reply appeals to rational transformation: through reflection, imagination, or exposure to narrative testimony, an agent can come to be motivated by considerations she previously ignored. The objection then arises that either (a) such transformation is rational only if the agent is already motivated to undergo it, in which case no genuinely new reason is acquired, or (b) the transformation is guided by norms the agent does not yet accept, in which case the internalist has tacitly smuggled in external reasons.

The exam question should ask the candidate not merely to summarize this dialectic but to determine whether it presents a false dilemma. In particular, the candidate should analyze whether an internalist can consistently maintain three distinctions: (i) reasons that are currently unmotivated but potentially internal; (ii) a transition from unmotivated to motivated that is rationally guided without presupposing external norms; and (iii) a meaningful difference between reasons that are genuinely external and reasons that are merely not-yet-internal. The candidate should apply this analysis to one concrete case—such as an agent who initially lacks motivation to assist strangers in crisis but, through exposure to narrative testimony, comes to see such assistance as required.

The deliverable should include:
- The exam question itself, specifying the level (PhD comprehensive), time limit (90 minutes), and approximate length (around 2,000 words).
- A model answer that makes the required conceptual moves and applies them to the concrete case.
- A marking scheme built around exactly five must-hit points, with marks assigned to each (totaling 20), and a one-line note for each point describing how candidates typically fail on it.

The difficulty should come from identifying the crux—whether rational guidance of transformation can be internalist without circularity—rather than from recalling labels. The model answer should be substantive enough that a candidate who merely summarizes Bernard Williams’s internalism or lists types of reasons would earn only partial credit.
