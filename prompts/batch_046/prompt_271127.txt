I am a retired physicist who has only recently begun systematic study of philosophy, and I am designing a diagnostic discussion guide for a small reading group on Zhuangzi's attitude toward death. I want a taxonomy in the form of a decision tree—built from the logical space of commitments rather than from a list of scholars—so that a participant can locate the view they actually hold by answering concrete case-questions, without first knowing the interpretive labels.

Please construct the tree with the following architecture. The root should split on whether death involves the dissolution of a discrete personal self or the transformation of a continuous substrate. From that root, branch by whether the transformation should be understood analogously to a physical conservation law (the substrate is preserved in altered form) or to a qualitative phase transition (the prior form is irrecoverably lost, though not annihilated). Next, split by the normative stance this ontology licenses: acceptance, celebration, indifference, or refusal to evaluate altogether. Then split by the interpretation of the butterfly dream: does it dissolve the boundary between life and death epistemically (we cannot determine which state we presently inhabit) or ontologically (there is no substantive distinction between the two states)?

At each split, label the branch with a concrete claim or case-response on which positions genuinely diverge—for example, whether mourning a dead friend is appropriate, or whether awakening from the dream proves that one was never genuinely the butterfly. The tree should contain exactly six leaves. Each leaf should be marked either with a standard, widely recognized interpretive name (if one exists without reliance on obscure citation) or as unnamed, followed by a single sentence stating its most awkward commitment—the intuition it must deny or the counterintuitive consequence it must embrace.

In addition, identify one combination of answers that the tree's logic permits but that no leaf occupies, and briefly diagnose the vacancy: is the combination incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored? Finally, flag any pair of leaves that your tree fails to separate, explaining the shared commitment that collapses them onto the same position.

Write in formal prose appropriate for a discussion guide. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or reading lists. Do not invoke specific modern interpreters whose names are not broadly familiar. Avoid verbatim quotations from the Zhuangzi text; if you refer to the butterfly dream or to the great transformation, paraphrase them without quotation marks. Ensure the entire instrument can be used by someone with no access to external sources.
