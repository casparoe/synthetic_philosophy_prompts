me and my friend got in a fight about that color-phi thing where a dot jumps across the screen and changes color but your brain fills in smooth motion. i said its a classic Orwellian revision in Dennetts sense -- you first experience dot A, then dot B, then your brain goes back and edits the memory so you think you saw continuous movement with the color switch happening in the middle. the first draft gets rewritten after the fact.

she said thats Stalinesque, not Orwellian. the brain never gives you discrete snapshots to begin with; it just constructs the unified trajectory with the color change baked in from the start, like a propaganda film edited before release. so theres no original record being corrected -- the false continuous story is the only version that ever existed at the personal level.

then i hit her with the timing problem. if its Orwellian, the actual experience of seeing green should be locked to the moment the second dot appears. but we feel like the color changed midway, before the second dot even arrived. that retroactive assignment proves the brain is revising a prior experience, right? she said no, thats exactly what predictive coding predicts: the brain generates the best hypothesis in real time, assigning the color change to the intermediate location because thats where the model predicts it belongs. calling it revision assumes theres a neutral first draft, which is begging the question. she also said the Orwellian view requires mental time travel -- a later event literally altering what it was like to be you 200ms earlier -- which sounds metaphysically nuts.

i pushed back with the feeling-tricked objection. if theres only ever one constructed narrative, why do people report feeling like their memory was tampered with when they learn about phi? shouldnt they just accept the story? she said thats just a meta-cognitive mismatch between expectation and report, not proof of an unrevised draft sitting in storage.
