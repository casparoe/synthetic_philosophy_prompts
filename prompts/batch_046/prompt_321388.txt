Apply it to a couple who choose not to procreate because they anticipate severe ecological suffering over the next century. In your answer, address whether a 'potentialist' view — that merely possible people have derivative moral interests — affects the argument.

Student answer: Benatar's asymmetry holds that avoiding suffering for a non-existent person is good, but failing to provide pleasure to a non-existent person is not bad. This is becuase there's no subject to experience the missing pleasure, whereas suffering is still a harm that is avoided. So if a couple expects that climate collapse will cause serious harm to any child they might have, not procreating prevents that harm. Benatar also thinks human lives are mostly net negative, which is why he supports human extinction. The non-identity problem backs this up: since potential people never exist, they can't be wronged by not being born. Even if we take a potentialist view and say possible persons have interests, the asymmetry still holds because any interest in avoiding pain outweighs any interest in gaining pleasure, since pleasure needs an actual subject but pain doesn't.
