ok so in my philosophy of mind reading group we've been stuck on self-deception for like months. the classic setup is: can you literally believe p and also believe not-p at the same time, knowing both are yours? Davidson says you need a split mind, Mele says motivated reasoning can do it without a literal split, and then theres the deflationists who say its just lying to others or being in denial, not a real contradictory belief state. everyone agrees on the examples—like the spouse who ignores the obvious affair signs or the smoker who 'knows' the stats but doesnt really believe they'll get cancer. but nobody moves.

my hunch is that the stall isnt about psychology at all but about what we mean by 'the self' in 'self'-deception. like, Davidson's split seems to need a synchronic partition—two mental subsystems at the same time—but Mele's account lets the belief and the counter-belief live in different contexts or time-slices, which makes it diachronic. and the deflationists are basically saying there's only one self-state and its just not updating properly. so are they actually disagreeing about personal identity conditions—synchronic vs diachronic—while pretending its about belief formation?

or is there a hidden empirical thing, like whether working memory can actually host contradictory representations without collapse? and what would even resolve this—some neuroscience finding, or just agreeing that 'belief' is too coarse and we need something like 'epistemic escrow' where you hold a proposition in a kind of suspended register? I want the diagnosis, not another taxonomy. tell me if the disagreement is verbal, about mental architecture, or bedrock intuition about agency, and whether anything—conceptual or empirical—could actually unstick it.
