How does she distinguish her position both from consequentialist justifications of the bombings and from pacifism? Furthermore, address whether her argument turns on a standard Thomistic formulation of the doctrine of double effect or on a more direct prohibition on murder, and explain why that distinction matters for her assessment of Truman's decision.

Student Answer:

Anscombe's essay opposing Truman's honorary doctorate at Oxford argues that the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki constituted murder. She defines murder as the intentional killing of innocent people, and she holds that this is absolutely prohibited regardless of consequences. Consequently, she rejects the consequentialist defense that the bombings were justified because they shortened the war and saved American and Japanese lives overall. At the same time, she is not a pacifist: she accepts that killing enemy combatants in a just war is permissible, and she distinguishes her position from pacifism on exactly these grounds. Her argument relies on the doctrine of double effect in its standard Thomistic form, which allows that harm to innocents is permissible only when it is genuinely unintended and proportionate to the good achieved. In the case of Hiroshima, the civilian deaths were not an unintended side-effect of attacking military targets but were themselves the mechanism intended to break Japanese morale; therefore the conditions of the doctrine are violated. Moreover, Anscombe understands intention in terms of the agent's explicit mental aim: had Truman not consciously willed the death of civilians, but merely accepted them as unavoidable collateral damage, the act might not have counted as murder in her view. Because Truman did direct the killing, he is guilty of murder. From this moral conclusion it follows necessarily that Oxford's decision to honor him is not merely imprudent but logically incoherent: an institution committed to justice and truth cannot celebrate a murderer without contradicting its own foundational principles. Thus her objection combines ethical analysis with a logical demonstration of institutional hypocrisy.
