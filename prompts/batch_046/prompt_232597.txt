Design a realistic, one-paragraph thought experiment set in a laboratory or field-science context—no exotic physics, no monetary lotteries—in which a senior researcher insists on maintaining an aging, expensive instrument rather than adopting a technically superior replacement, where the sole driving reason is the years of prior calibration and baseline measurements already sunk into the old apparatus, and where this persistence is not immediately dismissible as irrational. The case must isolate a single structural feature: the sunk expenditure is simultaneously the only carrier of an irreplaceable interpretive bridge that makes the historical dataset continuously readable, so that abandoning the instrument severs that continuity in a way a fresh start cannot repair. Reason entirely from conceptual structure; do not supply bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
