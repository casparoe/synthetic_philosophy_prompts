Write a short story in British English about a clinical xenobiologist and a philosopher of biology examining a synthetic organism constructed de novo with no evolutionary lineage. Its central pumping organ was engineered to filter metabolic waste, yet now circulates nutrients as an emergent side-effect. The philosopher maintains that, lacking any selected-for function, describing the organ as malfunctioning commits an etiological error; the biologist counters that its present causal-role in sustaining homeostasis is sufficient for a dysfunction diagnosis. Show, through their interaction over a proposed surgical correction, how this conceptual disagreement shapes practical judgement without either character delivering an explicit lecture. Keep the philosophical tension embedded in concrete detail—measurements, tissue samples, the creature's behaviour—rather than in abstract dialogue.
