After a long and rather heated argument with a friend last night about whether a nation can be guilty for harms it hasn't directly committed, I found myself returning to Jaspers's fourfold distinction from The Question of German Guilt—criminal, political, moral, and metaphysical guilt—but applied to an issue he never lived to see: intergenerational climate responsibility. Specifically, my friend insisted that present citizens of high-emission societies cannot bear any form of collective guilt for climate damages that will fall on future generations, since they never personally consented to the carbon-intensive institutions they inherited. I countered that Jaspers's category of metaphysical guilt—shared responsibility among those who remain alive and benefit rather than resisting—might extend across time in ways he did not consider. But I am not sure whether Jaspers would allow metaphysical guilt to bridge generations that never coexisted, or whether he would restrict it to contemporaries within a shared political life.

So: imagine Karl Jaspers, having read the climate science and the economics of carbon lock-in that emerged after his death, is asked whether present generations of wealthy nations bear metaphysical guilt toward future generations for institutionalized emissions they did not individually authorize. How do you think he would answer? In your response, use his four categories precisely—criminal guilt for direct acts, political guilt for institutional acquiescence, moral guilt for private self-examination, and metaphysical guilt for universal solidarity—to determine which, if any, apply across a temporal gap where the victims do not yet exist and the perpetrators have not chosen to remain silent in their presence. Address specifically whether inherited structural benefit without personal consent counts as the kind of remaining alive rather than dying in protest that metaphysical guilt requires, or whether the absence of co-presence dissolves even that universal bond. Also consider whether political guilt attaches to citizens of democratic states that continue to vote within carbon-intensive systems, even when the harmful consequences are deferred to people who have no representation now. I want to see if his framework collapses under temporal displacement, or if it reveals a hidden consistency in holding the living responsible for the conditions of survival they pass on.
