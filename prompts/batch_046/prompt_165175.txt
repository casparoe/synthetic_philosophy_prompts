I am preparing a philosophical profile for a small reading group on personal identity and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is my current set of commitments. First, I take the self to be a center of narrative gravity in the Dennettian sense: an abstract point in the space of stories rather than a metaphysical substance, though one with real causal and explanatory weight as an attractor that organizes memory, behavior, and social interaction. Second, I deny that there is any hidden fact about diachronic identity beyond the coherence and continuity of this narrative thread; in a split-brain scenario where both resulting streams exhibit equal narrative coherence, there is simply no further truth about which one is genuinely me. Third, I hold that phenomenal consciousness involves a first-personal givenness that outstrips any third-personal narrative structure—there is something it is like to be this center, and this qualitative dimension is not reducible to the story being told about it. Fourth, I believe moral agency and responsibility depend on being embedded in a narrative capable of representing intentions and reasons, but they do not require an underlying metaphysical ego. Fifth, I think that if a person's autobiographical narrative were gradually replaced module by module with synthetic memories and synthetic intentions—preserving relational coherence and causal links throughout—there would be no discrete point at which the original self dropped out; continuity is purely structural. Which pairs of these commitments pull against each other and with what severity? For each genuine tension, indicate what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Also identify any tensions that are only apparent and dissolve on closer inspection. Finally, tell me which single revision would eliminate the greatest number of conflicts.
