I'm trying to stabilize my position on Davidsonian interpretation before drafting a paper, and I want an internal consistency check on the following package. Here is where I stand:

(1) I accept the principle of charity in its strong, constitutive form: attributing to an interpreted subject a largely true and rational belief system is not a pragmatic heuristic but a necessary condition for counting that subject as having beliefs at all.

(2) I reject any sharp scheme-content distinction and deny that genuinely incommensurable conceptual schemes are metaphysically possible; apparent failures of translation are always limitations in our interpretive practice, not boundaries between separate conceptual worlds.

(3) I think radical interpretation is not restricted to linguistic behavior. Any being that participates in triangulation—mutual causal interaction with a shared environment mediated by another interpreter—can in principle be interpreted as holding beliefs, which includes some non-human animals and embodied artificial agents that causally interact with the world and with interpreters.

(4) I hold that charity is domain-sensitive in a specific way: in empirical and descriptive interpretation we should maximize agreement, but in moral interpretation we are permitted, and sometimes required, to attribute false moral beliefs rather than charitably reconstructing the agent's normative framework to preserve overall rationality.

(5) I endorse Davidson's anti-representationalist externalism: the content of belief is fixed holistically by causal relations to the world and by patterns of interpretive agreement, with no mediating layer of private mental representations or internal "ideas."

(6) I maintain that determinately contentful propositional attitudes depend on linguistic practice: without participation in a shared language, a being possesses only behavioral dispositions or proto-intentional states, not beliefs that can be determinately true or false.

My question is whether this set hangs together. Please audit these commitments: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, specify exactly what has to give and what the cheapest revision would be. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if revised or dropped, would clean up the most problems overall. Do not treat all conflicts as equally serious; I want to know where the real fault lines are and where I am helping myself to incompatible motivations.
