Here is my current package on meaning in life, and I want a consistency audit before I present it in a seminar. I hold: (1) Camus is right that the absurd is structural—our demand for cosmic justification meets the universe's silence—without this entailing nihilism; (2) a hybrid terrestrial account is correct, meaning a life is meaningful to the degree the agent is actively engaged in projects with objective value, where that value is not reducible to subjective satisfaction; (3) recognizing cosmic absurdity should intensify, not weaken, investment in terrestrial projects, since they are the only arena in which value can be realized; (4) heteronomous purpose—divine, evolutionary, or conventional—cannot contribute to meaning, because meaning requires authorship that external assignment undermines; (5) absent cosmic justification, there is no all-things-considered rational requirement to continue living rather than exit, except for contingent obligations to others that are themselves groundless; (6) the right response is not heroic rebellion but "ironic earnestness": full practical commitment coupled with sustained meta-cognitive awareness that the commitment lacks ultimate grounding; and (7) reflective equilibrium is unreliable here because our intuitions about cosmic significance are evolutionary byproducts, yet I still rely on it to adjudicate conflicts among terrestrial values.

Check whether this hangs together. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severely? For each real tension, specify what has to give and what the cheapest revision is. In particular: does (3) depend on a reason that (5) denies? Does (4)'s authorship condition undercut (2)'s appeal to mind-independent value? Does accepting (7) collapse once (4) rules out evolutionary sources? Rank the tensions by severity, identify the single revision that would clean up the most conflicts, and be frank about whether (1) and (2) actually conflict or dissolve on inspection. Answer in American English, and do not include bibliographies or reading suggestions.
