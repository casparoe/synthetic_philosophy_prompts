okay so my study group cooked up this argument about grounding and fundamentality and it feels like it proves there's literally no bottom layer to reality, but the conclusion is obviously nuts. i want to keep the idea that grounding is irreflexive and well-founded if i can, but i'm not sure where the slip is.

(1) If X grounds Y, then Y is ontologically dependANT on X and not more fundamental than X.
(2) Grounding is irreflexive and well-founded: no fact grounds itself, and every chain of grounding terminates in something fundamental.
(3) Call F the totality of all fundamental facts/entities.
(4) F is a totality, and totalities depend on their members in at least a weak ontological sense.
(5) So F is grounded in its members.
(6) Every member of F is fundamental, hence ungrounded. So F depends on things that depend on nothing.
(7) But then F's members are in F, which means F depends on itself via its members.
(8) That violates irreflexivity from (2).
(9) So F cannot exist.
(10) If there's no totality of fundamental things, then every fact is outside F, hence non-fundamental.
(11) So every grounding chain is infinite or circular, contradicting well-foundedness.
(12) Therefore the concept of fundamentality is incoherent.

where does this actually break? i feel like (4) or (7) is doing something shady with what 'depends on' means, but i can't name it. which premise should i reject, why did it look okay, and what's the cost? i'd rather save (1) and (2) if possible, but tell me what each rejection buys and costs. also is there a standard name for the mistake in treating F like it's just another fact in the domain?
