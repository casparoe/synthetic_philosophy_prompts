I am a novelist whose recent work imagines a mayoral election in a declining rust-belt town decided by three votes, and I have been invited to deliver the opening address at a symposium convened jointly by a university department of political philosophy and a regional coalition of election workers. The audience is evenly divided: on one side, scholars who have spent decades debating instrumental versus expressive accounts of voting; on the other, organizers who have registered voters in precisely the kind of county my novel depicts, and who have little patience for abstraction that ignores structural corruption. I want the address—approximately twenty minutes—to build its entire architecture around one scene from the book, in which a protagonist casts a ballot for a candidate she knows to be corrupt but less destructive than his opponent, then spends the narrative attempting to determine whether that act was an expression of her values, a coerced complicity, or something that fractures the conceptual frame of consent altogether. The philosophical problem of the lesser-evil vote must do real structural work: the speech should ask whether the ethics of voting collapses into a false binary between civic duty and personal expression, and whether an honest recognition of structurally bad options demands not a finer calculus but an entirely different vocabulary for what consent means under constrained choice. It must not comfort either half of the room. For the scholars, it should pose a genuine tension—whether expressive theories can survive cases where the available expressions are themselves manufactured—without relying on jargon that would alienate the organizers. For the organizers, it should honor the urgency of their practical work without turning that work into a philosophical ornament or implying that moral purity is available to them. The address should end not with resolution but with an open, uncomfortable question: if the duty to vote is real, can it survive an unflinching account of what that vote actually does to the voter who knows the frame is rigged? Please write this speech.
