I'm preparing a position paper on the Holocaust's place in comparative genocide studies and want a consistency audit of my commitments before I finalize the argument. Here is where I stand:

(1) The Holocaust is historically singular in that the Nazi genocide of European Jewry was pursued as a redemptive, non-instrumental end—total annihilation for its own sake—rather than as a means to territorial consolidation, labor extraction, or security, and this structural feature distinguishes it from colonial or security-driven mass killings.

(2) Bauman's modernity thesis is substantially correct: the Holocaust was enabled not by a regression to pre-modern barbarism but by specifically modern bureaucratic rationality—division of labor, technical efficiency, and moral distancing—without which its industrial scale would have been impossible.

(3) Comparative analysis with other atrocities—specifically the Herero and Namaqua genocide under German colonial administration, the Armenian genocide, and Soviet state violence—is epistemically necessary and morally legitimate, because identifying shared bureaucratic, racial-logical, or state-capacity elements illuminates the Holocaust's specificity by contrast rather than diluting it.

(4) Given that modern bureaucratic rationality remains structurally ubiquitous, Bauman's analysis implies that the conditions for large-scale, administratively mediated violence are not exceptional; at the same time, I maintain that the particular convergence of totalitarian state capacity, biological-racial ideology, and wartime conditions that produced the Holocaust was historically contingent and is not likely to recur in precisely the same configuration.

(5) The Historikerstreit of the 1980s should be understood not as a resolved historiographical dispute about method but as a politically motivated debate about German national identity, and its framing should not be permitted to police the boundaries of legitimate comparative historical inquiry.

Please audit these five commitments for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision for each genuine tension? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. Be specific about whether the tension lies between the motivations underlying (1) and (2), between (2) and (4), between (1) and (3), or elsewhere, and whether any commitment relies on a distinction—such as between instrumental means and non-instrumental ends, or between structural possibility and historical contingency—that collapses under pressure. I have not decided which commitment to abandon if forced; treat all five as live and do not presume I would drop the uniqueness claim or the modernity claim by default.
