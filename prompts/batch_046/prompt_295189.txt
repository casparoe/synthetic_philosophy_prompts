I'm preparing a consistency check for my philosophy of science seminar on induction and rational belief. Here is the package of commitments I've settled on, and I want to know whether they form a stable position or whether I'm implicitly trading on incompatible motivations.

(1) I take the formal no-free-lunch results for learning and optimization to carry an analog for inductive inference: averaged over all possible worlds or target functions, no inductive algorithm, prior, or projectibility criterion is universally optimal.

(2) I draw from (1) a qualified epistemic pluralism. I think the rational inductive strategy in any domain depends on substantive theoretical commitments about causal structure and on value-laden choices about which errors are tolerable—what is sometimes called inductive risk—rather than on a single canonical standard of rationality.

(3) I think Hume's problem of induction demonstrates that any defense of projecting observed regularities onto unobserved cases is irreducibly circular, but I regard this circularity as benign: it undermines foundationalist ambitions without undermining the rationality of induction itself.

(4) I think Bayesian updating provides the correct formal framework for modeling inductive reasoning, but I reject objective Bayesianism. There is no uniquely rational prior—no domain-independent principle of indifference or simplicity metric—that fixes belief across contexts; priors are constrained instead by our background theoretical commitments.

(5) I think Goodman's "grue" paradox is dissolved by appealing to the entrenchment of predicates in our linguistic and theoretical practice, but I also think that entrenchment is itself an inductive achievement. So the dissolution is circular in exactly Hume's sense, and I accept that circularity as benign rather than vicious.

(6) Despite the pluralism in (2), the circularity acknowledged in (3) and (5), and the rejection of a unique prior in (4), I maintain that particular inductive beliefs—such as my expectation that the sun will rise tomorrow—are objectively rationally justified. They are not merely instrumentally warranted, conventionally adopted, or relatively justified within a local framework.

Please audit these commitments. I want to know: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe each real tension is. For each tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs in terms of the rest of my view. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally problematic. Then tell me which single revision—dropping or weakening one commitment—would clean up the most conflicts overall.

Also, please say how confident you are in what you claim here, and single out the specific point about my commitments that you are least sure of.
