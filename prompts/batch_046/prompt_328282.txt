Next month I present a seminar paper to a mixed audience at an interdisciplinary conference on governance ethics. My thesis is that whether clientelism and bureaucratic gift-giving count as corruption should depend not on the presence of personal exchange within official relationships, but on whether the exchange generates an obligation that conflicts with the formal duties of the office—what I call “role-subverting reciprocity” versus “role-embedded reciprocity.” I argue that Western anti-corruption frameworks, especially those built into international transparency metrics, wrongly universalize a transactional, impersonal model of public office and thereby misclassify certain relational governance practices—such as long-standing municipal contracting traditions in parts of Scandinavia and administrative guidance networks in Japan—as corrupt when they may actually reinforce vertical accountability. I do not deny that bribery for private enrichment is corruption; my claim is that personalized exchange is not sufficient for it, and that applying Western legal categories universally risks both false positives and the erosion of locally legitimate accountability mechanisms.

The audience will include: two institutional economists who build corruption indices and treat standardized measurement as a prerequisite for cross-national reform; two anthropologists of bureaucracy who study gift economies and are already skeptical of Western normative imposition; and one comparative law scholar who specializes in international bribery conventions and believes universal legal standards are necessary for enforcement cooperation. They care about empirical measurability, cultural relativism, and legal interoperability, respectively.

Prepare me for the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased as each faction would phrase them. For each objection, tell me whether I should concede, hold firm, or partially concede, and identify the reply that must land for my argument to survive. Include the one objection I am hoping nobody raises—likely the one that attacks my distinction between role-subverting and role-embedded reciprocity as unfalsifiable or ideologically convenient. Point out the weakest point in my own position that I should defend rather than hope they miss. Finally, please indicate how confident you are in each part of your prep and explicitly single out the point about which you are least certain.
