I am preparing a philosophical profile for a graduate seminar on pragmatism and metaphysics, and over the past few weeks—partly through discussions with colleagues who work in philosophy of science and partly through my own attempt to apply Rortyan antirepresentationalism to debates about climate modeling—I have settled on a cluster of commitments that I fear may not cohere. I reject the mirror-of-nature picture: I do not think beliefs are mental representations that succeed or fail by corresponding to an independent reality, and I treat talk of truth as correspondence as a move within particular language games rather than a substantive philosophical relation. I accept that vocabularies are tools for coping, and that scientific progress is largely the replacement of one descriptive practice by another that proves more useful, not a march toward a pre-given true picture of nature. Nevertheless, I want to maintain that when a climate scientist claims that a particular feedback loop is accelerating warming, she is asserting something about the world that can be literally false in a robust sense—her error is not merely that her vocabulary has become socially unpopular or technologically obsolete, but that the world resists her description in a way that demands revision. I am also drawn to the thought that phenomenal experience supplies a kind of non-representational friction that shapes vocabulary change from the outside, constraining which descriptions survive, though I worry this smuggles back in exactly the representationalist notion of constraint Rorty seeks to eliminate.

Could you examine these commitments for consistency? I want to know which pairs pull against each other, which conflicts dissolve on closer inspection, how severe each tension is, and what the cheapest revision would be in each case—especially whether I can preserve both my antirepresentationalism and my intuition that scientific claims can be literally wrong about reality. Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate what must be given up for each repair, and identify the single revision that would resolve the most conflicts without rendering the overall position unrecognizable.
