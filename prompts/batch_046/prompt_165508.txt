For a graduate seminar on political philosophy, construct a decision tree that takes a student from the claim “liberty is essentially non-interference” through to one of six named positions. The tree should branch on four specific diagnostic questions: first, whether liberty requires only the absence of actual interference or also the absence of arbitrary power to interfere; second, whether structural conditions that shape choices without explicit commands—specifically, algorithmic recommendation architectures that render certain options invisible rather than forbidden—count as interference, domination, or neither; third, whether positive liberty requires merely the capacity to act on one’s authentic preferences or also the social conditions for self-mastery; and fourth, whether republican freedom is satisfied when citizens have democratic control over the rules, or only when they are not subject to anyone’s arbitrary will even with full consent. Each branch node should be phrased as a concrete institutional scenario rather than an abstract definition, so that a respondent can answer without already knowing the philosophical label. The six leaves should be: pure negative liberty; weak positive liberty (capacity for authentic action); strong positive liberty (social conditions for self-mastery); democratic republican liberty; non-domination republican liberty; and a hybrid “ecological liberty” view that treats the design of choice-environments itself as a primary domain of freedom, regardless of whether an identifiable agent interferes. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate—particularly whether the algorithmic-architecture branch collapses the distinction between republican domination and negative non-interference—and briefly explain what commitment separates them if they do diverge. Do not provide a bibliography or cite specific authors; build the taxonomy entirely from the logical space of the positions.
