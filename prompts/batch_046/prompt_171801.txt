As a science journalist writing on emerging governance, I need a reusable practitioner procedure — call it a convergence audit — for assessing whether any proposed global institution is structurally enabling a Bostrom-style singleton: defined here not as formal world government, but as the removal of competing veto centres alongside unified internal capacity to coordinate security and resource allocation. The audit must take the form of an ordered sequence of questions a research team can run in under an hour, specifying the answer thresholds that flag singleton-enabling architecture, the precise points where analysis stops and value judgement takes over, and an honest account of where the procedure will misfire. Apply it to three cases — a binding international AI safety board with enforcement powers; a voluntary opt-in carbon-credit coordination scheme; and a mandatory global biometric identity layer for health access, a proposal I distrust — with the biometric case producing a singleton-enabling verdict I will find unwelcome. Present everything as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: item one should lay out the ordered questions and thresholds; item two should mark the judgement stop-points and misfire risks; item three should apply the audit to the AI board; item four should cover both the carbon scheme and the biometric layer; and item five should state what the audit cannot determine.
