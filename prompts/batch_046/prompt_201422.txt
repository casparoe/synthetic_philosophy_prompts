I'm preparing a workshop proposal on reduction, emergence, and social ontology, and I would value your help sharpening the framing. My starting point is Dennett's distinction between cranes and skyhooks, together with his warning against greedy reductionism—the attempt to collapse a multi-layered phenomenon into its base in a single explanatory leap. Rather than applying this to consciousness, where the literature is well-trodden, I want to examine institutional reality: specifically, whether monetary or legal norms can be accounted for by what I call "scaffolded cranes", in which each institutional layer is constructed from prior layers without being ontologically eliminated, yet acquires downward causal constraints that greedy reductionism cannot capture. I suspect current debates in social ontology present a false exhaustion between Searle-style non-reductive institutionalism and eliminative naturalism, missing this intermediate architecture.

Could you write a concise proposal—approximately one page in scope—addressing the following: a precise statement of the research question; an explanation of why existing positions in both philosophy of mind and social ontology leave it open; a tractable method, perhaps conceptual analysis of a single case study (e.g., the ontological status of debt obligations); a clear description of what a successful result and what failure would look like; and an honest assessment of the limits for a workshop-length project. Please present this in British English, and do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, as I will supply those separately.
