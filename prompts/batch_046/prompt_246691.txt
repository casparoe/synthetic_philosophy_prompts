You are designing a reusable audit protocol for a regulatory body that reviews high-stakes predictive systems—clinical triage tools, credit-risk models, and hiring filters—on a fixed quarterly cycle. The auditors are technically literate but not machine-learning researchers; each audit receives eight hours, full input-output logs, and, where available, chain-of-thought or attention traces, but no ability to retrain models or inspect full parameter states. They must classify each system’s supervision regime as process-based, outcome-based, or hybrid, and document any dangerous divergence between the two.

Turn the philosophical distinction between process-based and outcome-based supervision into an operational decision procedure the auditors can apply repeatedly. Deliver it as a sequential checklist or flowchart in words, with ordered tests, explicit decision nodes, and clear stopping points where judgment is unavoidable and must be signed off. The procedure must handle three hard patterns: correct outcomes produced by reasoning traces that invoke prohibited proxies or spurious correlations; reasoning traces that appear normatively sound yet yield systematically biased outcomes for a protected subgroup; and opaque systems where traces are missing, compressed, or suspected to be post-hoc rationalizations.

As part of the procedure, inject and operationalize the distinction between process fidelity—whether an available trace genuinely represents the computation that produced the outcome—and process stability—whether the reasoning path shifts under counterfactual input perturbations that should not change the result. Use this to build a specific counterfactual log-test the auditor can run without retraining.

Provide worked applications to three systems: a transparent clinical model with readable traces, a black-box hiring filter with only outcome data, and a hybrid credit model where traces exist but are suspected to be post-hoc. Include one case where the procedure yields a conclusion the audit team will not want—such as recommending suspension of a system with excellent outcome metrics due to process-level failure, or approving an opaque system despite unobservable reasoning.

Specify which conceptual distinctions from the underlying philosophy must survive the simplification and which can be dropped for practical use. State openly what the procedure cannot do—for instance, whether it can detect misalignment that manifests neither in outcomes nor in any available trace—and flag the exact points where an auditor must exercise irreducible judgment rather than apply a rule.
