I've mostly bought into the Rietdijk-Putnam line that special relativity kills global presentism: the present is frame-dependant, so if every frame's present is ontologically equal we get a block universe where past, present and future all exist. But I want my students to actually hit it, not just repeat the move. So play it straight: what would count against this conclusion? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to awkward, including the standard conventionality reply but also the less-discussed stuff -- like whether the argument smuggles in a substantivalist metaphysics of spacetime points that a relationalist presentist could reject, or whether denying transitivity of the real-for relation (A can be real for B and B for C without A being real for C) breaks the inference without touching SR. Also, have block-universe defenders qualified the view into unfalsifiability by allowing local or causal presentness? If so, is that a problem for a metaphysical claim? Say what a committed defender would concede versus dismiss. Write in American English, keep it casual, and please no citations or reading lists -- I just want an honest exposure map.
