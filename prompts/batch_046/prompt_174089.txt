I've been working through debates on the species problem, and I keep noticing a particular pattern of theoretical decay for which I lack a precise name. A philosopher or biologist proposes a sharp species criterion—reproductive isolation, a fixed divergence threshold for monophyletic groups, or exclusive ecological niche occupancy. Counterexamples soon appear: hybrid zones that remain stable without collapsing into a single gene pool, ring species in which neighboring populations interbreed but the terminal ends do not, obligate asexual lineages that reproduce clonally, and cryptic species that are morphologically identical yet genetically distant. Rather than abandoning the criterion, defenders append individually reasonable exception clauses—"except in persistent hybrid zones below a specified gene-flow rate," "except for circular geographic arrangements," "except for parthenogenetic groups." Each addition is locally justified with empirical detail. Cumulatively, however, they transform the original criterion from a predictive decision procedure into a post-hoc summary of the groupings people already wanted to preserve. The patched rule now determines nothing that the accumulated examples do not already fix; yet it is still presented as the fundamental definition, with the exceptions treated as peripheral noise around a true signal.

I know this resembles the general idea of ad hoc auxiliary hypotheses, and it touches on Lakatos's notion of a degenerating problem-shift, but those frameworks are about saving scientific theories from falsification in general. What I am describing seems more specific: the gradual encrustation of a taxonomic boundary rule with defensible exemptions until the rule retains explanatory priority only in rhetoric, not in actual delimitation practice. Does this exact configuration have an established term in philosophy of biology—perhaps a specific diagnosis within the species-problem literature, or a concept like "taxonomic epicycles" applied to delimitation criteria? If there is no standard name, I want more than a coinage: I want the carving. How exactly does this differ from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis on one side, and from straightforward pluralism about species concepts on the other? Where are its boundaries—does it apply only when the exceptions are added serially to preserve intuitive groupings, or also when they are introduced preemptively? And if I were to name it, what definition and boundary cases would keep it distinct from both degenerating research programs and from the benign flexibility of a genuinely pluralistic taxonomy?
