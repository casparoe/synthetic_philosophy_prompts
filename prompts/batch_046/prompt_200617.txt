Can you make the case that the best fix for career-politician capture isn't pure term limits or pure lottocracy, but a "staggered shadow" hybrid? The setup: half the legislature is elected with hard two-term caps; the other half is filled by sortition for single, non-renewable six-year terms, with the two cohorts overlapping. The key twist is split authority—the elected members propose all bills, but the sortition members hold exclusive veto and ammendment rights. I want the normative argument, not logistics: why does this division respect democratic equality more consistently than either system alone? In particular, since the sortition cohort can't seek reelection, they aren't captured by party machinery, but voters still have direct electoral input through the proposal half. Please address the objection that this treats citizens unequally—some vote for representatives, others are randomly assigned representation—and also whether it secretly preserves a career class through the elected pipeline that eventually rotates into lobbying or advisory roles. I think there's a coherence argument here about separating agenda-setting from consent, but I want to see it actually built out. (Sorry if this is convoluted—it's late and my notes are messy.)
