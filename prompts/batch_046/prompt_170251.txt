Write a short dialogue between Maya and Jonas about the procreation asymmetry. Maya holds the usual view: there's a real moral reason not to create someone who'd suffer, but no matching reason to create someone who'd be happy—missing people aren't deprived, but actual suffering is a harm done. Jonas thinks that's a framing error. If suffering gives you a reason against bringing someone into existance, then happiness should symetrically give you a reason for it, and calling happy people "missing" just hides the symmetry.

Don't keep them in the clouds. Have them work through a case Maya invents on the spot: a clinic offers a "branching filter" that either produces one child with about 90% wellbeing potential or produces nothing at all, but there's a small risk the filter malfunctions and creates a severely suffering child. Maya argues that skipping the filter is morally neutral—no one is wronged—whereas running it is only okay if you can eliminate all risk of suffering, because then you're exposing a potental person to harm with no prior obligation to benefit anyone. Jonas pushes back: if happy lives have literally zero weight, why take any risk for them? And doesn't her view make the filter irrational, since you're gambling for a gain that counts for nothing?

Make them respond to each other's actual moves—Jonas should attack her "exposure without obligation" idea directly, and Maya should defend it without just repeating the textbook version. Keep it casual, like they're talking over coffee, and you can toss in a typo or two.
