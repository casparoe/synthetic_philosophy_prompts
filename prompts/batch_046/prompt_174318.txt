I'm preparing a two-page proposal for a six-month postgraduate research fellowship, and I want to sharpen a half-formed idea about Samuel Scheffler's 'afterlife conjecture'—the claim that human extinction is catastrophically bad not chiefly because of the deaths it involves, but because it eliminates the 'afterlife' of future generations in whose continuing projects present lives derive meaning. Most discussions treat this as a single moral phenomenon, but I suspect it conflates two conceptually separable mechanisms: what I will call 'narrative dependency' (the idea that present actions gain significance from being embedded in an open-ended collective story) and 'potentiality obligations' (the claim that present agents have direct reasons to bring future possible people into existence). The precise research question is whether Scheffler's argument survives once these are disentangled, and whether doing so reveals that only one mechanism genuinely supports our anti-extinction intuitions without committing us to an implausible species-level moral realism.

My approach will be strictly conceptual rather than empirical or historical: I will reconstruct the conjecture using distinctions available in Scheffler's own framework, then subject each mechanism to separate pressure tests drawn from anti-natalist arguments and population ethics—specifically, whether narrative dependency collapses into a psychological bias that rational agents ought to correct, and whether potentiality obligations can be grounded without attributing present moral claims to merely possible people. I am avoiding an empirical study of extinction attitudes or a historical survey of apocalyptic literature, since neither would resolve the conceptual tension and both lie beyond my resources. A successful outcome would be a clear taxonomy showing the mechanisms are independent, together with a defence of at least one that does not rely on the other; failure would be a demonstration that they are inseparable, or that both depend on premises that are independently unsustainable.

Given the six-month scope, I will confine myself to the conceptual reconstruction and the first round of objections, leaving full integration with the axiology of future populations for a subsequent project.
