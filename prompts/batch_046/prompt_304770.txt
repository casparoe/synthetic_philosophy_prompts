Write a travel-guide entry, in the deadpan style of a Lonely Planet page, for the fictional nation of Reflexia, whose legal and social institutions are built entirely around the idea that personal identity is a strange loop — specifically, that the self is not a substance but a self-referential symbol that emerges when patterns in a brain point back at the system that produces them. Cover the usual categories — entry requirements, local etiquette, currency and prices, a national holiday, and a practical-tips box — but treat the strange-loop ontology as an unremarkable bureaucratic reality. For instance: the visa office requires applicants to complete a recursive self-declaration form that must contain at least one reference to itself; citizens greet strangers not by name but by acknowledging the other person as a symbol in their own brain, which is considered polite only if done at the correct level of abstraction; and the country maintains a controversial "Loop Integrity Index," a graded bureaucratic metric that taxes individuals whose self-referential coherence falls below a statutory threshold, on the theory that a weak loop is still a loop but a broken one is a public-health hazard. Include a sidebar on the national holiday "Recursion Day," during which citizens are encouraged to trace their own mental representations upward through levels of abstraction until they reach the point where the symbol "I" points back downward. The satire should exaggerate real features of this view — that selfhood is pattern-based and graded, that it depends on maintaining reference across abstraction levels, that it can be disrupted without destroying the underlying physical system — without ever stating objections to it. Keep the tone entirely deadpan; the reader should recognize what is being ironized only through the absurdity of treating these philosophical commitments as travel logistics. Around 600 words, with at least one sidebar and one boxed "Practical Tips" section.
