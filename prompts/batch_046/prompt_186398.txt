After a long argument with a friend about whether Ubuntu can survive modern anonymity, I would like you to read the Wakandan ancestral plane scenes in Black Panther through a relational conception of personhood. My friend insisted that in Ubuntu, personhood depends only on present, living relations; I argued that some readings treat ancestors as active constituents of present personhood, not merely symbolic resources for an individual self. There is a particular tension here: if personhood is genuinely constituted by relations across time, then ritual interaction with the dead is not remembrance but participation in a shared self that spans generations. Does the film dramatise this stronger, intergenerational version, or does T'Challa ultimately remain an individual who merely consults his father? Please take both the film and the philosophy seriously, note where the work resists the reading, and answer in British English.
