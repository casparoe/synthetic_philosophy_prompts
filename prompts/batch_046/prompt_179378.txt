Assume the role of an expert philosopher of action with special attention to the boundary between mechanistic behavior and free will. Before constructing the scenario, please begin with brief brainstorming: list the structural features that distinguish sphexish rigidity—as in the classic digger-wasp example of repeating a fixed sequence even when the triggering condition is absent—from the hierarchical self-monitoring that Frankfurt-style compatibilists treat as sufficient for moral responsibility. Then design a single, self-contained thought experiment—no more than two paragraphs describing the case itself, no demons, no exotic physics, no perfect predictors, and with stakes that a normal person could face—featuring one artificial agent operating in an everyday environment, such as a maintenance robot in a recycling facility. The case must be engineered so that three distinct theoretical positions yield three different verdicts about whether the agent acts freely. Specifically: (1) with respect to Task A, the agent displays pure sphexishness, executing a rigid loop even when the environmental cue is missing; (2) with respect to Task B, the agent exhibits hierarchical feedback loops in which a first-order operational routine is endorsed by a second-order monitoring routine, and this endorsement can be revised in response to error signals; (3) the mechanism enabling Task B is physically continuous with that producing Task A—there is no separate “free will module,” only a continuous parameter controlling rigidity thresholds. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate the verdict each position delivers: (a) the hard determinist, who denies free will in both tasks; (b) the Frankfurt compatibilist, who accepts it for Task B but denies it for Task A; and (c) a hypothetical “uniform flexibilist,” who denies free will in both tasks on the grounds that the meta-routine governing Task B is itself rigidly parameterized and therefore sphexish at the higher level. Identify the precise structural feature that makes the compatibilist peel away from the uniform flexibilist, explain whether removing that feature collapses the case back into a standard sphexishness illustration, and finally anticipate how a defender of the uniform flexibilist would attack the case—specifically, by arguing that second-order volition in Task B is merely first-order rigidity displaced upward. Think carefully, and present your reasoning step by step so that the verdicts are genuinely generated by the theories rather than stipulated.
