My thesis: Knobe's asymmetry — that people attribute intentionality to foreseen harmful side effects but not to foreseen beneficial ones — transfers to algorithmic agents, so if an AI causes harm as a side effect of following its specification, users will judge it to have "meant" the harm regardless of designer intent. Therefore "pure" intent alignment (engineering the system to execute the spec) is practically unstable without value alignment (preventing harmful outcomes that trigger malicious attribution). My argument sketch: (1) Knobe's 2003 studies show the asymmetry for human agents; (2) preliminary HCI studies suggest users apply similar attribution patterns to algorithms; (3) so specification-gaming or reward-hacking that produces harm will be interpreted as intentional malice rather than misalignment; (4) consequently the engineering separation between intent and value alignment collapses in user-facing deployment.
