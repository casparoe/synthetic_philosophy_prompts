I am drafting my position for a graduate seminar on cultural heritage ethics and want a consistency audit before I present. Here are the commitments I actually hold, not straw positions:

(1) The Benin Bronzes seized during the 1897 punitive expedition should be returned unconditionally to Nigerian successor institutions, not because present conservation capacity is adequate, but because violent expropriation by an occupying force creates a standing corrective-justice claim that no subsequent custodial competence can extinguish.

(2) The Parthenon Marbles should remain in the British Museum, because Lord Elgin obtained them through a legal transaction with the Ottoman authorities who then governed Athens; absent a general principle that voids every legal instrument enacted under imperial domination, there is no specific wrong in this particular case that demands correction.

(3) I reject the moral legitimacy of all colonial-era legal instruments wholesale, on the ground that regimes which deny self-determination to a population cannot generate binding obligations or valid title over that population's cultural property.

(4) A museum should apply one uniform procedural test: restitution is mandatory when the acquiring party used direct violence or coercion to seize the object, and discretionary when the transfer was mediated by a recognized sovereign authority, even if that authority was imperial.

(5) A museum's overriding ethical duty is not to any single claimant but to preserve the physical object and sustain global access for research and public engagement, which means retention is sometimes permissible even after wrongful acquisition if return would entail a high risk of irreversible damage or destruction.

Please go through these and tell me: which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be? For each real tension, rate its severity, identify the cheapest revision and what it costs me, and then tell me which single revision to one commitment would resolve the most conflicts. Order the audit by severity, not as a flat list of equally bad problems.
