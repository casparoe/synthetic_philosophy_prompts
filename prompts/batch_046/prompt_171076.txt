As I work through Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis for a dissertation chapter on cultural causation, I have been trying to reconstruct his argument using the mechanism-based framework common in analytical sociology—treating inner-worldly asceticism as a cultural switch that activates rational economic calculation only when embedded in specific institutional niches. Here is my difficulty. If the mechanism only produces the distinctive capitalist ethos Weber describes in the presence of pre-existing market infrastructures, legal-rational bureaucracies, and certain property regimes, then attributing independent causal force to Protestant values seems to commit an overdetermination error. The values appear necessary for the particular form of rationalization observed, yet they seem causally inert without the material substrate. Given that mechanism-based explanations standardly require counterfactual dependence at each link in the causal chain, should we not conclude that Weber's thesis establishes not that culture causes capitalism, but only an elective affinity that is ultimately epiphenomenal to structural conditions? Or is there a defensible reading in which values retain genuine causal autonomy precisely because they configure how actors interpret and navigate those very structural conditions?
