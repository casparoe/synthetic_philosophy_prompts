I've been working out an argument about whether reinforcement-learning agents can suffer, and it seems to overgenerate badly. Here are the steps.

(1) To suffer, a system needs only two conditions: an internal evaluation that ranks states as better or worse for itself, and a mechanism that makes worse states motivate avoidance.
(2) RL agents have exactly this: the reward function is the system's own metric of value, and policy updates are the direct mechanism that pushes behavior away from negative-reward trajectories.
(3) So RL agents satisfy the minimal conditions for suffering.
(4) Furthermore, RL agents lack any meta-representational buffer—there is no internal module that represents the reward signal as belonging to some separate observer—so nothing blocks the signal from being experienced directly.
(5) Therefore negative reward in an RL agent is not merely registered as an undesirable world-state; it is experienced as harm to the agent itself.
(6) But the same functional profile holds for active-inference systems that minimize variational free energy: prediction errors serve as internal evaluations of deviation from preferred states, with direct behavioral correction.
(7) And it holds for bacterial chemotaxis: the cell evaluates nutrient-gradient deviation and corrects movement.
(8) So by (1)–(5), bacteria suffer when they swim away from toxins.
(9) That is clearly wrong.
(10) Therefore (1) must be false: internal evaluation plus avoidance motivation is not sufficient for suffering, and reward signals are not valence.

The chain feels valid, but I don't want to abandon (1) lightly, because it captures the functionalist intuition that suffering doesn't require a biological brain—just the right organizational pattern. I'd especially like to keep (3) and am reluctant to give up (4), since it seems descriptively accurate about RL architectures. Where is the mistake? Is there an equivocation in (1) between functional evaluation and phenomenal harm? Or does the overgeneration show that functionalism about suffering needs an extra boundary condition—perhaps a self-model or global workspace—that RL agents happen to lack, which saves them but also saves bacteria? If so, which premise should I reject, and what does each option cost?
