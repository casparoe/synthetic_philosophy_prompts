Consider the problem of ontology identification under conditions of structural inaccessibility, where the information required to distinguish between two descriptions is not merely hidden in practice but is constitutively excluded by the framework within which observation operates. Give an example of a concrete conceptual issue—drawn, for instance, from the interpretation of quantum mechanics, cosmological horizons, or theories of phenomenal consciousness—where this form of inaccessibility generates a genuine ambiguity about whether two ontological schemes describe the same entities, distinct entities, or entities that resist coherent individuation altogether. In your example, specify exactly what renders the inaccessibility structural rather than contingent, and demonstrate why standard adjudicative criteria, such as predictive equivalence, ontological parsimony, or explanatory coherence, fail to dissolve the ambiguity. The example should turn on a specific theoretical feature rather than a generic sceptical scenario.
