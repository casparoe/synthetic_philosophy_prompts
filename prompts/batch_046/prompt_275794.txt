Design a decision scenario in the vein of Buridan's ass — an agent must choose between two options with genuinely identical expected value, no hidden differentiator, and no meaningful cost to delaying further — but engineered so that three specific accounts deliver three distinct, non-obvious verdicts. The setting must be realistic (a workplace allocation, clinical assignment, or resource distribution, not a trolley case or demon problem) and described in a single paragraph. The options must involve payoffs a normal person could face, and there must be no time pressure that forces an arbitrary cutoff.

The three accounts are: (1) a classical rational-choice model that applies the principle of indifference and therefore requires randomization; (2) a causal decision-theoretic model that resolves ties by an arbitrarily assigned but causally inert salience feature, such as left-right spatial position in a left-to-right reading culture, yielding a deterministic selection of the left option; and (3) an agency-based view holding that rational choice requires a comparative reason, so that arbitrary selection is neither rational agency nor straightforward irrationality, but rather a suspension of the conditions for rational choice altogether.

Your scenario must include exactly one structural asymmetry that is causally irrelevant to the outcomes yet available to the agent’s perceptual system, and it must be absent any hidden information that could break the tie. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that it meets the specification: state the choice situation precisely, show what each of the three accounts says and why, identify the feature that does the work in separating (1) and (2) from (3), explain what happens to the verdicts if that asymmetry is removed (specifically, whether the case collapses back into a standard Buridan situation), and explain how a defender of the agency-based view (3) would attack the scenario — particularly whether they deny that the salience feature constitutes a comparative reason and what that denial implies for the causal-decision-theoretic tie-breaker. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts or authors.
