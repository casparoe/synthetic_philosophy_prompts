In the manuscript I am drafting, a tribunal applies karma strictly as a theory of moral desert, and its rulings to date are these: a merchant who knowingly sells tainted food and sickens customers receives severe negative karma; a merchant who sells the same food unwittingly, because his supplier lied, receives none, though the harm is identical; a merchant blackmailed at gunpoint into the sale receives none; a merchant who intends to poison but is intercepted before acting receives moderate negative karma; a healer who saves a life out of pure compassion receives full positive karma; and a healer who saves a life solely for social reputation receives partial positive karma. The tribunal must now rule on a programmer who freely publishes an open-source tool intended solely to advance public health, who could not have foreseen that malicious actors would adapt it to cause severe, widespread harm mediated entirely by others’ autonomous choices. For consistency with its own record, what should the tribunal decide—full positive, partial, none, or negative? Identify the closest analog among the six precedents, say whether the record genuinely underdetermines the verdict, and specify the principle the tribunal would be adopting with each possible choice. Then lay out the two or more rules that fit the first six rulings, state what each predicts in this case, give your best-guess verdict with reasons, and propose the single further case that would best distinguish which rule the tribunal is actually running.
