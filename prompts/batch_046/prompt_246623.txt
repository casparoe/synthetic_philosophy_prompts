I am applying for a one-semester independent-study grant in philosophy of science and need a polished proposal of roughly 1,500 words. The project examines whether the skeptical challenge posed by Boltzmann brains depends on a hidden, and usually unstated, theory of mental content. Most discussions treat the problem as purely cosmological or probabilistic: if a universe produces far more disordered, randomly assembled brains than ordered ones, then our belief that we are ordered observers is undermined by the base-rate. I want to investigate whether this inference only gets its bite on the assumption that mental content is anchored descriptively or phenomenologically—an assumption that allows a randomly assembled brain to genuinely possess the same cosmological beliefs despite lacking any appropriate causal, evolutionary, or functional history. If we instead apply a causal-historical or teleosemantic framework to mental representation, the Boltzmann brain may fail to possess the representational capacities required to form cosmological beliefs at all, which would dissolve the skeptical comparison class without requiring any change to the underlying cosmological model.

Write the proposal with these elements: a precise statement of the research question that cleanly separates the cosmological premise from the philosophical premise about reference and representation; an explanation of why standard responses—such as adjusting cosmological parameters to suppress Boltzmann-brain production, or appealing to self-locating evidence—leave this particular philosophical premise untouched; a semester-long method describing the arguments and conceptual distinctions you would examine, for instance comparing how different theories of mental content treat a randomly assembled cognitive system that token-identically reproduces a belief-state but lacks the normal causal or functional pedigree; a clear description of what a successful result would look like, specifically whether the skeptical threat is shown to be conditional on a contested philosophy of mind rather than on physics alone, and which considerations would decide between that outcome and the alternative that the problem remains purely cosmological; an honest assessment of scope, including which part of the project risks being too large—such as developing a full theory of mental representation versus applying existing ones—and how you would limit the inquiry accordingly; and a straightforward statement of what would count as the project failing, for example if the reference-theoretic distinction collapses under pressure or turns out to make no difference to the skeptical argument. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation requests; refer to positions and arguments by name where appropriate, and indicate that specific citations will be added in the final draft.
