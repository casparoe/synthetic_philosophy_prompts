I'm prepping a handout for my grad philosophy-of-mind course on the meta-problem of consciousness — specifically why we think physical explanations leave something out, not just whether they do. I want a sorting quiz that puts students into positions based on their actual commitments about the explanatory gap, not on which philosopher's name they recognize. Give me exactly five numbered items, each just a sentence or two, with no intro or conclusion. Items 1-4 should ask concrete diagnostic things: for instance, if you knew every physical fact about color vision, would you still feel there's a leftover "what it's like"; is that leftover feeling best understood as a conceptual confusion, a cognitive closure, a real ontological leftover, or just an illusion? Each should have two or three answer options that dont mention illusionism, phenomenal-concept strategy, McGinn-style closure, or brute emergence by name. Item 5 should be the scoring key mapping answer patterns onto those four positions plus one logical combo that stays empty, and flag which pair your tree cant actually seperate. No bibliographies, no citations, and phrase it all so a student who never read Chalmers can still answer.
