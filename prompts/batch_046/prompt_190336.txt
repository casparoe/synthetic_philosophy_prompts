I've been thinking about this a lot since my dad retired last month and suddenly lost the structure that seemed to give his days purpose. I was reading Schlick's essay where he argues that the meaning of life isnt about reaching some final goal but about doing things for their own sake, like play. It struck a chord, but I'm not convinced it's the whole story — especially after visiting my gran in a care home and seeing how people there maintain small rituals of value even when every larger purpose has collapsed. So before I commit to any theory of what makes life meaningful, I want the full spec: the desiderata any adequate account actually has to meet.

What must a theory of life's meaning deliver? Give me the requirements list — the cases it must handle (a life of pure care that never achieves anything final, a life of hedonism, someone whose purpose is destroyed but still finds value in small activities, and cases of telic suspension where goals are paused but not abandoned), the distinctions it cant collapse (between having meaning and feeling meaningful, between objective and subjective accounts, between play and mere distraction), and the psychological or ethical results it cant contradict. With why each is a requirement. Then flag any pair that looks jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, can Schlick's demand for play-like, autotelic engagement coexist with a requirement that meaning survive severe depression or disability where active engagement is severely limited? And tell me which single desideratum does the most damage to popular purpose-achievement models.

Keep the weight on the criteria rather than scoring, but briefly — how do Schlick's play-based view, a purpose-driven account, and a relational or commitment-based view do against the list? I mainly need the spec to think with, not a final verdict.
