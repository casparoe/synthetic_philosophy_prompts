I'm putting together a sorting quiz for a philosophy meetup I'm running next month — we got into a long argument about whether the past is "gone" or just "elsewhere in time," and I realized nobody actually knows which view they hold once you get past the buzzwords. So I want a taxonomy structured like a decision tree that takes someone from concrete intuitions to a named position (or an unnamed gap) in the presentism versus eternalism debate. Start with a split on whether temporal passage feels real: if you watch an old video of yourself as a kid, is that child literally still experiencing that moment, or is that experience over and done with? From there branch on ontology — do only present things exist, do present and past exist but the future doesn't, or do all three exist equally? Then split on whether change requires gaining and losing properties over time (A-theory) versus just having different properties at different times (B-theory).

Now I want to inject a somewhat weird angle I'm calling "prospective weight" — the idea that future events don't exist yet but still exert a kind of ontological pull on the present, not just causal influence but something closer to modal reality. Include branches asking whether future contingents carry this weight, and map out where that lands: is it compatible with presentism if the present is "weighted" toward a non-existant future? Does it collapse into eternalism? Flag any combination that looks incoherent, and note whether this is just an unexplored middle ground or actively unstable. Also include a branch on four-dimensionalism versus three-dimensionalism: if you think objects persiste by having temporal parts, does that force eternalism, or can you combine it with presentism?

Every node should be phrased as a concrete question someone can answer without knowing jargon, and each leaf should carry the position name — presentism, growing block, moving spotlight, B-theory eternalism, etc. — or "unnamed," plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment, like presentists having to deny dinosaurs ever existed or eternalists treating waiting for the bus as an illusion. Make sure to flag any pairs your tree can't really separate, and don't ask for citations or a reading list — I just need the instrument itself. There's probably a typo in here but that's fine.
