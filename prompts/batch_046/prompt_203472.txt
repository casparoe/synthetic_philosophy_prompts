Design a final-exam essay question for an upper-division philosophy of mind course on Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian. The question must not be answerable by reciting definitions; instead, present students with a hypothetical cognitive architecture that learns exclusively through backpropagation-driven updates to a hierarchical predictive model, without generating discrete testable hypotheses and without access to language or cultural artifacts. Ask students to determine whether this architecture represents a Skinnerian creature, a non-linguistic Popperian creature, or reveals a gap in Dennett's four-fold classification, and require them to defend their verdict with explicit attention to the role of internal simulation versus cultural scaffolding. Along with the question, provide a model answer of roughly 650–750 words and a marking scheme totaling 20 marks, divided into four graded dimensions: (i) accurate exposition of the four kinds with emphasis on the simulation/tool distinction; (ii) analysis of why predictive-gradient updating is neither pure Skinnerian conditioning nor Popperian hypothesis testing; (iii) assessment of whether abstract representational capacities in this architecture depend on Gregorian resources; (iv) a clearly argued conclusion about taxonomy revision versus assimilation, with explicit reasoning about error-cost asymmetry. For each dimension, specify the marks available, the conceptual move required for full credit, and the specific misconception that should reduce the score.
