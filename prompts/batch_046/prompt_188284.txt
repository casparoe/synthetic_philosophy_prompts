I have been working through debates about the color-phi phenomenon, in which a moving spot appears to change color at an intermediate position before the second-colored dot is actually presented. Among the proposed explanations I keep noticing a recurring structural difference. Some accounts treat conscious experience as constructed with a brief delay: once both stimuli have arrived, the brain retroactively adjusts the content of the earlier interval so that the color shift is reported as having occurred at the correct place and time—more like altering a historical record after the fact to remove an inconsistency. Other accounts deny that any contradictory intermediate percept ever reaches awareness; the first color at that location is suppressed or blocked before registration, so there is no erroneous trace left to correct—more like excising a figure from a photograph so that nothing remains to annotate. I do not know whether philosophers of mind have a standard label for this distinction between retrospective revision of experiential content and preemptive excision of contradictory traces in theories of conscious timing. Does it map onto an established pair of concepts? If so, how exactly do those concepts align with the two strategies I have sketched, and where do they fail to capture the difference? If there is no canonical name, what would be the sharpest way to formalize the boundary between them, and how should one distinguish either strategy from an ordinary post-hoc rationalization or auxiliary hypothesis?
