A funder has committed three years of support for a ten-person conceptual research program working at the intersection of population axiology and long-horizon policy design; they explicitly do not want a survey, bibliography, or citation list, but a defended agenda for limited effort. Your task: identify the three or four open questions or lines of work in population axiology—centered on the repugnant conclusion and its relatives—that are most upstream of whether and how population-size considerations should constrain real-world choices about climate mitigation, existential-risk reduction, and the evaluation of futures that may include potential beings with radically different welfare capacities (for example, technologically altered or non-biological substrates). For each item, defend its place in the ordering by addressing: (1) its dependency relations—what other questions it unlocks or blocks; (2) its tractability for philosophers working without empirical ML tools or web access; (3) the kind of conceptual result that would cause you to reorder priorities over the next few years; and (4) its direct bearing on policy-relevant trade-offs rather than on pure intuition-pumping. Before presenting the ranking, briefly brainstorm the criteria you are weighing—importance, tractability, neglectedness, and upstream dependency—and flag the sharpest trade-offs among them. Write concisely, avoid appeals to authority or lists of sources, and ensure the deliverable is an ordering with reasoning, not a literature overview.
