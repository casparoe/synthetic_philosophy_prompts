There's this guy in my decision theory reading group who's obsessed with evidencial cooperation in large worlds. He's pretty firm on a few things. He says if there's literally infinite copies of you scattered across a multiverse, all facing the exact same prisoner's dillema with the same values, then of course you should cooperate — your choice is evidencial weight for all of them. He also says if the copies are only near-perfect — like, same decision algorithm but with some noisy weights — you should still cooperate, as long as there's enough of them that the correlation doesn't wash out. But he says if the other agents have reasoning that's perfectly correlated with yours but they care about totally orthogonal stuff — paperclips vs suffering, with zero overlap — then don't bother, there's no acausal trade to be made. And he also says that even with just one other copy in a causally isolated pocket, facing the same choice with aligned values, you should still cooperate, because logical correlation doesn't need a crowd.

I'm less interested in whether he's right than in what rule he's actually running. So: do you think he'd say we should cooperate with a huge population of agents in other worlds who share our values and have reasoning that's about 80% correlated with ours, but they're facing a completely different decision problem — not a prisoner's dillema at all, but some resource-allocation thing? What's the further case — maybe involving correlation strength with only two copies — that'd best tell the competing rules apart?

More specifically: lay out the rules that fit his four verdicts, say what each predicts about that different-problem crowd, give your best guess with reasoning, and name the one extra case that'd actually distinguish them.
