"Arrow's theorem shows that with four conditions—unrestricted domain, Pareto, IIA, and non-dictatorship—no social welfare function can satisfy all four with three or more options. So the board hits the wall. The fix is Borda count and dropping IIA. Borda gives 2 pts to first, 1 to second, 0 to third. This respects Pareto: if everyone puts Alpha over Beta, Alpha gets more points. It also avoids dictatorship since every voter contributes equal weight. Borda was actually developed by Arrow's critics in the 1950s right after the impossibility result came out. The only failure is IIA—if you switch Gamma and Beta but keep Alpha's position, the points get redistributed among all three, so Alpha's score can shift, which is exactly the IIA violation. Because the aggregate score ranking is still transitive, the board gets universal domain, Pareto, and non-dictatorship all together without IIA."
