I'm taking a philosophy of science course and we got into anthropic reasoning after class, and now I'm trying to write down my actual views to see if they hold water. Here are my commitments:
(1) I'm on board with Bostrom's self-sampling idea: treat yourself as randomly drawn from your reference class.
(2) I reject self-indication, mostly because the presumptuous philosopher thing seems like a reductio - you shouldn't be able to boost a cosmological theory just by noticing it predicts more minds.
(3) I think the reference class should include any system with integrated information above a certain threshold, so synthetic minds count if they're conscious.
(4) I think the presumptuous philosopher only bites if you treat all multiverse theories as having equal priors; if you have strong empirical priors, the anthropic factor gets drowned out, so SIA isn't actually crazy in practice.
(5) I also think that if synthetic minds eventually outnumber us, SSA actually predicts I should probably be one of them, which is weird since I'm clearly biological right now, so I think we need to weight observers by subjective duration, not just count them.
(6) Finally, I think none of this applies to AI systems themselves - an AI shouldn't do anthropic reasoning about its own existence because it lacks the first-person indexical that makes sampling meaningful.

Do these hang together? I'm especially worried that (4) weakens my rejection in (2), adn that (5) is just an ad hoc patch to save (1) and (3). Also does (6) conflict with (3)'s substrate neutrality? Rank the tensions by severity, say which is just apparent, and tell me the cheapest fix that doesn't require me to drop substrate neutrality.
