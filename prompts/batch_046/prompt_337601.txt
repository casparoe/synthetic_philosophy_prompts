I'm on the university's spatial ethics comittee and we vote thursday on whether to greenlight a pilot called Gradient Privacy in the student commons. The proposal is adaptive architecture—smart glass, acoustic baffles, etc.—that calibrates transparency and sound opacity in real time using occupant preferences plus a "social connectivity score." The designers say privacy is best understood as user-controlled visibility: people should be able to dial exposure up or down to maximize wellbeing.

I'm skeptical because I think privacy isn't just a preference you optimize but a kind of structural inaccessibility—the default invisibility of certain spaces and moments that lets people maintain different social roles without them collapsing into one continuous profile. If the algorithm learns your patterns and adjusts the environment to match, it might eliminate the very separation between contexts that makes role experimentation possible. So I want the memo to recommend we approve funding only with a hard constraint: every zone must retain a physical mode of guaranteed opacity that the system cannot override, even if every user in the room requests full transparency.

Please draft the memo for the committee. Recommend a concrete course of action with a timeline—approve with the constraint, reject outright, or defer pending revision—and take seriously the designers' argument that user autonomy over visibility is the heart of privacy, not just a feature request. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble and don't restate the question back to me. Sorry if there's a typo or two.
