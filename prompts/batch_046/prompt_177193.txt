I'm trying to determine whether reinforcement learning agents can genuinely suffer, specifically by asking when a reward signal functions as affective valence rather than as a mere training gradient. Here are my intuitive verdicts on concrete architectures, which feel partly consistent and partly not.

Consider five setups. (1) A standard Q-learning agent receiving scalar rewards for task success: clearly no suffering; the signal is just a parameter update. (2) A model-based agent with an explicit interoceptive reward channel tied to homeostatic deviation—energy depletion, structural damage—where negative values directly penalize policies that cause such states: this seems genuinely aversive, analogous to pain. (3) The same interoceptive architecture, except the homeostatic data feed only the state encoder, not the reward function; rewards come entirely from an external task: despite tracking damage, I don't think it suffers. (4) A curiosity-driven agent rewarded for prediction error, which therefore seeks out dangerous, high-uncertainty environments and receives large intrinsic rewards there: its valence seems inverted—danger is "good"—so I deny it suffers in those moments, yet the reward is still tightly coupled to its internal dynamics. (5) A wireheading agent that learns to directly stimulate its reward mechanism and abandons all other goals: I lean toward saying it's in a state of artificial contentment, but I'm unsure whether interrupting that stimulation harms it or merely restores baseline functioning.

My difficulty is reconciling (2) and (4). Both couple reward directly to internal dynamics, yet one feels like suffering and the other like perverse enjoyment. Is there a precise principle—perhaps about whether the signal regulates homeostasis versus drives exploration, or about its causal role in belief updating versus policy optimization—that delivers all five verdicts? If not, which verdict should be abandoned, and what does the repaired principle predict for an architecture I haven't mentioned: one with an epiphenomenal "feeling" module that generates valence representations but plays no causal role in either learning or action selection?
