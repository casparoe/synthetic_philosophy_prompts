I've been sitting in on a grad seminar about obligations to future people and we've been circling the nonidentity problem for what feels like forever. Everyone can walk through Parfit's young-girl-and-depletion-policy case, everyone knows the standard person-affecting objection versus the impersonal total-utilitarian reply, but the discussion just loops back every session. Nobody moves an inch. Lately I've been wondering whether the stall is hiding something deeper about how each side imagines the counterfactual 'if we had acted differently.' It looks to me like one group is running a nearest-possible-world picture where the same individuals could exist with altered welfare, while the other is using a branching-actualist picture where changing the policy wipes out the specific people who'd be born. But neither camp ever makes that modal commitment explicite — they just argue about whether anyone is 'harmed' or 'worse off,' talking past each other with the same moral vocabulary. So is this actually a substantive ethical disagreement, a verbal mix-up about 'harm,' or two seperate metaphysical questions being fought under one name? And given whatever diagnosis you come up with, what would count as real progress — forcing the modal framework into the open, switching to a formal model of counterfactual identity dependence, or admitting the problem dissolves once you see the hidden machinery? I want an honest diagnosis that engages what each side actually says, not just another survey of Parfit versus the critics.
