Write a dialogue between two philosophers, A and B, who disagree about whether an autonomous reasoning system can consistently trust its own future conclusions. A defends a protocol she calls reflective arbitration, in which the system assigns credibility weights to its own upcoming reasoning traces but only updates them when an external verification escrow certifies that the update does not violate consistency conditions. B argues that this escrow fails to solve the Löbian obstacle: if the system can recognize the escrow as a reliable authority on its own consistency, then Löb's theorem applies to that recognition itself, so the obstacle is not eliminated but merely displaced onto the meta-level. A should respond by claiming that practical rationality requires only defeasible, heuristic self-trust bounded by fallible checks, not the provable closure conditions that generate the contradiction. B should counter that any heuristic strong enough to actually guide belief revision in the system's own reasoning will implicitly satisfy those closure conditions operationally, even if not explicitly. Have them engage each point directly rather than talking past one another, and keep the exchange focused on whether weakening logical omniscience genuinely dissolves the obstacle or only masks it.
