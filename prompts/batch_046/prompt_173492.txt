I am currently drafting a novel in which the central conceit turns upon an ant colony—known, in the manuscript's internal mythology, as Aunt Hillary—whose foraging patterns organise themselves into what I have come to call an Ant Fugue: a contrapuntal structure in which pheromone trails do not merely guide but answer one another across time, producing something analogous to thematic development rather than simple feedback loops. The philosophical question that keeps arresting my narrative progress is whether such a colony might possess a mind, not in the metaphorical sense, but in a manner that genuinely troubles our existing taxonomies of collective intentionality and phenomenal consciousness.

Given that I must resolve this for my characters rather than for an academic journal, I should like you to advise me—which open questions, lines of philosophical or theoretical work, and conceptual implications in this specific area ought to command my attention and, by extension, that of anyone attempting to think seriously about distributed cognition? I am less interested in a survey than in a defended agenda. For instance: should one prioritise the problem of phenomenal binding across spatially separated agents, given that Aunt Hillary's fugue-like dynamics seem to generate a form of working memory without any central integrator? Or is the more urgent task to clarify whether collective intentionality requires shared propositional attitudes, or whether a contrapuntal, non-propositional structure—such as the fugue—might satisfy a weaker but still genuinely mental criterion? Furthermore, which of these investigations are tractable without empirical neurobiology, and which depend upon it?

Please rank or defend an ordering of perhaps three to four such open problems. For each, explain why it is upstream of the others—whether resolving it would dissolve downstream debates about colony-mindedness, how tractable it is for conceptual analysis alone, and what kind of theoretical or observational development over the next few years might cause you to revise the ranking. I want the reasoning to be explicit about the trade-offs between importance, tractability, and neglectedness, since my novel's philosophical architecture will stand or fall on the coherence of that ordering rather than on any particular answer.
