Here is an argument my study group constructed. Each premise looks defensible in isolation, but the conclusion is hard to swallow, and I worry it proves far more than we intended.

(1) Moral objectivism is the view that moral claims are true or false independently of any individual’s or culture’s beliefs or attitudes.

(2) If a domain is genuinely objective, then fully rational, fully informed agents should eventually converge on the correct answers within it; persistent, rational, fully-informed disagreement is therefore strong evidence that there is no objective fact of the matter to converge upon.

(3) Cross-cultural psychological research reveals deep, stable moral disagreements—for instance, about obligations to aging parents, the moral significance of purity violations, and the relative weight of loyalty versus individual fairness—that persist even when participants share the same empirical information and reasoning capacities.

(4) So, for a broad range of moral questions, there is no objective fact of the matter; objectivism fails locally for those questions.

(5) Objectivism is standardly treated as a global, all-or-nothing thesis: either moral judgments as a class have objective status, or none do.

(6) Therefore moral objectivism is false across the board.

Now extend the reasoning to other domains:

(7) Expert logicians persistently and rationally disagree about whether to reject the law of excluded middle or about which formal logic is correct, despite shared formal competence and access to the same proofs.

(8) By (2) and (5), formal logic is not objective.

(9) Physicists persistently disagree about interpretations of quantum mechanics despite shared observational data and mathematical frameworks.

(10) By (2) and (5), physics lacks objectivity in those domains.

(11) But it is plainly absurd to deny objectivity to logic or to well-confirmed physical science merely because experts disagree about interpretations or foundations.

(12) Hence the argument proves too much, and one of its premises must be rejected.
