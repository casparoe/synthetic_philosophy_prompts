I'm putting together a sorting tree / taxonomy for a philosophy blog post about whether the agricultural revolution was actually a mistake — the whole hunter-gatherer affluence vs. origin-of-inequality mess. I want readers sorted by what they actually beleive about concrete cases, not by whether they call themselves "Sahlins-style" or whatever.

The tree should branch on four diagnostic commitments, each phrased as a concrete claim someone can agree or disagree with without knowing the literature labels:

1. Whether immediate-return foragers had more net leisure per capita than delayed-return agriculturalists (the time-budget framing), or whether comparing caloric output per hour worked is the only fair metric.
2. Whether skeletal evidence of declining health and stature post-transition is decisive, or whether it's mostly confounded by settlement density and pathogen load.
3. Whether storable surplus is a necessary condition for sustained social inequality, or if hierarchical ranking can stabilize without storable calories.
4. Whether the shift was reversible in principle at the time it happened — since calling it a "mistake" seems to require that an available alternative was foregone.

Build the tree from the logical space first: list the combinations of yes/no answers, then map actual positions onto the leaves, with "unnamed" for empty slots. Each leaf needs a one-sentence "most awkward commitment." I want at least eight leaves. Include the original-affluence view, the Diamond-style "worst mistake" view, the structural-inevitability view, and the institutional "storage-necessary-for-inequality" view.

Note any unoccupied combinations and briefly diagnose why they're empty — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored? Also flag if two named positions collapse into identical answer patterns and the tree can't seperate them.

Keep the tone casual, like a slack message to a colleague. Maybe drop a typo or two — "beleive," "seperate" — doesn't matter. Don't ask for bibliographies or reading lists; I just want the instrument itself.
