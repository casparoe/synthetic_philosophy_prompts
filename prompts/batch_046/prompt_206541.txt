Please specify the desiderata that an adequate philosophical account of the category "Western values" must satisfy in order to be analytically useful rather than merely rhetorical or polemical. For each requirement, state the specific conceptual failure it guards against—such as essentialism, anachronism, false universalism, or the collapse of descriptive sociology into normative endorsement—and mark whether you consider it non-negotiable or a negotiable preference of particular camps. Your list should address at least these dimensions: (1) coherence conditions for grouping heterogeneous norms under a single geographic-historical label without arbitrary exclusion; (2) criteria for distinguishing values that are genuinely shared across relevant societies from those that are selectively curated to construct a civilizational narrative; (3) the proper relation between describing what values are widely held in Western societies and endorsing them as normative standards; (4) how the framework must accommodate internal dissent, historical discontinuity, and the presence of the same values in non-Western traditions; and (5) whether the category can survive if some of its putative core elements—such as secular governance, individual rights, and democratic participation—are shown to be contested or absent in significant parts of the relevant societies. Then, briefly apply your criteria to the familiar "Enlightenment package" framing, identifying precisely where it meets your non-negotiable conditions and where it fails them. Keep the focus on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on a historical survey.
