Here is where I have settled on knowability, closure, and the formal modeling of knowledge, and I want an audit of whether the set is jointly tenable.

(1) I accept a restricted knowability principle: for every contingent true proposition p, it is metaphysically possible that some agent knows p; I do not extend this claim to necessary falsehoods, contradictions, or tautologies.

(2) I deny that knowledge is closed under logical consequence in general, but I preserve a restricted single-premise closure: if S knows p and S knows that p entails q, and q is cognitively available to S (not a distant or structurally complex consequence), then S can come to know q.

(3) I take Fitch’s paradox to show not merely that unrestricted closure is false, but that there is a deeper conceptual instability: the very idea of omniscient knowledge is in tension with the self-referential possibility of an unknown truth, and blocking the derivation technically does not dissolve the underlying pressure.

(4) I formalize epistemic reasoning with neighborhood semantics rather than Kripke frames, because neighborhood models allow an agent to stand in the knowledge relation to p without standing in it to every logical consequence of p.

(5) I hold that rational belief, unlike knowledge, is subject to a logical-omniscience norm: if one rationally believes p and rationally believes p→q, then one is rationally required to believe q, regardless of cognitive availability.

(6) I also accept that there can be “epistemically isolated” contingent truths—propositions that are in principle knowable, but only from an epistemic position that is incompatible with the agent’s knowing that very proposition (for instance, knowing it requires lacking some information that is part of the proposition’s content).
