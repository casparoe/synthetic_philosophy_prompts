I've been working through the conceivability argument for philosophical zombies, and I think I see a version that proves far more than physicalism's falsehood — it looks like it undermines every a posteriori necessary identity we take science to have discovered. Here is the chain, numbered.

(1) A zombie world — physically and functionally identical to ours but lacking phenomenal consciousness — is conceivable without contradiction.
(2) If a state of affairs is conceivable without contradiction, then it is metaphysically possible. (Call this the Conceivability-Possibility bridge, CP.)
(3) Therefore zombies are metaphysically possible.
(4) If physicalism is true, phenomenal consciousness is necessarily identical to some physical or functional state.
(5) So if zombies are possible, physicalism is false.

Now apply CP consistently across the board:
(6) We can conceive of a liquid that is observationally identical to water (clear, thirst-quenching, boils at 100°C at 1 atm) but composed of XYZ rather than H2O — call it z-water.
(7) By CP, z-water is metaphysically possible, which entails that water is not necessarily H2O.
(8) The same reasoning applies to heat (not necessarily mean molecular kinetic energy), gold (not necessarily atomic number 79), and every other scientific essentialist claim.
(9) So CP doesn't just refute physicalism; it collapses the distinction between a posteriori necessary truths and contingent correlations, leaving science without necessary discoveries.
(10) But this is absurd: we treat Kripkean identities like water = H2O as genuine discoveries, not as contingent labels.
(11) Hence CP, if taken seriously enough to license (3), is also taken seriously enough to destroy the modal framework that makes scientific knowledge possible — and in particular, the very idea that there are necessary physical truths that consciousness might or might not be identical to.
(12) So the zombie argument appears to be self-undermining: it uses CP to reach (5), but CP's consistent application reaches (9), which removes the ground for treating physicalism as a substantive modal claim in the first place.

Where exactly does this go wrong? I want to preserve the zombie intuition if I can — (3) still feels live — but I'm suspicious of (6)-(8). Is there a principled way to block the generalization from consciousness to chemistry without special pleading? Or should I reject CP at (2) and pay the price elsewhere? If I keep CP but restrict it, what restricts it? And if I reject it entirely, do I lose the ability to argue for any metaphysical possibility from imagination alone?

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
