I'm drafting the conceptual framework for a chapter on Weimar political thought and want a consistency check before I submit the outline. Here are my working commitments on Goethe's politics:

(a) Goethe is best interpreted as a constitutional conservative in a pre-liberal, Burkean register: he defends organically developed institutions against rationalist reconstruction, but—crucially—without Burke's commitment to representative government as the vehicle of that organic growth.

(b) His notion of Weltbürgertum implies a cosmopolitan political ethics that demands active cross-cultural civic engagement rather than retreat into private aesthetic cultivation.

(c) The political significance of Bildung is entirely indirect: it cultivates judgment-capable subjects, but carries no institutional entailments and should not be invoked to justify state-sponsored cultural or educational policy.

(d) Goethe's refusal to endorse the German nationalist movements expressed not elitist disdain but a principled anti-statism: the state is, at best, a necessary evil and an unfit object of patriotic devotion.

(e) I want to draw on this framework to defend a contemporary liberal-democratic order whose legitimacy rests not on popular sovereignty but on its success in sustaining the conditions for Bildung and cosmopolitan exchange.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what has to give, and indicate which single revision would resolve the most conflicts.
