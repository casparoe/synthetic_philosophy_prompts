I'm preparing my position for a presentation in a political philosophy seminar on conditional welfare and moral desert, and I want a consistency audit before I defend it. I hold: (a) workfare conditions are legitimate only when recipients have the effective freedom—in Sen's capability sense, not merely formal liberty—to comply; (b) benefit fraud is morally worse than passive non-compliance with work-search rules, not mainly because of fiscal loss but because it violates the expressive conditions of the welfare contract, signaling a refusal of social membership rather than an inability to contribute; (c) the public category of the "undeserving poor" should be eliminated entirely, since it systematically conflates moral luck with moral desert; (d) despite (c), I support both rigorous fraud detection and mandatory contribution requirements for able-bodied claimants, justified on reciprocity rather than desert grounds; and (e) if reciprocity genuinely requires contribution, then the state must first guarantee meaningful employment opportunities, which current programs manifestly fail to do. Which pairs genuinely pull against each other, which only appear to conflict and dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision to restore coherence? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, and for each indicate whether fixing it requires giving up a substantive commitment or only refining its scope.
