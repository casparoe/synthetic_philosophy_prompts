I run a small interview series for philosophy grad students and next week I'm talking to a politcal philosopher — we'll just call her the guest — who defends a really specific hybrid view about voting ethics. She thinks voting is primarily expressive rather than insturmental, but she also holds this hard-line 'non-complicity' rule: you have a strict duty not to cast a ballot for anyone who'd cause serious moral harm, even when they're clearly the lesser evil next to the other option. What's strange is she pairs that with a dilution argument, saying that in large-scale democracies your single vote is so statistically meaningless that the expressive value basically evaporates, so abstaining is often morally on par with voting — which seems to undercut her own reason for caring about expression in the first place. I need eight to ten interview questions that actually dig into the mechanics of her position, not generic stuff like asking what drew her to philosophy.

Specifically, I want at least one question with a follow-up on the tension between her dilution claim and her insistence that expression still matters — like, if the crowd drowns out your voice, why isn't abstention always the right call? Another on how her non-complicity constraint differs from a general duty not to support bad institutions, since voting is just one way to prop up a system. I also need something on strategic voting in primaries: if she thinks you shouldn't vote for the lesser-evil monster in the general election, does she allow voting for a slightly-better-but-still-bad candidate in a primary just to block the worst one? And please include a question about referendum voting where there's no candidate to be complicit with — does her framework apply there, or does she think direct democracy sidesteps the problem? For each of these, I want the follow-up keyed to the answer she'll probably give; for instance, if she says primaries are different because you're selecting from a pool rather than endorsing a final ruler, then the follow-up should press whether that distinction collapses once primary winners almost always go on to win the general. I was chatting with my roommate about this over coffee and we realized we don't even know if her view allows writing in candidates as a way to avoid complicity, so slip that in if it fits. Oh, and since I keep mixing up 'instrumental' and 'expressive' in my head, please phrase the questions clearly so I don't sound confused when I ask them.
