been chewing on origin essentialism after re-reading some old rationalist threads about whether you could've come from a different sperm/egg combo. i tried to formalize the intuition that my origin is fixed, but i think i accidentally proved that every human life is modally necessary or something equally broken. can you diagnose where it slips?

(1) suppose origin essentialism is false: then there exists a possible world W where i exist but originated from gametes G' (not my acutal G).
(2) in W, G' produces a zygote Z with different DNA, different phenotype, probably different name and life trajectory.
(3) if i'm identical to Z in W, then in the actual world my properties are contingent in a weird way - i could have had Z's body and history.
(4) but apply this not just to me but universally: for any person P, if origin essentialism is false for P, then P is identical to some other zygote-product in some other world.
(5) now count: there are roughly 10^600 possible human gamete pairings, but only about 10^11 humans have ever lived.
(6) if each actual person can map to a "counterpart" in worlds where origins differ, then in a world with random gamete fusion, almost every resulting person must be identical to some actual-world person.
(7) so in that world, you and i and everyone we know are all present, scattered across bodies with no relation to our actual lives.
(8) that's clearly nuts - it doesn't just allow different origins, it collapses all modal distinction between people.
(9) therefore origin essentialism can't be false; it must be neccesary that i come from exactly G, and so does everyone else.
(10) but then it's metaphysically impossible for my parents to have had a different child, which feels like it overfixes history way past what the intuition justified.

where's the error? i feel like step (3) sneaks in a counterpart-theory mistake, or maybe (6) does an illegal quantifier shift over worlds. i'd like to keep (1) and the intuition behind (9) without paying the price in (7). what's the least costly rejection?
