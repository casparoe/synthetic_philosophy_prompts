I am preparing discussion materials for an advanced seminar on axiology, and my colleagues and I have been debating whether the heterogeneity problem genuinely undermines hedonism or merely forces it to refine its ontology of mental states. To structure our disagreement precisely, I want you to rate five proposed answers to the question “what do all pleasures have in common?” on a scale of 1 to 10, where the score reflects how well the answer satisfies a specific dual criterion: first, it must preserve hedonism’s normative claim that pleasure is the sole intrinsic good without covertly importing other values; second, it must account for the obvious phenomenal diversity of pleasures—ranging from bodily warmth to intellectual triumph to moral satisfaction—without committing to an implausibly uniform mental quality. The five answers to evaluate are: (A) the quantitative-uniformity view that every pleasure shares an identical felt-quality and differs only in intensity and duration; (B) Mill’s qualitative-hierarchy view that pleasures form categorically distinct kinds arranged by dignity or nobility; (C) the representational-content view that pleasures are unified not by intrinsic feel but by being mental representations of favorable conditions; (D) the functionalist-dispositional view that pleasure is whatever state plays a specific reward-and-reinforcement causal role, regardless of subjective character; and (E) the deflationary family-resemblance view that “pleasure” has no essential common property, but this very heterogeneity is compatible with hedonism if we treat pleasure as a cluster concept anchored by practical use rather than metaphysical essence. For each answer, provide the numerical score and a concise paragraph of reasoning that identifies exactly where it succeeds or collapses under the dual criterion, paying special attention to hard cases—such as comparing the pleasure of solving a mathematical proof with the pleasure of being warmed by sunlight—where its proposed commonality seems most strained. Please do not include bibliographies, suggested readings, or citations; I need only the ratings and the argumentative reasoning.
