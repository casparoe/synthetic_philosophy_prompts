For an epistemology working group I have been asked to set out my profile on bootstrapping and easy knowledge so others can stress-test it. My commitments are these: I am a reliabilist about justification—beliefs are justified in virtue of being produced by processes that tend toward truth; I hold that particular perceptual beliefs about ordinary objects are justified non-inferentially, prior to any general defense of perceptual reliability; I reject the standard easy-knowledge challenge on the ground that deriving general reliability from particular successes is ordinary inductive support, not vicious circularity; I think scientific practice legitimately involves calibration loops, where instruments are constructed using theoretical assumptions and then deployed to test those same theories; I maintain a strict boundary between such benign mutual support and vicious circularity, defining the latter as a case in which belief B has no justification other than B itself; I also take first-person knowledge of one's current mental states to be direct, in that it does not depend on prior reliability claims about introspection, yet I do not see this directness as incompatible with reliabilism; and I think there is a mediating layer of methodological rules—concerning error correction and instrument calibration—that makes bootstrapping rational without reducing it either to pure empiricism or to pure theory. Audit this set: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which appear tense but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each, state the cheapest revision that restores consistency and what it costs. Then identify the single commitment whose revision would clean up the profile most, and lead with that bottom-line recommendation in your first sentence; follow with reasoning, no preamble and no restatement of the question.
