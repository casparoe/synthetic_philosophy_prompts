I've just finished a long and somewhat frustrating argument with a close friend about antinatalism, and I'm trying to articulate a specific argumentative pattern I kept noticing—one I don't have a technical term for. My friend explicitly endorsed what I understand to be Benatar's asymmetry: avoiding the creation of a suffering person is good because the absence of pain is good for that non-existent person, while avoiding the creation of a happy person is not bad because the absence of pleasure is not bad for them. She used this to defend a strong antinatalist position. But when the conversation turned to concrete life choices—whether she herself should eventually have children, whether adoption carries different moral weight than conception, or whether remaining childless is a kind of loss—she adopted a different framing without seeming to notice the shift. She spoke of her potential child as being "deprived" of a good life if she never conceived, of "missing out" on value she could have created, and of procreation as a way of "giving the gift of existence." Crucially, she never explicitly denied the asymmetry; she still maintained that absence of pleasure is not a harm. Yet she consistently treated non-existence as a baseline that can be improved upon for happy lives, while treating it as a baseline that ought to be preserved for suffering lives—an implicit double bookkeeping. The move was not straightforward equivocation, since she wasn't using "harm" in two different technical senses. Rather, she seemed to apply a person-affecting restriction selectively: treating potential people as lacking interests when that served antinatalist conclusions, but as having frustrated interests or being subject to deprivation when that served to justify, or to mourn, the decision to procreate.

Does this oscillation have an established name in the philosophical literature? Is it recognized as a specific failure mode of person-affecting views, distinct from ordinary psychological inconsistency and from the non-identity problem? If there is no established concept, how should it be distinguished—say, from a simple optimism bias about one's own potential offspring, or from a tacit shift to a total-utilitarian evaluation of worlds? I would be satisfied with a precise carving of the phenomenon, its nearest neighbors, and where its edges lie, with or without a canonical label.
