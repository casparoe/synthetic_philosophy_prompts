My background is in robotics and control engineering; I am fluent in linear dynamical systems, state-space representations, controllability and observability Gramians, disturbance dynamics, and the separation principle in stochastic control, but I have no formal training in philosophy. I would like you to restate the core Stoic position—namely, the division between what lies within our power, such as judgments, impulses, and the use of impressions, and what lies outside it, such as the body, external goods, reputation, and the outcomes of action—using precisely the vocabulary of control theory. Map the agent’s will onto the control input matrix, the external environment onto plant dynamics with disturbance and noise terms, and the Stoic injunction to concern oneself only with the former onto a normative claim about optimal control policy design. Be technically honest rather than metaphorical: show me exactly which mathematical structures correspond to the Stoic categories, and do not soften the translation to make it sound more philosophical than the equations allow.

Which elements translate cleanly—does the idea that one should not attempt to stabilize an uncontrollable mode map onto the Stoic refusal to worry about outcomes, and does the separation of estimation from control mirror the Stoic separation of judgment from result?
