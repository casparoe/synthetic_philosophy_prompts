Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain, rhyme scheme ABA with two refrains—about Maxwell’s demon not as a perpetual-motion hoax but as a figure for the physical cost of knowing. Let the first refrain be “the door opens” and the second “the record burns,” so that the form’s compulsory repetition enacts Landauer’s principle: every sorting of molecules into fast and slow carries an inseparable debt of erasure. The opening tercets should build the naive image of the demon at the gate, measuring velocity without effort; let the volta fall at the fourth tercet, where the reader sees that the demon is not stopped by mechanical friction but by the entropy of its own memory. In the closing quatrain, bring the refrains together so that opening and burning become a single motion. Do not resolve the tension into either triumph or defeat—leave the demon still working, still paying. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of physics recognizes the link between information and thermodynamic cost, and precise enough that it does not misrepresent the principle as mere metaphor.
