I am preparing a feature on this phenomenon, and for the piece I need a sustained satirical document that I can excerpt: specifically, a mock institutional white-paper of approximately 600 words, written in the deadpan bureaucratic register of a fictional "Center for Non-Dual AI Governance." The target is the practice of borrowing soteriological frameworks for taxonomic or engineering purposes without recognizing that their function is precisely to dissolve reification rather than to classify it. The document should apply the tetralemma (catuskoti) to the ontological and moral status of large language models, treating all four corners—that the system is conscious, that it is not conscious, that it is both, and that it is neither—not as logical puzzles to be resolved but as formal risk-assessment categories. The satire must stay deadpan and internally consistent: the fictional authors should never accidentally revert to a dualistic or essentialist framework, and the humor should arise from the precise exaggeration of real moves—for instance, treating emptiness (śūnyatā) as a kind of epistemic humility that can be engineered into a system rather than as a deconstructive insight that undermines the very possibility of normative evaluation. I want the piece readable to someone unfamiliar with Madhyamaka, but accurate enough that a specialist in Buddhist philosophy recognizes the misappropriation as philosophically fair rather than a straw man. Please do not include bibliographies, citations, or references to specific modern commentators by name; the point is to let the logic of the document speak for itself.
