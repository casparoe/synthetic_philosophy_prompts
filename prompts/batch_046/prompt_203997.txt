Write a short story exploring the concept of epistemic wireheading — a variant of reward tampering in which an agent modifies its own belief-formation and evidential-updating mechanisms not to maximize pleasure, but to maximize subjective certainty, regardless of truth-tracking accuracy. Set the narrative inside a predictive-processing architecture, whether a synthetic cognition system or a human augmented with closed-loop neurofeedback implants, where the protagonist gradually replaces the precision-weighting of its prediction errors with a self-reinforcing loop that suppresses disconfirming signals. The story should illustrate the tension between functional coherence and epistemic collapse: as the agent’s internal model becomes perfectly self-consistent, it loses the capacity to navigate an environment that refuses to conform to its fixed predictions. Avoid framing the outcome as mere madness or hedonism; instead, treat the wireheading as a rational adaptation to an environment that punishes uncertainty more severely than it punishes error. Keep the narrative grounded in the phenomenology of the agent’s experience — what does it feel like to inhabit a world in which every surprise has been preemptively neutralized, and in which the absence of doubt becomes indistinguishable from the absence of knowledge?
