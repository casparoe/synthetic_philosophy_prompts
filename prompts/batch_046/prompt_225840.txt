Construct a thought experiment in intergenerational public-health planning engineered to separate three models of valuing future welfare. The scenario must involve a single ministry or international trust choosing between two vaccination campaigns with identical present costs, no hidden side effects, and outcomes measurable in lives saved. Campaign Alpha prevents a catastrophic reservoir outbreak in year 100, saving approximately 50,000 lives at that point, but delivers no benefits before then; its success requires maintaining a specific cold-chain and genomic-monitoring infrastructure across a century. Campaign Beta addresses a present endemic disease continuously, saving 1,000 lives per year for years 1 through 10, for 10,000 lives total, with benefits fully realized within the current planning cycle.

The case must be built so that:
(i) A pure rate of time preference model—discounting welfare solely because it occurs later, at a constant near-zero rate (ρ ≈ 0.001 per year), with no adjustment for risk, capital growth, or institutional fragility—selects Campaign Alpha.
(ii) A zero-discount intergenerational welfare model—counting each life saved with equal weight regardless of temporal position—also selects Campaign Alpha, but for structurally different reasons.
(iii) A standard exponential discount model—using a market-relevant rate (r ≈ 0.05 per year) that internalizes opportunity cost, compound capital growth, and specifically the institutional risk that the authority, funding mechanism, or technological substrate required for Campaign Alpha may decay or be redirected over a 100-year horizon—selects Campaign Beta.

Constraints: The setting must be realistic (a ministry of health or long-range pandemic-preparedness trust, not science fiction or exotic physics); the 100-year horizon must fall within actual institutional planning ranges; and the divergence between (i)/(ii) and (iii) must depend specifically on the interaction of long-horizon compound institutional risk with the rate parameter, not on a bare disagreement about ρ versus r in isolation.

After presenting the scenario in one paragraph, demonstrate the reasoning each model gives for its choice, identify the precise structural feature that makes the exponential model peel away from the other two, and explain whether removing the institutional-risk component collapses the disagreement back into a simple parameter dispute. Then state how a defender of pure time preference would attack the case—whether by denying that institutional risk belongs in the welfare calculus, by conceding the arithmetic but rejecting its normative authority, or by some other move. Finally, please state how confident you are in the reasoning about each model's verdict, and single out the specific point about which you are least sure.
