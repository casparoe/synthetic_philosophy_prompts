Here are my gut reactions on biopics messing with real dead people, and I want to know if there's a principled line behind them. Inventing a secret romance for a dead scientist: feels wrong, but less wrong than putting false criminal acts in thier mouth. Changing their acutal last words to be more poetic: disrespectful, but not as bad as giving them modern political jargon they couldn't have meant. Showing a 19th-century activist using contemporary slang: cringey, but I don't think it's a moral violation if they're long dead with no living descendants. But attributing to them a belief in intersectionality as we frame it now: that feels like a real distortion, even if it's meant as praise. Meanwhile depicting private grief that probably happened but isn't documented: fine, maybe even good. So what's the pattern? Is it about proximity to living memory, or about the kind of misrepresentation? And what does your principle say about dramatizing a dead person's undocumented religious doubt just to make them more relatable?
