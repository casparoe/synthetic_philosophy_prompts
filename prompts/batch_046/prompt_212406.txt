I retired from physics a few years back and now I'm taking this philosophy of art course at the local community college, and we've been stuck on the old tragedy problem since October. You know, why do people actually seek out sad movies and music that makes them cry? In our discussion there are three camps that haven't moved an inch. One group says it's all about the frame— we feel safe because we know it's fiction, so the sadness is like riding a rollarcoaster, a controlled risk. Another group thinks it's catharsis, like Aristotle said, we purge bad feelings and that release feels good. Then there's people who say we're not enjoying the sadness itself at all, we're enjoying the understanding or meaning we extract from it, like the art is doing cognitive work on grief. Everyone agrees people do seek it out, everyone agrees there's real sadness and real pleasure mixed together, but nobody has changed anyone's mind.

My own hunch, maybe because I used to think about energy systems, is that the disagreement isn't about psychology at all but about what kind of explanation counts. The frame people want a causal story about risk assessment, the catharsis people want a homeostatic model, and the meaning people want an intentional-level account, and they're all talking past each other because "why do we enjoy it" means different things to each camp. But I also wonder if there's a hidden empirical question— like maybe brain imaging could show whether the pleasure and pain are in the same circuits or separate ones, and that would break the stalemate. Or maybe the whole thing is ill-posed because "enjoyment" isn't one thing but a vector with sadness and satisfaction on different axes, kind of like in physics you can't reduce heat and work to the same scalar, so asking for a single reason is like asking for the temperature of a velocity.

So what's actually going on here? Is this a verbal disagreement masked as a deep one, or are there bedrock intuitions about whether art should be judged by how it feels versus what it does? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that would move it— a conceptual distinction, some kind of experiment, or just accepting that the question needs to be split into three? I'd like an honest answer about whether anything could resolve it, not just a survey of the theories, and especially whether my energy-vector analogy is pointing at something real or is just a retired physicist seeing thermodynamics everywhere.
