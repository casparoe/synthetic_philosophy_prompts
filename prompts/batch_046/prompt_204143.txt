I want to defend a pretty strong reading of Whitehead's educational rhythm — romance, then precision, then generalization — not just as a teaching trick but as a claim about how any mind (human or not) actually acquires knowledge. The idea is that if you skip the romance phase you don't just get bored students, you get knowledge that literally can't be intergrated into experience, what he call's inert ideas. But I'm worried I'm overreading his metaphysics into pedagogy, maybe forcing acutal occasions into a classroom model that doesn't fit. What would actually count against this view? Would it be empirical — like studies showing people learn fine without romance? Or conceptual — that integration into experience is too vague to do work? Or metaphysical — that minds don't really pass through phases at all? I want an honest map of what's exposed here and whether defenders of the rhythm idea would concede any of these lines of attack, or if the view is well insulated and that's a problem in itself.
