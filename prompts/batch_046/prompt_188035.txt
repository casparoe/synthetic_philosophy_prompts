I sit on a regional grant panel that funds community programmes claiming to improve moral behaviour and social trust. Proposals come from both religious congregations and secular civic bodies, yet we have no framework for comparing them. Please design a reusable evaluation procedure — an ordered sequence of questions with clear stopping rules and explicit points where judgement, not fact, must decide the outcome — that a non-philosopher panel member could apply to any proposal. The procedure must distinguish three senses in which people might become "better": observable behavioural compliance, stable dispositional virtue, and the capacity for vulnerability-based trust rather than mere predictable cooperation. It should also address whether secular organisations can sustain the latter form of trust without transcendent grounding. Apply the procedure to two cases: a secular association that publishes all members' ethical failures to enforce radical transparency, and a religious group that addresses failures through private confession and ritual restoration, keeping no public record. Flag explicitly where the procedure cannot settle the comparison and demands a value choice from the panel. Write your response in British English, concisely but rigorously.
