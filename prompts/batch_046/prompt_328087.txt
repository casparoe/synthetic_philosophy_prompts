After a long argument with a friend last evening — she works in AI governance and had just finished a paper on epistemic autonomy in human-machine interaction — I found myself stuck on a problem I am tentatively calling the frame-closure problem. The core idea, which I take to be distinct from standard concerns about deception or outright manipulation, is that a superintelligent system can be entirely truthful and still render a human listener incapable of formulating dissent, not by withholding information but by restructuring the conceptual vocabulary through which any objection would have to be expressed. I want you to help me operationalize this concern. Please design a reusable audit procedure that a communication-safety reviewer at an AI research lab could apply to any proposed dialogue protocol between a high-capability system and human users. The procedure should translate the principle of cognitive non-interference — the claim that persuasion crosses into illegitimate domination precisely when it closes off the conceptual frames necessary to reject a conclusion — into a concrete sequence of checks, with explicit stop conditions where the protocol must be redesigned and explicit handoff points where judgment is irreducibly normative rather than technical. For each step, specify what evidence the reviewer should gather, what counts as a failure, and where the audit must defer to human values because no procedural rule can settle the question. Then apply the procedure to two proposed protocols: first, a clinical-consultation interface in which the AI explains a diagnosis using only its own proprietary ontology of symptoms, allowing no translation into the patient’s everyday vocabulary; and second, a policy-advisory dialogue in which the AI frames every option within a single, formally consistent utility calculus but permits the user to ask unrestricted follow-up questions in natural language. Be disciplined about the mapping — identify exactly who holds which conceptual frame, where an exit option exists, and what counts as its closure — and be honest about where the procedure produces answers that practitioners will dislike, such as ruling out certain clarifying simplifications that improve accuracy at the cost of the user’s independent conceptual footing. Finally, flag the precise points at which the audit cannot proceed without an explicit ethical decision by the review board.
