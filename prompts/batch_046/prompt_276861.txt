Consider the standard clinical protocol for dissociative identity disorder, in which therapists guide patients toward integrating distinct alter personalities into one continuous autobiographical narrative. What philosophical conception of the self does that integrationist goal presuppose? Does it tacitly adopt Dennett's idea of the self as a center of narrative gravity, treating integration as the consolidation of a single gravitational center? Or does it assume a pre-existing, substantial self that has been fragmented and must be restored? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates, evaluate its coherence, and specify how the therapeutic protocol would have to change if we replaced that view with a pluralist framework in which each alter is treated as an equally legitimate center of narrative gravity with no normative obligation to merge.
