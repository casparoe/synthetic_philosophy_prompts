Me and my study group cooked up this argument and now we can't see the exit. (1) To be rationally justified in holding any beleif, I need to have cleared away every relevant doubt. (2) Take any doubt — say, whether my eyes are deceiving me. That splits neatly into two smaller doubts: whether the lighting is weird, and whether my visual cortex is misfiring. (3) So clearing every doubt means I first have to clear half of them, then half of what's left, ad infinitum. (4) That's a Zeno-style infinite regress of prior steps. (5) Nobody can complete infinitely many steps in finite time. (6) So I never finish clearing doubts, hence I never have a rational belief. (7) But I obviously do have some. (8) So one of the premises is wrong. I want to keep (1) if I can, because it feels right that you shouldn't believe things with live doubts hanging over them. Which step should I actually reject, and what does giving it up cost me?
