I have no formal academic training, but I have been thinking independently about Dennett’s notion of the self as a centre of narrative gravity, and I have developed a half-formed concept I am calling narrative shear: the resistance that prevents two overlapping life-stories within one biological lifespan from collapsing into a single gravitational centre. I would like this turned into a polished, two-page research proposal for a six-month interdisciplinary workshop fellowship. The precise question is whether we can formalise a measurable notion of narrative shear that distinguishes ordinary personal development from genuine fractures in narrative identity, without relying on clinical categories of dissociation. The proposal should explain exactly why Dennett’s original framework leaves this gap open—specifically, his implicit assumption that one organism hosts exactly one centre and that continuity is primarily a matter of biological persistence—then set out the method I would actually follow month by month: refining the concept and constructing a taxonomy of first-person accounts drawn from published memoirs and reflective essays (not clinical literature); analysing whether the taxonomy maps onto intuitive judgments about identity continuity; and testing whether the resulting framework can predict, in hypothetical cases, when a person should be treated as one continuing self versus two sheared centres. It should also state clearly what result would count as failure—for instance, if narrative shear proves indistinguishable from ordinary belief revision, or if the taxonomy fails to yield inter-subjectively stable classifications. The scope must remain realistic for a non-specialist working part-time over six months, with no expectation of empirical laboratory work. Please write in formal British English, do not include a bibliography or citation list, and avoid requests for further reading that I would need web access to fulfil.
