(1) Hacking's looping effect: classifying people as K changes their behavior and self-conception, so the kind K is "interactive" rather than "indifferent."
(2) For strongly interactive kinds, there is no stable, pre-classification "true nature" that the classification tracks — the classification partly constructs what it describes.
(3) Psychiatric categories (autism, ADHD, BPD, etc.) are strongly interactive: diagnosis reshapes identity, treatment access, and even symptom presentation.
(4) So psychiatric categories don't track a pre-existing natural kind.
(5) If there's no natural kind being tracked, then saying "X really has ADHD" can't mean "X instantiates the natural kind ADHD."
(6) The only remaining interpretation is that "X really has ADHD" means "X satisfies the diagnostic criteria," which are themselves shaped by the loop.
(7) But then being "wrong" about a diagnosis can only mean "misapplied the criteria" — there's no deeper error like "missing the true underlying condition."
(8) Yet we routinely think psychiatrists can be wrong in a deeper way, e.g., misreading grief as depression or missing bipolar because of gender bias — errors that seem to miss something real about the patient.
(9) So either we abandon the idea that deep diagnostic error is possible, or we deny that psychiatric kinds are fully constructed by looping.
(10) But (3) is empirically solid, and (1)-(2) are definitional of Hacking's framework. So the only escape is denying the slide from (4) to (6) — but i don't see how to block it without sneaking essentialism back in.
(11) Therefore psychiatric diagnosis is never genuinely correct or incorrect, just a coordination tool with no truth-value.

this feels like it proves too much. i want to keep (1)-(3) because the empirical story about looping in psychiatry seems right, and i also want to keep (8) because clinical error feels real. so i guess i'm committed to rejecting something in the middle, probably (5) or (6). but when i try to reject (5) i end up saying psychiatric kinds are "real but constructed" which sounds like word salad. tell me which step is actually the weak link, why it looked plausible, and what rejecting it costs me. does it force me to accept some kind of weak essentialism, or can i keep a non-essentialist realism about mental disorder?
