Read Inception through metaphyscial disjunctivism—the view that veridical seeing and hallucination share no mental factor at all, so the argument from illusion fails. The movie treats dreams and waking life as subjectively identical except for Cobb's totem, which sounds exactly like common-factor theory: same experience, different cause. But I'm wondering if the totem is actually doing disjunctive work—letting Cobb directly know he's awake—or if it's just an empirical crutch that leaves the underlying mental state unchanged. And with nested dreams, if your "good case" check is itself dreamed, does that collapse the distinction disjunctivists want?
