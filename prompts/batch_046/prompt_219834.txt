Can you disambiguate the slogan into its distinct, precisely stated theses? For each reading, specify what kind of “function” or “functional explanation” is in view (computational role, causal organization, information integration, predictive-processing dynamics, etc.); whether the claim is that experience is logically independent of function, causally independent, epistemically unexplained by function, or metaphysically identical yet conceptually irreducible; whether the “no matter the complexity” clause introduces a quantitative claim about scaling laws, a qualitative claim that the mechanism is of the wrong kind, or a modal claim about all physically possible systems; and whether the thesis is a near-platitude, a substantive and defensible position, or actually false.

Then report which of these readings would genuinely support dismissing the possibility that a sufficiently scaled transformer could be phenomenally conscious, and which are being smuggled in without argument. In particular, does any reading tacitly depend on further assumptions—such as that consciousness requires biological substrate, non-computational dynamics, or a specific first-person accessibility that artificial architectures necessarily lack—that need separate defense rather than being carried by the slogan itself? I want a menu of interpretations with verdicts, so I can locate exactly where the disagreement lies rather than receiving a single gloss.
