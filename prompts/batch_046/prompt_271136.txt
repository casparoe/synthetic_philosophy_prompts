hey — i'm drafting a novel about a writers room that collapses over a single plot hole, and i got sidetracked into this logic thing called the discursive dilemma. i think i accidentally proved that committee-written fiction is literally impossible, which can't be right since my pilot was writen by four people. here's the chain. tell me which link breaks and what i pay for cutting it.

(1) A writters' room is a collective agent that holds beliefs about what's true inside the story-world.
(2) Rationality demands that this collective belief-set be consistent.
(3) For any agregation rule — premise-based or conclusion-based — with three or more writers judging premises P and Q and conclusion R, there's a profile of opinions where the rule forces the group to accept R even though every writer rejects R, or reject R even though every writer accepts it.
(4) So whatever rule the room uses, some plot-logic profile will inject a contradiction into the collective story-world that no individual intended.
(5) A protagonist's psychology is fixed by the collective belief-set about the world; if the world-logic is contradictory, the character's mental states become contradictory.
(6) But real writers' rooms produce psycholgically consistent characters across rotating staff.
(7) Therefore committee fiction should be impossible, yet it plainly exists.
(8) The only escape is denying (1) — that the room counts as one agent — but then there's no single author to hold copywrite or answer for plot holes.

i'd really like to hang on to (6) and (1), but (5) feels like the sleight-of-hand. which step should i drop, and what does dropping it cost?
