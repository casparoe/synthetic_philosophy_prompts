I'm developing an evaluation framework for behavioral criteria of mind-attribution in cases where direct access to internal architecture is impossible and the candidate entities span biological, synthetic, and collective substrates—ranging from locked-in human communicators and non-human animals to large language models and distributed swarm systems. Before comparing specific proposals, I want the full specification of adequacy.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate behavioral criterion for attributing minds must deliver—the edge cases it must adjudicate correctly (e.g., entities that match human linguistic output but lack persistent self-models, collective agents that solve problems without individual cognition, and agents operating on radically different temporal scales); the distinctions it must preserve rather than collapse (such as between attributive stance and ontological status, between functional equivalence and phenomenal equivalence, and between passing a domain-specific test and possessing generalizable cognitive capacity); the puzzles it must dissolve or explain (including the other-minds problem under substrate heterogeneity, the dual risk of anthropomorphic over-attribution and chauvinistic under-attribution, and the tension between linguistic and embodied behavioral channels as evidence); and the methodological constraints it must respect (for instance, not presupposing biological ontology, not requiring direct neural observation, and maintaining empirical falsifiability).

For each desideratum, state the motivating case or consideration behind it. Then flag which pairs you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable—such as whether complete substrate neutrality can coexist with a demand for explanatory adequacy about internal causal structure, or whether avoiding anthropomorphism can be maintained while using human-like linguistic behavior as primary evidence. Finally, identify the single desideratum that, if taken seriously, most severely undermines the classic Turing-test formulation as a sufficient criterion, with brief reasoning. Keep the weight on the criteria and their justification; reference particular theories only to illustrate a failure mode if needed.
