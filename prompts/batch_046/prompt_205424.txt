Write a speaker-prep sheet for a philosophy-of-action workshop presentation. The speaker will defend the following thesis: Kavka's toxin puzzle should not be solved by collapsing rational intention into rational action, nor by treating it as a mere variant of Newcomb's problem; instead, it reveals that rational agency operates with two distinct normative registers—strategic self-binding at the planning stage (where forming the intention to drink is rational because it secures the reward) and present-directed execution at the acting stage (where actually drinking is irrational because the reward is already secured and the cost is now unredeemed). The speaker rejects both the "single-rationality" view that forces the agent either to drink or to reject the deal, and Bratman's claim that prior intentions create non-instrumental reasons for action, proposing instead a two-register framework in which commitment-rationality and execution-rationality are normatively separable.

The audience is specific: two Davidsonians committed to a belief-desire model of action and skeptical of "planning" as a distinct mental category; one Bratman specialist who treats intentions as practical commitments that generate reasons; one decision theorist who maps every puzzle onto causal versus evidential decision theory and knows the Newcomb literature cold; and one cognitive scientist who studies precommitment mechanisms in addiction and will treat the puzzle as a folk-psychology version of a Ulysses contract.

Please draft the Q&A prep sheet in American English. Order the objections roughly as they will actually arrive, phrased the way each of these audience members would phrase them. For each objection, note what the speaker should concede, what they should hold, and where the reply is weakest. Include the specific weakest point in the speaker's own two-register position that they should prepare to defend rather than hope the audience misses. Finally, include the one objection the speaker is hoping nobody asks, with a brief note on why it is especially damaging. Keep the reply notes concise—suitable for a live defense, not a full paper. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation requests.
