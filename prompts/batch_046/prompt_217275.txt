Write a short story about an "asynchronous Thomson lamp"—a device toggled not by one accelerating agent, but by an infinite sequence of independent agents, each assigned to act at time 1 − 1/2ⁿ and able to observe the lamp's state only through a signal delayed by a fixed epsilon. Because of this delay, no agent ever sees the result of the immediately preceding toggle, yet by t = 1 every agent has acted exactly once with no final toggle remaining. Center the narrative on two philosophers debating whether the lamp has a determinate state at t = 1: one argues that the well-ordering of actions alone guarantees determinacy regardless of observational gaps, while the other insists that determinacy requires local causal continuity across adjacent steps, which is broken in the limit. Keep the philosophical tension focused on whether a supertask can yield a definite outcome when information never propagates across neighboring actions, and avoid resolving the paradox by appealing to physical laws or formal mathematics alone.
