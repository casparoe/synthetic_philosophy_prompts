I defend a restricted account of special composition according to which parts form a genuine whole precisely when they exhibit functional interdependence that generates emergent causal powers not reducible to any subset of the parts, provided this interdependence is anchored in a continuous spatiotemporal material substrate—so a living organism qualifies, a scattered constellation does not, and a machine whose synthetic components are fully interchangeable without functional loss fails to qualify despite its apparent unity. What observations, arguments, formal results, or conceptual developments would count decisively against this position, and which would merely be awkward? In particular, address whether the substrate-continuity clause collapses into an undefended biological chauvinism, whether emergent causation can be specified without circular appeal to the very wholes it is meant to explain, and whether defenders of this family of views have insulated themselves through ad hoc qualifications to the point of unfalsifiability—if so, is that insulation a defect for a metaphysical claim of this kind? Distinguish what proponents of restricted mereology themselves concede as damaging from what critics allege, and be willing to conclude that the position is either genuinely exposed or problematically shielded. Please write your answer in British English.
