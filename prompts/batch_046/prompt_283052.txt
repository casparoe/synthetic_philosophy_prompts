(2) The other said thats cheating -- youre changing the preference profile just to save liberalism. Sen's point is about minimal liberty inside a fixed social-choice framework; if preferences adapt to rights, youre not testing liberalism vs Pareto efficiency, youre just describing preference formation.

(3) The first came back that even with fixed preferences, Sen needs the weird ranking where each person cares more about the other than themselves in the middle spot. In reality people rank their own autonomy first, so the liberal outcome is usually Pareto optimal anyway, and the paradox is just a logical curiosity with no real bite.

(4) The second replied that logical possibility is enough to block liberalism and Pareto being joint axioms. She also said shared digital spaces make those "weird" interdependencies totally normal -- people do care about what content is visible to others.
