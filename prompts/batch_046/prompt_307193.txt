Please compare the classical modal-epistemic treatment of logical omniscience—where an agent’s knowledge is closed under every logical consequence within her accessibility relation—with the resource-bounded revision that restricts closure only to inferences executable within a fixed computational bound. Focus your comparison on a specific tension: under the bounded model, two agents with identical doxastic states but different processing capacities are assigned divergent knowledge sets, despite being first-personally indistinguishable in what they take themselves to know. Does this show that the tractability restriction solves the omniscience problem only by relocating it to a metaphysics of cognitive architecture? And which framework ultimately does better justice to the intuition that a mathematician can rationally fail to know a remote theorem without being epistemically defective?
