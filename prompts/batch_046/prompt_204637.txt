Consider the institutional practice in modern clinical governance where an opaque predictive model—whose internal weights and feature interactions are not interpretable even by its designers—may be authorized to flag life-threatening conditions with accuracy exceeding that of human specialists, yet hospital protocol requires that a licensed physician deliver the diagnosis to the patient using only explanations drawn from established pathophysiological mechanisms, even when the physician knows those mechanisms are not what actually drove the model's prediction. What philosophical commitments about the relation between competence and comprehension does this policy presuppose? Does it presuppose that epistemic authority or moral accountability necessarily requires human-level comprehension, regardless of predictive success? Reconstruct the implicit theory of reasoning embedded in this arrangement: is the human physician functioning as a comprehension-bearing placeholder for a system that possesses competence without comprehension, thereby distributing the two capacities across separate agents? In doing so, does the practice echo Darwin's development of evolutionary theory in the absence of any mechanistic understanding of heredity, or Turing's recognition that a device can execute correct reasoning without grasping the content of its operations? Examine whether the practice is internally consistent—if comprehension is genuinely indispensable for clinical justification, why is predictive competence alone sufficient to initiate the diagnostic pathway; if predictive competence is sufficient, why must the delivered explanation be human-comprehensible rather than merely accurate? Finally, distinguish what this practice strictly presupposes from what it merely accommodates, and indicate where it is philosophically underdetermined: could it be sustained by a view that treats comprehension and competence as fully separable goods, or does it tacitly commit to a stronger claim about the necessary unity of understanding and justification within a single reasoning agent?
