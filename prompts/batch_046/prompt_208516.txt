I defend a deflationary account of the cross-cultural divergence in folk morality documented within WEIRD psychology: the differences are not deep disagreements about moral ontology but largely stem from a measurement artifact coupled with functional convergence. On this view, the binding foundations—loyalty, authority, and sanctity—are not genuinely moral categories in non-WEIRD populations but domain-specific social-coordination norms that Western survey instruments misclassify as moral, because WEIRD cognition tends to treat morality as a unified, overriding domain rather than a cluster of separate practical registers. Meanwhile, the apparent universality of harm and fairness does not reflect shared normative commitments but only that cooperative societies everywhere face structurally similar coordination problems, yielding convergent behavioral heuristics that Western frameworks then interpret as moral universals. Consequently, the divergence does not undermine moral objectivity; it reveals that Western moral psychology has projected its own domain architecture onto diverse normative systems. Play it straight: what would count against this interpretation? I want a map of its exposure—distinguish observations or arguments that would be merely awkward (for example, further cross-cultural variation in the parsing of harm) from those that would be genuinely damaging, such as evidence that binding norms in non-WEIRD societies are experienced with the same categorical, deontological force as harm norms, or that the functional-coordination explanation for harm and fairness cannot account for the specific phenomenology of moral obligation observed across cultures. Which of these potential defeaters would a committed defender of the deflationary view have to concede, and which could they absorb by further qualification? Has the view already been insulated—by redefining what counts as genuinely moral—into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that insulation a problem for a position that presents itself as explanatory rather than revisionary?
