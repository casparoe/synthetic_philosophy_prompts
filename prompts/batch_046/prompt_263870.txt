I am a novelist developing a short fiction piece about a conservator of immersive digital heritage who restores artifacts that have no physical substrate—only distributed sensory protocols accessed through temporary neural interfaces. In my story, an attacker corrupts one artifact not by deleting its data, but by remapping its haptic profile so that anyone who engages it experiences a precise, lingering absence where form should be. The conservator argues that a real object has been genuinely harmed and that the users suffer a corresponding injury; the attacker maintains that there is only altered stimulation, no object, and therefore no harm beyond subjective disappointment. Write a brief narrative—focused on a single restoration session—that renders this philosophical conflict through concrete sensory and emotional detail rather than explicit debate. The story should treat the virtual artifact as possessing its own kind of reality without reducing it to either pure illusion or physical analog, and it should leave the ontological question genuinely open.
