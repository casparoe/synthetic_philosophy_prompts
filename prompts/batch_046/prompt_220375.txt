Write the complete desiderata that a theory of diachronic rationality must satisfy to count as genuinely dissolving money-pump objections, not merely displacing them. Specify the phenomena it must cover: which patterns of intertemporal preference reversal are permissible, which constitute exploitability, and what distinction—if any—must be preserved between being dynamically inconsistent at a single moment and being vulnerable to a sequential exchange. Include the case of a framing-sensitive pump, in which an agent accepts a cyclical trade when it is decomposed into discrete steps but rejects the identical net exchange when presented as one compound transaction; a adequate theory must say whether the pump's normative force survives framing invariance or collapses into a presentation effect. List which constraints are non-negotiable (e.g., avoiding guaranteed loss, respecting the agent's own evaluative judgments at each stage) and which are preferences of particular camps (e.g., preserving the independence axiom across time, requiring Bayesian belief updating, or forbidding any preference change absent new evidence). Identify whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given standard results about dynamic consistency and strategic reasoning. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; a brief, secondary assessment of how standard Bayesian updating, resolute choice, and sophisticated-choice frameworks fare against the list is welcome, but do not make it the center of the answer.
