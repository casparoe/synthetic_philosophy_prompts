I'm writing a scene where a biologist debates reduction with a novelist friend, and I've noticed a patern in science writing I can't name. A researcher explains some big behaviour — consciousness, culture, whatever — mostly through small-scale mechanisms, then slips in an 'except for the subjective bit' or 'except in living systems'. The exception isn't hidden; it's admitted right up front. But nobody adds them up. Nobody says 'that's four leaks now, your reduction is failing'. They just sit there, parked, and the theory carries on untouched. I know emergence is part of it, and Lakatos's protective belt sounds close, but this feels different because the exceptions are openly acknowledged rather than defended away. Is there an established concept for this specific move — the slow build-up of individully reasonable, admitted residues that never get summed into a critique? If not, what should I call it, and how exactly is it different from ordinary emergence-talk or from a standard auxiliary hypothesis?
