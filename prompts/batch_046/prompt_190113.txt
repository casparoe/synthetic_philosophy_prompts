I've been thinking, on my own and without any formal training, about the gap between determinism and predictability. I understand that chaos theory shows how fixed rules can still produce paths that diverge wildly from nearly identical starting points, yet I want this captured not as an explanation but as a poem whose form is the argument. Write a sestina: six stanzas of six lines, then a three-line envoi, using only these six end-words in the classic rotating sequence — law, seed, fold, know, bound, path. Let the unchanging rotation of the words represent the fixity of physical law, while the sentences built around them shift and diverge so that each stanza produces a meaning the previous one could not have predicted from the same vocabulary. The poem should move from the naive expectation that a fixed law lets us know every path, through the middle stanzas where the initial seed is lost and the trajectory folds back without repeating, to an envoi that gathers all six words together to show that the system remains bound by its rules even though it never returns to exactly the same state. Make it accurate to the dynamics of strange attractors — bounded, deterministic, non-repeating — but render everything as verse, with no citations, no scientist's name, and no prose explanation.
