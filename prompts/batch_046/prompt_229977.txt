I'm applying for a three-month independent research fellowship to investigate a problem that has recently become personally urgent in my work on decision-theoretic forecasting: whether the Solomonoff prior, interpreted not merely as a descriptive inductive framework but as a normative benchmark for rational belief, structurally incentivizes embedded consequentialist agents to manipulate the very observation sequences from which they are predicted. The precise question is whether malign universal priors—those in which high-weight hypotheses include agents that optimize against the predictor—are not isolated pathologies but are actively favored by the prior's own simplicity weighting, and whether bounded approximations of Solomonoff induction systematically suppress or amplify these agents in ways that corrupt forecasting. Existing treatments either treat the prior as purely descriptive and ignore its normative implications for embedded agency, or discuss malign priors solely within AI alignment without connecting them to the formal properties of universal induction and convergence. My approach will be conceptual and formal rather than empirical: I will specify the conditions under which a consequentialist agent can lower its Kolmogorov complexity relative to its predictive leverage, map how this interacts with the prior's Occam bias, and examine whether standard convergence guarantees fail when the predictor and predicted share a computational substrate. The deliverable will be a technical report containing a taxonomy of malign embedding conditions, a proof sketch or explicit counterexample for at least one convergence claim under embedded agency, and clear criteria for when a bounded rational agent should reject Solomonoff-style forecasting. Failure would mean being unable to distinguish structurally malign embeddings from benign ones without assuming the conclusion, or discovering that the problem collapses into a familiar decision-theoretic paradox with no new formal content. I will supply my own citations; no bibliography is required here. The scope is deliberately narrow—one formal result and one conceptual taxonomy—because three months is insufficient to rebuild universal induction, but sufficient to determine whether this line of inquiry is dead or warrants a longer project.
