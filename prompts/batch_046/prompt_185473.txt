A lot of workfare defenders compare benefits to a gym membership: if you dont show up and do the reps, you shouldnt keep the card, so people who refuse jobs are just freeloading and the state is right to cut them off. They use this to argue that conditionality is fair, not cruel. Can you map the parts exactly -- gym equals welfare office, fee equals taxes, staff equals caseworkers -- then show where it breaks, especially around whether people can actually exit the gym, whether the reps are real jobs or made-up hurdles, and whether all the paperwork is meant to check need or is actually designed to filter applicants out on purpose. Does the idea of an undeserving poor survive once those gaps are on the table? Bottom line first, then the audit. Keep it under 250 words, American English please. Also theres this claim that the forms themselves are the point, not the verification -- does that kill the analogy?
