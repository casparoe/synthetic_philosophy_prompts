I'm trying to figure out if my views on the seperateness of persons objection are actually consistent or if I'm just holding contradictory stuff without noticing. Here's my profile:

- I think Rawls's basic point is metaphysical: there's no "super-person" experiencing summed welfare, so treating people like cells in a body is wrong.
- But I also think total utilitarianism avoids the problem because summing values is just arithmetic, not consciousness-merging.
- I'm totally fine with tradeoffs inside one life—like suffering now for a bigger payoff later—even though my future self feels kind of like a different person.
- I don't think there's any rigorous way to compare utility across different people—no common currency.
- In small groups with real shared decision-making (like a family planning together), I think it's okay to talk about "our collective welfare" and average across members if everyone genuinely consents.
- The deepest reason separateness matters, in my view, is distributive: each person is an independent moral claimant, not a resource for other people's ends.

Can you go through these and audit them? I want the tensions ranked by severity, with the cheapest fix for each. Also tell me which single revision would clean up the most mess. And please flag any pairs that only look contradictory but actually dissolve on closer look—I don't want to fix fake problems. I'm writing this up for a reading group so I need to know what I'm actually committed to.
