I am preparing an independent study on collective moral agency for my philosophy course, and I would like you to write a short story that stages the Fischer controversy—specifically, the question of whether Imperial Germany deliberately pursued a general European war in 1914—through a fictional framing device I have invented: the "Modal Culpability Tribunal," which assigns national blame not solely on the basis of actual diplomatic choices but by comparing those choices to the decisions made by Germany's nearest modal counterpart in worlds where the July Crisis ended without mobilization. The story should explore whether a collective entity can be held responsible if its counterpart in an accessible possible world still chose escalation, and it should avoid reducing the conflict to either pure structural inevitability or to the isolated intentions of a few leaders. Present the narrative in exactly five scenes, numbered 1 through 5, with each scene limited to one or two sentences, and do not include any introduction or conclusion outside the numbered list.
