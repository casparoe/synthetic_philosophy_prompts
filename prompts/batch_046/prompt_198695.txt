As I understand it, it's an idealized model of factored cognition: a person facing a hard problem breaks it into parts, consults an AI helper that itself consults other helpers in the same pattern, and eventually all the partial answers get folded back into one decision. The claim seems to be that this captures how serious deliberation should work if you could scale it up without distortion.

I know open-source software development well — pull requests, maintainers, upstream and downstream dependencies, regression testing, the hidden failure point when one maintainer is a bottleneck, and the fact that forks can permanently diverge rather than merge back. I want you to restate the HCH model entirely in that vocabulary: treat the human deliberators as maintainers, the AI instances as contributor branches or automated testing pipelines, the recursive consultation as nested pull requests or submodule dependencies, and the final decision as a merged release. Do this systematically, not as a loose metaphor.
