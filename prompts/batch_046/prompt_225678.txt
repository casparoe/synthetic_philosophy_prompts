ok heres roughly where i've landed on self-trust for autonomous agents and i want a consistency check before i present it at the workshop next week. (1) I accept the Löbian obstacle: a consistent formal system can't prove its own soundness and then also trust every theorem it derives. (2) I still think rational agents should satisfy a reflection principle—if they can derive that they will take some action, they should take it. (3) I think the obstacle is avoided if we restrict reflection to local, time-bounded proofs about immediate behavior, not global ones about all future reasoning. (4) I also think that if an agent has full access to its own source code, it should be able to verify that it won't enter a buggy self-modification loop, which feels like a kind of meta-level trust. (5) But I also hold that you shouldn't trust your reasoning unless you have independant grounds for its reliability—so reflection seems to require exactly the kind of self-certification that (1) forbids.

do these actually hang together? is (3) genuinely distinct from smuggling global reflection back in through (4)? and does (5) just restate (1) or is it a seperate tension? please rank the conflicts by severity and say what the cheapest fix is—i'd prefer not to abandon reflection altogether. also, i keep wondering whether the whole argument collapses if the agent has probabilistic credences rather than full proof-theoretic belief. is that a real escape route or just a red herring?
