I'm designing a graduate seminar that treats Schrödinger's Vedanta-inspired metaphysics of consciousness as a live conceptual framework rather than a historical footnote. He argued that individual minds are not ontologically separate substances but localized manifestations of a single, undivided consciousness—a view he tied to the Upanishadic identity of Atman and Brahman. Recently, a colleague in physics insisted that this is merely biographical color with no bearing on philosophy of mind, which made me wonder whether we're missing genuinely open conceptual territory. In particular, I'm drawn to a framing that sounds strange but might be productive: if consciousness is fundamentally unified, then the standard "hard problem" may be misdiagnosed. The task might not be explaining how separate brains generate separate experiences, but rather explaining how one continuous field comes to present itself as fractured into subjective perspectives—something closer to an epistemology of differentiation than to a genesis of mind from matter.

Given that working hypothesis, I need to decide where limited intellectual effort should actually go. There seem to be several competing lines of inquiry: whether quantum-mechanical formalisms can be interpreted coherently without presupposing pluralistic ontology; how the problem of other minds transforms if mental separation is derivative rather than primitive; whether contemporary predictive-processing and global-workspace theories of cognition implicitly rely on the very individuation that Schrödinger rejected; and what ethical or epistemic consequences follow if one's own awareness is genuinely continuous with that of others. Some of these feel like dead ends that depend on premises the unity view undermines; others feel underexplored but tractable without specialized laboratory apparatus.

Could you rank the three or four most important open questions or lines of conceptual work in this space—taking Schrödinger's unity thesis seriously but not defensively—and defend the ordering? I want to understand which questions are genuinely upstream (resolving them would restructure the others), which are tractable for philosophers without empirical ML or neuroscience training, and which are likely overrated because they smuggle in the pluralistic assumptions the view calls into question.
