I've been taking a course on moral psychology and I'm trying to map out what I actually think about blame and resentment, kind of in a Strawsonian vein but with a twist I'm not sure about. Here are my gut verdicts on concrete cases. If a close friend promises to help me move then bails because they're depressed and overwhelmed, I feel hurt, but resentment seems wrong — like it misses the point entirely. A stranger cuts me off in traffic and flips me off: full-blown resentment feels totally fitting. Someone with Tourette's involuntarily shouts an insult at me: zero resentment, even though the words still sting. A child steals candy because they don't grasp ownership yet: no blame, just correction needed. A CEO knowingly destroys thousands of lives for a stock bump: strong resentment, clearly appropriate. Now the tricky ones. A person raised in an abusive cult that literally teaches violence toward outsiders is virtuous attacks me, then seems genuinely baffled and remorseful once they understand it's wrong — I feel a flash of resentment, but I also think maybe I shouldn't? Meanwhile, an AI hiring system rejects me because the engineers were negligent about bias, but no individual had any ill will; I feel something structurally like resentment toward 'the system,' though I'm not sure if that's a real reactive attitude or just frustration wearing a costume. Is there a coherent principle — maybe about quality of will, shared moral community membership, or what I'm calling 'moral addressability' — that gets all of these, or close? If not, which verdict should I drop? Please also predict what it says about a case I haven't listed, like resenting a sleepwalker who accidentally damages your property, and be honest about which judgments it can't accomodate. I keep flip-flopping on the cult case, so tell me if that tension is real or if I'm just being inconsistient.
