Before I evaluate particular accounts of internal and external reasons, I want the full adequacy specification for a theory in this area. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of practical reason must deliver if it is to make sense of the internal/external distinction — the phenomena it must explain, the distinctions it must preserve, the boundary cases it must resolve — with the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind each requirement. Include specifically: (i) standard Williams-type cases in which an agent’s present motivations either align with or diverge from what they seem to have reason to do; (ii) reasons across motivational discontinuity, such as radical value change, recovery from addiction, or deliberate self-modification, without collapsing into arbitrary presentism or implausible external imposition; (iii) the distinction between having a reason, having sufficient reason, and being rationally required; (iv) whether collective or distributed agency can generate reasons that are not reducible to the internal reasons of each member; and (v) what the theory says about reasons for agents whose motivational structures are partly designed or artificially constructed. For each desideratum, indicate whether it is negotiable or non-negotiable, and flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Then briefly score the main positions — Williams-style internalism, normative externalism, and hybrid or two-level views — against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves. Finally, separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
