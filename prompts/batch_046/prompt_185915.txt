question: Explain how hyperbolic discounting is meant to illuminate akrasia, then evaluate whether Davidson's ATC/prima-facie distinction survives if we tweak the quasi-hyperbolic model so the present-bias parameter β only "activates" after a 48-hour delay (call it delayed-activation present bias). In this framing, is weakness of will still an intra-agent failure, or does it become intertemporal conflict between planner and doer?

student answer (approx 450 words):

Davidson argues akrasia occurs when an agent acts against her all-things-considered judgment. He distinguishes ATC judgments from prima facie ones, and weakness of will is following the latter despite the former. Hyperbolic discounting gives a mechanistic story: people use βδ for future rewards, so as a reward gets closer its value jumps, causing preference reversal. This maps neatly onto Davidson—your ATC judgment says "exercise" but at t=0 the immediate reward of not exercising gets weighted by 1 instead of βδ, so you reverse.

If β only activates after 48 hours, then within that window discounting is purely exponential with rate δ. That means immediate temptations (within two days) are not hyperbolically inflated; they just decay exponentially. So akrasia in the moment—like eating the cake right now—can't be explained by present bias at all. Instead, weakness of will only shows up in planning: you at Monday make a plan for Wednesday that you at Wednesday (now inside the β-window) violate. So Davidson's single-agent picture collapses; we need Thaler-Shefrin-style planner/doer selves. The planner makes the ATC judgment on Monday, but the doer on Wednesday is governed by a different discount function.

Still, Davidson's principle of continence might survive in modified form. Even with delayed activation, the Monday-agent knows about the Wednesday bias and could use a commitment device. If she doesn't, that's a failure of rational foresight, which is close to Davidson's idea that you should act on your best available judgment. The parameter values β=0.85, δ=0.98 still generate dynamic inconsistency across the boundary, so the agent is technically time-inconsistent, which mirrors Davidson's claim that the akratic agent violates her own evaluative ranking.

One issue is that Davidson treats prima facie judgments as genuinely normative, not just preference-weighted. The hyperbolic model treats them as arithmetic outputs of the discount function. So there's a category mistake in saying Davidson's view is "the philosophical version" of hyperbolic discounting. But the structural parallel—reversal between evaluation at distance and evaluation up close—is real and illuminating.
