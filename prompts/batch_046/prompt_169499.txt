Analyze the Meiji restoration's rush to build heavy industry in the 1870s-80s — the state-run model factories and later privatization into zaibatsu — through Weber's thesis that culture can actually cause economic forms rather than just mirror them. The usual story is that Confucian and bushido values were mobilized so workers would accept factory discipline. But flip the lens: ask whether the Meiji oligarchs were using values instrumentally (Zweckrationalität) while workers experienced them as value-rational commitment (Wertrationalität), and whether that split breaks Weber's notion of an elective affinty between ascetic values and capital accumulation. Does Meiji Japan show values being manufactored top-down to fit an economic structure already chosen for geopolitcal reasons? If so, does that weaken Weber's claim that the Protestant ethic was a genuine cause of capitalism, or can he reply the same instrumental manipulation happened in 17th-century Europe? Keep the mapping tight — who holds which values, what the economic decision actually was, where the causal arrow points — and say honestly where Weber is silent rather than forcing him. No bibliographies needed, just stay with the concepts.
