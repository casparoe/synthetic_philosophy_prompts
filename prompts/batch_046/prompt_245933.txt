I'm trying to stabilize my position on phenomenal consciousness for an upcoming discussion group, and I want a consistency audit before I present it. Here is the package of commitments I take myself to hold:

(1) Illusionism is correct: there are no intrinsic qualitative properties, only representational states that systematically misrepresent themselves as possessing a non-functional, intrinsic phenomenal character.
(2) The hard problem is therefore a pseudo-problem; the explanatory gap is an artifact of conceptual confusion rather than an ontological deficit.
(3) Phenomenal concepts are genuinely special — they have a first-personal, indexical, transparent structure — but this uniqueness is entirely representational and does not entail any non-physical ontology.
(4) Introspection is best modeled as predictive-processing self-monitoring: it tracks functional organization with reasonable fidelity but is systematically distorted about the metaphysical status of its own outputs.
(5) Conceivability remains a fallible but real guide to metaphysical possibility, except in domains where our conceptual apparatus is distorted; zombie arguments fail not because conceivability is useless, but because phenomenal concepts are distorted in precisely the relevant respect.
(6) There is genuinely something it is like to be a system with sufficiently rich self-modeling architecture, and genuinely nothing it is like to be a thermostat; in both cases, however, this status is fully constituted by representational dynamics, leaving no residual phenomenal property.

My request: go through the pairs and tell me which are in genuine tension, which only appear tense but dissolve on closer inspection, and rank them by severity. For each real tension, specify the cheapest revision that restores consistency and what that revision costs. Then identify the single commitment whose weakening or abandonment would clean up the most overall tension. I am especially uncertain whether (6) is compatible with (1), and whether the specialness claimed in (3) undermines or supports the rejection in (5). Be precise about the logical relations, not just general impressions.
