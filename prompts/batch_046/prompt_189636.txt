I've bought into a Schlick-style view that lifes meaning isn't in hitting goals or working toward some end-state, but in play — activity done for its own sake where the process is the point. But I've added a twist: I think the real test now is whether any activity can stay genuinely playful once it's caught up in attention economies and productivity tracking, so I claim meaning requires structural sheltering from instrumentalization, not just the right mental attitude. What would count against this? Not just "people still enjoy hobbies," but what empirical result, conceptual distinction, or counter-case would show either that Schlick's play/work split is bogus, or that my sheltering condition is impossible or unnecessary? I want an honest map of exposure — which parts defenders concede are weak vs. which they fight — and take a definate stance: is this well-insulated or does it actually risk being wrong?
