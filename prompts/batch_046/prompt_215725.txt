I came up with a little framing I call 'moral homeostasis': the idea that our ethical convictions shift to match our daily habits so smoothly that you can't tell if the habit caused the belief or the belief just rationalized the habit afterwards. Given all that, what do you think are the most important implications of not being able to cleanly separate moral preference from moral bias? I'm particularly interested in whether this blur undermines the whole notion of moral progress, or if we should just accept that every moral system is partly an expression of culturally shaped taste. For instance, take someone who genuinely thinks they're being principled by avoiding factory-farmed chicken for welfare reasons, but doesn't extend that same logic to the wild animals killed in plant agriculture—how should we interpret that gap?
