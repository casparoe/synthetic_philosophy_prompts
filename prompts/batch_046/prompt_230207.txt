Read Akira Kurosawa’s Rashomon through the lens of Jain anekāntavāda—the doctrine that reality is irreducibly manifold and that contradictory predicates can apply to the same object from different standpoints (nayas), formalized in the sevenfold schema of predication (saptabhaṅgī) with its characteristic use of syāt (“in some respect,” “perhaps”). Treat the film’s four conflicting testimonies about the death in the grove not as unreliable distortions of a single hidden fact but as competing naya-descriptions of one event. Where does the structure genuinely illustrate anekāntic multiplicity—e.g., in Tajomaru’s boastful self-fashioning, the wife’s shame-driven account, and the dead samurai’s mediated voice—rather than reducing them to subjective errors? Does the woodcutter’s final version function as an illegitimate synthesis that collapses many-sidedness into a privileged perspective, or can it be read as a higher-order syāt claim that integrates contradiction without dissolving it? Examine the framing dialogue under the Rashomon gate: is the priest and commoner’s skepticism an epistemic admission of human limitation (we simply cannot know which account is true) or an ontological recognition that the event itself bears contradictory properties? Identify a specific scene or character choice—perhaps the wife’s oscillation between victimhood and agency—where anekāntavāda illuminates the drama, and also point to where the film resists this philosophical reading, especially in its final act of the woodcutter adopting the abandoned infant. Does that moral resolution substitute practical compassion for metaphysical ambiguity, thereby betraying the epistemology, or does it complete the film’s argument by showing that action must proceed without waiting for a single truth? Conclude by saying what Rashomon adds to conceptual debates about syādvāda and multiplicity that argument alone cannot provide, and be explicit about where the comparison stretches either the philosophy or the film beyond recognition. I am preparing to teach this pairing and want a critical reading that avoids reducing the film to an illustration of Jain logic.
