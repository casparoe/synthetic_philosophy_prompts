I've been reflecting on a disagreement I had with a friend about whether people with past mistakes are allowed to criticize others for doing something worse in a similar situation. In my volunteer group, I once forgot to lock the supply closet after an event, so some equipment was left outside overnight—nothing was stolen, but it was careless. Now, our coordinator has been repeatedly leaving donations unlocked in the same closet and selling some items online for personal profit. I have been vocal that he should be removed, but my friend argues that because I also failed to secure the closet once, I shouldn't be the one demanding consequences for him. To try to organize my thoughts, I came up with a rough personal guideline I call the scaled-reflection rule: you can criticize someone for misconduct in an area where your own worst comparable mistake was at least half as serious as theirs. By that standard, my negligent forgetfulness might still permit me to object to his intentional theft, since the harm and intent differ so much. But I don't know if that reasoning holds up, or whether any prior fault in the same general domain should simply disqualify a person from speaking out. Could you help me think through whether there is a fair, principled way to decide who is in a position to blame whom when both parties have fallen short in related ways? Please provide your answer in American English.
