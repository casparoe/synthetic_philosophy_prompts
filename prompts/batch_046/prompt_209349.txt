I run a philosophy workshop with about 10 undergrads, one hour. Theyve skimmed Zhuangzi's butterfly passage once. Design an activity—maybe a card game or structured role-play—where they actually feel the force of dream skepticism instead of just naming it. The rules should push them into an epistemic loop: they try to prove they're awake using evidence cards (memory, other people, consistency) but every validation has to come from another card drawn from the same pool, and unknown to them one pool is the "dream pool" with identical cards. They should construct the circularity themselves and notice they cant apply a wakefulness criterion without already assuming wakefulness. Then a debrief: map their moves onto the butterfly argument, be honest about what the game cant replicate (the first-person indexical shift), predict the failure mode where participants just perform skepticism for fun, and only name the paradox after they've hit it. No lecture up front. Keep it practical.
