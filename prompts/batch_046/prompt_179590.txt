(1) Alex argued it's classic masking: people still have a real disposition to enjoy obscure jazz, but the algorithm buries it under pop playlists so the disposition never gets triggered -- like putting a fragile vase in a steel box.

(2) Blake said that's actually a fink, not a mask. The engine doesn't just block the old taste; it actively rewires the conditions under which preferences form. Once you're fed pop long enough, the jazz-loving state is destroyed and replaced, not hidden away.

(3) Alex came back: if it were a true fink, quitting Spotify should restore your old taste right away. But people stay stuck in pop bubbles even after canceling, which suggests the original disposition was masked all along and just atrophied from disuse rather than being destroyed.

(4) Blake replied that atrophy is just slow finking over time. He also said that if masking were correct, we'd expect people to suddenly rediscover jazz the day they cancel, which rarely happens -- so the hidden-disposition story doesn't fit.

Please adjudicate each move: rate them from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify. Return a JSON object with keys "moves" (array of objects with fields "move": int, "rating": string chosen from ["clearly mistaken","mostly wrong","mixed","mostly right","clearly right"], "reason": string) and "bottom_line" (object with "who_is_more_right": string and "what_both_miss": string).
