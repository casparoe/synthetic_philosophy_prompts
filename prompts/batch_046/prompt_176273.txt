After a rather long and heated argument with a close friend about whether any artificial system could genuinely possess mental states, she offered me a summary of Daniel Dennett’s position on original versus derived intentionality that I now suspect is partially distorted, though I cannot quite pinpoint where. She described Dennett’s “two-bitser”—the minimal hypothetical system possessing only two bits of internal state, yet interpretable as holding beliefs and desires—as decisive proof that original intentionality does not exist at all. According to her, Dennett uses this example to show that all intentional content, including that of human beings, is entirely derived from evolutionary and functional design, leaving us no different in principle from books or computer programmes: we appear to mean things only because other interpreters adopt the intentional stance towards us. She argued that this renders Searle’s Chinese Room argument irrelevant, since Searle presupposes a genuine ontological gap between the syntactic manipulation inside the room and the semantic understanding of the operator, whereas Dennett’s instrumentalism dissolves that gap by treating both as merely different grades of derived interpretation. She went on to claim that Dennett is therefore an eliminativist about consciousness itself—if a two-bitser can be ascribed beliefs without any accompanying subjective experience, then consciousness is merely a redundant folk-psychological label we attach to sufficiently complex behavioural patterns. Finally, she concluded that the entire dispute over original and derived intentionality is a pseudo-problem generated by philosophers who erroneously believe there is some intrinsic, magical aboutness residing inside the skull.

Please assess whether this is a fair characterisation. I would like you to separate, clearly and in British English: (i) what is broadly accurate about Dennett’s view; (ii) what are standard simplifications that might mislead; (iii) what Dennett or Searle would explicitly reject; and (iv) whether her conclusion—that the debate is a pseudo-problem and that Dennett eliminates consciousness—actually follows from the premises she describes. Do not provide a bibliography or reading list; I am preparing this for a discussion group that will not have access to external texts.
