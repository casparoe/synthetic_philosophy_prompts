I'm working out my position on whether dreams are experiences and want a consistency audit before I commit to it in writing. Here is where I stand:

(1) With Malcolm, I hold that there is no criterion-independent way to attribute a dream to someone during sleep; the concept of dreaming is tied to waking report, so treating REM sleep as hosting "experiences" is conceptually muddled.
(2) Against Dennett's cassette model, I think the content of reported dreams is generated during sleep, not inserted at awakening—localized neural patterns during REM correlate with specific narrative elements reported later.
(3) Nevertheless, I deny these patterns are phenomenally conscious. I call them "proto-narrative traces": structured mnemonic residues formed while the global workspace is largely offline, carrying representational content but no first-person "what-it-is-like" quality.
(4) I take global workspace integration to be necessary for phenomenal consciousness, which is why ordinary REM dreams lack experience.
(5) I make an exception for lucid REM: here meta-cognitive monitoring is partly restored, so global workspace integration returns and the subject genuinely experiences the dream.
(6) Finally, I think the line between experience and false memory is graded; dream reports lie on a continuum with confabulated episodic reconstructions, both being post-waking narrative constructions built from fragmentary traces.

Do these hang together? Specifically: does (2) give sleep-state content an empirical reality that undermines Malcolm's conceptual point in (1)? Does (4) together with (5) commit me to an implausible bifurcation—phenomenally blank ordinary REM versus conscious lucid REM—and does that conflict with (3)? Is the tension between (3) and (6) merely apparent, dissolving once we separate the sleep-state trace from the waking reconstruction? Rank the tensions by severity, say which are superficial, and indicate the least costly revision that restores consistency without forcing me to abandon more than one commitment.
