I want to check if my commitments actually hang together or if I'm being inconsistent without noticing it. Here is where I stand:

First, I think the outer message is basicly dead without the frame message — the vibrations in a record groove aren't music until there's a needle, speaker, and decoding apparatus framing them, so meaning is primarily in the frame, not the raw signal. Second, I think humans have real inner messages — unlike a record player, we have a self-interpretive layer that can reflect on what we're processing. Third, I think when my inner message conflicts with the frame message, the inner message should win: if I read a poem and understand it differently than the author's framing intended, my interpretation is still valid meaning for me. Fourth, I'm a phyiscalist about all of this — it's just brain architecture and neural patterns, no ghost in the machine. Fifth, I also lean antirepresentationalist: I dont think mental states are pictures that match the world, they're more like tools for coping.

So do these five actually cohere? There's probably a tension between saying meaning lives in the frame (which sounds objective and physically fixed) and saying my inner message can override it (which sounds subjective and arbitrary). There's maybe another tension between being a physicalist and giving inner messages this special authoritative status — if everything is just brain states, why should one pattern dominate? But I also wonder if some tensions are only apparent. Can you audit these pairs for me?
