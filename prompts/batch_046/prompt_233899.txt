I have been studying Foucault's claim that power and knowledge are not opposed but co-constitutive—specifically his analysis of disciplinary institutions that produce the subjects they govern. I am trying to apply this to my school's reliance on standardized assessments and class rankings, which seem not merely to measure learning but to construct the ideal student. However, I also encountered his notion of the episteme in The Order of Things, the deep historical conditions that determine what can be thought within an era. This leads me to ask whether a high school's grading rubric might itself operate as an episteme for our generation, establishing the very conditions of what counts as knowledge. More fundamentally, if power genuinely produces knowledge rather than distorting it, does that mean there is no objectively correct solution to an exam—only responses that reinforce institutional authority? Or am I conflating two distinct frameworks—his archaeology of knowledge and his genealogy of discipline—and if so, does the argument that grading is purely an exercise of power collapse once the distinction is restored?
