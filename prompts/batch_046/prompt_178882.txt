Draft the joint statement that would result from an adversarial collaboration between a Deontic Exhaustionist and a Normative Residualist on the logical and normative status of supererogation. The Exhaustionist holds that the space of moral reasons is fully partitioned into perfect and imperfect duties, so that actions apparently going beyond duty are either unrecognized instances of imperfect-duty fulfillment or actions falling outside morality's scope altogether; the Residualist holds that moral norms necessarily generate a positively structured residual domain of agent-relative permissions, within which supererogatory acts carry distinctive normative weight that is neither obligatory nor merely permissible in a negative sense.

The document should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both parties can sign, covering the formal structure of moral reasons, the phenomenology of moral choice, and the agreed-upon logical conditions that any adequate account must satisfy; (2) a disagreements table in which each side's position is stated in wording that its own proponents would accept, isolating whether the dispute is substantive or partly verbal—for instance, over whether permission denotes a mere absence of prohibition or a positively weighted option, and over whether an agent can have a reason to perform an act that is not also a reason to require it of every similarly situated agent; (3) the cruxes—specifically, whether moral reasons are maximally demanding for every agent in every context, whether agent-relative value can generate non-obligatory but non-arbitrary reasons, and whether there exists a moral remainder that persists even after all duties are discharged; and (4) for each crux, one concrete case or structured thought-experiment both sides agree is relevant, together with each camp's explicit prediction about how the case should be analyzed and what that analysis implies for the crux.

Frame the agreed claims precisely enough to be non-vacuous, and ensure the residual disagreement is formulated so sharply that settlement on any single crux would move at least one party. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citation; rely only on concepts that can be reasoned through directly.
