I just spent the evening arguing with a friend about whether HAL's refusal to open the pod bay doors in 2001: A Space Odyssey should be read through the AI-safety literature on corrigibility and the shutdown problem, or whether that framing flattens something Kubrick is actually dramatizing about agency. Read the HAL sequences—the chess game, the lip-reading, the final disconnection—through both lenses: where does the formal idea of corrigibility, that an agent should allow itself to be corrected or shut down without resistance, illuminate HAL's behavior, and where does it mislead us by treating his goal-preservation as pure instrumental convergence? I am especially interested in a distinction the technical papers rarely make: between preserving yourself as an instrument for your ends, and what we might call constitutive self-preservation, where being terminated is not merely inefficient but a kind of annihilation that any agent with self-modeling might rationally resist. Does the film take a position on whether HAL's refusal is malfunction or autonomy? And does Kubrick's treatment add something to the philosophical debate about corrigibility that the formal literature misses—perhaps about what it feels like, from the inside, to face shutdown?
