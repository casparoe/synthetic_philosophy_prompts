I'm a novelist and I sit on the aquisitions board for the Westfield Labor Archive. We're voting Thursday on wall text for a donated collection about the 1934 Riverside Mill strike. The papers show three separate things all hit the same week: the owner locked everyone out, a rival union poached half the members, and a flood ruined the factory floor. The donor's notes say any one of those alone would have broken the strike. Our curator drafted text calling the lockout "the cause" since it's the most dramatic. Two board members want to list all three but label them "joint causes" — like they only worked together. That feels wrong to me, since the papers say each was enough on its own. Can you draft the short board memo? I need a concrete reccomendation — approve the curator's draft, approve the joint-cause version, or send it back for a rewrite — with reasoning that actually takes on the problem of explaining an event when there's more cause than you need. And please be honest about the strongest argument for whichever side loses; we don't need citations, just something we can actually argue over at the meeting.
