Student answer:
Carroll sets up Achilles and the Tortoise with A and B, and the Tortoise accepts both but refuses q unless Z is added as a premise. Then we need Z' to justify applying Z, then Z'', forever, so deduction can't be justified by piling on more premises. The real fix is to treat Z as a rule of infrence, not a statement inside the argument. This was Frege's move in his logicist system — he put the inference rules in the meta-language, so they don't sit inside the object-language and don't need premises to back them up. Once the rule is in the meta-language, the regress stops completely, because rules are just part of the framework.

Applying this to chain-of-thought: LLMs write every intermediate step and logical connection out in the prompt text.
