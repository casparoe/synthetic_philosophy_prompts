Write an essay on the controversy between Charles Peirce’s convergence theory of truth and William James’s pragmatic theory, specifically with respect to what might be called “framework-restructuring beliefs”—beliefs that do not merely predict or explain particular experiences, but alter the conceptual vocabulary through which subsequent experiences are interpreted and evaluated. Weigh Peirce’s claim that truth is the opinion fated to be agreed upon by an indefinite community of inquirers against James’s claim that an idea becomes true insofar as it helps us navigate experience by being assimilated, validated, and corroborated in practice. Address the objection that James’s practical criterion collapses into Peirce’s long-run convergence once verification is extended over an indefinite future, and the reply that Peirce’s ideal endpoint is normatively empty for beliefs that change the very standards by which agreement is measured. Reach and justify a specific conclusion: defend either that James’s view can accommodate framework-restructuring beliefs without surrendering to Peirce’s teleology, that Peirce’s view can account for deep conceptual change without losing its normative force, or that the two theories are addressing incommensurable dimensions of truth and therefore cannot be adjudicated on this ground.
