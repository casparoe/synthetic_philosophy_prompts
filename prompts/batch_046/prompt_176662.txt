Write a dialogue between a Hart-style positivist and a Fuller-style natural lawyer—imagine two tired grad students in a bar—debating whether a fully automated legal system (algorithms applying statutes with zero human judges, no moral reasoning, no purposive interpretation) still counts as law. The positivist should say law is just rules plus social recognition and efficency, so automation doesn't change anything. The natural lawyer should argue Fuller's internal morality requires human agents with shared moral commitments, otherwise it's just management. Make them actually talk back and forth—if recognition gets mentioned, address whether it needs a human recognizer; if efficency comes up, ask if efficency alone makes law. Keep it casual, throw in a typo or two. At the end, have them seperate what's pretty settled in legal philosophy from what's genuinely contested, and don't present any controversial claim as if it's the consensus.
