ok so i've been thinking about hedonism lately — partly because i'm trying to make sense of why some of the best periods of my life (writing, falling in love, running in rain) felt totally different from each other, and partly because a friend asked me why pleasure should matter at all if it's so heterogeniety. here's roughly where i've landed, and i want to know if it's actually consistent before i defend it in a seminar.

(1) well-being is purely hedonic: pleasure is good, pain is bad, nothing else is intrinsically good or bad for the person experiencing it.
(2) the heterogeniety problem is a red herring — pleasures don't need a shared 'feel'; what makes them all pleasures is being the object of a positive pro-attitude with the right direction of fit.
(3) i'm not a phenomenal hedonist — enjoying a mathematical proof and enjoying warm water are genuinely both pleasures despite having almost no shared phenomenology.
(4) but i also think that if you're on some drug that makes you feel euphoric about counting blades of grass, that mental state shouldn't contribute to your well-being, even though it's technically a pleasure.
(5) i want the view to stay purely subjective: what's good for you depends only on your actual mental states, not on whether they connect to anything real outside your head.
(6) and i think a pleasure only genuinely counts if you'd refelctively endorse keeping it — alienated or chemically induced pleasures that you'd reject upon reflection don't make your life go better.
