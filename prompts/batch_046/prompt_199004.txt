Design a realistic, non-science-fictional thought experiment in which a quantitative long-run demographic theory—explicitly Malthusian in structure but updated with a modern hidden feedback mechanism, such as a century-scale ocean-carbon saturation cycle that temporarily masks carrying-capacity limits—predicts a global food-security collapse by 2060. The theory is apparently falsified by abundant agricultural data through 2045, yet the predicted crisis arrives precisely in 2160 once the hidden buffer exhausts itself. Keep the scenario to one paragraph, with concrete numbers and a clearly specified rescue mechanism that was unobservable to the 2045 scientists.

Then show what three philosophical stances say about whether researchers should have retained the theory in 2045: a strict Popperian falsificationist; a Lakatosian defender of progressive research programmes; and a temporalist who allows that a theory can be correct but prematurely tested. For each, state the verdict and the reasoning, emphasizing why the hidden delay matters.

Next, isolate the single structural feature that generates the disagreement—namely, the invisibility of the rescue mechanism at the time of apparent falsification. Explain how removing that feature (making the saturation mechanism directly observable in 2045) transforms the situation so that the original theory is replaced by an empirically calibrated revision rather than being retained against counter-evidence, and indicate whether the three positions then converge or merely shift their disagreement to a different point. Finally, present the sharpest objection a committed Popperian could raise—that the case rigs the outcome by post-hoc stipulating an invisible savior that saves the theory from refutation—and respond to it honestly without appealing to authority, citation, or bibliographic reference.
