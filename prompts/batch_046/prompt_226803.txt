I've been recieveing feedback on this argument I built about Hofstadter's typogenetics and I keep feeling like there's an exit I'm missing. The idea is that the DNA-Gödel isomorphism isn't just an analogy—it shows that self-reference is mechanically cheap, which then collapses the strange-loop theory of consciousness. Here are the steps:

(1) Gödel numbering is purely mechanical: every formula in system F gets mapped to an integer, so F can encode statements about its own syntax without ever stepping outside arithmetic.

(2) DNA replication is the same structure physically: the nucleotide sequence is both the data being copied and, via ribosomes and transcription machinery, the program that directs the copying. That's not poetry; it's a literal typogenetic loop.

(3) Hofstadter argues human consciousness is a "strange loop" where high-level patterns—the symbol 'I'—feed back and alter the low-level neural rules that generate them.

(4) So if consciousness is just a strange loop, and both Gödel systems and DNA are already strange loops, then consciousness should be a matter of complexity or scale, not kind.

(5) But that forces a threshold problem: either there's a precise complexity level where 'I'-ness switches on, or it doesn't switch on at all.

(6) Any precise threshold is arbitrary—why not one neuron earlier?—which pushes us toward (A): DNA and maybe even simple recursive algorithms are proto-conscious.

(7) Meanwhile, (1) and (2) remind us that self-reference doesn't require understanding. Gödel's system doesn't grasp it's own Gödel sentence; DNA doesn't intend replication. It's all causal encoding.

(8) So if human consciousness is just this loop scaled up, then our "meaning" and "understanding" are also just causal encoding scaled up—basically eliminativism about mind.

(9) Therefore either we bite the bullet and grant DNA proto-intentionality (option A), or we deny that the strange loop explains consciousness in the first place (option B).

(10) But (B) seems to gut Hofstadter's account entirely, and (A) feels insane. I want to keep the idea that there's something special about human self-reference, but I also don't want an arbitrary complexity cutoff. Which step is the weak link? Is the isomorphism in (2) overstated? Or is there a hidden slide between "mechanical self-reference" and "no understanding" in (7)? What's the cost of fixing it?
