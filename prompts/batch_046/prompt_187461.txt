Here is an argument I have been turning over about the existential interpretation of eternal recurrence, and I cannot locate the fault. I present it with numbered steps.

(1) On the existential interpretation, eternal recurrence is not primarily a cosmological claim but a normative test: one must be able to will the infinite repetition of one's life exactly as it has been.

(2) To will the repetition of one's life as a whole requires willing the repetition of every constituent state, including states of extreme suffering or moral degradation, since the identity of the whole life depends on the identity of its parts.

(3) Crucially, this is not merely willing an abstract event-structure; it requires willing the recurrence of the suffering-subject—the same first-person conscious perspective enduring that suffering again and again without redemption or alteration.

(4) To will, from within that first-person perspective, the infinite recurrence of unredeemed suffering is either psychologically impossible or morally monstrous.

(5) Therefore, either no actual human can pass the test, or passing it requires embracing a form of moral perversity.

(6) Yet Nietzsche presents the capacity to pass this test as the defining achievement of the highest human type.

(7) So the existential interpretation sets a normative standard that is either unattainable or ethically corrupt.

(8) Consequently, the interpretation collapses: it cannot serve as a criterion for affirming life, since it demands either impossibility or monstrosity.

If I must preserve one claim, I would prefer to keep (3)—the idea that affirmation involves the recurrence of the suffering-subject rather than mere event-structures—because without it the test seems to dissolve into an abstract aesthetic judgment that never touches lived experience. But I want a definite diagnosis: which step should be rejected, why did that step seem defensible, and what does abandoning it cost for the broader interpretation? Give me a committed answer that takes a clear position on the step and prices the alternatives, rather than surveying every option and leaving me to choose. If the argument is valid and the cost must be paid at the premises, say so directly and defend which premise is least damaging to surrender.
