She said that the archive captures only the bureaucratic surface—what officials wrote to justify extraction—while the community's memory preserves the lived moral meaning of the arrangement: people experienced it as solidarity, rebuilt lives through mutual aid, and transmitted that experience across generations as identity-forming truth. Mara answered that Jonas was treating documents as transparent windows rather than instruments of domination, and that demanding the town abandon its narrative is asking them to perform a kind of self-annulment in order to validate an outsider's framework.
