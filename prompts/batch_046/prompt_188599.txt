You are a professional philosopher with expertise in practical reason, institutional ethics, and the philosophy of action. I have noticed a recurring configuration in long-standing institutions, and I do not know whether philosophy has a precise term for it. Consider two concrete cases. First, a university tenure system was originally designed in the 1960s to protect scholars from political purges; today, no administrator genuinely believes the campus faces comparable political danger, yet they continue to defend tenure by invoking “the foundational protective rationale of the institution,” treating that rationale as an independent normative weight that binds them regardless of their own beliefs. Second, a city zoning ordinance was enacted decades ago to separate heavy industry from residential neighborhoods for health reasons; the industry is long gone, the pollution risk is essentially zero, and the current planners privately regard the health justification as obsolete, yet they still cite “the health-protection rationale behind the ordinance” to block housing development, as if the reason itself—now disconnected from anyone who holds it—exerts direct constraining force. In both cases, the original reasoners have disappeared, the current actors do not endorse the original reason, and yet the reason is not abandoned; instead, it floats free, continuing to govern decisions without being anyone’s reason. How exactly does this differ from standard institutional path dependence or from the legal “dead hand” problem? Is it a specific variant of what is sometimes called “free-floating rationales”—reasons without reasoners—or does that concept cover only moral reasons that lack agents altogether, leaving this institutional version unnamed? If there is no established term, I want the carving, not just a label: what are the necessary conditions—must the original reason have been normative rather than merely instrumental? must the current actors be aware of their own disbelief?—and what boundary cases show where the phenomenon ends and ordinary bureaucratic conservatism begins? I am not asking for a reading list or citations; I want a disciplined conceptual analysis that tells me whether the thing I have noticed is already mapped, and if not, how we should map it.
