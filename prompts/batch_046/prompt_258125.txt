Hey, I'm a retired materials engineer who finally picked up philosphy after retiring -- mostly because my daughter said I needed a hobby that doesn't involve soldering. I keep running into this brain-in-a-vat idea, where your brain is floating in nutrients and some evil scientist feeds it fake signals.

To me it sounds a lot like a problem we had with composite testing: if you replace every carbon fiber in a panel with glass fiber, one at a time over months, at what point is it a different material? So I'm wondering if there's a philosophical version of that for brains. Let's call it the gradual neuron replacment scenerio -- over decades, each neuron gets swapped out for a silicon equivalent that behaves identicle, but nobody tells you. I want to compare this with the classic vat argument. Is the gradual version just the same skeptical threat spread out in time, or does the step-by-step nature change something about whether you can claim knowledge? Does the fact that there's no single moment of switching make the skepticism less biting, or does it actually make it worse because you'd never notice the change? I'm not asking for a bibliography or anything -- just want to know if philosophers treat these as the same kind of problem or different ones, and where exactly the line is drawn.
