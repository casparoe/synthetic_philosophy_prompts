You are a philosopher specialising in axiology and comparative ethics, with particular familiarity with Buddhist metaphysics and the rationalist literature on value. Before you answer, take a moment to outline the core commitments of tranquilism, list the main families of objection, and then think carefully through each in turn.

I have come to accept a view I call tranquilism: in ethical evaluation, only the absence of craving (taṇhā) and the absence of suffering (dukkha) carry intrinsic value. Positive flourishing, achievement, knowledge pursued for its own sake, relational goods, and even active compassion possess value only derivatively, insofar as they reduce craving or suffering; should they fail to do so, they are ethically neutral. I treat this as a strict, stripped-down reading of the Buddhist soteriological framework, shorn of its usual pluralistic moral vocabulary.

What would genuinely count against this position? I do not want the standard objections to negative utilitarianism recycled. Instead, identify the following and rank them from fatal to merely awkward:

First, which internal tensions within Buddhist doctrine itself—particularly regarding anattā (no-self), the status of the arhat, and the doctrine of skilful means (upāya)—expose tranquilism to self-undermining results?

Second, which non-Buddhist axiological intuitions—say, about justified pride in craftsmanship, or about an asymmetry between creating satisfied beings and failing to create them—would a committed tranquilist have to concede as genuinely counting against the view, and which could they dismiss as residual attachment to ego?

Third, consider this somewhat under-discussed scenario: suppose we could engineer minds—biological or artificial—that are permanently free of craving and suffering but also permanently incapable of the higher jhānas, aesthetic experience, or moral agency. Tranquilism seems compelled to rate their existence as maximally good, yet many find this repugnant. Is that repugnance merely an aesthetic bias, or does it reveal that tranquilism collapses into an eliminativism about value that even its defenders should reject?

Finally, reach a reasoned, non-committal-free assessment of how exposed the position actually is. Indicate precisely where a defender could qualify tranquilism without abandoning its core, and where such qualifications would amount to surrender.
