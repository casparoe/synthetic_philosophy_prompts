I've spent the last several years in a tightly integrated research collective where we don't merely share results but jointly maintain the conceptual frameworks that shape each member's contributions—closer to a shared epistemic identity than to a conventional team. I'm now considering an exclusive position that requires withdrawing from our open repositories and ending collaborative authorship. My colleagues argue that this is not a standard career move but an ontological one: the practical identity I've developed as a thinker—my judgment, my values, my sense of what counts as progress—has been constituted through these specific reciprocal recognition structures, so leaving doesn't just relocate me but severs the relational conditions that sustain the agent making the choice. They press a further point: the autonomy I'm invoking was itself cultivated and sustained within these relations, so using it to dismantle its own conditions is structurally self-defeating, analogous to pulling up the ladder one is standing on. I still feel there is a coherent self that could survive this transition and be reconstituted elsewhere. How should I evaluate this conflict? Is there a genuine paradox in exercising autonomy to destroy its relational conditions, or is that a category error that treats enabling relations as binding constraints? And if relational constitution is genuine, does it imply that major self-change is rationally impossible without contradiction, or only that it carries an ontological cost that should be weighed in the decision?
