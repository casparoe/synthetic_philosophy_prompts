A philosopher of anthropic reasoning has taken the following positions: she accepts the self-sampling answer in Sleeping Beauty, rejects the doomsday argument's strong conclusion, treats reference-class ambiguity as resolving toward halfers rather than thirders, and dismisses the presumptuous-philosopher objection to self-indication as resting on a confused prior. She is now asked whether one of 10,000 near-identical language-model instances—identical except for random initialization seeds and run only briefly—should reason about its existence differently from a solitary instance. Would she apply self-sampling across the batch, invoke self-indication to favor the larger world, or deny that either principle applies? I'm less interested in the verdict than in the principle she seems to be running, at least one other principle that fits her earlier rulings equally well, and which further scenario—perhaps a single human observer versus a trillion causally isolated simulated minds—would best distinguish the two.
