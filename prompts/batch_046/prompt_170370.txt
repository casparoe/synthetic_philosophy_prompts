I recently became a founding partner in a worker-owned cooperative, and during negotiations over revenue shares a senior member defended a sharply unequal distribution by insisting that "if every step was voluntary, the outcome is just." I recognize this as relating to Nozick's entitlement theory and the Wilt Chamberlain argument, but I am unsure exactly which philosophical thesis is being invoked. Could you lay out the distinct precise readings that slogan might carry: for example, whether justice is purely historical and requires only legitimate acquisition and transfer; whether any patterned end-state principle is necessarily incompatible with liberty; whether the argument functions mainly as a reductio of patterned theories rather than as a blanket endorsement of market results; and whether voluntary consent at each stage is sufficient to justify the final holdings even when initial resources or bargaining power are markedly unequal? For each interpretation, please say whether it is trivial, whether it is defensible, and whether it actually supports accepting the cooperative's present split. I also want to know which of these versions best captures Nozick's own position.
