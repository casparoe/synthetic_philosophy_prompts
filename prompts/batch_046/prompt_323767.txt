For an upper-level history of philosophy exam, draft one 90-minute essay question worth 20 marks on Hobbes's account of geometric knowledge in De Corpore, designed so that it cannot be answered by summarizing his nominalism or his admiration for Euclid; instead, require candidates to confront the tension between his claim that geometric figures are generated by motion—whether of bodies or of mental points—and his simultaneous insistence that geometric demonstration yields unconditional, necessary truths about natural bodies, and ask them to assess whether this undermines his materialist ontology. Present your response as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: item 1 should state the full exam question; item 2 should give the thesis and first argument of the model answer; item 3 should give the second argument and resolution; item 4 should lay out the five-point marking scheme with allocations out of 20; item 5 should name the misconception that separates an A from a B answer and state the penalty for it.
