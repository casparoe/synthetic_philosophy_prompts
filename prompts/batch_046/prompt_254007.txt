I am preparing the interview guide for a podcast episode with a metaphysician who defends a non-standard form of endurantism. Rather than claiming that objects are wholly present at each instant in the classical sense, she argues that diachronic identity is secured by primitive structural-continuity conditions that are causally directed and metaphysically prior to both spatial parts and temporal parts. She rejects the standard rotating-disc objection by denying that shape-properties must be instantiated in instantaneous stages, and she maintains that perdurantism illicitly imports a spatial analogy that collapses once we recognize the asymmetry of temporal succession and the directionality of causation. Please draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that go beyond restating the view. I want at least two questions that anticipate her likely reply—perhaps that structural continuity is primitive, or that causation supplies the needed asymmetry—each followed by a targeted follow-up that presses that reply further. Include one question about whether her account can accommodate radical fission or fusion without collapsing into a four-dimensional ontology, and one that asks what kind of empirical or conceptual finding could persuade her that endurance is an illusion rather than a primitive feature of ordinary objects. Keep the tone formal and philosophical; avoid biographical or generic opening questions, and do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
