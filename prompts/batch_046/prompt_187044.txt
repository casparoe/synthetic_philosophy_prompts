I sit on my university's faculty affairs committee and we're deadlocked over a proposed change to our parental leave provison. Right now both parents get 12 weeks, but they have to take it in the first year and it runs concurently—so in a dual-earner household with two tenure-track jobs, they basically have to choose who stalls their pipeline. A faction wants to let parents split and stagger leave across the first three years, with each parent holding an independant entitlement. The opposition says this turns leave into strategic career-planning rather than child-welfare, and it violates equality since single parents still only get 12 weeks total.

Please draft the committee's decision memo—reccomend either keeping the current rule, adopting the staggered split, or a middle path with conditions. Give a clear bottom line and a timeline for implementation if change is advised. You should take seriously the worry that we're conflating distributive justice with individual career optimization, but also engage the idea that care labor is non-fungible across time—meaning you can't just "bank" care hours and cash them in later, so the temporal structure of leave actually matters for justice. Write it like a working doc we could circulate Thursday, not an abstract essay. Don't worry about being polished; our last memo had "provison" misspelled twice.
