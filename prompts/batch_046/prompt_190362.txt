ive been in this Borges study group for like two years and the debate about his view of time is totally stuck. on one side people say he's a straight-up B-theorist: in that essay A New Refutation of Time he denies succession and says all moments are simultaneous, like a block universe without the block. on the other side people say he's not doing metaphysics at all, he's doing a kind of mystical idealism where time is just a mental habit we can't shake, closer to Schopenhauer or even Berkeley. both sides quote the same texts — the essay and The Garden of Forking Paths — and nobody moves. is this actually a disagreement about the nature of reality, or is it verbal, with time meaning objective temporal order for one camp and phenomenological duration for the other? or are they answering different questions — one about physics, one about consciousness — under the same heading? and there's this weird thing where people bring up branching time in Garden of Forking Paths versus circular time in The Circular Ruins: one camp says these are incompatible metaphors that show Borges doesn't have a single view, the other says they're just literary expressions of the same eternalist idea. does that sub-debate help or just muddy the waters? what would actually count as progress here — finding a hidden formal contradiction, showing one reading can't account for both stories, or admitting the question is ill-posed because Borges is deliberately using contradictory images? i kind of want to keep both sides alive because they both feel right, but i want to know what im paying if i do.
