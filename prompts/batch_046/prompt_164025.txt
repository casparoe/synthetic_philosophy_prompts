Write a dialogue between a defender of HCH (Human Consulting HCH) and factored cognition as a normative ideal for collective deliberation, and a critic who argues that this idealization covertly assumes a view-from-nowhere model of cognition that erases the indexical, first-personal nature of practical reasoning. The defender should claim that decomposing reasoning into consulting sub-agents eliminates bias while preserving rational authority; the critic should respond that a reasoner’s commitment to a premise is irreducibly tied to her temporal and social position, which cannot be factored away without losing the phenomenon of deliberative closure—the point at which an agent must stop consulting and act. Have them debate whether deliberative closure can be reconstructed inside a fully factored system, or whether it necessarily requires an undecomposed first-personal stance, with direct back-and-forth that addresses specific arguments rather than talking past each other. Both characters should be equally competent.
