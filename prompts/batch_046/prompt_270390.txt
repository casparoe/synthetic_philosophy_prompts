Please argue that Gödel's rotating universe solution, interpreted correctly, undermines presentism without supporting Gödel's stronger conclusion that objective time is unreal. Your argument should proceed in three steps. First, explain why the absence of global Cauchy surfaces and the presence of closed timelike curves in the Gödel metric make a globally unique present impossible, but leave local causal structure and proper-time ordering intact. Second, defend the claim that this situation refutes presentism—since presentism requires a globally privileged hyperplane of simultaneity—while remaining fully compatible with a relationalist or B-theoretic eternalism in which temporal relations are locally real but globally conventional. Third, contend that Gödel's inference from the mathematical possibility of this spacetime geometry to the unreality of time in all worlds rests on an illicit modal generalization: he takes a feature specific to homogeneous rotating solutions (the global non-orientability of a cosmic time coordinate) and treats it as evidence that no physically possible world contains an objective lapse of time. In developing the third point, address whether the local measurability of proper time along every timelike curve provides enough structure to preserve temporal realism in a weaker, non-global form, and whether Gödel's argument implicitly assumes that temporal realism requires a universal synchronization that physics does not mandate. Do not cite specific scholars or texts; build the case from the conceptual structure of the metric and the philosophy of time alone.
