I have recently learned of credible reports of serious moral misconduct by an artist whose work I have long admired, and I find myself trying to hold together reactions that feel incompatible. Before presenting my view to a reading group, I should like an audit of consistency.

Here are my commitments:

1. Aesthetic autonomism: a work's artistic value supervenes solely on its internal perceptual, formal, and expressive properties, with no essential dependence on the creator's biography or moral behaviour.
2. Moderate ethical criticism: when a work expresses a morally defective perspective in an aesthetically integrated way, that defect can diminish — though not nullify — its artistic merit.
3. Separation of work from artist: knowledge that the creator was vicious should not alter my aesthetic evaluation of the finished piece, even if it changes my willingness to display or fund it.
4. Ambient moral valence: some works carry a diffuse ethical atmosphere that is not reducible to explicit propositional content; I hold that this atmosphere is partly constituted by the work's reception history and by the public moral standing of its creator.
5. Restricted interpretive licence: if a work is genuinely ambiguous about its ethical stance, I may legitimately use knowledge of the artist's character to resolve that ambiguity, provided the resolution does not feed back into the work's aesthetic valuation.
