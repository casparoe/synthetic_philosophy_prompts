I sit on the Council on Practice and Care at our monestary — yes, spelling is not our strong suit — and we need to vote by next Tuesday on whether to pilot a new intake protocol for our eight-bed hospice wing. Right now we basically do first-come-first-served guided by the senior monks intution, but one of our lay board members proposed this framework she calls a dharma-calculus. For every applicant you score three things: intensity of current dukkha, the realistic likelihood that being in our environment supports actual vimutti rather then just comfort, and the cetana-quality of the care team — meaning whether they can treat the person without greed or aversion creeping in, weighted like a mental-factor multiplier from the Abhidhamma lists. She frames it as negative-utilitarian but with Buddhist psychology baked in, and says if we refuse to measure we are just hiding implicit bias behind spiritual language.

The council is split pretty sharply. Some monks say quantifying spiritual potential turns karuna into accounting and that treating sila and cetana as variables in an equation makes them instrumental rather then intrinsic — precepts are supposed to be cultivated for their own sake, not optimized like resources. Others argue the Bodhisattva vow actually demands we prioritize the worst suffering if we have the means, and that pretending intuition is neutral is just letting unexamined hierarchy decide who suffers more. I need you to draft the memo I will circulate before Thursdays meeting: recommend approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions for a 6-month trial, with a concrete timeframe and whatever guardrails you think are necessary. You need to treat the strongest argument for the losing side honestly, not just dismiss it. And please, say explicitly how confident you are in the recommendation and single out the specific point where you feel least sure — maybe it's whether cetana can actually be assessed without self-deception, or whether vimutti is even a legitimate outcome metric for hospice care, I genuinely dont know.
