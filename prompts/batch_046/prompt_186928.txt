Read Mary Shelley’s Frankenstein through the lens of the philosophical zombie debate and the conceivability argument for metaphysical dualism. The creature is assembled from dead tissue, acquires language and reason through observation, and reports intense inner suffering—yet Victor and the other humans consistently treat him as lacking any genuine consciousness, as though physical human form combined with intelligent behavior still leaves open the possibility of an absence of inner experience. Your task is to construct a critical reading that takes both the novel and the standard Chalmers-style conceivability argument seriously. Where does the narrative support the idea that a physically and behaviorally identical being could lack phenomenal consciousness—perhaps in Victor’s refusal to grant moral standing despite the creature’s manifest capacities? Where does it resist that idea, by giving the reader direct narrative access to the creature’s first-person anguish in ways that make denial of his consciousness seem willful rather than reasonable? Further, consider whether the creature is better understood not as a phenomenal zombie but as an inverse case: a being with full phenomenal consciousness who is socially and functionally treated as dead or absent, and ask whether this inversion exposes a weakness in using conceivability alone to establish metaphysical conclusions, since conceivability runs in both directions but ethical recognition runs only one. Do not reduce the novel to an illustration of the argument; instead, identify what the novel adds to the debate—particularly regarding the moral stakes of denying consciousness to a being that demands recognition—that a standard thought experiment involving twins and logical possibility misses. You may assume familiarity with major plot points and scenes—the creature’s self-education, his demand for a companion, Victor’s refusal and subsequent abandonment—but do not quote the text verbatim; paraphrase specific moments. End by stating whether the novel ultimately vindicates or undermines the claim that phenomenal zombies are genuinely conceivable.
