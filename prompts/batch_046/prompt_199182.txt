I'm preparing questions for a podcast interview with a philosopher who recently advanced a revisionist thesis about incommensurable values and hard choice. Rather than treating hard choices as rational failures resolved by reason-creation—along the lines Ruth Chang has defended—she argues that genuine value conflicts exhibit what she calls value interference, a structural relation in which realizing one value actively undermines the social or practical conditions necessary for another, as distinct from mere value collision, where trade-offs are painful but compatible. She applies this framework to the debate over the agricultural revolution, claiming that the transition from hunter-gatherer life to agrarian society was not a simple case of exchanging affluence and leisure for security and surplus, but an instance of value interference: the agrarian restructuring of labor and hierarchy destroyed the very conditions—dispersed social organization, immediate return, gender egalitarianism—that made hunter-gatherer affluence meaningful, rather than merely substituting one good for another.

Write me a sequence of interview questions that press her specifically on the interference/collision distinction. The questions should anticipate her standard replies: that interference is identifiable by a phenomenological signature she terms normative residue—the persistent sense that the unchosen value still makes a claim that cannot be fully honored—whereas collision produces only regret; that Chang's account of creating reasons covertly assumes value monism by treating all hard choices as resolvable through rational commitment; and that critics of the agricultural revolution misunderstand the transformation of leisure into ritual or seasonal downtime rather than its elimination. Include a follow-up asking what historical or anthropological evidence could count against the interference interpretation of agrarian transitions, and conclude with a question about whether her view entails a form of practical paralysis whenever values interfere irreducibly, or whether she can accommodate decisive action without betraying the unchosen value.
