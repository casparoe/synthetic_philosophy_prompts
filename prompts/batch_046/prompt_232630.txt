I'm writing a feature on whether physics has dissolved the present, and I've constructed an extension of the Rietdijk-Putnam argument that my editor thinks proves too much—perhaps not just presentism, but any view that ties consciousness to temporal location. Here is the chain.

(1) In Minkowski spacetime there is no invariant hyperplane of simultaneity: for spacelike-separated events E and F, some inertial frames place E before F, others reverse them, and still others make them simultaneous. (2) So if presentism is true and "now" denotes an objective slice, physics offers no fact of the matter about which slice is correct; presentism must either invoke a metaphysically privileged frame that physics cannot detect, or abandon global presentness for a localized version in which each worldline carries its own present. (3) But human experience is not punctate: it has a "specious present"—a brief duration of a few seconds within which events feel co-present. (4) In special relativity, the measured duration of an interval is frame-dependent (time dilation): a span that lasts Δt in one frame lasts γΔt in another. (5) Therefore the temporal thickness of the specious present is frame-relative: relative to a rapidly moving observer, my "now" might span minutes, or shrink to milliseconds. (6) If being fully conscious requires falling within a specious present of appropriate duration, then whether a mental state in another observer counts as conscious is frame-relative. (7) Yet we do not treat consciousness as frame-relative: accelerating past a colleague does not seem to truncate or extend their awareness. (8) To block this, one might treat the specious present as purely psychological, with no metaphysical boundary. (9) But then presentism is emptied: if the present's edges are psychologically plastic and physically frame-dependent, there is no objective fact about which mental states are present, past, or future—only local, incommensurable reports from each worldline. (10) So the argument seems to show that tying consciousness to presentness forces us either into an undetectable privileged frame or into a scattered ontology of worldline-bound "present islands" with no intersubjective tissue, undermining not just presentism but the coherence of shared temporal reality.

I feel the slip is somewhere between the physics of duration and the metaphysics of consciousness, but I cannot name the exact step. Which premise should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost—does it save presentism only by severing it from consciousness, or does it require accepting that consciousness itself is frame-dependent? If forced to preserve one claim, I would keep (7). Please conclude with Verdict: [the step number you reject].
