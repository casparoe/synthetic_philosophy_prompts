Write an essay about whether artifacts realy have politics, not just in how people use them but baked into their design. Focus on the tension between people who say tools are neutral until someone picks them up, and people who think a bridge or an algorithm can be democratic or authoritarian on its own. Then apply it to this specific case: imagine a cooperative farm using smart irrigation gates that automatically cut water to fields with overdue payments. The manufacturer claims its just a neutral flow controller, but the cooperative members argue the gate encodes a market logic that undermines their collective ownership norms.

Weigh both sides seriouslly. Is the gate political in itself, or only when the coop decides to link it to payment data? Could you redesign the gate to stay technically identical but avoid the political effect, or is that impossible? Reach a conclusion about where the politics actually lives and justify it. Don't just list philosophers — argue through the case.
