I'm putting together a taxonomy for my philosophy blog on consequentialist cluelessness, but with a specific twist I call the "cluelessness cascade" — the idea that in long-range domains it's not just that outcomes are unknown, but that the tools we use to reduce uncertainty (models, expert delegation, scenario planning) add thier own layers of opacity. I want a decision tree, each branch labeled by the claim that splits it.

Start with: is the cascade real or overstated? Then: is it a problem unique to consequentialism or does it hit every framework? Then: do epistemic tools mitigate it or deepen it? Then: what's the practical response — abandon direct calculation for rules, stick with approximate expected-value reasoning despite the opacity, or restrict our moral concern to near-term effects?

Each leaf should carry a position name (or 'unnamed') and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Make sure these appear: the approximate-EV view, the rule-based retreat, the 'every framework is equally clueless' view, and the near-term restriction view. Note any unoccupied branch combos and say briefly why — incoherent, unattractive, unexplored? Flag pairs your tree can't really separate, like some forms of rule-consequentialism and satisficing. Use a concrete case: should someone leave local charity work for AI governance, given both have wildly opaque long-range outcomes? Keep it casual, a typo or two is fine, and don't bother with citations or reading lists.
