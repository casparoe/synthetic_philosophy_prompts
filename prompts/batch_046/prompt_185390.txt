Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on technological determinism — specifically whether artifacts have politics embedded in their material form, or whether politics only lives in the social relations around them.

Camp one: the hard determinists. They think physical features of an artifact can directly require or forbid certain social arrangements — not just make them easier or harder, but actually embed a political logic in the thing itself.

Camp two: the relational constructivists. They say an object is politically neutral until it gets woven into practices of design, repair, governance, and resistance; the politics is in the network, never in the object alone.

Write the document they'd both put their names to. Structure it like this: first, a numbered list of agreed claims about what actually happens when a technology reshapes power relations — things both sides observe and don't dispute. Second, a table of disagreements, with the determinist's wording in one column and the constructivist's in the other, phrased so each camp recognises its own view without feeling misrepresented. Third, the cruxes — the sub-questions where if you settled them, at least one side would shift. I suspect the main ones are: can a material feature be politically active without a community that interprets it? and does the long-term stability of an artifact's social effects count as proof that those effects were embedded?

For each crux, propose a concrete hypothetical: a distributed mesh irrigation system that automatically rations water based on field size, rolled out in two different governement contexts — one with strong local councils, one with centralised control. What would each camp predict we'd see? What observation would actually move them?

Don't include reading lists or citations. Don't name-drop obscure theorists unless you can paraphrase without quotes. And check whether part of their fight is just verbal — if so, identify which part and why. Write it in casual British English, like notes for a working group; a typo or two is fine.
