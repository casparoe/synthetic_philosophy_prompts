Below are five commitments I hold regarding Hofstadter's strange-loop conception of the self, and I want an audit of whether they form a coherent package or whether hidden contradictions force me to abandon at least one.

(1) The first-person 'I' is best understood as a strange loop: a self-referential symbol that emerges from substrate-level neural dynamics, and it is a real pattern rather than an illusion or mere fiction. (2) The loop is in principle substrate-independent: gradual replacement of neural tissue by functionally equivalent artificial components would preserve the loop, provided the causal and informational architecture is maintained. (3) Nevertheless, the loop possesses a coherence threshold tied to what Hofstadter describes as a centre of narrative gravity; if that centre fragments — as in severe dissociative states or certain profound alterations of consciousness — the self genuinely dissolves, even though lower-level processing continues uninterrupted. (4) Consequently, I reject strong eliminativism: the self is not a useful fiction but an actual pattern instantiated in the dynamics, comparable in reality to a vortex or a running programme. (5) At the same time, I am drawn to the view that in deep contemplative or psychedelic conditions where first-person narrative continuity collapses entirely, there is literally no subject present — not merely an altered subject, but an absence of the loop itself.

Are these jointly tenable? Specifically, does (2) commit me to preserving the loop across any substrate-level preservation, thereby undermining (3) and (5)? Or can I consistently maintain that substrate continuity is necessary but not sufficient, and that narrative coherence is the decisive factor? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what giving it up would cost me, and distinguish genuine conflicts from apparent ones that dissolve once one separates substrate continuity from pattern coherence. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then provide the reasoning with no preamble and no restatement of the question. Answer in British English.
