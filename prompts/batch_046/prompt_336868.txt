i run a grad workshop on historical sociology, about 10 people, 90 min, no prep. design a classroom game that makes them feel why "collapse" is mostly an accounting glitch — using the Bronze Age eastern med, classic maya lowlands, and Rapa Nui. split them into groups of 3, each gets a hidden metric card: ceramic diversity, royal inscriptions, pollen, or oral-tradition continuity (the last one is the twist — it almost never leaves traces but the people kept going). they have 15 min per society to reach a single verdict — collapsed, transformed, or reconfigured — but they can only cite data that fits their metric, and they must hide their card until the vote. payoff: if the group picks "collapsed" but the hidden oral-tradition card says continuity was high, everyone loses a point; if they resist the collapse label when metrics disagree, they gain one. materials: just index cards and a timer. debrief should map the hidden-card problem onto real debates — especially how the maya "collapse" was mostly elite writing stopping, not people vanishing — and be honest that the activity cant reproduce the actual archaeological ambiguity, so you have to supply that in the wrap-up. also tell me what predictably breaks the first run — probably participants will distrust the oral-tradition card as "fake data" and over-weight the pollen. dont ask for bibliographies or citations, keep it self-contained.
