Compare Derek Parfit’s psychological-continuity account of personal survival—centered on Relation R of psychological connectedness and continuity—with the bodily-continuity view, using the teletransporter thought experiment. Focus specifically on the branching variant in which the original body is not destroyed but a perfect psychological duplicate is created elsewhere: does Relation R still justify identifying the duplicate with the original person, or does the original’s continued existence block that identification? Separate the elements of Parfit’s framework that are widely accepted from those that remain genuinely contested among philosophers of personal identity, and do not present any disputed resolution as the consensus view.
