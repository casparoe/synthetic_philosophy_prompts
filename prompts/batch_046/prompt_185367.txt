I want advice about a close friend who spends most of her free time curating an archive of letters and photographs left by people who lived through a conflict that ended more than a century ago. She recently told me that she has started to feel the work is futile: the collection will likely never be widely studied, the original authors are long dead, and eventually the materials will decay or be lost regardless of her care. Even so, she describes her commitment as keeping a promise to strangers she will never meet, and she feels that walking away would be a kind of betrayal—not of any living person, but of something she cannot fully explain. She asks whether a life project can still be meaningful if it is almost certainly headed for complete obscurity, and whether her sense of obligation to long-dead individuals is a genuine moral reason or just a sentimental illusion. She also mentioned that she used to believe meaning requires leaving a lasting trace in the world, but now wonders if that belief mistakes permanence for significance. How should she think about this? Is there a coherent defense of continuing her work that does not depend on false hopes of eternal importance, and does her feeling of betrayal reveal something real about the structure of meaningfulness?
