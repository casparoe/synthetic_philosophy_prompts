Following a lengthy and rather frustrating argument with a friend about the actual explanatory status of Max Weber's thesis, I want a decomposed credence rather than a qualitative defense. My friend maintained that Weber's Protestant ethic is ultimately a redescription of institutional transformation—specifically, the dissolution of monastic property restrictions and the codification of contract law in Reformed territories—disguised in the vocabulary of values, and that every supposed cultural cause collapses into material or legal antecedents upon serious historical inspection. I countered that there may be an autonomous causal pathway in which theological commitments regarding predestination and the concept of vocation reshaped intertemporal preferences and risk-calculation among economic agents in ways that formal institutions could not fully determine, especially in contexts preceding modern banking structures. But I admit my position feels more like a hunch than a calibrated judgment.

Therefore, please state your actual credence—between 0 and 1—that internalized religious values, of the kind Weber identifies, constitute an independent causal factor in the emergence of modern capitalist behavior, rather than being epiphenomenal to institutional and material conditions. Decompose this into three terms with explicit probabilities: first, the prior that values can in principle drive macroeconomic behavior without being reducible to institutional incentives, perhaps through altering subjective discount rates or collective risk tolerance at scale; second, the likelihood that Weber's observed correlation between Calvinist regions and capital accumulation survives rigorous controls for property-rights security, trade-network density, and pre-existing legal frameworks; third, the probability that culture is sufficiently well-bounded as an explanatory concept here, rather than functioning as a residual category for variance that institutional explanations fail to capture. Show how they combine into your final number. Then identify which term is the weakest link in your reasoning, and specify what form of evidence—whether a natural experiment in economic history, a conceptual clarification of cultural causation, or a formal model of value-driven preference formation—would most significantly shift your overall credence. I am not asking for a bibliography or a survey of secondary literature; I want the reasoning made explicit, with its vulnerabilities marked.
