I am preparing a discussion for a philosophy-of-literature seminar on Leo Tolstoy’s aesthetic theory and the tension between that theory and his own narrative practice. Please read Tolstoy’s novella The Death of Ivan Ilyich through the lens of his essay What Is Art?, concentrating on his view that true art communicates a sincerely felt emotion so that the audience is infected with the same feeling, and that false art is marked by obscurity, artificial technique, and dependence on specialized education that excludes ordinary people. Examine the major turning points in the story—Ivan’s growing awareness that his bureaucratic life is hollow, his terror before the black sack, and the final flash of light and pity—to determine whether the novella operates as Tolstoyan true art: does it make the horror of inauthentic living and the relief of acceptance universally available, or do the specifics of a nineteenth-century Russian legal milieu and the highly stylized, chronologically inverted narrative structure restrict its emotional reach to an educated elite, reproducing exactly the class-bound exclusivity Tolstoy condemns?

Please also weigh the further complication that Tolstoy, in his later religious period, effectively repudiated his own major novels as sinful vanity and turned instead to simplified moral tales meant for peasants. Does this later renunciation alter how we apply his aesthetic criteria to Ivan Ilyich, or does it expose a contradiction in his theory between the demands of artistic sincerity and the demands of moral edification? Take both the work and the philosophy seriously: do not reduce the novella to an illustration of the essay, and do not bend Tolstoy’s concepts to fit the plot. Show precisely where the idea illuminates Ivan’s choices and where the text resists—especially whether the deathbed epiphany, which is intensely private and barely articulated, can genuinely transmit feeling to the reader in the way the theory requires. End by identifying what the novella adds to philosophical debates about art, feeling, and mortality that pure argument does not supply.

Write in American English. At the close of your response, please say how confident you are in your central interpretation and point out the specific claim you are least sure about.
