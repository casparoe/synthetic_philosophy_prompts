Please argue that prioritarianism escapes the leveling-down objection only if its distributive weights are interpreted as linear and fully fungible across individuals. Defend the stronger claim that, under a convex, threshold-sensitive weighting scheme—where benefits near a critical subsistence floor receive exponentially greater priority—prioritarianism actually endorses a restricted form of leveling-down in cases involving structurally incommensurable goods: resources or advantages held by the better-off that cannot be transferred downward without being destroyed, such as certain positional goods or non-convertible status markers. In such cases, reducing the welfare of the advantaged can be required not to improve the absolute position of the worst-off directly, but to prevent the distortion of the prioritarian calculus that occurs when incommensurable surpluses accumulate at the top. Respond to the objection that prioritarianism is concerned solely with absolute well-being levels rather than relative positions, and show why the incommensurability condition blocks that defense without reducing prioritarianism to pure egalitarianism. Be precise about how the convexity of the weighting function interacts with the non-transferability constraint, and clarify exactly where the boundary lies between permissible redistribution and the kind of leveling-down that remains objectionable even on your view.
