As a novelist accustomed to constructing plots through dramatic irony, focalized knowledge, and the tension between character agency and authorial design, I ask you to translate the classic Death in Damascus scenario—and the associated philosophical dispute between causal and evidential reasoning about it—into the vocabulary of narrative craft. Treat the situation as if it were being plotted by an author who has predetermined the ending: a protagonist encounters Death in Damascus, receives a warning, flees to Aleppo, and dies there by falling masonry. Restate both philosophical positions—the view that the protagonist ought to remain in Damascus because leaving causally produces the fatal encounter in Aleppo, and the view that he ought to flee because remaining correlates with being sought—using only concepts from fiction writing: plot causality versus thematic foreshadowing, diegetic knowledge (what the character can act upon) versus extra-diegetic knowledge (what the reader or author already knows), narrative inevitability, the economy of counterfactual plot branches, and the distinction between an event being causally necessitated within the story-world and being symbolically prefigured by the narrative structure. Be precise about how each position would be articulated by a writer deciding whether the protagonist's flight is a genuine intervention into the plot or merely an enactment of an already-written destiny.

Then provide a residue report. Identify which philosophical claims survive translation cleanly into narrative terms, which survive only with distortion—for example, whether "causal intervention" requires smuggling in an author-god capable of rewriting diegetic events, or whether "evidential correlation" collapses into nothing more than dramatic irony for the reader. State clearly whether there are elements of the philosophical disagreement that have no counterpart whatsoever in how novelists think about fate, plot architecture, and rational choice under predetermined outcomes. I am especially interested in whether the residue indicates that my craft vocabulary genuinely lacks a formal distinction between correlation and causation inside a fictional ontology, or whether it reveals that part of the philosophers' divergence is an artifact of framing rather than a substantive disagreement about rational action. Do not supply bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want the analysis to stand on its own conceptual merits, without reliance on external reference.
