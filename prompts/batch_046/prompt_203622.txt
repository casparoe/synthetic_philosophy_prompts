My roommate Sam and his partner Jordan got into it last night about whether dreams are actually experiences, like that old Malcolm vs Dennett debate but with their own spin. Sam started by saying dreams can't be experiences because the sleeper can't respond or correct mistakes in real time — if you're not awake enough to check, there's no experience happening, just brain noise that gets narrated later. Jordan said that sounds too quick: people report vivid colors, fear, even reasoning in dreams, so there's clearly some kind of mental life going on, not just post-hoc storytelling.

Sam said those reports are always reconstructed after waking, so you're not describing a live experience, you're describing a memory of a story your brain assembled on the spot — like that cassette idea Dennett talked about but he doesn't call it that. Jordan brought up lucid dreamers who can do pre-planned eye signals to researchers while asleep, which proves there's real-time awarness and not just a tape playing back.

Sam countered that eye signals only show minimal arousal, not full phenomenal experience — it's like being half-awake, not proof of rich dream-worlds. Then Jordan threw in this idea they called "phenominal attenuation": maybe dream experience isn't on/off like Malcolm and Dennett both assume, but dialed way down — sensory modality is suspended but intentional content is still there, kind of like seeing through fog.

Sam said that's unfalsifiable because we have no way to measure "dimness" except through the same waking reports you're trying to explain, so it smuggles experience back in by definition. Jordan said then he's just being a skeptic about other minds generally.

Can you walk through their exchange and say who's right at each step? Don't just pick a winner — tell me where each of them lands a real point and what they're both missing, especially about whether "phenominal attenuation" solves anything or just renames the problem.
