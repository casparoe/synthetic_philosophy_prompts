If David Hume were alive today and heard philosophers talking about the meta-problem of consciousness — not the hard problem of why brain stuff produces feeling, but the puzzle of why we intuit there's an explanatory gap at all — what do you think he'd say? I'm specifcally wondering if he'd treat that gap-intuition as just another custom or habit of association, the way he does with causation, or if his bundle theory of the self makes him think there's something special about how we access our own mental states. There's also this framing that the meta-problem is easier than the hard one since it's only about explaining a belief, not consciousness itself — do you think Hume'd buy that, or would he argue explaining why we feel the gap is every bit as mysterious?
