Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on the question: is honoring sunk costs always irrational?

Camp A (normative individualism) holds that giving any weight to unrecoverable past expenditure in present choice violates the separability axioms of rational decision theory and is indefensible, even in strategic, collective, or identity-constituting contexts.

Camp B (strategic commitment) holds that in observable, multi-agent settings with endogenous preference formation, honoring unrecoverable costs can serve as a credible commitment mechanism or stabilize intertemporal preferences, so that labeling the behavior a "fallacy" illegitimately applies an individual-level normative framework to collective or long-horizon decision problems.

Produce a structured document containing:

1. Agreed claims: precise statements both sides can sign regarding the formal separation of past and future costs, empirically documented psychological mechanisms, and the conditions under which costs are genuinely unrecoverable.

2. A disagreements table: for each residual dispute, state Camp A’s formulation and Camp B’s formulation in wording each camp accepts. Focus especially on whether rationality should be evaluated ex post or ex ante, whether collective-level evaluation is legitimate, and whether identity-constituting projects alter the normative status of unrecoverable investment.

3. Cruxes and predictive tests: identify the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side. Include one microdomain crux—specifically, whether continuing to maintain a failing open-source software dependency solely because of prior contributor investment counts as rational commitment or fallacious reasoning—and specify what evidence (for example, long-term contributor retention patterns or ecosystem survival rates) both sides agree would bear on it, recording each side’s predicted outcome in advance.

4. Verbal diagnosis: state whether any portion of the dispute reduces to ambiguity in “honoring,” “sunk cost,” or “irrational,” and specify exactly which portion.

Keep the statement formal and precise, avoid all citations and bibliographic references, and do not include quotations from specific texts.
