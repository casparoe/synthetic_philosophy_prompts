As a doctoral student in philosophy of mind working on artificial cognition, I want to fix the evaluation criteria before comparing particular theories of belief attribution to AI systems. Write the desiderata an adequate account must satisfy: present exactly five numbered points, each in one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, in American English. Point 1 should specify how the theory distinguishes a system that genuinely tracks false beliefs for predictive success from one that merely correlates token distributions, with the motivating case behind the distinction. Point 2 should state whether the account must preserve a boundary between derived and original intentionality, and why that distinction matters for engineered systems lacking evolutionary history. Point 3 should address the tension that arises when full design-stance transparency—complete knowledge of weights and architecture—fails to eliminate the utility of intentional interpretation. Point 4 should identify which architectural facts about current transformer-based models, such as the absence of persistent causally integrated internal states with belief-like functional roles, the theory must accommodate without contradiction. Point 5 should flag which of these demands are genuinely non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences, and within that same point state how confident you are in the overall framework and single out the one desideratum you are least sure is correctly formulated.
