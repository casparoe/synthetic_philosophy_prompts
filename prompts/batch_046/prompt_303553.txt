I'm preparing a podcast interview with a philosopher who has recently advanced a reconstructed, neo-Bradleyean account of propositional unity. She argues that Bradley's regress is dissolved not by adopting an external relational tie—whether Russellian, structuralist, or truth-functional—but by treating the terms of a proposition as internally differentiated moments within a single concrete universal, so that "R relates a to b" is never a separate constituent but a pattern of articulation intrinsic to the universal itself. She extends this to falsehood: false propositions are genuinely unified structures that fail to be instantiated in reality, yet they are not independent non-actual entities; rather, they are modal modifications of actual articulations, which allows her to avoid negative facts without collapsing into a Meinongian realm of subsistent objects. I want to press her on the mechanics of this view without letting her retreat to the claim that internal differentiation is merely primitive.

Please draft a sequence of interview questions—each paired with a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give—that covers the following four pressure points. First, if no external relation binds the terms, how does she distinguish a genuinely unified false proposition from a mere list or an unstructured aggregate, and what is wrong with the deflection that internal differentiation is just a basic fact about concrete universals? Second, for complex falsehoods—say, the conjunction that a is not F and b is not G—does she need a second-order articulation that binds the negations together, and doesn't that threaten a new regress at the level of meta-articulation rather than dissolving the original one? Third, if true propositions are made true by concrete universals, does she commit to a form of truthmaker maximalism in which every true proposition demands its own maximally specific universal, and how can she avoid that consequence without reintroducing external relations between universals and propositions? Fourth, what psychological or logical evidence does she take to support internal differentiation, given that she denies introspection reveals any relational "glue" in judgment, and doesn't that leave her account empirically underdetermined in a way that Russellian or structuralist rivals are not? For each, include the anticipated deflection and the sharper follow-up that makes it progressively harder to use.
