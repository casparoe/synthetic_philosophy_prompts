Our audience has no formal logic, though they are fluent in rule-governed reasoning from laboratory and engineering practice. I want the session to turn on a tension that popular accounts usually gloss over: leaving a formal system is not an unqualified intellectual advance, because one trades the derivational certainty available inside the system for the broader perspective gained outside it, and I want this cost to be structurally present in the carrying example rather than tacked on as a moral.

Please design the explanation: the precise order in which the pieces must be introduced, with each step justified by what it depends on; the one concrete scenario that should carry the idea—specifically, an analog to the MU-puzzle built around a closed laboratory calibration protocol whose internal rules can verify consistency among measurements but can never derive the value of the external physical standard, so the audience feels the jump in terms they know; what that scenario must include and what it must deliberately omit so the tension between internal rigor and external reference stays sharp; the predictable misconceptions this audience will bring—especially the inclination to dismiss formal rules as arbitrary game-rules, the opposite inclination to romanticize “jumping out” as an intuitive or mystical leap, and the overgeneralization that every formal system is “incomplete” in the same way—together with the exact point in the sequence where each should be defused; a brief check for understanding that reveals whether the idea has actually landed rather than received polite assent; and what technical elements (for instance, Gödel numbering, recursive axiom schemas, or full formalization of the MIU system) should be openly set aside at this level, with me stating that exclusion explicitly.
