I run a philosophy workshop for about 16 postgrads, 75 mins. Design an in-class game that makes them feel how intuition pumps persuade even after you've shown them the machinery. Use a custom scenario — not the violinist or trolley — about a "partial upload" where one person's neural patterns get copied into two subtrates with slightly different emotional weights, and participants have to vote anonymously on whether we owe loyalty to both copies, one, or none. The rules should split them into small groups who must defend their vote using only analogie, not principles, then reveal that the logical skeleton is identical to a division-of-labour case they've already rejected. Include the vote-before and vote-after structure, a debrief that maps the shift onto narrative transportation and affective framing, an honest note that the activity can't reproduce the first-person phenomological pull that makes the pump feel immediate, and a warning about what'll go wrong — probably people refusing to admit their vote flipped. Say what argument or finding would change your mind about whether intuition pumps genuinely mislead rather than just expose hidden commitments.
