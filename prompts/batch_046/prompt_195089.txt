I have come to hold a somewhat specific view about historical contingency—partly inspired by chaos theory, but applied in a way that differs from the usual "for want of a nail" story. I think large-scale historical trajectories are not uniformly sensitive to small causes; rather, they are punctuated by rare "bifurcation windows," brief intervals when institutional structures fail to absorb perturbation, during which micro-events (a misdelivered diplomatic dispatch, a localized epidemic in a capital, a misinterpreted order) can cascade into radically different long-term outcomes. Outside these windows, I assume strong path dependence and what I would call "institutional damping": societies absorb minor disruptions and revert to stable trajectories. So contingency is real, but it is spatially and temporally confined rather than pervasive.

What would count against this view? I am not asking for the standard reminder that historians cannot rerun experiments. I want an honest map of its exposure. Which kinds of evidence or argument would genuinely undermine the claim that contingency is localized to these windows? For example: comparative historical cases in which major civilizational divergences clearly occurred without any identifiable structural bottleneck or micro-trigger; formal models or simulation results showing that institutional structures never actually dampen perturbations in the way I describe; or arguments that the very distinction between a "window" and normal path dependence collapses into post-hoc rationalization. Please rank these potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Distinguish what a committed defender of this view should honestly concede as counting against them versus what they could legitimately dismiss by refining the concept of a window or of damping. Also, please say how confident you are in each part of your assessment, and single out the specific point—perhaps whether institutional damping is empirically distinguishable from retrospective storytelling—about which you are least sure.
