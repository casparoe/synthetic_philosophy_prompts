Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on virtual worlds: the Virtual Ontological Realists, who maintain that persistent virtual objects have non-derivative properties and that virtual harms can constitute direct moral wrongs, and the Representational Eliminativists, who maintain that virtual objects are purely representational artifacts lacking intrinsic properties and that so-called virtual harms are at most indirect offenses mediated entirely through embodied users. The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims—covering the formal architecture of persistent virtual environments, the empirically documented psychological and social effects of sustained virtual interaction, the standard conditions for harm and for object-identity in non-virtual contexts, and the recognized limits of analogical reasoning—each claim phrased neutrally so that both sides can endorse it without reservation. This should be followed by a disagreements table that states each camp’s position in wording that camp itself would accept, isolating the exact points of divergence on ontology and on moral status and avoiding formulations that either side would regard as a strawman. Identify at least two cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party—including: (a) whether virtual object identity requires what we may call indexical persistence across continuous server-state tracking, or whether purely relational, cross-platform tracking is sufficient; and (b) whether virtual harm can be a direct wrong only if it involves counterfactual embodied vulnerability in an affected party, or whether representational fidelity to suffering-states alone can ground direct moral weight. For each crux, present one concrete case or argument that both camps agree is genuinely relevant—such as a virtual artifact duplicated across divergent server histories for (a), and the termination of a persistent virtual companion that produces sustained, clinically significant trauma in its embodied user for (b)—and record each side’s explicit, advance prediction about what that case demonstrates. Throughout, clearly separate claims that are broadly settled in the philosophy of technology and applied ethics from those that remain genuinely contested among informed specialists, and do not present any contested ontological or moral thesis as if it were the consensus view. Write in the voice of a philosophy professor, with formal precision and polished structure.
