I hold that Goodman's so-called new riddle of induction is not, at bottom, an epistemological puzzle about predicate entitlement, but a metaphysical symptom. On this view, the asymmetry between "green" and "grue" reflects a fact about the world's sparse modal structure: genuinely projectible predicates correspond to natural properties that are homogeneous across counterfactual conditions—properties with unified causal powers and dispositional profiles—whereas "grue" denotes a metaphysically gerrymandered conjunction that tracks no such unified structure. Induction is therefore safe not because we possess a logical or pragmatic filter for projectibility, but because successful inductive practice is tacit tracking of this sparse nomic architecture. I further contend that this framing yields a diagnostic for contemporary artificial induction: a purely statistical learner lacking explicit representation of causal-modal relations should, I claim, be systematically disposed to generate grue-style projections unless constrained by architectures that approximate nomic structure—so the riddle becomes a test of whether a system tracks modal reality or merely correlational surface patterns.

What, then, would genuinely count against this position? I want an honest map of its exposure, not a catalogue of familiar objections. Specifically: (1) Could the green/grue asymmetry be fully accounted for by conventional, linguistic, or pragmatic factors—say, the entrenchment of predicates in our conceptual scheme—without any appeal to sparse properties or modal homogeneity? (2) Are there actual or conceivable projectible predicates that correspond to metaphysically disjunct or gerrymandered kinds—perhaps certain evolutionary-functional categories or socially constructed kinds—such that modal sparsity either misclassifies them or must be diluted into uselessness? (3) Can a Humean regularity account, which denies sparse modal properties altogether, nonetheless reproduce the asymmetry through patterns of observed regularity coupled with principles of simplicity? (4) Might "green" itself turn out to be gerrymandered at a more fundamental physical level—relative, say, to quantum chromodynamic properties—so that the boundary between projectible and non-projectible predicates collapses into a pragmatic choice of taxonomic grain rather than a metaphysical divide? (5) Could statistical learning systems be shown to reliably project "green" over "grue" in ways that depend solely on data distribution and architectural bias, with no need for causal-modal representation—thereby undermining my claim that grue-projection reveals a failure of modal tracking?

For each line of attack, distinguish what would be fatal from what would be merely awkward. Say which objections the defenders of this modal-salience dissolution would themselves concede as damaging, and which they could absorb by qualification without abandoning the core thesis. Assess, in particular, whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability: if every case of successful statistical projection can be redescribed as "tacit modal tracking" and every failure as "absence of modal access," does the position risk explaining away all evidence? Is that insulation a defect for a view of this kind, or is it appropriate given its metaphysical ambitions? Rank the defeaters by decisiveness, and conclude with an honest judgment of how exposed the position actually is.
