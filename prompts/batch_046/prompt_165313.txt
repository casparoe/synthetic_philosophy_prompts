I was talking with a coworker last week about why people in Germany seem to wait at red lights at 3am with no cars around, while Americans usually treat that as a stupid rule if no ones watching. She said American folk morality is clearly better -- rules are only tools, and the only real moral authority is individual conscience, so blindly following them is how you get bureaucratic evil. I said that sounds nice but conscience is just self-interest with good branding, and without some sense of external obligation that doesnt depend on my feelings, social order just falls appart. She came back that "just following orders" is literally the danger of the German view -- people hiding behind rules to avoid moral responsibility. I argued thats missing the point: if your conscience only checks rules you already dislike, then youre still just following rules you happen to like, which isnt autonomy, its preference. So the American model secretly needs people to feel guilty about breaking rules, which is just internalized obedience with extra steps. She then said maybe thats exactly right -- theres no pure "outside" authority, just social contracts we internalize differently -- and Americans are more honest that the obligation is contingent on our endorsement, while Germans pretend its external even when theyve fully bought in. We cant agree. Can you go through our exchange step by step and say whos right at each move? Specifically: (1) her claim that individual conscience is the better moral foundation, (2) my reply about conscience being unreliable and external obligation being necessary, (3) her counter about bureaucratic evil and responsibility, (4) my point that conscience-checks are just internalized preference, and (5) her final claim about contingency and honesty. For each, rate it from clearly wrong to mostly right and explain why. Then tell me whos more right overall, and what both of us are missing -- especially about whether the difference is really about structure or just about how people experience the same obligation differently.
