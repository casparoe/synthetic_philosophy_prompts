I'm a doctoral student in epistemology writing my dissertation chapter on self-locating belief and probabilistic updating. I need a classification instrument for the debate over the Sleeping Beauty problem that sorts positions by what they actually commit to, not by the labels philosophers attach to themselves. The audience is other philosophy graduate students who understand basic Bayesian conditioning but have not necessarily memorized the specific literature on centered worlds.

Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy with the following properties. First, it must be built from the logical space of commitments rather than from a list of named views: begin with four or five concrete diagnostic questions—each phrased as a specific case or claim about Beauty's rational credences, not as "do you agree with X?"—that genuinely separate the positions. The questions should cover: (i) how Beauty should update upon being awakened on a Tuesday awakening, given she knows she might have been awakened on Monday; (ii) whether being told "it is Tuesday" provides evidentially redundant information or genuinely new centered information; (iii) whether frequency reasoning over awakenings is legitimate even when the underlying chance is fixed; and (iv) a variant case I want included—call it the Memory-Wipe Variant—in which Beauty is told on Monday that she will be woken on Tuesday regardless of the coin, but her Monday memories are erased before any Tuesday awakening, and she is asked on Tuesday whether the coin came up heads. This variant should function as a diagnostic: different positions should yield different answers about her Tuesday credence.

Second, the tree should have six to eight leaf nodes. These must include: a standard halfer position, a standard thirder position, a double-halfer position, and at least one position that denies there is a uniquely correct credence (e.g., due to ambiguity in the reference class for self-location). Where combinations of answers are logically possible but unoccupied in the literature, list them in an "unoccupied branches" section with a brief diagnosis—whether the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag any pairs of named positions that your tree cannot separate and explain why.

Third, the taxonomy should explicitly distinguish positions that agree on Beauty's Monday credence (1/2) but diverge on her Tuesday credence, since that is where most of the philosophical action lies. It should also note whether a position treats the indexical "I am awake now" as evidentially transparent or as carrying substantive information about the world.

Please deliver the result as a JSON object with the following schema. The top-level keys must be: "diagnostic_questions" (array of objects, each with "question_text", "answer_options" [array of strings], and "separates_on" [string describing the logical distinction]); "leaf_nodes" (array of objects, each with "path" [array of answer choices leading to this leaf], "position_description" [brief, without citation], and "monday_credence" and "tuesday_credence" [each a number or "undefined"]); "unoccupied_combinations" (array of objects with "path" and "diagnosis"); "inseparable_pairs" (array of objects with "pair_names" and "reason"); and "variant_case_analysis" (object with "case_name" set to "Memory-Wipe Variant" and "predicted_credence_patterns" as an object mapping position descriptions to expected answers). Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not ask me to verify paper titles. The instrument should be usable by someone who cannot search the web.
