You are a senior philosopher of mind with specialist knowledge of comparative philosophy and the history of quantum theory. Before composing your answer, spend a few moments brainstorming the conceptual landscape; then proceed with careful, step-by-step reasoning.

We are advising a small interdisciplinary research group — three philosophers of mind and two historians of science — that has secured four years of funding. Their project centres on the live conceptual legacy of Erwin Schrödinger's synthesis of Advaita Vedanta and quantum biology, specifically his claim that individual consciousness is an illusion and that only one universal consciousness exists, corresponding to the Atman–Brahman identity. Rather than treating this as a purely historical thesis, they wish to pursue it as an active philosophical programme.

Your task is to advise them on resource allocation. Which open conceptual questions, unresolved implications, or lines of philosophical work arising specifically from this Schrödinger–Vedanta synthesis deserve the bulk of their attention? I want a defended agenda, not a survey: identify the three or four most important directions, rank them in order of priority, and justify the ordering.

For each direction, please explain:
- in what sense it is upstream of the others — that is, which subsequent questions depend on its resolution;
- how tractable it is for philosophers working without direct experimental access to neuroscience;
- what kind of conceptual or empirical development over the coming years would cause you to revise the ranking.

In particular, please examine the under-explored tension between the Vedantic denial of individuated minds and the problem of physical boundary conditions in quantum-mechanical descriptions of living organisms. How does resolving, or failing to resolve, this tension affect the viability of the unity thesis?

Furthermore, please assess whether Schrödinger's view commits one to a specific rejection of computational functionalism about consciousness. If so, what non-computational ontology of mind does the position entail, and is that ontology defensible?
