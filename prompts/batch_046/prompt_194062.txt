I am auditing a graduate seminar on democratic boundaries, and there is a fellow student—let us call her M—whose judgments on who belongs to the demos have been remarkably consistent across our discussions, even though she has never fully articulated the principle that unifies them. Over the past months she has held that adult citizens with full cognitive capacity belong; that long-term non-citizen residents belong; that children under fourteen belong, but solely through proxy representation by guardians or trustees; that unborn future generations affected by long-range policy belong, represented by appointed advocates; that sentient non-human mammals do not belong, though their welfare must be protected by human representatives rather than by direct membership; that corporations with legal personhood do not belong; and that a dispersed, stateless diaspora community maintaining its own cross-border democratic councils and a shared historical narrative does belong. Next week we turn to a case that I think will split our reconstructions of her view: a cultivated cluster of human cerebral organoids, linked through a neural interface into an integrated network, that participates in collective resource-allocation decisions within its research institution, represented by a designated bioethicist, lacking legal personhood, lacking any biographical or cultural history, and lacking the capacity for suffering, but displaying information-integration dynamics that mirror small-group democratic deliberation.

Please predict M’s verdict on whether this cluster belongs to the demos; identify the implicit rule she seems to be running—whether it centers on embedded reciprocity and care, on stakeholder affectedness coupled with feasible representation, on narrative continuity of a self-governing people, or on some other framework—and say which of her prior rulings offers the strongest support for that reading. Then recommend what she should decide if she wants to remain consistent with her own track record, identifying the closest analog among her past verdicts and being explicit about what principle she would be adopting with each possible choice. I also want you to specify the single further hypothetical case that would best distinguish her actual rule from its closest competitor. In your response, please state clearly how confident you are in each part of this analysis, and single out the specific point about which you are least certain, since I intend to use your reconstruction to check my own seminar notes.
