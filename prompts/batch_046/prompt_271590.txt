Consider a philosopher—call her P—who assigns an inside-view credence of 0.85 to the claim that supererogation constitutes a genuinely distinct deontic category, not reducible to imperfect obligations or agent-relative permissions, based on her direct assessment of conceptual distinctions in paradigmatic cases (the saint, the hero). She then considers an outside-view parameter: suppose that in normative ethics, expert positions on the ontological status of deontic categories reverse at a hypothetical but well-estimated base rate of roughly 40% per generation, and that peer disagreement among equally credentialed specialists in this exact sub-question is currently split near parity. What's your actual credence that, upon full epistemic reflection, P should weight her inside-view reasoning at no more than 50% and therefore arrive at a posterior closer to 0.60 than to her original 0.85? Decompose your answer into four terms: (1) the probability that the outside-view reversal rate is genuinely applicable here rather than distorted by terminological drift or disciplinary fashion; (2) the probability that inside-view reasoning about conceptual divergence—e.g., whether 'ought' and 'may' can come apart in a single act-description—is systematically more resilient to reversal than the base rate suggests; (3) the probability that epistemic modesty demands domain-sensitive weighting—say, that modal and conceptual claims merit less discounting than empirical predictions—such that the correct adjustment lands nearer 0.70; and (4) any other factor you judge relevant, with a one-line justification and a rough number. Show roughly how these combine (multiplicatively, additively, or otherwise) into your final figure. Then identify which term you are least confident in and specify what philosophical argument or finding about expert disagreement dynamics would shift it most.
