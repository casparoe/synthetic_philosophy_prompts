Construct a decision tree for classifying philosophical responses to the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, intended for students who understand special relativity’s denial of absolute simultaneity but have not yet mapped the logical space. The root node asks: “Does the absence of a globally privileged hyperplane of simultaneity entail that all spacetime events exist on an equal ontological footing?” From there, branch first by whether one treats the problem as ontological (about what exists) or as semantic (about what our temporal language tracks). At the second level, ask whether temporal passage is fundamental, frame-dependent, or illusory. The tree’s leaves must include: (a) four-dimensional eternalism with no passage; (b) a “moving spotlight” that retains one present slice despite lacking global simultaneity; (c) frame-relative presentism in which each inertial frame hosts its own present slice, but the slices do not combine into a single global manifold; and (d) a dynamical rejection that blocks the inference from “no unique present” to “all times real” by restricting existence to a frame-independent now. Add a fifth leaf for a logically available combination that appears unoccupied—diagnose whether its emptiness stems from incoherence, instability, or mere neglect. Every node must be phrased as a concrete yes/no or choice question that a student could answer without already knowing the position labels. Flag any pair of leaves the tree genuinely fails to separate, and explain why they collapse into one another. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or named scholars beyond the Rietdijk–Putnam framing itself.
