I do not have any formal training in philosophy, but I have been thinking on my own about whether beauty is objective, and I have arrived at a view that tries to hold a middle position. I think Hume is essentially right that judging beauty involves sentiment rather than pure reasoning, but I also think his notion of a standard of taste is not just a sociological observation about what expert critics happen to agree on. Instead, I understand it as a kind of normative calibration: just as we correct a faulty thermometer by comparing it to a standard instrument, we correct a faulty aesthetic judgment by comparing it to the refined sentiment of qualified observers. On this view, beauty is neither a mind-independent property like mass nor a purely private feeling like a headache. It is what I would call calibrated intersubjectivity—real enough that there are correct and incorrect answers, but constituted by the practice of judgment itself rather than discovered like a physical law.

What would have to be true for this position to be wrong? I want an honest assessment of how exposed it actually is, not a survey of philosophers who disagree. Which observations about real aesthetic disagreement—across cultures, historical periods, or among recognized experts—would be fatal rather than merely awkward? Would it damage the view if expert critics consistently and irreconcilably divided over a specific work, or can that already be explained away? What about the charge of circularity: we seem to identify true judges by their agreement with other true judges. If that circularity is unavoidable, does my view collapse into pure conventionalism? And what if aesthetic experience turns out to be more like color perception, where disagreement might indicate different perceptual apparatus rather than error? Would that undermine the calibration analogy, or could the view absorb it?

Please distinguish between defeaters that should genuinely move me—evidence or arguments that would force me to abandon the middle ground—and ones that defenders of calibrated intersubjectivity could concede without damage. Say also what this view predicts that pure subjectivism and full objectivism do not, so I can see what risks it actually takes. Finally, be direct about whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability: if every disagreement can be dismissed by saying one party lacks refinement, is that insulation a problem for a view of this kind? I would like the answer written in American English.
