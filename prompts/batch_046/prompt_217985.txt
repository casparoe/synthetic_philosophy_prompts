I hold that privacy is best understood not as a right of informational control or as a protected sphere of intimacy, but as a structural condition of "contextual opacity": the capacity to maintain genuinely incompatible self-presentations across distinct social domains without being compelled to integrate them into a single, coherent public identity. On this view, privacy's value is not instrumental to autonomy or welfare, but constitutive of the conditions under which self-revision becomes possible—specifically, by permitting compartmentalized experimentation that need not be reconciled into a narrative available to all observers. I therefore take transparency, not opacity, to be the exceptional state that requires explicit justification.

What would count against this position? I want an honest map of its exposure: which empirical findings about behavior under surveillance, anonymity, or cross-context disclosure; which conceptual distinctions (for instance, between opacity and deception, or between healthy compartmentalization and pathological fragmentation of the self); and which normative arguments from rival accounts—such as the claim that transparency is a precondition of democratic accountability, that opacity erodes the trust required for cooperation, or that a unified public identity is itself a moral achievement—would genuinely threaten the view rather than merely complicate it? Which of these defeaters would a committed defender of contextual-opacity privacy concede as damaging, and which could be absorbed by qualification without collapse? Does the position risk anything testable, such as a prediction about when transparency actually enhances rather than inhibits self-revision, or has it been rendered unfalsifiable by definitional moves that treat any successful integration as proof of prior opacity? And if it turns out to be well insulated against counterevidence, is that insulation a virtue of theoretical stability or a vice indicating empirical emptiness? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say whether the view's defenders have anything they should genuinely fear.
