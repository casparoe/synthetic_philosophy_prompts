I direct a small philanthropic fund that makes sequential, non-recoverable grants to high-risk scientific research. Every quarter I must split limited capital across competing proposals, where payoffs mix knowledge production with reputational risk and where failure shrinks our future capacity to bet. I need a reusable decision procedure—grounded in Kelly betting and geometric rationality—that staff without finance backgrounds can run in about an hour per proposal. It should specify, in ordered steps: how to translate subjective scientific probabilities and heterogeneous, non-monetary payoffs into an edge and odds ratio without false precision; how geometric-mean survival constraints modify the Kelly fraction when proposals are correlated or when organizational continuity requires a capital floor; the exact check sequence with explicit stop points where arithmetic ends and a value judgment takes over, including a hard threshold where the calculated fraction threatens institutional survival; and a frank statement of what the procedure cannot settle. Apply it to three cases: a single large commitment to a controversial geoengineering pilot; a split bet between two competing neuroscience mechanisms that share hidden methodological risk; and a proposal where the Kelly fraction exceeds our risk budget and the board will likely reject the result on intuitive grounds despite the math. In each application, flag which step is a judgment call disguised as calculation, note where correlation or non-ergodicity breaks the simple formula, and show exactly where the procedure produces an answer its users will not want to accept. End by listing the categories of decision this protocol is not equipped to make.
