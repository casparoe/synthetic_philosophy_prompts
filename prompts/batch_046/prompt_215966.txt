I sit on an advisory council that issues recommendations on institutional designs for what we term normative density — the proportion of a social domain governed by explicit, enforced rules rather than tacit, negotiated coordination. Our published precedents include the following judgments.

We endorsed a surgical unit's protocol requiring collective review for every procedural deviation, irrespective of patient outcome. We endorsed a research consortium's practice of determining authorship through informal contribution tracking without arbitration. We endorsed a municipal framework that assigns criminal penalties to structural building-code violations while leaving aesthetic guidelines purely advisory. We endorsed a university honor system that expels for plagiarism but permits students to self-certify mental-health accommodations without documentation. We rejected a logistics cooperative that applied the same enforcement apparatus — identical reporting, sanction, and appeal procedures — to both warehouse-safety protocols and break-room etiquette norms.

We now must advise on a proposed permeable jurisdiction: a residential zone divided into micro-districts, each selecting its own tightness level for shared resources such as water-use quotas and noise limits. Residents may relocate freely among districts; violations in one district do not affect residency rights in others. The proposal mandates uniform monitoring technology across all districts, but sanctions range from automatic financial penalties to purely social acknowledgment depending on each district's selected regime.

What should our recommendation be if we wish to cohere with our own track record? Which prior judgment is the closest analog, and which features of the proposal map onto it? Our precedents appear compatible with more than one organizing principle — for instance, a principle that calibrates enforcement to the objective risk or irreversibility of the domain, and a principle that permits local differentiation so long as exit is available and monitoring is transparent. Under each principle, what is the verdict? Which principle would we be adopting if we endorsed the proposal, and which if we rejected it? Finally, specify one further hypothetical case — not on our current docket — that would clearly discriminate between these two principles.
