I have been thinking about Hilbert's hotel—where an already full infinite hotel can still take in more guests—and about Cantor's proof that not all infinities are the same size. I have also read about finitism, the view that no actual infinite collection exists and that infinity is only ever a potential, never-completed process. What I want to understand are the most important implications of taking actual infinities seriously, rather than treating them as mere mathematical conveniences. In particular, Hilbert's hotel seems to violate the principle that the whole is greater than its part, since the whole hotel and one of its parts can contain the same number of guests. Must we therefore abandon that principle in other areas—such as reasoning about physical space, time, or ethical duty—or can the contradiction be confined to mathematics? I am not asking for a historical survey, but for the key consequences that should shape how a non-specialist judges whether infinite things can genuinely exist.
