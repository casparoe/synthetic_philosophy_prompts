Two colleagues in a philosophy of science reading group argued over whether evolutionary accounts of scientific reasoning commit what Daniel Dennett calls “greedy reductionism” or instead construct a proper “crane.” The specific claim under dispute was that the institutional norms of science—peer review, replicability, falsifiability—can be fully accounted for as evolutionary byproducts of social status competition, without invoking any autonomous normative level. (1) Alex said this is textbook greedy reductionism: it attempts to jump straight from reproductive fitness to epistemic norms, skipping the intermediate levels of cognitive architecture, institutional scaffolding, and normative self-correction. A legitimate crane, he insisted, must construct each level out of the materials available at the level below, not collapse them into one another. (2) Blake replied that evolutionary scaffolding is precisely what a crane looks like at its bottom rung—fitness pressures build social cognition, which builds institutions, which build norms—and that denying this supporting structure is what actually turns the explanation into a skyhook, because it treats normative autonomy as hanging from nowhere. (3) Alex countered that Blake had smuggled in a skyhook of his own: for evolutionary selection to favor truth-tracking, there must already be a stable environment with success conditions worth tracking, so the explanation presupposes the very reality of objective predictive success that the evolutionary story was supposed to ground. Without that presupposition, predictive success is just another behavioral pattern, not a reason. (4) Blake responded that predictive success needs no metaphysical “truth” to do its work—it is purely instrumental—and that Alex was conflating multiple supporting cranes (status competition, cooperative foraging, cognitive offloading) with a single greedy reduction. He added that Alex’s demand for an “autonomous normative level” is itself the skyhook, since it insists on a rung that is not built from anything below. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, justify each rating briefly, say who is more right overall, and identify what both parties are missing about the relationship between cranes, skyhooks, and reduction in this specific case.
