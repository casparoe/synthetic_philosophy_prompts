Write a 60-minute, 20-mark final-exam essay question for an upper-level Kant seminar testing universalizability. Use this non-canonical maxim rather than false promising: “In collective actions where individual causal contributions are opaque, I will deliberately avoid learning my specific role, in order to preserve my self-image as morally good without incurring the costs of revision.” Ask students to apply both the contradiction-in-conception and contradiction-in-will tests under the Formula of Universal Law, and to assess whether the Formula of Humanity is violated independently of any FUL failure; they must identify whether any failure is logical or volitional. Provide the exam question, then a model answer that opens with its bottom-line verdict in the first sentence—whether the maxim fails FUL, FH, both, or neither—followed by reasoning with no preamble and no restatement of the question. Also provide a marking scheme built around five must-hit points totaling 20 marks, with each point assigned marks and a one-line note on the typical student error. Keep everything formal, do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and do not hinge the answer on specific editions or secondary texts.
