I am organizing a 90-minute workshop in philosophy of mathematics for fourteen graduate students—some trained in logic, others in metaphysics or history of science—who are familiar with Hilbert's hotel but tend to treat finitism as a position that collapses on first inspection rather than as a structurally coherent procedural stance. I want a runnable in-class simulation, not a discussion guide or lecture outline, that forces the group to inhabit the tension between actualist and potentialist treatments of infinity before anyone names the problem. Divide the room into two competing hotel-management teams, each with four assigned roles: a Registrar who tracks the booking ledger, an Arrivals Coordinator who announces new guests, a Rules Auditor who checks that actions stay within the team's permitted ontology, and a Spokesperson who negotiates with the other team. Give the Actualist team a token economy representing completed resources: they may survey the entire infinite set of rooms, reorder them globally, and accommodate arrivals by shifting all existing guests according to a predefined pairing function. Give the Potentialist team an identical initial ledger and arrival sequence—including a standard shift-all-guests challenge and then a Cantor-style anti-diagonal arrival indexed against the current assignment—but restrict them to extending their booking list step-by-step; they may never invoke the totality of rooms as a finished object, only apply a local extension rule to the present finite state. Use a shared token payoff scheme in which both teams earn reputation points for accommodating every arrival without eviction, with bonus tokens awarded if they can explain their move to the opposing team without invoking the forbidden ontology. Provide a precise timeline in three phases—Initial Setup (15 minutes), Sequential Arrivals (40 minutes), and Cross-Examination (15 minutes)—followed by a 20-minute debrief. In the debrief, map each crisis point in the simulation onto a specific feature of the actualism versus potentialist finitism debate: for instance, show how the Actualist team's global reorder corresponds to treating infinity as a completed totality available for quantification, while the Potentialist team's struggle with the anti-diagonal arrival reveals whether local extendability can be maintained without surreptitiously smuggling in a completed set. Predict what will go wrong on the first run—likely that the Potentialist team will invent an ad hoc meta-rule that mimics global access—and advise how to handle it without breaking character. Finally, be explicit about what the game rules can genuinely reproduce—namely, the procedural coherence of always being able to extend a finite construction—and what they cannot reproduce, which is the philosophical justification for denying that a completed infinite collection is ever an object, a point the facilitator must supply verbally. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the activity should be fully self-contained for participants who have no outside access.
