Here's her take, more or less verbatim: Confucian role ethics denies there's any fixed inner self. Instead, you are literally just the sum of your relationships—father, son, ruler, subject, friend. Moral life is just learning the proper rituals (li) for each role and performing them correctly. Because identity is totally constituted by these roles, there can't be any universal moral principle that applies to everyone regardless of position; the obligations of a father and the obligations of a minister share no common ground. She also said that 'shu'—which she called reciprical empathy—is only operative inside a given role, not a bridge between roles or a basis for treating strangers with concern. From this she concluded that role ethics is fundamentally incompatble with egalitarian politics and human rights talk, since rights presume a pre-social individual with inherent worth, which Confucians reject. She added that modern philosophers who revive this view do so mainly to justify hierarchical authority, and that challenging social roles literally threatens your existence as a person.
