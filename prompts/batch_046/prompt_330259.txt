Here's an argument my roommate came up with and now I can't find the hole. (1) A money pump only works if your prefferences cycle over the same outcomes: you pay a little to switch A→B→C→A. (2) Diachronic rationality means those rankings shouldn't cycle across time, even for the same agent. (3) But if you learn anything, your beliefs update, and with them your expeceted-utility orderings shift—maybe you prefer risk today but safety tomorrow after seeing a crash. (4) So any learning agent has cyclical preferences across time slices, just mapped onto different belief states. (5) That means every learner can be pumped by a bookie who offers trades right after each update. (6) But then diachronic rationality is impossible unless you're totally stuborn and never learn. (7) So either we reject learning or we reject the money-pump criteron. Where does it break? I'd rather keep (3) but I'm suspicious of (4).
