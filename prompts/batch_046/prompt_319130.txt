In my philosophy of action reading group, a debate about sphexishness and its bearing on free will has stalled in a way that feels increasingly sterile, despite agreement on all the underlying facts. The dispute centers on Sphex ichneumoneus and the philosophical uptake of its ritualized burrow-inspection behavior. One faction—call them the Deflationists—treats sphexishness as purely descriptive: a subpersonal algorithmic loop characterized by high rigidity and minimal branching, which human cognition also exhibits at the neuronal level, so that labeling a process 'sphexish' carries no special metaphysical or moral weight. On their view, flexibility is merely quantitative—more nodes, more feedback, more conditional branching—without any qualitative threshold that separates mechanisms from agents. The opposing faction—the Essentialists—grants that human brains contain algorithmic loops but insists that genuine agency requires a distinct kind of flexibility: not just more branching, but a second-order capacity to suspend, revise, or endorse first-order routines, and that sphexishness precisely marks the absence of this capacity, which they take to be a necessary condition for moral responsibility. Both sides accept the same ethological descriptions of the wasp, both are familiar with the standard philosophical framing of its ritualized behavior, and both concede that human action is rarely so mechanically fixed. Yet after a year neither has moved. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not about empirical neuroscience or about the wasp at all, but about whether 'flexibility' functions as a functional-technical term or as a normative threshold that demarcates agents from mechanisms—essentially a verbal disagreement masked as a metaphysical one, perhaps compounded by the two sides answering different questions (causal explanation versus ascriptive practice) under the single heading 'sphexishness and free will.' Is this diagnosis correct? Or is there a hidden formal question—say, about whether graded flexibility can be modeled without circular appeal to agency—doing the real work? Diagnose the structure of the impasse using what each side actually says about rigidity, branching, and second-order monitoring, and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress given your diagnosis: a conceptual distinction between algorithmic and normative rigidity, a formal model of second-order suspension, a neuroscientific result about meta-cognitive loops, or an argument that the framing itself is ill-posed because it conflates mechanism with determinism.
