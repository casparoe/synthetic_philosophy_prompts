I'm in a philosophy of science seminar and we got into this argument about whether Malthus was actually wrong or just right but early. My study partner wrote out this chain that seems to show if we rescue Malthus we have to rescue every dead theory, which I don't want. Here are the steps: (1) Malthus's mechanisim is geometric population growth vs arithmetic subsistence growth, so eventually positive checks must occur. (2) For about two centuries global food production has outpaced population, so the check hasn't arrived. (3) Defenders say he isn't falsifed, just in predictive escrow — the mechanisim is intact but masked by temporary tech fixes like synthetic fertilizers. (4) If absence over a long timeline can't falsify a mechanistic prediction, then no long-run mechanistic claim can ever be falsified, since any failure can be escrowed. (5) Take calori-ism: heat was a substance, and that was abandoned. (6) A calori-ist could escrow that too — eventually conditions will reveal heat behaving like a substance, so it was right but early. (7) So the rescue strategy applies identically to every discarded mechanistic theory with a long enough horizon. (8) Therefore either all dead theories are equally unfalsified, or Malthus is genuinely falsified. (9) But treating calori-ism and Malthusianism as equally alive collapses the science/pseudoscience distinction we care about. (10) So consistency forces us to admit Malthus was actually falsified.

I really want to hang onto (3) because I think long-run predictions deserve some patience, and I want to hang onto (9) because I don't want to revive dead physics. But they seem to squeeze each other. Is the weak link (4), which assumes escrow works the same for masked mechanisms and contradicted ones? Or is it (6), which smuggles in a false analogy between delayed outcomes and disproven mechanisms? Tell me exactly which numbered step to reject, why it looked okay at first, and then price out the alternatives: what does giving up (3) cost for people who think predictive patience is rational, what does giving up (4) do to falsificationism, and is there a Lakatos-style research-programme middle ground that saves both without buying the universal rescue?
