For a philosophy of biology reading group I am convening, I have been asked to write down my current commitments on the units of selection debate so that the others can audit them for consistency rather than debate the literature in the abstract. My position is this: in standard evolutionary dynamics I am a gene-selectionist—genes are replicators, organisms are vehicles or interactors, and selection at higher levels is generally reducible to lower-level accounting; I reject group selection in large, unstructured biological populations as an independent causal force, treating kin selection instead as a special case of gene-level frequency change; at the same time, I maintain that during major evolutionary transitions—such as the origin of multicellularity or obligate eusociality—the unit of selection genuinely shifts upward, and that explaining these solely in gene-level terms misses the causal autonomy of the collective as a unit; I also hold that in human cultural evolution, groups are the primary units of selection, particularly when institutional norms solve collective-action problems, and that here the gene-level framework is largely irrelevant; finally, I insist that evolutionary explanations in psychology should be required to identify specific genetic mechanisms or recognized developmental pathways, otherwise they collapse into unfalsifiable adaptationism. I am aware that these commitments may not sit together cleanly, but I am not sure where the fault lines are.

Please go through the set systematically: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but can be reconciled on closer inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, specify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision costs me—whether it forces me to abandon gene-level primacy, to restrict my anti-group-selectionism, or to weaken my methodological demand on psychological explanations. In particular, address whether my acceptance of upward shifts in evolutionary transitions and cultural evolution can be made consistent with my rejection of biological group selection without invoking an ad hoc distinction, and whether my demand for genetic mechanisms in psychology undermines my willingness to accept cultural group selection. Conclude by recommending the single revision that would eliminate the most inconsistencies with the least damage to my core views, and note whether the remaining tensions are deep enough that I should treat my position as pluralist rather than unified.
