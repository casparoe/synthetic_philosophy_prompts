I'm a doctoral student in public administration studying how bounded-rationality frameworks apply to algorithmic governance, and I'm working through a counterfactual about Herbert A. Simon. Suppose he were alive today and advising on the design of public-sector satisficing algorithms—systems that do not maximize a single welfare function but instead search for policy configurations meeting a vector of minimally acceptable thresholds across incommensurable dimensions (distributive equity, procedural transparency, fiscal sustainability) and halt exploration once all aspiration levels are crossed, even though global optima remain unknown and feedback is delayed. These algorithms operate in organizational environments that closely mirror those Simon analyzed: large, hierarchical, with diffuse stakeholder preferences and severe cognitive and computational constraints.

Drawing on his distinction between substantive and procedural rationality, his critique of comprehensive optimization in organizational settings, and his framing of artificial intelligence as part of a science of the artificial, what institutional safeguards or design principles do you think Simon would insist upon? Specifically, how would he assess the risk that embedded aspiration levels become endogenous—adjusting downward automatically when constraints bind—thereby converting bounded rationality from a methodological commitment into a technically neutral disguise for lowered standards?

If forced to choose between two regulatory mandates—ex ante public disclosure of the full aspiration vector and its weighting rules, versus ex post audit trails documenting every halted search path and the reasons exploration terminated—which do you think he would prioritize, and what would that preference reveal about whether he ultimately placed greater trust in substantive outcomes or in procedural legitimacy? Finally, what further hypothetical case—perhaps involving real-time adaptive aspiration adjustment in a multi-agent bureaucratic system—would best distinguish a genuinely Simonian position from a superficially similar but essentially technocratic one?
