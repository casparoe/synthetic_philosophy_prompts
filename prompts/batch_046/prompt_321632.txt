Write a joke that turns on the internal logical tension within the precautionary principle—specifically, the gap between its strong formulation, which treats absence of full scientific certainty about harm as sufficient reason to prohibit an action, and its weak formulation, which merely shifts the burden of proof onto the proponent—when both are applied symmetrically to a single policy dilemma. It should be intelligible to someone familiar with environmental law and risk governance, but should not rely on obscure citations, named scholars, or bibliographic references. Avoid generic jokes about being overly cautious; the comedy should arise from the structural interaction between the principle's asymmetry-breaking purpose and the double-uncertainty of the geoengineering case.
