I'm working out my position on well-being and the heterogeneity problem for hedonism, and I want a consistency check before I present it. Here are my commitments:

(1) Hedonism is true in the welfare domain: pleasure and only pleasure is intrinsically good.
(2) The classic heterogeneity objection succeeds descriptively—there is no uniform phenomenal quality shared by gustatory enjoyment, intellectual satisfaction, and somatic comfort; they differ in subjective character as radically as colors differ from sounds.
(3) This descriptive heterogeneity does not defeat hedonism because pleasures are unified normatively, not phenomenally: they are all states that generate non-derivative reasons to continue or reproduce them.
(4) Phenomenal concepts are conceptually independent of any functional or physical description—knowing the complete neuroscience and decision-theoretic profile of a pleasure state does not entail knowing its subjective character.
(5) Therefore, in aggregating welfare, only intensity and duration matter; phenomenal kind is welfare-irrelevant, so one unit of intellectual pleasure is substitutable for one unit of bodily pleasure.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. For each real tension, rate its severity, identify the cheapest revision, and say what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most problems with the least damage. Be specific about whether (3) can survive (4), and whether (5) is licensed by (2) and (3) together or needs an independent argument.
