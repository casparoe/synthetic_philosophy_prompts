Prepare me for the Q&A after my seminar talk defending the claim that Schrödinger’s Vedantic monism was not biographical ornament but structurally guided his quantum ontology—specifically, that his resistance to discrete observers and to particle multiplicity derives from the Advaita doctrine of a single, non-differentiated consciousness (ekātman) rather than from purely physical arguments. The audience is an interdisciplinary philosophy-of-science seminar: three analytic philosophers of mind (mostly physicalist, skeptical that biography explains theory), two historians of physics (who treat Schrödinger’s mysticism as late-life eccentricity unrelated to his 1926 equation), one comparative philosopher familiar with Śaṅkara’s Advaita, and several physicists who accept decoherence but reject consciousness-based interpretations. Draft the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased as each faction would phrase them. For each objection: state its core, whether it carries real force, what I should concede up front, what I must hold, and a concise reply deliverable in about two minutes, with a brief note on where that reply is weakest. Identify the weakest point in my own position—where I risk conflating metaphysical unity with quantum non-separability—and advise how to defend it without sounding defensive. Finally, include the one question I am hoping the Vedanta specialist does not ask: whether my reading collapses Schrödinger’s monism into solipsism, given that Advaita denies plural consciousnesses altogether. Frame everything conceptually; do not ask for citations, reading lists, or direct quotations from Schrödinger’s texts.
