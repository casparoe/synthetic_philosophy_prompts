I'm working on a longform piece for my science-and-ideas newsletter about self-trusting AI — you know, teh weird loop where a reasoning system tries to apply its own logic to itself and ends up in Löb's theorem territory. I was chatting with a researcher last week who mentioned something she called "delegated epistemic frames," where an agent splits its verification layer into a primary reasoner and a secondary auditor with deliberately weaker modal strength, and I want to use that as a branch point in a taxonomy I'm building for readers.

Can you write me a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts actual positions on the Löbian obstacle? Start with a root question about whether the agent applies its belief-revision norms reflexively to its own outputs. From there, branch on whether it treats its proof-checker as inspectable or black-box, then on whether it permits fixed-point constructions in its policy layer. I need leaves that include the standard ones — cautious non-reflexivity, full collapse, restricted modal-frame approaches — plus that delegated-frame idea I mentioned, and maybe one for agents that just hard-cap their self-reference depth. Every branch should be phrased as a design choice or concrete commitment, not as "are you a Löbian skeptic?" so people sort by what they'd build, not by label. Note any empty slots where a combination is technically consistent but nobody seems to defend it (is it incoherent or just ugly?), and flag pairs your tree genuinely can't separate — like if two different architectures end up behaving identically under stress testing. About eight to ten leaves, no bibliography or citation requests — my readers don't have journal access and I want them reasoning from first principles anyway.
