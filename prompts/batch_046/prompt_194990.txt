First, the standard self-sampling problem: if technologically mature civilizations run ancestor simulations, does my status as a conscious agent place me in a reference class that makes being simulated likely? Second, what I want to call the recursive prior problem: if the base rate of simulations depends on whether civilizations survive to reach maturity, then longtermist efforts to ensure survival may simultaneously lower the prior that I am simulated, creating a feedback loop that standard treatments ignore. Third, an axiological versus epistemic distinction: even with high simulation credence, does the argument reduce the expected moral weight of real-world far-future projects (axiological dampening), or only our confidence that we can influence them (epistemic dampening), and can these be separated? Fourth, the "simulation altruism" reversal: if simulated minds are morally considerable and vastly outnumber real ones, might the argument amplify rather than reduce far-future obligations?
