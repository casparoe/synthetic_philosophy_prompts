I'm in this seminar on epistemology and we were going through Wittgenstein's On Certainty, specifically the hinge idea—those background beliefs that stand fast and don't need explicit justification. And I started noticing something about how universities and funding bodies actually operate: they have these massive audit protcols for research integrity—every data point needs documentation, every conflict of interest disclosed, every statistical method justified—but there's absolutely no mechanism for a researcher to defend, or be challenged on, basic hinges like "induction works" or "the lab hasn't been replaced by a perfect simulation overnight." The intitutional framework just treats those as outside the scope of review, not as claims that happen to be well-supported. So I'm wondering what philosophical commitments that design choice actually presupposes. Does it only make sense if you buy Wittgenstein's view that hinges form a separate category structurally immune to doubt, or could it be compatible with a fallibilist picture where everything is revisable in principle but we just bracket deep background stuff for practical reasons?

There's also this concept I came across—maybe it's called hinge encroachment?—where the practical stakes of a situation can shift which beliefs get treated as hinges. If that's a real thing, then shouldn't audit protocols vary based on stakes? Like, shouldn't high-stakes clinical trials have different "hinge exemption" rules than low-stakes theoretical physics? Or am I misapplying it? I want to understand whether the institutional silence about hinges strictly requires a Wittgensteinian framework, or whether it's philosophically underdetermined and could just as well be licensed by pragmatism or even certain skeptical positions that just happen to bracket the same things. Can you separate what's settled in the literature from what's actually contested? And please don't present one interpretation of On Certainty as the consensus view—there seem to be real disagreements about whether hinges are beliefs, rules, or just dispostions, and I'd like to know where that leaves the institutional practice.
