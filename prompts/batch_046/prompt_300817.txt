I am helping organize a weekend outdoor film screening in a municipal park with a small collective of friends, and by Thursday we must commit to renting either a standard projector package or a more expensive weather-resistant setup with a retractable cover and waterproof cabling. In the course of negotiating logistics, two separate people have approached me with side agreements tied to Saturday’s weather. A local equipment vendor proposed that I pay her $180 if it rains, while she pays me $120 if the skies stay clear. Separately, one of my co-organizers offered a counter-arrangement: if it is sunny, I owe him $160, but if it rains, he owes me $100. I told both of them that, in my own judgment, there is roughly a 60 percent chance of sunshine, though I am genuinely unsure of the precise figure—my confidence feels more like a band somewhere between 55 and 65 percent rather than a single sharp point.

When I sat down to evaluate whether to accept either offer on its own terms, both seemed marginally favorable using my best-guess figures. Yet I realized that if I were to enter both contracts simultaneously, I would lose a fixed sum regardless of whether it rains or shines. I pointed this arithmetic out to both parties; the vendor said I was being overly cautious and that separate social agreements should not be merged into one mental ledger, while my co-organizer insisted that rational decision-making naturally allows independent risks with different people. Despite these reassurances, I feel there is a problem deeper than bad portfolio design—something defective in the way I am holding my beliefs about the weather together with these specific stakes. Is my discomfort a sign that my probabilities are internally inconsistent in some principled way, or is the apparent contradiction merely an artifact of treating two personal promises as parts of a single logical system?

I want to stress that I am not seeking financial advice about the contracts themselves—the sums are small enough that walking away is trivial. What I am trying to understand is whether there is a genuine rationality-based defect in maintaining these beliefs alongside both offers, particularly given that my confidence is spread across a range rather than anchored to one exact number, or whether I am simply overthinking an unfortunate coincidence of social commitments.
