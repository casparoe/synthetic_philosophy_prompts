My friend and I just spent several hours arguing about the problem of universals, and we left convinced that we were partly talking past each other. I want the document we should have produced together: a formal adversarial-collaboration summary between a moderate immanent realist and a deflationary resemblance nominalist. The realist holds that universals exist in re, are multiply exemplifiable, ground genuine resemblance and causal uniformity, and do not require a Platonic realm; the nominalist holds that no shared entities exist, that resemblance is primitive, and that predication is an act of grouping particulars by family resemblance without ontological commitment to a universal.

Draft the joint statement in three parts. First, a numbered section of claims both sides can sign: what the problem demands of any adequate account (explain predication, explain resemblance, accommodate negative properties such as holes and absences, and account for mathematical structures like primality); the agreed data (two red apples, the absence in a ring-shaped object, the number seven being prime in every instance); what neither side is committed to (no transcendent forms, no commitment that all predicates correspond to entities); and the formal constraints both accept (any ontology must distinguish genuine sameness from mere similarity and must not multiply entities beyond explanatory need).

Second, a clearly delineated disagreements section, with each disagreement phrased in wording that the relevant side accepts for its own position: (a) whether there is literally one entity shared between two red apples; (b) whether negative properties like being a hole require a universal of absence or can be explained as configurations of present matter; (c) whether mathematical universals (e.g., being prime) are the same kind of entity as physical ones, if they exist at all; (d) whether resemblance can be primitive without circularity; and (e) whether causal powers individuate universals or universals individuate powers.

Third, identify the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party. I take two central cruxes to be: (1) whether an ontology of holes, shadows, and other absences can be sustained without negative universals; and (2) whether the explanatory work done by universals in causal reasoning can be replaced by primitive resemblance relations among tropes or particular configurations. For each crux, specify one concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's explicit prediction. For example, both should agree that if a physical process can generate and eliminate holes purely by rearranging matter with no change in shared entities, that bears on crux (1); the realist should predict that the hole still instantiates a relational universal of absence, while the nominalist should predict that the hole is nothing over and above the arranged matter and needs no universal. Similarly, both should agree that if two physically identical samples of a substance produce identical causal effects across contexts, that bears on crux (2); the realist should predict that a shared universal explains the uniformity, while the nominalist should predict that primitive resemblance among particular causal powers suffices.

Keep the tone formal and precise. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific obscure theorists; describe positions structurally. Ensure that no disagreement is framed as merely verbal: if part of the dispute dissolves under analysis, say which part and why. The result should read like a document both philosophers could sign, with the residual disagreement made exact.
