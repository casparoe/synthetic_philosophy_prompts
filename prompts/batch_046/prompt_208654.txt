I serve on my city's Digital Public Arts Funding Board, which approves installation proposals for public spaces and sets binding conditions for their display. I work as a software engineer and have no formal training in philosophy or art history. For Thursday's vote we must decide whether to fund an interactive proposal called "Recursive Mirror," in which a custom diffusion model generates real-time portraits of passersby overlaid with text fragments drawn from early-twentieth-century writings on racial justice. The collective behind it defends the work's deliberate emotional manipulation—what they term "affective steering"—by invoking W. E. B. Du Bois's view that art is always propaganda, and specifically that Black artists should deploy beauty and emotional appeal as instruments for equality and collective uplift. They claim the installation extends this logic into algorithmic culture: because every generated image depends on biased training data, all AI imagery is already propaganda, and rendering that mechanism visible to the viewer is itself a form of uplift.

Opposition members, including a community historian and a digital-rights advocate, do not deny that propaganda can serve justice, but they argue that Du Bois tied propaganda to human moral agency, communal accountability, and the dignity of the audience; replacing the accountable human artist with an opaque neural network transforms uplift into extraction. They also object that the proposal lacks any opt-out or transparency mechanism for the emotional profiling embedded in the generation pipeline, and they warn that approving it would establish a precedent allowing any technically sophisticated work to invoke philosophical exemption from informed-consent norms.

Please draft the formal decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approval, rejection, or approval with enforceable conditions—and specify a review time frame. The memo should treat Du Bois's position seriously: first explain why it might genuinely support the artists' framing, then explain why the opposition's reading is stronger or weaker, without simply restating both sides. It should directly address what I have been calling "algorithmic ventriloquism"—the problem of politically loaded imagery being produced by a system that has no accountable speaker behind it, so that no one can answer for the propaganda being made. Finally, it should propose two or three concrete criteria the board can apply to future proposals that invoke philosophical justifications for manipulative or opaque design, written in language that non-philosopher board members can use in practice. Write in a polished institutional tone, avoid requests for bibliographies or citations, and do not rely on access to sources beyond general knowledge.
