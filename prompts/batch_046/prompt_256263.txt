I am drafting a novel that interrogates the boundary between organism and environment, and I need a short, precise story—no more than a few paragraphs—to serve as its conceptual core. Rather than rehearse the familiar virus debate, center the narrative on a synthetic reef consortium in which engineered polyps and symbiotic bacteria share one distributed metabolism and reproduce only as an undifferentiated lattice. The characters should be scientists or caretakers forced to decide whether this lattice is one life, many lives, or neither. Without lecturing, let the plot test what criteria—autopoiesis, genetic unity, bounded metabolism—fail or survive, and allow the emotional weight to rest entirely on the ambiguity itself, with no narrative resolution. Write in a literary, restrained register, and do not invoke any external authorities or texts.
