Write a deadpan parody of a federal emergency-management field manual—approximately 700 words, in the voice of an official Standard Operating Procedure document—for the fictional Republic of Aequalia, whose legislature has codified the non-aggregative principle in rescue ethics associated with the philosopher John Taurek. The manual, titled SOP-77: Non-Aggregative Disaster Response, should govern first responders who are legally prohibited from using victim headcounts to prioritize rescue operations. Include formal sections: Purpose and Scope; Definitions (defining terms such as Moral Weight, Numerical Irrelevance, and Pairwise Comparison); Operational Protocols (procedures for conducting blind lotteries between rescue sites regardless of population density, rules forbidding census-taking at disaster scenes, protocols for resolving ties without implicit summation, and guidelines for distributing identical rescue assets between a single-occupancy dwelling and a multi-occupancy structure); Enforcement and Compliance (penalties for responders who exhibit aggregation bias, such as instinctively favoring a collapsed stadium over an isolated cabin); and a Frequently Asked Questions appendix that addresses practical objections—scale, urgency, resource efficiency—in flat bureaucratic language. The satire should exaggerate genuine features of the non-aggregative view—its rejection of summing lives, its reliance on one-to-one moral comparison, and the difficulty of maintaining institutional consistency—while making visible, without ever stating explicitly, the standard objections: the arbitrariness of lottery mechanisms, the hidden aggregation involved in designing those mechanisms, the moral indifference to magnitude, and the operational chaos of ignoring numbers entirely. Maintain sustained deadpan irony throughout; the absurdity should emerge from the bureaucratic rationalization itself, not from winking, caricature, or invented misrepresentations of the position. Do not include citations, bibliographies, references to living individuals, or explicit moral commentary outside the manual's voice.
