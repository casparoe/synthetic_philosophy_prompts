The debate I have in mind concerns Integrated Information Theory (IIT), especially the claim that consciousness is identical to maximally irreducible integrated information—phi—in a physical substrate. Defenders, working from the theory’s axioms, maintain that the exclusion postulate solves the combination problem and that phi furnishes both necessary and sufficient conditions for consciousness. Critics respond along several well-known lines: that phi is computationally intractable and therefore empirically empty; that the theory yields panpsychist or panprotopsychist consequences for very simple systems; that the exclusion postulate is ad hoc absent independent motivation; and that observed correlations between high-phi architectures and reportable consciousness establish correlation, not constitution. At the same time, some recent internal discussions have floated treating phi as graded rather than binary, a move that sits uneasily with the exclusion postulate’s all-or-nothing logic, yet defenders have largely absorbed this tension through reconstrual of the axioms rather than abandonment of the postulate itself.

Despite this, the disagreement has persisted for well over a decade with remarkably little convergence. Empirical work—comparing phi-related measures in wakefulness versus anesthesia, or in split-brain preparations—is routinely interpreted by each camp as confirming its prior commitments. My working hypothesis is that the stalemate is not primarily empirical but structural: the two sides operate with different explanatory targets, with defenders constructing a normative-formal account of what consciousness is and critics demanding a predictive-detection or mechanistic-decomposition framework in the style of mechanistic explanation. I also suspect that an unacknowledged ambiguity drives part of the conflict, namely whether “integration” denotes a property of causal mechanisms or of informational state spaces, so that the sides are answering different questions—one about constitution, one about correlation and control—under the single heading “theory of consciousness.” Additionally, I wonder whether bedrock intuitions about parsimony and naturalness do hidden work that neither side can independently argue for.
