I have been thinking about the gap between deliberative mini-publics and full legislative sortition, and I want to stress-test the theoretical distinction with a real case that is usually celebrated as a success story without being dissected as a mechanism. Analyze the 2016–2018 Irish Citizens' Assembly on the Eighth Amendment — specifically the body of 99 citizens selected by stratified random sampling across sex, age, social class, and region, charged with deliberating on constitutional abortion provisions, and ultimately recommending repeal with legal access up to twelve weeks without restriction, a recommendation that was then submitted to a national referendum for ratification rather than enacted directly — through the lens of lottocracy and classical sortition theory.

I want a disciplined two-directional mapping, not a narrative of Irish constitutional politics. First, identify which components of the process correspond to the sortition mechanism proper, which to the deliberative overlay (facilitated discussion, expert testimony, structured agendas), and which to the democratic ratification layer; specify the success conditions each layer was designed to satisfy — epistemic quality of outcomes, descriptive representation, insulation from partisan capture, democratic authorization — and whether these conditions are jointly satisfiable or structurally in tension within a single institution. Second, address the selection technology itself: stratified sampling with demographic quotas guarantees broad representativeness, but it abandons the equal ex ante selection probability that defined the Athenian kleroterion and that defenders of modern sortition typically cite as the foundation of democratic equality. Is this still sortition in any meaningful sense, or has it collapsed into statistical representation with a random component, and does that undermine one of the standard justifications? Third, examine the referendum dependency: does the fact that the Assembly could only recommend, not decide, show that pure lottocratic authority is politically unstable in modern states, or does it reveal that the Assembly's value was always instrumental — a device for transforming preferences rather than exercising legitimate decision-making power? Finally, say where the theory breaks: which feature of the Irish episode does the standard literature on sortition fail to anticipate, what does the episode show about whether the core justification is epistemic, democratic, or anti-corruption, and what happens to your analysis if the ratification referendum is conceptually removed from the design? Be explicit about which structural features are doing the theoretical work, and avoid treating the case as either a pure confirmation or a pure refutation.
