I've been trying to work out whether structural explanations of Nazi Germany are compatible with the counterfactual claim that without Hitler there would have been no Holocaust. The structuralist view, as I understand it, holds that conditions in Weimar Germany—economic collapse, the humiliation of Versailles, the fragility of democratic institutions—made some form of authoritarian nationalist takeover highly probable, which seems to imply that Hitler was in some sense replaceable. Yet many historians argue that the specific decision for total genocide required Hitler's unique ideological obsession and his particular authorization, suggesting that without him we might have seen a conventional authoritarian regime but not the precise machinery of the Final Solution. My confusion is this: if I accept the structuralist account, am I logically committed to the claim that the specific content of Nazism—its particular racial science, its camp system, its eastern expansionist ideology—would have emerged essentially unchanged under a different leader? Or can structural explanation be restricted to predicting a generic right-wing dictatorship without specifying its ideological details, in which case Hitler's irreplaceability concerns only the particular flavor of the outcome rather than the structural inevitability of authoritarianism itself? More pointedly, does treating Hitler as necessary for the Holocaust actually falsify structuralism, or does it only show that structural conditions determine the range of possible regimes while individual agency selects within that range?
