Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaberation between two camps on whether the agricultural revolution was a mistake, but dont frame it just as leisure versus labour—inject the distinction between 'option-value affluence' (flexible mobility and distributed risk-pooling in foraging networks) and 'caloric security' (surplus storage and demographic scaling in farming societies).

Camp one holds that hunter-gatherers enjoyed high option-value affluence with low inequality and high autonomy, and that agriculture locked populations into hierarchical storage economies that reduced per-capita welfare. Camp two holds that caloric security created the conditions for trajectory welfare over generations—knowledge accumulation, institutional complexity, and eventually modern health gains—and that judging prehistory by instantaneous leisure misses the point.

Produce a document both sides could sign. Use this structure:

- Joint Agreed Claims (around 200 words): formal points both accept—e.g., that the transition was geographicly patchy, that skeletal records in some regions show health declines with early farming, and that no single metric captures welfare across societies.
- Where We Part Ways (around 250 words): precise wording for each camp's position, phrased so each accepts its own side. Cover whether option-value and caloric security are incommensurable, and whether welfare can be aggregated across generations.
- The Cruxes (around 200 words): sub-questions that, if settled, would move at least one side—e.g., whether mobility actually reduced catastrophic risk, or whether storage economies necessarily generate hierarchy.
- Tests and Predictions (around 150 words): specific evidence or arguments both agree would bear on the cruxes, with each side stating in advance what theyd count as disconfirming.

Write in British English. Keep it casual, a typo or two is fine. No citations, no reading list, no direct quotations—paraphrase anyone you mention.
