Write a sestina—thirty-nine lines, unrhymed—on Wittgenstein’s notion of family resemblance. Use these six end-words in standard rotation: game, thread, rule, language, life, likeness. Let the sestina’s compulsory reshuffling of the same terms across stanzas embody the philosophical point: that concepts like “game” are not united by one essential feature shared by every instance, but by a network of overlapping similarities, like threads in a rope. In the envoi, where all six words must appear together in two tercets, do not resolve them into a single definition; instead, keep them adjacent and unreconciled, showing why the demand for a common essence misses the mark. The poem should be precise enough that a reader of Wittgenstein’s later philosophy recognizes the argument—especially the thread metaphor and the resistance to essentialism—without direct quotation. Maintain a formal, contemplative tone.
