The audience is split: half are her colleagues in cognitive science and philosophy of mind who know the predictive-processing literature well, and half are family members and university administrators with no background in these debates. Please write a speech of roughly five minutes when spoken, and let the philosophical idea do real structural work rather than serve as ornament. Because Dr. Vasquez genuinely holds that consciousness in this technical sense is an illusion, the remarks must avoid treating her inner life, memories, or sense of agency as metaphysically privileged or as evidence of a non-physical soul; yet they must also avoid sounding dismissive of the richness of her experience to the non-specialists. Include one brief concrete scenario—perhaps a moment of scientific insight or personal loss—showing how this protocol operates, so both halves of the audience can see why the framework illuminates a life rather than diminishing it. Present the entire speech in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if writing a letter; do not include bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
