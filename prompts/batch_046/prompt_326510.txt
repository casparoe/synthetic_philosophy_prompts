He claimed that within the dialectic all slave labor remains recognitive because the products are consumed by the master, meaning the slave never escapes being "for-another." True freedom, on Jonas's reading, requires mutual acknowledgment, which is absent here; at best the slave achieves a stoic withdrawal that only resolves in universal ethical life. Mara countered that Jonas treats recognition as a static binary—present or absent—whereas Hegel describes a dynamic process in which the slave's growing independence structurally inverts the relation. She added that if the slave's labor produces an objective world the master must inhabit, then recognition has been inverted rather than deferred, and calling this mere withdrawal misses the dialectical movement entirely.
