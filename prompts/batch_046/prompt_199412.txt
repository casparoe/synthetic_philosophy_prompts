Take the Late Bronze Age collapse circa 1177 BCE — not the pop-history version, but the actual archeological record: palaces at Mycenae and Hattusa burn, Linear B disappears, the Sea Peoples show up, yet local pottery and village settlement continue in a lot of Greece and the Levant. Analize it through the lens of "collapse as institutional decoupling" — the idea that what actually breaks is the top-heavy palace redistribution network, not the underlying society, and that calling it "collapse" is a category mistake that treats the state as identical to the civilization. I want a careful mapping: which parts of the episode the decoupling frame explains well (loss of writing, end of long-distance trade), which ones it misses or misdescribes (actual depopulation in parts of Anatolia, the centuries-long technological loss of literacy), and what the episode in turn shows about the frame. Does it prove that "collapse" is just a narrative we impose on adaptive reorganization, or is there a real failure mode that decoupling cant capture? I know the Diamond vs Tainter debate; dont just rehearse it — apply the frame honestly and say where it breaks.
