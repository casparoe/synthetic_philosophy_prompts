Write a dialogue between two philosophers—one defending Norman Malcolm's view that dreams are not experiences in the relevant sense, and one defending Daniel Dennett's view that dream reports are narrative constructions assembled upon awakening rather than memories of prior experiential episodes—focusing specifically on the problem of "phenomenal ownership" in dreams where the dreamer reports identifying with a distinct narrative agent rather than with their waking self (for instance, reports of being an elderly stranger in an unfamiliar city, with no recognition of one's actual bodily identity or life history). Each speaker should address whether this kind of content supports or undermines their position: the Malcolm-inspired interlocutor should argue that the absence of a continuous waking subject makes the attribution of experiential ownership incoherent, since there is no verified subject to whom the experience belongs; the Dennett-inspired interlocutor should argue that the very construction of an alien narrative agent demonstrates that dream content is fabricated post-hoc, but should also confront the worry that if narrative construction can generate a fully specified experiential subject, then the boundary between "having an experience" and "constructing a story about an experience" collapses in ways that threaten Dennett's own distinction. Ensure that neither speaker is a strawman, that they respond directly to each other's points about verification conditions, narrative assembly, and personal identity, and that the dialogue concludes with both acknowledging a shared conceptual difficulty—namely, whether maintaining the experience/construction distinction requires presupposing the very criterion of diachronic personal identity that the dream case appears to suspend. Keep the exchange formal and tightly argued, with each speaker pressing the other on whether their account can accommodate the first-person authority of the dream report without either reducing it to fiction or inflating it into veridical experience.
