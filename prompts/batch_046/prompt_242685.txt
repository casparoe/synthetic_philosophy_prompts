I serve on the renewal-review panel of the National Foundation for Theoretical Science, and we must vote next Tuesday on whether to extend five-year funding to the Institute for Cosmological Landscapes. The Institute works exclusively on string-theoretic multiverse models for which direct empirical testing is widely held to be impossible in principle. In their proposal, they defend a methodology they call inter-theoretic redundancy elimination: a landscape framework gains credence when it demonstrates that rival parameter sets require ad hoc adjustments that violate established mathematical dualities, leaving only a narrow modal region—within which our observed constants sit—as structurally stable. They further argue that anthropic self-location reasoning transforms selection effects into predictive success: because observers necessarily inhabit regions compatible with existence, the high prior probability that our constants carry within the landscape counts as confirmation rather than bias.

The panel is split. One faction holds that extending standard empirical criteria—predictive risk, reproducible observation, falsifiability—to this domain is a category error, and that without empirical risk the Institute is practicing mathematics rather than science; the other faction holds that theory-choice has always included non-empirical virtues such as coherence, simplicity, and explanatory breadth, and that the Institute's criteria are rigorous formal extensions of those virtues applied to a domain where experiment is unavailable by construction. Our chair has asked me to circulate a short decision memo before Tuesday's meeting. Please draft it: recommend one concrete course—full renewal, renewal with specific conditions and evaluation milestones, or denial—together with a clear time frame for implementation or review; connect the philosophical problem of non-empirical theory confirmation to our institutional mandate to support scientific rather than purely mathematical inquiry; engage seriously with the strongest argument for the position you reject, explaining precisely where it succeeds and where it falls short; and specify, if you choose a conditional path, exactly what methodological, philosophical, or structural benchmarks the Institute must meet, and by what dates, for funding to continue or to convert to a different status.
