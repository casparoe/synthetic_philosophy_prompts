For my independent study in philosophy of mind, I have been working through the distinction between the hard problem of consciousness and the meta-problem, and I constructed an argument that now seems suspicious because it proves too much. Here are the steps:

(1) The meta-problem asks why we judge that purely physical accounts fail to capture the essential nature of conscious experience.

(2) Any complete explanation of that judgment must appeal either to direct phenomenal acquaintance with what is missing, or to purely third-person cognitive mechanisms such as framing effects, evolutionary heuristics, or introspective limitations.

(3) If the explanation appeals to direct acquaintance, then the subject already possesses non-physical knowledge of consciousness, which resolves the hard problem by assumption rather than explaining the judgment away.

(4) Therefore, solving the meta-problem requires option (2): a fully physical story about how brains generate reports of an explanatory gap.

(5) But if a fully physical story explains why we report a gap, then that report is epistemically undermined; it reflects neural wiring rather than an actual absence in physical explanation.

(6) Consequently, successfully solving the meta-problem eliminates the hard problem altogether.

(7) By strict analogy, if cognitive science explains why physicists report conceptual strangeness about quantum entanglement, then quantum entanglement is not genuinely strange; the judgment dissolves with its psychological explanation.

(8) That result is clearly wrong, since explaining the psychology of a belief does not automatically dissolve the belief's content.

(9) Therefore the chain from (1) to (6) must be flawed, yet I cannot see where it breaks without also breaking (8).

My question is: which premise or inference should I reject? I am most reluctant to abandon (5), since explaining away a judgment typically weakens its authority, and I want to preserve (8). If I keep both, I must either deny the analogy in (7) or deny the exclusivity claim in (4). Which rejection is least costly, and what does each option commit me to regarding how introspection relates to physical explanation?
