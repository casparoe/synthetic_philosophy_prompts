Please embed a hypothetical scenario in which synthetic biologists engineer a distributed, self-modifying lineage that propagates traits horizontally across a network rather than vertically through reproductive isolation, thereby challenging the standard assumption that speciation requires discrete breeding boundaries. The question should require candidates to analyse whether this case undermines the biological species concept, whether a pluralist or monist response is more defensible, and how treating species as dynamic processes rather than static kinds alters the ontological stakes. Provide a model answer of approximately 700 words written in American English, together with a marking scheme that allocates 20 marks across five specific moves—distinguishing process ontology from essentialism; articulating the horizontal-transfer challenge; evaluating pluralism without collapsing into relativism; determining whether the case is genuinely novel or merely an extreme instance of known bacterial exchange; and identifying the normative implications for conservation taxonomy. For each move, state the marks available, the particular misconception that should lose credit, and the precise difference between an A-level and B-level answer. Write in British English, avoid requests for bibliographies or citations, and ensure the question is unanswerable without confronting the conceptual tension directly.
