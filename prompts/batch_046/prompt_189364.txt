I spent most of last night in a long argument with a friend about whether the societies of the Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean, the Classic Maya lowlands, and Rapa Nui actually collapsed, or whether they merely changed form. We kept returning to the same impasse. I pointed to the disappearance of palace economies, the end of large-scale monument construction, and the loss of writing systems, and argued that something essential had ended—comparable to a person dying even though their atoms continue to circulate in other bodies. My friend countered that the same populations kept farming, speaking derived languages, and maintaining local ritual practices, so the society had not died but had been reconfigured—more like a person who survives total amnesia and lives on with new memories and intentions. It occurred to me that we were applying two different implicit standards for persistence through radical change: one that demands continuity of institutional consciousness, shared elite culture, and administrative form, and one that demands only biological and subsistence continuity of the underlying population.

Does this specific disagreement—using criteria for personal survival to adjudicate whether a society has endured or perished—have an established name in archaeology, anthropology, or philosophy of history? Or is it an unrecognized analogical move that lacks a literature of its own? If the framing is recognized, I want to understand how it relates to existing distinctions such as social death versus cultural transformation, or systemic collapse versus adaptive reorganization, and where my friend’s and my positions map onto them. If it is not recognized, I want a precise carving: what are its necessary conditions—must institutional discontinuity coincide with demographic persistence?—and its boundary cases, such as gradual drift without a clear break, or institutional survival despite near-total population replacement? I am not looking for a bare label or a list of sources; I want to know whether the phenomenon itself is already delineated, and if not, how to define it so that neither of us can smuggle our conclusion into the definition.
