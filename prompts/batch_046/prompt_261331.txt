I am a doctoral student in jurisprudence and philosophy of law, seconded part-time to a parliamentary drafting office that processes approximately forty proposed meta-rules per quarter—provisions that alter, suspend, or repeal the very mechanisms authorising their enactment. Each proposal must be screened within thirty minutes by a drafter who holds no qualification in logic or legal philosophy; the office head, a senior counsel, signs off on all outputs, but she expects the initial screening to be reproducible, transparent, and documented rather than intuitive. I need a reusable procedural protocol—not an essay, not a literature review—that translates the core conceptual distinctions of legal self-reference into an ordered checklist with explicit stop conditions, so that the same drafter can apply it consistently without consulting me each time.

Please construct the procedure so that it can be run by this practitioner without theoretical training, but preserve the distinctions that actually matter: between hierarchical authority and reflexive authority; between procedural circularity (a rule governing its own amendment mechanism) and substantive circularity (a rule altering its own content conditions); and between resolvable interpretive loops and genuine logical paradoxes of the kind associated with constitutional self-amendment. For each step, specify:

- The exact test or question to apply;
- What counts as a passing or failing answer;
- The branch taken on each outcome (‘proceed’, ‘revise’, ‘reject’, or ‘escalate to senior counsel’);
- The explicit point, if any, where the procedure halts and requires a normative judgement rather than a logical determination.

Then apply the procedure to three hypothetical drafting proposals:

A. A constitutional clause providing that this clause may only be amended by the procedure defined within this clause, with no reference to any external constitutional provision;
B. A delegated legislative instrument that permits a minister, by statutory instrument, to suspend the parent Act’s provisions conferring the minister’s own rule-making powers, subject only to parliamentary approval by simple majority;
C. A ‘recursive sunset’ provision stating that all delegated authorities—including the authority to renew or amend this provision—expire automatically after five years unless reaffirmed by a body whose authority derives solely from this same provision.

For case C in particular, show how the procedure produces an unwelcome result: that the clause, intended as a democratic accountability mechanism, is classified not as a manageable review cycle but as an unresolvable regress of authority that must be rejected or radically restructured. Finally, state explicitly what the procedure cannot do: which kinds of self-reference it will misclassify, which institutional contexts it assumes (for instance, that there exists a recognisable rule of recognition external to the clause being tested), and where a drafter must still consult legal counsel rather than rely on the checklist.

Please present the response in British English, in formal academic register. Do not provide bibliographies, citation lists, or footnote references to specific published works; describe any named theoretical distinctions in your own words rather than quoting them verbatim.
