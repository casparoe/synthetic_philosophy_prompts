I was reading about it on my own after a podcast mentioned coal being near Manchester, and I started making lists. Britain had patents and coal near factories; China had a huge unified market but coal was far away in the north. So then I thought, wait, if historians just list what Britain had that China lacked, isn't that like saying "Britain industrialized because it was Britain"? I tried to fix the problem by asking "why Britain and not the Ottoman Empire?" since they also had coal in the Balkans and a big market, but my friend said I'm just shuffling deck chairs. I guess what confuses me is whether "why Britain and not China" is even a real question, or if it's circular by design. If the answer is always a checklist of British advantages, aren't we just describing Britain instead of explaining the divergence? And is there a way to ask it that doesn't assume Britain was destined to win?
