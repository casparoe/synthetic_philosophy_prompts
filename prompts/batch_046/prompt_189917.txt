okay so i've been chewing on the heterogeniety problem for hedonism — like, what do the pleasure of nailing a hard proof and the pleasure of really good pasta actually share? — and my study partner cooked up an argument that feels like it proves way too much.

(1) Hedonism says the only thing that makes a mental state good is that it's pleasant.
(2) So every pleasure has to share one intrinsic phenominal property — call it P — that does all the moral work.
(3) Take sensory pleasure (taste) and intellectual pleasure (insight). Strip away the sensory content and the cognitive content, and there's nothing left that feels identically the same.
(4) So P can't be a real modality-independant quality; there's no common currency of feeling across domains.
(5) Therefore hedonism fails, since it needs P and P doesn't exist.
(6) But wait — the same move kills every monist value theory. Desire satisfaction, knowledge, virtue, whatever: they all need some single good-making feature that shows up in wildly different mental states. If cross-modal property identity is impossible, all of them collapse.
(7) That can't be right, because at least some monisms seem defensible. So the argument has to be broken, but every premise looks okay to me.

where's the weak link? is step (3) wrong — maybe there's a thin affective valence that survives stripping? is (4) wrong — maybe hedonism only needs one shared evaluative relation, not one shared feel? or is (6) wrong — maybe other monisms dodge the problem because they don't require a phenominal property at all? tell me which step to reject and what giving it up costs, especially whether it saves hedonism specifically or just saves monism in general. also, is there a hidden equivocation between feeling the same and having the same value-ground?

i want a real diagnosis, not just neuroscience says there's a reward signal. if there's actually a formal or conceptual distinction that fixes it, say so, but don't just wave at it.
