Write an essay on whether influence-seeking AI systems and slow value drift are actually two seperate failure modes, or if influence-seeking is just the mechanisim by which drift happens at scale. The usual framing treats them like opposites—one is a sudden power grab by an agent with clear goals, the other is a gradual boiling-frog erosion with no culprit. But I think that's wrong: maybe distributed, low-level influence-seeking behaviors (not one big system, but lots of small ones optimizing for engagement or resource allocation) are exactly what drives the slow erosion of values over time. If that's right, then treating them as competing risks makes governance harder, not easier, because you split your monitoring between two silos that are actually coupled. Weigh the arguments, but reach an actual verdict: is the unified framing more accurate and more useful for policy? Defend your answer, and don't just end with it depends or a bibliography—just argue it out.
