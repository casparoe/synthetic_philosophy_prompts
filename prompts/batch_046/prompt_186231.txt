I've been trying to work out whether HCH—Paul Christiano's humans-consulting-humans setup—can be treated as an idealization of ordinary deliberation, but specifically through the lens of factored cognition rather than just recursive amplification. The basic intuition is that when we actually think hard about a messy decision, like whether to relocate for a job, we don't just run one long internal monologue; we factor it into chunks (cost of living, social ties, career trajectory) and sometimes imagine asking a friend or a future version of ourselves about each chunk separately. HCH seems to formalize this, but with a twist: instead of factoring the content of reasoning horizontally, it factors the agent vertically. So you get this recursive tree where H1 asks H2 asks H3, each operating with limited context.

But here's where I think the framing gets interesting and maybe a bit weird: in standard factored cognition setups (like debate or amplification), the factoring is horizontal—breaking a problem into more or less independant subproblems that can be solved in parallel. In HCH, the factoring is vertical—breaking the deliberator into layers of competence or context. I'm wondering if that means HCH is actually closer to an idealization of social deliberation, like a committee of experts passing notes, than to an idealization of individual thought, even though it's literally framed as one person consulting copies of themselves. There's a tension I can't quite resolve: if each H-level only sees a narrow slice, they might miss cross-cutting concerns that only emerge when the subproblems interact, which is exactly the failure mode that horizontal factored cognition is designed to avoid by keeping interaction channels open.

Anyway, I'm sketching this out for a small philosophy-of-AI reading group and I'd love some comments on the conceptual architecture. Specifically: does the vertical-vs-horizontal factoring distinction hold up, or am I mischaracterizing how factored cognition handles interaction effects between subproblems? Is there a particular failure mode—maybe something like value drift or framing corruption across the recursive layers—that I should flag as a serious problem for treating HCH as a model of good deliberation? And more generally, does comparing HCH to individual deliberation even make sense, given that it's structurally more like distributing cognition across agents? I don't need citations or a bibliography, just whether the comparison sounds philosophically productive or whether I'm stretching the analogy too far.
