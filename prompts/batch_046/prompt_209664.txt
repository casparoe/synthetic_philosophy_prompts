I've kinda bought into this hybrid version of the procreation asymmetry — not exactly Benatar's original, but a tweak where the asymmetry (creating suffering people is wrong, not creating happy ones isnt wrong) only survives if we combine a person-affecting restriction with an indexical lock on actual-world subjects. The idea is that if modal realism is true and every possible person exists somewhere, then saying "no one is deprived of happiness" falls apart unless moral reasons are indexed to this world specifically, not spread across modal space. So my view is that reproductive ethics should stay asymmetrical, but only because we implicitly tie harm and benefit to the world we actually inhabit. What would count against this? I want honest defeaters: arguments that modal realism doesnt actually break the "no deprivation" claim because deprivation needs a world-specific counterfactual baseline; worries that this makes the asymmetry hostage to a metaphysical view most people reject; maybe psychological data about how people actually think about reproductive obligations; and whether the indexical clause is just an ad hoc patch that makes the view unfalsifiable. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a defender of this hybrid view would actually concede versus dismiss, and tell me if there's a live risk that the position has been qualified into emptiness. Also, does tying the asymmetry to actual-world indexing change how we should think about obligations to future generations in climate ethics, or is that a seperate problem?
