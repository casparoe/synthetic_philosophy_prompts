last night two friends of mine, jordan and casey, got into it after watching a truely depressing film. jordan said she loved it even though it wrecked her, and casey said thats exactly the paradox — why do people chase out art that makes them feel bad?

casey started with catharsis: we watch tragedy to purge negative emotion, like aristotle argued. jordan said thats mostly debunked now, people usually feel worse or just drained afterwards, not cleansed. casey agreed but said then it must be emotional practice — a safe rehearsal for real grief. jordan pushed back: if its practice, why rewatch the same sad movie? you already know the ending, so youre not learning anything. casey said maybe its not about learning, its about confirming you can survive the feeling.

then jordan brought up this idea she half-remembered — that real-world sadness always carries an "indexical commitment" or action demand: you have to comfort someone, fix a problem, carry guilt. but in fiction the sadness is suspended — you feel the affect but none of the obligations. so its pure grief without the relational cost. casey said that sounds neat but then why dont people just binge sad news documentaries? thats real suffering with no obligation to act. jordan said cause tragedy structures the sadness into a meaningful arc — you get closure and significance that random real grief doesnt provide. casey said okay but then youre not explaining why sadness is enjoyable, youre explaining why narrative closure is enjoyable, and it just happens to be sad. she also said if its really about suspending obligations, burnt-out people should seek tragedy more, but often they avoid it entirely.

they asked me to referee and i honestly couldnt. can you go through their exchange step by step and say whos right about which point? not just overall — i want the point-by-point breakdown. also is this even a real paradox or just us mislabeling a preference for meaningful affect? and what are they both missing — maybe some distinction theyre collapsing? definately dont just say "it depends on taste", dig into the argument.
