I'm trying to fix my view on metaphysical grounding before a workshop and want a consistency audit rather than a lecture. Here is where I stand:

(1) Grounding is well-founded and irreflexive: every chain of metaphysical dependence terminates in fundamental entities, and nothing grounds itself.

(2) Non-reductive physicalism about mind is correct: all mental facts are fully grounded in physical facts, but phenomenal properties are conceptually and metaphysically irreducible.

(3) The grounding relation itself is not fundamental; particular [A grounds B] facts hold in virtue of the intrinsic natures of the relata together with necessary connecting principles.

(4) There are no brute necessities: whenever distinct properties or facts are necessarily connected, there is a ground for that connection.

(5) In voluntary action, mental decisions are causally efficacious in producing bodily movement, and this causal contribution is not fully decomposable into that of the underlying neural states.

(6) Talk of ontological "levels" is only a heuristic; strictly speaking there are just fundamental entities and what is grounded in them.

Go through these pairwise. Which combinations are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severely? In particular: does (3) together with (1) generate a vicious regress about the ground of grounding, or does that dissolve once we distinguish particular instances from general principles? Does (2) sit comfortably with (4), or does the irreducibility of the phenomenal make the mental-physical link look like a brute necessary connection? And does (5) survive once (2) and (3) are taken together—am I implicitly committed to downward exclusion that rules out the distinctive causal contribution I want to preserve? For each real tension, rank it by severity, say what has to give, and identify the cheapest revision. Then tell me which single commitment I should drop or weaken if my aim is to preserve the core physicalist picture while cleaning up the most problems at once.
