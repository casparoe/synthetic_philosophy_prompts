Friend A said the whole point is that patterns are only real if they're detectable without importing intentional language — like, if you can't spot the pattern from pure physics, then calling it "real" is just a polite word for useful fiction. Friend B said that's backwards: the intentional stance and design stance often reveal genuine compressions that the physical stance literally can't see because the physical description is astronomically long, and that doesn't make them fictions, it makes them stance-relative realities.

Then A pushed back: okay, but then "real" just means "good for predicition," which is instrumentalism dressed up. If I predict a thermostat with beliefs about temperature, and you do it with quantum field theory, we're just picking tools. B said no, it's not arbitrary — the pattern has to be counterfactualy robust and achieve actual algorithmic compression, not just fit one case. So there's a constraint.

How do we say one's more real without just assuming the answer? B said the intentional stance fails for LLMs because it breaks under out-of-distribution prompts — it's not counterfactualy robust — whereas the design stance generalizes. A said that's just saying the intentional stance is a bad model for LLMs, not that it's ontologically second-class.

Then B brought up that even the physical stance requires abstraction — you ignore thermal noise, quantum fluctuations, etc. There's no "pure" physical view. A said that's an epistemic limitation, not proof that reality is stance-dependent.
