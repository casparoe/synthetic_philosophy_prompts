Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps in my high-school philosophy debate group on whether "Western values" is a coherent analytical category, not merely a rhetorical label. Camp A holds that the phrase denotes a historically specific, non-circular cluster of normative commitments—procedural justice, individual autonomy, secular public reason, and market-based coordination—that share a genealogical origin in particular post-Enlightenment traditions and can be applied symmetrically across contexts. Camp B holds that the phrase lacks stable membership conditions; its content shifts with argumentative purpose, serving mainly to mark an in-group/out-group boundary in geopolitical and domestic rhetoric, so that attempts to list its essential contents always end in circularity or ad hoc exclusion.

Produce the document in four parts. First, a numbered list of claims both camps can sign—covering actual patterns of usage in public argument, which values are typically invoked, and whether historical genealogy can be separated from normative evaluation. Second, a disagreements table stating each camp's precise position in wording that camp itself would accept. Third, identify the cruxes, including specifically whether the category survives what I call the "deployment-coherence" test: for the category to be analytically useful, its membership conditions must remain stable when applied symmetrically to domestic policy debates and to international diplomatic rhetoric, without ad hoc adjustments. Fourth, for each crux, present one concrete argumentative case or hypothetical policy scenario that both sides agree bears on it, together with each camp's explicit prediction of what that case reveals. Where possible, indicate whether part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, and state which part and why. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; write only from the conceptual resources both camps and a well-prepared reader already possess.
