I've been following philosophy of mind discussions for years, particularly around the meta-problem of consciousness, and I've noticed a recurring argumentative pattern that I can't place precisely within the standard taxonomy of fallacies or theory-ladenness. The pattern goes like this: a researcher develops a functional theory — centered on global broadcasting, integrated information, or precision-weighted prediction-error minimization — and demonstrates that it tracks reportability, attentional access, or behavioral control with impressive fidelity. Critics then ask why that functional architecture should be accompanied by any phenomenal experience at all. Rather than bridging the gap with an independent mechanism, the response is often a definitional identification: phenomenal consciousness just is global availability, or just is integrated information, or just is the relevant form of error minimization. What's distinctive is the selectivity. The theorist applies this identification precisely to those systems already judged conscious by pre-theoretic intuition, but withholds it from structurally analogous processes in artificial systems, peripheral neural circuits, or non-biological implementations. So the identity claim doesn't function as a general ontological reduction; it functions as a localized semantic relocation that occurs only where the explanatory gap was already present. It is not quite begging the question, since there's an independent theoretical argument for the functional architecture. It is not a standard ad hoc auxiliary hypothesis, because the functional theory itself isn't modified — only the semantic scope of consciousness is restricted post hoc. And it differs from straightforward illusionism, because phenomenal experience is retained rather than eliminated; it is merely declared co-extensive with a pre-selected functional profile. Does this maneuver have an established name in philosophy of science or philosophy of mind? If not, what distinguishes it from legitimate type-identity claims, and where exactly does it fail — at the level of explanatory adequacy, at the level of modal scope, or in its reliance on pre-theoretic intuition to draw boundary conditions?
