Here is an argument that extends coherence arguments for expected utility maximization into epistemic decision-making, and I cannot locate the exit.

(1) The standard Dutch book argument demonstrates that if an agent's preferences over gambles violate the axioms of expected utility theory, a bookie can construct a sequence of bets that guarantees her a net loss.
(2) This result requires only that the agent is willing to accept bets at her stated odds and that she has a consistent preference for avoiding sure losses.
(3) Now consider that an agent might form precise credences not merely about external states of affairs, but about the outcomes of her own future reasoning—for example, she assigns credence 0.75 to "if I spend one hour analyzing problem P, I will reach the correct solution."
(4) If coherence applies generally to preferences over acts, then it must also govern her preferences over cognitive acts: she should choose whether to analyze P, suspend judgment, or seek more evidence by maximizing expected utility relative to these self-referential credences and her epistemic values.
(5) Specifically, if the weighted disutility of reaching an erroneous conclusion exceeds the utility of reaching a correct one, coherence recommends avoiding analysis of P altogether.
(6) By extension, every feature of an epistemic policy—how long to study, which hypotheses to prioritize, when to update—must be globally optimized like a portfolio of financial bets.
(7) But this seems to eliminate open-ended inquiry, since the value of investigating an unknown domain is precisely that its payoff has not been quantified in advance. Thus coherence arguments appear to forbid rational curiosity.
(8) I want to keep (1) and (2) intact, and I am reluctant to claim that belief-formation is entirely exempt from decision-theoretic evaluation. Which step should be rejected, and what does that rejection commit me to? Is the error in treating reasoning outcomes as bettable commodities, in applying coherence across self-referential probabilities, or elsewhere?
