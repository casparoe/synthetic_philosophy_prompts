Write a dialogue between two philosophers—Dr. Aris Thorne and Dr. Lena Voss—who hold opposing views on whether honoring sunk costs is ever rational. Thorne defends the standard economic-philosophical position: unrecoverable past expenditures carry no normative weight; rational choice is strictly forward-looking, and treating sunk costs as reasons commits a category error between causal explanation and normative justification. Voss argues that this forward-only framework neglects a distinct rational commitment she terms "continuity-constituting investment," in which abandoning a long-embedded project can inflict a non-instrumental harm on practical identity that is not reducible to emotional attachment or loss aversion. The dialogue must contain genuine back-and-forth: Voss should respond specifically to Thorne's claim that any identity-based reason is merely disguised preference, and Thorne should distinguish legitimate identity-preservation from irrational stubbornness without dismissing it outright. Ground their exchange in one concrete case they both examine: a mathematician who has spent fifteen years developing a formal system that now appears internally inconsistent, deciding whether to salvage part of it or abandon the framework entirely. Neither speaker should be a strawman; Thorne should concede that practical identity constrains rationality in domains like promise-keeping, and Voss should admit that her view risks licensing infinite persistence. The dialogue should not resolve, but each position should emerge sharper. After the dialogue, present a brief structured summary in a table with exactly three columns—"Point of Agreement," "Core Disagreement," and "Cost of Each View"—with one row for each speaker summarizing their stance.
