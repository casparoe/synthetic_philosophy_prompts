I have been studying Gödel's modal ontological argument, in which he defines God as possessing all positive properties and derives that being God-like is possibly instantiated; he then applies an S5 modal framework to show this property is necessarily instantiated in every world. This leads me to a confusion. If the derivation is formally valid, and necessary existence entails existence in the actual world, then it seems atheism is incompatible with the logical structure of properties—unless one simply rejects the axioms outright. Many critics reply that modal logic cannot bridge possibility to actual existence, but I wonder whether they are implicitly relying on modal realism, where other worlds are concrete entities like ours, to drive that objection. Could it be that Gödel's argument actually runs on a much thinner, actualist semantics, and that the real weakness lies elsewhere—perhaps in the claim that all positive properties are compossible, or in treating logical consistency as sufficient for metaphysical possibility?

Please separate what is settled about Gödel's formal derivation from what remains genuinely contested—particularly whether the axioms about positive properties are philosophically defensible, whether the S5 move genuinely establishes actual existence without hidden ontological commitments, and whether modal realism is even relevant to his framework—without presenting any one contested interpretation as the consensus view.
