I'm putting together the take-home final for my grad seminar on epistemic humility—mostly phil of mind and science students who think they already get Tetlock's inside/outside distinction but have never actually applied it to philosophical reasoning itself. I want one essay question that forces them to use both views to assess a specific, kind of contrived case: a philosopher claims that because she can clearly imagine a zombie world without physical properties, physicalism is false, and she assigns 90% confidence. The prompt should make them explain why her inside-view confidence is unreliable without just saying "people are biased," and then construct an outside-view reference class that actually applies to modal arguments in philosophy of mind—dealing with the fact that reference classes are usually about empirical predictions, not conceptual ones, so they cant just copy-paste the forecasting literature. They also need to address what epistemic modesty requires here—does she have to lower her credence, or just change the way she holds it?

Please write the exam question, a model answer around 700 words, and a marking scheme with five must-hit points totaling 20 marks. For each point, give the mark share and a one-line note on the classic mistake—like treating the outside view as just "other philosophers disagree" or thinking modesty means suspending judgment entirely. The difficulty should come from the crux: they shouldn't be able to answer by summarizing Tetlock or listing biases, but actually have to do the work of adapting an empirical forecasting framework to a non-empirical domain.
