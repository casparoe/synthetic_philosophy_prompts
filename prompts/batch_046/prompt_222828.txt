Restate Augustine's absolute prohibition on lying — covering his taxonomy of falsehoods from harmful deception to playful or protective lies, and his insistence that any intentional falsehood corrupts the speaker's soul regardless of outcome — in the vocabulary of formal protocol verification and static type safety. Treat truthful utterance as maintaining a communicative invariant between speaker and hearer, and lying as an unsound type coercion that violates the contract irrespective of downstream utility. For each component of the view — soul-corruption, erosion of communal trust, the irrelevance of benevolent intent, and the graded seriousness of different lies — indicate whether it translates cleanly, requires distortion to fit, or has no analog at all. Finally, assess whether the untranslatable residue shows that Augustine's argument necessarily relies on a metaphysics of the soul that protocol ethics cannot represent, or whether it reveals that parts of his prohibition were rhetorical rather than structurally essential.
