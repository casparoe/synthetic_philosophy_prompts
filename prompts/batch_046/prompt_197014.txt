I sit on our university Governance Reform Commitee and we're split over a proposal to scrap the faculty senate and replace it with a direct democratic assembly of all staff and students, every decison by simple majority. The student advocates keep citing Spinoza's claim that democracy is the only form where sovereignty isn't transferred away from the people, but the faculty worry he's not endorsing pure majoritarianism. I need the board's decison memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — laid out in exactly five numbered items, each just a sentence or two, with no intro and no conclusion. You should ground the reasoning in Spinoza's actual view that democratic power has to be guided by common adequate ideas rather than just head-counting, and you must honestly address the strongest point from the side you don't take.
