I have recently retired from experimental physics and have begun studying philosophy of science with some seriousness. I have assembled what I take to be a coherent profile regarding objective chance, quantum mechanics, and rational belief, and I would like a rigorous consistency check before presenting it to a reading group. Here are my commitments:

(a) The Principal Principle governs rational credence: if an agent knows that the objective chance of an event is x and lacks inadmissible evidence, her rational degree of belief in that event ought to be x.

(b) Quantum mechanics reveals genuinely ontic, irreducible chances in nature, not merely epistemic limitations arising from ignorance of hidden variables.

(c) Nevertheless, evolutionary debunking arguments apply with full force to our folk concept of chance and probability: our intuitive grasp of randomness was shaped by selection pressures for practical prediction, not for metaphysical accuracy.

(d) Therefore, reflective equilibrium is the correct philosophical method here, but only on the condition that we first discount our untutored intuitions about chance and randomness by a substantial factor.

(e) In cases of persistent expert disagreement—such as the interpretation of quantum mechanics—rational agents ought to defer to the weighted majority opinion of the relevant scientific community regarding the correct assignment of objective chances.

(f) All empirically adequate interpretations of quantum mechanics are metaphysically on a par; there is no deeper fact of the matter that distinguishes them.

Please audit this set. Which pairs genuinely pull against one another, which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve on closer inspection, and for each genuine tension what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity, specify the cheapest fix in each case, and indicate which single change to my profile would eliminate the greatest number of conflicts. I do not know in advance where the problems lie, so treat none of these commitments as obviously expendable.
