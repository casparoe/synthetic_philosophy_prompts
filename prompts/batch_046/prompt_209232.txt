Write an essay on whether Quine’s commitments to ontological relativity, the indeterminacy of translation, and the continuity of ethics with empirical science leave any coherent space for moral realism. Focus especially on whether the underdetermination Quine identifies in translating mentalistic and referential discourse extends analogously to normative vocabulary—so that rival moral ontologies could be empirically equivalent yet genuinely incompatible—or whether moral discourse escapes that underdetermination because it fails to meet the empirical constraints Quine demands of serious ontology. Weigh the principal arguments on both sides, reach and defend a specific verdict about whether Quinean naturalism is compatible with objective moral facts, and carefully separate what is settled about Quine’s actual positions on value and ontology from what remains genuinely contested among specialists in his philosophy. Do not present a disputed interpretive claim as the established consensus.
