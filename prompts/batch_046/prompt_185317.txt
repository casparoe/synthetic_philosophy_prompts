I'm trying to formalize the counter-majoritarian objection to judicial review, and I think I've isolated its logical structure. The objection seems to presuppose that democratic legitimacy is a scalar quantity that increases monotonically with the number of citizens whose preferences are enacted into law. If we reject that scalar model and instead treat legitimacy as a vector composed of two independent dimensions—procedural authorization and substantive rights-protection—then a constitutional court that strikes down a statute does not diminish democratic value; it merely reweights the vector. Given this reframing, shouldn't the normative debate shift away from asking whether unelected judges ought to overrule legislatures, and toward asking what institutional architecture maximizes the weighted integration of these dimensions? More precisely, if one grants that procedural consent and rights-protection are incommensurable goods rather than interchangeable units on a single scale, does the counter-majoritarian difficulty dissolve entirely, or does it reappear in a different form—perhaps as a problem about who gets to fix the weights?
