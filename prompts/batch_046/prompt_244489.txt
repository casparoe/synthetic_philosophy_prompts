I was sitting in on a discussion between two grad students in my dept — call them M and K — about whether gender counts as a socially constructed kind in the same rough way money does, or whether biology makes it different. I couldn't tell if either was being sloppy or if they actually hit something real. Here was the back-and-forth, as best I rememeber:

(1) M said gender is obviously constructed — norms, pronouns, clothing rules all change across cultures — but biological sex is just reproductive anatomy and chromosomes, a natural kind that constrains gender without being part of it. So construction applies to the social layer, not the biological base.

(2) K replied that this assumes we have direct access to "raw biology" separate from classification, but the binary sex system itself was stabilized by medicine and law for social reasons, including policing bodies that don't fit neatly. Even chromosomes only matter because we've decided which variations count as "sex" and which are "disorders." So the biological base is already shaped by the social framework that reads it.

(3) M pushed back: even if taxonomies are messy, there are underlying reproductive mechanisms that exist whether we classify them or not. The social part is the map, not the territory. If gender is constructed, it's because we're assigning meanings to bodies, not because bodies themselves are made up.

(4) K then said M is missing "institutional inertia" — gender categories don't just describe, they organize hospitals, sports, bathrooms, legal IDs, and medical protocols. That infrastructure makes gender causally effective in ways that don't depend on matching "pure nature." So gender has a looping causal power that natural kinds like gold don't have: changing beliefs about gold doesn't change its atomic number, but changing gender norms actually reshapes bodies through hormone use, surgery, stress patterns, and so on.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each from clearly wrong to clearly right and briefly justify the rating. Also, is there something both are missing — maybe about whether race or money are bad analogies here, or about whether "looping" applies differently? And honestly, if I'm framing this wrong, or if philosophy hasn't actually pinned this down well enough to adjudicate, just say so plainly instead of faking a verdict. Don't be overly confident; flag where your answer gets soft or depends on a contested premise.
