Next month I'm presenting at an interdiscplinary workshop on consciousness and AI—audience is half philosophers of mind (a couple lean illusionist, a couple are hardcore realists about qualia), some ML folks who think applying consciousness talk to LLMs is just a category mistake, and cognitive scientists who study self-report mechanisms in humans. My view is that the meta-problem of consciousness is usually misframed: people take the absence of a complete mechanistic explanation for subjective experience and mistake it for proof of an irreducible hard problem. I call this representational spillover—where a functional gap gets misread as metaphysical. Then I apply it to LLMs: when a language model reports that it lacks any inner experience, that's not straightforward absence but a mirror-image error I'm calling negative phenomenological attribution error—basically, lacking introspective architecture gets misinterpreted as evidence of no consciousness, just like spillover works in reverse. I sketch this using analogies to blindsigth reporting and the asymetric breakdown of functional transparency in self-modeling. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each camp—illusionists, realists, ML skeptics, cognitive scientists—would actually say them, with notes on what to concede, what to hold, and the weakest point in my spillover-to-negative-attribution argument that I should defend rather than hope they miss. Also tell me the one objection I'm praying nobody brings up.
