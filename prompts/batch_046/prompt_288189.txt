Consider the following essay outline and central argument.

Essay working title: Stance-Dependent Real Patterns and the Limits of Physical Explanation

Core claim: Daniel Dennett’s concept of real patterns—regularities that support reliable predictions and explanations—need not be interpreted as requiring stance-independent existence. A stronger position can be defended: some real patterns are not merely easier to detect from the design or intentional stance, but are ontologically dependent on the adoption of those stances because they involve irreducibly normative properties such as malfunction, success, and goal-directed error correction.

The essay distinguishes two grades of real patterns. Weak real patterns are those that a physically omniscient observer could, in principle, derive from microphysical dynamics, even if the computation is intractable; examples include planetary trajectories and protein-folding pathways. Strong real patterns, by contrast, are constituted by the ascription of goals, functional roles, and normative evaluations—properties that lack physical correlates except through the lens of the intentional stance. The argument holds that evolutionary convergence produces design patterns that are physically heterogeneous yet functionally identical; the pattern is real, but its reality consists in the normative coherence of the functional description rather than in any shared physical substrate.

The essay then addresses a central tension: if strong patterns are stance-dependent, does this reduce them to subjective projections rather than genuine features of the world? The proposed resolution is that stance-dependence does not entail subjectivity, provided the patterns are intersubjectively robust, counterfactually stable, and indispensable for prediction within their domain. The physical stance remains valid but incomplete—not because it overlooks hidden physical facts, but because the domain of strong patterns is defined by explanatory practices that physical vocabulary cannot translate without loss.

1. Assess whether the distinction between weak and strong real patterns is conceptually coherent, or whether it ultimately collapses into a difference in computational tractability rather than ontological kind.

2. Evaluate whether the proposed resolution succeeds: can stance-dependence be reconciled with realism about patterns without collapsing into instrumentalism?

3. Identify any points where the argument may misrepresent Dennett’s own commitments regarding real patterns, the intentional stance, or his form of anti-reductionism.

4. Suggest one concrete refinement or objection that would strengthen the essay’s defense of strong pattern realism.
