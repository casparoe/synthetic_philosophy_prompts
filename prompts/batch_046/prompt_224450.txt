I want the requirements list organized around four clusters: (i) derivational conditions — which axioms must be shown to follow from coherence constraints alone without circular appeal to the EU representation itself, with special attention to independence and continuity in intertemporal and conditional choice settings; (ii) robustness conditions — which well-known violations and anomalies the argument must accommodate or survive without ad hoc rescue (for instance, Allais-style violations of the independence axiom, ambiguity-averse preferences of the Ellsberg kind, and framing effects documented in the behavioral-economics literature); (iii) boundary conditions — the distinctions the argument must preserve rather than collapse, particularly between epistemic rationality governing belief revision and practical rationality governing preference formation, and between static one-shot choice and commitments that bind an agent across time; and (iv) consistency conditions — the formal results from non-expected-utility representation theory and dynamic choice theory with which the coherence argument must remain compatible. For each cluster, give the specific motivating case or theoretical result that makes the condition mandatory rather than optional, and say whether the cluster is negotiable if one is willing to restrict the domain of application. Then identify the single condition that, in your view, does the most damage to the standard dynamic-consistency arguments for EU maximization, with brief justification. Weight your answer toward the criteria and their rationales; brief scoring of standard contenders is acceptable but secondary. A table summarizing the clusters is fine.
