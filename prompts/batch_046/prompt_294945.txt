Write a reusable decision procedure—ordered questions, sufficient answers, explicit stop-points, and marked judgment flags—that a clinical funding board with no philosophical training could run in thirty minutes to classify whether a proposed condition qualifies for coverage, translating the naturalist and normativist theories of health and disease into practice. The naturalist criterion treats dysfunction as statistical deviation from a species-typical reference-class norm regardless of subjective experience; the normativist criterion requires failure of valued functioning that is either subjectively harmful or socially evaluated as undesirable. The procedure should specify where it stops with a technical ruling (include, exclude, or defer to a values committee) and must flag the steps where evidence runs out and a non-technical decision-maker must take over. Apply it to three cases: (1) a hypothetical state of chronic adaptive hypervigilance, in which stress biomarkers are statistically abnormal and subjective distress is severe, but the state measurably reduces error rates in high-stakes decision environments; (2) an extended culturally embedded mourning practice that exceeds Western clinical duration norms but is socially affirmed and not experienced as harmful by participants; and (3) a population-specific genetic immune variant that is statistically common in that group, produces no subjective suffering, and slightly lowers resistance to a common pathogen. Include at least one case where the procedure delivers an exclusion the board will likely resist, and state precisely what the procedure cannot settle.
