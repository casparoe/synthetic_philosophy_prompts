From this, she draws two further conclusions: first, that Plutarch’s aim is essentially hagiographic, meaning he suppresses unflattering material to construct perfect exemplars for wholesale imitation, which explains why Christian hagiographers later adopted his format; and second, that biography for Plutarch is therefore a form of applied ethics that outperforms philosophical treatises because it delivers concrete rules for action rather than abstract principles.” I have the sense that she is conflating Plutarch with later traditions and overstating his determinism about character, but I am not sure exactly where the distortions lie or whether her practical conclusion about biography supplying rules survives correction.

Could you audit this summary with precision?
