There's this certification protocol some audit boards use for "epistemically humble" AI where they ask the model to rate its own uncertainty on a batch of prompts, then score those ratings by comparing them to the model's own accuracy on a held-out slice drawn from the exact same dataset. So the system is basically checking its own homework with its own answer key. That looks like a straight-up case of the bootstrapping problem—using a faculty to vouch for itself—but they treat it like a valid metric. What view of justification or self-knowledge does that presuppose? That circular validation is harmless for artifical systems, or that coherence alone can ground knowledge? Reconstruct the implicit epistemology, say whether the practice strictly needs reliabilism or is compatible with externalist views that reject easy knowledge, and tell me what the audit would have to change if it actually took the circularity objection seriously.
