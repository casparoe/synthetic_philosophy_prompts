I spent thirty years as a biophysicist studying feedback regulation in cellular signaling, and since retiring I have been working through philosophy of action with the same expectation of precision I once demanded from kinetic models. I find the literature on free will frustratingly unspecific: philosophers seem to conflate several quite different achievements—being the genuine source of one’s actions, having open alternatives at the moment of choice, being responsive to reasons in a non-derivative way, and retaining authorship despite deterministic causation—without clarifying which of these are genuinely worth wanting and which are merely nostalgic residues of a pre-scientific psychology. Before I read any further defenses of compatibilism, libertarianism, or revisionism, I want the full specification first.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate taxonomy of the varieties of free will worth wanting must actually deliver. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or conceptual distinction behind it—for instance, why the theory must preserve a gap between possessing a capacity for choice and actually exercising it (analogous to the biological distinction between competence and performance), why it must distinguish moral-responsibility-level control from mere autonomy, and why it must handle cases in which an agent’s reasoning remains intact but their predictive world-model has been externally corrupted—say, by an algorithm that injects false priors into a decision system without disrupting logical inference itself. I want you to flag any pair of desiderata that look jointly unsatisfiable—perhaps the demand for deep alternative possibilities conflicts with the demand for integrated self-knowledge under determinism—and to identify which single requirement eliminates the largest share of popular positions. Then, briefly, score standard compatibilism, event-causal libertarianism, and agent-causal or revisionist views against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria rather than on the grading.

If this request rests on a confused premise—particularly if treating “free will worth wanting” as a unified target that admits one master specification is itself a category mistake—please say so plainly instead of complying politely. Write your answer in American English.
