You are a professional philosopher of education with deep expertise in distributive justice and experiential pedagogy. Before drafting, think carefully about the structural dynamics of dynastic advantage and brainstorm at least two distinct mechanics—beyond simple cash transfer—through which inherited capital reproduces itself across generations, then select the stronger one for the simulation. After a prolonged, unresolved argument with a close friend about whether inheritance taxes can ever genuinely secure equality of opportunity—given that dynastic wealth operates through intergenerational risk buffering, compound cultural capital, and social-network gatekeeping rather than through direct bequests alone—I want my students to feel that structural logic in their bones rather than debate it in the abstract.

Design a runnable classroom activity or structured simulation for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on justice and equality. The setting is sixteen students in a seminar room, eighty minutes total, with only paper, pens, and a whiteboard available. The participants should come away seeing how formally equal rules of advancement interact with inherited, multi-dimensional advantages to produce radically unequal outcomes that feel genuinely “merited” to the advantaged participants.

The deliverable must include:

1. A brief setup and rules—including hidden roles, initial endowments, or asymmetric information—arranged so that students encounter the compounding effect themselves before the facilitator names it.

Constraints:
