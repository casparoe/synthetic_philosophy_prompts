I am a high school junior writing for my philosophy elective, and I would like you to compose a satirical parody in the form of a deadpan school-district Terms of Service and Course Syllabus for an elective titled Bounded Rationality: The Satisficing Curriculum. Write approximately 500 to 600 words in the bureaucratic voice of an administrator who treats Herbert Simon's theoretical framework as binding institutional policy. The target of the satire should be twofold: the popular reduction of satisficing to a self-help slogan about lowering standards, and the neglect of Simon's more technical distinctions—namely the adaptive dynamics of aspiration levels, the separation of procedural from substantive rationality, the interaction between cognitive architecture and environmental structure often called the scissors metaphor, and the claim that satisficing is not a failure to optimize but rather an optimal response to search costs. The humor must derive from literal application of these ideas: students ought to be penalized for attempting global optimization, aspiration thresholds should be adjusted administratively based on prior performance, and ecological rationality should be listed as a physical-education requirement. Maintain sustained deadpan irony throughout, with no authorial winking, so that a reader acquainted with decision theory sees the exaggeration as conceptually fair rather than invented. Once the parody is complete, append a brief analytical note—distinct in tone and separated clearly from the satirical text—that identifies which claims about bounded rationality are well established, which interpretations remain genuinely contested among specialists (for instance, whether satisficing functions mainly as a descriptive model or can also serve as a normative ideal under severe resource constraints), and where the piece takes deliberate liberties with Simon's arguments for comedic effect. Please do not present any contested view as the consensus, and avoid asking for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
