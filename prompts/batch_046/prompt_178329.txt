You sit on the restitution committee of a national museum that holds both Benin Bronzes and Parthenon Marbles. A coalition of claimant communities has proposed a 'custodial reciprocity' model: immediate transfer of legal title to the originating groups, coupled with a 30-year renewable custodial agreement under which the museum retains physical possession, provided it meets audited conditions on climate control, ritual access, and a prohibition on commercial photography. Trustees are divided—some see this as satisfying moral obligations without sacrificing public access; others argue it splits legal ownership from physical custody in a way that undermines restitution itself. Draft the committee's formal written opinion for circulation before next week's meeting. Recommend whether to accept, reject, or amend the proposal, with a concrete, time-bound course of action. Your reasoning should treat the strongest objection to your position seriously, distinguish the moral legacy of colonial acquisition from the practical governance of shared objects, and be composed in British English.
