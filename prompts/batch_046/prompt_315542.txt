As a novelist drafting a legal thriller set in a fictional commonwealth, I should like you to compose a deadpan bureaucratic form—modelled on a municipal permitting application—for the issuance of an Individual Speech License under the Harm-Prevention Statutes of the invented jurisdiction of Aethelred. The form should function as a sustained parody of the administrative logic that results when Mill's harm principle is translated from philosophical argument into regulatory apparatus. Please include the following sections, rendered entirely in the flat, procedural idiom of government paperwork: first, an applicant declaration distinguishing self-regarding from other-regarding utterance, using checkboxes that quietly erode the boundary through invented categories such as pre-reflective collective affect and anticipatory reputational externality; second, a risk-assessment matrix that treats direct physical harm, indirect moral contagion, and epistemic pollution as quantifiable and commensurable variables; third, an exemption clause for truth-seeking discourse that requires the applicant to demonstrate, via an invented standardized rubric, that the proposed speech will produce a net increase in correct beliefs rather than merely cause offense; and fourth, a notarized affidavit in which the applicant waives claims to the liberties of thought and discussion if the speech is later adjudicated to have generated unquantified discomfort within a protected demographic. The satire should remain deadpan—no explicit condemnation, no authorial winking—so that a reader familiar with Mill recognizes the form as a reductio of his own distinctions: the self-other boundary, the separation of legal penalty from social stigma, and the assumption that harm can be objectively calibrated. The piece should be approximately 600 words. Do not cite real-world statutes or living officials by name; invent all form numbers, institutional titles, and demographic classifications. The humor must arise from the internal consistency of the bureaucracy with Mill's premises rather than from invented absurdities foreign to his framework.
