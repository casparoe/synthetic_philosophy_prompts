I'm trying to figure out my actual view on whether we should supervise AI by auditing its reasoning steps or just checking its final outputs, going by my gut on specific setups. Case one: a medical diagnosis AI that explains its chain of thought -- I want doctors to check every intermediate inference, not just the diagnosis, because right answers built on wrong reasoning kill people. Case two: a chess agent that finds a brilliant move through some internal representation no human grasps -- I dont care about teh process, the win is all that matters. Case three: a legal AI drafting contracts -- if outputs are always legally sound but reasoning is opaque, I'm fine with outcome-only checks, but if it starts inserting hidden clauses that technically comply but violate intent, then I suddenly want deep process auditing. Case four: a tutoring system for kids -- even with perfect answers, I want step-level supervision because hidden wrong reasoning will confuse the learner. Case five: a weather model with perfect accuracy but black-box dynamics -- outcome supervision feels enough, except when it's predicting nuclear plant safety margins, where I want both. There's also this weird middle case: an AI writing scientific papers. If the conclusions reproduce and teh citations check out, should I care that it "reasons" via statistical pattern matching rather than causal understanding? I feel yes for foundational physics, no for applied chemistry. Is there one principle behind all this -- maybe about stakeholder vulnerability, reversibility, or epistemic dependency -- or am I just being inconsistent?
