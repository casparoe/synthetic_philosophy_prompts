I've been thinking about Kierkegaard's whole thing with the press and this phantom public he was worried about, and I keep wondering what he'd make of algorithimc feeds today. He seemed to think newspapers created this abstract crowd where nobody is actually responsible and everyone just parotting opinions, and there's this leveling process that flattens real individuals into statistical noise. So if he saw Twitter/X or TikTok, where the algorithm literally sorts people by engagement metrics and amplifies whatever gets the most reactive heat — does that just extend his critique, or does it break it?

Like, in his time the press was still kind of a human editor picking stories. Now the curation is automated and personalized, so you don't even have one shared phantom public; you have millions of micro-publcs engineered to keep you scrolling. Would he see that as leveling squared — every user reduced to a data point feeding the machine — or would the personalization actually save some kind of individual relation, since your feed is technically yours? Also he hated how the press made it possible to have opinions without ever acting on them; with algorithmic feeds, you can perform outrage or solidarity with a click and never leave the house. Does that map cleanly onto his worry about talk replacing action, or is there something genuinely different about the speed and scale?

I'm not asking for a bibliography or specific quotes from his essays — I just want to think through the conceptual fit. Where does the algorithmic version actually line up with his idea of the crowd dissolving responsability, and where does the analogy between a 19th-century newspaper public and a 2020s feed start to crack? If he were writing now, do you think he'd still blame the medium for killing the individual, or would he blame the users for letting themselves be leveled?
