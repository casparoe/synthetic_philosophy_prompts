I'm auditing my own view of societal collapse for a seminar and want to know if the pieces actually fit together. Here is where I stand: (1) I treat the Late Bronze Age disruptions and the Classic Maya abandonment of lowland cities as reconfiguration rather than collapse—core technologies, religious frameworks, and social hierarchies persisted in dispersed or transformed form; (2) I regard Easter Island as a genuine collapse, marked by severe demographic contraction, the cessation of moai production, and the dissolution of competitive chiefdom structures, driven by ecological overshoot; (3) I hold that "societal failure" is not an objective property but always relative to an observer's normative framework or to the internal goals of the society assessed; (4) despite (3), I judge Easter Island to be an unambiguous case of failure rather than neutral change, precisely because the society's own institutional goals—monumental construction, maritime connectivity, and chiefly competition—were destroyed; (5) I propose that collapse should be defined by rapid, severe demographic and economic contraction relative to baseline, independently of whether we normatively label it failure; and (6) I deny environmental determinism: ecological stress produces collapse only when mediated by rigid institutional structures or path-dependent decision-making, not by resource scarcity alone. Which pairs are in real tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity, specify for each whether (1), (3), (4), (5), or (6) has to give, and explain whether my rejection of determinism in (6) saves (2) from contradicting my framework-relativity in (3).
