I was at the diner last night with my cousin Jake and my sister Marisol and they got into it about whether philospohy actually makes progress or if it's just people talking in circles forever. I don't have a philosophy degree or anything, just been wondering about this myself after reading some random forum posts. They went back and forth four times and I couldn't tell who was winning. Here's how it went.

(1) Jake said philosophy is basically a failure at progress because there's never agreement. Scientists figured out gravity and DNA, but philosophers are still arguing about free will and consciousness after thousands of years. If there was real progress they'd converge like science does, but they don't, so it's just opinions with extra steps.

(2) Marisol pushed back saying that's not fair because science also had huge fights for centuries—like whether light is a wave or particle. She said philosophy's job isn't always to find one answer but to clear up confusion, and we've definatly made progress there. She said now we understand the free will debate is really about compatibilism versus libertarianism, which is a sharper map than people had in ancient times.

(3) Jake said mapping more options isn't progress, it's just making the mess bigger. Real progress means throwing away wrong answers, but philosophy never buries its dead. Dualism is still in textbooks, he said, and nobody ever proves it wrong for good. So if wrong ideas stick around forever, how is that progress?

(4) Marisol said philosophy does bury ideas—logical positivism is basically dead now, and some old dualist arguments got knocked down. She also said comparing philosophy to science misses the point: philosophy is more like conceptual engineering, where you redesign the concepts people use to think, so progress is measured by whether the tools work better, not by reaching one final answer. She asked if fixing a broken idea of justice isn't progress just because there's no lab test for it.

Can you adjudicate this move by move? Rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Also please say how confident you are in these ratings overall, and point out the one move you're least sure about.
