ok so ive been worrying lately about whether im in the reference class for people who get laid off next quarter, and i ended up down a rabbit hole on the reference class problem. ive convinced myself that the whole thing dissolves if you do what im calling causal anchoring: you dont sample over all people who share some description with you, you condition strictly on your actual causal trajectory — the specific chain that put you in this job, with these skills, under this manager. counterfactual versions of me who took different paths dont belong in the class, so the ambiguity about "which class am i in?" just collapses. for practical predictions like job risk, this means using the narrow causal base rate, not the broad statistical bucket.

what would actually count against this? i want the honest exposure map: which arguments, intuitions, formal results, or practical failures show that causal anchoring either fails or that the ambiguity is unavoidable? which ones would a defender of this view have to concede are genuinely damaging, and which are just awkward? also, is there a danger that ive qualified the view into unfalsifiability — like, if any counterexample gets dismissed as "not the right causal trajectory"? and do the predictions this makes actually diverge from just using the broad base rate in real life? rank the defeaters from fatal to merely annoying.
