Construct a diagnostic taxonomy — a branching tree in which each split is a concrete question and each leaf is a named or unnamed position — for the debate about which varieties of free will are worth wanting. Angle the taxonomy specifically on cases of predictive self-transparency coupled with optimized preference formation: an agent knows with near-certainty what she will choose, her first-order and second-order preferences align perfectly, and those preferences were shaped by an external, predictable optimization process (behavioral design, evolutionary tuning, or algorithmic curation) that she now fully endorses upon reflection.

The root should present one such case in concrete terms and ask whether the agent is morally responsible for a harmful action she predicted, endorsed, and executed. From there, build the tree using logical splits rather than a list of author names. Required internal branches must include: (1) whether moral responsibility requires the ability to do otherwise (leeway) or only appropriate identification with one's motives (sourcehood); (2) whether the origin of endorsed preferences undermines their authority even when the agent transparently accepts them; and (3) whether the "worth wanting" criterion applies to first-order choices, to second-order volitions about one's character, or fails to apply at all when both levels are products of the same external optimization.

Leaves should cover at least: hierarchical compatibilism; source incompatibilism or libertarianism; hard determinism or eliminativism about moral responsibility; a revisionist view that redefines "worth wanting" as autonomy rather than desert; and one unnamed combination that is logically coherent but rarely defended. For each leaf, state in one sentence its most awkward commitment within this specific scenario.

Because standard taxonomies often conflate hierarchical compatibilism with source-based views on this exact case, flag one pair of named positions that your tree cannot cleanly separate, and explain why they yield identical answers across your diagnostic questions.
