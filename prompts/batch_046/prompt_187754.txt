Construct a thought experiment in which luck egalitarianism recommends compensating a victim of reckless choice while denying compensation to a victim of pure bad luck, reversing the standard expectation. Constraints: a realistic institutional setting (a municipal emergency-services budget, not a trolley or spaceship), one paragraph, and the reversal must not come from misapplication or an obviously defective rule; it should fall out of an optimal institutional rule that luck egalitarians genuinely have reason to adopt. After the scenario, state the verdict each theory gives and why, identify exactly which feature of the case does the work, explain what happens if that feature is removed, and include a brief note on how a relational egalitarian critic would attack the case.
