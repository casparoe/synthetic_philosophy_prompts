Write the desiderata that an adequate theory of the meta-problem of consciousness must meet — that is, an account of why consciousness presents itself as generating an explanatory gap even if physicalism is true. For each requirement, provide the motivating case, distinction, or empirical result behind it; flag which are non-negotiable and which reflect preferences of particular camps; and identify any subset of desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable, with the reason.

Your list should include and address the following:

- The intuition-preservation condition. The theory must either dissolve or systematically explain the persistent first-person intuition that phenomenal character is not captured by functional or neural descriptions, without merely dismissing it as a cognitive error. Behind this lies the asymmetry between self-attribution and other-attribution of conscious states, and the resistance of this intuition to correction by neuroscientific education. Mark whether treating this intuition as veridical is negotiable.

- The empirical-consistency constraint. The account must be compatible with established findings on blindsight, split-brain coordination failures, and global workspace dynamics. Specify which results are genuinely constraining for any meta-theory and which are interpreted differently by competing camps (for instance, whether global workspace theory explains away the gap or merely relocates it).

- The phenomenal-residue condition. The theory must explain why some subjects continue to report that something remains unexplained — a lingering phenomenal residue — even after they accept a complete physical-functional story. This should not be reduced to pure linguistic confusion, yet it must also avoid reintroducing an ontological gap. State explicitly whether satisfying this condition together with strict physicalism forces an illusionist conclusion, and flag that pair as potentially jointly unsatisfiable if you think so.

- The evolutionary-continuity requirement. If phenomenal consciousness is not uniquely human, the theory should explain why the meta-problem — the sense of a hard explanatory gap — is disproportionately acute in human reflective self-consciousness rather than distributed across comparable cognitive architectures in other species.

- The no-ontological-collapse rule. A non-negotiable prohibition on explaining the epistemic gap in ways that secretly depend on an irreducible phenomenal property or on an unexplained primitive. Contrast this with the negotiable preference for ontological monism over explanatory pluralism.

- The inter-subjective-testability desideratum. The theory should predict or accommodate observed differences in the strength of the hard-problem intuition across populations, cultures, or cognitive styles, so that the meta-problem is treated as an empirical phenomenon to be explained rather than as a universal a priori datum. Note whether this conflicts with treating the intuition as rationally mandatory.

Keep the conceptual weight on the criteria themselves: which demands are genuine and which are prejudices of one camp, whether the phenomenal-residue condition and the no-ontological-collapse rule can be jointly satisfied without collapsing into illusionism, and which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates. Then, briefly, score three existing approaches — illusionism in the vein of Dennett or Frankish, the phenomenal-concept strategy, and a type-B physicalist account — against your list. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
