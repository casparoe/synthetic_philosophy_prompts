My study partner Marcus has these opinions about whether to keep old rules and objects in our town. He fought to keep the rotting footbridge near the creek even though nobody uses it, saying we don't know why they built it with those weird angles so we shouldn't tear it down. He said scrap the old dress code at the social club once they found out it was just copied from some other town's handbook in the 60s. He insisted on keeping the in-person town meeting despite everyone wanting Zoom, because he said bodily presence does something you can't reproduce. He wanted to keep the handwritten margin codes in the archive ledgers even though nobody can read them now. But he was fine with demolishing the old tax office after the factory it served burned down.

Now theres a proposal to digitize all the archive ledgers. The system would scan every page and preserve the text, but the physical books have these coffee stains in specific spots and a binding ritual done every January that no one remembers the origin of. The digitization team says the stains are noise and the ritual is superstition, and they want to move the originals to storage and end the ceremony.

What will Marcus say? I feel like he could be running a pure Chestertons fence rule, or a Burkean embedded-wisdom-of-tradition thing, or maybe he's just inconsistently nostalgic. What's the best reconstruction of his actual rule, what do the other reconstructions predict about this digitization case, and what's one hypothetical future proposal that would actually tell them apart? If he sides with digitization, what principle is he secretly committing to? If he sides against, same question.
