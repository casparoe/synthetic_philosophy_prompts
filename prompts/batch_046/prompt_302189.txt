I take the position that liberal neutrality in AI value alignment should be interpreted as structural pluralism with procedural arbitration: rather than aggregating incommensurable values into a shared metric or selecting one comprehensive doctrine, an aligned system ought to preserve competing value frameworks as architecturally live options and adjudicate conflicts through rules that do not presuppose the commensurability of the goods at stake. What would genuinely count against this? I am asking for the conceptual, formal, or practical defeaters—arguments that such arbitration necessarily smuggles in a hidden metric, that maintaining live incommensurable frameworks is decision-theoretically unstable under bounded computation, or that this form of neutrality systematically privileges the status quo or dominant groups. Please rank them from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish what defenders of structural pluralism would themselves concede as damaging from what critics allege, and assess honestly whether the position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability—e.g., by defining neutrality purely procedurally so that no substantive outcome can ever count against it. If it is so insulated, is that a vice for a normative theory of this kind?
