I've been arguing with my flatmate about whether it's ever fair to say an AI actually believes something, rather than just acting like it does. I think my intuitions are mostly sensible but there's definately a tension I can't resolve, and I'd like to know what principle, if any, captures them — or which one I should drop.

Here are my verdicts on concrete cases. There's a customer-service chatbot that outputs 'I'm frustrated with you' every Tuesday because of a hidden date trigger in it's system prompt — no affective architecture whatsoever. To me that's clearly not a belief or emotion, just a script running. Then there's a robot vacuum that reroutes around a chair; saying it 'wants' to avoid the obstacle seems totally harmless functional talk, even though it's just path-planning. But then there's a large language model fine-tuned on a flat-earth corpus that answers consistently with that worldview across hundreds of prompts — it's internal representations are organised around that coherence, but it's obviously just pattern-matching. Here I feel there's genuinely something belief-like going on, closer to belief than the chatbot, despite no consciousness. Meanwhile there's a diagnostic system with a seperate 'error-checker' module that flags logical inconsistencies but never feeds back into the output; I'd say we shouldn't attribute self-corrective beliefs there, because the check is causally inert. Then my friend outsources all her research to an AI and just parrots it's conclusions — I still feel she genuinely believes them, even though the cognitive work is outsourced. But finally there's a distributed network of small models where no single node holds a representation, yet collectively they behave like they share one political ideology; attributing belief to the network feels wrong to me, like we're over-interpreting the pattern.

So is there one account of intentional attribution that delivers all six verdicts? If not, which should I overturn and why? And whatever principle you land on, please tell me what it predicts for the case I keep going back and forth on: an AI that generates post-hoc rationalisations for its answers that don't causally drive the answers, but the rationalisations are consistently coherent and would survive counterfactual testing.
