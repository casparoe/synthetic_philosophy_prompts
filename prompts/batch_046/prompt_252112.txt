in my reading group people keep comparing Fitch's paradox to a self-referential password system -- the idea is that the knowability principle promises every true statement has a "key" (someone could come to know it), but then you feed the system the statement "this statement is unknown" and the whole thing locks itself out. they use it to argue the principle is just broken by design, like security software with a built-in backdoor. can you audit how far this holds? give me the correspondence table -- password, lockout, backdoor, the admin who issues keys -- then the places it breaks. especially, does it smuggle in that knowledge is access-controlled rather than representational? and does the conclusion that the principle is "broken by design" survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy carrying the argument the whole time?
