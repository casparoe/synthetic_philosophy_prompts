Introduce a formal language L with a one-place truth predicate Tr(x) and a unary operator F, read as φ is semantically fixed (its evaluation is invariant under reflective language expansion). Define the sentence λ by the equivalence λ ↔ ¬F(Tr(⌜λ⌝)). The exam question should ask the student to do three things: (a) determine whether λ exhibits the syntactic self-reference required by Tarski's hierarchy, and whether the hierarchy can assign it a stable level; (b) assess whether Kripke's fixed-point construction can include λ in the extension of Tr without contradiction, specifically examining whether F introduces an instability that prevents a stable fixed point; and (c) evaluate whether a dialetheist can consistently hold that λ is both true and false, and identify what non-explosive control mechanism (for instance, relevance restrictions on entailment) must be in place to prevent this acceptance from being ad hoc. Together with the question, provide a model answer of about 350–450 words and a marking scheme that awards credit for five specific analytical moves—distinguishing syntactic from semantic self-reference in λ; explaining why Tarskian stratification either applies vacuously or breaks down; diagnosing the oscillation or gap in Kripke's construction caused by F; articulating the dialetheist's commitment to a constrained contradiction; and avoiding the common error of treating λ as identical to the standard strengthened liar. Keep the entire response—question, model answer, and marking scheme—within 800 words. Write in formal academic prose. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; do not invoke obscure named theories outside standard curricula; rely solely on the machinery defined above and on widely recognized frameworks (Tarskian stratification, Kripkean fixed points, dialetheism).
