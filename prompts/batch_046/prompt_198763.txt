In debates over individual moral responsibility for collective climate harm, a common analogy compares one person's carbon footprint to a single drop added to an ocean that is already rising: the drop is causally redundant, yet the ocean is made of drops. Third, address the misconception that causal overdetermination dissolves moral responsibility, and pinpoint exactly where in the reasoning that move should be blocked. Fourth, propose whether a different analogy — one that preserves structural dependence but avoids the false implication of causal invisibility, perhaps involving network signaling or threshold dynamics — would be more accurate, specifying what it must keep and what it must drop.
