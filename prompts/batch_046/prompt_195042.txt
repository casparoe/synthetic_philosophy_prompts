They emphasize Gödel's remarks on the ideality of time and argue that if the field equations allow a cosmos in which an observer can, in principle, return to her own past, then temporal passage is at best a local approximation rather than a feature of reality itself. On the other side, interpreters closer to John Dawson and to working relativists dismiss the model as physically unrealistic: it requires perfect cosmic rigidity, conflicts with observed isotropy and Hubble expansion, and was never presented by Gödel as an empirical claim about our universe.
