Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on mental-health disorder classification. Camp A defends strict biostatistical naturalism: a condition is a disorder only if an internal mechanism operates at statistically subnormal efficiency relative to its evolutionary design, irrespective of subjective harm or social setting. Camp B defends integrated normativism: disorder ascription necessarily includes an evaluative judgment that the condition causes harm or undermines valued functioning, and biological dysfunction without harm is never sufficient. Both camps have argued past each other on cases such as trauma-induced hypervigilance that improves objective survival metrics while producing severe subjective distress, and neurodivergent cognitive profiles that are statistically atypical yet ecologically advantageous in specific niches.

Your task is not to adjudicate but to produce the constructive document both sides could sign. Organize the response in three parts: (1) Agreed claims—at least four bullet points of shared factual premises (e.g., evolutionary mismatch occurs, neuroplasticity modifies mechanisms, suffering is intersubjectively variable, clinical taxonomies are contested); (2) Precise disagreements—state each camp’s position in neutral wording both accept, isolating whether biological function can be assessed independently of ecological context and whether harm is conceptually prior to dysfunction; (3) Cruxes and predictive tests—identify the sub-questions that would move at least one side, specifically (a) whether a mechanism can be statistically normal within a reference class yet pathological in a specific environment, and (b) whether harm judgments can be standardized without smuggling in hidden functional baselines. For each crux, propose one concrete hypothetical case or empirical pattern both agree is relevant, and record in advance each camp’s prediction about it.

Use formal, polished language. Keep the entire response to no more than 400 words. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for further sources.
