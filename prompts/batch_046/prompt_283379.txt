I'm developing a diagnostic taxonomy for my dissertation in normative ethics and need a decision tree—not a list of named schools—that sorts suffering-focused positions by what they actually commit to, aimed at other graduate students in moral philosophy. The tree should have about five to six leaves and be built from the logical space of commitments rather than from philosopher-labels.

Begin with a scope node: does the view grant direct moral consideration both to wild-animal suffering and to hypothetical future digital minds that might experience suffering without any corresponding pleasure? Next, branch on the asymmetry claim—whether happiness or well-being has intrinsic positive value, or is merely the absence of suffering. Then include a trade-off node: can the prevention of suffering be overridden by gains in non-welfare goods such as knowledge, aesthetic value, or ecological complexity? Follow with a priority node: is suffering-reduction lexically prior to other obligations, or weighted but defeasible?

The leaves should include: (1) strong negative utilitarianism that accepts asymmetry, broad scope, and lexical priority; (2) a prioritarian suffering-focused view with broad scope but permitting trade-offs; (3) classical utilitarianism that weights suffering and happiness symmetrically yet discounts distant future suffering steeply; (4) an unnamed position that accepts asymmetry and broad scope but denies intervention obligations on grounds of epistemic humility about ecological or technological side-effects; and (5) an ecological preservationist view that rejects digital-mind moral status and treats wild suffering as non-intervention-worthy because natural processes carry independent value.

Phrase every internal node as a concrete commitment a respondent could endorse without knowing which leaf it leads to. Finally, flag any pair your tree fails to separate—particularly whether the prioritarian and the unnamed epistemic-humility views collapse together when both reject lexical priority—and briefly diagnose any empty logical branch, such as a view that denies asymmetry, extends scope to digital minds, and still treats suffering-reduction as lexically prior.
