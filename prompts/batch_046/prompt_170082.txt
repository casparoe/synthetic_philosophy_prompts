Write an essay on whether the folk morality of procedural order and rule-following—exemplified by the German emphasis on Ordnung, predictable coordination, and the intrinsic wrongness of deviation—has a stronger normative claim than the American folk morality of individual conscience, principled exception, and the duty to disobey unjust rules. Do not treat this as a cultural survey; instead, focus on a specific conceptual tension. When a democratically enacted but morally corrupt law demands action that violates a citizen's core conviction, is the primary wrong of disobedience the erosion of social coordination and mutual predictability, or is it the betrayal of one's own moral agency? Weigh the argument that order is a precondition for any rights to be secure against the argument that conscience is the only reliable brake on collective injustice. Reach a definite conclusion about which value ought to prevail in such hard cases, and defend it with substantive reasons rather than appeals to national character. In your analysis, introduce and evaluate a distinction between breaking a rule because the rule lacks moral authority and breaking a rule despite its retaining some residual authority that makes the act genuinely costly—ask whether this distinction undermines one of the two moral frameworks or reveals a hidden convergence between them.
