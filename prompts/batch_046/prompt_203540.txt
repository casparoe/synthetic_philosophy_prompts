I've been arguing about Molinism with a philosophy of religion reading group for months, and we've hit the exact same wall every session. The Molinists maintain that God possesses middle knowledge of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom—true propositions like "if Peter were placed in circumstance C, he would freely deny Christ"—and that this knowledge is pre-volitional, allowing God to actualize worlds without causally determining the choices within them. The critics respond with the grounding objection (what makes these contingent counterfactuals true prior to creation if they are not grounded in actual decisions?) and with the worry that infallible foreknowledge of free acts renders those acts inevitable in some sense incompatible with libertarian freedom. We have traced every standard version of these replies—the Ockhamist claim that counterfactuals are grounded in God's will rather than in created events, the distinctions between strong and weak actualization, and the various attempts to show that knowledge does not entail causation—yet nobody in the room shifts position, not out of stubbornness but because the objections and defenses seem to pass through one another without making contact.

My working theory is that the disagreement is not fundamentally about divine foreknowledge or libertarian freedom at all, but about a hidden modal-metaphysical assumption I will call counterfactual stability: whether an agent's free responses to circumstances form a single, coherent modal profile that can be known as a determinate unit, or whether they fragment across nearby possible worlds in ways that make "Peter in C" insufficiently well-defined to support a unique counterfactual truth-value. If that is right, then Molinists need stability for middle knowledge to be knowledge of something genuinely determinate, while critics implicitly deny stability but never defend that denial on its own terms, smuggling it into talk about "grounding" and "inevitability" instead. Is my diagnosis correct? Or is there a substantive disagreement about freedom, explanation, or epistemic access that I am missing beneath the surface? I want a precise diagnosis—whether the stall is verbal (we are using "free choice," "knowledge," or "grounding" in different ways), whether it masks a bedrock clash about modal metaphysics or personal identity across worlds that neither side can argue for, or whether it conceals a conceptual question about the structure of practical reasoning that could in principle be resolved. Tell me what each side actually commits itself to regarding the determinacy of counterfactual behavior, not a survey of the literature, and say what—if anything—would count as genuine progress: a distinction between kinds of modal knowledge, a formal result about the logic of counterfactuals, a reconceptualization of agency, or an honest argument that the deadlock is permanent and why.
