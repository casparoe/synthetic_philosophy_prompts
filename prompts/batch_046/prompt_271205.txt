Write a dialogue between two friends—call them Jordan and Sam—who are arguing whether race, gender, and money are all "made up" in the same way. Jordan thinks money is the obvious social construct since it's literally just paper we agreed has value, but race feels different because people always point to family trees and skin color. Sam thinks that's backwards—race is the pure invention with no real biological backbone, while money is tied to actual labor, scarcity, and physical exchange so it isn't just imaginary. They should actually respond to each other: Sam should challenge Jordan's idea that shared belief is enough to keep something real, and Jordan should push back on whether labor makes money less constructed. Have them use gender as a middle example, and get them wondering whether the real split is between things that need constant everyday reinforcement—like performing gender or policing racial boundaries—versus things that run on autopilot once institutions are set up, like currency. Neither should sound like an idiot. Keep it like a late-night kitchen-table talk, not a lecture. No reading lists, and dont worry about citing anyone.
