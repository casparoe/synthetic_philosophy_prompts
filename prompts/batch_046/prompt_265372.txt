I am drafting a short philosophical essay and would like your comments. The working title is "Residual Phenomenality and the Inversion of Global Workspace Theory."

The core argument runs as follows. The standard Block-inspired distinction treats phenomenal consciousness (what it is like) and access consciousness (availability for reasoning and report) as conceptually separable, with global workspace theory taken to explain only the latter through global broadcasting of information. I want to challenge the framing that phenomenal consciousness is the unexplained remainder once access is accounted for. Instead, I propose that phenomenality should be understood as the structural signature of locally integrated information that exceeds a coherence threshold for cross-modal binding but fails to reach the ignition threshold required for entry into the global workspace. On this view, split-brain cases do not show phenomenal consciousness without access; they show that local integration can achieve a proto-phenomenal state precisely because it is blocked from global broadcasting, not despite that block. Consequently, global workspace theory is not incomplete in leaving out phenomenality—it mischaracterizes phenomenality as an independent layer rather than as the specific profile of integration-failure relative to workspace entry conditions.

Please comment on three things: (1) whether this inversion is conceptually coherent or whether it collapses phenomenal consciousness into a mere mechanistic description of failed broadcasting; (2) whether the appeal to local recurrent processing and ignition thresholds introduces an empirical commitment that makes the argument vulnerable to neuroscientific refutation in ways a purely conceptual argument should avoid; and (3) whether the essay's framing unfairly treats standard defenders of global workspace theory, since they might reply that broadcasting failure is not the same as local integration success, and that my residual account conflates absence of report with presence of phenomenology.

Please conclude with a one-line summary of your central critique, beginning with 'Summary:'.
