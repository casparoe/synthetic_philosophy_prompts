I defend a restricted hybrid on the epistemic regress: infinitism governs justification for occurrent self-knowledge, where grounding a mental-state belief in another belief generates an open-ended hierarchy with no non-circular terminus, whereas foundationalism governs perceptual and memorial beliefs about the external world, terminating in non-inferential sensory access. What would genuinely count against this domain-split? Rank the defeaters by severity: first, arguments that the accessibility constraint applies uniformly and therefore cannot be selectively relaxed for first-person cases; second, phenomenological or formal results suggesting that perceptual justification is itself hierarchically structured in a way that mirrors self-knowledge; and third, considerations about the unity of consciousness implying that all justification shares a single architecture or that the first-person/third-person boundary is unstable. For each, indicate whether a committed defender of the hybrid should concede damage, dismiss it as relying on a false uniformity assumption, or absorb it by refining the split. Finally, assess whether this position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability—if every apparent counterexample is met by redrawing the domain boundary, is that a methodological vice for a thesis about the structure of justification?
