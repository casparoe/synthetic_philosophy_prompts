Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers debating the distribution of consciousness across non-human animals. One, advocating a graded-substrate position, argues that phenomenal consciousness need not be all-or-nothing but can be partially realized in anatomically distributed neural systems—such as the semi-autonomous adaptive networks in octopus arms—without requiring a centralized global workspace. The other, defending an integrated-threshold view, maintains that subjective experience necessarily depends on unified information integration within a single functional architecture, and that complex local reflex learning in peripheral tissue therefore remains non-conscious regardless of its sophistication. Have them respond directly to each other: the graded-substrate advocate should address the integrationist's worry about the absence of reportable unified experience, while the integrationist should answer the charge that requiring centralization arbitrarily privileges mammalian cortical structures. Keep the exchange focused on the conceptual relationship between neural distribution, information integration, and the boundaries of minimal sentience.
