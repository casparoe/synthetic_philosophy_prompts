I am a novelist attempting to think through a philosophical problem that lies beneath the mechanics of craft. The familiar distinction between knowing how and knowing that—between the ability to perform a skill and the possession of articulable truths about it—seems clear enough in ordinary cases: one knows how to balance on a bicycle without necessarily knowing that the gyroscopic forces involved are negligible. But I want to probe an intermediate territory closer to writing fiction: the structural intuition by which an experienced novelist recognizes, in the midst of drafting, that a particular reversal of fortune will feel inevitable rather than contrived, without being able to state the governing principle.

Some philosophers treat this intuition as pure knowing-how, an embodied competence that resists translation into propositions. Others treat it as compressed knowing-that: a dense cluster of tacit propositions about narrative causality that remain below the threshold of conscious access. The difficulty is sharpened by what I will call retroactive explicitization. Once the novel is finished, the same writer can often produce a precise theoretical account of why the structure succeeds—identifying, for instance, the precise moment when dramatic irony hardens into tragic inevitability. The conceptual question is whether this post-hoc analysis uncovers propositional knowledge that was already present, though submerged, during composition; or whether it generates genuinely new knowing-that that played no operative role in the original act of craft.

If the former, then narrative expertise collapses into a hidden intellectualism: the writer always knew the rules but could not access them in real time. If the latter, we need an account of how knowing-how can be non-propositional yet systematically open to propositional extraction only after the fact. Please explain this conceptual tension in depth: distinguish carefully between the intellectualist and anti-intellectualist readings of craft, show why retroactive explicitization creates a distinctive problem for each, and indicate which view you find more defensible. I am not asking for empirical studies of cognition or for citations to philosophical texts—only for a rigorous conceptual analysis that a writer could use to understand the nature of their own expertise.
