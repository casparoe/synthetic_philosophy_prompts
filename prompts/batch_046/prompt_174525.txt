Read Christopher Nolan's *Interstellar* through the lens of the Rietdijk-Putnam argument for eternalism: the claim that special relativity's relativity of simultaneity makes presentism ontologically unstable, since different inertial frames would disagree about which distant events exist. The film dramatizes extreme temporal separation through gravitational time dilation on Miller's planet and, more radically, through the tesseract sequence in which Cooper observes and influences his own past. Ask whether the film treats these relations as connections between equally real four-dimensional locations—thereby illustrating the block universe—or whether it preserves a metaphysically privileged present anchored to Cooper's subjective urgency. Focus especially on the bookshelf scene: does the visual representation of Murph's room as a traversable spatial object support the Rietdijk-Putnam analogy between time and space, or does the emotional demand that he act "now" to save her smuggle presentism back into the narrative? Conclude by saying what this cinematic case contributes that the philosophical argument alone cannot: does rendering relativistic time travel emotionally intelligible strengthen eternalism, or does it expose an unacknowledged dependence on presentist intuition?
