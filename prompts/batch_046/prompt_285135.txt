I am helping design a two-year research program for a small group working at the intersection of epistemology and formal reasoning. Our starting point is Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise: the observation that applying a logical rule to premises in order to reach a conclusion seems to require accepting that rule as an additional premise, generating a regress unless one allows circularity or an unjustified stopping point. Rather than treating this as a settled historical curiosity, we want to treat it as a live constraint on how we should think about the justification of deduction—both in human cognition and in artificial systems that perform chain-of-thought inference without explicit meta-level endorsement of each rule.

We have limited resources—essentially one senior researcher and a graduate student for about eighteen months—so we cannot pursue every angle. Among the live issues we are considering are: whether the tortoise problem reveals a genuine epistemic deficit in deductive reasoning or merely a category mistake about the nature of logical rules; whether non-circularist responses (such as those appealing to implicit knowledge, logical pluralism, or pragmatic entitlement) can be made precise enough to serve as design principles for automated reasoning systems; and whether the problem takes on a different shape if we distinguish local justification of individual inferences from global justification of an entire deductive framework, especially in systems that learn inference patterns rather than being hard-coded with them.

Given these options and the constraint that the work must be philosophically substantive yet not depend on empirical results we cannot gather in this window, which two or three open questions or lines of work should we prioritize? I want a defended agenda, not a survey: specify the criteria you are using (for instance, upstream dependency on other debates, tractability within our timeframe and skill set, or relevance to contemporary issues in automated reasoning), explain the trade-offs that cause you to deprioritize the others, and indicate what kind of conceptual result over the next year or two would cause you to reorder your ranking.
