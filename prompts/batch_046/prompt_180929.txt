I am drafting the constitutional framework for a speculative polity in my next novel, and I want a rigorous taxonomy—presented as a decision tree with branch nodes phrased as concrete institutional choices—to classify the variants of predictive-market governance that could plausibly emerge. The tree should begin from the commitment that policy ought to be guided by forecasted welfare metrics rather than by direct deliberation alone, then branch through four binary or ternary decision nodes: whether the market forecasts are binding or merely advisory; whether the value function that the market optimises is fixed ex ante or can be revised by a separate democratic mechanism; whether participation in the forecasting layer is open, credential-gated, or randomly sampled; and whether there exists a retroactive veto window in which realised outcomes dissolve prior contracts. Each leaf should correspond to one named or clearly described institutional form—classic futarchy, advisory prediction markets with legislative supremacy, nested expert-jury markets with democratic value-setting, open-participation welfare optimisation with irrevocable commitment, and at least one combination that the logical space permits but that remains institutionally unexplored—together with a brief diagnosis of why that cell is occupied or vacant. Where two leaves are not genuinely separable by the preceding nodes, flag the ambiguity explicitly rather than inventing a distinction. The instrument is intended for readers of philosophical fiction who have no access to external sources, so do not include bibliographies, citation requests, or appeals to obscure academic nomenclature; instead, inject one deliberately unconventional mechanism—perhaps a shadow market that runs counterfactual forecasts on rejected policies—to ensure the taxonomy is not merely a restatement of familiar introductory material. Write in polished British English, with each node phrased as a question a citizen-designer could answer without knowing the taxonomy's labels in advance.
