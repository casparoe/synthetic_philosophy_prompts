my friends jules and riley got into it after a phil seminar (i was just sitting there with my coffee). jules laid out galen strawsons basic argument: you can't be truly responsible for what you do because you're not responsible for your nature, and you're not responsible for your nature cause you're not responsible for its causes, and so on ad infinitum. so true moral responsibility needs you to be causa sui, which is impossible.

riley said thats only a problem if responsibility requires being a self-created god. in real life when we blame someone for being cruel we just check whether the cruelty flows from their own stable values and reflective attitudes - not whether they invented those values ex nihilo.

jules pushed back that if thats all we check then strawsons argument is trivial - just "you can't be god" - and misses the point. the point is our practices implicitly demand ultimate authorship, which is why they're metaphysically unfounded even if we dont realize it.

riley replied that strawson is wrong about what our practices presuppose. "this person is cruel" just means the cruelty is genuinely theirs, not an alien imposition like a tumor. that doesnt require self-creation, just that it's not forced.

then jules brought up someone who deliberately engineers their own character over years - using self-binding, habit reversal, all that - to become more callous. at some point their current bad nature is partly the result of earlier choices they made. doesnt that break the regress?

riley said no, those earlier choices came from prior selves with prior motives. you never reach ground floor. but jules said then "genuinely theirs" collapses into "caused by their brain," which applies to tumors too, so riley needs a normative boundary. and if there's never a ground floor of self-creation, any boundary is arbitrary.
