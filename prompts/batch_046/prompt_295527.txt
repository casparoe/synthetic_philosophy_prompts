Write a short research proposal (approximately 800–1,000 words) for a three-month independent study, addressing Albert Camus’s opposition to capital punishment and its uneasy relation to his philosophy of rebellion. Frame the project precisely: Camus’s essay opposing the guillotine treats state execution as premeditated murder that annihilates the solidarity rebellion requires, whereas his study of rebellion permits limited violence when it preserves human solidarity. Your proposal must resolve whether this anti-death-penalty stance derives from absurdist ethics, from a standalone humanist appeal, or from an implicit distinction between bureaucratic administrative killing and visible rebellious violence—a distinction Camus never fully articulates but which may underpin both texts. Explain why interpretations that treat the essay as mere moral outrage or as a direct application of absurdist logic fail to settle this question.

Set out your method: a close conceptual reconstruction comparing the normative conditions for legitimate violence in the rebellion study with the structure of the argument in the execution essay, rather than a citation-heavy literature review. State explicitly which comparative angles—for instance, with contemporaneous existentialist writings on punishment, or with later philosophical analyses of sovereign power—must be excluded to keep the project within three months, and justify those exclusions. Describe what a successful result would look like (for example, showing that the implicit distinction holds or collapses under pressure) and what would count as failure (finding the texts irreconcilable without importing external premises). Provide an honest scope assessment: identify which primary texts will be analysed in full, which sections must be treated selectively, and how you will avoid constructing unverifiable bibliographic claims without specialised archival access.

Conclude by arguing why resolving this ambiguity matters for contemporary debates about state punishment, without appealing to comprehensive surveys of penal philosophy or requesting a bibliography. Write in formal British English, using British spelling and punctuation conventions throughout (e.g. analyse, behaviour, centre).
