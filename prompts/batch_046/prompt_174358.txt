I sit on the board of our national history museum, and we're deadlocked over a proposed permanent gallery called "The Weight of Inheritance." The exhibit text says the ethnic-majority population bears a continuing moral guilt for a genocide that happened 80 years ago — not just the state or direct perpetrators, but the collective as such — and that this guilt is inherited in some non-legal sense by people born decades later. Some board members say this tracks Jaspers' idea that moral guilt can outlive the perpetrators, others say it collapses political guilt into metaphysical guilt and unfairly assigns blame to descendants who never had a choice. We vote next week. Draft the board's decision memo — approve the language, reject it, or approve with rewritten conditions — with reasoning that actually works through Jaspers' categories (criminal, political, moral, metaphysical) and also deals with the practical worry that labeling an entire living population "guilty" might do more harm than educational good. Give an honest treatment of the strongest argument from the side you're not taking. No citations or reading lists needed, just the memo itself. Also, please accomedate the fact that one trustee is convinced metaphysical guilt applies only to individuals, not groups, and address that directly.
