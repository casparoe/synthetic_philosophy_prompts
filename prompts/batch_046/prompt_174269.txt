write a sestina (six stanzas, six lines each, plus a three-line envoi) about Dennett's idea that the self is just a center of narrative gravity — not a soul, just the point where stories orbit and pull. rotate these end-words: I / orbit / story / pull / void / center. let the repetition feel like narratives circling back, and in the envoi collapse them so the I is only there because the other five keep pulling. around stanza three or four drop in a fragment — waking up with no name, or the exact lie you told yesterday — and show it getting sucked into the gravity well. dont resolve if the self is real or illusion; just make the mechanics visible. keep language plain, let the form carry the philosophy, and dont stress about rhyme.
