I am revising the syllabus for my graduate seminar on Hellenistic ethics and its modern receptions, and I need a diagnostic taxonomy for the session on Stoic self-regulation. The central problem is how contemporary revivalists apply Epictetus’s distinction between what lies within our power and what does not—what I will call the dichotomy of control—to domains that ancient Stoics never systematically addressed: collective institutional action, emotional regulation in clinical contexts, and algorithmically mediated attention. I want the taxonomy structured as a decision tree with four branching nodes. The root should ask whether the interpreter treats the dichotomy as strictly exhaustive or as admitting a middle sphere of influence that is neither fully internal nor fully external. The second node should ask whether emotional responses are classified as direct objects of control, as indirect byproducts of judgment alone, or as partially shaped by external framing that still permits a Stoic response. The third node should ask whether collective structures—laws, workplaces, digital platforms—can be proper objects of Stoic ethical concern without collapsing the boundary between self and circumstance. The fourth node should ask how algorithmic curation of information is categorized: as an external circumstance to be endured, as a judgment-forming environment that falls within our power to navigate, or as a hybrid that undermines the dichotomy altogether.

Each leaf should carry a label—such as strict revivalist, CBT-integrated Stoicism, political Stoicism, or unnamed—together with a single sentence stating its most awkward commitment, particularly where the view must either deny that platform design shapes our judgments or concede that the dichotomy is unstable in mediated life. Please flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate on the basis of these four questions; for example, if popular self-help Stoicism and clinical CBT Stoicism converge on every node, note that explicitly rather than inventing a distinction. Additionally, identify any logically possible combination of answers that appears unoccupied and briefly diagnose whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The handout is for advanced undergraduates and graduate students, so the language should be precise but not jargon-heavy, and please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific editions or secondary sources, since the exercise is meant to be completed without external research.
