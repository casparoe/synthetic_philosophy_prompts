As a doctoral student in philosophy of science working on AI epistemology, I am trying to orient my research agenda around the problem of eliciting latent knowledge from large language models, but specifically around a framing that I think is underdeveloped: the distinction between representational extraction and counterfactual disposition tracking. Most of the literature I encounter treats elicitation as revealing pre-formed beliefs or representations hidden in weights, but I am concerned that what we actually measure are stable counterfactual dispositions to assent under specific interrogation conditions—dispositions that may not correspond to any unified representational state. Within that framing, I want to understand which open questions should actually command limited research resources over the next several years.

Specifically, I see at least three clusters of unresolved issues. First, the ontological cluster: whether successfully elicited content satisfies standard conditions for knowledge or belief (justification, truth-tracking, absence of defeaters) or whether it is better understood as a predictive regularity that happens to be verbally articulable. Second, the methodological cluster: whether current elicitation protocols suffer from what I will call an "interrogation asymmetry"—a systematic bias toward propositional, linguistically formatted outputs that may obscure procedural, indexical, or non-propositional representations, and whether this asymmetry can be corrected without introducing new artifacts. Third, the strategic cluster: which safety-relevant evaluations genuinely depend on resolving the first two clusters, and which would remain unchanged regardless of how those questions are answered.

Given these clusters, I am asking for a defended, dependency-aware prioritization rather than a survey. Which of these questions is upstream of the others? For instance, does the strategic cluster collapse into the ontological one—so that if elicited content is not genuinely representational, then no safety audit based on belief-elicitation can be trusted—or can strategic evaluation proceed independently? Which questions are tractable with current interpretability and evaluation tools, and which require conceptual breakthroughs that are not yet visible? What criteria—importance to AI governance, tractability within a doctoral timeline, neglectedness in current funding portfolios, or downstream dependency—should govern how a small research group allocates effort among them? And finally, which lines of inquiry should be actively deprioritized because resolving them would not substantially alter either the theoretical framework or the practical safety landscape?

Please defend your ordering explicitly, indicate where you think the dependencies run in both directions, and avoid treating all three clusters as equally urgent.
