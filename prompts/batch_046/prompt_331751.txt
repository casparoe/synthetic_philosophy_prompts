Having spent a career calibrating measurement instruments, I have lately been trying to understand Nozick’s tracking conditions for knowledge through the lens of tolerance-range specification, and I have applied them to the old closure problem. Here is an argument I built; each step looks defensible, yet the conclusion strikes me as wrong. (1) Nozick’s sensitivity condition: S knows that p only if, in the nearest possible worlds where p is false, S does not believe p. (2) Epistemic closure: if S knows p and competently deduces q from p, then S knows q. (3) Suppose S knows p and p strictly entails q. Consider the nearest worlds in which q is false. (4) In all such worlds, p is false. (5) By (1) applied to p, in worlds where p is false that lie within p’s relevant modal range, S does not believe p. (6) Because these are precisely the nearest q-false worlds, they must lie within that same modal range for p; hence in them S lacks belief in p. (7) Since S’s belief in q is formed solely through deduction from p together with the known entailment, lacking belief in p entails lacking belief in q. (8) Therefore, in the nearest worlds where q is false, S does not believe q. (9) Applying (1) now to q, S tracks q and so knows q. (10) Hence closure is unavoidable: whenever one knows p and sees that p entails q, tracking is automatically transmitted to q. (11) But then if I know I have hands, I must know I am not a handless brain in a vat, since that skeptical scenario is a world in which “I have hands” is false. (12) Nozick denies that we know we are not brains in vats, yet his tracking apparatus seems to guarantee exactly that knowledge once hand-knowledge is granted. (13) So either tracking collapses into full closure and skepticism is defeated, or the reasoning from (5) to (7) conceals a mistake. I wish to preserve both tracking and the anti-skeptical modesty that closure can fail; if I must surrender one, I prefer to keep tracking intact. Where exactly does the argument fail—particularly in the move from (5) through (7)—and what does each available repair cost?
