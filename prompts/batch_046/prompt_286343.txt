Here are my gut calls on when a community's living memory should override the historical record and vice versa, and I can't see the pattern. There's this small coastal town that remembers refusing a colonial land deal in 1892, but the signed treaty is in a state archive; I think the record should win there, even if it unravels their resistance identity. Then there's a mining village that remembers a strike-breaking massacre in 1911 that census and coroner reports say was just a riot with two deaths; I still think the archive should correct them, because the solidarity built on a false casualty count feels wrong. But then there's a diaspora group that keeps alive a founder-hero named Velas who never shows up in any ship manifest or church register; I want them to keep telling the story, since the ethical model he provides is real to them even if the person isn't. There's also an indigenous community whose oral origin places them coming from the inland mountains, but genetic and soil-core data point to a coastal origin; I lean toward letting their memory stand, because the mountain narrative is tied to ceremonial obligations that don't make sense with the coastal version. And there's a factory town where everyone remembers the 1983 closure as a deliberate betrayal by the owner, though bankruptcy filings show it was just market collapse; I think the betrayal narrative should stay, because it captures a structural power relation the paperwork hides. So I have record winning for the treaty and the massacre, but memory winning for the hero, the origin, and the closure. What's the principle? Is it about whether the error serves solidarity vs distortion? Or about whether the correction destroys something irreplaceable? I'm especially stuck on this language case: a community remembers that their dialect was formally banned in schools in 1954, but attendence logs and board minutes only show it was 'discouraged' with no penalty. The revival movement hinges on the ban narrative. I go back and forth -- part of me thinks 'discouraged' is close enough to let the memory stand, part of me thinks precision about state violence matters. Can you lay out a principle or a small set of candidates that gets close to all of these, say exactly which ones it has to drop, and tell me what it predicts for the language-ban case?
