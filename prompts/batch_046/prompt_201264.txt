Predict Isaac Newton's response to a contemporary theological-physical argument that merges the Everettian multiverse with modal realism: the claim that every quantum branch is ontologically real, that God actualizes all possibilities simultaneously, and that this eliminates the need for direct divine intervention in a single world-history because prophecy can be satisfied across branches rather than in one linear timeline. In framing his reply, draw specifically on Newton's anti-Trinitarian conception of the Father as the sole supreme governor, his conviction that God actively corrects irregularities in planetary motion rather than leaving nature to run on fixed laws alone, his interpretation of biblical prophecy as describing a single actual future, and his rejection of a diffuse "world soul" or immanent vital principle in nature. Would Newton regard this multiverse theology as preserving or violating the unity of divine will? Would he see branching histories as compatible with prophecy, or as undermining the singular historical trajectory he believed scripture revealed? And would he classify the proposed omnipotence-through-branching as a sophisticated form of the very pantheistic or Stoic errors he warned against in his theological writings?
