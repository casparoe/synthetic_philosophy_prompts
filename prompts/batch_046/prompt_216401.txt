Me and my roommate got into this about Friedman's F-twist and now I can't find where the argument breaks. (1) Friedman says unrealistic assumptions are okay if the model predicts well. (2) So the truth of a model's core assumptions doesn't matter; only predictive accuracy does. (3) Take two climate models: one assumes the earth is flat but corrects its equations to match observed temperatures, another uses standard spherical geometery. Both predict the same warming curves. (4) By (1)-(2), we should accept both models as equally good science. (5) But accepting both means accepting that the earth is flat and not flat, which is incoherent. (6) So the F-twist must be wrong, or at least can't apply across the board. I'd really like to keep (1) — unrealistic assumptions do get used successfully — and (3) seems like a real possibility. Which step do I reject, and what does it cost me? Is there a hidden slide between predictive acceptance and ontological commitment?
