Having recently begun volunteering at a wildlife rehabilitation center that relies on automated behavioral-monitoring systems to regulate feeding and assess stress indicators in recovering mammals, I have become unsettled by the possibility that our technological framework treats animal behavior as purely mechanistic input-output relations. This personal concern has led me to reconsider the philosophical stakes of Descartes' position on animal minds. Please describe the most important implications of Descartes' denial of rational souls and conscious experience to non-human creatures—his mechanistic ontology of the bête-machine—for how we ought to ethically evaluate and design automated care systems that interpret animal suffering. In particular, address whether adopting a strictly Cartesian framework undermines moral obligations rooted in recognizing subjective experience, or whether such obligations can be sustained independently of metaphysical commitments about animal consciousness.
