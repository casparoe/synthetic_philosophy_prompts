I am preparing an interview for my philosophy podcast on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism—the classic challenge that pleasures as diverse as tasting wine, solving a proof, and feeling relief seem to share no common phenomenal quality. Next week’s guest is a philosopher—unnamed in our correspondence—who defends a functional-computational variant of hedonism: she denies that pleasures are unified by any intrinsic “feel,” and instead argues they share a formal role as states that are non-instrumentally motivating and that update a single practical “value register” in the agent’s reasoning, regardless of sensory modality. She claims this rescues hedonism from objections running back to Sidgwick and developed in recent analytic literature, and further that the problem of higher-order pleasures—enjoying one’s enjoyment—dissolves because second-order states can operate on the same register without duplicating a felt quality. I want the interview to be technically precise but accessible to an informed general audience.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that do not ask her merely to restate the position. I need at least one question pressing whether her functional account collapses hedonism into a form of desire-satisfaction theory or instrumentalism about value; at least one confronting the phenomenological intuition that there is still a common “warm glow” across pleasures; at least one probing the possibility of “alien pleasures”—states that update her value register but feel nothing like human enjoyment; and at least one asking whether her dissolution of higher-order pleasure undermines the traditional hedonist explanation of why we value reflecting on our lives. For each of these four areas, include a direct follow-up keyed to her two most likely replies: first, that the value register is autonomous from the content of desire; second, that phenomenology is a confabulated narrative about functional updates rather than evidence of a shared quality. The remaining questions should build progressive pressure, moving from internal coherence to comparative advantage over rival views, and should anticipate the standard moves in the literature without letting her retreat to generic slogans.

Begin your response with the interview questions themselves, presented cleanly with no preface, commentary, or framing text; after the questions, provide brief reasoning explaining why these particular pressure points were chosen and how they build on one another. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for sources in either the questions or the reasoning.
