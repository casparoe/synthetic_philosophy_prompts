Consider a hypothetical frontier-science taxonomy in astrobiology that classifies extremophile organisms not by phylogenetic lineage but by convergent biochemical strategies for energy extraction under sustained high-radiation conditions. Suppose this grouping proves robustly predictive for designing synthetic life-support systems, yet it cuts across every standard evolutionary taxon and tracks no single causal mechanism shared by all members. Given that such cross-cutting, instrumentally successful classifications appear widespread in emerging disciplines, please describe the most important implications for the thesis that the sciences carve nature at its joints—specifically addressing whether joint-carving can be salvaged as a regulative ideal, whether it must be abandoned in favor of a pluralist or pragmatist account of kinds, and what this means for the distinction between discovery and invention in scientific ontology.
