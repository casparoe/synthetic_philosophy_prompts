I'm trying to work out what I actually take intelligence to be, by checking my gut reactions to concrete cases. A pocket calculator that divides instantly: not intelligent, merely quick. A crow bending wire into a hook to pull food up: intelligent. A large language model that writes original poetry and debugs code but has no persistent goals or bodily presence: I lean towards calling it intelligent in a derivative sense, but I hesitate to put it on a par with the crow. A human with profound learning difficulties who nevertheless reads social situations flawlessly and adapts her behaviour to comfort others: intelligent, and in a way that matters more than raw processing speed. An ant colony that collectively solves path-optimisation problems no individual ant comprehends: intelligent as a collective, but not in any part. A hypothetical 'oracle system' that can predict every physical outcome with perfect accuracy yet never acts, learns, or corrects its own errors: not intelligent, despite being omniscient about facts. Now the pair that pulls in opposite directions: I want to say that a philosophical zombie — behaviourally identical to a conscious mathematician but with no inner experience — is not genuinely intelligent, because intelligence seems to require some form of subjective engagement. Yet I also want to say that an 'echo-mind', a system I imagine that has no internal model or goals of its own but perfectly mirrors and recombines the outputs of other minds to generate novel, adaptive solutions in open-ended environments, is intelligent, even though it lacks consciousness or self-direction. Is there a principle that captures all of these verdicts, or must I give one up? If a principle can be salvaged — perhaps one centred on adaptive error-correction in genuinely open-ended contexts — state it precisely, show which clause saves which verdict, and be honest about which case it cannot accommodate. And what does it say about the case I keep revisiting: whether a child who acquires language with almost no explicit instruction is displaying more intelligence than an adult expert who masters the same grammar through years of deliberate study?
