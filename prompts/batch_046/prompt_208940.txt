You are grading an exam for an advanced philosophy of mind course. The question posed to students was: "Explain the Lucas-Penrose argument that human mathematical reasoning cannot be fully captured by a single formal system F. Distinguish Lucas's original formulation—particularly his use of Gödel's theorems—from Penrose's later variant, and evaluate whether the argument survives if the mechanist rejects one fixed system in favor of an inter-translatable family of formal systems whose axioms are updated through empirical feedback loops rather than pure deduction. Also address the fallibility objection."

Below is a student's answer. Please assign a letter grade (A through F) and provide point-by-point comments that justify the assessment. Identify what is accurate and well-explained, and flag any subtle errors—such as conflated theorems, misattributed views, or steps that do not strictly follow—without rewriting the answer. Adopt the neutral tone of an instructor or TA; do not indicate whether you personally accept the argument's conclusion.

Student answer:
"Lucas argued that for any consistent formal system F with arithmetic, Gödel's second incompleteness theorem produces a sentence G_F stating its own unprovability in F. Since F cannot prove its own consistency, it cannot derive G_F, but a human mathematician can recognize F's consistency and therefore see that G_F is true, demonstrating that human reasoning transcends F. Penrose later abandoned this consistency-based approach and replaced it with a direct argument from the halting problem: if human reasoning were algorithmic, one could construct a Turing machine that replicates the human's judgments and then present it with its own Gödel number, generating a contradiction. Hence mechanism is impossible. Regarding the family-of-systems reply, if the family is recursively enumerable then Gödel's result applies to their union; moreover, any empirical update rule is part of the computable architecture, so the overall framework remains formal and Gödelizable. The fallibility objection is irrelevant because Lucas requires only that humans can sometimes recognize consistency, not that they are infallible. Therefore the argument withstands these modifications."
