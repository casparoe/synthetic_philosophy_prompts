I am preparing a framework for evaluating non-standard proposals in the epistemology of other minds, and before I assess any particular theory I need the full specification of adequacy conditions. Write the desiderata that any account of how we justify attributions of mental states to entities other than ourselves must satisfy if it is to count as genuinely solving the problem rather than dissolving it by redefining terms. The list should cover: (i) the concrete cases it must handle correctly—including not only standard human interactions but also misattribution and correction of mental states, the distinction between simulated and genuine affect in artificial or synthetic agents, and collective or distributed cognitive systems where mental-state ascription may apply to a group rather than to an individual substrate; (ii) the conceptual distinctions it must preserve—particularly between epistemic justification for attributing a mental state and causal or perceptual access to the underlying realization, and between knowing that a subject is in a given state and knowing what that state is phenomenally like for that subject; (iii) the constraints from adjacent fields it cannot violate, such as the absence of direct perceptual access to others' neural states, the predictive and normative dimensions of mental-state attribution revealed by cognitive science, and the requirement that the account not collapse into a private-language framework. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or theoretical pressure that makes it non-negotiable, and indicate which ones are genuinely universal versus which reflect the prejudices of particular camps (for example, those demanding phenomenological transparency versus those satisfied with functional equivalence). Then tell me which single requirement does the most damage to the traditional trio—behaviorist, analogical-inferential, and direct-perception accounts—and briefly why. If you see a joint unsatisfiability among any subset of these requirements given standard commitments in philosophy of mind, mark it explicitly; that tension is part of what I need mapped. I want the weight on the criteria and their motivating tensions, not on a scorecard of named historical theories.
