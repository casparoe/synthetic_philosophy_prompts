I hold the view associated with Moritz Schlick that the meaning of life is not a matter of achieving a final purpose or telos, but resides in activities pursued for their own sake — what can be characterized as structurally ludic engagement. On this account, labor that is purely instrumental is existentially empty, whereas play, artistic creation, or contemplative practice carry meaning precisely because they are not subordinated to further ends. I want to know what would genuinely count against this position, not merely what sounds counterintuitive. Specifically: could there be a form of deeply instrumental work that is experienced as intrinsically meaningful without being reconceived as disguised play? Would a scenario in which automation eliminates all instrumental necessity, leaving only recreational activity, show that meaning requires a dialectical tension with constraint rather than pure ludic freedom? Which formal conditions — non-subordination to further goals, absence of external compulsion, or some other structural feature — are genuinely essential, and which could be relaxed without collapsing the view? Rank these potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say whether the position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability by redescribing any apparently meaningful labor as hidden play. I am particularly interested in whether the view can accommodate the intuition that some people find profound significance in precisely the kind of goal-directed, world-transforming effort that Schlick's framework seems to dismiss as empty.
