For a graduate seminar in moral philosophy, specify the complete set of desiderata that any contractualist resolution of the aggregation problem must satisfy if it is to remain within T. M. Scanlon's reasonableness-based framework without collapsing into a form of utilitarianism—particularly for large-scale collective harm scenarios (e.g., climate-induced displacement or structured triage under scarcity) in which individual complaints appear numerically overwhelmed. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or theoretical commitment (for example: the separateness of persons, the non-aggregability of particular burdens, the distinction between pairwise and group-level justification, ex ante versus ex post evaluation of risk, and fidelity to Scanlon's contractualist formula). Identify which of these are genuinely non-negotiable within contractualism and which are instead preferences of consequentialist or hard-deontological critics. Then determine whether any subset—especially the joint demand for pairwise reasonable rejectability and threshold-sensitive aggregation—is unsatisfiable given the internal logic of reasonable rejection, and if so, explain what that impossibility reveals about the boundaries of the framework. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citation recommendations; the deliverable is the specification and defense of the criteria themselves.
