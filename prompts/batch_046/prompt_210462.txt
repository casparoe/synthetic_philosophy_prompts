If the task of ontology identification is to fix what exists by requiring that every genuine ontological difference correspond to a difference within the informational closure of an embedded observer, then any region that is causally and informationally isolated—such as the interior of a black hole or an unobservable cosmological epoch—must be assigned either no ontology or an ontology identical to its absence. This treats metaphysical commitment as coextensive with epistemic reach. But is the mistake here in assuming that ontology identification is governed by informational closure at all, or is there a deeper tension in allowing an ontology to contain elements that are, by their nature, inaccessible to any identification procedure?
