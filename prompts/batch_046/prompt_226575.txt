I am a third-year undergraduate currently enrolled in a seminar on contemporary epistemology, and I am preparing an interview for our department’s student philosophy podcast. Our guest is a philosopher—let us call her Dr. Elena Voss—who has recently developed a view she terms “source-separated bootstrapping.” According to her account, circular reasoning is not intrinsically vicious; rather, it can be epistemically productive when the premises and the conclusion draw on causally or informationally distinct evidential pathways, even if both depend on the same underlying cognitive faculty. She applies this framework to the classic “easy knowledge” problem—where a subject seems to derive knowledge that a table is red from knowledge of its appearance together with knowledge that the lighting is not deceptive—by arguing that the appearance-belief and the exclusion-belief both stem from a single perceptual event, so the loop is “single-source” and therefore fails to transmit justification. By contrast, she allows that if the exclusion-belief were supported by an independent testimonial or instrumental check, the circularity might be benign and knowledge could indeed be bootstrapped. I want eight to ten interview questions that do not merely ask her to restate this framework. Specifically, I need at least one question about how she individuates evidential pathways without collapsing into the mental-state taxonomy she explicitly rejects; one about the chicken-identification scenario, in which a subject first classifies a bird as a chicken and then uses general background knowledge about chicken appearance to reinforce that very identification, and whether her single-source diagnosis applies there; and one about whether her view makes bootstrapping available only to agents who possess explicit meta-cognitive access to their own source-structure. For each of these, please include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—particularly if she appeals to subpersonal information-tracking, to pragmatic encroachment on knowledge, or to a distinction between personal and subpersonal levels of justification. Finally, I would like a closing question that asks what kind of empirical finding or carefully constructed thought experiment could genuinely undermine her account, given that she has a tendency to absorb apparent counterexamples by re-describing the underlying source-structure rather than revising her theory. The tone should suit an advanced undergraduate audience that has read the basic literature on bootstrapping and easy knowledge but is not assuming specialist familiarity with her particular framework.
