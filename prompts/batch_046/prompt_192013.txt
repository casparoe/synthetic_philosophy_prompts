Write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines followed by a three-line envoi—on the omnipotence paradox, but set aside the familiar stone and frame the problem modally: an agent is defined as able to actualize any logically possible state of affairs, and the paradoxical task is for that agent to actualize a state in which the agent itself permanently lacks all power to actualize anything. Do not resolve the tension by dismissing the task as logically impossible or by redefining omnipotence into harmlessness; let the poem’s form carry the philosophical load. Use these six end-words—power, task, world, absence, command, limit—and rotate them through the strict sestina pattern (1-2-3-4-5-6, then 6-1-5-2-4-3, and so on). Map the stanzas to logical moves: the first stanza establishes omnipotence as the unrestricted actualization of worlds; the second introduces the self-annulling task; the third shows the contradiction that arises if the agent succeeds (the world now contains the agent’s absence from power); the fourth shows that failure also seems to breach omnipotence; the fifth examines the retreat to a restricted domain of permissible tasks; the sixth returns to the opening terms transformed, now entangled. In the envoi, compress all six end-words into a single image that holds the loop open—neither vindicating omnipotence nor dissolving it, but displaying the recursive trap itself. The poem should be precise enough that someone who knows the modal formulation recognizes each move, and compelling enough that a reader unfamiliar with the paradox feels the structure tighten. Keep the tone serious and unsettled; do not sermonize about divine perfection, do not shrug into mystery, and do not close the circle.
