I recently joined the governance board of an intentional community that is explicitly trying to design a domain-specific normative culture: tight, enforceable rules around ecological footprint and financial transparency, but loose, informal norms around personal expression and work schedules. I am tasked with drafting the internal guideline for how we decide, proposal by proposal, whether a new rule belongs in the tight column or the loose column, and whether adopting it risks triggering what I have started calling a tightness spiral—where one formal rule generates institutional pressure for others. I need this turned into an actual decision procedure my fellow board members can run without specialized training: a sequence of ordered tests, with explicit pass/fail conditions, clear stop points where the procedure returns do not regulate, stop points where it returns regulate but include a sunset clause and review trigger, and honest flag points where the choice is irreducibly a value judgment rather than a factual determination. The procedure should be grounded in the conceptual distinction between tightness functioning as a threat-response mechanism and tightness functioning as a pure coordination device, and it should account for the risk that adding rules in one domain will bleed into adjacent domains. Apply the procedure to three concrete proposals: a mandatory composting audit with monetary penalties for non-compliance; a suggested, non-binding schedule for shared tool maintenance; and a binding arbitration clause that must be invoked before any interpersonal conflict can be escalated to the board. Please write the answer in American English.
