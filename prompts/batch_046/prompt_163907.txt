I'm preparing my position for a seminar on Stoicism's modern revival and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is where I stand: (1) The dichotomy of control should be read as a normative filter on first-order practical reasoning—direct concern only toward what is genuinely within our power (prohairesis), with equanimity toward all external outcomes. (2) The contemporary revival is philosophically sound precisely because it jettisons ancient Stoic cosmology—universal determinism, divine logos, and cosmic sympathy—retaining only the ethical psychology. (3) Nevertheless, I remain a compatibilist about free will: our choices are genuinely ours in a manner that grounds virtue and responsibility, even if causally embedded. (4) Applied consistently, the dichotomy entails that in social or political activism we ought to evaluate our conduct solely by the justice of our intentions, not by collective outcomes, since the latter depend on innumerable external variables. (5) At the same time, I hold that moral responsibility extends to the foreseeable but not certain effects of our actions—including emotional or interpretive harms mediated by others—because agency generates a moral ecology that spills beyond the boundary of prohairesis. (6) Finally, I take modern CBT's cognitive-restructuring techniques to be essentially secularized Stoic practices, effective precisely because they maintain the metaphysical neutrality of the dichotomy.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision for each tension? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. For each tension, specify what would have to give and what that costs. Then tell me which single revision—giving up one commitment or modifying its scope—would clean up the most inconsistencies with the least overall damage to the view.
