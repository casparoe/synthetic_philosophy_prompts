I spent much of last evening in a prolonged argument with a close friend about whether evidential cooperation in large worlds represents a genuine rational commitment or a seductive symmetry fallacy, and I remain genuinely unsettled. Rather than resolve our disagreement prematurely, I have decided to test my own neutrality by grading an exam response on this exact topic. The exam question posed to the student was this.

Question: In a one-shot prisoner's dilemma played within a universe large enough to contain near-duplicate agents facing structurally identical decision problems, explain how evidential decision theory can justify cooperation in the absence of direct causal influence. Then specify the precise condition—or conditions—under which this justification dissolves, carefully distinguishing between a breakdown in statistical correlation and a breakdown in shared causal origin.

The student submitted the following answer.

Answer: The agent should cooperate because selecting cooperation does not merely predict but actively activates the same decision algorithm in all her duplicates, producing cooperation in them through a non-local causal influence mediated by shared programmatic structure. This effect requires a world large enough to contain copies. The reasoning collapses if the universe is too small to host duplicates, and it also collapses if the duplicates are not physically identical to the agent, since without physical identity there is no transmission mechanism linking her choice to theirs.

Please assign this answer a specific grade (on a conventional letter or percentage scale) and provide detailed, point-by-point comments that justify it. Identify precisely where the reasoning is sound, where it illicitly substitutes causal influence for evidential correlation, where it mischaracterizes the dissolution conditions—particularly regarding physical identity versus shared causal origin—and whether it adequately addresses both correlation failure and shared-origin failure as required. Assess only the logical and conceptual accuracy of the argument; do not indicate whether you personally endorse evidential cooperation in large worlds.
