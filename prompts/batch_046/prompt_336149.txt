Right, I'm trying to work out what I actually think about internal versus external reasons by going off my gut on cases, not by reciting Williams. Here are my verdicts:

1. Dave smokes, enjoys it, and has zero desire to quit. Even though it's slowly killing him, I don't think he has a reason to stop. The health fact alone doesn't reach him.

2. Sarah has never liked classical music and actively avoids concerts. Someone tells her she'd adore Wagner if she just tried Parsifal properly. I think she currently lacks a reason to go, even if she would love it afterwards.

3. But here's a twist: a woman takes a drug that suppresses her desire to be honest, and now she doesn't want to return a lost wallet. I still think she has a reason to return it, and that reason doesn't dissapear just because her current motivation is missing. So I'm not a pure internalist?

4. I promised my neighbour I'd water her plants while she's away, but now I'm shattered and really can't be bothered. I feel I still have a reason to do it, and it's not just "I'd feel guilty" — it's tied to the fact I said I would.

5. This one's hard: a climber reaches a summit for fun and learns there's a hidden local rule that anyone who gets up there must donate £50 to a charity he despises. He never knew about the rule beforehand. Once he finds out after reaching the top, does he have a reason to pay? Part of me says no, because he never endorsed the rule. Another part says yes, because he triggered it voluntarily.

6. Lastly, my kid asks if monsters are real. I tell her they aren't. But imagine a philosopher argues I have an "external reason" to tell her they are real because it might make her more cautious in dark places. I think that's completely wrong — there's no reason for me to lie just because some abstract good is served.

So is there one principle that captures all of these, or do I have to throw one out? And what does it predict for a case I haven't mentioned: someone is in a deep coma with no chance of waking, but there's a machine that could keep the body going for decades at enormous cost to the family. She once signed a paper saying do whatever saves money, but she never really took it seriously. Does she have a reason — now, in that state — for the family to switch the machine off?
