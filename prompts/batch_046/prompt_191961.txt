Write a deadpan parody of a corporate compliance memo—approximately 500 words, formatted as numbered sections with sub-clauses—from the "Office of Ontological Integrity" at a fictional synthetic-biology firm, announcing a new internal standard for certifying the "proper function" of engineered tissues and implantable organs. Do not name philosophers, cite texts, or include a bibliography; instead, embed the conceptual distinctions in invented terminology ("ancestral-selection baseline," "contribution-weighted role," "null-heritage waiver"). Keep the irony sustained and deadpan—absurdity should arise from following the logic of the debate, not from inventing a fake disagreement. End with a single-paragraph "Executive Summary for Non-Technical Stakeholders" that accidentally collapses the distinction by defining proper function as whatever the organ is currently doing if it was also selected for doing it, without noticing the circularity. Hard limit: 550 words maximum.
