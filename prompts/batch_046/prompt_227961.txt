Next week I lead a fifty-minute discussion section for second-year philosophy students who have read Descartes' Meditations I–II but keep treating the cogito as a syllogism with a suppressed major premise. I want them to see exactly what it proves—whether the existence of a substantial self, or only the occurrence of thought—and why the inference does not depend on a general principle that whatever thinks exists. Plan the session, not the explanation itself: the dependency order in which the pieces must be introduced (what must be in place before the doubt-filter can do its work), the one concrete scenario or mental exercise that should carry the idea and what it must include and exclude, the misconceptions they will bring or form—especially the syllogism reading and the slide from "I exist" to "I am a thinking substance"—and the precise point in the sequence where each should be headed off. Include one closing question that reveals whether they have grasped the logical dependency rather than memorized the slogan. State explicitly what standard commentary—say, the Cartesian circle, the wax argument, or the real distinction—should be omitted at this level and why. Organize your response as: carrying example; dependency order; misconception map with defusion points; check question; omission with justification. Keep it to about 350 words.
