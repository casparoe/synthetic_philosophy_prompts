I am drafting a philosophical dialogue for a novel set in an alternate 1970s, where a cybernetic planning ministry attempts to realize Lange’s trial-and-error socialism through early computer networks. To ground a character’s skepticism, I composed the following argument, but its conclusion undermines the very distinction the novel depends on, and I cannot locate the precise flaw. (1) Mises demonstrated that rational economic calculation requires actual market prices for capital goods; without exchange, planners have no rational basis for allocating resources. (2) Lange conceded this but argued that a central board could iteratively adjust shadow prices until supply matched demand. (3) Hayek replied that the deeper problem is dispersed, tacit knowledge—local conditions known only to actors on the spot—which no board can ever fully gather or formalize. (4) If this tacit-knowledge barrier applies to any non-market coordination, then it applies not only to state planning but to any large organization that replaces price signals with hierarchical command and data aggregation. (5) Modern multinational corporations—indeed, any complex firm—do exactly this: they coordinate dispersed production through internal algorithms and authority rather than through internal markets. (6) Therefore, if Hayek’s critique is correct, such firms should be systematically incapable of rational coordination and should collapse or stagnate. (7) They clearly do not; they function and adapt. (8) So either Hayek’s critique is false, or it applies selectively in a way that saves firms but not states—a distinction for which the argument gives no basis. Where does this overreach? I am committed to preserving (3) if possible, and I would prefer to keep (1) intact, but I need a definite diagnosis: identify the precise step that fails, explain why it seemed valid, and tell me explicitly what I must surrender—whether the universalization in (4), the empirical claim in (6), or the symmetry in (8)—if I hold onto the premises I value. Do not lay out options neutrally; take a stand and defend it.
