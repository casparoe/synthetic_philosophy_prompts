I'm applying for a six-month conceptual research fellowship and need a polished one-page proposal. The domain is AI governance, specifically the interaction between the alignment tax and the competitiveness constraint. The standard framing treats the alignment tax as a uniform performance cost and assumes that competitive markets will always erode it, producing a race to the bottom. I want to challenge that monolithic picture by asking whether different kinds of alignment investment are differently exposed to competitive pressure.

The precise question: Can alignment interventions be taxonomized by whether they are externally observable and imitable by rival firms—think of public-facing capability adjustments versus internal organizational governance protocols—and does this distinction change whether the competitiveness constraint actually applies? More specifically, is there a category of safety work that functions as a non-imitable internal resource, meaning it can be sustained even under intense market competition rather than being competed away?

Why existing answers leave this open: Discussions of the alignment tax tend to aggregate across techniques—referencing scalable oversight, constitutional methods, or interpretability tools—without asking whether each is visible to competitors and copyable. Meanwhile, economic reasoning about competitive dynamics typically treats innovations as either fully excludable (patents) or fully non-excludable (spillovers), leaving organizational culture and internal decision procedures in an undertheorized gray zone. No existing framework maps that economic distinction onto the specific landscape of AI safety investments.

The approach: I will construct a conceptual taxonomy of alignment strategies based on observability and imitability, then apply reasoning about durable competitive advantage—specifically, the idea that resources which are valuable but difficult for rivals to replicate can survive price competition—to each category. Rather than treating safety as a pure cost, I will examine whether certain internal alignment structures might be complementary to long-term firm survival, which would revise the standard prediction that all safety measures are competitively unsustainable. The deliverable will be a framework that funders or policymakers can use to identify which alignment investments are vulnerable to competitive erosion and which are structurally protected.

Success would be a robust conceptual distinction that survives objections—for example, that all internal practices eventually leak, or that the competitive advantage of organizational culture is negligible at AI development scale. Failure would be demonstrating that the distinction exists in principle but cannot be operationalized, or that every meaningful alignment intervention ultimately falls into the observable, imitable category.

Scope and limits: This is a philosophical and conceptual project, not an empirical study of firms. I will not gather proprietary industry data. The argument proceeds through conceptual analysis and analogies to existing work on organizational capability and competitive strategy. The timeline allows for refining the taxonomy, testing it against standard counterexamples, and writing the framework document.

Please separate what is settled—such as that competitive pressure exists in frontier AI development, and that some alignment techniques reduce near-term capabilities—from what is genuinely contested, including whether organizational culture can serve as a durable competitive moat in this sector, and whether the alignment tax is best modeled as a uniform scalar cost. Do not present a contested claim from economics or AI strategy as if it were the consensus view among specialists in either field.
