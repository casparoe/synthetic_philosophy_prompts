I am a doctoral student in jurisprudence working on the boundary between the harm principle and legal moralism, and I've found myself stuck on a problem that the classic literature does not seem to address directly. In my seminar on H.L.A. Hart and Lord Devlin, we spent considerable time on the Wolfenden Report context and the question of whether private immoral acts between consenting adults should be criminalized. But lately, in conversations with colleagues working on technology regulation, I've been wondering how the Hart-Devlin framework applies to synthetic media—specifically, the production and dissemination of AI-generated intimate imagery that depicts identifiable individuals without their consent, or entirely synthetic pornographic representations that involve no real-world subjects at all.

Write an essay that takes this application seriously. First, reconstruct the core of the Hart-Devlin disagreement: Hart's insistence that law should only prevent harm to others, and Devlin's counter that society has a legitimate interest in preserving its shared moral fabric even in the absence of direct harm. Then apply this structure to the synthetic-media case. On the Hartian side, evaluate whether the absence of a tangible victim (in the fully synthetic case) or the presence of a non-physical harm (in the deepfake case) undermines the moralist argument. On the Devlinian side, assess whether the public's moral revulsion at synthetic representations can ground legal prohibition in the same way that revulsion at homosexuality was claimed to do, or whether the analogy fails because representation is not action. Reach and defend a clear conclusion: does the Hart-Devlin debate, properly understood, license moralistic regulation of synthetic intimate imagery, or does it collapse once we recognize that the morality at stake is not anchored in interpersonal harm? Your answer should be disciplined about the mapping—do not force the framework to speak where it is silent—and should say explicitly where the debate's original terms need revision rather than simple extension.
