I am a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I have drafted an outline for a short essay comparing Mencius and Xunzi on human nature through a conceptual framework I understand well: software system architecture. Before I expand it into a full draft, I would appreciate critical comments on whether the framing is philosophically coherent and whether it illuminates the classical positions rather than distorting them.

The working title is Default Configurations and Legacy Patches. The central claim is that Mencius treats human nature like a kernel compiled with four minimal but functional moral tendencies—compassion, shame, deference, and discernment—often translated as the sprouts. In this reading, moral development is not the installation of new software but the protection of the build environment so that the existing tendencies compile correctly; moral failure is a runtime error caused by corrupted input or poor environment, not by flawed source code. By contrast, Xunzi treats human nature as a legacy codebase whose default execution flow is driven by self-interest and resource competition. Left ungoverned, the system produces social failure. Ritual and righteousness are therefore not internal updates to the kernel but an external governance layer—similar to a containerization or policy framework—that redirects execution flow without altering the underlying binary.

The outline has three sections. The first introduces the Mencian kernel analogy and asks whether describing the sprouts as functional modules wrongly implies they are already sufficient for moral behavior, when in fact Mencius insists they require active cultivation and environmental support. The second develops the Xunzian legacy-system analogy and examines whether calling ritual a governance layer captures Xunzi's idea that humans can study, practice, and genuinely internalize ritual, or whether the metaphor wrongly suggests ritual remains purely external enforcement. The third compares the metaphysical commitments: Mencius's position seems to carry a teleological assumption that the kernel was authored with intentional design, whereas Xunzi's account appears purely descriptive and materialist, closer to observing legacy behavior patterns without positing a designer.

I am specifically concerned about four issues. First, does framing Mencius's sprouts as modules misrepresent his view that they are merely incipient tendencies requiring sustained cultivation, rather than functioning subroutines? Second, does the governance-layer metaphor for Xunzi obscure his claim that through disciplined practice the framework becomes genuinely internalized, not merely imposed? Third, is it accurate to say that Mencius's view requires nature to be designed for goodness while Xunzi's does not, or is that distinction too sharp? Fourth, the outline currently avoids any comparison to modern evolutionary psychology or behavioral economics; should I include such a comparison, or would it risk anachronism that weakens the conceptual argument?

Please do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations. I am writing from my own synthesis of the texts and want feedback on the dependency structure of the argument, the accuracy of the analogies, and which misconception a technically trained but philosophically novice audience is most likely to form—particularly whether they will confuse Xunzi's view with simple behaviorism or confuse Mencius's view with innate moral completeness. If any part of the outline depends on a specific textual attribution or date that I have likely misremembered, flag it in general terms without supplying a corrected citation.
