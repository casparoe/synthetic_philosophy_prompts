Here is my philosophical profile on the nature of emotion. I want an audit of whether these commitments hang together as a unified view.

My commitments:
1. Emotions are essentially evaluative judgments (or appraisal patterns) about one's situation relative to one's concerns — a broadly cognitivist stance.
2. A distinctive phenomenal feel is necessary for emotion; without it, a cool evaluative judgment is not an emotional state.
3. Emotions are rationally assessable: they can be fitting or unfitting to their objects, and this normative evaluation applies even to involuntary emotional responses.
4. Emotional consciousness has a transparency feature: we experience our emotions as direct presentations of value in the world, not as internal mental representations. This transparency is compatible with the judgment view only if we understand emotional judgment as a non-inferential, embodied uptake rather than an explicit propositional attitude.
5. Non-human animals and pre-linguistic human infants experience genuine emotions, not merely reflexive arousal — which suggests that full conceptual appraisal is not strictly necessary for emotion.
6. I deny that emotions are perceptions in the strict sensory sense (like vision or touch), because sensory perception is not governed by norms of rational appropriateness in the way that emotional judgments are.
7. I endorse a dispositional-embodiment view: bodily changes partially constitute emotional judgment by structuring practical orientation, but the bodily profile alone is insufficient without an evaluative framing.
8. Emotional education works by jointly reshaping bodily dispositions and conceptual frameworks, so that an identical physiological pattern can come to represent different values across developmental or cultural contexts.
