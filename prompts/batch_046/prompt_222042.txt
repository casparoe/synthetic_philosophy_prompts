I'm giving a seminar defense next week for a paper arguing that Carnap's principle of tolerance — the idea that there's no uniquely correct logic or language framework — actually extends into political philosophy and undermines the usual "Carnap as social engineer" reading. I claim it pushes him toward a kind of institutional pluralism about planning, where no single economic framework gets privileged as "scientifically correct," which makes him closer to an anti-paternalist liberal than to a central planner. The audience is a philosophy of science seminar with a couple political theorists; most of them have read Neurath and Carnap's planning writings and think I'm downplaying the technicratic side. I need a Q&A prep sheet, not a bibliography.

Structure the reply like this:

- Audience profile and smell-test (about 3 sentences): who they are, their commitments, what they'll attack first.
- Ordered objections (5 or 6): each phrased in their voice, with (a) the real force, (b) what I should concede up front, (c) a ~2-min reply, (d) where that reply is weakest.
- Opening concession (1 short paragraph): what I should admit so I'm not playing defense the whole time.
- The one reply that has to land (2-3 sentences): the pivot that saves the thesis if it works.
- The dreaded question (the one I'm hoping nobody asks): phrase it, say why it's dangerous, and give the honest non-dodge answer.

Keep it tight and strategic — no citation requests, no reading lists, just the layout I can memorize before I go in. Definately include the objection about Neurath's unitary science program, since that's the one they'll use to say I'm splitting Carnap from his own circle.
