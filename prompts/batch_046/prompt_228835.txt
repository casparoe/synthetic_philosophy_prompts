Consider the institutional pattern visible in several liberal democracies that have recognized same-sex marriage: the law extends marital status to same-sex couples on formally gender-neutral terms, yet retains family-law mechanisms originally structured around heterosexual reproduction. Specifically, the marital presumption of parentage remains biologically anchored—the spouse of a birth mother who is male is presumed the father by virtue of the marriage—while the non-birth mother in a legally equivalent same-sex marriage typically must complete a separate second-parent adoption or judicial parentage order to obtain parallel legal status, even where donor conception was used and the biological asymmetry is identical. Concurrently, the state permits religious celebrants and, in some jurisdictions, civil registrars to decline participation in same-sex marriages on the grounds that the institution possesses an objective nature—frequently characterized as a conjugal or reproductive union—that they would misrepresent by solemnizing.

What philosophical account of marriage does this combination of practices presuppose, if any? Does the asymmetry in parentage rules imply that marriage is still understood, at the institutional level, as a reproductive union into which same-sex couples enter only analogically or exceptionally? Or can the difference be explained administratively—by the need to avoid false biological presumptions—without committing to any substantive view of marriage's purpose? Furthermore, does the coexistence of gender-neutral access with conscience exemptions grounded in an "objective" conjugal nature reveal an implicit institutional dualism: marriage treated simultaneously as an egalitarian contract available to any two adults and as a biologically ordered institution with a specific normative structure? If so, are these two conceptions consistent within a single legal framework, or does the practice depend on keeping them in separate compartments—access rules in one, parentage and exemption rules in another? Finally, distinguish what this arrangement strictly requires from what it merely accommodates: if the underlying view were replaced by a purely contractual, companionship-based, or egalitarian-partnership theory of marriage, which specific mechanisms—the adoption asymmetry, the biological presumption itself, the exemption structure—would become philosophically unjustifiable, and which could remain intact on purely administrative or liberty-based grounds?
