Write a dialogue between two friends, J and R, talking over coffee about Bernard Williams's idea that in some situations there's one thought too many. J thinks this only applies to moral dilemmas where you treat people as interchangable units in a utilitarian calculus — like saving your spouse because you calculated the greatest good, not out of love. R argues the same structure shows up in aesthetic commitment: a painter who abandons a canvas after realizing her passion was partly driven by market value, then picks it back up only after calculating it will impress a specific critic. J says that's different — in the aesthetic case she's just filtering out bad reasons, not adding an alien motivation. R pushes back that the corruption isn't in recognizing the market value, it's in letting a strategic calculation become the reason she continues. They should actually respond to each other's arguments, neither sounding like a strawman, and keep it casual with maybe a typo or two.
