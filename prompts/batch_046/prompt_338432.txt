My verdicts on when a mental state is actually conscious, not just processed, and I'm trying to see if there's a higher-order principle that fits or if I'm contradicting myself. If someone has blindsight and can navigate around obstacles without feeling like they see anything, I say there's zero visual consciousness there - no higher-order state, no experience. But then I think about a baby screaming from a burn, or an octopus flinching from heat - they probably don't have thoughts about their thoughts, yet I'm convinced they feel it consciously. Meanwhile if you built an AI that literally stores a representation of every computation it runs and can report "I am now processing red," I don't think that's conscious at all - it's just recursive bookkeeping. Then there's the flow-state painter who hasn't thought about herself in hours but is vividly experiencing color and texture - definatly conscious, even though there's no meta-cognitive monitoring happening. And in dreams, where the brain is generating scenes without external input and self-reflection is often broken, I still think the dreamer is having real conscious experiences. So my pattern seems to be: no consciousness without some integretion, but also consciousness can happen without explicit self-monitoring, and explicit self-monitoring without integration isn't enough. Is there a coherent higher-order account that saves all these? Or do I need to drop the idea that consciousness requires a representation of the mental state? Maybe I need something like "higher-order availability within a global workspace" but then I have to explain why the AI's recursive storage doesn't count as availability. What's the principle, and which of my verdicts does it make me abandon?
