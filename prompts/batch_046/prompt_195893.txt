I'm covering the decades-long stalemate in philosophy of medicine between naturalists, who treat health as statistically normal biological functioning, and normativists, who insist that labeling a condition a disease already embeds a judgment about harm or undesirable deviation. The dispute remains unresolved even over concrete cases — for instance, whether psychiatric disorders are diseases in the same sense as heart failure — and my working theory is that the impasse is not about values versus facts at all, but about a hidden conceptual split that neither side operationalizes: whether health is an intrinsic property of an isolated organism or a relational property of ecological fit between organism and environment. I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict: is this disagreement verbal, substantive but masked, or a sign that both sides answer different questions under one name? Lay out what each position actually says, identify whether a hidden empirical issue — evolutionary design, clinical taxonomy, or standards of evidence — is really driving the deadlock, and say honestly whether any distinction, formal result, or change of question could actually resolve it, or whether no progress should be expected and why.
