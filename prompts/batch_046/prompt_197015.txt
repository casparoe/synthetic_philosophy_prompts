people in pain philosphy keep using the car-alarm analogy for asymbolia: the sensory component is the siren, the affective/motiational part is the owner rushing out, and the asymbolic patient is like the owner who hears it but doesn't care or move. they use it to argue pain's badness lives entirely in the urgency, not in the raw feel.

audit it for me. give me the correspondance table - what plays siren, owner, car, theft, the shrug - then where it breaks. especially: if the owner never reacts, is it still functioning as an alarm? and does the analogy sneak in the idea that sensations are intrinsically neutral, which is half the debate?

here's the twist: some philosophers distinguish 'not caring about pain' from 'actively finding it interesting or neutral-positive.' the car alarm has no analog for that - it's either panic or indiference. does that gap matter for whether asymbolia proves pain isn't bad?

also, compare briefly to congenital insensitivity: are they mirror images or different failures entirely? and if the alarm analogy collapses, is there a better one - maybe taste, or a warning label?
