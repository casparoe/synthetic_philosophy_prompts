I was discussing quantum foundations with some friends from my study group after reading several threads on rationalist forums about the Everett interpretation, and we reached a disagreement that I have not been able to resolve to my satisfaction. Specifically, I have been trying to understand why Everettian mechanics requires a special derivation of the Born rule at all. If the universal wavefunction evolves deterministically according to the Schrödinger equation, and every outcome corresponding to a nonzero amplitude is realized in some physically real branch, then it seems objective chance should drop out entirely—there is no randomness, only branching. Yet the decision-theoretic arguments I have encountered claim that a rational agent should still assign credences proportional to |ψ|² when making bets about future observations.

What I do not see is why we are not permitted to treat all branches with perfect symmetry. If, in some meaningful sense, I will experience every branch—because there will be copies of me in each—then calculating my expected future welfare seems to require integrating over all branches with uniform weight. Weighting some branches more heavily than others, purely on the basis of their squared amplitude, looks like smuggling in an unjustified metaphysical bias: it treats amplitude as a kind of thickness or density of reality that the physics itself does not obviously privilege. Some respondents have suggested that this misses the point because self-locating uncertainty means I should care about which specific copy I will turn out to be, but that framing sounds to me like it presupposes a continuous personal identity or even a non-physical soul that Everettian mechanics explicitly rejects. So is my core error that I am treating personal identity across branches as too robust, or is there a deeper mistake in assuming that deterministic branching eliminates the need for probabilistic reasoning altogether—perhaps by conflating first-person epistemic uncertainty with third-person ontological multiplicity?
