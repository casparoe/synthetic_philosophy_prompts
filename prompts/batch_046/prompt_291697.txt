I am a doctoral student in moral philosophy working at the intersection of metaphysics of responsibility and penal theory, and I have come to defend a specific variant of retributivism—let me call it structural-proportionality retributivism. On this view, punishment is justified not by matching a scalar quantity of harm with a scalar quantity of suffering, but by establishing an isomorphic correspondence between the hierarchical structure of the offender’s culpable practical reasoning and the formal structure of the sanction imposed. Consequential considerations—deterrence, rehabilitation, incapacitation, or expressive social benefits—are not merely outweighed but categorically irrelevant to justification, though they may permissibly influence the selection of a sanction from among those that are structurally equivalent. I want to understand what would genuinely count against this position, beyond the familiar charge that retributivism is barbaric or that consequentialism offers a more parsimonious institutional explanation.

Specifically: which empirical findings in the psychology of punishment or the sociology of penal institutions would undermine the claim that justification is genuinely independent of consequential effects? Which conceptual arguments demonstrate that the notion of structural correspondence either collapses into a disguised form of consequentialist balancing or rests on an incoherent metaphysics of practical reason? Are there cases—perhaps involving collective wrongdoing, systemic injustice, or offenders whose practical reasoning is radically disordered—where the view either yields intuitively wrong results or risks unfalsifiability by retreating to ever more abstract descriptions of the culpability-structure? I am especially interested in whether a committed defender of this view can consistently concede any defeater without abandoning the core commitment, and whether the insulation of the view into unfalsifiability is itself a philosophical defect rather than a protective feature. Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguishing those that critics typically advance from those that a structural-proportionality retributivist should themselves recognize as damaging, and include an honest assessment of whether this position risks anything substantive or has been qualified into invulnerability.
