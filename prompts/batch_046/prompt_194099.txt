Then there's what I'm calling the Spot-Check model—where higher-order evidence only licenses local revision in the specific domains where you have direct reason to think the error is active, leaving other beliefs untouched. I'm trying to figure out which one actually handles epistemic akrasia better—like, if I genuinely believe my statistical reasoning is off but I still can't shake the conclusion I drew from it. Does the Echo-Chamber view just force me into total agnosticism? And does the Spot-Check view let me keep the belief in a way that's rational or just rationalized? Could you compare them specifically on how they treat the case where the higher-order evidence itself might be tainted—say I only think I'm biased because a rival lab told me so?
