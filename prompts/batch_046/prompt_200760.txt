I have been asked to set out my philosophical profile on scientific idealisation and modelling for a seminar audit, and I should like to know whether the commitments actually cohere. Here is the set:

1. I accept Milton Friedman’s F-twist: unrealistic assumptions in a scientific model are justified solely by their contribution to predictive accuracy.
2. I extend this to climate science and macroeconomics, holding that parameterisations in general circulation models and infinite-horizon growth assumptions are legitimate precisely because they are predictively indispensable, not despite being physically or behaviourally distorted.
3. I believe predictive success is necessary but not sufficient for model adequacy; structural transparency—knowing exactly how each unrealistic assumption feeds into the output—is also required.
4. I maintain that genuine explanatory understanding requires at least some model components to approximate real causal structures; robust instrumental success alone does not deliver understanding.
5. I hold that for a model to be scientifically respectable, there must exist a principled de-idealisation pathway—an explicit, stepwise procedure by which unrealistic assumptions can in principle be relaxed without destroying the model’s predictive structure.
6. I think that in climate modelling, certain parameterisations—such as sub-grid cloud-process representations—are conceptually irremovable at any tractable resolution, yet this does not diminish the model’s scientific legitimacy.
7. I am a scientific realist about the entities and processes described by mature climate models, despite accepting that every representational layer involves deliberate distortion.
8. I propose that models ought to be assessed against an ‘epistemic risk budget’ that weights predictive failure differently across domains, rather than by uniform global accuracy metrics.

Please audit these commitments pair by pair. For each pair in genuine tension, state how severe it is, identify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency, and say what that revision costs me elsewhere in the profile. For pairs that only appear to conflict, explain why the tension dissolves on inspection. Order the genuine tensions by severity. Finally, tell me which single revision—whether to one commitment or to the relations between them—would clean up the most tension overall, and advise whether, given the rest of my profile, that revision is one I ought actually to make.
