I am a high school student writing a research paper on how modern nations construct narratives about their own age, and I have noticed a specific pattern for which I cannot find a precise label. I would like to know whether it is an established concept in the literature.

Here are two concrete examples. In the nineteenth century, a newly unified state revived a medieval coronation ceremony, but altered the oath so that it pledged loyalty to a modern constitution rather than to feudal lords; within a few generations, school curricula presented this modified ceremony as ancient proof that constitutional rule was inherent to the national character. In another case, a government in the 1920s selected a folk costume from a single rural province, promoted it nationwide as the traditional dress of the entire population, and then used its image in late-twentieth-century tourism campaigns to claim that the nation had always possessed a unified culture.

What strikes me is not merely that traditions were invented, nor that citizens imagine their nation as a continuous community across time. Rather, there is a further step: the invented tradition is deployed as retroactive evidence that the present political order is the natural, timeless expression of an ancient people, and the fact of its own recent fabrication is systematically obscured in that very deployment. The tradition becomes a kind of self-concealing proof.

Is there an established scholarly term for this exact configuration—the strategic use of an invented tradition to naturalize the modern state while hiding the invention itself? If there is, how does it differ from the general idea of an invented tradition or from ordinary myth-making? If there is not, can you propose a precise definition, specify its boundary conditions, and explain how it relates to but remains distinct from related concepts such as historical revisionism and the social construction of national identity?

Finally, please indicate what kind of argument or evidence would lead you to change your view about whether this is a genuinely distinct phenomenon or merely a sub-case of something already well-described, and please point to where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
