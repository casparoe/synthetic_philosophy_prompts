The Honor Council addresses offenses against collective symbols or communal rituals—defacing a memorial, disrupting an institutional ceremony, public expressions of contempt for shared values—via public censure and symbolic restitution; here harm is construed as damage to a collective reputation, with the community itself as the injured party. The third pillar, which the drafts call the Victimhood Registry, permits any student who reports feeling morally contaminated by exposure to an offensive idea—without alleging direct interpersonal harm—to petition for formal institutional condemnation of the speaker, mandatory moral-testimony sessions for the accused, and an official record of victim status for the petitioner; here harm is construed as a kind of porous, symbolic injury that confers special procedural standing and requires communal acknowledgment.
