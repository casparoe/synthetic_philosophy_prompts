For my seminar on moral responsibility, I am trying to determine whether any theory can survive Galen Strawson's Basic Argument without collapsing into hard determinism or appealing to an uncaused mental event. Before I evaluate the standard replies, I want to set out precisely what an adequate response must deliver. Write the desiderata: the conditions that any theory preserving genuine moral responsibility must satisfy in the face of Strawson's regress of self-formation, with the motivating case behind each, and a clarification of whether it bears on diachronic self-constitution, synchronic control, or both. In particular, the list should cover: avoiding an infinite regress of prior responsible agents; accounting for responsibility for actions that flow from character traits one never originally chose; distinguishing rational determination of the will from mere causal determination; and remaining compatible with the absence of uncaused mental events. Flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable, and briefly indicate where compatibilist revisionism, hard determinist denial, and agent-causal libertarianism stand against the criteria. Keep the weight on the conceptual requirements and their motivating cases rather than on a full taxonomy of positions.
