I'm preparing a feature interview for my magazine's ideas section with Dr. Elena Vasquez, a philosopher of science who defends what she calls stabilized counterfactualism — a hybrid position that accepts Pearl-style do-calculus and Woodward-style invariance as the correct framework for causal discovery in empirical science, but insists that the underlying metaphysical analysis of causation remains fundamentally counterfactual-dependence-based in the Lewis tradition, not manipulability-based. She claims that standard interventionist accounts fail to distinguish genuine causal production from mere statistical stability in redundant causal structures, and she proposes a revised notion of quasi-dependence under invariant interventions to resolve classic preemption cases without collapsing into pure counterfactualism.

I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the hybrid view. At least one question should probe how she responds to the charge that her reliance on counterfactual dependence makes her account vulnerable to the familiar problem of non-actual dependencies in cases of late preemption — specifically, whether her appeal to invariant interventions actually solves the problem or merely relocates it. Another should ask about the epistemic status of causal claims in complex adaptive systems where intervention is practically impossible but counterfactual reasoning is still available. I also want a follow-up for each main question that anticipates her most likely reply and presses further: for instance, if she says that invariance conditions are what ground the counterfactual truths, the follow-up should ask whether that makes her view secretly interventionist after all. Throughout, please separate what is settled in the philosophy of causation — such as the distinction between epistemic and metaphysical analyses, and the existence of preemption problems — from what remains genuinely contested, particularly her claim that counterfactual dependence retains metaphysical priority. Do not present her hybrid position as the consensus view; frame the questions so that they treat her synthesis as one contested option among well-established alternatives.
