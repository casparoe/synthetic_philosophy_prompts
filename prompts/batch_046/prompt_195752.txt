Write a deadpan parody of an internal evaluation memo issued by a fictional bureaucratic body—the Commission on Historical Ontology Status (CHOS)—tasked with certifying whether a traditional narrative has crossed the threshold from “legend” into “history.” The memo should evaluate three case files: the Trojan War, King Arthur, and the Exodus. Frame the evaluation as if the Commission applies rigorous, quantifiable criteria that are actually arbitrary and culturally loaded—such as “Stratigraphic Echo Density,” “Genealogical Drift Coefficient,” and “Source Autonomy Index”—and render verdicts that reveal hidden asymmetries (for example, one narrative receives provisional historical status largely because of modern geopolitical investment rather than evidential superiority). The voice should be that of an over-formal, jargon-heavy institutional report: numbered sections, bullet-pointed findings, passive constructions, and clinical detachment. The satire should target the practice of drawing sharp epistemic boundaries between myth and history in popular and scholarly discourse, exaggerating the way evidentiary standards are selectively relaxed or tightened based on a narrative’s religious, literary, or national function rather than on consistent methodological grounds. The piece should be approximately 700 words. Do not include bibliographies, citations, footnotes, or references to specific scholars by name; instead, invent institutional protocols and metrics as needed. The humor must arise from the internal consistency of the bureaucratic logic—each invented criterion should genuinely illuminate a real historiographical tension (such as the circularity of using texts to date archaeological layers that are then used to validate the texts)—so that a reader familiar with the debates recognizes the exaggeration as fair. Maintain sustained irony throughout: the narrator should never wink, and the reader should be able to tell exactly what is being ironized without explicit signaling. End with a formal recommendation that inadvertently collapses the very distinction the Commission was created to enforce.
