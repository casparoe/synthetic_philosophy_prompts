I'm auditing a course in comparative epistemology and have found myself defending a stronger-than-usual reading of Jain anekāntavāda—one I now want to stress-test rather than polish. The position is not the familiar tolerance for complexity, but the claim that every substantive predication is logically incomplete unless explicitly indexed to a standpoint (naya), and that the syād-vāda schema—usually rendered as epistemic modesty—should instead be treated as a formal constraint on well-formed assertions. On this view, saying that an object is red is malformed unless qualified by parameters specifying the respect of predication, the conditions of observation, and the comparative frame; apparent contradictions in metaphysics arise not from genuine conflict but from suppressed indexical failure. I take this to dissolve stalemates about persistence, moral properties, and vagueness by revealing them as clashes between unmarked standpoints rather than substantive disagreements.

What would have to be true for this to be wrong? I want an honest exposure map, not a list of standard objections I can already answer. Which formal results—in the semantics of indexicals, in non-classical logics, or in the metaphysics of properties—would show that standpoint-indexing is a pragmatic convenience rather than a logical requirement? What intuitive cases or comparative analyses would demonstrate that the framework either collapses into familiar contextualism or adds nothing that a disciplined bivalence-plus-qualification strategy cannot capture? Which predictions does it make that rival frameworks do not—perhaps about how philosophical disagreement ought to be resolved, or about which paradoxes it should dissolve cleanly—and have any of those predictions already failed in practice? I also want to know what a committed defender of this interpretation would themselves concede as genuinely damaging: is there an internal tension between holding that all standpoints are equally valid and holding that some qualifications are logically mandatory? Finally, assess whether the view has been insulated into unfalsifiability—if every counterexample can be absorbed by adding another indexical parameter, is that flexibility a theoretical virtue or a symptom of emptiness, and does that matter for a thesis presented as a logical framework rather than a hermeneutic attitude? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish what critics allege from what defenders would grant, and be willing to conclude that the position is either well-exposed or genuinely well-defended.
