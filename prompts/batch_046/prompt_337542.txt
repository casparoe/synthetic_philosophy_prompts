I have been working through, on my own, a decision puzzle commonly called the hitchhiker problem: a traveler is rescued from a desert by a driver who demands payment only after reaching town, knowing that once safe the traveler can refuse with no consequence for the past ride. In online discussions I have found two persistent camps. The Commitment Camp holds that rationality includes honoring advantageous commitments, so the traveler should pay even though the ride is over, because being a promise-keeper is a stable, valuable property of the agent. The Causal Camp holds that rational action requires being able to causally influence an outcome, so once the ride is finished, paying is merely discarding resources with no backward effect.

These two sides have argued for years without producing a shared document. I want you to draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between them. The deliverable should be a single document containing: (1) a numbered section of claims both camps genuinely accept about the scenario, the knowledge states of the traveler and driver, the nature of rational choice, and the absence of legal enforcement; (2) a disagreements table stating each remaining dispute in neutral language that each side would recognize as its own view; (3) the cruxes — sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one camp; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or piece of reasoning both sides agree bears on it, with each camp's explicit prediction recorded in advance.

I ask that two specific cruxes be included. The first concerns whether rationality can demand actions with no causal influence on outcomes one cares about. For this, both sides should consider the Memory-Wipe Variant: the driver can erase the traveler's memory of the agreement upon arrival, eliminating any future psychological benefit from paying or regret from refusing. Both camps should agree this removes standard self-interest, and each should state whether payment remains rational here and why.

The second concerns whether an agent can rationally design her own future decision-procedure. For this, both sides should consider the Design-Your-Own-Successor Case: the traveler will be replaced tomorrow by a psychologically identical copy who will face an identical hitchhiker choice, and the original can now program the copy's decision rules, though she will not exist to experience any result. Both camps should agree this isolates the design question from personal survival, and each should record its prediction about whether programming the copy to pay is rational.

Please ensure that disagreement formulations avoid loaded framing — do not present one view as mere psychology or the other as ignoring reality. The document should be something both parties could sign, with only the precise residual disagreements left standing.

Begin the response with the joint statement document itself. Do not include a preface, commentary, or restatement of this request. Present the document in a clear format: agreed claims first, then the disagreements table, then the cruxes with their associated cases and predictions.
