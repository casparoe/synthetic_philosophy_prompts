Write an essay on the Pasadena paradox, construed as a decision scenario in which both playing and declining a gamble yield undefined expected values—usually because payoffs and corresponding losses scale exponentially with the length of a sequence of fair coin flips. Examine the dispute over whether such undefined expectations genuinely render the options incommensurable, or whether rational preference can still be justified by appeal to dominance over bounded truncations, symmetry-breaking risk aversion, or a precautionary constraint against entering states with non-integrable downside risk. Reach and defend a clear conclusion—either that one option is rationally mandated despite the undefined expectation, or that the appropriate response is principled suspension of choice together with a rejection of the problem as ill-formed.
