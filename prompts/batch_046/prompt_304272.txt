Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of constitutional precommitment—understood in the Ulysses-contract tradition—must satisfy if it is genuinely to justify entrenched constitutional provisions that bind future democratic majorities. The specification should cover: the phenomena it must explain (e.g., rational self-binding versus mere path-dependent inertia, collective binding of future generations who did not consent, and the distinction between constraining outcomes and constraining decision-procedures); the conceptual distinctions it must preserve (e.g., between precommitment and paternalism, between substantive and procedural entrenchment, and between binding preferences and binding institutional designers); the results from democratic theory or social choice it must remain consistent with (e.g., conditions for legitimate authority across time, non-dictatorship, and the difficulty of aggregating intertemporal preferences without loss); and the hard cases it must resolve (e.g., amendments that alter the amendment rule itself, and commitments made by transient majorities to heterogeneous future polities). For each desideratum, give the motivating consideration or case behind it, and flag which are non-negotiable conditions on democratic legitimacy and which are preferences particular to one camp (such as strict determinism about future preferences, or the insistence that precommitment be reversible only by unanimous consent). Then address the following specific twist: can a Ulysses contract legitimately extend to binding not just future majorities but future constitutional designers—those who might seek to revise the rules of revision—without either begging the question or generating an infinite regress of meta-commitments? Identify any pair of desiderata you take to be jointly unsatisfiable in light of standard no-go results, and briefly indicate how the principal existing approaches—Elster’s two-level account, Holmes’s model of self-binding constraint, and a purely proceduralist alternative—measure against your list. Write in British English, keep the weight on the criteria and their tensions rather than on a historical survey, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
