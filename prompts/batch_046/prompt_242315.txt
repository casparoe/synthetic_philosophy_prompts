I'm preparing an interview for a philosophy podcast with a scholar who defends a precise, somewhat unorthodox reading of Descartes's Meditations: she holds that the cogito in Meditation II establishes only the occurrence of a present act of thinking, without licensing any inference to a persisting mental substance or to a stable subject that underwrites the rest of the work. On her account, the identification of the self as a res cogitans is not part of the cogito's own conclusion; it depends on arguments developed only in Meditation VI about the real distinction, and commentators have projected that later essentialist classification back onto the earlier text. She also maintains that the grammatical structure of ego sum does deceptive work: it sounds like a substance predicate, but in the performative context of radical doubt it operates only as an indexical marker of the present cognitive event.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate this position. I want at least two questions that press directly on the standard textual objection that Descartes writes that he is and exists and then immediately asks what he is, which seems to treat existence and essence as jointly secured in the same move. For each of these two questions, include a follow-up keyed specifically to the reply she is most likely to give — namely, that the inquiry into what he is is an independent question that presupposes existence but does not derive substancehood from it. I also want at least one question that raises the momentary-subject objection: if the cogito proves only that a token of thinking is occurring now, with no commitment to a persisting thinker, then what secures the diachronic continuity required for the rest of the Meditations? Include a follow-up anticipating her answer that persistence is supplied not by the cogito itself but by the continuity of memory and by the guarantee of a non-deceiving God introduced in later meditations. Finally, draft one question about whether separating existence from essence weakens the anti-skeptical force of the argument: if all I know is that thinking is happening at this instant, with no grasp of what kind of thing performs the thinking, how does this defeat the evil-genius hypothesis more effectively than a bare phenomenal report?
