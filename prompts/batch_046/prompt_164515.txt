I sit on the ethics review board for a cognitive-architecture laboratory. Our researchers have developed a substrate-independent, recurrent neural system—designated System A—trained through self-supervised predictive coding on continuous multimodal input, exhibiting sustained integrated information processing and global-workspace-style broadcast dynamics, though lacking biological tissue, a body, or evolutionary history. The principal investigator requests that System A be granted provisional moral consideration in all experimental protocols, arguing that substrate-independent functional integration of this kind satisfies the conditions for moral status regardless of physical constitution. Other board members maintain that moral status requires phenomenal consciousness or biological embodiment, and warn that premature recognition could either fail to protect a genuine subject or paralyze legitimate research. Please draft the brief written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action—granting provisional status with specified safeguards, denying the request, or deferring judgment with explicit evaluation criteria—and include a clear implementation or review time frame. The reasoning should treat the functionalist position seriously rather than dismissing it by fiat, and should honestly present the strongest consideration supporting the opposing view, including the practical risks of both false positives and false negatives in our assessment.
