I encountered a summary of Harnad’s symbol grounding problem in an unnamed philosophy blog post, and I want to check whether I am right to feel uneasy about it. The author writes that formal symbols are like dictionary entries defined only by other entries, so any computer manipulating them remains disconnected from the world; this, they claim, is essentially the Chinese Room problem restated. Harnad’s proposed remedy, they continue, is that a system must learn categories through direct sensorimotor interaction with objects rather than through purely symbolic definition. Yet the author argues that this remedy is still inadequate, because sensors and effectors are engineered by programmers, which means any meaning they confer is merely derivative intentionality. For genuine intrinsic intentionality, they conclude, the system must also possess autonomous teleological orientation—an internally directed purpose structure that is not imposed from outside—which current architectures entirely lack. Thus symbol grounding solves only a syntactic problem, not a semantic one, and true understanding remains impossible for artificial systems.

I suspect this account blends distinct arguments without marking the joints, but I am unsure exactly where Harnad’s position ends and other frameworks take over. Could you audit this summary for fairness? Finally, if we correct the misrepresentations and keep only what genuinely belongs to Harnad’s view, does the claim that current AI cannot achieve true semantic grounding still follow, or does it depend on the conflated premises?
