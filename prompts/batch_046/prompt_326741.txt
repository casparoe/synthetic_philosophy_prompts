For a feature I am preparing on the persistence of classical philosophical puzzles in light of modern neuroscience, I want to understand why Molyneux’s problem is still treated as a live empirical and conceptual question rather than as solved. Given that research on cross-modal plasticity and parietal-cortex models now describes spatial shape as encoded in genuinely amodal formats—representations that are neither specifically tactile nor specifically visual but available to both modalities—the standard formulation of the problem seems to rest on a hidden presupposition. It appears to assume that a concept such as “cube” acquired through touch is stored as a modality-bound representation, so that gaining sight requires constructing a new correlation between previously isolated sensory formats. But if the underlying geometric representation was already amodal, then the restoration of vision should immediately activate the existing model, and recognition ought to be instantaneous without any prior learning. So why do philosophers and cognitive scientists continue to frame the debate around whether tactile-visual correlation is conceptually necessary, rather than treating Molyneux’s scenario as demonstrating only that sensory access—not the conceptual content itself—can be temporarily interrupted?
