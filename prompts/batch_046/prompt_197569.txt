But there was this lab tech, Maria, who actually did all the underwater transect measurements and kept her own notebooks in a different language—she was an immigrant and her writing never made it into the official record, probly because she wasn't "author" material back then. Now a museum wants my papers for a "complete" exihibit on the project, and I happen to still have Maria's old notebooks, which I saved after she left. But there's a catch: parts of them are water-damaged and unreadable. So I fed what remains into an AI reconstruction tool, together with the PI's logs, to generate plausible missing pages in her style and language. The museum thinks this is brilliant and wants to display the reconstructed pages right next to her real ones, labeled as part of her archive.

I'm wondering if donating my own notebooks plus these AI-filled gaps actually fixes the silencing, or if it just makes the absence prettier. If I present the reconstructed parts as Maria's work, am I giving her a voice she never had, or putting synthetic words in her mouth? And is there something wrong with me—still the retired scientist with the box—being the one who decides what gets saved and what gets generated?

I feel like there's a philosophical structure here about power and who gets recorded, but I can't name it. Where does the wrongness lie, if anywhere? Is it in the original silence, in my reconstruction, or in me acting as gatekeeper now?
