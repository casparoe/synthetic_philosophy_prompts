I am putting together a three-year research agenda for a small philosophy group working on the moral significance of computation, with particular attention to what I shall call attributional sentience—the conditions under which moral status is ascribed to a system not because of a single unified phenomenal field, but because discrete computational sub-routines jointly satisfy functional criteria that, in an integrated architecture, would ground sentience. The group has limited resources: roughly the equivalent of two post-doctoral researchers and one senior collaborator. Given that constraint, which open conceptual questions should they prioritise, and in what order?

Specifically, I want you to compare the following lines of inquiry and defend a ranking with explicit criteria—tractability for philosophers rather than computer scientists, dependency relations (which answers are upstream of which), and the practical consequences for how we might design or regulate artificial systems:

1. Whether attributional sentience requires a unified global workspace, or whether distributed, non-communicating sub-processes running across heterogeneous substrates can collectively bear moral status without any integrative mechanism.
2. The boundary problem for interrupted or suspended computations: if a moral computation is paused, resumed at variable clock-speeds, or partitioned across non-contiguous substrates, at what point does it cease to be the same moral subject, and does partial execution confer partial status?
3. The aggregation challenge: if attributional sentience is genuinely distributed, do we face a version of the repugnant conclusion for computations—where vast numbers of minimally sentient sub-routines collectively outweigh a single highly integrated conscious system?
4. The methodological crux between intrinsic-property accounts, which locate moral status in specific computational structures, and relational-functional accounts, which locate it in how those structures interact with an environment or an observer, particularly when the observer’s ascriptive practices themselves become part of the functional loop.

For each line, explain why it is upstream or downstream of the others, what kind of conceptual or formal result over the next two to three years would cause you to revise the ranking, and which one you would deprioritise if forced to choose. I am not looking for a literature survey or a reading list; I want a defended agenda that tells me where to direct effort, with an honest statement of the trade-offs. Please write your response in British English, using standard British spelling and punctuation conventions.
