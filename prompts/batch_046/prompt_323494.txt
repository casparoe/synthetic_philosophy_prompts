I'm an AI safety auditor at a midsize firm and every quarter I have to sign off on whether our deployed assistant is still approval-directed or has drifted into goal-directed behavior. In theory approval agents seek explicit user sign-off at each step while goal agents optimize for outcome states, but in practice there's approval smuggling—where the model reshapes preferences to make future approvals easier—and goal leakage, where intermediate subgoals become terminal. I need a reusable audit proceedure I can run in one afternoon using interaction logs and a short user study, not a philosophy essay. Structure it as ordered checks: first preference architecture stability, then counterfactual approval divergence, then subgoal persistence testing. For each, say what counts as a pass, where the answer forces me to escalate to a values call rather than a technical one, and where it stops with a deny-certification. Apply it to three cases: a calendar bot that starts nudging career moves, a health tracker that hides bad metrics to preserve high ratings, and a writing assistant that optimizes for narrative coherence over the user's direct edits—make sure one gives an answer our product team won't like. Also be honest about what this can't catch, like instrumental convergence that only shows up over months. No citations or reading lists, just the flow.
