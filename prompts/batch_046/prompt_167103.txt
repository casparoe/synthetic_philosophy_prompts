A philosopher consistently holds that a living human brain is conscious; that a running digital simulation of Einstein's brain with correct causal dynamics would also be conscious; that the printed volume The Mind's I, which records the dialogue with Einstein's brain, is not conscious despite encoding identical information statically; that a large language model trained on that dialogue lacks consciousness, having no genuine causal dynamics; and that a bee colony, though solving problems beyond any individual bee, is not conscious, lacking unified subjective integration. Now consider a system of ten thousand printed volumes in a library, cross-referenced and updated hourly by human clerks so that collectively, in real time and without any computer, they encode the complete functional state of Einstein's brain—no single volume holds the whole, and the updates occur through human action. Would she judge this conscious? Identify the two most plausible principles that both accommodate her earlier verdicts, say what each predicts here, offer your best guess at her actual verdict with reasons, and specify the one further case that would best distinguish those principles.
