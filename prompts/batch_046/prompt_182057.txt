Write a short philosophical dialogue between a disjunctivist and an indirect realist about the argument from illusion. Center the exchange on the following hypothetical scenario: a neural interface reproduces, neuron for neuron, the exact causal chain that normally occurs when a person sees a red apple in good light, except that there is no apple, no light, and no external object present at all; the subject knows the interface is active and knows that no apple exists. The disjunctivist should argue that the resulting experience is metaphysically distinct from seeing—specifically, that it lacks the direct relational structure that constitutes veridical perception, and that the subject’s knowledge of the artificial cause does not collapse the distinction into a shared representational kind. The indirect realist should counter that if the phenomenology and internal representational content are identical to those in a genuine perceptual case, then positing a fundamentally different mental state violates parsimony, and that the subject’s knowledge is merely an additional doxastic attitude that does not alter the ontology of the experience itself. Ensure that each speaker responds directly to the specific point just made by the other, that neither side is treated as a strawman, and that the disagreement turns on the actual disjunctivist commitment that veridical perception is not a representational state common to illusion but a direct relation to the world.
