I am writing to ask for help drafting a short independent-research proposal. I have no academic background—my professional training is in supply-chain management—but for the past year I have been reading medieval philosophy on my own and have developed a half-formed idea about Al-Ghazālī’s occasionalism that I want to make rigorous enough to present to a small workshop or apply for a part-time study grant.

Most treatments present occasionalism as a purely metaphysical claim: God alone is the true agent, and fire does not burn cotton by any power inherent in the fire itself; the fire is merely the “occasion” for divine action. I wonder whether this framing misses a second, normative layer. Perhaps occasionalism is better understood not only as denying secondary causation in nature, but also as imposing a disciplined constraint on rational expectation: it withholds from observed regularities any entitlement to predict unobserved cases, not because the world is chaotic, but because attributing independent efficacy to created things involves a conceptual overreach that reason should refuse. If so, occasionalism might be compatible with a carefully delimited scientific practice—one that tracks correlations descriptively without claiming causal explanation—while the real tension lies not with empirical science but with a metaphysical interpretation of scientific success.

I need a two-page proposal for a three-month, part-time project. Please include: (1) a precise statement of the research question, specifically whether occasionalism can be reconstructed as an epistemic norm about predictive entitlement rather than merely a denial of natural causation, and whether this reconstruction allows science and occasionalism to coexist; (2) an explanation of why the two dominant readings—the “pure denial of secondary causation” interpretation and the “theological determinism” interpretation—leave exactly this question open; (3) the method: which conceptual distinctions and which standard translations of Al-Ghazālī’s relevant discussions I should work through, in what sequence, using only conceptual analysis and close reading rather than archival or empirical research; (4) what a concrete result would look like, whether that is a reconstructed framework, a specific argument resolving the tension, or a clear demonstration that the tension is unavoidable; (5) what would count as the project failing; and (6) a realistic week-by-week scope for approximately twelve weeks at part-time intensity.

Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I will supply all scholarly references myself. Write in a formal register suitable for a small grant or workshop pitch, and frame the project so that it can be executed without institutional library access or online research tools.
