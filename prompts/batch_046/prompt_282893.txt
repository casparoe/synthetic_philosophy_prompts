The familiar debate frames the hole argument as a direct challenge to manifold substantivalism: if diffeomorphism-related metrics represent the same physical situation, then spacetime points cannot be individuated independently of the fields defined on them. Your proposal must not rehearse this dialectic. Instead, investigate whether the argument's force depends on an unstated inference from representational redundancy—the mathematical fact that the formalism permits multiple descriptions of the same field configuration—to ontological indeterminacy, the metaphysical claim that there is no fact of the matter about the identity of points across those descriptions.

State the precise research question: does the hole argument establish ontological indeterminacy only if one assumes that representational redundancy in a physical theory must map one-to-one onto ontological structure, or can a relationalist about spacetime accept the redundancy as a feature of the mathematical representation without committing to the stronger claim that point identity is metaphysically indeterminate?

Explain why existing answers leave this open. The standard relationalist response accepts the argument's conclusion but treats representational redundancy as a feature of the mathematics rather than of the world, while standard substantivalist responses—those appealing to primitive point identity or to hyper-essentialist properties of points—deny the premise that diffeomorphism-related metrics are physically equivalent. Neither camp, however, has systematically examined whether there is a coherent intermediate position: one that accepts the physical equivalence of the metrics, denies that this entails a failure of point individuation, and instead locates the redundancy in the formulation of dynamical laws rather than in the ontology of points.

Outline the method.
