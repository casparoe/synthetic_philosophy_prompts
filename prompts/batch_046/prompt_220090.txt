I was chatting with a freind who's a science editor and she said the best way to show the error is through fiction, not another explainer graph. The protagonist should be a counselor or a student who slowly realizes that heritability is about variance across a population in a specific enviroment, not about what's fixed in an individual — maybe she notices that the "high heritability" track collapses as soon as the curriculum changes, which shouldn't happen if the score measured innate limits. Include one small bureaucratic detail that captures the confusion, like an intake form asking parents to declare their child's "non-genetic variance share." Make it human and a little absurd, not a lecture. I don't need citations or anything academic, just something that makes the conceptual mistake feel concrete.
