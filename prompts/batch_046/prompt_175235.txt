Write a villanelle about Descartes's cogito in which the two mandatory refrains enact the interpretive problem that the proof establishes only an instantaneous, indexical existence—use refrains such as In this thought, I am and No line survives the now—so that each repetition of the first asserts present certainty while each repetition of the second withdraws diachronic or substantial commitment. Structure the six tercets and final quatrain so that the rhyme scheme (A1 b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 A2) mirrors the philosophical arc: the opening tercets build the naive reading of a persisting immaterial self discovered by introspection; the middle tercets introduce the indexical limitation, treating the I as functioning like here or now rather than as a referring name for a substance; and the closing quatrain pairs the refrains without resolving them, leaving the reader with the point-certainty of the present act of thinking and the absence of any warranted inference to a res cogitans that endures. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of mind or history of philosophy recognizes the specific tension between the immediate intuition and the illicit projection to substantial identity, rather than offering a generic allusion to the cogito. Avoid bibliographies, citations, or named scholars; let the conceptual architecture—indexicality, temporal limitation, the gap between intuition and substance—carry the view entirely within the verse.
