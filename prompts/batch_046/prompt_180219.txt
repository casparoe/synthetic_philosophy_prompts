I am a high school senior taking an honors seminar on philosophy of religion, and after an intense discussion with classmates about whether contemporary physics revives the argument from design, I find myself fixated on a counterfactual that my textbook never addresses. We recently read selections from David Hume's Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion, and I have been trying to project his reasoning onto debates that arose only after his death in 1776 — in particular, the cosmological fine-tuning argument grounded in precise measurements of the cosmological constant and the resonant energy level of carbon-12 that permits the triple-alpha process, a condition often labeled the Hoyle state. My peers argue that these calibrated constants are merely a modern version of the watchmaker analogy that Hume already dismantled, but I believe the dialectic has shifted because the standard contemporary reply invokes multiverse hypotheses — eternal inflation generating an immense landscape of domains with varying physical laws — which depend on entities that are in principle unobservable. Given Hume's well-known strictures against extending analogical reasoning beyond observed experience and his deep skepticism about induction, I want to understand how he would likely assess this specific exchange if he were confronted with the current astrophysical data.

Specifically, would he treat the multiverse explanation as a more extravagant violation of his epistemological limits than the design inference itself, or would he regard both as symmetrical abuses of analogy? And how might he respond to the anthropic selection argument, which claims that life-compatible constants require no further explanation because observers can exist only in regions that happen to permit life? Would he see this reasoning as genuinely dissolving the puzzle, or as a circular maneuver that still fails to justify the inductive leap from observed local regularities to universal cosmic order? I am not seeking a bibliography, citation list, or summary of secondary literature; I only want a careful reconstruction of Hume's probable stance, paying close attention to how his empiricist commitments interact with the particular structure of modern cosmological reasoning.
