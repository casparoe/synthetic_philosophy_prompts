After observing the ant colony behind my compost bin rebuild its tunnels following last week's flooding, I have been returning to Hofstadter's Ant Fugue and wondering whether there is literally one mind in the mound, or only thousands of small ones, or some intermediate structure that fits neither picture. I would like a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on whether an ant colony can have a mind, built from diagnostic questions rather than from a list of named theories. The root should ask whether there is a unified subject of experience at all—the "Aunt Hillary" ambiguity: is there one entity that is the colony, or is every experience necessarily localized in an individual ant? From there, branch on whether functional integration without phenomenal binding is sufficient for mindedness, and then on whether the absence of a central nervous system is a contingent implementation detail or a deep architectural disqualifier. Each leaf should carry a position name (or "unnamed" if unoccupied) and, in one sentence, its most awkward commitment. Please flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate, and for each unoccupied combination of answers, briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; construct the tree entirely from the logical space of these answers.
