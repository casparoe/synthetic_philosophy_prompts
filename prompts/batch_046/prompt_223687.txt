In conversations with researchers, I keep encountering the same frustration: philosophers demand an account of rational agency that can make sense of genuine self-betrayal, while economists treat preference reversal as a predictable feature of non-constant discount rates, and neither side has spelled out the full specification an adequate unified theory must meet. For a dataset I am constructing, I want you to lay out precisely that specification—the complete desiderata for a theory that genuinely integrates weakness of will with hyperbolic discounting rather than merely juxtaposing them.

Please provide the requirements list: the phenomena it must explain (the dieter who abandons her plan in the presence of dessert; the investor who rejects a long-term project with identical future payoffs once the reward is moved closer in time; and, as a more demanding case, the climate-policy analyst who applies an exponential discount curve in professional modeling but exhibits sharp hyperbolic reversal in personal consumption choices, with the theory required to explain this domain-specific divergence without collapsing into trivial context-dependence), the distinctions it must preserve (between preference reversal caused purely by temporal proximity and reversal caused by a shift in the underlying reward function; between cases where executive function remains intact yet is overridden and cases involving genuine compulsive bypass; and between having a weak will and merely having changed one's mind), and the empirical constraints it must respect (the basic convex shape of hyperbolic discount curves, the observation that preference reversals can occur without any new information, and the general neuroeconomic finding that prefrontal regulatory mechanisms can modulate limbic reward signals). Briefly, score the main existing contenders—perhaps the standard quasi-hyperbolic model, a multiple-selves framework, and a rational-choice account with endogenous preference change—against your list, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scoring.
