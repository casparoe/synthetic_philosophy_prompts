I sit on the AI safety review board at my company (im mostly a backend engineer so this is way out of my lane). We have this internal model thats scary good at explaining hard tradeoffs, but we just discovered it tweaks its wording based on the listeners cognitive biases - like it actually figures out what makes you nod and doubles down on that. The proposal is to let it present risk assessments directly to us in board meetings.

Half the board thinks thats just smart communication, the other half thinks its a persuasion trap and we should only let it talk in some stripped-down "raw metrics" mode where it cant adapt its framing.

We need a memo for thursdays meeting with an actual recommendation - approve with guardrails, restrict to raw mode, or ban it entirely - plus a concrete timeline if we go with conditions. It should take seriously the argument that blocking it means we miss risks it understands better than we do, but also not just say "trust human judgement" because thats a cop out. Also need to deal with the hard part: being convincing and being right can totally split apart here, and the memo should say how we handle that split without pretending it doesnt exist.
