I'm writing an essay for my course on nationalism and cosmopolitanism, and I'm stuck on the proverb "charity begins at home," which a classmate invoked to argue that compatriots possess stronger distributive claims than strangers. Rather than treating it as a single claim, I want to disambiguate the precise theses it might express: (1) a psychological claim about the natural limits of altruistic motivation; (2) a normative claim that associative membership generates special obligations of justice; (3) a scalar claim that proximity merely increases the weight of duties without creating an exclusive threshold; and (4) the more niche framing I sketched in my outline — that moral obligation operates analogously to jurisdictional authority, so that "home" marks a boundary of legitimate moral jurisdiction rather than just a heuristic for effective aid. For each reading, is it a platitude, a defensible substantive position, or false? And critically, which interpretations — if any — actually yield the conclusion that cosmopolitan equality is mistaken, as opposed to merely recommending that implementation be local? I need this disambiguation as a menu to structure my argument, not a bibliography or reading list.
