Design a realistic, one-paragraph biological scenario—preferably involving synthetic or horizontally transferred genetic material—that isolates the divergence between etiological and causal-role theories of biological function. The case should be engineered so that the etiological account (function as the effect for which the trait was historically selected) clearly assigns one function, while the causal-role account (function as current contribution to a system-level capacity) clearly assigns a different, incompatible function; the divergence must arise from the theories themselves, not from stipulation. Constraints: no science-fictional elements, no perfect predictors or demons, realistic microbiology or ecology; the origin of the trait must be transparent (e.g., engineered for metabolic task A) but its current causal integration in wild populations must serve task B, with no plausible selection history linking B to the trait's presence. Present the scenario, then demonstrate that it meets each specification: state the verdict of each theory with reasoning, identify the exact feature of the case that generates the split, explain what the verdicts converge to if that feature is removed, and show how a defender of the disadvantaged account could attack the case. Finally, take a committed position—defend one account as giving the more plausible reading of the case, with explicit reasons, rather than presenting both as equally viable. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the argument should stand on its own.
