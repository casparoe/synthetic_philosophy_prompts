I've been trying to understand the hole argument in GR and now I'm worried it eats everything. Here's the chain I can't escape:

(1) Einstein's hole argument shows that if spacetime points have intrinsic identities—haecceities—apart from the metric and matter fields, then applying a diffeomorphism that's the identity outside a matter-free region but not inside produces two physically distinct solutions with identical conditions everywhere else. So determinism fails.

(2) The usual fix is anti-substantivalism: points don't have individual essences; they are only individuated by their relational properties in the metric.

(3) But the argument doesn't depend on gravity specifically. Any continuous structure with localized symmetries has the same form. If entities in that structure have intrinsic identities, you can apply a localized symmetry inside a "hole" and get observationally identical but ontologically distinct configurations.

(4) Personal identity over time works like this: I'm arranged in a social-psychological structure (my relationships, memories, social roles) that has symmetries—e.g., if two people have exactly isomporphic relational profiles, swapping them doesn't change the social facts.

(5) So if I have an intrinsic identity apart from those relations, then there's a "social hole" where my relations are temporarily undefined (say, during deep sleep or amnesia) and I could be "swapped" with an isomorphic counterpart without anyone noticing. That undermines determinism about who I am tomorrow.

(6) Therefore, to save determinism about personal continuity, I must deny that I have intrinsic identity apart from my relations—just like spacetime points.

(7) But that means if my relational profile changes enough, I'm literally not the same person, and there's no fact about whether the swapped version is me. This seems to dissolve personal identity entirely.

(8) So either the hole argument's anti-substantivalist resolution is wrong, or personal identity is an illusion.

I feel like there's a sleight of hand between (3) and (4), or maybe in (5) I'm treating a social gap like a physical hole, but I can't name the exact mistake. Which step should I reject? If I want to keep (2)—that spacetime points lack haecceities—do I still have to swallow (7), or is there a way to block the generalization without giving up the resolution for spacetime? And if I reject (2) instead, what does that cost me for GR?
