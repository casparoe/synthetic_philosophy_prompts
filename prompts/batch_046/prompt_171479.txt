I serve as a senior evaluation officer at an international development agency, and I must sign off on policy briefs that cite “cultural values” or “work ethics” as causal explanations for economic outcomes, or that propose changing such values as policy levers. I need a reusable decision procedure—grounded in Weber’s thesis that values can exercise causal efficacy through elective affinity rather than direct mechanical force, and in the philosophical problem that explaining with values risks tautology, reduction to material interest, or post-hoc labeling—that I can run in about thirty minutes without external sources. The deliverable should be a flowchart in words: ordered tests, what counts as a sufficient answer at each, explicit stop points with a reject, explicit points where judgment is unavoidable and the procedure hands off to a person, and an honest statement of what the procedure cannot settle. Keep distinctions that change the sign-off—such as whether a value operates through a specifiable mechanism of meaning versus serving as a rationalization, and whether elective affinity can be shown without collapsing into institutional explanation—but drop theoretical apparatus that does not alter the decision.

Apply the procedure to three briefs: (A) a proposal attributing low entrepreneurship in a Southeast Asian region to “Buddhist detachment values” and recommending value-change workshops; (B) a brief linking industrial growth among a minority religious group to “Protestant-ethic-style asceticism” despite strong confounding by institutional and tax incentives; and (C) a brief I personally support, attributing sustained cooperative irrigation management to “communal solidarity values,” but where the mechanism linking value to collective action is underspecified and institutional water-right rules provide an alternative explanation. For each, state the verdict, the deciding step, whether the judgment-flag was raised, and—specifically for (C)—that the result is unwelcome to me.

Present the procedure steps in a table with columns: Step | Test or Question | Pass Criterion | Judgment Call? (Yes/No) | Exit Action if Fail. After the analysis, provide a JSON object with the following fields: case_id (string: "A", "B", or "C"), brief_summary (string), verdict (string), deciding_step (string or integer), judgment_flag_raised (boolean), unwelcome_result_for_asker (boolean, true only for C).

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; I must apply this unaided. Flag the cases the procedure cannot settle.
