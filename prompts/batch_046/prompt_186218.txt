Write a deadpan satirical manifesto, roughly 500 words, for a fictional parliamentary caucus called the Abolitionist Caucus, which seeks election solely to enact legislation dissolving all political parties—including itself. Adopt the earnest, bureaucratic voice of a standard campaign platform, but derive every organizational rule and policy stance directly from Simone Weil’s actual arguments against parties: that they suppress individual judgment, manufacture collective passions, demand unconditional allegiance, and substitute collective opinion for truth. The satire should arise from institutional contradictions—fundraising, message discipline, membership rolls, and voter outreach all violate the caucus’s own commitments. Invent one specific procedural mechanism (for example, an “attention audit” or a “gravity check”) that applies a concept from Weil’s metaphysical writings—such as gravity (pesanteur) or the moral necessity of sustained attention—to the practical logistics of campaigning. Keep the irony sustained and legible without winking; a reader should recognize that the satire extends Weil’s critique into an absurd institutional form rather than misrepresenting it. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; work only from the arguments themselves.
