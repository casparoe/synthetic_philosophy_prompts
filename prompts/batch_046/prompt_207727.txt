After arguing for hours with a friend about whether "left" and "right" still denote coherent ideological structures or have collapsed into pure coalitional tribalism held together only by shared enemies, I want a poem that holds the tension open rather than resolving it. Write a 14-line sonnet with a clear volta at line 9. In the octave, present the two sides as mirror coalitions defined not by internal consistency but by mutual negation—each existing chiefly to exclude the other—and arrange the rhyme scheme (for example, ABBA ABBA) so that rhymes couple lines voiced from opposing sides, making the division formally interdependent from the start. At the volta, shift to the suspicion that the opposition is rotational rather than essential: both sides perform the same boundary-maintenance logic, merely rotating the axis of inclusion and exclusion. In the sestet, let the rhyme scheme collapse—either by converging on a single repeated sound or by abandoning rhyme altogether—so that the formal dissolution tracks the conceptual one. Render the mechanism precisely enough that someone familiar with the debate recognizes the structure, not merely the labels. Begin with the poem itself; include no preface, commentary, or explanatory text outside the verse.
