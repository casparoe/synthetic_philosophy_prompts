I am composing the inauguration address I will deliver next Friday upon taking office as president of my high school's student government. Our campus has long been divided between two organized student blocs—one devoted to sustainability policy and one to budget transparency—that function with slogans, endorsements, and loyalty expectations that feel less like debate societies and more like political parties. I want to structure the speech around Simone Weil's argument that political parties should be abolished because membership in them structurally suppresses independent judgment: the group manufactures collective passions that overwhelm individual reasoning, and members gradually cease to think for themselves, substituting a collective voice for their own. The audience will include philosophy teachers who may recognize the reference, parents and administrators who will not, and students who belong to both blocs. The address should run approximately four minutes and must operate as a real inauguration—acknowledging my election, outlining a brief vision, and thanking supporters—rather than as a lecture. However, Weil's critique must shape the speech structurally: I should not frame my victory as a mandate for either bloc, I must avoid celebrating partisan loyalty, and I need to address both groups in a way that asks them to hold their commitments personally rather than collectively, without demanding that they abandon them. An informed listener should recognize that the philosophical framework determines what I can and cannot say, even though the language should remain accessible to everyone present. Please write the address.
