I'm preparing a profile for a workshop on collective cognition and want a rigorous consistency audit before I present. Here is my current position on whether an ant colony—along the lines of the ant-fugue scenario, where individual ants function analogously to neurons in a distributed network—can constitute a mind. (a) I accept substrate-independent functionalism: mental states are defined by their causal-functional roles, not by the biological material that realizes them. (b) I nevertheless deny that such a colony possesses phenomenal consciousness or counts as a single subject of experience, even when its global information-integration metrics exceed those of some conscious vertebrates. (c) I endorse the extended-mind thesis in principle: cognitive processes can genuinely extend beyond an organism's boundary when there is continuous, reciprocal feedback with an external resource. (d) I hold that collective intentionality in social insects is ontologically derivative: the colony's goal-directed behavior reduces without remainder to interactions among individual ants, none of which bears the relevant mental state in an original form. (e) I maintain that consciousness requires not mere integration but what I call synchronic narrative binding—a unified temporal window in which representational contents are co-available to a single point of view—which pheromone-based coordination, lacking any central integrator with sustained temporal continuity, cannot achieve. (f) I am committed to methodological individualism in cognitive science: any adequate explanation of mental phenomena must ultimately bottom out in properties of individual organisms rather than in irreducible group-level dynamics.

Please do not merely list options and leave me to choose. Take a definite, committed stance: go through these commitments pair by pair, identify which are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each state what the least costly revision would be and what it costs me conceptually. Pay special attention to whether (e) is an ad hoc rescue of (b) that undermines my functionalism in (a), and whether (c) and (f) can coexist without collapsing into either a rejection of extended cognition or a hidden acceptance of group minds. Then tell me which single revision—dropping or modifying one commitment—would clean up the largest number of tensions, and defend that choice rather than presenting it as one possibility among many. I want the audit ordered and decisive, not a balanced menu.
