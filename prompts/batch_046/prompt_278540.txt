I am constructing a taxonomy for an advanced moral-epistemology workshop on whether moral preferences can be cleanly separated from biases without circularity. I want a branching tree—ordered from foundational metaphysical commitments to applied diagnostic criteria—that sorts positions according to how they answer four successive splitting questions.

First split: Is the preference/bias distinction metaphysically substantive (there exist genuinely unbiased moral preferences that can serve as normative anchors), or is it purely instrumental (all preferences are shaped by contingent causal histories, and "bias" is just a label for those shaped by influences we happen to reject)?

Second split, for the substantive branch: Must an unbiased preference satisfy reflexive authorization—that is, the agent must be able to endorse the preference's causal origin from a standpoint that is not itself constituted by that preference?

Third split, for the instrumental branch: Is there an objective asymmetry in distorting mechanisms—call it the calibration asymmetry—where some influences systematically reduce cross-agent predictive accuracy about welfare, while others do not, allowing "bias" to be defined functionally rather than normatively?

Fourth split, cross-cutting both branches: When a preference survives what I will call the adaptive-pressure test—being formed under conditions of severely constrained choice and resource scarcity—does that survival count as evidence of bias (adaptation to oppression) or as evidence of robustness (selection for genuine value-tracking)?

Each leaf should carry the name of an actual defended position or be marked "unnamed," together with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment (for example, that it must treat deeply held moral convictions as epistemically equivalent to taste preferences, or that it requires agents to achieve an impossible reflexive purity before trusting any judgment). Include at least one logically possible combination of answers that remains unoccupied, and briefly diagnose whether its emptiness is due to incoherence, instability, or mere neglect. Also flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate, explaining which shared commitment blurs the boundary. The taxonomy should stand entirely on its logical structure; do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and phrase every node so that someone can answer it without already knowing which philosophical label it maps onto.
