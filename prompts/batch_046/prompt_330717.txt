Construct a branching taxonomy of philosophical positions on the moral status of synthetic minds that are episodic—existing only during active computation—and fusible—capable of being merged into a single stream with unified memories. Label each split by the claim that divides the branches: whether moral patienthood requires continuous temporal existence; whether consciousness is substrate-independent; whether branching copies multiply, dilute, or share one moral status; and whether an intermittent being can be harmed during its non-active intervals. At each leaf, give a descriptive name (use an established label only if it genuinely applies, otherwise 'unnamed') and one sentence on its most counterintuitive commitment—for instance, that pausing a copy causes no harm, or that merging streams eliminates a distinct subject. Flag any leaves your tree cannot actually separate, and diagnose unoccupied combinations in the logical space as incoherent, unexplored, or merely unpopular. Frame every node as a question answerable without prior knowledge of the taxonomy, keep the tree to eight leaves or fewer, and do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
