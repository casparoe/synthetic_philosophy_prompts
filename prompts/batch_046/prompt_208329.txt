I have noticed a recurring stalemate in scholarship on Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz’s defense of women’s education, and I want to know whether the pattern has a name. Here is the phenomenon. In her response to ecclesiastical criticism, she repeatedly frames her own study as a private exercise in obedience rather than a public claim to authority, deploying standardized disclaimers of ignorance and submission to religious oversight. One interpretive camp reads these disclaimers as strategic irony—performative humility that exposes the contradiction of demanding intellectual formation from women while punishing them for displaying it. The opposing camp reads the identical disclaimers as structurally binding and sincere—proof that she sought only an exceptional personal exemption, not universal access to learning for women. The symmetry is what interests me: both sides agree on the textual facts and on her social position as a religious woman writing under episcopal scrutiny; they disagree only on whether that position renders her rhetoric subversive or accommodating. Each side treats the same rhetorical feature as decisive evidence, so the debate never advances to new material; it oscillates between mirror-image conclusions anchored in the same biographical premise.

Is there an established concept in hermeneutics or feminist historiography for this kind of deadlock—where a single rhetorical feature serves as symmetrical, opposite-direction evidence for contradictory readings, with both readings tied to an identical premise about the author’s institutional position? I am not asking about ordinary textual ambiguity, because the feature is not neutrally polysemous; it is actively weaponized in opposite directions. Nor am I asking about the intentional fallacy, since both camps claim to recover her intent, just opposing ones. If a named phenomenon captures this exact configuration, I want to know it together with its boundaries—what distinguishes it from strategic ambiguity on the author’s part, from simple disagreement over evidence weighting, and from standard equivocation. If there is no established term, I want a coined name and a precise definition that explains why the deadlock is stable rather than resolvable by closer reading, especially one that specifies what must be present for the symmetry to hold and what would break it.
