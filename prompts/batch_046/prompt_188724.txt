Two people I know have been arguing about an old city ordinance that banned unlicensed street vending. At first it was enforced with heavy fines and police removal to drive out informal sellers, but now it is widely accepted as protecting public health and fair competition. One friend says Pascal was essentially right: justice is whatever is established, and once people have built their lives and trust around the rule, the original coercion has done its work and disappeared into legitimate custom. The other friend says this is exactly the gap Pascal never closes—if justice is defined by what force establishes, then there is no independent standard to call the ordinance unjust even if the licensing board now rigs approvals for wealthy applicants. She thinks Pascal secretly relies on a hidden moral standard when he says force should serve justice, but he never explains where that standard comes from if not from force itself. They asked me to referee. Please go through their exchange point by point and say who is right about what. In particular, is there a conceptual issue about whether established custom can ever truly be separated from the coercion that founded it, and does Pascal's framework actually provide the tools to make that separation, or does it just describe how power makes itself look reasonable?
