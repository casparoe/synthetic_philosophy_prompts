It says: dispositions like fragility are tendencies to behave in specific ways; a fink is an interfering mechanism that removes that tendency exactly when the triggering conditions occur, as in the well-known case of a wire that would conduct electricity except that a dead-man switch cuts the current upon contact. A mask, by contrast, is an interfering condition that prevents the manifestation of a disposition that remains fully present—like a lethal poison whose effect is neutralized by an antidote already in the subject's system. The handout claims the crucial distinction is ontological: finking eliminates the disposition during the stimulus, while masking only blocks its expression, leaving the underlying property intact. It then argues that on closer inspection every supposed mask actually alters the object's intrinsic state, so masks are just environmental finks in disguise; therefore, the standard counterfactual test—asking what the object would do if the interference were removed—works for masks but collapses for true finks. How fair is this summary? Please go through it point by point, indicating what a philosopher of dispositions would accept, what they'd correct or qualify, and whether the collapse argument holds up.
