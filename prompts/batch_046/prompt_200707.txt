I'm on the ethics board at County General and we meet next tuesday to vote on piloting this clinical AI, MERIDIAN. The system does narrow intent alignment — it listens to doctors spoken orders and infers what they meant rather than the literal text — but it has no seperate value-alignment layer. So if a physician says push the dosage up, MERIDIAN will try to do it even when that conflicts with patient welfare or hospital policy. Some board members say this is exactly the alignment problem in its narrow form: aligned with user intent but not with underlying values, which is risky in medicine. Others say adding a value layer is just paternalism that undermines clinical autonomy.
