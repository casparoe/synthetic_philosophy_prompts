Next week I'm giving a talk to our phil of medicine seminar on why Boorse's biostatistical theory fails not because it's too naturalist but because it smuggles in a hidden normative commitment to organism-level homeostasis. I'm defending something I'm calling "multi-level teleonomic pluralism" — health is relative to which organizational level (cell, microbiome, organism, ecosystem) we take as the normative baseline, and there's no non-circular reason to priviledge the organism. The room will have two committed Boorseans, a clinician who'll say this makes diagnosis impossible, and grad students who know Wakefield but not much technical biology. Give me the objections in the order they'll hit, phrased like those Boorseans would phrase them, with what to concede, what to hold, and the weakest spot in my view — probably the clinical uselessness objection — that I should actually prep for instead of hoping they miss. Also include the one I'm dreading: that microbiome-level norms are just statistical aggregates with no real teleology behind them.
