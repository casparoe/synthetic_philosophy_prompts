okay so i was trying to mash together the KK principle with luminosity and ended up in this weird loop about belief revision that i cant exit. heres the argument, probably has a hidden slide somewhere:

(1) KK holds: if i know p then i know that i know p, at least for rational agents.
(2) Luminosity: if my credence in p is above 0.9 and the conditions for knowledge are met, then i know im in that high-credence state.
(3) So if i know p, then by (1) i know i know p, which by (2) means i know im in the high-credence state, and then applying KK again i know i know i know p, etc. infinite iteration.
(4) Now imagine my credence in p slowly drifts down from 0.99 to 0.85 over an hour as i process new evidence. theres no sharp cutoff i can point to.
(5) At t=0 i had all those infinite-order knowledge states about my high credence.
(6) Knowledge is preserved unless i encounter a defeater that i recognize as such.
(7) Since the drift was gradual and sub-personal, i never recognized a specific defeater at a specific moment.
(8) So either i still have all that infinite-order knowledge (which means i still know p, contradicting my current belief that p is false), or there was a precise instant where i lost it.
(9) But i cant identify that instant, and it feels absurd to say knowledge vanishes at some hidden millisecond.
(10) Therefore either KK is false for empirical beliefs, luminosity is false, or knowledge can disappear without me knowing it disappeared.

the step that feels slipperiest to me is (6) - is there a hidden premise about preservation? or is the iteration in (3) illegitimate? i really want to keep KK if i can but im also suspicious that luminosity smuggles in false precision about mental states. which premise do you actually reject, and what does giving it up cost? especially, does this mess with the standard anti-luminosity argument (the cold-case thing where you dont know exactly how cold you feel)? also if we replace 'credence' with 'feeling of knowing' does the paradox get stronger or weaker?
