After a recent medical diagnosis made the fragility of biological parameters suddenly personal, I have found myself returning to the cosmological fine-tuning problem—not out of abstract curiosity, but because I now need to know whether the contingency I feel in my own body has any analog in the structure of physical law itself, and whether that analog actually demands an explanation. Before I commit to any particular response—multiverse, design, brute fact, or something else—I want the full specification of what an adequate account must deliver. Write the desiderata that any theory addressing the apparent fine-tuning of fundamental physical constants must meet. For each requirement, state the phenomenon or conceptual distinction it must preserve: for example, the difference between tuning of dimensionless constants versus dimensional ones; between local and global parameter-space measures; between explaining why values fall within a life-permitting range versus predicting specific values; between accommodating observed values and accommodating counterfactual stability; and between consistency with quantum field theory and cosmological observation versus accommodating speculative extensions of physics. Indicate which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable and which reflect camp-specific commitments, such as ontological parsimony, avoidance of modal realism, or the demand for unique predictive output. In particular, identify whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable—for instance, if insisting both on a deterministic derivation of constant values and on full empirical equivalence with current textbook physics proves impossible. Also flag which single desideratum, if treated as absolute, eliminates the largest class of existing candidate explanations. Please separate what is settled among physicists and philosophers of science from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present a disputed interpretive claim—such as the precise probabilistic weight of life-permitting ranges or the legitimacy of anthropic reasoning—as though it were the consensus view.
