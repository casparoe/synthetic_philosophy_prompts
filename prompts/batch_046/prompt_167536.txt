So at dinner last week my roommate Mara and her coworker Leo got into a real argument about whether "Western values" even refers to anything coherent. Mara said its mostly a rhetorical blank check: people toss democracy, free speech, gender equality, capitalism, secularism, and individual rights into the same bucket, but actual Western countries disagree sharply on half those things, and plenty of non-Western societies embrace others. Leo said that just makes it fuzzy around the edges, not useless -- like "sports" or "art" -- and fuzziness isnt fatal.

Mara answered that its worse than fuzziness; its strategic ambiguity, because politicians invoke "Western values" precisely to avoid defending particular claims, so the phrase works like moral camouflage rather than a real concept. Leo said thats a critique of misuse, not proof the category is empty -- you can misuse "justice" without justice being fake. Then Mara pointed out that lists of "Western values" almost always leave out labor protections or social democracy, even though those are huge in actual Western countries, which shows the phrase isnt describing the West but prescribing a specific ideological package. Leo came back that selective highlighting happens with every cultural label, and asked if she could name a single cultural category that survives perfect consistency. They turned to me to referee and I honestly had no idea who was right.

Can you go through their exchange move by move and say whos right about what? And tell me what, if anything, both of them are missing -- maybe some distinction between describing a culture and performing an identity claim that changes how we should evaluate the phrase? I want the breakdown point by point, not just an overall winner.
