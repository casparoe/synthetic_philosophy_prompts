After a long argument with a friend about whether the value of friendship can be separated from the value of the beautiful experiences shared within it, I keep returning to G. E. Moore’s claim that beauty and friendship are intrinsic goods bound together in an organic unity rather than summed parts. Write a short story about an archivist in a near-future institution tasked with preserving human flourishing by isolating aesthetic experiences and social bonds into discrete, quantifiable records. Through her work she should discover—without referencing any philosophical texts—that extracting a moment of awe from the friendship that made it intelligible strips both of their value, and that the two goods are structurally interwoven in a way that resists decomposition. The narrative should illustrate, rather than state, why reducing these goods to separable components destroys exactly what made them valuable in the first place.
