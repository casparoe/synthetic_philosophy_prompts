There's this weird tension in how the Nuremberg and Tokyo tribunals actually worked. On one hand they invented retroactive crimes — crimes against peace, crimes against humanity — and applied them to people who'd never agreed to those rules, which looks like pure victors' justice. On the other hand they went out of their way to prove individual guilt for each defendant, rejected collective punishment, and wouldn't accept "I was following orders" unless there was literally no moral choice left. They also used this hybrid procedure where some parts looked like a normal criminal trial and other parts looked like a historical commission writing the record.
