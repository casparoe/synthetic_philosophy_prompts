Bell's theorem is standardly interpreted as showing that any hidden-variable reconstruction of quantum correlations must violate parameter independence. Yet the Reeh-Schlieder theorem in relativistic quantum field theory demonstrates that local observable algebras associated with spacelike-separated regions do not factorize the global vacuum state, which seems to be a failure of precisely the kind of regional separability Bell's locality condition requires. Given this, shouldn't we conclude that standard quantum field theory already violates Bell locality, and therefore that Bell's result is not a distinctive problem for hidden-variable models but rather an unavoidable feature of any quantum theory? What is wrong with treating the Reeh-Schlieder non-factorizability as the quantum-mechanical analog of Bell-violating non-locality?
