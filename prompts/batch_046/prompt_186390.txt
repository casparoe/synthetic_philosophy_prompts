Specify the desiderata that a theory of punishment and moral desert must satisfy to count as adequate, with the conceptual case or tension motivating each. Your list must cover: (i) why proportionality between offense and sanction remains a hard constraint even if determinism undermines libertarian free will; (ii) how to withhold punishment from clear non-agents (severe cognitive disability, infancy) without collapsing the theory into pure forward-looking social control; (iii) a substantive response to the manipulation argument that does not merely relabel compatibilist conditions; (iv) a principled distinction between genuinely deserving punishment and being useful as a deterrent example, tested against a case in which punishing an innocent person maximally prevents harm; (v) an account of remorse that explains its sentencing relevance without circular appeal to desert; and (vi) consistency with established empirical results on rehabilitation and recidivism. For each desideratum, state whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp, and flag any subset you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—particularly if determinism plus backward-looking proportionality generates an in-principle tension. Then briefly score Daniel Dennett’s evolutionary, game-theoretic compatibilist account and Gregg Caruso’s free-will-skeptical public-health model against the list, noting where each fails and whether that failure is fatal on your criteria. Finally, apply the full specification to one constructed hard case: a defendant whose remorse and behavioral reform are pharmacologically induced and sustained; say exactly what your desiderata demand in this scenario and whether either contender can deliver it without contradiction. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating tensions rather than on extensive exposition of the two views.
