I host a podcast on metaphysics and have recently found the topic of many-sided truth personally unavoidable: after receiving three authoritative but mutually incompatible medical assessments of a chronic condition, I realized that my instinct to demand “the one correct diagnosis” felt less like rational discipline and more like an arbitrary refusal to tolerate contradiction. I am now interviewing a philosopher who defends a contemporary, non-relativist version of Jain anekāntavāda—specifically the view that reality is intrinsically multi-aspected, that the seven-fold syādvāda scheme offers a logical framework for holding apparently contradictory predicates without collapsing into dialetheism, and that no single naya (standpoint) is metaphysically exhaustive. I want the interview to press where the literature genuinely presses, anticipating the standard replies and cutting them off rather than letting them become deflections. Please lead with the bottom line: present the full sequence of eight to ten interview questions first, with no preface, no restatement of this request, and no introductory commentary. After the questions, provide a brief strategic note explaining the ordering and where the pressure points lie. Write in American English. The questions should include at least one on the self-application problem (if anekāntavāda claims no single perspective is exhaustive, is that claim itself exhaustive, and if not, why accept it?); at least one on whether syādvāda is genuinely distinct from dialetheism or paraconsistent logic, with a follow-up keyed to the likely answer that syāt functions as a scope restrictor rather than an affirmation of contradiction; at least one on how the view distinguishes legitimate multi-aspectual description from mere epistemic humility or standpoint pluralism; and at least one on whether an infinite-attribute ontology makes object-identity impossible, with a follow-up keyed to the probable reply that identity is preserved through a core substrate (jīva or dravya) that persists across standpoints. For each question, include a brief follow-up designed to block the retreat that “this is just a research program, not an objection.” The guest should be treated as an unnamed contemporary defender of the view, not as a historical figure, and the questions should turn on the actual content of the position rather than on biography or generic philosophy-of-religion framing.
