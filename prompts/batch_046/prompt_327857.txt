Question: What are the three core conditions for valid consent, and how do deception, coercion, and incompetence each undermine one of them? Provide an example for each.

Student answer:
Valid consent needs three things: the person has to be competent, the information has to be honest and complete, and the choice has to be free from pressure.

First, deception breaks the information condition. If a doctor lies about what a procedure involves, the patient cant really agree to that specific thing. Even if the doctor just leaves out some risks, thats still deception and it makes the consent invalid, because the patient wasnt fully informed.

Second, coercion breaks freedom. Coercion means someone uses a threat to make you agree, like saying theyll hurt you if you dont sign. Its different from persuasion, where they just give you reasons. But economic pressure can also be coercion if the offer is too big to refuse, like paying someone a ton to be in a study. That still removes real choice.

Third, incompetence breaks the competence condition. A person has to understand what theyre agreeing to. Competence is usually all-or-nothing: either you can make medical decisions or you cant. So if someone is temporarily confused from medication, they arent competent right then and any consent they gave is void, even if they seemed okay earlier.

Overall this covers the main points.
