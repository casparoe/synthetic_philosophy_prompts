Two members of a rationalist study group I sit in on got stuck in a long back-and-forth about Hofstadter’s MU-puzzle and the idea of jumping out of the system (JOOTS). I want a point-by-point adjudication, not a balanced “both sides have a point” summary, and I’d like you to treat it as a live disagreement rather than an exercise in exposition.

Here is how the exchange went.

(1) The first said that solving the MU-puzzle requires seeing an invariant—the number of I’s is always 1 or 2 mod 3, never 0—yet the four transformation rules never mention arithmetic, divisibility, or quantification over derivations. So the solver genuinely steps outside the formal system defined by those rules to see a limitation the system itself can never express. That, she said, is exactly JOOTS: human cognition transcends the formal framework it started in, which means any purely formal or algorithmic account of reasoning is necessarily incomplete.

(2) The second replied that this romanticizes hierarchy. The invariant is provable inside Peano arithmetic, which is just a stronger formal system; Gödel’s theorems already describe this ladder precisely. Every apparent jump is promotion to a meta-system, not escape. There is no mystical outside—just an infinite stack of formalizations—so calling it JOOTS mistakes routine meta-reasoning for irreducible cognition.

(3) She came back with a sharper distinction: being able, in principle, to embed a proof in PA is different from actually performing the representational restructuring that lets a human see the invariant in the first place. Discovery is not derivation. She added that recognizing the invariant requires induction over all possible derivations, which already presupposes an ontology of actual infinity that a strictly finitist formal system never asserts. So the cognitive act is prior to any particular formalization and involves an ontological commitment the original rules lack.

(4) He countered that this makes discovery sound like magic. A mechanized search over arithmetic properties of string states—systematically testing invariants—could reproduce the restructuring without consciousness or any irreducible “outside” perspective. The subjective feeling of insight is just the brain registering a representational upgrade. If an AI could perform such meta-systematic search, then the jump is fully algorithmic, merely operating one level up. The burden is on her to show there is something in human insight that cannot, even in principle, be decomposed into hierarchical formal search plus stronger ontological assumptions.

Neither would concede. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Say who is right about which specific claim—particularly about whether JOOTS is best understood as meta-system promotion, as representational restructuring, or as something else entirely—and identify what, if anything, both parties are missing, perhaps about the gap between proof and discovery or about whether ontological commitment resolves the dispute.

In particular, say what would change your mind: for instance, would a demonstrated case of an AI discovering an analogous invariant through purely syntactic meta-search without representational restructuring shift your verdict? Or would a rigorous argument that all invariant-recognition necessarily invokes an unformalizable semantic step alter it? And flag explicitly where your reasoning is most likely to fail—whether in overestimating the formalizability of discovery, in conflating subjective insight with objective logical structure, or elsewhere.
