If the Marquis de Condorcet were alive today and confronted with modern proposals to apply jury-theorem logic to collective inference in large language model ensembles—where individual models share extensive training-data overlap and therefore generate errors that are structurally correlated rather than statistically independent—how do you think he would assess the validity of such aggregation? Would he treat the shared epistemic substrate as a fatal violation of his independence condition, thereby denying any convergence guarantee, or might he reformulate the theorem to accommodate a bounded form of dependence that still permits some collective advantage over single-model judgment?
