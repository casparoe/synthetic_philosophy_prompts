Quine's essay targets two empiricist dogmas. First, he rejects the analytic-synthetic distinction because attempts to define analyticity—through synonymy, definitional reduction, or Carnap's state-descriptions—are circular or just push the problem back. Second, he rejects reductionism, the view that every meaningful statement can be translated into discrete reports of immediate experience. Following Duhem, Quine argues theories face experience as a corporate body: if a prediction fails, we can save any statement, even logical ones, by tweaking auxilliary hypotheses elsewhere in the web of belief. This holism shows there's no principled boundary between empirical and logical revision; they're revisable in the same way. Actually, Quine takes over Carnap's internal/external framework to preserve a pragmatic version of analyticity—logical rules become internal conventions of our language framework, fixed for convenience. So Quine doesn't destroy the split, he relocates it into pragmatics.
