I am serving as an external grader for a graduate seminar on moral agency, and I need an objective assessment of one examination response that has divided my colleagues. The exam asked students to reconstruct Galen Strawson's Basic Argument—that true moral responsibility requires being the cause of oneself in mental respects, which generates an impossible infinite regress of self-formation—and then to apply it to a case in which an agent's practical reasoning is partly constituted by enduring institutional protocols, what the prompt called "structural scaffolding," that she did not originally author. Specifically, students had to determine whether distributing the causal basis of action across agent and scaffold introduces a legitimate stopping-point for the regress, or whether it merely relocates the problem.

The student's answer is as follows. "Strawson's Basic Argument proceeds in three stages. First, you are responsible for what you do only if you are responsible for the mental state that directly produces the action. Second, to be responsible for that mental state, you must have intentionally caused yourself to be in it through a prior exercise of your will. Third, that prior exercise itself requires being responsible for the mental state behind it, leading to an infinite regress. Since no one can complete an infinite chain of self-creation, true moral responsibility is impossible. Applying this to structural scaffolding—say, an institutional protocol that shapes deliberation—shows that the argument can be interrupted. The scaffold is not part of the agent's original mental nature; it is an external resource that she gradually internalizes. Once she has endorsed and maintained the protocol over time, it becomes part of her practical identity, but its origin lies in her act of adoption rather than in a prior mental state. Because adoption is an observable event that does not require a further mental cause—she simply chooses to integrate the protocol—the regress stops there. The agent is therefore responsible for actions that flow through the scaffold, not because she created her entire mental nature ex nihilo, but because she exercised genuine choice at the point of adoption. Thus distributing the causal basis across agent and scaffold actually weakens Strawson's conclusion: it replaces the impossible demand for total self-creation with a more modest requirement that one be responsible for the specific points at which one incorporates external structures into one's reasoning."

I am particularly interested in whether the student keeps Strawson's concern about being responsible for the way one is distinct from generic deterministic worries, and whether the analysis of adoption respects the logical structure of the regress rather than treating it as an empirically interruptible chain.
