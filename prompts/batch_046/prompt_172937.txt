Question 3: Does philosophy make genuine progress? Distinguish cumulative progress from problem dissolution, and apply the distinction to the free will debate.

Student answer:
Philosophy definitely makes progress, but mostly through dissolution rather than accumulation. Take Frankfurt's hierarchical model: he shows that when your second-order volitions align with your first-order desires, the determinism problem just dissolves—theres no contradiction left to solve. This is classic Wittgensteinian dissolution, where philosophical puzzles are basically language muddles that vanish once you map the conceptual structure right. Meanwhile, virtue ethics shows cumulative progress because philosophers like Foot and Anscombe kept Aristotle's core framework intact and only corrected small mistakes in his view of practical wisdom. So philosophy moves forward either by making fake questions disappear or by slowly refining answers that were mostly correct already. Anyone who says philosophy is stagnant is ignoring both mechanisms.
