As a retired molecular biologist who has recently turned to moral philosophy, I am examining Robert Nozick’s hybrid framework—broadly characterized as a Kantian side-constraint for people together with a utilitarian calculus for animals. From his discussion I have reconstructed the following implicit verdicts.

First, a neurotypical adult human being may never be sacrificed or used without consent for the aggregate welfare of others, regardless of the magnitude of benefit.

Second, a sentient but non-rational mammal—say, a laboratory pig—may be subjected to proportionate harm if that harm produces a substantial net welfare gain for other sentient beings and no less harmful alternative exists.

Third, a chimpanzee displaying mirror self-recognition, emotional complexity, and advanced tool use may still be enrolled in mildly invasive research that benefits its own species, provided the welfare calculus is favorable.

Fourth, a purely digital processing system that lacks consciousness, embodiment, and evolutionary history carries no direct moral status, though indirect obligations may arise through human interests.

The new case I wish to consider is a bioengineered chimera: developed from porcine stem cells, gestated in a sow, legally registered as livestock, yet modified to possess human-equivalent rational autonomy, linguistic competence, self-consciousness, and moral reasoning.

Please predict how Nozick’s framework should rule on whether this being may be used as a mere means for the welfare of others. Identify the principle that best explains the four verdicts above—distinguishing, for instance, a biological-species boundary from a functional-rational-agency boundary—and state the competing principle that fits the same pattern equally well. Specify which single further case would most sharply discriminate between these two interpretations.

Finally, please indicate what argument or empirical finding would cause you to change your prediction, and point to the step in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to failure.
