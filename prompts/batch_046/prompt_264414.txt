Write a 14-line blank-verse poem structured as a strange loop: line 1 and line 14 must be identical except for a single word substitution that shifts the speaker from an arithmetical formula to a conscious subject. In the octave, trace Gödel's construction—Gödel numbering allowing a formula within a consistent formal system to assert its own unprovability—without using technical terms as ornament. At the volta (line 9), turn to Hofstadter's analogy in GEB: that self-awareness might emerge not across hierarchical levels (a tangled hierarchy) but when a symbol system bends back upon itself within a single level. Let the rhyme scheme and repetition do philosophical work—specifically, let the closing couplet echo the sounds of the opening couplet but invert their sense, so that the loop closes without deciding whether it produces genuine mind or only its mirror. Keep the distinction between a tangled hierarchy and a strange loop implicit in the rhythm and rhyme, not stated didactically, and leave the tension unresolved.
