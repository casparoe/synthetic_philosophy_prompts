Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for hinge epistemology — the cluster of issues around Wittgenstein's On Certainty, hinge propositions, and the framework within which ordinary doubt operates. I want the tree built from the logical space first, with named positions placed inside it only after the branching questions are fixed.

Use two concrete hinges as test cases at the nodes: the empirical hinge "My hands exist" and the methodological hinge "Induction is reliable". Begin with the claim that a hinge functions either as a genuine truth-evaluable belief or as a pre-propositional enacted stance (a practical commitment that never takes the form "I believe that p").

If the answer is that it is a belief, branch on whether it carries a special epistemic status — default entitlement, certainty, or knowledge — or whether it sits entirely outside the epistemic economy, serving only as a scaffolding condition for inquiry. If it carries special status, branch again on whether that status is intrinsic to the rational structure of inquiry itself or derived from social and pragmatic convention. If it sits outside epistemics, branch on whether it can be revised only through non-rational shifts — conversion, social drift, or what we might call hinge fatigue — or whether accumulated counter-evidence can overturn it like an ordinary belief.

If the answer is that it is a non-propositional enacted stance, branch on whether the stance is still rationally assessable as appropriate or inappropriate to a form of life, or whether it is entirely arational, with assessment applying only to the actions it enables.

Place the following named positions as leaves where the branches lead: Moorean common-sensism; Wittgenstein's framework view (propositional but non-epistemic); Crispin Wright's entitlement view; Moyal-Sharrock's enacted-hinge view. Also place an unnamed rationalist injection: the view that hinges are propositional structural priors with effectively infinite Bayesian weight, so that their "standing fast" is a feature of rational belief-updating rather than a distinct epistemic category. Include a further leaf for hinge anarchism — the view that hinges are purely conventional and enjoy no rational privilege — and diagnose whether it occupies a coherent spot or collapses into conventionalism within one of the other branches.

Then run both test hinges through the tree and flag any pair of leaves that cannot be genuinely separated by the answers they give to these concrete cases (for instance, whether Wright's entitlement view and the structural-prior view diverge on any practical commitment about induction). Identify any unoccupied combinations in the logical space — such as non-propositional hinges that are rationally revisable by direct counter-evidence — and say briefly whether the gap looks like incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect.

Every branch node must be phrased as a concrete claim or case that a reader could answer without already knowing which philosophical label it leads to.
