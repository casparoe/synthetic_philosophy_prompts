On her view, projectibility is not fixed by linguistic entrenchment or historical usage, but by whether a predicate tracks an invariant feature of the causal mechanism that generates the observed regularity. She argues that green is projectible because it corresponds to a stable chlorophyll-based reflectance process, whereas grue fails because it requires an unmotivated discontinuous causal switch at an arbitrary time t, with no supporting mechanistic structure. I need a sequence of questions that press this position where the literature traditionally presses Goodmanian accounts, anticipate her standard replies, and make it progressively harder for her to deflect criticism by saying that an objection merely points to a research program rather than a genuine problem. In particular, the questions should challenge the non-circularity of identifying the true causal mechanism without already relying on inductive projection; they should anticipate her likely response that interventionist experiments can identify mechanisms independently of induction, and then ask what specific experimental outcome could genuinely disconfirm her causal-indexed criterion rather than being absorbed as evidence of a hidden mechanistic switch. The sequence should conclude by asking whether her view ultimately collapses into a form of natural-kind essentialism, and, if she denies this, how she distinguishes her mechanistic projectibility criterion from standard essentialist accounts—without appealing to bibliographies or lists of prior authors. Please write the questions in a tone appropriate for a specialist colloquium Q&A.
