I spent thirty-five years in condensed-matter physics before turning to philosophy in retirement, and I am trying to fix a consistent position on Wittgenstein's private-language argument. My thinking is still shaped by instrument calibration and measurement-error analysis, and I want to know whether the commitments below can stand together or whether my scientific habits are smuggling in contradictions. Give me an audit, not a survey: rank the tensions by severity, say for each what the cheapest fix is and what it costs, identify the one apparent conflict that dissolves once the commitments are precisely stated, and then take a definite stand—tell me which single commitment I should revise, defend that choice, and say whether the revision forces me to abandon the measurement analogy, the reality of phenomenal privacy, or first-person authority.

Here are my commitments:
(1) The private-language argument succeeds: a speaker who introduces an internal symbol for a private sensation possesses no criterion of correct reapplication independent of seeming correct, so the distinction between correct use and mere appearance collapses.
(2) First-person authority about one's own mental states—whether one is in pain, for example—is real and is not reducible to behavioral or dispositional criteria.
(3) The analogy between mental-state reporting and scientific measurement is sound in one respect: both require calibration against shared standards, and without intersubjective check there is no operational difference between a miscalibrated instrument and one that is "off" in an undetectable, private way.
(4) Phenomenal concepts genuinely refer to private, subjective qualities—there is something it is like for me to feel this particular ache—that public language does not fully capture.
(5) A subject may legitimately consult memory of past mental states as an internal check—like a researcher consulting her own dated lab notebook—to correct or confirm present reports.
(6) First-person error is possible: I can sincerely and directly report being in pain when I am not, or overlook a mild discomfort that is actually present.

Please be committed in your answer: do not present a menu of equally viable revisions. State which pair is in the worst tension, which conflict is only apparent, and what the least damaging single change to this set is.
