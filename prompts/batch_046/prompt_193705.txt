ok so i've been trying to sort out my views on weakness of will and hyperbolic discounting and i'm not sure they hang together. here's roughly where i'm at:

1) i think akrasia only counts if you have an all-things-considered judgment that X is best and you still do not-X. if your preferences just shift over time thats not weakness, thats just changing your mind.

2) i buy the hyperbolic discounting model - people don't discount exponentially, they discount steeply near the present. but i think it's mostly descriptive, a bias, not something normative.

3) still, i think sometimes it's rational to weight near-future outcomes more heavily if your future preferences are genuinely constructed in the moment rather than revealed. so hyperbolic weighting isn't always a mistake.

4) i think the best fix for procrastination is Ulysses contracts - locking yourself in so your future self can't back out.

5) but i also think my future self isn't really "me" in the same practical sense, so binding them feels more like coercing a roommate than actual self-control.

6) and i think if you genuinely judge the salad is best but reach for fries anyway, thats guilt-worthy akrasia. but if you just overvalue the fries because of the hyperbolic curve, that's not guilt-worthy, thier just being human.

do these hang together? i feel like there's hidden tension in the middle. for one thing, doesn't (1) assume there's a stable "all-things-considered" judgment across time, which (3) and (5) seem to deny? and is (4) consistent with (5) - if my future self is like a roommate, why am i coercing them instead of bargaining? also, (2) and (3) look like they clash - is hyperbolic discounting a bias or can it be rational?

can you audit this? rank the tensions by severity, say what has to give in each case, and tell me if any of the clashes i'm seeing are only apparent and dissolve once you look closer. also, if there's a hidden contradiction i'm missing, flag it.
