student answer:

Moore's paradox comes from G.E. Moore, who noticed that sentences with the form p but i dont belive p feel wrong even if theyre logically consistent. For example, it might actually be raining and i might genuinely not know, so the content isnt contradictory. Moore himself explained that this happens because asserting p automatically expresses belief in p, so saying both together is like contradicting yourself. This is why past tense versions are fine — saying it was raining but i didnt belive it — and why saying it about someone else works too. The same reasoning covers the commissive form where i believe not-p, since both clash with my assertion of p. Regarding AI, since large language models lack any belief state, they can generate these sentences with no paradox at all; the absurdity only arises for agents who actually hold beliefs, so the problem doesnt apply to machines.
