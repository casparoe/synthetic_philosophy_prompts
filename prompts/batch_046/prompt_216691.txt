Present it as a polished, bureaucratic document: numbered sections, submission checklists, footnotes in a smaller voice, and the dry tone of a university press style guide.

The satire should target the institutional domestication of paraconsistent logic, particularly the move from treating contradiction as catastrophic error to treating it as a manageable, even productive, administrative resource. Exaggerate this by requiring authors to submit “Contradiction Impact Assessments” with every manuscript, distinguishing between “load-bearing contradictions” (those essential to the argument’s structure) and “ornamental inconsistencies” (those included for stylistic effect only). Invent a fictional office—the Paraconsistency Compliance Unit—that audits submissions for proper formatting of true contradictions according to the press’s proprietary dialetheic style guide.

Ground the humour in genuine logical distinctions. Without quoting any real philosopher, allude to the difference between dialetheism proper (the claim that some contradictions are literally true) and mere paraconsistency (the denial that contradiction necessarily entails triviality via the principle of explosion, ex contradictione quodlibet). Refer to Graham Priest’s defence of dialetheism only as an historical footnote in the press’s founding charter, avoiding any invented publication dates, page numbers, or direct quotations. Let the principle of explosion be suspended in the document not as a philosophical thesis but as a cost-cutting measure—“triviality containment protocols” are expensive to maintain.

The piece should run to approximately 650 words. It must remain readable to a non-specialist, yet precise enough that a logician recognises you understand relevance logic, dialetheic negation, and the distinction between rejecting explosion and embracing true contradictions. Maintain sustained irony: never wink, never break character, yet ensure the reader can tell exactly what is being ironised.

Write in British English, in a formal, polished register. Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the document should cite only its own internal memoranda and fictional compliance officers.
