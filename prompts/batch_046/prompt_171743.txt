I have been reading about the problem of mental causation in my spare time, and I think I understand the basic puzzle: if every physical event already has a complete physical cause, then mental properties—if they are not identical to physical ones—seem causally redundant, like a shadow that cannot move the object it accompanies. Rather than asking for a literature survey, I want to test my own understanding by asking you to evaluate six proposed escape routes that I have sketched out below.

Please rank the following strategies from most defensible to least defensible in preserving genuine mental causation without fully abandoning physicalism. You may group them into tiers if some feel roughly equivalent.

(A) Identity or realization: mental properties are not ontologically separate from the physical properties that realize them, so there is only one causal chain with two descriptions, not two competing causes.

(B) Higher-level efficacy: mental properties make no difference at the fundamental physical level, but they are genuinely causal at macroscopic scales where physical descriptions are computationally or conceptually intractable, analogous to how software can cause hardware behavior without violating physics.

(C) Physical incompleteness: the closure of the physical domain is false because quantum-level indeterminacies create causal openings that mental properties can systematically fill.

(D) Systematic overdetermination: mental and physical causes both operate, producing redundant but real causal paths; the mental cause is not preempted because both are necessary for the full explanation of the effect.

(E) Proportionality without competition: mental properties cause effects in proportion to their specificity, and physical properties—being overly broad—do not actually compete with them; the exclusion argument assumes a false principle that more specific causes are always preempted by broader ones.

(F) Eliminativism about mental properties: mental language tracks useful patterns but there are no mental properties with causal powers; we preserve mental talk as a heuristic while accepting that only physical properties do the work.

For each tier or rank, explain in a sentence or two why it lands there. Then please be explicit about what would change your mind: identify the specific empirical result or philosophical argument—for instance, a demonstration that downward causation violates energy conservation, or a proof that proportionality fails in all non-mental domains—that would cause you to reorder your evaluation. Finally, flag the point in your reasoning that you consider weakest, especially regarding strategy (E), since I am not fully sure whether proportionality can really block exclusion without collapsing back into (A).
