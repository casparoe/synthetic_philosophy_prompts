The appendix in humans: I think it's current function is immune support, not digestion, since that's what it does now even if that's not it's original purpose. A tumor that secretes insulin and accidentally saves the host from diabetes: definitely not the tumor's function, that's just malfunction with a lucky side-effect. But then there's bacteria with a mutated enzyme that can't do it's ancestral job anymore, yet the mutation lets them digest plastic, and now they're selected for in polluted environments. I want to say plastic digestion IS the enzyme's function now. The human chin has no function—pure spandrel from jaw reduction. A synthetic gene drive inserted into mosquitoes to suppress reproduction: I'd say suppressing reproduction IS it's function, artificial or not. But I'm torn about a heart removed for transplant and kept pumping in a machine—is it still functioning, or does function require being inside a living organism? And there's a pseudogene everyone thought was junk, but it makes a protein that blocks a specific virus. Is viral defense it's function?

So is there a single principle—maybe mixing etiological and causal-role conditions—that saves all these? Or do I have to sacrifice the tumor intuition to save the bacteria one, or vice versa?
