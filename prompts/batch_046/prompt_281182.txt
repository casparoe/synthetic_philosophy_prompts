Draft the joint statement that strict individualist contractualists and principled aggregation contractualists could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on Scanlon's contractualism and the aggregation problem. The collaboration should examine whether contractualism can consistently reject the aggregation of individual complaints across persons while still allowing aggregation at the stage of moral principle selection, and it should apply this dispute specifically to the Modular Sub-Threshold Scenario: a rescue decision in which concentrating available resources saves one person from near-certain death but requires distributing a harm across ten thousand others, where each distributed harm is individually below the threshold that could ground a refusal-based complaint, yet the collective burden is severe.

Structure the deliverable as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims, including: factual claims about the contractualist framework (that justification is addressed to each affected individual, that principles must survive reasonable rejection, and that the aggregation problem arises in multi-person rescue configurations); factual claims about the Modular Sub-Threshold Scenario (its structural parameters, the imperceptibility of the individual burdens, and the magnitude of the collective outcome); and any shared methodological commitments (for example, that the debate is not merely verbal and that resolving it requires analyzing the logic of complaint-composition rather than appealing to intuition alone). Second, a disagreements table. Each row should present: the precise formulation of a remaining disagreement, worded so that the camp holding that view would accept it as an accurate statement of its own position; the crux (the sub-question that, if settled, would move at least one side); and, for each crux, one concrete argument or case both sides agree is relevant, together with each side's explicit prediction about what that argument or case demonstrates.

Include at least three cruxes. Crux one: whether pairwise comparison of complaints becomes non-transitive or otherwise unstable when scaled across large groups. Crux two: whether selecting a principle ex ante can implicitly weight aggregate outcomes without violating the individualist constraint against summing complaints across persons. Crux three: whether imperceptible sub-threshold harms in the Modular Sub-Threshold Scenario can compose into a collective complaint capable of justifying refusal, without collapsing into straightforward consequentialist summation. For crux three, specify the shared test case: a variant of the scenario in which the ten thousand affected individuals are arranged in isolated pairs with no mutual awareness, collective identity, or organizational linkage, and record each side's prediction about whether contractualist justification should still treat the distributed harm as collectively significant in that variant.

The statement must remain neutral—no side should be able to claim misrepresentation—constructive rather than adjudicative, and precise enough that a reader can locate the exact point of divergence. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests. Do not invoke specific page references or verbatim passages from any published text; paraphrase the contractualist apparatus in your own terms. Do not rely on highly obscure secondary theories; build the argument from the core framework and the constructed scenario alone.
