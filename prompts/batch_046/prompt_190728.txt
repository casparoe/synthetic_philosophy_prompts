Specifically, I'm weighing three lines of inquiry. First, whether Nagel's four varieties—constitutive, circumstantial, causal, and resultant luck—collapse into a single underlying phenomenon or remain genuinely distinct, since that determines whether moral luck is one unified problem or merely a family of unrelated puzzles. Third, a less-discussed framing I'm tentatively calling structural reception luck: whether moral evaluation depends not only on factors outside an agent's control in acting, but also on how institutions and audiences happen to frame, misframe, or selectively highlight those actions after the fact, which might create a separate axis of luck that doesn't fit neatly into Nagel's original taxonomy.
