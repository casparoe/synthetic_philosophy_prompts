I'm trying to determine the exact conditions under which a symbol genuinely drives physical substrate change, using a hypothetical framework I call careenium mechanics. A careenium is a lattice of interlocking micro-mechanical nodes capable of configuration shifts; a simmball is a discrete token that can be embedded in or detached from that lattice, bearing a syntactic inscription. Here are my concrete verdicts on seven cases, and I want to know whether a coherent principle captures them all.

Case 1: A functional careenium receives an embedded simmball inscribed ROTATE-LEFT; the lattice rotates left. Verdict: the symbol pushed the substrate.

Case 2: The identical simmball is placed adjacent but not embedded; the careenium remains still. Verdict: proximity without embedding produces no causal push.

Case 3: The careenium rotates left due to thermal noise, and afterward an observer embeds a ROTATE-LEFT simmball to record the event. Verdict: the symbol describes the motion but exerts no causal force on it.

Case 4: Two identical careenia receive embedded ROTATE-LEFT and ROTATE-RIGHT simmballs respectively, and rotate in opposite directions. Verdict: the specific syntactic content determines the substrate transition, confirming genuine symbol-driven causation.

Case 5: A ROTATE-LEFT simmball is embedded, but the careenium's drive nodes are mechanically broken; nothing moves. Verdict: the symbol is present but fails to push, because substrate readiness is necessary.

Case 6: The careenium is broken to always rotate left regardless of input, and an embedded ROTATE-RIGHT simmball is present; the lattice still rotates left. Verdict: here the substrate overrides the symbol's content, so the symbol is causally inert despite being embedded.

Case 7: A simmball bears the ambiguous inscription ROTATE, and the surrounding node-state of the careenium resolves it to left rotation. Verdict: I feel torn—both the symbol and the substrate context are necessary, but I cannot tell whether the symbol pushed anything specific, since without that context the inscription alone would not have determined left over right.

My difficulty is that Cases 1 and 4 suggest content-specific counterfactual dependence, Case 5 makes substrate conditions necessary, Case 6 allows substrate conditions to nullify content, and Case 7 looks like joint causation without a dominant symbol. Is there a precise principle—perhaps involving counterfactual dependence on syntactic form given fixed substrate conditions—that saves Cases 1 through 6 but forces me to overturn my ambivalence in Case 7? Or must I accept a more complex, disjunctive account?

Please state your principle precisely, indicate which clause is motivated by which case, predict its verdict for an unlisted scenario (a simmball is embedded with ROTATE-RIGHT, but thermal noise alters its inscription to ROTATE-LEFT after embedding but before the rotation completes), and tell me honestly which of my seven verdicts the principle cannot accommodate and whether sacrificing that verdict is worth the gain in unity.
