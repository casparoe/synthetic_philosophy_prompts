A philosophy podcast I listened to summarized illusionism about phenomenal consciousness in the following way: "Illusionists like Keith Frankish and Daniel Dennett maintain that phenomenal consciousness—the qualitative what-it-is-likeness of experience—is not a real feature of the world but a kind of fiction our brains construct. There are no actual phenomenal properties, such as the redness of red or the painfulness of pain; there is only access-conscious information processing together with a false meta-representation that these processes have an intrinsic qualitative character. This dissolves David Chalmers's hard problem, because once you see that phenomenal properties never existed, neuroscience only needs to explain why we generate that particular false report, not explain how physical processes produce real qualitative feels. The mechanism, according to this view, involves representational transparency applied to introspection: just as a transparent visual medium hides its own representational structure and lets us seem to see the world directly, introspection hides its own representational structure and lets us seem to encounter qualitative properties directly. Critics often reply that if consciousness is an illusion, there must be a conscious subject who is being fooled, but illusionists answer that the subject is nothing over and above the very information-processing system that produces the false report. The podcast concluded that illusionism is therefore the only position fully consistent with physicalism, and that anyone who insists on real phenomenal properties is implicitly committed to some form of property dualism."
