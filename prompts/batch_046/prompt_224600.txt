I am drafting an essay on hierarchical failure modes that extends Hofstadter's distinction between clean hierarchies and strange loops. The standard strange loop—as in Escher's Drawing Hands—involves a direct violation of an inviolate level separation: the plane that draws and the plane that is drawn collapse into one another. I want to examine a different failure mode, which I am calling interpretive dissolution, in which the boundary layer remains structurally intact but loses its executive authority because its content becomes subject to reinterpretation by the very system it constrains. In institutional terms, this is the gap between a constitution being formally supreme and being semantically hollowed out by the institution that interprets it; in logical terms, it resembles a meta-language that is syntactically fixed but pragmatically variable.

My essay proposes a trichotomy: (A) stable hierarchy with an inviolate level, (B) Hofstadter-style strange loop with explicit level-crossing, and (C) interpretive dissolution, where the loop is hidden inside the boundary layer rather than crossing it. I am using Drawing Hands as the image for (B), but I need to know whether (C) is genuinely distinct from a disguised version of (B), and whether there is an established term for a boundary that is structurally present but functionally porous. I am not asking for references, citations, or reading lists; I want conceptual sharpening only.
