I am constructing a decision-tree taxonomy for a long-form post aimed at rationalist and effective-altruism readers who have followed debates on wild-animal suffering and artificial sentience but are not philosophers of mind by training. The subject is gradated moral status: how philosophical positions distribute concern across entities that fall between unambiguous moral patients and unambiguous objects, with particular attention to insects, adaptive thermostats, and synthetic architectures that sit in the blurry middle. I need the tree built from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of named theories, so that a reader can locate their view by answering concrete diagnostic questions without knowing what label attaches to it.

The root split should ask whether phenomenal consciousness is necessary for any non-derivative moral status, or whether functional analogues of aversion and reward—described below—can ground partial status on their own. From there, branch on whether status is binary or genuinely continuous; if continuous, whether the gradient tracks integrated information, behavioral flexibility, or evolutionary relatedness. At each internal node, place a concrete case that forces separation: a nematode with nociception but no central integration; a thermostat running predictive error-minimization without any affective valence; a hypothetical fragmented valence architecture, meaning a distributed system in which localized negative-feedback loops never converge into a global workspace or unified self-model, so that each subsystem behaves like an isolated pain module without a single subject that suffers; and a split-brain human with hemispheres that show independent avoidance behavior.

Each leaf must carry either the name of a real position if one occupies it, or the marker unnamed, followed by one sentence describing its most awkward commitment—such as being forced to deny concern for sleeping humans, or being forced to extend partial concern to well-tuned climate-control systems. The tree should explicitly flag pairs of positions it cannot separate on these cases, explaining whether the overlap is substantive or merely an artifact of the cases chosen. Unoccupied combinations of answers should be noted and briefly diagnosed: is the gap incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored?

Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests; do not ask me to verify titles, dates, or attributions; and do not invoke obscure named frameworks that a well-read but non-specialist model could not be expected to have memorized—describe any needed technical distinctions inside the taxonomy itself. The deliverable should be formal and polished, sized so that there are roughly eight to ten terminal leaves, and written so that someone answering the tree needs only their own intuitions about the cases, not prior knowledge of the literature.
