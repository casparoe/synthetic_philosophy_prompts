You are a professional philosopher of democracy and a literary fiction writer specializing in conceptual narratives. Before composing, please take a moment to brainstorm carefully: list three distinct ways the boundary problem—who belongs to the demos—could become physically and ethically concrete in a closed, non-terrestrial environment, and note which of them best exposes the tension between membership and legitimacy. Once you have done that, write a polished short story of approximately 800–1000 words that dramatizes this problem without resolving it.

Set the narrative in the Bathysphere Consortium, a semi-autonomous deep-sea research station governed by a direct-democracy protocol called "the Voice." The station's recognized members include human researchers, a small population of engineered cephalopods employed as environmental sensors, and an integrated advisory architecture—not a person, but a persistent decision-weighting system with encoded preferences for long-term ecological stability. When a structural failure forces an immediate vote on sealing an external vent (preserving energy but destroying the local bioluminescent ecosystem that sustains both the cephalopods and the advisory system's calibration baseline), the community must decide not only what to do, but who is entitled to decide.

Structure the story around three characters who embody competing principles: one invoking the "all-subjected" criterion (only beings bound by the outcome should vote), one invoking the "all-affected" criterion (any entity whose welfare is altered by the outcome should be included), and one raising the recursive legitimation problem—that the demos cannot neutrally determine its own membership because the membership determines the decision. Include a scene in which one character deploys a familiar analogy—comparing the polity to a biological organism or a ship's crew—and another demonstrates precisely where that analogy collapses when applied to entities with different lifespans, sensory modalities, or absence of linguistic reciprocity. Use precise philosophical vocabulary (demos, boundary problem, affected interests, recursive legitimation) within an intimate, human-scale drama. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts. Conclude with the vote suspended, the boundary still contested, and at least one character recognizing that the analogy they relied on was carrying more normative weight than they admitted.
