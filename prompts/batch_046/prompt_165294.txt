Compare epistemicism and supervaluationism about vagueness, but restrict the comparison to normative status predicates—specifically whether an artificial system with advanced synthetic cognition but no biological substrate falls under person, moral agent, or rights-bearer—rather than to standard observational predicates like bald or tall. For each view, explain how it treats the borderline case: does the epistemicist claim that there is a sharp but unknown boundary in the properties that ground these statuses, and is that claim more or less plausible when the grounding properties are themselves conceptually contested rather than empirically hidden? Does supervaluationism’s strategy of admissible precisifications resolve disagreement about such synthetic entities, or does it merely relocate the dispute to the choice of precisification? Then assess which framework offers the more coherent account for legal and moral adjudication in these cases, and identify the specific objection—whether arising from semantic indeterminacy or from the practical demand for decisive judgment—that poses the sharper challenge to each view.
