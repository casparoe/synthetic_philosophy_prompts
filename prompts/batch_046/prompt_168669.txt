I'm taking a philoshophy of mind module and we got onto Black's spheres in the context of Leibniz's principle of indiscernibles. I tried to extend the idea to mental properties and now I've got an argument that seems to show phenomenal duplication is literally impossible, which feels like it's proving way too much. Here are the steps I wrote down:

(1) Black's two spheres share every qualitative property — size, colour, internal structure, and even relational ones like "being two metres from a sphere of radius R" — yet they're numerically distinct, so qualitative identity doesn't entail numerical identity.

(2) Apply the same structure to minds: imagine two people, call them Twin-A and Twin-B, with identical beliefs, sensations, and psycholgical histories.

(3) If they're still numerically distinct, there must be some property one has and the other lacks.

(4) The only candidate is an indexical property like "being me" or "being the subject of this very thought."

(5) But indexical properties aren't qualitative in the relevant sense; they're primitive thisnesses disguised as mental content.

(6) So Twin-A and Twin-B don't actually share all mental properties — each has a unique self-referential property the other can't possess.

(7) Therefore genuinely identical mental lives across distinct subjects are impossible; phenomenal duplication is incoherent.

(8) If duplication is incoherent, then psychological-continuity theories of personal identity collapse, because they depend on the possibility that my mental life could be continued in another body or another subject.

(9) But the argument also seems to imply that no two people can ever be mentally alike in any deep way, since any shared mental content is always undercut by divergent indexical framing.

(10) That can't be right — twins and close friends clearly share mental states in meaningful ways.

So I think one of these steps is sneaking in an equivication between qualitative mental content and the indexical mode of presentation, but I can't quite locate it. Which premise should I give up, and what does rejecting it cost? I'd especially like to keep (1) and (8) if possible, but I'm open to paying elsewhere.
