Brentano died in 1917, long before either the Chinese Room argument or the development of computational theories of mind, yet his insistence that mental acts possess an intrinsic, irreducible directedness—what he called the intentional relation—seems directly relevant.

Specifically, I want you to predict Brentano's likely response if he were presented with the following modified scenario: imagine not a single wandering two-bitser whose state-meanings shift with environmental context, but a distributed network of such minimal systems embedded in a shared ecological niche, where no individual node assigns meaning to another, yet the collective behavior of the network tracks environmental features in a way that allows an external observer to interpret the whole as representing states of affairs. Dennett might argue that this network exemplifies purely derived intentionality, since each node's contribution is physically identical regardless of interpretation and the representational content depends entirely on evolutionary or design history. Searle, by contrast, might contend that unless at least one component of the network possesses original intentionality—an intrinsic aboutness rooted in biological or mental substance—the collective still lacks genuine representation. Given Brentano's commitment to the view that intentionality is the mark of the mental and cannot be reduced to physical relations or external interpretation, how would he likely adjudicate this dispute? Would he deny that the distributed network has any intentional states at all, reserving original intentionality for unified psychic acts? Or might he allow that collective systems could possess a derivative form only if they are causally integrated into the intentional life of an organism that already has original intentionality? Please develop your prediction in detail, addressing specifically how Brentano's rejection of physicalist reductionism interacts with Dennett's claim that the two-bitser's internal nature never changes even as its meaning does, and whether Brentano would see the ecological embedding of the network as altering the ontological status of the derived meanings assigned to its parts. Reach a clear conclusion about where Brentano would stand, and justify it by appealing to the core tenets of his empirical psychology rather than to anachronistic assumptions.
