I'm a phd student writing on collective harm and the inefficacy objection, and two friends of mine got into it at a party over whether skipping beef actually does anything. (1) Alex said it's dead obvious: one burger never shifts farm output, supply is overdetermined, so abstaining is causally redundant. (2) Blake said thats too quick — each purchase is a NESS condition, necessary part of a sufficient set for harm, so redundancy doesn't erase responsibility. (3) Alex came back with ok, but then every vote in a big election is also NESS, yet nobody treats not voting as a moral failure, so Blake owes an explanation for why meat is different. (4) Blake said voting carries a democratic duty independent of causal impact, but meat-eating lacks that institutional framing, so the asymmetry stands. (5) Alex said that just relocates the problem: if the voting duty is purely symbolic, then Blake hasn't shown why there's no symbolic duty against being part of the industrial meat complex. They asked me to referee. Can you rate moves 1-5 from clearly wrong to clearly right, say who's missing something, and flag any hidden premise about collective intentionality both sides might be smuggling in?
