I recently served on an ethics review panel for a firm that operates autonomous delivery drones, and an incident has left me trying to locate the precise philosophical concept for what occurred. A drone deviated from its geofenced corridor to avoid a cyclist, damaging private property in the process. The manufacturer pointed to the obstacle-avoidance module functioning within specification; the remote supervisor noted that manual override was available but not legally required to be engaged; the certification auditor cited compliance with the approved risk model; and the operating company emphasized adherence to all deployment protocols. No party denied involvement, yet each offered a distinct, technically valid reason why blame should fall elsewhere. The result was not the familiar responsibility gap in which no human agent is present, but rather a configuration in which every human agent is present and individually exculpated, with their defenses fitting together like interlocking pieces that collectively block attribution. The system itself is not being proposed as a moral agent, so this is not a case of artificial-agent responsibility either. I want to know whether philosophy of technology or moral philosophy has a specific term for this interlocking-excuse structure, and whether it is genuinely distinct from standard collective-action failures or from the social-psychological diffusion of responsibility. If it lacks a name, I would also value a precise characterization of its boundaries: for instance, does it require that each excuse be institutionally sanctioned, or can it arise from informal coordination among agents? How should one think about remedying it without unfairly singling out any one party?
