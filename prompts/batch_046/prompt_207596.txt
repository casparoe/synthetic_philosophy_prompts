Analyze Einstein's refusal of the Israeli presidency in November 1952 as a case study in the consistency of his Spinozist determinism. Einstein maintained that free will is an illusion sustained only by human ignorance of causal chains, and he replaced belief in a personal God with what he called a cosmic religious feeling rooted in the rational harmony of nature. Apply this framework precisely to the refusal: when Einstein told Ben-Gurion that he lacked the natural aptitude and experience to deal properly with people and to exercise official functions, was he describing a causally determined outcome that he could no more alter than the orbit of a planet, or was he invoking a rational autonomy that his metaphysics denies? Examine whether his deterministic worldview undermines the moral weight he clearly attached to the decision—particularly his lifelong pacifism and his sense that accepting the office would violate a destiny shaped by universal law rather than by choice. Assess whether the episode supports reading Einstein's determinism as a genuinely lived philosophical commitment that structured his ethical self-understanding, or whether it functioned mainly as a methodological stance for physics that he quietly suspended in practical life.
