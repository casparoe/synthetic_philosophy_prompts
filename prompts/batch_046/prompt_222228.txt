Construct a decision-tree taxonomy of philosophical positions on the tension between the inside view and the outside view, together with the demands of epistemic modesty. The root question should ask whether a reasoner may ever legitimately privilege detailed reasoning about the particular features of a case over reasoning drawn from a reference class of apparently similar cases.

Each branching node must take the form of a concrete diagnostic case or claim—phrased as a question answerable without knowing the philosophical labels—so that the tree sorts respondents by their substantive commitments rather than by the names they favour. The tree must include the following three cases as nodes: first, whether you should lower your confidence that you will finish a multi-year personal project when the base rate for people with your credentials is poor, despite your intimate knowledge of your own motivation and circumstances; second, whether you should expect a specific constitutional reform to reduce corruption when comparable reforms in other jurisdictions have mostly failed, but you grasp the unique institutional mechanics of this case; third, how you should respond to a disagreement between two equally credentialed experts on climate policy when you can follow the reasoning of only one side.

The leaves must represent named positions, but build the taxonomy from the logical space: present the branching questions and their combinations first, then locate the actual positions within that space. The tree must accommodate at least these views: hard outside-view primacy; hard inside-view primacy; domain-partitioned contextualism, which distinguishes predictive from normative or institutional judgements; skeptical dissolution, which holds that every so-called outside view is merely another agent's inside view and the distinction therefore collapses; modest integrationism, which holds that both inputs carry weight but that conflict requires downward adjustment of confidence rather than selection of one view; and reference-class voluntarism, which holds that the choice of reference class is itself an inside-view decision, leaving no genuinely independent outside check.

For every combination of answers that yields no named position, provide a brief diagnosis of whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Specifically, flag whether the tree can genuinely separate the skeptical dissolutionist from the reference-class voluntarist; if it cannot, state precisely what additional diagnostic question would distinguish them. The tree should contain between six and eight leaves.

Write for an audience of advanced undergraduates or intellectually engaged readers who are not expected to supply citations, bibliographies, or reading lists. Do not ask for references to specific authors or texts. Use British English spelling and punctuation throughout.
