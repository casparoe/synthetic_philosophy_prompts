I am a novelist serving as the literary representative on the National Commission for Biotechnology and Human Flourishing. By month's end we must circulate a formal opinion to the Minister of Health regarding Proposal E-47, the Abolitionist Germline Initiative, which petitions for state-funded, voluntary embryonic editing of polymorphisms associated with nociceptive amplification and chronic dysphoria, with the stated long-term objective of rendering both physical and existential suffering biologically obsolete within three generations. Proponents invoke the hedonistic imperative—the claim that biotechnology ought not merely treat pathology but engineer the absence of suffering itself—while dissenting commissioners contend that the proposal collapses therapy into enhancement, risks generating a population deprived of the contrastive experience that underwrites moral agency and narrative identity, and tacitly embraces a negative-utilitarian calculus that equates all negative valence with disvalue regardless of its constitutive function in human achievement. I ask for the Commission's decision memo—approve, reject, or approve with stringent conditions—including an actionable bottom line, a concrete implementation or moratorium time frame, and reasoning that gives full weight to both the abolitionist position that suffering is an unalloyed evil and the strongest opposing view that a life stripped of suffering's possibility may represent not an improved human condition but a diminished one. Specifically, the memo should address whether experiential—not merely propositional—knowledge of suffering is necessary for the moral and aesthetic understanding presumed by democratic citizenship and literary culture, and it should propose precise safeguards or prohibitions that do not rest on unverified empirical predictions about future generations. The document should be written in a formal, polished register appropriate for institutional stakeholders and should conclude with a recommendation the Commission could adopt without further revision.
