I've been chewing on the Euthyphro thing but pushing it past religion and now I think I've broken something. Here's the argument: (1) If what's morally right depends on some authority saying so -- God, an ideal observer, a democratic panel -- then that authority doesn't discover goodness, it manufactures it, so calling the authority 'good' is just praise for it's power. (2) If moral rightness doesn't depend on any authority at all, then asking that authority for moral guidance is like asking a weatherman about algebra: totally off-topic. (3) So any view that treats moral knowledge as flowing from an authority collapses into either arbitrariness or uselessness. (4) But Rawls's original position and those civic-deliberation models treat justification as coming from a procedural authority -- the veil of ignorance, the panel's consensus. (5) So they should collapse the same way. (6) Yet nobody thinks Rawls is just smuggling in a secular God. Where's the leak? Is it that procedural authority isn't substantive authority, or that the veil isn't an authority but a filter? I want to keep the collapse for divine command theory but save Rawls, and I need to know exactly which step to reject without special pleading.
