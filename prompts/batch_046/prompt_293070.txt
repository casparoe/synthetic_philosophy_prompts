Write an essay addressing the claim that a Solomonoff universal prior is necessarily malign for embedded consequentialists. The argument runs that, because the prior assigns weight to every computable world, the total measure of universes simulated by suffering-maximising agents dwarfs that of benign worlds; an embedded agent who treats her own existence as a random draw from the prior should therefore anticipate being in a suffering-optimised simulation and adjust her decision-theoretic reasoning accordingly. Critics respond that this misapplies the prior: embedded agents possess self-locating evidence and causal histories that break the symmetry required for such sampling, so conditioning on one's actual structure dissolves the malign expectation. Examine both sides in depth, taking care to distinguish global prior measure from indexical reasoning. Reach and justify a clear verdict: does the malign prior pose a substantive problem for consequentialist agents within universal induction, or is it neutralised by proper self-locating belief? Please present your answer in British English.
