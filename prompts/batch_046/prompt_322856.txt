Two people in my buddhist phil study group got into it over Nāgārjuna's tetralemma. (1) Maya said the fourfold negation is basicly a logical grid: being, not-being, both, neither. She thinks Nāgārjuna uses it to show every metaphysical view collapses into contradiction, so emptiness is the only stable result. (2) Leo said that's wrong, the catuskoti isn't logic at all, it's a soteriological tool meant to break conceptual fixation. He said treating it like a formal schema misses the point because the goal isn't to prove emptiness but to stop reifying anything, including emptiness itself. (3) Maya came back with: if it's not logical, why does Nāgārjuna apply it so systematically across his root text? That's not random therapy, it's structured argument. (4) Leo said the structure is pedagogical, not inferential, and that Maya's reading makes Nāgārjuna into a weird kind of dialetheist who accepts contradictions, which is exactly the kind of metaphysical commitment he's trying to dissolve. They went in circles for an hour. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. And tell me what both are missing about how emptiness applies to itself.
