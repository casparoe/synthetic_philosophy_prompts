Write a dialogue between a Kantian international-relations theorist and a realist critic. They should debate whether Kant's federation of free republics, as outlined in his essay on perpetual peace, could legitimately admit a member-state governed by direct democracy rather than representative institutions, given Kant's explicit preference for a republican (representative) constitution with separated powers. The Kantian should defend admission by appealing to Kant's distinction between the form of sovereignty and the form of government—arguing that a democratic sovereignty can still be paired with republican government—while the realist should counter that direct democracy collapses legislative and executive authority, thereby destroying both the internal stability and the peaceful external orientation Kant requires. Each speaker should respond directly to the other's specific claims about the link between internal constitutional structure and the propensity for war, rather than talking past one another.
