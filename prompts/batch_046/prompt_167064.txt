been thinking about moral particularism vs generalism and there's this pattern I keep seeing. someone proposes a neat principle like 'deception is wrong', particularists throw in counterexamples, so the generalist adds clauses for emergencies, then more edge cases show up, and eventually the principle is so loaded with conditions that it's basically just a long description of the original scenario with a normative label slapped on. it's not just that principles are complex -- it feels like generalism is overfitting to avoid being falsified. I know Dancy has reason holism where reasons flip polarity across contexts, but this seems different: it's the principle itself collapsing into a case report. does this specific failure mode have an established name? and if not, what exactly distinguishes it from a genuinely complex but still general principle, versus just particularism wearing a rule-shaped mask?
