I sit on a university board that reviews institutional proposals for potential violations of liberty, treating liberty in the philosophical sense that encompasses negative, positive, and republican conceptions. Our track record is as follows. We approved a corporate wellness program that offers reduced health premiums to employees who meet biometric targets, where the targets are set by a published algorithm, results are reviewed by an independent appeals panel, and employees may opt out at any time without losing base coverage; we found no liberty violation. We rejected a residential lease contract, operating in a severely supply-constrained housing market, that permits the landlord to terminate the lease or alter terms with twenty-four hours' notice for any reason and provides no appeal mechanism; we found a liberty violation. We approved with conditions a state education mandate requiring all secondary students to complete a standardized civic literacy module, on the ground that students with religious or philosophical objections may substitute an equivalent independent study program of comparable rigor; we found no liberty violation. We rejected a private debt-relief program that requires participants to transfer all financial decision-making authority to an appointed trustee for five years, with the trustee exercising sole discretion and withdrawal permitted only upon repayment of all forgiven debt with interest; we found a liberty violation. We approved a municipal ordinance requiring new multi-family developments to include affordable housing units according to a fixed statutory formula, noting that no individual official possesses discretion over the allocation; we found no liberty violation.

The new proposal before us is a mandatory "cognitive flourishing" program for all citizens aged eighteen to twenty-five. Participants must enroll in an AI-guided system that personalizes recommendations for sleep, consumption, social interaction, and information diet according to a democratically ratified framework of well-being. The system has no human operator, adapts continuously to each user's stated long-term goals, and operates without discretionary human intervention. Participants may exit without financial penalty at any time, but while enrolled they are contractually bound to follow the system's recommendations. The state provides the service without charge and justifies the mandate as necessary to correct for systematically distorted preferences.

We need a decision that coheres with our own precedents: should we approve, reject, or approve with conditions? Identify the closest analog in our record and explain whether it genuinely determines the outcome. If our precedents underdetermine the case, state explicitly what principle we would be adopting with each possible choice. Then identify the two or three plausible rules—drawn from negative liberty, positive liberty, republican liberty, or a hybrid—that jointly fit all five of our earlier verdicts, say what each rule predicts about this proposal, and give your best-guess prediction of our verdict with the reasoning that favors it over the alternatives. Finally, specify one further hypothetical case that would clearly distinguish the leading rule from its nearest competitor. I am less interested in the bare answer than in the consistency reasoning, the rule extraction, and the diagnostic case.
