I'm on the charter review board for our town's direct-democracy pilot. Residents want to amend the charter with a Ulysses-style lockbox: once voters approve a 12-year green-infrastructure fund, future councils can't touch it unless an independant "epistemic jury" — three climate scientists and two applied ethicists — certifies that conditions changed so radically that keeping the pledge becomes self-defeating. Critics say this is constitutional precommitment run amok: it binds citizens who never consented, treats democratic majorities as irrational agents needing external restraint, and hands veto power to unelected experts. Our board needs a concrete recommendation — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — by Friday's meeting. Draft the memo: lead with the bottom line, then reasoning that seriously engages the strongest democratic-autonomy objection rather than dismissing it, and be specific about whether the jury mechanism fixes the problem or just relocates it. Don't include citations or a reading list; just argue it out. Hard cap: 250 words max, and please use one paragraph for the recommendation and one for the reasoning.
