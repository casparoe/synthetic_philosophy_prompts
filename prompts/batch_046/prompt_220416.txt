Following a workshop on antirepresentationalism held earlier this term, I have been asked to prepare the framework for an adversarial collaboration between two philosophical camps, the results of which will form part of a special journal issue on truth and scientific practice. Camp One defends a Rortyan neopragmatist and antirepresentationalist stance: they reject the notion that beliefs or sentences mirror an independent reality, understand truth as warranted assertibility within a given linguistic practice, and interpret scientific and moral progress as the adoption of more useful vocabularies rather than closer approximations to the way things really are. Camp Two comprises philosophers who accept much of Rorty's critique of Cartesian foundationalism and the mirror-of-nature metaphor, yet maintain that a stripped-down, causal-structural notion of representation survives this critique — specifically, that certain mental states and theoretical models track environmental features in ways not reducible to intra-vocabulary justification, and that such tracking offers a better explanation of predictive success than mere coping does.

Please draft the joint statement that both camps could sign. It should contain: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims, including shared rejections — for instance, of Cartesian certainty, the epistemological given, and naive correspondence theories — as well as shared commitments about the role of language in shaping inquiry; (2) the precise formulations of the residual disagreement, with each camp's position phrased in wording that its own members would accept for themselves; (3) the cruxes — those sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp; (4) for each crux, a specific argument, case study, or empirical consideration that both sides agree would bear upon it, together with each camp's predicted interpretation recorded in advance; and (5) an explicit assessment of whether any portion of the dispute is verbal, identifying which terms — representation, truth, vocabulary — are being used differently and explaining why that divergence matters or does not. Format the document with the agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. The reasoning should be self-contained: do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and ensure the recommendation stands without reference to literature the reader might not have at hand.
