I have just finished a long, frustrating argument with a close friend about whether citizens have genuinely stronger moral claims on one another than on strangers, and I want to stop debating the abstraction and build something I can actually use. I serve as a senior program officer for a global health foundation that must split a fixed annual grant pool between domestic health interventions and international ones; I face these allocations repeatedly, the board requires a written justification for each decision within thirty minutes, and I have no philosophical training beyond what I remember from an undergraduate course. I need a reusable decision procedure—an ordered checklist of questions, with criteria for what counts as a sufficient answer to each, explicit stop points where the answer must be no, explicit hand-off points where the remaining question is one of values rather than facts, and a clear flag for where the procedure will misfire or demand judgment that cannot be eliminated. The conceptual framing I want operationalized is not the standard “compatriots get extra weight” view, but a more specific, gate-based version of moderate associative duty: compatriot priority is not a constant distributive coefficient but a conditional gate that activates only when domestic institutions that citizens collectively maintain are failing to secure a baseline good for members, and even when activated it functions only as a lexical tie-breaker within a pre-committed budget band, never as a global multiplier over foreign lives. Please construct the procedure around that framing, apply it to three proposals—funding a domestic mental-health clinic serving uninsured citizens versus distributing malaria nets abroad; funding a domestic vaccination program for a rare but painful disease versus responding to an acute international outbreak; and a case where the domestic institutional failure gate is clearly not met but emotional and political pressure to fund domestically is intense—ensuring that at least one application yields an answer I will not like, and conclude by stating precisely what the procedure cannot settle and where an officer must still sign their own name to the values call. Lead with the bottom-line summary of the procedure’s core logic in the very first sentence, then present the reasoning and the worked cases with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
