Having spent three decades in experimental physics and only recently begun a serious study of philosophy of language, I am preparing the final examination for an advanced undergraduate seminar on speech acts and performative utterances. I wish to avoid the usual textbook recitation of Austin’s taxonomy or the familiar wedding-vow illustration. Instead, I want students to grapple with a problem I repeatedly observed in my former laboratory: when a principal investigator signs a calibration certificate declaring an instrument verified, or when a peer-review panel formally pronounces an experimental result replicable, these utterances appear to establish the institutional conditions under which subsequent measurements count as valid, rather than merely describing a pre-existing state of affairs. At the same time, their authority seems to depend on descriptive accuracy about precision and error margins.

Please compose a single essay question suitable for a 60-minute response of approximately 800 words. The question should ask students to determine whether such scientific certification utterances are genuine performatives in Austin’s framework—specifically, whether they satisfy the conventional-procedure and accepted-conventional-effect conditions—or whether they are ultimately constative reports that acquire institutional force only through separate administrative mechanisms. The prompt must require students to address: (1) the distinction between explicit and implicit performatives in institutional settings; (2) the problem of infelicity when the speaker lacks proper authority or when the underlying descriptive conditions fail; (3) whether Austin’s tendency to exclude scientific discourse from performative analysis can be sustained once institutional authority is taken seriously; and (4) whether treating certification as performative collapses the distinction between making a fact and reporting one in ways that threaten scientific objectivity.

Provide a model answer of about 700 words that executes the required conceptual moves, and a marking scheme totaling 25 marks distributed across five specific criteria. The scheme should clearly distinguish an A-level answer—which must identify the tension between Austin’s claim that performatives are neither true nor false and the scientific demand that certification remain answerable to descriptive accuracy—from a B-level answer that correctly applies Austin’s categories but misses this institutional-descriptive tension. For each criterion, state the marks available, what an excellent response contains, and the typical misconception that should cost marks.

The question should be answerable from a solid grasp of Austin’s core framework together with conceptual reasoning about scientific practice; do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and do not rely on obscure secondary literature.

Conclude your response with a single line beginning Summary: followed by one sentence stating the core philosophical tension examined.
