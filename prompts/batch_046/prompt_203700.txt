I am drafting a chapter on Galileo’s hermeneutics and have constructed an argument that seems to push his accommodation principle past its breaking point. Here is the chain: (1) In his Letter to the Grand Duchess Christina, Galileo distinguishes scripture’s soteriological authority from its use of accommodated, phenomenal language when describing nature. (2) He holds that when a natural conclusion is genuinely demonstrated—not merely probable—and conflicts with a literal scriptural reading, we must reinterpret that passage non-literally. (3) He treats heliocentrism as such a demonstrated conclusion, so Joshua 10:12-13 must be read phenomenologically rather than as a literal geocentric claim. (4) Apply (2) consistently: the bodily resurrection involves violations of biological decay and gravitational mechanics, processes that Galileo’s own standards of demonstration treat as regular and well-established. (5) So by (2), the resurrection narratives should also be accommodated—read as metaphorical or phenomenological—unless the analogy can be blocked. (6) Galileo clearly rejects this result; as a devout Catholic he affirms literal resurrection. (7) The standard block is that miracles are singular divine suspensions of natural law, not counter-examples to demonstrated science, which describes ordinary regularities. (8) But Galileo’s own text permits God to do what is naturally impossible; if so, one could invoke the same suspension for geocentrism (God could maintain a stationary earth despite mechanics), undermining the principled basis for accommodating Joshua. (9) Therefore either Galileo must apply (2) consistently and accept a non-literal resurrection, or he must abandon (2) and lose his defense of Copernicanism, since both cases involve physically anomalous events that God could cause. Which step should I reject? In particular, does (7) rely on a distinction between “natural law” and “miracle” that Galileo can maintain without begging the question against geocentrism, or is there a hidden equivocation in (4) or (8) that saves his position? If I want to keep (2) intact, what exactly am I paying?
