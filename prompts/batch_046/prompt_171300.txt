I'm trying to prep a discussion for my philosophy of mind group on the extended mind thesis, and I want to use Christopher Nolan's Memento (2000) as a kind of test case. Leonard Shelby's whole system — the Polaroids, the handwritten notes, the tattoos — looks like a textbook example of Clark and Chalmers' coupled system, except that in the film those external stores are constantly being tampered with, and Leonard himself writes false leads to keep his revenge going. So I'm wondering: does the film actually illustrate the parity principle, or does it dramatize exactly why the trust-and-glue conditions matter? Where Leonard's notes are automatically endorsed and easily accessible they seem like part of his mind, but when Natalie hides pages or Teddy lies, the vulnerability of the external store seems different in kind from internal deception. Does Memento confuse epistemic unreliability (not knowing what's true) with the metaphysical claim that cognition extends, or does it know the difference and use it? Also, there's that twist where Leonard deliberately creates a new false belief — is that self-deception in the usual sense, or something weirder because part of his mind is literally outside his skull and being rewritten by himself? I'd like a reading that doesn't reduce the movie to an illustration of Clark, and that also doesn't distort the thesis to fit it's plot. Specifically: which scenes make the extended mind idea look plausible, which ones resist it, and what does the film add to the philosophical debate that an armchair argument about Otto and Inga misses? I'm writing this up informally so feel free to be loose, but I want it honest — if the film is just using amnesia as a gimmick, say so.
