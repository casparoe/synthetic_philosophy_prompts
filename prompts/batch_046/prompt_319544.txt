I want to fix the standard of adequacy for any framework that tells an agent how to act when she faces deep consequentialist cluelessness — that is, when she cannot assign meaningful probabilities to the long-range outcomes that her action might set in motion. Before I survey particular proposals, I need the desiderata spelled out in one place. Specify what the framework must be able to do: which puzzles it must dissolve, which distinctions it must preserve, and which results it must avoid collapsing into, with the concrete case or consideration behind each requirement. Be sure to include not only first-order outcome ignorance but also recursive value drift, where the agent’s present evaluative commitments may not be endorsed by her future self, and inter-theoretic incommensurability, where competing outcomes resist comparison under any single metric. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, distinguish between those that are genuinely non-negotiable and those that are merely prejudices of a strictly maximising framing, and say what purpose the framework is meant to serve that makes one list more appropriate than another. Then, very briefly, indicate how a maximally robust strategy, a satisficing threshold model, and a virtue-based delegation approach fare against your list. Present everything as continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, and conclude with an explicit note on what specific argument or observed failure in practice would change your mind about which requirements are essential and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
