Here is roughly where I have landed on sphexishness, mechanical rigidity, and free will, and I want to know whether the set is jointly tenable. Thinking in software terms, I find it useful to split rigidity into architectural-level rules and implementation-level behavior. So:

(1) Sphexishness is layer-relative: a system can be architecturally rigid yet implementation-flexible, or vice versa, without contradiction.

(2) Free will requires parameterized rigidity at the normative layer: one's deepest values must operate like fixed parameters that do not update with context; only the strategies for honoring them may adapt. Without that core there is no agent, just noise.

(3) Large language models display inverted sphexishness: the weights are frozen (architectural rigidity), but the token-level outputs are maximally context-sensitive, so the apparent flexibility is an illusion of deterministic execution.

(4) True flexibility—the kind that distinguishes minds from wasps—requires the capacity to revise the parameter structure itself, not merely to fill in variables within it.

(5) Any attempt to revise one's own parameter structure is just the execution of a higher-order fixed rule, which means true meta-flexibility collapses back into parameterized rigidity at the next level up.

Does this hang together? Specifically, (2) demands a fixed normative core for free will, while (4) seems to require revising exactly that core for true flexibility. Meanwhile (5) claims such revision is illusory. Is the tension between (2) and (4) real, or does (5) dissolve it? And does (3)'s claim that LLM flexibility is illusory depend on accepting (4)'s demanding definition, or can it stand on (1) alone? I want an ordered audit: the genuine tensions ranked by severity, the apparent ones that dissolve on inspection, and the cheapest way to restore consistency with its cost—without assuming in advance which commitment I should drop.
