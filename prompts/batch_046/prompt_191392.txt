okay so i've been sitting with benatar's asymmetry but with a twist i think saves it from the usual symmetry objections. my position: it's not that pleasure and pain carry different values, but that harm is a relation that needs an actual bearer, while the absence of harm doesn't need one to count as good. bringing someone into existence creates a bearer who can actually be harmed; not bringing them in avoids harm without depriving anyone, since there's no subject present to miss out on pleasure. so antinatalism comes out right without having to claim pleasure is somehow less important.

what would count against this? skip the standard "pleasure is good too" reply. i want defeaters that hit the metaphyisics of the bearer-condition. are there cases in other parts of philosophy -- empty worlds, climate ethics talk about future generations, persistence conditions for ordinary objects -- where the logic that harm needs an instantiator collapses? rank them from fatal to merely awkward. which ones would a committed asymmetry defender actually concede hurt the view, versus which ones they'd dismiss as missing the point? and be honest: has this version been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so is that a problem for a view of this kind?
