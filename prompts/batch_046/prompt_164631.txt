Compare Weber's Protestant ethic thesis with the analytical distinction between culture as a "filter"—selectively legitimizing and stabilizing practices that arise from material conditions—and culture as a "motor"—independently generating institutional forms that lack adequate materialist precursors. Focusing specifically on Weber's notion of elective affinity (Wahlverwandtschaft) between Calvinist inner-worldly asceticism and the capitalist spirit, determine whether his argument treats religious values as a filter that made rationalized capital accumulation culturally viable, or as a motor that causally produced its distinctive institutional form. In doing so, evaluate whether either interpretation allows Weber to avoid the charge that he explains macro-historical economic transformation by appealing to subjective values, and identify precisely where the explanation remains vulnerable to the problem of explaining social outcomes with culture.
