Analyze the 1815 Tambora eruption and the 1816 "Year Without a Summer" through the framework of non-human historical agency, using the distinction between causal efficacy and agential capacity rather than treating climate as a deterministic backdrop. Focus on three outcomes commonly linked to the eruption: the New England agricultural collapse and grain riots, the typhus and famine mortality in Ireland, and the accelerated geographic spread of cholera out of the Ganges delta during the same climatic window. For each case, specify what the volcano actually changed in material conditions, what depended on human institutional responses—poor-law administration, colonial grain markets, sanitation infrastructure—and where attributing "agency" to the climate event clarifies the causal chain versus where it risks obscuring distributive and political choices. Conclude by saying what this episode demonstrates about the perils of environmental determinism and whether treating volcanoes, climate shifts, or epidemic germs as "historical actors" requires a theory of mediated agency that rigorously distinguishes conditions from actors.
