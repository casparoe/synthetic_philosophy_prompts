Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, two refrains with rhyme scheme A b A / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 / a b A2 / a b A1 A2—about George Berkeley’s monetary theory in The Querist, but read it through the lens of his immaterialism. Treat coin and credit not as commodities with hidden material worth, but as pure mental signs: a counter for labor already done, whose being is entirely conventional and therefore, for Berkeley, entirely idea-dependent. Use one refrain that returns on the economic claim that coin is only a counter for past labor, and a second refrain that returns on the metaphysical claim that gold, like all sensible things, has no being unperceived. The poem should take a definite interpretive stance—that Berkeley’s economics and his immaterialism are not separate doctrines but mutually reinforcing, with money serving as a limit-case of mind-dependent being—rather than hovering between them. Let the final quatrain bring the two refrains into direct collision without resolving the tension. Keep the philosophical content precise enough that a historian of early modern philosophy recognizes Berkeley’s actual view that money measures debt and labor rather than possessing intrinsic value, not a loose metaphor.
