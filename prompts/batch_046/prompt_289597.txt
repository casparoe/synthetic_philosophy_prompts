Make an argument that the delegation of complex reasoning to artificial intelligence systems need not erode human understanding, provided the human user retains what might be called architectural oversight—the capacity to determine which questions are worth asking, which constraints apply, and how outputs should be interpreted within a broader theoretical framework—while ceding only the intermediate inferential steps to the machine. Address the objection that genuine understanding requires direct cognitive engagement with every link in a chain of reasoning, and defend the view that understanding is better conceived as a property of a properly structured human-machine cognitive system rather than as a purely individual mental achievement. Specifically, argue that the real danger to understanding is not delegation itself but an asymmetry in which the machine determines the problem space while the human merely validates outputs, and that avoiding this asymmetry allows for a form of distributed epistemic agency that can deepen rather than diminish comprehension.
