I've been playing with this idea that the regress problem only bites if you picture justification as a straight line, but if you model it as a directed graph with cycles inside bounded domains then the trilemma kind of collapses. There arent basic beliefs in the old foudationalist sense, just high-weight entry nodes, and circularity isnt vicious as long as it doesnt jump domains. So you dont need infinite chains either. What would actually count against this? I want the defeaters sorted from fatal to just anoying — like, is there a formal result showing graph-structures with loops always collapse into circular reasoning, or a case study where cross-domain loops seem unavoidable? Also, has this been qualified into unfalsifiability: can I always redraw the domain boundary to save it? And what would defenders of this kind of graph-coherentism themselves admit is a real problem versus what critics say? Be honest about whether the view risks anything.
