I am a doctoral student in philosophy serving on my university's Committee on Public Memory and Institutional Naming. We must deliver a recommendation by next month's session on the central lecture hall, presently named after Elias Vorn, a nineteenth-century industrialist whose endowment founded our science faculty but whose private papers and institutional records show he financed eugenicist research societies and accumulated capital through colonial extraction systems. The administration has asked us to choose among three courses: retain the name with an interpretive plaque, rename the hall after a figure with no comparable moral liability, or replace the honorific with a neutral geographic designation while preserving Vorn's name solely in an internal historical registry. In framing our reasoning, I want the committee to distinguish three dimensions I will call spatial inheritance, honorific endorsement, and historical indexing: spatial inheritance asks whether present users of a building enter into a continuing moral relation with its namesake; honorific endorsement asks whether retaining the name actively praises that figure's full legacy; and historical indexing treats the name merely as a pointer to the past without evaluative weight. Please draft the formal opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend one of the three options with a concrete implementation timeline, and it should engage seriously the strongest argument for retention—namely, that removing Vorn's name risks sanitizing institutional complicity rather than confronting it—without simply restating legal definitions of commemoration. Write entirely in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as one would write a formal letter or email.
