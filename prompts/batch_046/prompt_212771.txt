Okay so heres a thing that happened in my county. We had this big vote — like 65% yes — to let people build small rental units in their backyards, cause housing is crazy expensive and everyone wants to help. The state legislature even passed a bill saying towns can do this. But then our state supreme court — seven judges, all appointed for 12 years, never have to run for reelection — struck down the local law last month. They said it violates an old 1950s zoning rule that says only single families can live on one lot. Nobody's enforced that rule in like forty years, but technically it's still on the books.

Now people are fighting about it. One group says its totally undemocratic for a bunch of unelected judges to cancel a vote that thousands of people made, plus the legislature agreed. The other group says the court is actually protecting democracy, because that old zoning law was passed by voters decades ago, and it represents the will of the community over time — not just whoever showed up this year. They also say the current voters are mostly homeowners who'd benefit, but future renters and people who haven't moved here yet dont get a vote, so the court is standing up for them.

I'm stuck in the middle. I dont want some "it depends" answer — I want you to actually pick a side and defend it. Is it legit for appointed judges to kill a popular law like this? And is there any real difference between saying they're protecting future people versus just letting them do whatever they want? Please take a clear stand and dont just list both sides.
