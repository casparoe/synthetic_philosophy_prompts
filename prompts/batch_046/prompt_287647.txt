I sit on the regional development authority's framework-review panel and we're stuck on this proposal that wants to overhaul zoning using what the authors call an "explicative planning framework." They say all our current planning terms — "fair distribution," "community character," "sustainable growth" — are vague in exactly the way Carnap criticized philosophical language, so we should replace them with precisely defined quantitative metrics like carbon-per-capita targets and traffic-flow coefficients. Once we do that, they claim planning becomes a purely empirical, internal-to-the-framework activity, and the choice of the framework itself is just a pragmatic, external decision we make once at the start. Some of us think this is a smart application of Carnap's internal/external distinction and his idea that value judgments aren't cognitive assertions but expressions of attitude; others think it's sleight of hand, because picking the metrics already bakes in value commitments, and Carnap's own writing on politics and planning suggests those external choices need explicit democratic legitimation rather than being buried in technical language. I was arguing about this with my roommate last night and they kept mixing up Carnap with Popper, which didn't help. We need a memo by Thursday recommending approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, with a concrete timeline if we go with conditions, and it needs to treat the strongest Carnapian defense of the proposal seriously rather than just calling it bureaucratic overreach.
