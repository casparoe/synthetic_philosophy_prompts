me and my roommate got into it for like two hours last night about whether a country can still be guilty for stuff that happened generations back, like colonial violence or slavery. i was mostly just listening and throwing in questions, but they kept going back and forth. she kept bringing up this german philosopher Jaspers — the one who wrote about four kinds of guilt after the war — and said he basically proves nations can't be morally guilty, only politically responsible. he said wait no thats not right, Jaspers actually shows the opposite if you read him with modern eyes. anyway heres how it went.

(1) she said collective guilt across generations is conceptually broken because guilt needs a mind — intention, remorse, awareness — and groups dont have minds, especially not people who werent born yet. she said Jaspers split guilt into criminal, political, moral and metaphysical, and only political guilt sticks to the collective, and even then its about citizenship not inherited sin. so calling a modern nation "guilty" is just mixing up categories.

(2) he pushed back with this idea he called "continuity guilt" — not inherited guilt, but guilt that keeps occuring as long as the same institutions and benefit structures stay in place. he said if a state still operates the legal and economic machinery built on past crimes and hasnt made structural repair, then the current collective is actively continuing the wrong, not just inheriting it. he said Jaspers missed this because he was focused on postwar germany where the regime had actually collapsed, so he never had to model institutional persistence.

(3) she said thats just causal responsibility dressed up in fancy words. she said "continuity" is doing all the work: you can trace benefits back forever, but guilt requires a subject who can stand trial or feel shame. a corporation can be fined but thats legal fiction, not real guilt. she said without a principled line between benefiting-from and being-guilty-of, his view makes every living person guilty for everything in history, and thats clearly wrong.

(4) he said legal persons exist precisely because we treat entities across time as responsible agents, and a nation-state is one of them. he said feelings are irrelevant — we dont ask if a corporation feels remorse before we dissolve it for fraud. he said her "needs a mind" argument assumes guilt is always psychological, but institutional guilt is about legitimate demands for repair directed at the present collective agent, not about inner states.
