I want to understand precisely what claims that one-liner could be expressing, because the argument she needed seemed to depend on a specific, unstated formulation rather than on the aphorism in general.

Could you lay out the distinct, precise theses hidden in “revolutions devour their children” as a menu of readings—distinguishing, for example, a structural claim about the inevitable escalation of violence within revolutionary coalitions; a psychological claim about the paranoia and purity-testing of revolutionary elites; a normative claim that radical transformation necessarily morally undermines itself; a historiographical claim specific to the contingency of 1794 and the factional dynamics of the Convention; and perhaps a sociological claim about the displacement of founding generations by bureaucratic successors? For each reading, say whether it amounts to a platitude, whether it is defensible as a general law of revolutionary politics, whether it is historically false, and which broader interpretation of the French Revolution—class struggle, ideology, or contingency—it implicitly assumes. In particular, identify which of these precise formulations actually licenses my colleague’s move of using the slogan to block analogies between Thermidor and contemporary reform, and which formulations would render that blocking move invalid. Do not reduce the phrase to a single meaning; show me which interpretation does the argumentative labor in the room I was in.
