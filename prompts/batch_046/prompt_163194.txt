Construct a one-paragraph, realistic hospital-triage thought experiment—no science fiction, no infinite populations, no perfect predictors—engineered so that standard act-utilitarianism and negative utilitarianism both recommend denying an expensive life-extending intervention to Group A and redirecting resources to palliative care for Group B, while a suffering-focused prioritarian view recommends funding Group A's treatment. The convergence of the two utilitarian positions must not be coincidental: it must depend on a specific structural feature of the case, namely that the life-extending treatment for Group A produces a state in which the patients experience low-grade chronic suffering that fully offsets their welfare gains, so that maximizing total welfare and minimizing total suffering align only because the welfare gains are internally hollowed out by embedded suffering. After the scenario, lead with the bottom-line verdict for each theory in the first sentence, then show the step-by-step reasoning, identify exactly which feature does the work, demonstrate what happens to all three verdicts if that feature is removed (e.g., if the treatment produced welfare gains with no embedded suffering), and explain how a prioritarian defender would attack the case—specifically by arguing that treating cross-personal suffering cancellation as legitimate violates the separateness of persons. Keep the scenario under one paragraph and avoid stipulating the verdicts; derive them from the theories applied to the case.
