Write a one-page research proposal for a philosophy-of-social-choice term paper, suitable for a single semester. The project should center on Harsanyi's aggregation theorem: given individual rationality conditions and social impartiality conditions, both social welfare and social belief reduce to linear weighted aggregates of individual von Neumann-Morgenstern utilities and subjective probabilities. Standard treatments take the aggregation weights as exogenously fixed and assume each agent's utility depends only on outcomes, with no structural dependence on the aggregation rule itself. The proposal should frame a specific twist that these presentations suppress—agents with recognition-sensitive utility functions, in which agent i's evaluation of an outcome is non-linearly modulated by whether i receives positive weight in the social aggregation, including a discontinuity at w_i = 0 that interacts with outcome valuation. State the precise formal question: can a social welfare function still be decomposed into a linear weighted sum when individual utilities contain such a procedural-recognition term, or does linear separability break down? Explain why existing answers leave this open—the standard axioms either exclude meta-preferences by fiat or absorb procedural concerns into the outcome space without modeling the structural dependence on weights—and propose a method that tests compatibility with Harsanyi's own axioms rather than importing an external moral framework. Specify what a positive result would look like (perhaps a generalized linear form with state-dependent or threshold-sensitive weights) versus a failure result (collapse of linear aggregation requiring a non-Harsanyian rule), and assess honestly whether formalizing the discontinuity and sketching the proof or counterexample is tractable within one semester. Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or literature review by reference; frame the proposal using only the named conceptual positions and formal conditions. Keep the tone formal and the scope realistic.
