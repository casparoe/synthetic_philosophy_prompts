I'm taking this course on economic history and we've been arguing about the socialist calculation debate—specifically Lange's iterative price thing versus Mises and Hayek. I noticed a pattern I can't name. Someone proposes a decentralized planning fix: local managers adjust production based on surplus and shortage signals, no central planner needs to know everything. Then the critique comes that those managers still need to know the social opportunity cost of capital, or how to weight different goods, or what welfare weights to apply. So the "knowledge is dispersed" problem doesn't get solved; it just moves up a level, into the rules that govern the local adjustments. I saw the same structure with old computer-planning ideas—algorithm optimizes allocation, but somebody still has to code the objective function and decide what counts as a cost. Is there an established term for this specific failure mode where solving an information gap at one layer just creates a deeper, structurally similar gap at the meta-layer? It's not quite infinite regress, since each level—prices, capital pricing, welfare weights—is genuinely different. How's it differnt from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis, and if there's no real name, what would you call it?
