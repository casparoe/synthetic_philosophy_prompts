Hey, I need help writing a retirement toast for Dr. Elena Voss — she's leaving the east-side clinic after 35 years. Half the room will be clinicans and grad students who actually read Szasz; the other half is family who just think she was a good listener. The occassion is a dinner next Saturday.

She never bought the standard "mental illness is just like diabetes" line. She was into Szasz's actual point — that calling distress "mental illness" isn't a flat lie, it's more like a charter myth: it doesn't describe a natural fact so much as organize who gets help, who gets blamed, and who gets put away. I want the toast to do real structural work with that idea: I shouldn't praise her for "curing diseases" or "reducing stigma" in the generic nonprofit way, because she'd reject the framing. But I also can't just lecture the family about medicalization.

Can you write this? Please think like a speechwriter with a philosophy background. Start by brainstorming three ways the charter-myth framing changes what praise sounds like, and one pitfall if the family hears it as saying she never believed anyone was in pain. Then give me the toast in these sections, in order:

- Opening hook (around 100 words, casual, set the scene)
- The frame (around 180 words, explain charter-myth vs false fairy-tale in plain language, no citations)
- What she actually did (around 180 words, apply the frame to her career without using DSM codes or book titles)
- Closing toast (around 80 words, raise the glass, avoid false comfort)

Keep it casual — there's probably a typo or two in this prompt, that's fine. Don't ask for a reading list or quote anyone verbatim. Make sure both halves of the room can follow it.
