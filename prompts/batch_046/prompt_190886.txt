okay so i've been going back through Dennett's old intentionality essays and the Searle exchange, and i'm genuinely stuck on why this hasn't moved in decades. Dennett introduces the two-bitser - this minimal gadget with just two internal states that you can interpret as having beliefs or desires if you take the intentional stance. He says the intentionality there is real, not just make-believe, even though its derived from our interpretive practice. Searle says thats wrong: only brains have original intentionality with the right causal powers, and everything else - including the two-bitser - just has derived, as-if intentionality.

Every time i read the back-and-forth it feels like they're not actually hitting each other. Is this a verbal disagreement about what real means? For Dennett it seems tied to predictive success and explanatory utility; for Searle its tied to intrinsic biological causation. Or is there a hidden empirical claim about whether functional organization alone can produce the right kind of mental causation? Or maybe theyre answering totally different questions - Dennett asking what makes intentional attribution rational, Searle asking what makes it ontologically fundamental.

Here's a slightly weirder framing that might be the hidden axis: Dennett treats intentionality as graded - the two-bitser has a little, we have a lot, its all on a spectrum of design complexity. Searle treats it as binary: either you have the specific brain-based causal powers or you dont, full stop. If thats the real disagreement, is there any conceptual distinction or formal result that could break the deadlock? Or should we just accept its a framing difference and move on?

Also be honest: is there a substantive question about AI intentionality hiding in there, or is this just philosophy being philosophy?
