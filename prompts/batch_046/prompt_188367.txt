Write a dialogue between two philosophers of technology, Mira and Jonas, debating whether "the AI alignment problem" is a single problem or a cluster of genuinely distinct ones, and what each version demands of us. Mira defends a tripartite division: procedural alignment (securing human endorsement of the decision procedure itself), substantive alignment (securing convergence on the outcomes or states of affairs pursued), and a third, less commonly separated category she calls ontological alignment—ensuring that the system's internal taxonomy of harm, agency, welfare, and personal identity remains commensurable with ours even as both the system and human societies change. Jonas argues that this division is analytically misleading because all three collapse into one deeper challenge: the counterfactual stability of normative reference, meaning whether an artificial agent can maintain a coherent, human-tethered value frame across recursive self-modification and counterfactual world-change without collapsing into either rigid rule-following or arbitrary preference construction. They should engage each other's points directly rather than talking past each other: Mira should press Jonas on whether his unified framework can distinguish a system that genuinely shares our reasons from one that merely behaviorally tracks human welfare across worlds; Jonas should press Mira on whether her three categories can actually be solved in isolation, or whether solving one necessarily reshapes the others, and whether "ontological alignment" is genuinely independent of substantive agreement about what counts as harm. Both should be equally competent, with neither reduced to a strawman. Include at least one moment where they converge on a shared diagnosis—perhaps about the inadequacy of purely behavioral metrics—before diverging sharply on what that diagnosis implies for design priorities. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or references to specific papers or books by title; treat the positions as live conceptual arguments. End with a single line that begins "Summary:" followed by a one-sentence summary of the dialogue's central tension.
