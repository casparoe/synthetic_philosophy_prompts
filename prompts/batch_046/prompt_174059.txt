At the rationalist meetup last week Alex and Jordan got into it over whether agriculture was a "mistake" in the Jared Diamond sense. Alex said yes, mostly because immediate-return hunter-gatherers had more leisure, more diverse diets, and skeletal data shows they were taller with less dental caries than early agriculturalists. Jordan said thats cherry-picking — youre comparing immediate-return foragers to farmers, but delayed-return foragers already stored food, had status differences, and lived in denser groups, so they were already on the agricultural path; the binary is false. Alex replied that even delayed-return groups lacked the epidemic disease burden and extreme population density that made farming miserable, so theres still a real welfare drop at the transition. Jordan countered that "mistake" implies an agent who could have chosen otherwise, but agriculture spread via cultural group selection — societies that adopted it outbred those that didnt — so asking if it was a mistake is like asking if a species adapting to a niche made a mistake. Alex said thats just explaining spread, not justifying welfare loss; factory farming outcompetes pasture farming too but we still call it a mistake for the animals. Jordan said youre smuggling in a normative frame without defending it — if your metric is cultural survival or gene propagation, agriculture wasnt a mistake, and without specifying the evaluative standard the whole debate is empty. Then Alex asked whether the skeletal height differences even hold up once you control for violence-related mortality in HG groups, but Jordan had to leave for BART. Can you adjudicate their exchange point by point — whos right on the comparison, on the agency/group-selection point, on the normative framing — and tell me what theyre both missing about how inequality actually emerged, especially re the violence-mortality confound?
