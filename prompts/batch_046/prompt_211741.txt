I am writing an essay for my graduate course on collective rationality and social choice theory, and I would like to compare two frameworks for understanding failures of group judgment. The first is the classic discursive dilemma introduced by List and Pettit, in which a committee applies majority rule separately to the premises of a logical argument and thereby arrives at a collectively endorsed conclusion that contradicts the premises that received majority support. The second is a graded-credence variant that I have been developing for the assignment: instead of binary acceptance or rejection, each committee member assigns a numerical confidence value between 0 and 1 to each premise, these values are aggregated through linear pooling, and a collective conclusion is affirmed only if the pooled confidence exceeds a fixed threshold.

Please compare these two frameworks with respect to the following points. First, determine whether the impossibility result—that no aggregation rule can simultaneously satisfy universal domain, anonymity, and logical consistency—carries over to the graded setting, or whether the continuity of credence values allows the contradiction to be avoided. Second, examine whether linear pooling introduces a distinct form of collective irrationality, such as a discontinuity at the decision threshold that produces logically unstable collective beliefs even when every individual is fully coherent. Third, assess which framework poses the more fundamental challenge to the normative ideal that democratic deliberation should yield rational collective judgments.

Do not include bibliographic references or reading lists; base your analysis solely on the conceptual architecture described above. Please conclude with a single line beginning exactly 'Verdict:' that summarizes in one sentence which framework you regard as the deeper obstacle to collective rationality.
