You serve as the lead pedagogical designer for a residential rehabilitation center that treats adults recovering from traumatic brain injuries affecting executive function and practical reasoning. The center's clinical director has adopted John Locke's theory of the mind—specifically that simple ideas originate in sensation and reflection, that complex habits form through associative repetition, and that education should cultivate rational autonomy through structured experience rather than through authoritarian imposition—as the governing framework for all cognitive rehabilitation modules. You need a reusable decision procedure that your interdisciplinary team can apply to any proposed twelve-week curriculum module before it is approved for a resident cohort.

Write the procedure as an ordered sequence of stages, each with an explicit stop condition. For every stage, specify: the precise question the team must answer; what counts as sufficient empirical or observational evidence; whether the outcome is a factual determination or a values judgment (and, in the latter case, which party—clinical staff, the resident, or a designated family representative—must provide signed authorization before proceeding); and the point at which the procedure terminates with a rejection of the proposal. Include dedicated checkpoints for tensions between Lockean autonomy-preservation and clinical safety mandates, marking exactly where judgment cannot be eliminated by the procedure alone.

Then apply the procedure to three proposed modules: one teaching financial decision-making through simulated marketplace interactions with real monetary stakes; one teaching emotional self-regulation through highly repetitive ritualized exercises accompanied by minimal explanatory content; and one teaching social reasoning through unguided direct observation of live group dynamics without structured reflection prompts. In your analysis of the emotional-regulation proposal, demonstrate how the procedure yields a verdict that conflicts with standard occupational-therapy best practice, identify the specific stage that generates the conflict, and explain whether the Lockean framework permits a clinical override or requires acceptance of the result. Finally, specify two resident cognitive profiles for which the procedure is explicitly not designed and must be suspended, and state what the procedure cannot determine about long-term outcomes regardless of how rigorously it is followed.
