After a long and unresolved argument with a friend about the statue and the clay, I want us to produce an adversarial-collaboration document that both of us could sign. I defend a dominance view: the statue is identical to the clay arranged statue-wise, and the differences in survival conditions come from relational or contextual properties rather than from a numerically distinct object. My friend defends constitution-without-identity: the statue and the clay are distinct entities that share matter and spatiotemporal location, but differ in modal, historical, and sortal properties. Please draft the joint statement.

Structure it as follows. First, a numbered list of claims we both accept: about physical co-location; about the asymmetry in survival conditions (squashing destroys the statue but not the clay); about the empirical possibility of gradual atomic replacement over time; and about what art conservators and legal institutions actually treat as continuous across repair. Second, a disagreements table with two columns—one phrased in wording I would accept, one in wording my friend would accept—covering: (a) whether the term statue denotes a new object or a mode of presentation of arranged matter; (b) whether temporary intrinsics such as shape are genuinely possessed by a distinct object or only derivatively possessed by arranged matter; (c) whether diachronic identity requires continuity of matter, of form, or of both together. Third, identify the cruxes: is there a substantive ontological disagreement here, or is part of our dispute verbal—specifically, about whether object should be read as maximal continuous body versus functional-artifactual kind; and does a particular replacement scenario bear on it? For that scenario, inject this twist: imagine that over several decades every original clay atom is replaced with synthetic polymer that preserves weight, shape, and artistic function, so that none of the original matter remains. State exactly what each of us predicts: does the statue survive? Does the clay survive? Would either of us revise our view if museums and courts treat the result as the same artwork? Finally, say clearly whether any portion of our disagreement is merely verbal, which portion, and why.
