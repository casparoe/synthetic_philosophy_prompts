Please compare two specific frameworks I am trying to keep distinct: the Ant Fugue model and the Aunt Hillary model.

In the Ant Fugue model, colony-level cognition is analogized to a contrapuntal fugue. Each worker functions as an independent voice that enters with shared thematic material—say, a pheromonal gradient or a task-response pattern—and any collective mental property is nothing over and above the interwoven relational structure among these voices. There is no conductor, no central representation, and no single subject of experience; mental properties, if present, are distributed across the temporal architecture of interaction itself. The model predicts that removing any individual worker, even an experienced one, alters the harmony but does not dissolve the fugue.

The Aunt Hillary model, by contrast, treats apparent collective intelligence as parasitic on one keystone integrator—an older worker whose decision-rules are copied, deferred to, or chemically amplified by the rest of the colony. On this view, the swarm behaves as if it has beliefs or goals only because this one node serves as a bottleneck for information integration; remove Aunt Hillary, and the remaining workers revert to disjoint reflex arcs with no collective psychology.

Compare these two models with respect to the conditions each sets for legitimately attributing a mind to the colony.
