Write a sestina using the end-words promise, execute, fail, speak, bind, and verify. Take Austin’s distinction between constative and performative utterances—where to say something can also be to do something—but apply it to a self-executing digital agreement rather than a spoken vow. Let the sestina’s spiraling repetition enact the recursion of verification: place “execute” on lines where code runs, “speak” on lines where no human voice is present, “bind” where obligation is created, and “fail” where a felicity condition is not met. Move each stanza between describing terms (constative) and triggering obligations (performative), and in the envoi ask whether the absence of a speaker removes the performative force or transfers it to the network itself. The repetition should suggest that the utterance is never finished being verified. Render the concepts in your own lines without quoting Austin; make the mapping accurate enough that someone familiar with speech-act theory recognizes how authority, procedure, and sincerity are replaced by cryptographic checks and consensus, and make the poem coherent on its own for a reader who knows none of that.
