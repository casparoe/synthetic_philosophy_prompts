In discussions contrasting communitarianism with liberalism, I repeatedly encounter the slogan "the self is always already embedded" deployed as a direct refutation of liberal individualism. Could you disambiguate the precise theses this phrase might express—distinguishing, for instance, ontological constitution, epistemic situatedness, normative dependence, and modal counterfactual reliance on social structures? For each reading, indicate whether it genuinely contradicts core liberal commitments or can be accommodated within them, and specify which version a critic of liberalism actually needs to make their objection succeed. I am particularly interested in whether treating embeddedness as a modal claim about counterfactual identity-dependence rather than as a constitutive claim avoids the usual liberal rebuttals, and if so, whether that modal version is defensible on its own terms.
