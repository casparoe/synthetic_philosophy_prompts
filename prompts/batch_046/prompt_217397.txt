Write the desiderata that any adequate account of economic calculation in decentralized planning must satisfy to count as resolving the socialist calculation debate on its own terms, rather than circumventing it. The account must handle: (1) the knowledge problem as Mises and Hayek articulated it—dispersed, tacit, and temporally specific information that resists central aggregation; (2) the computation and convergence problem raised against market-socialist iterative mechanisms—whether algorithmic price adjustments can reach equilibrium at scale within relevant time horizons; and (3) a constraint I am introducing, which I call institutional memory decay: any planning mechanism must remain interpretable and correctable by successive generations of human agents, not merely reach optimal allocations in a steady-state model. For each desideratum, specify the phenomenon it protects, whether it is negotiable or non-negotiable, and which existing position—Misesian market fundamentalism, Lange-type iterative market socialism, or contemporary algorithmic central planning—faces the gravest threat from it. Then identify whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given current results in distributed computation and organizational epistemology. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on a historical scorecard.
