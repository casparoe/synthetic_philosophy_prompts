I am a retired field biologist—formerly working in taxonomy and ecology—who has only recently begun serious study of philosophy, and I have joined a reading group of other retired scientists now working through classical Chinese texts. We have spent several months on Gongsun Long's white-horse dialogue and the broader concerns of the School of Names, and we have crystallized into two camps that I think can be stated precisely enough for an adversarial collaboration. The first camp holds that the dialogue is fundamentally an argument about predication and logical class membership: the term designating the kind operates at a different level from the term designating a qualified instance, so the denial that a white horse is a horse expresses a logical point about subset relations rather than a claim about physical reality. The second camp holds that the dialogue is best read as an early metaphysical argument about essential properties versus accidental modifications, structurally analogous to asking in biological taxonomy whether a phenotypic variant—say, an albino individual of a mammalian species—remains fully and unqualifiedly a member of its kind, or whether the accidental property introduces a genuinely distinct ontological category.

Please draft the joint statement that representatives of these two camps could both sign after a sustained, good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want, first, a numbered section of agreed claims, including about the dialogue's internal logical structure, about interpretations both sides reject as misreadings, and about the relevance of scientific classification analogies. This should be followed by a disagreements table in which each camp's residual position is stated in wording that its own members would accept without distortion. Next, identify the cruxes—the specific sub-questions (for example, whether the argument is primarily about language-use or about the ontology of kinds) such that settling them would move at least one camp. For each crux, present a concrete case or thought-experiment drawn from biological taxonomy or philosophy of science that both sides agree bears on the issue, and record in advance what each camp predicts the case will show. If any portion of the dispute dissolves upon analysis—say, if both sides are using a central term in different but compatible senses—identify explicitly which portion and explain why the disagreement was verbal. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
