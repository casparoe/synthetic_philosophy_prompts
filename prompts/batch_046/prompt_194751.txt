I've been obsessed lately with this inside-view vs outside-view thing ever since I started hanging out with people who do prediction markets. The analogy everyone uses is that the inside view is like driving with GPS turned off and just reading road signs, while the outside view is checking the traffic statistics for that exact route at that exact time. They use it to say you should always trust the data over your gut feeling about whether you'll make it to the interview on time. But then my roomate used it on me when I'm deciding whether to quit my job for grad school — he said I'm being arrogant about my "special case" and should just look at the base rate of people who regret it. I feel like the driving analogy is hiding something about causality? Can you lay out the actual mapping: what's the GPS, what's the road signs, what's the traffic data, and where does it break because career decisions don't have stable referance classes like highway traffic? And does his conclusion — that I should always defer to statistics — actually survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the GPS image just smuggling in the idea that personal reasoning is always biased?

Also, I was thinking about epistemic modesty and whether using the outside view is actually humble, or if it's just another way to win an argument. Like, if I say "well my company culture is toxic in a specific way that isn't in the dataset," is that inside-view arrogance or just knowing my own context? I'm not asking for a bibliography or anything, just trying to figure out if there's a better analogy than driving — maybe something that captures when you actually do have private information that the base rate can't see, without making me sound like I'm just making excuses.
