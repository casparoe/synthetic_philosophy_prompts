I edit a science and ideas magazine and we keep getting pushback that our headlines make climate models sound more certain than they are -- not exactly lying, but something Orwell warned about, language that hides sloppy thinking. I want to turn that idea into a procedure our copy editors can run on every university press realease, with about ten minutes and no philosophy PhD required.

The specific problem: press releases use phrases like "potentially transformative impacts" or "a paradigm shift may be on the horizon" to dress up weak results. I also want the checklist to catch what I'm calling probabilistic euphemism -- when a 95% confidence interval gets translated into "significant risk" or "unlikely" with out the numbers, which feels like the dead metaphors Orwell hated but applied to statistics.

Write the procedure: an ordered checklist of 4 or 5 steps, each with a quick test sentence. Fix the role (copy editor, every release, stakes are public trust, sign-off needed from senior editor if flag is ambiguous). Give three worked examples: one where it correctly kills a bad headline, one where it wrongly flags a good sentence (that's the answer I won't like), and one probabilistic euphemism case. Mark the points where judgemnt can't be replaced by a tick box and the editor just has to decide. Also say clearly what the procedure can't do -- where it'll misfire no matter how you tune it. No bibliography needed, just something they can tape to the wall.
