Hey, I'm a retired chemist who got into philosophy of science after leaving the lab — mostly Kuhn, a little Popper, you know how it goes. I was chatting with my friend over coffee about something weird I keep seeing in the literature now. You have these AI-driven studies, especially in materials and neuroscience, where the algorithm finds a correlation or a compound that works, the results replicate fine in other labs, but there's literally no mechanistic story behind it. It just sits there as an orphan finding — everyone trusts it, cites it, but nobody can fit it into the bigger theoretical modle we teach grad students. It's not like a falsification that kills a theory; the old framework just... ignores it, and the finding lives in its own little box.

So I've been wondering, if Thomas Kuhn were around today — he missed all of this, obviously — what would he make of it? Not the replication crisis, which I think he'd recognize as normal science having a bad day, but specifically this situation where reproducible, valid science comes out of tools that don't speak our theoretical language at all. Would he say we're in a paradime shift where human explanation is being replaced? Or would he say this is incommensurability with a twist — not two human worldviews clashing, but science itself splitting into an explanatory branch and a predictive black-box branch that don't translate into each other? I don't want a bibliography, just your best guess at what he actually would have argued, and whether this breaks his model or extends it.
