Okay so I've been chewing on Carroll's old tortoise-and-Achilles thing and I think I pushed it somewhere that breaks all reasoning, but I can't find the exit.

(1) Carroll shows that if Achilles wants to justify applying modus ponens to "A" and "If A then B", he has to add the rule of inference itself as another premise.
(2) That new premise needs its own license, and you get an infinate regress of meta-rules.
(3) So explicit deductive justification never actually reaches bedrock; it's always unfinished.
(4) The same loop hits probabilistic reasoning: to apply Bayes' rule you need a meta-norm that says "update your credence now", which needs its own justification, etc.
(5) So every normative rule of thought -- logical or probabilistic -- is caught in the same unfinished chain.
(6) But being rational just is being disposed to follow the correct rules of thought.
(7) Therefore rationality can never be explicitly justified, and anyone who claims they're reasoning is just describing a psychological habit.
(8) That feels wrong -- when I do a proof I genuinely think I'm reasoning, not just reporting a brain-state. So where's the slip?

I really want to keep (3) and (4) because the regress looks real in both cases. Is the mistake in (6), that rationality isn't about following explicit rules? Or is it somewhere else, and what do I pay if I drop that step instead?
