I am taking a graduate seminar on democratic theory and have become convinced by a particular version of the sortition argument that I have not seen fully articulated in the standard literature. The view is that electoral democracy is defective not merely because representatives fail to mirror the demographic composition of the electorate, but because the selection mechanism—campaigning, fundraising, and partisan competition—acts as a filter that is negatively correlated with the epistemic virtues relevant to sound legislation: intellectual humility, tolerance for ambiguity, and the willingness to revise preferences in light of evidence. On this account, a randomly selected legislative body does not just approximate descriptive representation in the statistical sense; it preserves what I call the 'full distribution of practical reasoning styles' that elections systematically suppress. I take this to be a constitutive, not merely instrumental, claim about democratic equality: if the point of democracy is to distribute political power without regard to merit or status, then any selection procedure that rewards ambition and rhetorical skill is in principle aristocratic, regardless of how universal the suffrage. Sortition is therefore the only selection method consistent with democratic equality properly understood.

I want to know what would genuinely count against this position—not generic objections to citizens' assemblies, but the specific defeaters that target the epistemic and constitutive claims together. Which empirical results from actual sortitive bodies (for instance, the Irish Citizens' Assembly or comparable mini-publics) would undermine the claim about cognitive diversity? Which normative arguments about political accountability or authorisation could show that random selection violates rather than realises democratic equality? And is there a conceptual vulnerability in defining democratic equality so narrowly that no electoral mechanism could ever satisfy it—making the view unfalsifiable in a damaging way? I am especially interested in whether the Condorcet jury-theorem style reasoning often invoked to support collective wisdom collapses under the conditions of rotation and repeated deliberation that real sortition requires, and whether a committed defender of this view should concede that as fatal or merely awkward.
