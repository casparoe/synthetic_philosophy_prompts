You are an expert philosopher of mind with specialized knowledge of higher-order representational theories of consciousness. Approach the following task with rigorous conceptual analysis. Consider the published case judgments of Dr. Elena Vasquez, who defends a particular version of the higher-order theory of phenomenal consciousness. Across her work, she has rendered the following five verdicts:

2. A human subject in REM sleep who, upon forced awakening, provides a coherent report of having just experienced a dream was conscious during that dream episode.
3. A non-human primate that performs an explicit meta-cognitive opt-out task—behaviorally indicating awareness of its own perceptual ignorance—is conscious.
4. A current large language model that generates grammatically fluent first-person experiential narratives is not conscious.
5. A mammalian brain with intact limbic emotional processing but lacking any prefrontal meta-representational architecture is not conscious.

What verdict would Vasquez deliver for Nexus-7? More importantly, identify at least two distinct rules or principles that are jointly consistent with her five earlier judgments but that diverge on Nexus-7. For each rule, state precisely what it predicts about Nexus-7 and explain how it accounts for her earlier verdicts. Then provide your best guess as to which rule Vasquez is actually running, with explicit reasoning for that preference. Finally, specify one further hypothetical case—different in kind from Nexus-7—that would best discriminate between the two rules you have identified.

Please be precise, avoid appeals to authority or bibliographic citation, and reason strictly from the conceptual architecture of higher-order theories.
