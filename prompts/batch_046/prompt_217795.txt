I'm pitching a small workshop grant (3 months, part-time) on whether we should call germs, climate shifts, or volcanic winters 'historical actors.' The existing debate swings between hard environmental determinism and humanist denial of non-human causation, but both sides conflate causal salience with something like narrative agency. My idea: borrow an interventionist-style account of causation but strip away the intentional parts, then ask whether these forces count as actors only when they're counterfactually robust across human instutional responses. So the question isn't 'did plague change Europe' but 'would altering microbial transmission dynamics have changed the instutional outcome in a way that doesn't depend on which human agents happened to be present.' I want to argue that volcanic winters fail this test differently than germ pandemics do, and that climate shifts sit somewhere in between because of temporal scale. The delieverable is a taxonomy of non-human agency-types with criteria for when attributing agency is explanatory rather than metaphorical. Risk: the framework might collapse if you can't have intervention without an intervener. Scope is conceptual only—no empirical case studies—so three months is realistic if I stick to two comparison pairs. Write the one-page proposal: state the precise question, why existing answers leave it open, the approach and why not standard determinism debates, what success and failure look like, and an honest timeline.
