Having spent my career in experimental physics and only recently turned to philosophy, I find myself drawn to cases that isolate one variable rather than rehearse familiar debates. To that end: design a decision scenario—realistic, confined to a workplace or public-health institution, and describable in a single paragraph—in which the standard alignment between libertarian paternalism and hard paternalism is inverted. Specifically, engineer the case so that the libertarian paternalist, who ordinarily endorses choice-preserving nudges but rejects coercive bans, is compelled to support an explicit prohibition, while the hard paternalist, who permits coercive welfare-promoting restrictions, refuses that prohibition and instead advocates for a non-coercive default architecture that leaves the harmful option technically available. The inversion must fall out of the internal logic of the nudge mechanism itself; do not achieve it by rendering the libertarian irrational or secretly consequentialist. Introduce one structural feature that is not standard in the familiar cafeteria-line or retirement-plan examples—specifically, a transparent framing rule coupled with an irreversible opt-out protocol that requires an agent to disclose, to an algorithmic auditor, a private mental-state evaluation, thereby destroying the privacy and reversibility conditions that libertarian paternalism requires to count as autonomy-preserving. After presenting the scenario, provide: (a) the precise policy verdict each theory delivers and the reasoning behind it; (b) identification of the feature that does the reversing work, with an explicit statement of which conditions of legitimate nudging it violates; (c) an analysis of what happens if that feature is removed—does the case collapse back into the standard nudging-versus-mandate comparison?; and (d) the response a defender of libertarian paternalism would mount, particularly whether they can reject the feature as an illegitimate nudge without conceding that all nudges share this defect. Do not invoke perfect predictors, demons, or exotic physics; do not request bibliographies, citations, or lists of further reading; and do not depend on highly specialized literature unknown outside a narrow subfield—write so that the conceptual machinery can be evaluated on its own terms.
