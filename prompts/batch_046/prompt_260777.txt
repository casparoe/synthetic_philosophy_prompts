I am a novelist giving the retirement address for my long-time editor, whose list was devoted to fiction that dissolves the boundary between waking life and dream. The audience is split between philosophers familiar with Zhuangzi and family members who are not. I want the speech to take the butterfly dream not as Cartesian scepticism about verification, but as an ontological point: the self that would ask whether it is dreaming has already dissolved into transformation (hua), so the distinction is not solved but inhabited. This should do real structural work—the address must honour her career without saying the books were "merely dreams" or that they revealed a hidden reality, both of which falsely assume a stable observer. Instead, it should present her editing as a practice of sustaining that transformation for her authors, and it must comfort the family without turning into a lecture. Please write this five-minute address in British English, using no verbatim quotations from texts, requesting no sources or citations, and letting the philosophical idea shape what can and cannot be said rather than serve as decoration.
