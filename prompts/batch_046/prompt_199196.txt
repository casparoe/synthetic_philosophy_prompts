Write an essay on whether automating nuclear retaliation actually solves the credibility problem in brinkmanship, or if it just swaps one paradox for a weirder one. I'm tired of the usual humans-are-too-emotional-to-pull-the-trigger take — it feels like people treat credibility as purely psychological when it's really epistemic. If you build a dead-hand style system or some AI-mediated escalation ladder, the adversary has to believe it's real and won't misfire, but if it's an opaque black-box they also can't verify its decision rules, which seems to reintroduce ambiguty about whether you'll actually escalate. So does perfect automation destroy the rational ambiguity that makes brinkmanship stable, or does it create a new kind of commitment device? Weigh both sides but don't end on it depends — reach a verdict on whether credible automated deterrence is even conceptually coherent, and say if smaller states with shaky second-strike verifcation should just avoid the whole thing.
