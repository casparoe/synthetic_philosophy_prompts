I'm a doctoral student in philosophy working on Hume's aesthetics, and for the opening of my seminar presentation I want a brief poetic interlude—roughly 16 lines, in the form of a pantoum—to capture a tension at the heart of Hume's "Of the Standard of Taste" that often gets flattened in secondary literature. Specifically, the circularity in identifying the "true judge": we seem to locate the standard of taste by appealing to the verdicts of qualified critics, yet we identify those critics precisely by their conformity to the standard. Rather than resolving this circle, I want the poem's formal repetition—the way the pantoum's lines cycle back and swap positions—to do the philosophical work: each return of a line should feel like both a confirmation and a presupposition, so that by the final stanza the reader senses that calibration and circularity are inseparable. The voice should be that of an ideal critic who is self-aware about this bind, not a polemicist. The tone should be restrained and contemplative. Please compose this in American English, and avoid any bibliographic references or reading suggestions, since this is intended as a standalone reflective piece.
