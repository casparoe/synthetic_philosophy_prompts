The basic setup is familiar: if there are sufficiently many causally disconnected copies of me distributed throughout a large universe, then under evidential decision theory my local choice becomes statistical evidence about what those copies choose, so cooperation with them can be rational even absent causal influence. But I'm trying to understand a more subtle issue that arises when we try to formalize what counts as "the same decision procedure" across copies. Does evidential cooperation still apply uniformly across the field, or does it generate a kind of "evidential locality" where I should only weight copies within a certain correlation radius? And if so, how do we avoid smuggling causal locality back into the evidential calculus in a way that undermines the very point of evidential reasoning?

In particular, please address whether the agent's reasoning about "what I would do" can be localized in index space, and what this implies for the stability of cooperative equilibria when copies near the boundary of a correlation region face slightly different evidential weights.
