I've been stuck in a back-and-forth with a friend in my physics-philosophy reading group about whether string theory's multiverse landscape is even a scientific problem or just a methodological dead end. One of us keeps saying that if you can't empirically distinguish between 10^500 de Sitter vacua, then you've given up on falsifiability and you're basically doing mathematical theology. The other side—me, mostly—argues that we're not abandoning empiricism, just shifting to a different mode of confirmation: using swampland conjectures, consistency conditions with quantum gravity, and teh fact that no competing framework reproduces the same low-energy effective field theories without hitting the same landscape. But we've been going in circles for months and nobody budges.

My working hunch is that we're not actually disagreeing about physics or even about Popperian demarcation. I think the stall comes from a hidden mismatch in what we think 'theory confirmation' is supposed to do. For me it's about explanatory closure—getting a constrained structure that makes rival theories look ad hoc. For my friend it's about predictive risk—if your framework allows anything, it explains nothing. But maybe there's also a verbal tangle: we both say 'multiverse' but I mean the parameter space of consistent vacua, while he hears a literal ontology of parallel worlds. So is this a substantive disagreement about standards of evidence, a verbal confusion over 'explanation', or two people answering different questions—one about ontology, one about methodology—under the same headline? And if it's the latter, is there anything that could actually move us—maybe a formal result showing the landscape is discrete and computable, or a philosophical distinction between predictive exclusion and explanatory saturation? Or is the question just ill-posed because we haven't agreed on whether physics owes us uniqueness at all? Diagnose it with reasons, not a survey of interpretations, and be honest about whether any experiment, theorem, or conceptual distinction could break the impasse, or if we should just accept that we're talking past each other.
