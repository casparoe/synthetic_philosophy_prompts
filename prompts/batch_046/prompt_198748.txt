I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of mind, and I am preparing a closing piece for a small discussion group of fellow scientists now reading in the field. I would like a Petrarchan sonnet—fourteen lines of iambic pentameter, octave and sestet, with the volta falling exactly at line nine—on Mary’s room and the knowledge argument. The poem should treat Mary’s monochrome chamber not as mere ignorance but as a fully populated global workspace: in the octave she possesses complete access-consciousness of every physical fact about color vision—wavelengths, opponent processes, neural firing patterns—available for report and reasoning, yet structurally lacking phenomenal character. The volta at line nine should mark the moment she steps out and sees red, not as the downloading of one more data file but as the arrival of phenomenal consciousness that was never within the broadcast architecture of access. The sestet should hold open, without sermon or resolution, whether this gap genuinely undermines physicalism or only reveals that physical knowledge can be exhaustive yet phenomenally empty. Please let the form do this work structurally: the octave’s closed rhyme (ABBA ABBA) should feel like a systematically mapped system, while the sestet’s turn (CDECDE or CDCDCD) should feel looser, as if rhyme itself is searching for a correspondence the prior workspace could not generate. The poem should be accurate enough that a philosopher of mind recognizes the access-phenomenal distinction and Jackson’s argument, rather than merely naming them, and coherent enough that a reader unfamiliar with the debate can still feel the tension. After the poem, please include a brief reflective note—no more than a short paragraph—indicating where the poem takes liberties with Jackson’s original formulation or with Block’s distinction, and stating clearly what argument or consideration would lead you to revise the interpretation embedded in the poem: for instance, if physicalist accounts of phenomenal concepts can fully explain Mary’s new knowledge without positing non-physical properties, or if global workspace theory can be shown to accommodate phenomenal content within its broadcasting architecture, and where the reasoning is most likely to fail.
