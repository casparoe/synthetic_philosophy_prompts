Please write a final-exam essay question for an upper-level seminar on philosophy of well-being (45 minutes, 30 marks, expected answer around 600 words). The question should address the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — that pleasures such as tasting wine, grasping a mathematical proof, and feeling schadenfreude appear to share no single phenomenal quality — but must not be answerable by reciting a standard textbook summary. To keep the issue concrete, frame it around three specific cases: a cognitive pleasure in sudden mathematical insight, a bodily pleasure in sustained physical exertion, and a malicious pleasure taken in a rival's failure. Ask students to evaluate three competing proposals for what unifies these cases: (1) that all pleasures share an identical 'hedonic tone' that is merely masked by diverse content; (2) that pleasure is not a felt quality at all but a uniform pro-attitude — being pleased that P — which can take any intentional object; and (3) that pleasure is defined functionally as whatever positively reinforces the behaviour that produces it. The question should require students to explain, for each proposal, whether it genuinely unifies the three cases as instances of one kind of intrinsic good, or whether it collapses hedonism into desire-satisfaction theory, functionalism, or a pluralist account of value. In particular, it must press them on whether proposal (2) can accommodate malicious pleasure without rendering hedonism normatively implausible, and whether proposal (3) solves heterogeneity only by abandoning the intuition that pleasure is essentially experiential. Include a model answer of about 600 words that demonstrates the required conceptual moves — distinguishing qualitative content from intentional content, applying each proposal to all three cases, identifying the specific collapse risks, and defending a clear conclusion — together with a marking scheme that separates an A answer (all three proposals applied correctly to all three cases, both collapse arguments identified, the normative tension in malicious pleasure addressed, and a defended verdict given) from a B answer (accurate description of proposals and cases, but missing at least one collapse argument or failing to resolve the normative problem). Write in British English.
