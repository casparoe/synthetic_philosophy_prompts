I teach a philosophy of biology seminar, about 10 grad students, 90 min. Design an activity that makes them feel the species problem without using Darwin's finches—use engineered chimeras with cross-kingdom gene transfer and gene-drive hybrids where reproductive isolation only collapses in the F2 generation. Give them cards for five organisms and three criteria (interbreeding, genetic coherence, ecological niche) and make them classify each. Then hit them with a conflict: one needs patent protection (must be distinct) and another needs endangered-species status (must belong to a wild species). They defend thier choices in pairs. Only after they've committed reveal the philosophical names—realism, pluralism, eliminativism. Debrief needs to map each crisis onto one of those, warn me that students will probably smuggle essentialism back in via 'design intent' and how to stop it, and admit the game can't reproduce deep evolutionary time so I'll have to supply that part. No lecture up front, keep it hands-on and a little chaotic.
