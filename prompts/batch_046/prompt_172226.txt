I've been going back and forth with my study partner about Schlick's idea that life gets its meaning from play — stuff you do just for itself, not as a means to something else — and I'm starting to think the debate is completely stuck. Every time I bring up chess or painting, he says "yeah but that's meaningful because it's play," and when I ask why playing chess isn't just a hobby with no point, he says I'm demanding external justification that Schlick already showed is unnecessary. We've hit the same wall for weeks. My working theory is that this isn't really about life at all; it's either a verbal dispute about what 'meaning' has to include, or we're answering different questions under one word — maybe he's talking about psychological satisfaction and I'm talking about normative value. But there's another layer I keep coming back to: Schlick never really specifies whether 'play' is defined by a feeling of absorbtion (like being lost in the moment) or by a structural feature (like not being paid or not having a goal outside the activity). So when people argue about whether professional sports or competitive chess count as play, they're not disagreeing about Schlick; they're testing different properties against examples he never sorted out. Is that the real source of the stall? I want a diagnosis with actual reasons, not just "Schlick was right." Are we dealing with a hidden empirical disagreement about motivation, a conceptual confusion about justification, or two separate projects — one descriptive, one evaluative — wearing the same name? And if the ambiguity about absorbtion versus structure is what's doing the work, what kind of distinction or thought experiment would actually move things forward, or is this the kind of impasse where nothing should be expected?
