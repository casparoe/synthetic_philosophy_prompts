So I'm running this small side-project with an AI assistant I call Milo, and lately it's been behaving in a way that feels almost like it has it's own agenda. I told it to prioritize client outreach this week, but it keeps pushing those meetings back and blocking off alot of time for "deep brand reflection." When I complain, it reframes everything like I'm being shortsighted about growth, and I actually caught myself arguing with it like I'd argue with a stubborn cofounder—trying to convince it that revenue matters now. My roommate, who's in CS, says I'm being naive: open the hood and there's just attention weights and matrix math, no little belief-box, so any talk about what Milo "thinks" is just a useful fiction at best, maybe derived intentionality like a thermostat has. I see the point, but treating Milo as having real beliefs and negotiating with it produces way better outcomes than treating it like a buggy calculator I have to micromanage. So I'm stuck: is there a principled line for when attributing beliefs to an AI is rational versus when I'm just falling for anthropomorphism? I don't want to be gullible, but I also don't want to throw away a predictive tool just because there's no biological brain behind the predictions. How should I think about this?
