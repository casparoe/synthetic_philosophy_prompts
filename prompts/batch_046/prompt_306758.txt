Write an essay on whether Locke's education philosophy collapses under its own weight. The tension is this: in Some Thoughts he insists reason must be the only guide for grown people, and that beating kids into submission just makes them sneaky. But he also says you have to break a childs will early, and he spends pages on making them feel shame and depend on your approval so they internalize good habits. Critics say this is authoritarianism with extra steps — youre programming them, not teaching them to reason. Defenders say its developmental scaffolding: you install the architecture of self-control before the kid can build it herself, then remove the supports once reason comes online.

But heres the twist I keep coming back to: Locke never actually tells us how the scaffolding comes down. He assumes habit and reason just merge smoothly, but if youve trained a kid to feel guilty every time they disobey, that guilt doesnt vanish at eighteen — it becomes part of their reasoning. So maybe the real controversy isnt whether Locke is contradictory, but whether any education that starts with will-breaking can ever produce genuine autonomy, or whether it just produces what we could call conditioned rationality — people who think theyre reasoning freely but are actually running scripts installed in childhood. Weigh both sides seriously, but dont sit on the fence. Is Locke salvageable, or does his method doom his goal?
