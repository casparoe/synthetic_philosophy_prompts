For an upper-level philosophy seminar of 14 students, 80 minutes, design a structured role-play that makes participants feel why collective intentionality is not reducible to summed individual intentions—specifically, the gap between parallel I-intentions and a genuine we-intention that can bind members even when they privately dissent. The scenario: the group must collectively draft and sign a one-paragraph “joint charter” distributing a scarce shared resource (symbolic points that determine the seminar’s next discussion topic) among four sub-groups. Assign hidden interpretive frames without disclosure: some participants must read every collective utterance (“We choose...”) distributively (each individual chooses), others collectively (the unitary group chooses), and one or two must act as “dissent-channel” members who can only register disagreement through silence or a single veto token. Rules should prevent private clarification—no asking “what do you really mean?”—and require physical signing of the charter before anyone reveals their frame. Include specific roles (facilitator, recorder, veto-holder, silent observer), a three-phase timeline (drafting under constraint, collective defense to a mock “external party,” revelation of frames), a symbolic-payoff scheme that converts points into a real but small seminar consequence, and a debrief that maps the drafting failure, the signing moment, and the post-revelation breakdown onto the conditions for shared cooperative activity versus plural-subject joint commitment. Be explicit about which feature—likely the normative irreversibility of joint commitment once the charter is signed—this single-session activity cannot reproduce and must be supplied by the facilitator. Keep the framing technical enough to avoid generic “teamwork” exercises—refer to the distributive/collective ambiguity in first-person plural ascription—without requiring participants to know the literature by name in advance. End with an honest prediction of what will go wrong the first time it is run and how to recover.
