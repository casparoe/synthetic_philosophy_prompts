I am drafting a novel whose underlying metaphysics is strictly occasionalist in the manner of Al-Ghazālī: no created being possesses causal efficacy, and God alone produces every event in successive instants, with apparent natural regularities being nothing more than invariable divine habits. I need a short story, not a treatise, about a master clockmaker who realizes—through the intimate physical language of gears, oil, and silence—that his instruments do not genuinely cause one another to move, but are separately recreated at each tick with only the appearance of transmission. Let the philosophical insight emerge through sensory detail and action rather than exposition, and trace how this revelation transforms his understanding of craftsmanship, responsibility for a shattered mechanism, and grief for a dead apprentice. Keep the narrative grounded in the workshop's tangible reality, avoiding explicit theological argument.
