I'm opening a small workshop on embodied skill tommorrow. Half the audience are philosophers who've been fighting about the absent-minded driver problem since grad school, the other half are retired long-haul drivers who lived it every day. I want about five minutes of remarks built around a twist I'm calling the 'phantom passenger' scenario: the body is driving, the narrating self has clocked out, yet the foot still hits the brake for a deer in the lane. Use that to frame what's actually at stake about responsibility and expertise—so the philosophers see why it isn't just automatic behavior, and the truckers see why philosophers care about their autopilot moments. Don't turn it into a bibliography or a lecture; keep it conversational, and maybe acknowledge that the two groups probably mean 'attention' in seperate ways.
