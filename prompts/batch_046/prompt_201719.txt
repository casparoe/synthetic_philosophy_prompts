Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between the Inter-Theoretic Aggregationists and the Lexical-Breakpoint Robustness defenders could both sign, concerning how rational agents ought to act under deep moral uncertainty.

The Aggregationists hold that when an agent distributes non-zero credence across competing normative theories—some consequentialist with continuous cardinal scales, others deontological with lexical constraints, others virtue-based with incommensurable goods—the correct decision procedure is to construct inter-theoretic exchange rates (perhaps via a reference lottery or a shared metric of well-being) and then maximize credence-weighted expected moral value. They allow that exchange rates may be imprecise, but maintain that imprecision should be handled through standard decision-theoretic smoothing (e.g., sensitivity analysis or ambiguity-averse weighting) rather than by abandoning aggregation altogether.

The Robustness defenders hold that if any credible theory assigns a discontinuous or lexical priority to avoiding a specific class of outcomes—call them breakpoint violations—then no continuous exchange rate can legitimately translate those violations into the currency of other theories. Near a breakpoint, they argue, aggregation becomes unstable: arbitrarily small shifts in credence can produce arbitrarily large swings in the prescribed action. Consequently, they defend a constrained maximin or robustness rule: avoid breakpoint violations unless the credence in the theory that posits them falls below a contextually determined threshold, regardless of the expected-value calculus elsewhere.

Your task is to produce the document such a collaboration should yield. Include:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. These should cover: (a) the formal problem of inter-theoretic comparison; (b) the conditions under which two theories genuinely conflict versus talk past one another; (c) what both sides agree must be explained by any adequate decision rule (e.g., respect for the agent's actual credences, avoidance of arbitrary dictation by one theory, practical applicability); (d) the shared recognition that small credence shifts can alter prescriptions in some models; and (e) any agreement about what evidence or argument would be genuinely probative—distinguishing verbal disputes from substantive ones. Where the dispute is partly verbal, identify which part and explain why.

2. A disagreements table (or structured list) stating the precise residual disagreements. Each disagreement must be phrased in wording that its own side would accept as a fair characterization of their view, and that the opposing side does not reject as a straw man. At minimum, cover: whether inter-theoretic exchange rates are coherent in principle; whether discontinuities within individual theories necessarily generate discontinuities at the meta-level; and whether there exists a non-arbitrary method for setting the robustness threshold near a breakpoint.

3. The cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. I suspect the central cruxes are: (i) the coherence and circularity of constructing exchange rates without presupposing the very normative conclusions being compared; (ii) the "breakpoint instability" thesis—whether the mapping from credence distributions to prescriptions is genuinely discontinuous, or whether smoothing techniques restore continuity without loss; and (iii) the normative status of robustness itself—whether avoiding worst-case breakpoint violations is a rational requirement or an unjustified bias toward caution. For each crux, provide a concrete hypothetical case or formal argument that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each camp would predict about the outcome or force of that case.

Please present the entire document in British English (e.g., behaviour, centre, analyse, programme). Write in American English spelling and grammar in the prompt itself, but the requested answer should use British conventions. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific works, as the answer must stand without external verification. Ensure the framing is sufficiently in the weeds—introducing the technical notion of breakpoint instability and inter-theoretic exchange-rate smoothing—so that it cannot be answered by generic pre-training recitation, but do not invoke obscure named theories or figures that a well-trained model might not reliably recall.
