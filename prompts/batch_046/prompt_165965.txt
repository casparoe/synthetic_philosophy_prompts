I am drafting the final exam for an upper-level seminar on biography and moral philosophy. Write one essay question that forces students beyond summarizing Plutarch’s Parallel Lives and modern secular hagiography. The conceptual crux should be what I will call the “virtue-transfer fallacy”: the reader’s tendency to treat the narrative coherence of one exemplary life as sufficient justification for exporting its traits into different contexts. The question should ask students to assess whether Plutarch’s paired structure—specifically the formal synkrisis that closes each pair—actually blocks this fallacy, or whether it merely displaces it onto the comparison itself, and whether single-subject modern biography (e.g., founder hagiographies) is structurally more vulnerable because it lacks this pairing mechanism. The prompt should require students to identify both the blocking mechanism and the displacement risk. Provide the exam question, then a model answer of no more than 550 words that takes a definite position: argue that the pairing blocks the fallacy locally but displaces it globally, and specify exactly which feature of the synkrisis does the epistemic work. Follow this with a marking scheme totaling 20 marks, fitting within 150 words, that separates an A answer (which diagnoses the local block and global displacement) from a B answer (which describes the pairing without analyzing its epistemic function). Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and do not invoke obscure theories or specific texts that require verification; the argument should stand on conceptual analysis of narrative exemplarity alone. Keep the entire response tight and formal.
