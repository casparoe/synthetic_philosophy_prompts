My board is fighting about whether to fund it over a sure thing that helps a thousand people now. I need a reusable procedure my team can actually run, not a philosophy essay.

Please write a decision procedure — checklist style, steps in order — for evaluating grant proposals that claim infinite or astronomically large future benefits. Ground it in whatever infinite ethics actually knows about comparing infinite values: the failure of simple multiplication, cardinality problems, the epsilon-lexicographic trap, and where a critical-level cutoff makes sense. Then run it on three cases: (1) the one-in-a-million infinite-future proposal; (2) a proposal that saves exactly 10,000 lives now with zero future effect; and (3) a proposal where the 'infinite' claim was a math error but there's still a large finite upside. Include at least one case where the procedure rejects but I personally think it should win, and flag the spots where the procedure just breaks down entirely. Keep it practical: we have a week, no math PhD on staff, and the board has to sign off on anything over fifty thousand.
