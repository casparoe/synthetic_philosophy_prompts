After a long argument with a friend about Marx, I am trying to determine whether exploitation and alienation are two descriptions of a single mechanism or genuinely independent philosophical critiques. By exploitation I mean the structural extraction of surplus value that arises from the asymmetry between labor-power sold as a commodity and capital that controls the means of production; by alienation I mean the worker's estrangement from the product, the labor process, species-being, and other workers. My friend insisted that for Marx these are inseparable, because the separation of the worker from control over production is the common root that makes both surplus extraction and subjective estrangement necessary. I proposed a counter-case: imagine a democratically governed enterprise that still must extract surplus labor to survive competitive market pressures, where workers exercise meaningful collective control over the production process yet the economic relation remains technically exploitative. Would Marx regard this arrangement as alienated, or does alienation require a specific subjective experience of powerlessness that democratic control could eliminate even while surplus extraction continues? I want to know whether exploitation can exist without alienation, whether alienation can exist without exploitation, and which concept carries the fundamental normative weight in Marx's philosophy—whether we are dealing with one theory articulated twice, or two distinct theories that happen to converge under capitalism.
