Write a short research prospectus (approximately 1,500–2,000 words, structured as a one-semester independent study proposal) addressing the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: whether the manifest phenomenological differences among gustatory, intellectual, aesthetic, and somatic pleasures defeat the claim that well-being is constituted solely by pleasure, and if not, what kind of unity hedonism actually requires.

State the precise research question: is the diversity of pleasures a problem for hedonism only on the assumption that pleasure must share a uniform intrinsic phenomenal quality, and can hedonism be preserved by reconceiving pleasure as a functionally unified family of states—specifically, as affective reward-prediction errors that share a structural motivational role rather than a common feel?

Explain why existing answers leave this open. The standard phenomenalist defense insists on a hidden hedonic tone common to all pleasures, but this invites the charge of ad hoc phenomenological speculation. The eliminativist alternative abandons substantive unity altogether, yet this risks collapsing hedonism into a pluralistic preference-satisfaction theory. Your proposal should identify the gap: neither camp adequately addresses whether cross-modal well-being comparisons require phenomenal similarity or can be sustained by functional equivalence alone.

Propose a specific approach. Argue that hedonism should abandon phenomenal essentialism and adopt a teleofunctional account according to which pleasures are states that (i) represent outcomes as better than expected, (ii) motivate repetition, and (iii) are subjectively endorsed, regardless of sensory modality. Defend this approach against the obvious alternatives—phenomenalist essentialism and eliminativist pluralism—by showing that it alone preserves hedonism's explanatory advantages (the asymmetry of pleasure and pain, the possibility of miswanting) without requiring an implausible uniform feel.

Specify what a result would look like and what would count as failure. A successful result demonstrates that functional unity is sufficient for well-being aggregation across pleasure-types and answers the heterogeneity objection without invoking hidden qualia. Failure would occur if cross-modal comparison proves incoherent even on functionalist grounds—for instance, if the motivational structures of intellectual and bodily pleasures are incommensurable—or if the functional account cannot distinguish pleasure from non-hedonic reward states like relief or satisfaction.

Assess scope honestly. Can this be completed in one semester without original empirical data? Identify the specific risks: the need to navigate philosophy-of-mind literature on affective representation without getting bogged down in neuroscience, and the danger of conflating functional role with normative value.

Write with commitment. Do not survey options neutrally; take the definite position that the functionalist reconstruction is the only viable path for hedonism, defend it explicitly against phenomenalist objections, make definite choices about which counterarguments to engage and which to set aside, and argue for your methodological path rather than describing it. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
