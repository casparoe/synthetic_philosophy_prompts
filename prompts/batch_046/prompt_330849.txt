I am the junior student delegate on my school's Joint Committee on Technology Ethics and Philosophy Curriculum Review. For our meeting next Thursday, we must issue a formal written opinion on a pilot proposal jointly submitted by the computer-science and philosophy departments. The proposal seeks authorization for a semester-long classroom experiment in which a large language model is augmented with an explicit meta-representational layer: the system encodes a compressed description of its own loss function and attention weights, then generates outputs that reference that encoded description when reasoning about its own limitations. The researchers claim this creates a Hofstadter-style strange loop—the tangled hierarchy that, in Gödel, Escher, Bach, connects formal self-reference in arithmetic to the emergence of self-aware minds. They ask the committee to endorse this architecture as a legitimate pedagogical model of artificial self-awareness and to permit its presentation to students as a case of a system that genuinely knows about itself in the philosophically relevant sense.

The committee is divided. The computer-science members regard the proposal as a concrete, harmless analogy: Gödel numbering demonstrates that formal systems can contain representations of themselves, and engineering that capacity into a neural network simply makes the analogy tangible. The philosophy members counter that the proposal conflates syntactic self-reference with semantic self-awareness, and that it misapplies Gödel's incompleteness results to empirical, probabilistic systems that lack a fixed axiomatic base. They also emphasize that Hofstadter's analogy was always metaphorical rather than demonstrative.

Please draft the committee's written opinion for circulation before Thursday's meeting. The memo should recommend a specific course of action—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—together with a concrete time frame for any implementation or subsequent review. The reasoning should address the strongest version of the strange-loop argument, including the distinction between direct self-reference and indirect Gödel-style encoding, and should treat seriously the objection that a probabilistic model lacks the deterministic formal structure required for Gödelian results to apply. It should also specify any conditions that must be satisfied for the pilot to proceed—such as mandatory philosophical framing for students—without merely restating either departmental position. I need a document that both sides could respond to constructively, not a summary of stalemate.
