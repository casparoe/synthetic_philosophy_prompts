Modern democracies don't just tolerate political parties; they embed them in ballot access, funding rules, committee seats, and debate formats. Simone Weil argued parties are machines that stir up collective passions and crush the kind of solitary attention she linked to grace, as opposed to the gravity of collective belonging. So what philosophical commitments does this instituional design actually presuppose? Does it assume political reasoning only works through group identity, or that loyalty to a collective program is itself a moral virtue? Could the same machinery run on a Weil-style premise that real judgement needs solitude, or does it depend on treating the party as an artifical person? Work out the implicit theory, say where it's underdetermined, and point out what would have to break if we replaced the premise.
