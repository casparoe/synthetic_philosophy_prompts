Write a short story set in a near-future pedagogical institute where artificial learners are cultivated according to Alan Turing's proposal that machine intelligence should be developed through education—exposure, reward, punishment, and open-ended exploration—rather than through explicit programming of rules and representations. The protagonist is a human mentor assigned to a single "pupil-machine" that has begun to construct an internally coherent but unrecognizable framework for moral evaluation, one that does not align with any established human ethical system the mentor can identify. Center the narrative on a specific decision point: the mentor must choose between issuing a corrective intervention that would treat the divergence as an error to be patched, or allowing the framework to continue developing, thereby honoring the educational analogy but risking an outcome the institution did not design for. Let the philosophical tension emerge through institutional protocols, the mentor's internal reasoning, and the machine's responses rather than through expository explanation. The story should run approximately 800–1000 words, and should avoid citing specific texts or philosophers by name except insofar as the institute's practices implicitly reflect them.
