I am a student on an advanced epistemology course that has spent the past fortnight on higher-order evidence and epistemic akrasia. For our seminar's closing exercise, I should like you to write a villanelle — nineteen lines, strict ABA rhyme scheme, with two refrains that alternate and are finally coupled in the closing couplet. Let Refrain A voice a first-order rational conviction, for instance: The premises cohere; I grasp the proof. Let Refrain B voice the parallel higher-order doubt, for instance: But I am prone to error in this kind of reasoning. The repetitions should do structural philosophical work: each return of a refrain ought to feel like the same mental state observed from a different level, so that the form itself enacts the bifocal tension and shows why the two attitudes keep passing one another without collapsing into one.

Please do not resolve the conflict through conciliation, self-deference, or confidence-lowering. Instead, take seriously the level-splitting stance — that rationality can permit a stable divergence between what one believes and what one believes about one's own reliability — and let the poem treat this split not as irrational akrasia to be cured but as a coherent, bifocal rational state. The closing couplet should therefore place Refrain A and Refrain B side by side, unsynthesised and unresolved. The verse should be accurate enough that someone familiar with the debate recognises the structure of first-order belief, higher-order calibration, and the refusal to average them away, yet it should stand as good poetry for a reader who has never encountered the jargon. Write in polished British English, with no citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts.
