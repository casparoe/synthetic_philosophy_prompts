My opening: in my old field we used to model molecular bonds as interactions, not as little hooks sitting between atoms. Reading your work on the unity of the proposition, I see you argue that if a relation R connects A and B, then you need further relations to connect A-to-R and R-to-B, and so on forever. But in quantum entanglement two particles share a single state — there's no third object linking them, just an internal correlation. So isn't entanglement exactly the kind of internal relation you defend? And if every relation is internal like that, doesn't multiplicity disappear entirely — don't all propositions collapse into one undifferentiated reality? How do you stop the regress without losing the very differences your propositions are meant to express?
