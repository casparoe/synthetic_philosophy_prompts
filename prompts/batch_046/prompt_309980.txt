I'm currently enrolled in an upper-level history of philosophy seminar on early modern method and the birth of modern political science. For my midterm essay, I want to examine a tension in Hobbes's work that we have discussed only briefly: Hobbes famously insisted that geometry was the only science that had achieved demonstrative certainty and that civil philosophy should be reconstructed on the same model. At the same time, he spent years attempting to solve the quadrature of the circle and related classical problems, efforts that were ridiculed by mathematical contemporaries and are generally regarded as failures. Write an essay analyzing whether Hobbes's personal failures in geometric practice undermine his epistemic authority in claiming that politics can be made as certain as geometry, or whether these failures actually illuminate the specific kind of certainty he sought. In particular, engage with the distinction Hobbes draws in De Corpore between the method of reasoning from first principles and the art of construction or calculation. Does his inability to construct certain figures indicate that political science must rely on a different kind of foundation—perhaps on authoritative definition and authorization rather than on spatial intuition—or does it suggest that Hobbes fundamentally misunderstood the geometric method he wanted to emulate? Weigh both interpretations carefully, but do not end on an agnostic note: defend a specific verdict about what Hobbes's geometric failures reveal about the status of his political science.
