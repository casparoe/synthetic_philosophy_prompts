There's this deadlock in the philosophy of psychiatry around Hacking's looping effects that's been circling since the 90s and I can't tell if it's substantive or just people using "social construction" in incompatible ways. Side A says that with conditions like autism or ADHD, applying the diagnosis changes how people understand themselves and behave, so the category feeds back into the population it describes — this makes the boundary of the disorder unstable and undermines treating it as a natural kind with sharp edges. Side B replies that feedback is normal in human sciences, it doesn't erase the underlying neurobiology, and looping is just a measurement problem not an ontological one. But every exchange I see ends with Side A saying you're ignoring the perfomative dimension and Side B saying you're conflating causation with classification. Is this actually about ontology vs epistemology? Or are they answering different questions — one about whether autism is "real", the other about why prevelance rates keep climbing? I also want to throw in a weird second-order idea: what if clinicians and patients start looping the loop — like people learn the diagnositic script from social media or clinical manuals and start performing symptoms that fit the DSM criteria precisely, so the loop stops tracking an underlying trait and starts tracking literasy in psychiatric taxonomy? Call that meta-looping. Does adding that change the diagnosis of the debate, or is it just more grist for the same mill? Diagnose the stall concretely — is it verbal, is there a hidden empirical question about prevalence, are the sides using different standards for what counts as a natural kind — and tell me what progress would even look like here. A formal model? A study that separates meta-looping from first-order looping? Or is the whole framing ill-posed? Don't just pick a winner; I want the structure of the stall itself.
