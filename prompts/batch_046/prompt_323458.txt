For an upper-level seminar in formal epistemology, draft a 90-minute essay exam question on the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents, together with a model answer of about 600 words and a 20-point marking scheme. The question must not be answerable by simply reciting the standard unbounded derivation in modal arithmetic; instead, require students to examine a bounded agent that trusts its own reasoning only for proofs below complexity n, and then to evaluate whether adopting a typed trust hierarchy—where an agent at level k may endorse reflection principles only for levels strictly below k—genuinely dissolves the obstacle or merely relocates it via a diagonal argument on the type assignment itself. Specify the relevant formal framework (e.g., GL restricted to an appropriate fragment with bounded proof predicates) and the philosophical stakes concerning reflective closure. The marking scheme should separate an A answer, which must contain the formal restriction, the typed-trust proposal, the relocated diagonal argument, and the philosophical point that boundedness fails to secure self-trust, from a B answer, which typically recites the classic obstacle and treats boundedness or typing as an obvious fix without engaging the relocation.
