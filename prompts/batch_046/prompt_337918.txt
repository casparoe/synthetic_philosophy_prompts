Before I teach the doctrine of double effect in clinical triage, I want the full spec for an adequate normative framework. Write the desiderata: what it must deliver — the cases it has to get right (classic switch, bridge with the fat man, ventilator withdrawal where harm is foreseen but not intended, and the algorithimic allocation case where no individual agent "intends" anything), the distinctions it can't collapse (intended as means vs mere side-effect, causing vs allowing, individual vs institutional agency), the puzzles it has to dissolve (like why direct harm feels different even when numbers are identical). For each, give the motivating case. Flag any pair of desiderata that can't be jointly satisfied — I think "no distributed intention" and "double-effect still applies" might clash. Then briefly score the standard Catholic double-effect view, a utilitarian scorecard, and a rights-based veto against the list. Don't cite sources, just reason it through.
