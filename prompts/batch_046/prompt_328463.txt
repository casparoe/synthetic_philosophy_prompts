I've been trying to map out where I actually stand on psychiatric ontology for a seminar I'm running on Hacking, and I'm not attatched to whether my pieces actually fit. Here's the rough package: (1) psychiatric kinds are interactive kinds—classifications like dissociative identity disorder loop back and reshape the people falling under them, so the kind itself shifts over time. (2) But for schizophrenia I want to say there's a pretty invariant biological substrate; looping might alter symptom expression or help-seeking, but it doesn't reach the core neurobiological reality. (3) So looping doesn't make psychiatric kinds unreal—it's more like a feedback loop that sculpts a real but malleable phenomenon, not pure social construction. (4) Still, I think taxonomy should aim for dynamic stability: categories responsive enough to loop in constructively useful ways, but stable enough that clinicians can predict course and response. (5) And I reject both hard biological essentialism and full-blooded social constructionism, obviosly.

Does this hang together? I'm especially worried that (2) is sneaking in an ad hoc split—if looping can reshape the kind for DID but not schizophrenia, am I just drawing a line where it's convenient? Or is there a principled way to separate interactive from indifferent features within the same taxonomy? Also, does (4) commit me to dampening looping, which might conflict with (3)'s idea that looping is part of what makes the kind real? Rank the tensions by severity—don't just list them as equally bad—and tell me the cheapest revision that cleans up the package. I'm not attatched to any particular move here, I just want it sorted befoer I present.
