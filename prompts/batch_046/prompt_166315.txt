As a novelist constructing a narrative set within a pluralistic moral ecology, I am seeking a rigorous conceptual scaffold rather than a polemic. Draft the joint statement that might emerge from an adversarial collaboration between two camps: those who interpret cross-cultural differences in folk morality—particularly the documented divergence between WEIRD and non-WEIRD populations—as surface-level noise around a universal, evolved moral architecture; and those who interpret the same differences as evidence that moral cognition is deeply shaped by local socio-ecological conditions, with WEIRD intuitions representing a narrow, historically anomalous subset. The document should be formatted in two parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: include shared acknowledgments about which empirical patterns are robust (for instance, variation in the weighting of purity-based versus harm-based reasoning, differences in the scope of cooperative obligations, and framing effects observed in resource-allocation paradigms), about the limits of current sampling, and about what a genuine universalist or constructivist position must explain without contradiction. Second, a disagreements table that states each camp's precise position in wording that its own proponents would accept, avoiding straw formulations; identifies the cruxes—specifically, whether moral objectivity requires convergence at the level of intuitive judgment or can be preserved solely at the level of reflective reasoning capacities, and whether the ecological prevalence of a moral norm within a particular environment counts as any kind of evidential support for its normative validity; and, for each crux, presents a concrete hypothetical case or observed empirical pattern that both sides agree is relevant, followed by each side's explicit predictive interpretation of that case. The statement should not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citation; it should remain meaningful to a reader without web access; and it should treat the disagreement as substantive rather than merely verbal, though if any portion of the dispute dissolves upon clarification, that dissolution should be noted explicitly with the reason given.
