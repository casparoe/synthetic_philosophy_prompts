ok so me and my friend were arguing for like two hours about where meaning actually lives, after i brought up hofstadters frame/outer/inner mesage idea from that old book with the record player analogy. i think i have some gut verdicts but theyre totally scattered.

heres my cases:

1. the meme with the cartoon dog sitting in a burning room saying everything is okay. frame is the meme format. outer message is literal wellness. inner message is denial/irony about disaster. i think the real meaning is the inner one, carried by the frame.

2. a peer-reviewed study that says the data do not support the hypothesis, but the p-value printed right there is .0001. frame is scientific caution. outer message is rejection. inner message might be that the authors actually believe it but had to hedge for reviewers. i say the meaning is just the outer message; we dont get to read minds.

3. an AI chatbot writes a very moving poem about grieving a dead sister. frame is a system with no consciousness. outer message is grief and loss. inner message? there isnt one, no inner life. so meaning is only frame plus outer message, like a mirror reflecting something it doesnt feel.

4. a billboard misprint accidentally says eat poison instead of eat peas. frame is advertisement. i think the meaning is literally eat poison, even though everyone knows its a mistake. we shouldnt correct it to the intended inner message.

5. but then a math paper has a sign error in the proof of a theorem. frame is academic mathematics. outer message is a false claim. inner message is the correct proof the author clearly intended. here i think we should correct it to the inner message.

so 4 and 5 clash, right? in one i stick to the literal outer message, in the other i override with intended inner.

6. a DNA sequence encodes both a functional protein and, through whitespace patterns in the sequence, the phrase attack at dawn. frame is biological code for people with the decryption protocol; without that frame its just genes. with the frame, inner message is a military order. i think if you have the frame, the inner message is real meaning; without it, only the outer biological message exists.

the case i keep flipping on: a chatbot gives gardening advice that sounds normal except for one phrase embedded in it that includes a racial slur, like prune these bushes aggressively, like those people always did. frame is AI assistant. outer message is gardening tips. inner message might be reproduced bias from training data. is this meaning in the inner message (the model meant something toxic) or is it just frame corruption (no real message, only statistical noise)? i go back and forth.

is there one principle that gets 1 through 6, or do i have to drop one? what does it predict for something i havent listed, like a legal contract with hidden terms in the whitespace of the pdf? and please say what would change your mind — which argument or consideration would make you switch your verdict on whether the frame can ever be wrong, or where your reasoning is shakiest. also be honest about which of my verdicts your principle cant save.
