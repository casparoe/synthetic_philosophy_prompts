I am preparing for a graduate seminar on metaphysics and the philosophy of language, and we have spent several sessions circling Bradley's regress without ever stepping back to ask what we are actually demanding from a solution. Rather than comparing particular theories head-to-head, I want the criteria articulated first: what must any adequate account of the unity of the proposition actually deliver in order to count as having solved the problem, not merely relocated it?

Write out the full list of desiderata. The theory must explain how a proposition differs from a mere aggregate of its constituents—why "Socrates is wise" constitutes a single thing with a determinable truth-value rather than two items sitting adjacent to one another. It must preserve the intuition that understanding a false proposition is grasping the very same unified content that could be true under other conditions, rather than apprehending disconnected fragments that fail to cohere. It must respect the distinction between the internal unity of the proposition and the external unity of a state of affairs or fact, since conflating the two dissolves the regress only by changing the subject. It must accommodate propositional attitudes—believing, doubting, asserting—without requiring that every attitude involves a direct relation to a fully constituted fact in the world. And it must achieve all of this without generating a vicious infinite regress of unifying relations, yet also without treating unity as an utterly brute, inexplicable primitive if that primitive can be shown to be doing exactly the explanatory work that the original relating-relation was meant to do.

For each desideratum, state the specific conceptual pressure or case that motivates it, and classify it as genuinely non-negotiable or as a preference belonging to a particular camp—such as ontological parsimony, compatibility with a naturalist metaphysics, or a preference for structural complexity over primitive relations. Crucially, indicate whether you think any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, especially whether the demand for a non-arbitrary, internally structured unity can be satisfied without either reinstating the regress or abandoning structural complexity altogether. Then, very briefly, map how three broad families of approach fare against the list: a view that takes exemplification or predication as a primitive relation; a view that treats propositions as structured entities with a non-relational mode of combination; and a deflationary or sententialist view that denies propositions possess the kind of internal structure that would generate the regress in the first place. Do not supply a bibliography or reading list; I need this specification to stand on its own.
