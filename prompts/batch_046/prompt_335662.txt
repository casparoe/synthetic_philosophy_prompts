You are a professional philosopher specializing in axiology and the philosophy of well-being. Before you begin writing, take a moment to outline the strongest arguments on each side of the controversy, then proceed to construct the essay step by step with conceptual precision.

The view in question—call it Tranquilism—holds that well-being is constituted exclusively by the absence of craving (tanha) and the absence of suffering (dukkha). On this account, pleasure, achievement, love, knowledge, and aesthetic experience possess no intrinsic value; at best they are instrumentally valuable insofar as they diminish craving or prevent suffering. Positive states are not goods in themselves; only the negative space—the absence of craving and pain—counts.

Write a rigorous essay weighing whether Tranquilism offers a correct account of well-being. Your essay should do the following:

1. Present the core motivation for Tranquilism, drawing on the asymmetry between suffering and pleasure (that suffering demands explanation and remedy in a way pleasure does not) and on the claim that craving is the root of discontent. Be precise about what the view denies: it is not merely that suffering is worse than pleasure is good, but that only the absence of negative valence has axiological weight.

2. Develop two serious objections that move beyond familiar complaints. First, address the Agency Neutrality problem: Tranquilism must evaluate the well-being of a fully rational agent who has extinguished craving but retains autonomous capacities—for instance, continuing to teach or create out of non-craving habit—as identical to that of a being in a permanent affectively neutral, motivation-suppressing brain state with only reflexive behavior. If Tranquilism cannot distinguish these, is that a virtue or a reductio? Second, address the Instrumental Collapse problem: if all positive goods are purely instrumental to the absence of suffering, then in a hypothetical world where suffering is structurally impossible, art, science, and friendship have zero value. Does Tranquilism commit us to a kind of axiological nihilism about human culture in a post-suffering utopia?

3. Consider a defense of Tranquilism that attempts to accommodate these worries without importing positive goods—perhaps by arguing that agency and culture are themselves subtle forms of craving, or that their preservation is justified only as risk-management against future suffering.

4. Reach a clear, defended conclusion. You may defend a purified Tranquilism, a modified version that adds a minimal non-hedonic condition (such as the preservation of rational agency without craving), or reject Tranquilism in favor of a pluralist account. Your conclusion should explicitly state what feature of well-being your verdict preserves that Tranquilism either successfully captures or fatally omits.

Write in a formal philosophical register. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Do not invoke highly specialized technical frameworks that depend on obscure literature unless you explain them fully in the text. Focus on conceptual clarity and argumentative force.
