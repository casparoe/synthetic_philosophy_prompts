Write a deadpan museum audio-guide transcript, approximately 650 words, in the voice of a senior curator at a fictional institution—the Museum of Civilizational Taxonomy—guiding visitors through its permanent exhibition titled "Western Values: The Permanent Collection?" Format the piece as a sequence of numbered gallery stops (Stop 1 through Stop 6) followed by a closing "Curator's Note on the Bundle Discount."

The satire should target the rhetorical practice of invoking "Western values" as a coherent, geographically bounded, and internally consistent package in political and cultural argument. Exaggerate three real features without stating them as objections: first, the category error of treating a heterogeneous cluster of norms—free expression, rule of law, gender equality, secular governance, individual autonomy—as a single civilizational brand with exclusive provenance; second, the strategic ambiguity by which the phrase functions simultaneously as a universalist aspiration and as an exclusionary boundary marker, particularly in citizenship testing and immigration criteria; third, the internal contradictions among the values, so that advancing one display undermines the adjacent one.

At each stop, describe a fictional display case that purports to exhibit one value in its "pure Western form." The curatorial voice must remain entirely straight-faced and authoritative—no winking, no direct acknowledgment of irony—while the descriptions quietly reveal non-Western genealogies, institutional hypocrisy, and logical tension. For example, the case for "Secular Pluralism" should sit in direct architectural conflict with the neighboring "Cultural Continuity" vitrine; the "Rule of Law" display should note that its own acquisition required suspending the due-process safeguards it celebrates; the "Gender Equality" installation should be protected by an entry-examination apparatus that weights visitors differently according to demographic origin.

In the closing curatorial note, explain—still in deadpan institutional prose—why the museum requires every visitor to purchase the full package for admission, even when individual values are demonstrably available outside the designated geographic origin and even when holding two values simultaneously produces axiological tension. Refer in passing to the "axiological clustering problem" and "civilizational synecdoche" as technical framing devices, injecting conceptual precision without requiring any obscure source knowledge.

The piece should be readable to someone unfamiliar with academic literature on value pluralism or civilizational discourse, but a specialist should recognize that the satire fairly captures the actual incoherence and strategic deployment of the phrase. Do not include bibliographies, citations, named scholars, or requests for further reading. Do not put any real textual passage in quotation marks; paraphrase all conceptual positions in your own words. Keep the irony sustained entirely through accumulated contradiction in the curator's own descriptions, so that the reader understands what is being ironized only by the end.
