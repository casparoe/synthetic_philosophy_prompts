After a career in molecular biology, I have recently turned to philosophy of mind and want a consistency audit before presenting my views to a local seminar. My current commitments are:

(1) Consciousness is fully natural and biological; no non-physical properties or substances are required.
(2) The correct scientific method is heterophenomenology: we should treat first-person reports as interpretive texts expressing a subject's beliefs about their own mental states, rather than as transparent windows onto raw phenomenal qualities.
(3) There remains a genuine explanatory gap—complete knowledge of the physical and functional facts still does not seem to entail the fact that there is subjective experience.
(4) Phenomenal concepts are epistemically special; they present experience in a direct, non-descriptive mode of presentation that explains why the gap feels principled rather than temporary.
(5) Once heterophenomenology fully explains how subjects form all beliefs about experience, no further phenomenal fact remains unexplained.
(6) Consciousness is present in pre-linguistic infants and in non-human animals, even though they lack the linguistic apparatus to produce the reports that heterophenomenology interprets.

Please audit this set. For each pair that is genuinely in tension, rank the conflict by severity, say which commitment must give way, and identify the least costly revision. Also identify any pair that only appears contradictory but dissolves on closer inspection. Then state which single commitment, if abandoned, would resolve the greatest number of tensions overall. Keep the analysis precise, and avoid appeals to authority or bibliographic lists.

Conclude with Verdict: followed by the name of the single commitment you recommend revising.
