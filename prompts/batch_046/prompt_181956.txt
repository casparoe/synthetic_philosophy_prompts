I have been helping design a course on comparative cognition, and the distinction between Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures has become unexpectedly personal: I keep wondering whether my own reasoning about animal minds is itself an instance of Gregorian scaffolding, and whether that undermines the taxonomy from within. A colleague recently walked me through an argument that seems to collapse the four kinds into one, and I cannot locate the slip. Here is how it runs:

(1) Gregorian creatures are defined by their capacity to deploy external representational vehicles—language, diagrams, symbolic rules—to generate and evaluate candidate behaviors offline, thereby escaping the trial-and-error costs borne by Popperian creatures.
(2) Once a creature possesses this capacity, every subsequent cognitive task, including those that appear purely perceptual or affective, can in principle be mediated by these vehicles, because the creature can always bring representational scaffolding to bear on the task at hand.
(3) Therefore, in any Gregorian creature, even apparently direct Darwinian selection (for instance, reflex withdrawal from heat) or Skinnerian conditioning is actually governed by an underlying representational architecture that interprets and modulates the response.
(4) But if all cognitive processes in such a creature are representationally mediated, then there remains no cognitive domain that operates in the non-representational, direct manner characteristic of Darwinian, Skinnerian, or even Popperian minds.
(5) So either Gregorian creatures possess no direct cognitive processes whatsoever—which seems biologically absurd, since they still have retinas, spinal reflex arcs, and hormonal responses—or the presence of Gregorian capacities transforms every other capacity into a Gregorian one.
(6) The latter option collapses Dennett’s four-way taxonomy into a single kind: any creature capable of Gregorian cognition is, in every respect, Gregorian.
(7) Yet we want to maintain that a human being is Gregorian with respect to mathematical reasoning but Darwinian with respect to the pupillary reflex; if (5) is true, that distinction is illusory.

The conclusion feels like a reductio, but each premise looks defensible on its own. Is the mistake in (2), where I slide illegitimately from "can deploy representational scaffolding" to "does deploy it everywhere"? Or is there a hidden equivocation between representational mediation and representational governance—between a process being influenced by representations and being constituted by them? Or should I bite the bullet and accept that the taxonomy describes evolutionary stages rather than coexisting capacities, so that a Gregorian creature is indeed Gregorian through and through, and the appearance of lower-level directness is merely a phenomenological illusion? I want to preserve the intuition that human beings genuinely combine all four kinds, but I need to know which premise to reject and what that rejection costs for the coherence of the framework.
