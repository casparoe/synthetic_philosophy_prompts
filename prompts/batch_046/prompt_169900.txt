I'm trying to sort out what I actually think about originality in this one microdomain I've been obsessed with lately — the replication of ceremonial masks in a workshop tradition where apprentices spend years copying the master's pieces exactly, including intentional imperfections like a hairline crack near the left eye. Here are my gut verdicts on cases, and honestly their kinda inconsistent.

Case 1: An apprentice carves a mask that reproduces every tool mark and that signature crack. I say that's a copy, not an original, period.

Case 2: The master makes a mask with the crack deliberately placed. The apprentice copies it exactly. Still a copy, easy call.

Case 3: The apprentice, now working alone, accidentaly shifts the eye spacing a few millimeters but keeps the crack. Over time the village starts using this version for actual rites, and the old version gets treated like an archive piece. Here I feel like it's become a new original, not just derivative — but I can't really defend why.

Case 4: A museum 3D-scans the original master mask and prints it in resin for ritual use when the wood is too fragile. I want to call this still just a copy, maybe even worse — but structurally it's taking over the functional role just like case 3 did. That's the pair I keep going back and forth on.

Case 5: A factory elsewhere injection-molds the shape in plastic with the crack included. Not original, clear.

Case 6: The apprentice's altered version from case 3 gets scanned and printed in resin. I think that's a copy of the new original, which feels right. But then why is case 4 different? I need that tension resolved.

Is there a principle — maybe about causal history, communal uptake, or the maker's intentional state — that captures most of these? Or do I just have to drop one verdict to keep things simple? And what should I predict for a case I haven't listed: the master himself later carves a mask that accidentaly matches the apprentice's altered eye spacing, without ever seeing it? Would that be an original, or a copy of a copy he doesn't know about?
