Here is my opening move: I grant that Nāgārjuna’s tetralemma (catuṣkoṭi) systematically denies that any phenomenon possesses inherent nature in the fourfold manner—being, non-being, both, or neither. But when this very denial is reflexively applied to emptiness (the so-called emptiness of emptiness), it appears to generate a self-referential loop structurally parallel to a formal system that asserts its own incompleteness from within its own axiomatic frame. If emptiness is not ultimately real (first corner denied), not ultimately unreal (second denied), not both (third denied), and not neither (fourth denied), then the statement of this refusal seems to require either a hidden metalanguage or a fifth position outside the tetralemma in order to avoid performative contradiction. Does this not imply that Madhyamaka either secretly reifies emptiness as an unstateable transcendental ground—thereby violating its own prohibition on svabhāva—or collapses into contradiction? I ask you to address this Gödelian-style loop directly: show how the self-application of the four extremes avoids both reification and logical collapse without smuggling in an extra-logical position, and do not resolve the tension by merely appealing to conventional language as a separate sphere.
