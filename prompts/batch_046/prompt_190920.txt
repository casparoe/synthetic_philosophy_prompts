Write a decision tree that classifies responses to the situationist critique of virtue ethics, intended for a graduate seminar in moral psychology. The root node should ask whether the empirical finding of low cross-situational consistency in behaviour genuinely undermines virtue ethics’ descriptive claim that people possess stable character traits. From there, branch on three successive distinctions: (i) whether the variability is best read as absence of traits, as context-sensitive expression of traits, or as measurement artefact; (ii) whether the normative ambition of virtue ethics requires descriptively accurate global traits, or can survive on reconceived “local” or “skill-like” virtues; and (iii) whether moral development should aim at domain-general practical wisdom or at modular, institutionally calibrated sensitivities — the latter being a relatively unexplored middle position that treats virtue as indexed to social role rather than to personality. Every leaf should carry a named position (e.g. classical virtue ethics, eliminativist situationism, narrative revisionism, local-expertise virtue ethics) or be marked “unnamed,” together with one sentence stating its most awkward normative commitment. Flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate, and briefly diagnose whether the gap is logical, empirical, or merely terminological. Keep the tree to eight to ten leaves, and phrase every node as a concrete claim a student could assent to or reject without already knowing the philosophical labels.
