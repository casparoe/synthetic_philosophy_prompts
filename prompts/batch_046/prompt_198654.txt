For a graduate seminar on non-human moral status, please construct a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts actual philosophical positions on whether moral considerability comes in degrees running continuously from insects downward to thermostats. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of named theorists: each internal node should pose a concrete diagnostic question about a specific capacity or case—whether an entity with nociception but no centralized integrative cortex (e.g., a decapod crustacean) possesses non-zero status; whether a system with homeostatic error-correction but no predictive modeling of aversive states (e.g., a thermostat, or a plant regulating turgor pressure) has any moral status whatsoever; whether gradation is unidimensional along a single scale of sentience or requires separate, non-commensurable axes for sentience, agency, and ecological embeddedness; and whether there exists a non-arbitrary zero-point below which status is genuinely nil, or only an asymptotic decay toward negligibility. The leaves should carry named positions where established ones exist—radical continuous gradualism, threshold sentientism, multi-axis vector gradation, functional homeostatic minimalism, relational or ecological gradation, and binary rights-based exclusion—plus at least one explicitly unnamed combination; each leaf should include one sentence stating its most awkward commitment (for instance, that it must treat a highly optimized climate-control network as bearing a vanishing but technically non-zero claim, or that it collapses the moral difference between a honeybee and a bacterium into a mere quantitative gap). Map out every combination of branch-answers the tree permits; flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate, noting whether they differ only in metaphysical framing rather than in case-verdicts. Identify which logical slots remain unoccupied and briefly diagnose each—whether the combination is incoherent, normatively unattractive, or merely unexplored. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the deliverable is the diagnostic instrument itself. Keep the tree to eight leaves or fewer, and phrase every node as a question that a student could answer on the basis of case-intuitions alone, without already knowing which philosophical label it leads to.
