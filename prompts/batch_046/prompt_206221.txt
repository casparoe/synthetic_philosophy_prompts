I've been trying to figure out how anyone actually knows what happened in like ancient greece or rome, not just the stories we repeat. I read that Herodotus would check different accounts against eachother, and Thucydides only trusted people who actually saw stuff happen. But the whole debate about distant past knowledge feels totally stalled - one side says with enough cross-checking and archeology we can be pretty sure, the other side says it's all just reconstructed narratives with too much noise and bias. Is this actually about evidence, or is it about what counts as knowing something? I keep thinking about this weird case: what if we found a complete library from 300 BC but every book was written by the same guy with a clear political agenda, yet everything he said matched up with other fragments we have? Would that prove we can know the past, or would people just say the other fragments are corrupted too? I want to understand what kind of disagreement this really is, and whether there's anything that could actually move it, or if we're just stuck with different gut feelings about history.
