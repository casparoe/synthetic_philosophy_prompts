okay so i've been trying to work out this argument about marx and i think it proves way too much but i can't find the crack. here's the chain:

(1) for marx exploitation isn't just unfair pay, it's the specific structural extraction of surplus value: capital buys labor-power at its exchange-value but realizes more in production, and that gap is appropriated.

(2) alienation isn't just feeling bad at work, it's the objective condition where your life-activity becomes an alien power dominating you—specifically because the product belongs to capital and the labor-process is organized by valorization, not by you.

(3) so alienation is conceptually downstream from exploitation. without the capital relation extracting surplus value, there's no structural mechanism turning your product and process against you.

(4) now imagine a fully democratic cooperative: workers collectively own the means of production, plan output together, and there's no seperate class taking surplus value. exploitation in marx's technical sense is gone.

(5) but in this cooperative people still do tasks assigned by collective need rather than personal choice, still use machinery and knowledge they didn't individually create, and still see thier output distributed according to rules they didn't single-handedly decide.

(6) call this the mirror problem: if alienation is defined as my labor serving purposes i don't individually endorse, then democratic planning doesn't fix it, because in any society larger than one person, interdependence means my work serves collective purposes i don't fully control.

(7) so the objective conditions marx calls alienation—loss of control over product, process, and species-being—persist even without exploitation.

(8) therefore either alienation doesn't require exploitation, which means capitalism isn't specially harmful and marx's critique loses its specificity, or exploitation hasn't actually been abolished, which just means "exploitation" equals "any division of labor" and the term becomes useless.

(9) but both outcomes seem wrong—marx clearly thought socialism would reduce alienation, and he clearly meant exploitation to distinguish capitalism.

where do i reject? is it step (3) that overstates the dependence? or step (5) that misdescribes democratic control? or is there an equivocation between "control by capital" and "control by collective" that i'm missing?
