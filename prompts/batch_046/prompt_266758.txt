I am preparing a presentation for my metaphysics seminar on time and change, and I want to check whether my commitments form a coherent package before I defend them. Here is where I have landed:

(1) I am a presentist: only entities that exist at the present time exist; past and future entities have no being whatsoever.
(2) I reject eternalism and the growing-block theory, primarily because I think they make temporal passage mysterious—if every time is equally real, then the distinction between a fixed past and an open future collapses into a subjective indexical.
(3) I hold that genuine change requires a "thick present," a minimal duration with internal micro-intervals, within which an object can instantiate incompatible properties at different sub-intervals without ever existing outside the present moment.
(4) I accept the physical framework of special relativity and therefore deny any globally privileged reference frame or absolute simultaneity relation.
(5) I ground truths about the past in present "causal-historical traces"—concrete present properties of objects whose content depends on prior causal processes—rather than in abstract propositions, ersatz times, or presently existing past entities.
(6) I maintain that the future is genuinely open: there are no true future-tensed propositions now, and modal facts about what might occur depend on present dispositional properties rather than on concrete alternative worlds.

My request is for a consistency audit. Which pairs of these commitments actually pull against one another, which only appear to be in tension, and what is the least costly revision to restore coherence? I am especially concerned that my thick-present account of change (3) may relocate the temporary-intrinsics problem inside the present rather than solving it, and that my reliance on causal-historical traces (5) may conflict with presentism's denial that past relata exist (1). I also worry that the frame-dependence entailed by (4) undermines the objectivity of the thick present defended in (3). Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each whether it is real or merely apparent, identify the cheapest fix for the real ones, and tell me which single revision—if any—would clean up the most problems without forcing me to abandon my core presentist and anti-eternalist motivations.
