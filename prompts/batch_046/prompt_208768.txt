You sit on the ethics oversight board for a neurology consortium running a multi-year trial in which patients with progressive cortical degeneration receive synthetic neural prostheses that replace biological tissue layer by layer, with the patient remaining conscious and socially interactive throughout. At each replacement milestone the board must decide whether the subject still possesses the standing to consent, inherit prior legal obligations, and be held responsible for actions mediated by the augmented system—questions that turn on philosophical theories of gradual replacement and psychological continuity. Translate that theoretical framework into a reusable decision procedure your board can execute at every milestone: list the empirical and normative tests to apply in sequence, specify the thresholds for continuation versus halt, identify the exact nodes where judgment about values must replace measurement, and state the conditions under which the board must treat the entity as a successor person rather than the continuing patient. Present this as a flowchart in words with explicit stop, continue, and hand-off points, and note where the procedure is likely to misfire. Then apply it to three cases: (1) stable autobiographical memory and emotional temperament through 60% replacement; (2) sharp behavioral divergence after 40% replacement despite intact episodic memory; and (3) at 75% replacement, the patient requests termination and revocation of prior consent, but the synthetic substrate predicts with high confidence that this request stems from altered reward circuitry rather than authentic preference. For case three, show the procedure’s output even when that output conflicts with the family’s and clinicians’ intuitions, and state clearly what the procedure cannot adjudicate.
