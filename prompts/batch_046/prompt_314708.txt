I'm drafting a position paper on temporal asymmetry for a philosophy of physics reading group, and before I circulate it I want a rigorous consistency audit of my core commitments. I hold that (1) the thermodynamic arrow is the sole fundamental arrow of time, with both psychological and causal direction reducible to local entropy gradients rather than to any primitive orientational feature of reality; (2) the underlying dynamical laws—classical Hamiltonian mechanics and unitary quantum evolution—are strictly time-reversible with no hidden orientational structure at the microphysical level; (3) there is nevertheless a single, globally valid distinction between past and future that applies across our cosmological region and is not merely perspectival, indexical, or frame-dependent; (4) the extreme low-entropy initial boundary condition of the observable universe is a brute contingent fact that should be accepted as given rather than explained by a deeper law, symmetry-breaking mechanism, or typicality argument within a multiverse; and (5) in hypothetical regions where local entropy happens to decrease over macroscopic timescales—large enough to support structured observers—the rational inhabitants would correctly identify the direction of local decrease as "the past," since the psychological arrow tracks the local statistical gradient and not any global temporal coordinate.

These commitments strike me as broadly physicalist and reductionist, but I worry that (3) conflicts with both (1) and (5), and that (2) renders (3) deeply mysterious unless I smuggle in an unexplained global constraint. At the same time, I think some apparent conflicts dissolve once one distinguishes ontological claims about boundary conditions from explanatory claims about local phenomena—perhaps (3) concerns the actual global structure while (1) concerns only derivative local patterns. I also want to preserve (4) without being pushed into a multiverse framework that treats our boundary condition as statistically typical. Could you examine exactly this cluster and tell me: which pairs of commitments are genuinely incompatible, which only appear to conflict because they operate at different explanatory levels, and what is the least costly revision if consistency requires giving something up? Please rank the tensions by severity, and for each indicate whether resolving it would force me to abandon my reductionist framework or merely refine a local claim about observers or boundary conditions. I am not asking for a literature survey or citation check—just an internal coherence audit of this specific combination.
