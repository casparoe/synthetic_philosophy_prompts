I have recently shifted my research toward philosophy of science and become convinced by a restricted form of structural realism that I have not seen defended in exactly these terms, and I want to know what should dislodge it. The view distinguishes two kinds of survival across theory change: mathematical structural continuity, which concerns the relations among observable phenomena, and mechanistic continuity, which concerns the actual causal processes that produce those phenomena. I hold that structural realism is defensible only in domains where mechanistic continuity is in principle unobservable or incoherent—chiefly fundamental physics, such as quantum field theory—whereas in biology, psychology, and social science we should be mechanistic realists or instrumentalists about structure, because there we can and do track the underlying mechanisms, and when those mechanisms are overturned the corresponding structure should be abandoned with them.

What would count against this? I want an honest assessment of whether there are decisive cases—say, in evolutionary biology—where the mathematical structure (for instance, population-genetic equations) survives radical mechanistic revision (for instance, a complete overturning of our molecular understanding of inheritance) in a way that shows structural continuity is genuinely independent of mechanistic continuity in that domain. I also want to know whether my restriction insulates structural realism in its permitted domain: if any apparent mechanistic discontinuity at the fundamental level can always be dismissed as merely apparent, then the view risks unfalsifiability, and I want to know whether that is a problem for a view of this kind or a legitimate boundary condition. Please distinguish evidence that would be decisive from evidence that would merely be awkward, say what defenders of unrestricted structural realism would concede as counting against my restriction, and identify where your reasoning is most likely to fail—particularly regarding the boundary between domains where mechanism is accessible and where it is not. Finally, say explicitly what would change your assessment: whether a specific historical case study, a conceptual distinction I have missed, or a formal result in philosophy of science.
