Consider the transcendental reading of Kant’s essay on perpetual peace: rather than treating the federation of republics as an institutional remedy to war, this view holds that perpetual peace is the logical consequence of republican sovereignty properly understood, because absolute state sovereignty is conceptually incompatible with the idea of universal right that grounds republican legitimacy itself. On this reading, the abolition of standing armies and the federation are not contingent policy choices but conditions for the coherence of sovereignty. What would have to be true for this interpretation to fail? Which textual elements or conceptual distinctions in Kant’s account—such as the separation between domestic right and international right, or the voluntary nature of the federation—resist being absorbed into a transcendental argument? Which rival readings (institutionalist, realist, or the view that Kant is merely proposing a pragmatic league) would need to be correct instead? Rank the defeaters: is there an internal contradiction that would be fatal, or are the objections merely awkward qualifications about the scope of the argument?
