I'm on the steering commitee for our university's pilot program on AI-assisted instruction. We're deciding whether to approve Phase II, which rolls out an adaptive grading and discussion-moderation system across all first-year seminars, cutting grad TA hours by about a third and redirecting savings into a research travel fund. Some members say this is just budget efficiency—like buying better photocopiers—while others insist it restructures pedagogical labor in a way that maps onto Marx's formal vs real subsumption distinction. They want me to draft a memo for Thursday's vote recommending approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, and it needs to take the Marxian framing seriously rather than treating it like jargon, but still be readable by admins who haven't read Capital. There's also a specific twist in our pilot: the system doesn't just grade faster, it redefines what counts as a "good" contribution by weighting rhetorical patterns that predict exam scores, so the remaining TAs end up supervising a labor process whose criteria they don't control. The memo should have a clear bottom line, concrete conditions if we go that route, and an honest engagement with the strongest argument on the other side. Don't end with a reading list—just the recommendation and reasoning.
