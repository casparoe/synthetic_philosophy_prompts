I serve on the governance board of the Meridian Open-Source Foundation as an engineering representative, with no formal training in philosophy or political theory. At Thursday’s meeting we must decide on Proposal 7, which would amend our charter to allow corporate sponsors to purchase weighted votes in elections for the Technical Steering Committee—specifically, one additional vote per ten thousand dollars contributed in the preceding fiscal year—while volunteer maintainers retain a single flat vote regardless of labor contributed. Proponents argue that tying voting power to financial exposure aligns incentives and stabilizes funding; opponents, including several long-tenured maintainers, contend that this converts collective governance into a priced commodity and will systematically marginalize non-commercial voices. Please draft the board’s written opinion for circulation before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete course—approve outright, reject outright, or approve with binding conditions—along with a clear implementation timeline if conditions apply. The reasoning must treat the opponents’ commodification concern as a serious structural objection rather than dismissing it as sentimentality, and it should address whether there is a defensible distinction between this vote-weight mechanism and ordinary sponsorship perks such as logo placement or advisory seats. Keep any theoretical framing implicit and avoid bibliographic references, since the board needs actionable reasoning, not a literature survey. Finally, include a brief meta-note at the end of the memo stating how confident the recommendation is in its overall judgment and explicitly identifying the single point—whether conceptual, empirical, or normative—about which it is least sure.
