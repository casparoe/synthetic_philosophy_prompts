I am designing a diagnostic handout for a graduate metaphysics seminar on temporal persistence, and I want to avoid the usual exercise in which students simply pick between "endurantism" and "perdurantism" by name. Instead, I need a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts respondents by their answers to concrete cases and conceptual pressures, constructing the logical space of positions first and then mapping named theories onto the leaves.

Please build a tree with at least six branch nodes, each phrased as a standalone question that a student could answer without already knowing which philosophical label it corresponds to. Begin with gradual cellular replacement over a decade: does the pre-replacement subject survive? Then proceed to a fission case in which perfect psychological duplication produces two simultaneous candidates with equal claim to the original mental life. Next, include a modal branch asking whether the same present person could have existed with an entirely different origin story—say, different parents, a different childhood trauma—provided the current psychological profile is preserved. Add a collective-persistence node: if every citizen and every territorial boundary of a nation-state is replaced over generations, is the nation the same entity? Follow with a question about whether spatiotemporal continuity is necessary, or whether relational patterns alone—functional roles, causal connections reconstructed after a gap—could ground identity. At each split, specify the answer-combination that defines a leaf; locate endurantism, four-dimensionalist perdurantism, stage-theoretic analogues, and at least one relational or narrative hybrid within the tree; and identify any logical combinations that remain unoccupied, briefly diagnosing whether the gap stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect in the literature. Explicitly flag any pair of named positions that your tree cannot cleanly separate—particularly where endurantist and perdurantist answers coincide on practical cases despite divergent metaphysical rationales. Finally, include a brief scoring guide that explains how to interpret mixed commitments across branches, so that a respondent who answers consistently with one leaf on biological replacement but with another on modal origins can be mapped onto an unnamed hybrid rather than forced into an ill-fitting standard category.
