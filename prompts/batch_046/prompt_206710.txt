I am presenting a seminar paper defending D-weighting, a framework for infinite-population ethics that rejects both totalist aggregation and pure person-affecting restrictions. Specifically, D-weighting assigns lexical priority to any finite present population over any merely infinite future benefit, but within the infinite tail permits comparison by local density of well-being rather than by limit values. I am speaking to a mixed philosophy seminar: two philosophers of mathematics with expertise in well-orderings and non-Archimedean fields; three philosophers working on longtermism and unbounded expected-value reasoning; two Kantians who reject intergenerational aggregation on dignity grounds; and one decision theorist specializing in unbounded utility functions.
