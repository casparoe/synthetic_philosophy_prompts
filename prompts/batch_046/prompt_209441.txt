I am writing the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate course in early modern political philosophy. Please draft one essay question on Spinoza's theory of democracy that students cannot answer by merely summarizing his claim in the Theological-Political Treatise that democracy is the most natural form of government. In particular, ask them to evaluate whether these exclusions are only contingent historical limitations, or whether they expose a hidden aristocratic logic in Spinoza's concept of the multitudo: namely, that democracy is not rule by all but rule by those capable of independent rational judgment, making it structurally closer to an aristocracy of reason than to modern egalitarian democracy. Provide a model answer of about 700 words, and a marking scheme built around four must-hit analytical moves (marks assigned out of 20 total, with each move weighted), plus a one-line note for each on the typical mistake that costs points. Do not ask for bibliographies or citations.
