I've been working through the standard tripartite taxonomy of liberty—negative, positive, and republican—while preparing a graduate seminar on freedom, and I've settled on a reductionist position that I'm not fully convinced is wrong but worry is insulated. The view is that republican freedom (non-domination) is not a genuinely independent concept but collapses into a compound of negative liberty (the absence of actual interference) and a specific, institutionally embedded form of positive liberty (the effective capacity to resist arbitrary interference should it arise). On this account, being free from domination just is enjoying both conditions simultaneously; there is no further "republican residue"—no distinct relational harm of subordination that persists even when interference is absent and resistance-capacity is robust. I find this attractive because it dissolves the apparent conflict between liberal and republican traditions without multiplying entities, but I want to stress-test it honestly. Play it straight: what would have to be true for this reduction to fail?

I want an honest assessment of how exposed the compound-reduction view actually is. In particular, distinguish between objections that would be decisive—say, a case or conceptual result showing that domination persists even when both negative and positive conditions are fully satisfied—from objections that are merely awkward, such as the practical difficulty of measuring "effective resistance-capacity." Say also what a defender of the reduction could themselves concede as counting against it, not only what republican critics would say: for instance, if there is a relational harm that survives the compound conditions, would the reductionist have to deny its moral relevance, or could they absorb it by refining the positive-liberty component? And be willing to conclude whether the position is well insulated—perhaps by definitional maneuvering around resistance-capacity—or whether that insulation is a problem because it renders the view unfalsifiable. Which assumptions of the reduction could in principle fail, what would our normative framework have to look like for each, and which of these escape routes are live rather than merely logically possible?
