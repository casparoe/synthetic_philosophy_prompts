I am preparing a single seventy-five-minute graduate seminar in philosophy of mind for students who have encountered Dennett only through excerpts and who consistently conflate heterophenomenology with either eliminativism or a crude behaviorist protocol. My goal is to present heterophenomenology strictly as a methodological stance—treating first-person reports as theoretical posits about an intentional system's self-model rather than as epistemically privileged testimony—without letting the discussion collapse into metaphysical debates about whether qualia exist. To do this, I need a teaching plan that lays out the dependency order: what conceptual apparatus must be secured first (the intentional stance, the ontology versus methodology distinction, and what I am calling the model-report gap, which is the difference between using an utterance as data about an internal self-monitoring architecture versus treating it as transparent access to phenomenal reality), followed by the single carrying example. Rather than the familiar artificial-intelligence scenario, I want to build the session around a clinical confabulation case—specifically, a split-brain subject offering verbal justifications for actions initiated by the non-dominant hemisphere—because it sharpens the methodological question of whose report is being modeled and with what authority. I expect three misconceptions in sequence: that heterophenomenology is merely eliminativism disguised; that it grants the scientist authoritarian priority over the subject's own experience; and that it applies only to pathological or artificial systems, leaving normal human introspection untouched. For each misconception, specify the precise point in the arc where it should be anticipated and defused. I also want to know what to leave explicitly outside this session—likely the full metaphysics of the intentional stance and competing neurophenomenological programs—and I need one cold diagnostic question that, if answered correctly, proves the students understand heterophenomenology as a constraint on scientific method rather than a skeptical denial of consciousness. Please conclude with a one-sentence summary of the recommended pedagogical arc as the final line.
