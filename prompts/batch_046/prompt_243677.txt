Write an essay evaluating whether capital punishment can be morally justified in a legal system that has eliminated all correctable procedural error, leaving only an irreducible, randomly distributed false-positive conviction rate of 0.4 percent—meaning that for every 250 executions, one involves an innocent defendant, and this risk cannot be reduced by further institutional reform. Weigh retributive arguments (particularly proportionality and the moral significance of intentional killing), deterrence arguments (both marginal and general, considering whether the risk of executing the innocent undermines the legitimacy required for deterrence to function), and the independent moral weight of knowingly imposing an irreversible punishment with a nonzero, structurally unavoidable error rate. Reach and defend a specific conclusion: either that capital punishment remains justified under these conditions, that it is unjustified precisely because of them, or that its justification depends on a further condition you explicitly state (for example, the severity of the offense or the magnitude of the risk). Do not treat the execution-risk as a practical problem awaiting a technical solution; treat it as a permanent structural feature of any capital-justice system.
