hey, i've been trying to nail down what "socially constructed" actually means beyond the usual hand-waving, and my verdicts on specific cases are all over the place.

Race: the census boxes (Black, White, Asian, Hispanic) are definatly made up. They don't track biology well at all. But racial gaps in health or income feel real in a way that wouldn't disappear if we stopped using the words. So it's constructed, but sticky.

Gender: also constructed. The roles and norms are made up. But gender identity feels like something you can self-declare in a way race isn't — you can't just opt into being Black for affirmative action, but you can opt into being a woman (or out of being one) in most social contexts. So gender seems to have a different mechanism.

Money: the US dollar is pure collective agreement plus the Fed. But Bitcoin is also pure collective agreement, with no Fed. Both are "money", yet one collapses without state backing and the other doesn't. So institutional backing can't be what makes something constructed.

Then there's the tension: doctors use race in risk calculators, which makes race feel "real" medically, but we also say gender identity should be respected over biological sex. If both are constructed, why do we treat self-ascription as authoritative for gender but not for race? And why is marriage — which is also institutional — so easy to opt out of, while language is constructed but you can't really opt out of having a native tongue?

My half-baked principle: a kind is socially constructed if its boundaries depend on agreement rather than mind-independent facts, and its "stickiness" depends on institutional embedding plus how hard it is to opt out. Race is sticky because institutions use it; gender is sticky but opt-in/out is easier; money is sticky if there's an enforcer.

But then marriage is institutional and opt-out-able. Language is sticky and hard to opt out. So my principle is shaky.

Is there one coherent account that saves all these verdicts, or do I have to drop one? And how does it apply to something like "addiction" or "criminal record" — are those in the same bucket, or do they need a different box?

Sorry for the ramble, there's probably a typo in there. What's the actual principle, if there is one?
