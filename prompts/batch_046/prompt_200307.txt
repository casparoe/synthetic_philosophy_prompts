Please give me your credence—written in British English—that Leibniz's principle of identity of indiscernibles is false in the following variant of Black's spheres: two qualitatively identical spheres sit at antipodal points in a one-dimensional circular space of circumference exactly four metres, so that every relational property is perfectly symmetric and no non-symmetric feature individuates them. Decompose the credence into: (a) the probability that weak PII (which permits relational discernibility) is the formulation worth defending; (b) the probability that this circular topology genuinely eliminates all asymmetric relational properties; (c) the probability that two objects can be numerically distinct without any discernible difference at all; and (d) any further factor you judge relevant. For each factor provide a number and one line of justification, then combine them. State which factor you are least confident in and what conceptual argument or modified scenario would most shift it.
