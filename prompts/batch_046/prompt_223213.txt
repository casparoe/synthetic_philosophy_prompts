The setup is that Joel gets his memories of Clementine erased, then during the procedure realizes he wants to keep them, but the tech is already running. So there's this tension between what Joel wants *right now* (to hide her in some corner of his mind) and what the "extrapolated" version of Joel — the one who signed up for the erasure in a calmer state, who understands the full causal history of the relationship — supposedly wanted. The film seems to treat the post-erasure Joel as a different person, or at least a diminished one. But from a CEV-style view, you might think that if the erasure produces a more coherent preference set (no more obsessive longing, no more contradictions between love and pain), then maybe the post-erasure Joel is actually closer to his idealized self. I'm asking: does the film take a side? Where does it show that Joel's resistance to erasure is an authentic preference rather than just a transient emotional attachment? And where does it maybe confuse "what I want after knowing everything" with "what preserves my narrative continuity"?
