I've been chewing on Bernard Williams's idea that opera's implausability — people singing thier deaths, all that — isn't a bug but the feature that lets real emotional and moral stakes come through without the usual social camouflage. I want to go a step further and say the vocal line acts like a kind of moral X-ray: the more artificial the convention (think coloratura over a corpse), the more transparent the character's practical reasoning becomes, in a way spoken drama just can't match. So here's my position: opera's authority comes from this transparency, and implausability is the price of honesty.

What would count against it? I don't mean "it feels fake," I mean serious defeaters — audience cognition studies showing we process sung grief differently and not more honestly, cases where absurd staging obscures moral complexity rather than revealing it, or the worry that this collapses into a generic "unrealism is good" claim and loses any specific grip on opera. Rank them: fatal, awkward, or just noisy?
