Consider the following argument, which I take to be valid but whose conclusion I find intolerable, and tell me which premise or inference I should reject and at what cost.

(1) In a standard Frankfurt case, an agent acts from her own reasons-responsive mechanisms while a potential controller remains causally inert; she lacks alternative possibilities yet is morally responsible, which shows that the principle of alternative possibilities (PAP) is false.

(2) The reason responsibility survives is not merely the absence of an actual coercer, but that the actual causal source of the action lies within the agent's own psychological structure — a sourcehood condition is satisfied, and a leeway condition is not required.

(3) In a deterministic world, there is no counterfactual controller, and every mental state of the agent is fixed by prior laws and conditions; nevertheless, the proximate causal source of any action remains the agent's own psychological structure.

(4) By (2), if sourcehood is satisfied and leeway is absent, responsibility is preserved; determinism removes leeway but leaves sourcehood intact, so determinism should be compatible with full moral responsibility.

(5) Now consider a brainwashing case: an external agent implants a desire in a subject, who then acts on it through her fully functional reasons-responsive mechanisms; the implanter is now in the past and causally inert, and the psychological structure is internal to the subject.

(6) By parity with (3) and (4), the brainwashed subject satisfies sourcehood and lacks leeway in exactly the same way as the determined agent; therefore she should be fully responsible.

(7) But brainwashing is a canonical excuse, which suggests that sourcehood alone is insufficient and that some form of leeway — or at least historical independence — is required.

(8) If we restore a leeway requirement to avoid (6), then we must also deny (1), because Frankfurt cases precisely show agents acting without leeway.

(9) Thus we face a dilemma: either accept that brainwashing never excuses (denying the move to 7) or concede that Frankfurt cases do not defeat PAP (denying 1–2).

Where is the hidden error? I want to preserve (1) and (7), but I am unsure whether I can do so without smuggling in an ad hoc distinction between determinism and brainwashing.
