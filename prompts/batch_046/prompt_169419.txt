As a novelist drafting a multi-volume speculative fiction series, I am constructing collective decision scenes—ship councils allocating lethal risk, hive-mind parliaments redistributing consciousness, tax-collecting republics funding universal education—in which groups justify distributive choices by aggregating welfare. I need a reusable, fifteen-minute decision procedure, grounded in the separateness of persons objection to utilitarianism, that I can run on any scene without consulting a philosopher. The procedure must translate the principle that society is not a single agent distributing goods among its parts into an ordered checklist: the boundary conditions to verify, the accounting checks that reveal when utility is being treated as fungible across discrete lives, the institutional reciprocity requirements that prevent moral collapse, and the precise points where judgment cannot be eliminated and I must sign off as author. It should stop with a definitive "no—this violates separateness" when harm to one subject is treated as internal to an aggregate good, continue with conditions when reciprocity preserves individual standing, and halt at an explicit judgment gate when narrative identity is partially merged (for instance, split consciousness or shared memory architectures). I want the hard cases built in: apply it to (1) a captain ordering one crew member into fatal radiation exposure to save the vessel; (2) a republic taxing wealthy citizens to fund education for all, where the taxed may also benefit; and (3) a hive-mind reducing node Alpha’s self-awareness to expand node Beta’s cognition, where personal boundaries are ambiguous. Include one case in which the procedure returns an answer I will not want for the narrative—specifically, that a dramatically intended justification is morally impermissible rather than tragically necessary—and state plainly what the procedure cannot do, such as weighing the magnitude of aggregate welfare against the boundary violation, resolving partial-identity disputes, or substituting for my authorial choice about narrative ethics. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the deliverable must be operable by me alone, with clear flags for misfire and a small set of test conditions I can apply to dialogue and plot logic.
