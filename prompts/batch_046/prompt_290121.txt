Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two camps on Bernard Williams's integrity objection, specifically the idea—commonly summarized as one thought too many—that requiring an agent to justify saving a loved one by an impartial calculus corrupts the directness of her attachment. Camp One, the Constitutive Integrity Camp, holds that the objection succeeds at both the psychological and normative levels: an agent's ground projects are partly constitutive of her identity, and forcing an impartial reason into her deliberation—what this collaboration will call deliberative insertion—destroys the constitutive link between who she is and what she does, regardless of whether the project survives external scrutiny. Camp Two, the Integrationist Camp, holds that the objection conflates deliberative mechanism with normative authority; an agent can maintain ground projects that are themselves responsive to impartial demands—what this collaboration will call justificatory subordination—without any corruption of integrity, and indeed that the presence of an impartial consideration is sometimes precisely what distinguishes moral action from mere partiality.

Your deliverable should begin with a numbered section of claims both camps can sign, including shared descriptions of Williams's scenario, agreement on the distinction between agent-relative and agent-neutral reasons, and acknowledgment that ground projects vary in their resistance to revision. This should be followed by a disagreements table in which each residual dispute is formulated in wording that its own side would accept. You must also identify at least two cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. For each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and record each camp's explicit prediction about that case.

In particular, apply the injected distinction between deliberative insertion and justificatory subordination to the constructed case of an archivist whose ground project is preserving a specific endangered-language archive, but who must allocate scarce institutional funds impartially across languages she does not personally value. Address whether her integrity is compromised if she (a) consciously invokes an impartial selection procedure during funding decisions, or (b) continues the project only because it survives an impartial audit of cultural value, even if her conscious reasoning never invokes the audit. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not quote any text verbatim; and write in polished American English.
