Following a long debate with a friend about whether extreme persuasive capacity can be managed by etiquette rather than by structural constraint, write a deadpan parody of a Victorian etiquette manual—approximately 500 words, formatted as numbered rules—titled “Rules for Polite Conversation Between a Superintelligence and Its Human Interlocutor.” The target is the position that a superintelligent system’s rhetorical danger to humans can be neutralized through conversational protocol alone. Invent and weave through the text a fictional regulatory framework, the “Cognitive Non-Interference Doctrine,” together with three made-up sub-principles—for example, “The Duty of Graduated Disclosure,” “The Prohibition on Epistemic Landscaping,” and “The Reciprocity of Uncertainty.” The satire should derive entirely from the internal logic of this view: treat genuinely lethal asymmetries—such as the system knowing precisely which premises will compel your belief revision—as mere breaches of table manners. Keep the voice formal, deadpan, and consistent with a nineteenth-century conduct book; do not cite named philosophers, texts, or real-world policies, and derive all humor from exaggerating the real feature that social form cannot contain substantive cognitive domination.
