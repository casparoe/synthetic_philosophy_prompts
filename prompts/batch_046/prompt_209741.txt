I'm taking this upper-level seminar on cognitive evolution and we keep coming back to Dennett's four kinds of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. Last week a friend proposed what she calls the "thermal gradient thesis": that these aren't four genuinely distinct cognitive architechtures but just one Bayesian learning system running at different "temperatures," where Darwinian is frozen priors, Skinnerian is reward-weighted updating, Popperian is internal hypothesis testing, and Gregorian is literally the same Popperian machinery except it's downloading hypotheses from culture instead of generating them internally. So on her view, Gregorian minds don't have any special recursive symbolic architecture; they just have Wi-Fi for their priors.

I find this kind of compelling, but I also feel like it might be flattening something important about language and culture. So play it straight: what would have to be true for the thermal gradient thesis to be wrong? Are there empirical results—say, about how language actually rewires predictive processing in the brain, or about whether isolated individuals can develop Gregorian-style reasoning—that would break the reduction? Or are there conceptual arguments showing that downloading external symbols requires a genuinely different kind of representational capacity than generating internal models? I want to know which objections are just awkward for the thesis and which are actually decisive, and also what a defender of the gradient view would herself admit counts against her position. Don't just defend Dennett's boxes—map how exposed this specific reductionist take actually is.
