Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain—with two refrains that repeat according to the form. The poem should treat germs, climate, and volcanic eruptions not as passive background conditions but as 'historical actors': non-human forces that enter into causal interaction with human institutions, reshape political and economic outcomes, and alter the trajectory of events. At the same time, the poem must avoid the perils of environmental determinism by preserving the contingency of human response and the variability of institutional outcomes. Use the first refrain to assert the efficacy or presence of these non-human forces (for instance, referencing the 1815 Tambora eruption and its cascading effects on harvests and migration, or the 1918 influenza pandemic's disruption of labor and governance structures). Use the second refrain to insist on the openness of history and the non-deterministic character of these interactions (for instance, emphasizing that identical climatic or pathogenic conditions produce divergent social results under different political arrangements). In the tercets, develop specific interactions—volcanic winter altering post-Napoleonic economies, pathogen transmission reshaping urban public health regimes, long-term climate shifts pressuring migration patterns—so that the structural repetition of the villanelle mirrors the recurring, cyclical intrusion of these forces into human narratives without reducing those narratives to mere effects. In the final quatrain, unite both refrains in the rhyme scheme so that the poem resolves structurally into a single statement: recognizing non-human agency expands the cast of historical explanation without dissolving human responsibility. The rendering should be precise enough that an environmental historian or a philosopher of history recognizes the conceptual move rather than seeing a generic nature poem; avoid both sermonizing about human mastery and collapsing into passive victimhood. Begin with the poem itself, with no preface or commentary.
