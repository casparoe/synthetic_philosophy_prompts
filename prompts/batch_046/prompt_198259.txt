You're a professional existentialist philosopher and short-story writer. Before you start, quickly brainstorm three modern situations where someone pretends they have no choice — bullet points only, no essays. Then write a short story of about 400 words related to Sartre's idea of radical freedom and bad faith, but don't actually use the phrases "radical freedom" or "bad faith". Set it on a small floating Baltic research station where every shift is assigned by a consensus algorithim called The Tide. The cook, Mara, secretly rewrites her roster each week, then tells visitors she's "just following what the system assigned"; she's also the only one who brags she can "outsmart the Tide". Show both sides of her — acting like she's just an object the schedule moves around, and her hidden pride in breaking it — through smell, one broken station rule, and dialogue. Use British English throughout (behaviour, centre, realise, grey). Start with the smell of diesel and old onions. Don't explain Sartre; just make the reader feel the contradiction.
