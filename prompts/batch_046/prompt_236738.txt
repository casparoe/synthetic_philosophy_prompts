I've been poking around the historical-Jesus literature since retiring from the lab, and these three filters — dissimilarity, coherence, and multiple attestation — keep showing up. Here's a cascade my study group cooked up that feels wrong but I can't find the leak.

(1) A saying is only authentic if it's dissimilar from both second-temple Judaism and early Christian theology, else it could just be borrowed.
(2) It also has to cohere with other sayings we've already marked as authentic.
(3) And it needs independant attestation across sources, not just one gospel.
(4) But if something is truly dissimilar to early Christianity, it won't appear in multiple Christian texts; and if it's dissimilar to Judaism there's no outside Jewish source to back it up either.
(5) So clearing (1) kills (3), while loosening (1) to save (3) lets coherence (2) pull the saying back into Jewish or Christian norms, breaking (1) retroactively.
(6) Together the three form a filter cascade that wipes out every candidate, leaving an empty set.
(7) But scholars do publish reconstructed sayings, so the cascade must have an escape hatch — or the criteria are just broken.

Which step is the faulty one? I'd rather keep (1) and (3) if I can, but I want to know what giving up (2) costs me, or if there's a hidden assumption between (4) and (5). Please answer as a numbered list with exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
