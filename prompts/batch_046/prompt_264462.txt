I am preparing to defend, before a joint seminar of evolutionary biologists and institutional sociologists, the thesis that costly signaling—specifically the handicap principle developed in evolutionary biology—offers a more explanatory framework than pure error-reduction for why certain scientific subfields maintain methodologically ornate practices that do not improve predictive accuracy. My target case is the widespread adoption of multi-level Bayesian hierarchical models with diffuse priors in small-N social-science studies, where the computational burden and presentational complexity far exceed the informational gain available from the data. I argue that this functions as a handicap: only researchers with substantial technical capital and institutional backing can bear the cost of proper implementation, making adoption a credible signal of in-group competence and commitment rather than a rational response to uncertainty. The audience includes evolutionary biologists who have worked on Zahavi-style signaling in animal behavior, a sociologist of science who studies peer-review rituals, and several quantitative methodologists who use these exact models in their own research and regard them as standard robustness practice. I need a prep sheet ordered by how likely and damaging each objection will be, phrased in the vocabulary each faction would actually use: from the biologists, whether I have quantitative measures of signal cost and receiver benefit; from the sociologist, whether I am collapsing institutional function into individual strategic display; from the methodologists, whether I am mistaking necessary robustness-checking for wasteful ornament. For each, I want to know what I should concede upfront—particularly regarding the risk of unfalsifiable just-so storytelling—what I should hold firm on, and the weakest point in my own position that I should prepare to defend rather than hope goes unnoticed. Crucially, I need the single reply that must land against the methodologists' claim that complexity is always defensible as sensitivity analysis, because if that reply fails, the proposal collapses. I also want explicit warning about the objection I am secretly hoping nobody raises: that if signaling explains every methodological choice, then no practice can ever be criticized as genuinely irrational, which undermines the normative force of my own critique and turns my argument into a self-defeating relativism.
