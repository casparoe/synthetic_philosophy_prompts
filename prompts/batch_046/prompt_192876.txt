okay so i've been in this bio-philosophy discussion group for a few months and we keep hitting the same wall with the etiological vs causal-role debate about biological function, and it's driving me a little nuts. one camp — basically the wright/millikan line — says a trait's function is whatever it was selected for in evolutionary history, so malfunction is failing to do what you're there for. the other camp — cummins and friends — says function is just whatever a part actually contributes to a system's capacity, so selection history is irrelevant and malfunction is just statistical deviation from the role. we keep running through the heart, the appendix, pseudogenes, and nobody budges. but lately i've been thinking about synthetic microbiomes engineered for bioremediation: bacteria designed de novo with zero evolutionary lineage that still clearly break down toxins within a larger system. that seems to push hard on the etiological side, but also feels like cheating — like we're smuggling in an engineering concept of 'function' that biologists don't actually use.

so my question is: is this stall a verbal disagreement about wether 'function' is an evolutionary concept or a systems concept? are they answering different questions — one about why a trait exists, one about what it does now — or is there a deeper hidden disagreement about normativity, like wether malfunction requires some kind of design or purpose? i want a real diagnosis using what each side actually commits to, not just "both have points". and tell me what progress would even look like — a formal distinction, an empirical case study, or just admitting the debate splits into two? also, is there something existant in evolutionary developmental biology that breaks both accounts?
