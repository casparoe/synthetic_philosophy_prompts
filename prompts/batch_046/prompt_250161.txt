for my philosophy-of-well-being seminar, about 15 people, 90 minutes. design a classroom game that makes them feel the split between hedonism, desire-satisfaction, and objective-list theories through Nozick's experience machine — but inject this twist: the machine also induces adaptive preference formation, so whatever life it feeds you, you come to want it. that should make desire-satisfactionists squirm in a way the standard version dont.

split them into three "Well-Being Subsidy Boards," each secretly assigned one theory as their constitutional rule. they write 2 eligibility rules for a Partial Machine (6 months real-time, simulated career, suspended body, preferences slowly align with simulation). then apply rules to three applicant cards: a dying musician wanting simulated fame, a healthy anxious grad student wanting to feel accomplished, and an elderly person wanting to correct one past mistake with perfect fidelity.

roles: board chair, dissent recorder, applicant advocate. timeline: 10 min rules-writing, 20 min deliberation, 10 min vote, rest reveal/debrief. incentive: the board whose rules survive a "public audit" (other boards attack them) gets to pick the seminar snack next week — small stakes but real enough to care. debrief should map each vote onto the philosophical crux: did they block the musician because pleasure isnt enough? approve the grad student because preferences are satisfied even if manufactured? reveal the hidden theories only after voting so they feel the disagreement before naming it.

also include what will predictably go wrong the first run — probably boards peek at each others rules, or they ignore the adaptive-preference twist and treat it like the ordinary machine — and how to handle it. and be honest about what the activity cant reproduce: the actual first-person phenomenology of plugging in, and the empirical/psychological question of whether adaptive preferences are genuinely ours. no lecture up front, no requests for citations or reading lists, keep it runnable without web access, and leave an apostrophe out here or there.
