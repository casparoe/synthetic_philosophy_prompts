I've been going back and forth with a friend about honor culture, dignity culture, and victimhood culture, and whether any of them is actually morally better. I tried to formalize the intuition that dignity culture wins because it's unconditional, but the argument turned into a reductio and now I'm stuck. (1) People usually defend dignity culture by saying it assigns worth unconditionally, while honor culture makes status depend on public recognition and victimhood culture makes it depend on having suffered wrong. (2) If worth is genuinely unconditional, then no action — even serious wrongdoing — can diminish it. (3) But punishment and blame seem to presuppose that wrongdoers have lost some standing, or at least that their moral position is different in a way that justifies holding them to acount. (4) So dignity culture either has to reject punishment altogether, which sounds implausible, or it has to admit that worth can be reduced by bad acts. (5) Once you admit that, dignity culture is just another conditional-status system, no different in structure from honor or victimhood frameworks. (6) So the claim that dignity culture is morally superior can't rest on unconditionality. I feel like something slipped between (2) and (3) — maybe I'm mixing up "worth" with "desert" or "standing" — but I can't pin it down. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? I'd rather hang onto (1) and (3) if possible.
