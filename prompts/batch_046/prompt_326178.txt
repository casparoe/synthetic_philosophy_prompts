Here is the summary, attributed to an unnamed recent essay in philosophy of action:

Sphexishness, named after the digger wasp Sphex ichneumoneus whose nesting routine is famously rigid, is the property of executing a fixed behavioral loop regardless of environmental feedback. In the philosophy of action, an agent is sphexish if they cannot deviate from a predetermined action-sequence even when the initiating conditions are satisfied only by accident. The essay distinguishes hard sphexishness, where the loop is invariant across all nearby possible worlds, from soft sphexishness, where deviation is physically possible but never actualized. Most compatibilists, it claims, concede that hard sphexishness undermines free will but defend soft sphexishness as compatible with genuine agency, because soft sphexishness permits counterfactual elasticity—the capacity to have acted differently had circumstances been slightly altered—which is said to be all that freedom requires. The essay then argues that this defense collapses: counterfactual elasticity is defined precisely in terms of the modal flexibility that sphexishness denies, so invoking it to save soft sphexishness is circular. Consequently, any deterministic account of human action is at least softly sphexish, and therefore fails to secure real free will.

In particular, address whether counterfactual elasticity is a recognized concept in the literature or an ad hoc construct, whether the compatibilist position is fairly represented, and whether the circularity objection actually holds once the terms are disambiguated. Finally, assess whether the essay’s conclusion—that determinism cannot ground free will because it entails soft sphexishness—follows from the corrected premises.
