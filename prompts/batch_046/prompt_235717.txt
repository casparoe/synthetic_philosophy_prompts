Compose a joint statement arising from an adversarial collaboration between a commemorative-pluralist historian and an evidential-integrity advocate, concerning how democratic societies employ narratives of national defeat and loss drawn from the Confederacy’s Lost Cause, the Masada siege, the Kosovo Field myth, and the Dunkirk evacuation. The statement should include: (i) a numbered section of agreed claims, among them an agreed four-part taxonomy—catastrophic defeat, redemptive withdrawal, martyrdom narrative, and foundational trauma—that distinguishes how these four cases operate in collective memory; (ii) precisely worded residual disagreements, each formulated so that its own camp could accept the wording of its side; (iii) the cruxes, defined as sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one party; and (iv) for each crux, one concrete historical comparison or philosophical argument both sides accept as bearing upon it, with each side’s predicted interpretation recorded in advance. Present the disagreements and cruxes in a table. Conclude by stating whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal, specifying which part and why. Write the response in British English.
