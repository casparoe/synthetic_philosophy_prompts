Ibn Khaldun then extended this logic to dynastic life-cycles through the concept of asabiyya, treating group solidarity as a measurable social force that necessarily peaks in the founding generation and decays across exactly three generations. Spengler universalized the pattern into a morphological biology of cultures, each passing through an inevitable seasonal sequence ending in a terminal winter of civilizational exhaustion from which no renewal is possible. Toynbee, in this account, added the mechanism of challenge and response, but retained the deterministic core: failure to respond produces an irreversible downward spiral structurally identical to Spengler's winter. The essay concludes that these four thinkers, despite differing contexts, discovered one underlying cyclical mechanism, and that modern linear-progress narratives reject this synthesis only because they commit what is often associated with Whitehead as the fallacy of misplaced concreteness—treating an abstract pattern as a concrete causal agent.
