Moritz Schlick died in 1936. In his essay on the meaning of life he maintained that purpose-driven labor is essentially meaningless because all goals lie in the future and are never possessed in the present; by contrast, he held that genuine meaning is found in playful activity (Spiel) that is valued for its own sake and experienced now. Suppose Schlick were alive today and confronted with two developments: first, the widespread automation of labor by artificial intelligence, which proponents claim will finally liberate human beings for lives of pure play; second, the emergence of fully immersive virtual environments that allow users to engage in risk-free, endlessly customizable playful experiences without bodily engagement or real-world consequence. Drawing on his distinction between work and play, his insistence that meaning must be present rather than projected, and his logical-positivist rejection of metaphysical consolation, how do you think he would evaluate whether these conditions realize his ideal of a meaningful existence, or whether they instead hollow out the very conditions—embodiment, risk, present engagement—that made play meaningful for him?
