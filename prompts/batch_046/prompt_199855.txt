As a retired physicist only recently turned to philosophy, I find the modern revival of Stoicism's dichotomy of control—the division between what lies within our power and what does not—too often presented as self-help rather than as a serious conceptual framework. Before I evaluate any contemporary defense, I need the full desiderata. Specify what an adequate modern account must deliver: the boundary cases it must handle (partial control, collective agency, probabilistic outcomes), the distinctions it must preserve rather than collapse (influence versus control, internal judgment versus its behavioral expression), and the findings from behavioral science or social psychology it cannot contradict without losing credibility. For each, give the motivating consideration; flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable; and then take a committed stand—identify which single requirement excludes the largest number of popular Stoic revivals, and which requirement popularizers treat as non-negotiable but ought to be abandoned.
