I'm putting together a handout for a grad workshop on whether there's a real moral differnce between lying and misleading, and I want a decision-tree taxonomy — not just a roll-call of philosophers — that sorts positions by what they actually commit to. Build it from three diagnostic splits: first, whether a speaker who engineers a false belief through selective silence or ambiguous framing has done something structurally identical to lying (think of it as a 'channel-blocking' versus 'signal-corrupting' distinction, but phrase the branch as a concrete claim someone can agree or disagree with); second, whether the wrongness lives in breaking an explicit assertion-norm or in exploiting the listener's reasoning autonomy regardless of form; third, whether being in an institutional role — doctor, regulator, spokesperson — collapses the distinction because the audience has a claim to direct information, not merely technically truthful output.

Each leaf should carry either a named position if people actually defend it, or be marked 'unnamed', plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment — for instance, the view that misleading is always permissible having to swallow that deliberately ambiguous risk warnings are morally neutral. Note any empty combinations and briefly diagnose them: incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag any pairs the tree can't separate — say, if a strict Kantian line and a contractualist line answer all three splits identically but diverge only on meta-ethical grounding, say so plainly.

Please write it in British English, keep the tone casual — a typo or two is totally fine — and please seperate what's settled (that both lying and misleading involve intentional deception) from what's genuinely contested among people who actually know the topic, especially around whether implicature can bear the same moral weight as explicit falsehood. Don't present a contested reading as the consensus. No bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests — this needs to work for someone without web access.
