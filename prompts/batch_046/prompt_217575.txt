ok so my study group got stuck on Strawson's Basic Argument and we pushed it into epistemology and now it feels like it swallows rationality whole. here's the chain:

(1) true moral responsability requires being the ultimate source of your action.
(2) but you only act the way you do because of your character, and you can't be responsible for your own character since that'd require already having the right character to shape it.
(3) so true moral responsibility is impossible.

then we went:

(4) beliefs are just mental actions, so the same regress hits them.
(5) if you're never the ultimate source of a belief, you're never blamewothy for holding it.
(6) but if you're never blameworthy, there's never rational pressure to revise it — evidence can't oblige you.
(7) so Strawson's argument implies that argument and peer review can never actually make anyone change there minds.
(8) that's obviously too much.

we think the hidden slide is between moral blame and rational obligation, but we're not sure which step to kill. which one should we reject, and what does giving it up cost? can we keep Strawson's point about actions but block the extension, or do we have to abandon the whole framework?
