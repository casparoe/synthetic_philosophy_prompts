I am trying to work through a notoriously difficult passage in Avicenna’s psychological writings—the suspended-man or flying-man thought experiment. The scenario describes a human being created fully formed and suspended in empty space, blindfolded and with all limbs separated so that no tactile, proprioceptive, or external sensory input reaches him at all. Despite this total sensory deprivation, Avicenna claims that the individual would nevertheless affirm his own existence and be immediately aware of himself.

What makes the passage hard for me is the tension it creates with Avicenna’s broader epistemology. In other parts of his corpus he maintains that human cognition ordinarily begins in sensation and that the intellect initially depends on sensory-based representations to form concepts and judgments. The suspended man, however, seems to possess a direct, non-representational acquaintance with his own essence that bypasses sensory mediation entirely.

A reading I recently came across sharpens the problem further. It proposes a minimal interpretation: perhaps the flying man does not actually grasp the substantial nature of his soul, but only registers the bare occurrence of a conscious state—an awareness without essential content or determination, closer to a pure that-it-is than to a what-it-is. On this view, Avicenna’s argument establishes only that self-awareness can survive the absence of bodily sensation, not that the soul is a self-subsisting substance known through its essence.

Could you help me understand what the passage actually licenses? Specifically, does Avicenna’s description entail that the suspended man knows the substantial essence of his soul, or does it support only the thinner claim that first-person presence can occur without bodily determination? And if the stronger substantialist reading is correct, how might a defender of Avicenna reconcile it with his usual commitment that even intellectual cognition requires prior sensory engagement? I would especially value help in seeing where the text leaves room for ambiguity and where a careful reader should resist reading modern assumptions back into it.
