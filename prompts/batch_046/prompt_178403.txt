I'm trying to work out where I actually stand on remembering and forgetting, partly after skimming Margalit and Rieff but mostly going off my gut. Here are my verdicts on cases — tell me if there's a principle that gets them all, or which ones I have to drop.

Case one: a survivor of a genocide insists on keeping a public archieve naming every collaborator, even though the community wants to move on. I think she's right — there's a duty not to let the record vanish.

Case two: a victim of long-term abuse burns every photo and refuses ever to mention the abuser's name, not because she forgives him but because she wants his presence erased from her mental life. That feels permissible, maybe even healthy.

Case three: a government deliberately shreds its own war-crimes archives to foster national healing. That feels wrong — like institutional betrayal.

Case four: parents never tell their adopted adult child, letting them build a false family narrative. Not fine, even if it spares them grief.

Case five: a town holds an annual remembrance of a famine, but over time stops mentioning that the government caused it, keeping only the mourning. That feels like a kind of corruption — the remembering becomes hollow.

Case six: a man still vividly remembers a minor betrayal by a schoolfriend forty years ago and refuses to let it soften. I'm torn — part of me says he's clinging to poison, part says loyalty to his own past self requires keeping the knowledge sharp. I realise there's tension here: in case two I think the victim can erase; in case six I think maybe she can't, or shouldn't? And in case one I think we must preserve, but case five shows preservation without content is useless.

My half-baked principle is something like mnemonic loyalty runs upward with victimhood: you owe remembrance to those harmed, including yourself when you were harmed, but you don't owe remembrance to harm you caused, and institutions owe it downward to citizens. But that doesn't quite cover case four or six.

Is there a cleaner principle — maybe about whether forgetting serves the victim's autonomy versus serving the perpetrator's comfort? Or about the difference between organic decay and curated deletion?
