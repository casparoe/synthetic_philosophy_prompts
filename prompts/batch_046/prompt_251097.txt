I am a philosophy professor advising an interdisciplinary consciousness-research unit that evaluates first-person reports from subjects in prolonged sensory-deprivation protocols, locked-in syndrome communication assessments, and structured contemplative training. I want to translate Avicenna's flying-man reasoning—specifically his claim that self-awareness can survive the total suspension of bodily and sensory content—into a reusable clinical assessment procedure that a non-philosopher researcher can apply consistently across cases.

The deliverable should be an ordered decision protocol (a flowchart in words, with explicit decision nodes) that determines whether a given first-person report genuinely instantiates the structure of the flying-man case, or whether it covertly depends on residual bodily framing, implicit mnemonic scaffolding, reconstructed narrative identity, or linguistic-temporal sequencing. At each stage, specify the passing criterion, the failing criterion, and the points where mechanical application is impossible and a judgment call—with its associated risk of error—is unavoidable.

The protocol must be designed for repeated use, not for a single case, and should explicitly address three hard-case categories: (1) reports that deny all bodily presence yet rely on temporal ordering or grammatical subject-position; (2) reports from subjects with minimal preserved interoception (e.g., residual cardiac or respiratory awareness); and (3) reports describing "pure existence" that embed evaluative, emotional, or teleological content potentially derived from prior embodied experience.

Apply the procedure to the following three hypothetical reports:
A. From a sensory-deprivation subject: "There is awareness, but nothing is present except the bare fact of being aware. No body, no duration, no words—only that it is."
B. From a locked-in patient communicating via eye-tracking: "I am present, but I cannot locate my body. Still, a specific fear persists—not of anything named, just fear itself."
C. From a long-term meditator: "There is no self to observe, only awareness turning back upon itself; and it is peaceful, as my practice has taught me it should be."

For each, show what the protocol yields, and identify the one case where it returns a verdict the research unit is likely to resist—specifically, that a report intended to exemplify pure flying-man awareness must be downgraded because hidden structural dependencies survive the explicit denial of content. Then specify two inherent limits of the procedure: one arising from philosophical disagreement about whether self-reference can ever be genuinely non-bodily, and one arising from the clinical setting's own framing effects (interview structure, prior instruction, communicative medium) that the protocol cannot fully neutralize.

The procedure should not depend on bibliographic references, citation practices, or knowledge of niche interpretive traditions; it should be executable by a researcher with general philosophical literacy but no specialized training in medieval philosophy or phenomenology.
