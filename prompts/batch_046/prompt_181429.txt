You serve on a municipal public-health board that must evaluate drug-policy reform proposals — for instance, adopting Portugal-style decriminalization of possession, or introducing a regulated cannabis market. Write a reusable decision procedure your board can run on any proposal. Ground the procedure in the philosophical distinction between autonomy as mere non-interference and autonomy as diachronic self-governance: the capacity of a person to maintain a coherent practical identity across present and future choices, where institutional structures (referral pathways, education, trafficking controls) either support or erode that coherence. Structure it as ordered questions with explicit decision rules: for each gate, specify what empirical or normative condition must be satisfied, what counts as sufficient evidence without legal expertise, and where the procedure must stop and flag a value judgment for a board vote rather than yielding an automatic recommendation. Then apply the procedure to two cases: (a) decriminalizing possession of small-quantity cannabis with mandatory health assessment and no criminal record; (b) legalizing commercial cannabis sales with minimal age-gating and no health-referral mechanism. For case (b), show how the procedure reaches a negative assessment that conflicts with a simple libertarian intuition — that removing criminal penalties is by itself adequate — and identify precisely where human judgment must take over because the failure concerns values, not facts. Include a brief note on the procedure’s likely failure modes, especially when a proposal mixes decriminalization of use with unregulated supply. Keep the steps executable in a single meeting.
