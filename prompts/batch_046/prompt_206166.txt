If Dennett's multiple drafts model eliminates the Cartesian theater by distributing narrative fragments across neural processes without a central audience, then every conscious experience appears to be nothing more than the draft that happens to win an editorial competition at a given moment. But this seems to preserve the very structure Dennett rejects: the site of editorial convergence operates like a central screen, except that it is distributed across time rather than localized in space. Consequently, the model seems to replace a spatial homunculus with a temporal one, and the problem of explaining why there is a unified subject of experience is transformed into the problem of explaining why successive drafts cohere into a single narrative thread. Given that the core disagreement therefore concerns whether centralization can be satisfied by temporal integration rather than spatial localization, is the dispute between Cartesian theater defenders and multiple-drafts theorists genuinely about the architecture of consciousness, or is it primarily a verbal disagreement about what counts as a center?
