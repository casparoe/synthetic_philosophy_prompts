Write an essay on whether Popper's problem of verisimilitude can be solved without importing normative judgments about which theoretical errors matter most. Popper proposed that false theories could be compared by their logical and empirical content, yielding an objective measure of closeness to the truth, though he later acknowledged that the formal apparatus for quantitative comparison faced serious obstacles. Contemporary defenders sometimes argue that an ordinal ranking of theories by their survived severe tests can substitute for a precise metric, while critics reply that deciding which tests are genuinely "severe" and which predictive failures are damaging requires prior methodological commitments about explanatory importance and domain centrality. Assess both sides carefully, weighing whether a purely descriptive account of scientific progress is available, but reach a definite conclusion: can verisimilitude function as an objective property of theories, or does every attempt to measure progress toward the truth necessarily encode evaluative choices about what counts as a significant mistake?
