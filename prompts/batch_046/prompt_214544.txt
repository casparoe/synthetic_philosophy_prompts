I am preparing materials for an advanced applied-ethics seminar on moral obligations in aquatic food systems. Please construct a taxonomy of ethical positions on the welfare of farmed fish and decapod crustaceans, formatted as a branching tree. Each internal node should be labeled by the specific normative or empirical claim that splits its child branches, phrased as a yes/no or either/or question that a participant could answer without knowing the position names. The root node should distinguish those who deny that farmed aquatic animals have morally relevant welfare interests from those who affirm it. From the affirmative branch, split first on whether rapid population turnover in crustacean farming—where individual lifespans are far shorter than the time horizons of welfare reforms—activates a replacement-sensitive moral calculus, so that reducing per-cohort suffering may not reduce aggregate suffering if replacement rates remain constant, versus treating each cohort as an independent moral unit regardless of turnover. The next split should ask whether the primary harm to address is individual nociceptive experience, population-level density stress, or ecological displacement of wild stocks. Then split on whether the appropriate response is incremental welfare reform (stunning protocols, density caps, enriched environments) or structural abolition, with a further sub-branch asking whether reform is pragmatically self-defeating because it extends the industry’s social license and delays abolition. Include a cross-cutting branch asking whether wild-animal suffering provides a comparative baseline that renders farmed suffering a net moral improvement, is ethically irrelevant, or makes farming obligatory when it substitutes for predation. At each leaf, provide a name for the position if one is recognized in broader animal-ethics discourse (for example, abolitionist with replacement-sensitive reasoning, welfare-reform density-focused, ecological-comparativist) or label it unnamed; then add one sentence describing its most awkward or counterintuitive commitment. Where the tree leaves logical combinations unoccupied, flag them and briefly diagnose whether the gap seems due to incoherence, practical unattractiveness, or mere neglect. The taxonomy should treat fish and decapod crustaceans separately only where biological differences in lifespan, nociceptive architecture, or farming density genuinely alter the ethical calculus, and it should not depend on any citation, bibliography, or specific empirical study.
