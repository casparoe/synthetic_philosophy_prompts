Consider the following tension within Gricean pragmatics. A speaker can apparently deploy a conventional implicature trigger—say, a contrastive conjunction or an evidential adverb—not to activate its standard conventional force, but to bracket that very force and thereby generate a higher-order conversational implicature about her epistemic posture or rhetorical distance from the content. This creates pressure on Grice’s claim that conventional implicatures are non-cancellable and tightly bound to specific linguistic forms.

Please rank the following five theoretical responses to these bracketed-trigger cases, assigning each a tier (S, A, B, C, or D) according to how well it preserves Grice’s core distinction between what is said and what is implicated without collapsing conventional implicature into conversational implicature:

1. The null-contribution analysis: the trigger belongs to what is said but carries no semantic content, leaving all pragmatic work to the conversational level.
2. The layered-convention analysis: conventional triggers can carry a meta-conventional layer that explicitly suspends their first-order force.
3. The full-collapsing analysis: all implicatures are calculable from the Cooperative Principle, and the conventional/conversational boundary is merely heuristic.
4. The explicit-cancellation analysis: conventional implicatures remain intact but can be cancelled by an explicit meta-linguistic frame without generating contradiction.
5. The misanalysis analysis: apparent bracketed uses are actually ordinary conversational implicatures that happen to use conventional trigger-words accidentally.

For each tier placement, provide a one-sentence justification focused specifically on whether the response respects Grice’s insistence that conventional implicature is form-bound and non-cancellable. Then conclude with a brief judgment about which tier best balances fidelity to Grice’s original architecture with explanatory coverage of the bracketed cases, and state whether any of the lower-tier responses could be rehabilitated by a minor modification rather than abandonment.
