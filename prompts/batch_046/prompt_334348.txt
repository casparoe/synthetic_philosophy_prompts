I just finished a long argument with a friend about what Descartes's cogito actually proves. She insists that unless it establishes me as a persistent, immaterial substance—a res cogitans with enduring identity—then it fails as a foundation, because all you've shown is that some thinking occurs now, with no guarantee the same thinker exists a moment later. I think she's assuming that epistemic certainty must come packaged as a metaphysical subject, but I'm unsure whether that's her error or mine. Could the cogito's achievement be purely structural—showing that doubting one's own existence is performatively self-defeating—without delivering any ontology of the self? And if so, are the standard objections about Descartes illicitly inferring a thinker from a thought misdirected, since they treat a methodological point about the conditions of doubt as if it were meant to be a proof of substance? I can't tell whether the real confusion lies in her demand for a soul, or in my attempt to rescue the argument by stripping away the metaphysics Descartes believed he had earned.
