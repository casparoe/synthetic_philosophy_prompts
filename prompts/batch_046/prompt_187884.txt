Analyze the principal implications of the causal exclusion argument for mental causation, particularly regarding the standing of first-person phenomenological reports in clinical psychiatric evaluation. Consider a case in which neurobiological mechanisms furnish a complete causal account of a patient's behavioral and affective symptoms. How does the exclusion argument affect whether the patient's subjective narrative retains independent explanatory power, or whether it is relegated to epiphenomenal status? And what does this entail for the legitimacy of integrating experiential testimony with brain-based diagnostic categories in psychiatric practice?
