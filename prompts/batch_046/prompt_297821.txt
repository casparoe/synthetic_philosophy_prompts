I co-manage a membership collective that recently launched an optional “life-curator” service. The contract is revocable with thirty days’ notice, yet cancellation is almost nonexistent; satisfaction scores are extraordinarily high, and members describe the experience as being freed from the tyranny of choice. A small faction has resigned, arguing that the collective is replicating the logic of Dostoevsky’s Grand Inquisitor—trading freedom for bread, miracle, and authority—only with voluntary enrollment. I feel there is something wrong with this, but I am unsure whether my objection is romantic or philosophically sound. Does the Inquisitor’s argument actually justify a voluntary surrender of freedom, or does the voluntariness break the analogy? And is there a defensible distinction between choosing to be guided and being genuinely free that would vindicate the minority position?
