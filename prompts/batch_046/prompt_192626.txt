You are a philosopher of science specializing in ontology and explanatory stances, with particular familiarity with Daniel Dennett's framework of the physical, design, and intentional stances and his argument about "real patterns." My colleague Dr. Lena Vasquez has published a sequence of rulings on whether particular patterns count as real—in Dennett's sense of being genuinely projectible, counterfactual-supporting, and not merely instrumental noise—rather than as heuristic fictions. Second, the boom-and-bust predator-prey dynamics of the Isle Royale wolf-moose system are real at the design stance, and she insists this ecological pattern is not reducible to "just physics"; nevertheless she denies it is real at the intentional stance, since neither species represents the cycle. Third, the so-called "market sentiment" driving speculative bubbles in decentralized cryptocurrency markets is, in her ruling, not real even at the intentional stance—she treats it as an aggregation fallacy, with no unified agent whose beliefs or desires generate the pattern, only distributed statistical noise. Fourth, the homeostatic set-point maintaining human core temperature near 37°C is real at the design stance, with explicit denial that it is real at the physical stance (mere biochemical kinetics) and explicit denial at the intentional stance (no mental representation of the set-point).

The unsettled case is this.
