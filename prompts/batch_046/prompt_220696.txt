Please set out the most important implications of the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents for what I shall term a "staged-trust revision protocol" in automated reasoning. The architecture is thus not self-trusting in the global sense that has traditionally been analysed: trust is distributed, conditional, and localised to adjacent pairs of stages, with each successor engineered to preserve a form of epistemic hygiene relative to its predecessor. Finally, discuss what these considerations imply for the feasibility of incremental self-improvement in formal reasoning architectures, especially with regard to the tension between local epistemic responsibility and global coherence.
