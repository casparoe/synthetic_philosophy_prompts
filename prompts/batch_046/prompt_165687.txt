I am helping design an adversarial-collaboration protocol for a graduate seminar on experimental philosophy and moral cognition. Two colleagues have spent a semester arguing about the Knobe effect—specifically, whether the asymmetry between harm and help attributions in side-effect vignettes reflects a deep conceptual link between intentionality judgments and normative evaluation (the conceptual-essentialist camp), or whether it is fully explicable by pragmatic implicature, conversational norms, and framing artifacts (the pragmatic-deflationist camp). Rather than continue talking past each other, they have agreed to produce a single joint statement that separates genuine disagreement from verbal confusion.

Please draft the document they should have signed. Structure it in three parts. First, a numbered section of at least six claims about the Knobe effect, experimental methodology, and the limits of current data that both camps can accept without reservation—claims that are neither vacuous nor slanted toward either side. Second, a table or clearly demarcated list stating the exact residual disagreements, with each camp’s position phrased in wording its own advocates would recognize as accurate and its opponents would accept as a fair characterization rather than a straw man. Third, identify the three deepest cruxes—the sub-questions such that resolving at least one would genuinely move one side closer to conceding ground—and for each crux specify a concrete hypothetical case or experimental prediction that both sides agree would bear on it, recording in advance what each camp predicts the outcome or evaluation would be.

To prevent the collaboration from collapsing into well-trodden ground, both parties have agreed to ground their disagreements in a specific, somewhat novel extension they term the “opaque-intermediary problem”: vignettes in which an agent initiates a process (for example, deploying a self-learning optimization system or a long-chain ecological intervention) that eventually produces a side effect, but the causal path involves autonomous intermediate steps whose outcomes the original agent neither foresaw nor directly controlled. The joint statement should treat this extension not as empirically settled, but as the shared testing ground for their cruxes. The document should be formal, polished, and appropriate for a signed academic working paper. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not invoke highly specialized formal models that depend on expert memorization of niche literature. Conclude with exactly one line that summarizes the single deepest crux you have identified, enclosed in \answer{...}.
