I have been trying to formalize my own intuitions about rational belief revision when an agent learns information structured as a conditional, or when the conditioning event was initially assigned probability zero, especially in variations on the Judy Benjamin scenario. I am taking a course in formal epistemology and find that my judgments across analog cases do not obviously collapse into standard Bayesian conditioning or Jeffrey conditioning, but I do not want a bibliography—I want to know whether a coherent updating principle exists, perhaps one that treats intra-partition ratios as invariant under outer-partition shifts, or one that takes conditional beliefs as primitive. Here are my verdicts on five concrete situations.

First, in the classic Judy Benjamin setup: Judy assigns prior probabilities 0.9 to being in headquarters, 0.05 to front-lines sector Alpha, and 0.05 to front-lines sector Bravo. She then receives direct, authoritative information that she is in the front lines, with no further specification. I judge that her credence in Alpha should be about 0.5, preserving the internal 1:1 ratio within the front-lines branch, rather than being tied to the prior absolute weight of 0.05.

Second, a meteorological analog: a forecaster assigns 0.6 to climate model M and 0.4 to model N. Within M, the conditional probability of rain given clouds is 0.9; within N, it is 0.4. She observes clouds, but then receives an independent satellite report that falsifies M entirely. I think her credence in rain should drop to about 0.4, as if the model space has collapsed and only the surviving model’s conditional governs her belief. Yet I am unsure whether this is conditioning on “M is false” in an expanded sample space, or whether model-selection probabilities must be updated separately from the conditional structure inside them.

Third, suppose Judy initially assigns probability 1 to being in headquarters, yet she also holds a conditional belief that if she were in the front lines, she would be in Alpha with probability 0.8. She then receives a direct report that she is in the front lines. Despite her prior absolute credence in headquarters being 1, I feel she should shift her credence in Alpha to roughly 0.8, treating the conditional as primitive rather than derived from a joint distribution. But does that commit me to saying that conditional beliefs can survive the total elimination of their antecedent’s prior probability?

Fourth, a historian assigns 0.95 to document D being authentic, and holds that if it is a forgery, it was produced by forger A with probability 0.7 and by forger B with 0.3. She then notices a feature F that she realizes she would certainly have observed had D been authentic, but she does not observe F. I think she should redistribute her remaining 0.05 credence according to the 0.7/0.3 split. Yet if she initially assigned zero probability to the specific absence of F, standard conditioning seems blocked. Should the update depend on whether the absence was already in her initial sample space?

Fifth, consider an agent who learns not a factual proposition but a counterfactual: she is told that if the election had gone differently, policy P would have been enacted. She had previously assigned probabilities over worlds in which the election went differently, but assigned zero weight to worlds in which P was enacted. After learning the counterfactual, she assigns high credence to P in those worlds. I am unsure whether this should be modeled as updating on a conditional with a zero-probability antecedent, or as something structurally different altogether.

Is there a single principle—stated precisely, with clauses motivated by each case—that delivers all of these verdicts? If so, please state it, indicate what it predicts for a case I have not considered (for instance, an agent who learns that a previously considered impossible event has occurred and must revise both her unconditional beliefs and her conditional beliefs within that event), and be explicit about which of my five intuitions it forces me to drop and why.
