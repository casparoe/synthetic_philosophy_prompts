If real patterns exist only insofar as they license predictions from the physical, design, or intentional stance, then consider adversarial perturbations in deep neural networks: physically imperceptible alterations to input pixels that reliably trigger specific misclassifications, producing a stable statistical signature predictable only when we model the network's internal feature hierarchy. To treat this signature as a real pattern rather than a measurement artifact, must we adopt the intentional stance—thereby attributing beliefs or representational goals to the network—or can the design stance alone license the prediction? Furthermore, since the pattern vanishes under a purely physical-stance description of raw tensor operations, does its stance-dependence prove it is merely a useful fiction, or is it analogous to a center-of-mass trajectory that remains real despite being absent from particle-level descriptions?
