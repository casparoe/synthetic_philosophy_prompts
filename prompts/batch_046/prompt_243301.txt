So I was reading about how some development NGOs run entrepreneurship mindset workshops in rural areas where they teach people to be more future-oriented and risk-tolerant, basically treating poverty as partly a cultural problem of values. Meanwhile the same organizations will build a well or fund a road if its a different grant. What theory of causation does that split presuppose? Like, do they have to be Weberians who think values can actually drive economic outcomes on their own, or can a materialist explain why they'd still do culture-training? And is there an implicit commitment about whether culture is something you can engineer the way you engineer infrastructure? I'm thinking of Weber's protestant ethic stuff but not just the thesis itself—more the problem of explaining with values. If we took seriously that culture is only ever an elective affinity with material conditions and never an independent cause, would these programs even make sense, or would they have to become pure material redistribution? Also, why do we treat bad culture as a cause of poverty in these programs but never treat good culture as a cause of wealth in the same explicit way? What's the asymmetry doing philosophically?
