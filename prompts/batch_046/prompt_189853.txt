in my reading group we keep hitting a wall on delegated performatives—like when a chatbot puts up a content warning or a recorded voice says youre being monitored. The conventionalists say its a real speech act because the institution authorizes it and listeners respond with the right normative attitude, so speaker absence doesnt matter. The intentionalists say its hollow: no mental state behind the utterance means no illocutionary force, just a causal trigger like a smoke alarm. We've been stuck for months and im starting to think were mixing up two issues—whether performatives need a speaker-mind, and whether institutional framing can substitute for it—but i cant tell if thats the real stall or if theres a hidden empirical disagreement about how people actually treat these warnings. Diagnose it: is this verbal, substantive, or two questions under one name? And what would actually count as progress here—a conceptual split between authorization and intention, or an experiment on listener uptake, or should we just admit the question is ill-posed?
