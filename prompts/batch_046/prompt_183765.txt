I am a doctoral student in political philosophy writing a dissertation chapter on the logical architecture of liberal rights within social choice theory. I am examining Sen's liberal paradox—the tension between a minimal liberal principle that grants each individual decisive authority over a pair of social alternatives within a personal domain and the Pareto criterion for collective choice, traditionally illustrated with two agents and a single observable consumption choice. Rather than asking for an exegesis of the classic formulation, I want to evaluate how five structural repairs perform conceptually when the preference profile retains the interdependence that generates the contradiction but is embedded in a framework that distinguishes first-order personal choices from second-order coordination norms.

Consider these five proposed resolutions:

(i) The Pareto-abandonment strategy: retain minimal liberalism in full but reject the Pareto principle as a universal constraint on social choice, accepting that individually protected domains may block collectively preferred outcomes.

(ii) The strict self-regarding restriction: weaken minimal liberalism so it applies only when an agent's preference ordering over the pair is invariant to the other agent's state—thereby excluding the paradox-generating preferences on the grounds that they are not genuinely self-regarding.

(iii) The mechanism-constraint revision: preserve both principles but apply liberal rights only to the design of the social-choice procedure, not to outcomes, so that a result falling inside an individual's domain is permitted provided the mechanism that selected it was rights-respecting.

(iv) The two-level preference dissolution: split each agent's input into first-order preferences over states within one's domain and second-order meta-preferences over social coordination; aggregate the first-order inputs via minimal liberalism and the second-order via a Pareto-sensitive rule, then resolve conflicts through lexical priority of the second-order.

(v) The relational-autonomy dissolution: reject the admissibility of the paradox preferences altogether, arguing that they are formed under conditions of non-autonomous interdependence; reconstruct the preference profile under conditions of relational autonomy, which eliminates the contradiction without formally altering either minimal liberalism or Pareto.

Please rank these five strategies from 1 (most defensible as preserving the normative core of liberal sovereignty without covertly abandoning one of the conflicting principles) to 5 (least defensible). For each rank, provide a concise justification—no more than two sentences—focusing specifically on whether the resolution clarifies the scope of rights or instead dissolves the tension by manipulating inputs or abandoning aggregation principles. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I need only the conceptual evaluation.
