Student answer:

'Darwinian creatures are basically hardwired — behaviors shaped by evolution, like spiders weaving webs. They can't learn individually. Skinnerian creatures learn through trial and error with direct reinforcement from the environment; Skinner's pigeons are the standard example, and they need actual rewards or punishments to change behavior.

Popperian creatures can run simulations internally. Instead of physically trying dangerous actions, they generate hypotheses and reject bad ones inside their minds. This comes straight from Karl Popper's philosophy of science, where theories are tested and falsified. Because the selection happens entirely in the head, a Popperian creature doesn't actually need environmental feedback to learn — the internal loop is enough.

Gregorian creatures are named after Gregory Bateson, and they're essentially Popperians that gained language. Language lets them share those internal hypotheses with others, which is just faster communication. Cultural tools like writing or computers are basically external storage for these shared ideas — they don't change the underlying cognitive mechanism, which is still internal testing plus talking. So humans are Gregorian mainly because we talk and build software.'
