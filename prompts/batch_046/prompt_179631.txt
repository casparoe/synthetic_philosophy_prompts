I am working up notes for an essay on Van Fraassen's Reflection principle and would appreciate targeted comments before developing them further.

The principle, as I understand it, requires that an agent's present credences align with her rational expectation of her future credences: if she is now sure that she will later assign probability r to A, her current probability in A should be r.

My central claim is that this assumes diachronic semantic stability, which fails under deep conceptual revision. Consider a case in which a theoretical term central to a scientific framework is reconceptualized across what I will call a framework shift. At time t, the agent assigns credences within conceptual scheme C to propositions involving that term. At time t+1, she operates within scheme C', where the corresponding propositions have different conditions of application and different inferential roles. Because she cannot now represent the contents of C', she cannot form the expectation at t of her credence at t+1 in A that Reflection demands. The principle therefore presupposes that all belief revision is evidential and semantically transparent, but if revision can be framework-dependent, Reflection is either inapplicable or demands a meta-language unavailable to the agent.
