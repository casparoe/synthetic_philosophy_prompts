Meanwhile, standard expected-utility evaluation should declare the expectation undefined and therefore refuse to generate any recommendation, effectively treating the gamble as rationally incomparable to the status quo.

Keep the scenario realistic: the stakes should be monetary amounts a normal person could face, and there should be no appeal to perfect predictors, demons, or exotic physics—just the coin-toss mechanism and the payoff schedule. Finally, include an honest note on how a defender of standard expected-utility theory would attack this case, for example by arguing that the discount factor is an ad hoc modification that smuggles in risk aversion under the guise of evaluation. Reach a brief conclusion about whether the case successfully isolates the role of undefined expectations in rational choice.
