I've been playing with a kind of hybrid take on Einsteins hole argument. The usual story is that general covariance plus substantivalism lets you slide the metric around inside a hole and get different futures for the same initial conditions, so determinism dies. The standard reply is point-coincidence: only the coincidence of fields and matter matters, so the slid metrics are physically identical. But I'm wondering if there's a hidden middle option. Suppose you grant that spacetime points have a kind of weak individuality — not full haecceity, but enough that you can track them via the geodesic structure they inherit from the global metric outside the hole. Then the hole argument might be revived not as an attack on determinism, but as showing that relationalism about points collapses into a weird form of essentialism about the boundary conditions. So my position is: the hole argument actually exposes a tension in relationalism, not in substantivalism, and the point-coincidence reply saves determinism only by smuggling in boundary essentialism.

What would actually count against this? I'm not lookin for "here's why relationalism is wrong" in general — I mean specifically, what observation, formal result, or conceptual move would break this particular framing? Would a discrete spacetime model (like causal sets) kill it by removing points altogether? Would a proof that boundary conditions can't be essentialized without breaking diffeomorphism invariance? Or is this position just insulated because I can always retreat to saying "well then the boundary conditions are physically real"? I want the defeaters ranked: fatal vs awkward vs things I'd just dismiss. Also, am I misreading the point-coincidence reply — does it actually avoid essentialism about boundaries?
