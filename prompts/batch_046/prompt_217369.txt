Write a Petrarchan sonnet spoken in the voice of Newton’s bucket—i.e., the rotating vessel itself—about the substantivalist–relationist dispute over whether the concavity of the water proves absolute space. The octave should render the bucket’s phenomenology precisely: water climbing the sides, the distant stars apparently irrelevant, the sense that something genuinely turns even when every local body is at rest. At the volta (line 9), introduce the relationist rejoinder—Mach’s principle, or the idea that rotation is defined only relative to the distant masses of the universe—without resolving it. The sestet should hold both positions in suspension: the water remains curved, the cosmos remains far, and the final image should be a stalemate rather than a synthesis. To make the form do philosophical work, use “hard” mechanical rhyme sounds in the octave (space / face / case / place) and “soft” relational sounds in the sestet (turn / learn / concern / burn), so the sonic shift itself tracks the move from absolute to relational framing. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows Newton’s bucket argument and Mach’s principle recognizes the exact moves being made, yet it should stand as a poem for a reader who knows neither. Do not resolve the tension; leave it open, as the debate itself remains.
