I have recently begun curating a small interdisciplinary exhibition on the transition from medieval to early modern natural philosophy, spanning roughly 1350 to 1650, and in interviewing historians and reviewing recent scholarship I have repeatedly encountered a pattern for which I lack a precise name. The pattern works like this. When I ask whether labels such as "the Scientific Revolution," "the Renaissance," or "the Middle Ages" denote real historical epochs with discrete boundaries, scholars almost uniformly reply that these are retrospective historiographical constructs—useful names invented long after the fact, not natural kinds that existed in the minds of the people who lived through them. They warn vigorously against reification and anachronism. Yet in the very same conversation, these scholars explain particular texts, conflicts, or institutional changes by appealing to the internal logic or causal power of the period itself: they will say that a certain mode of inquiry "flourished because it was the Renaissance," or that tension between authority and observation "is characteristic of the Scientific Revolution," treating the label as if it possesses coherent boundaries and explanatory force. I have seen the same move with "the Middle Ages": historians reject the idea of a uniform dark interlude, but then organize causal narratives around what that era "lacked" relative to later ones, as though the absence itself were a property of a discrete container.

This is not merely employing a heuristic, because the denial of ontological reality is explicit and insistent; nor is it simple reification, because the scholars are genuinely aware of the construct-status and repeatedly affirm it. It seems closer to a sustained oscillation between ontological denial and functional deployment, often coupled with defining the period negatively against a later standard. Does this specific configuration have an established name in philosophy of history or historiography? How exactly does it differ from an ordinary Weberian ideal type, from reification proper, and from what is sometimes called strategic essentialism? If there is no precise term, what should we call it, and what are its boundary conditions—for instance, must the period be defined partly by absence or negation relative to a subsequent era, or can the pattern occur with any retrospective label that is simultaneously disavowed and causally invoked? I would appreciate a carefully reasoned answer in American English.
