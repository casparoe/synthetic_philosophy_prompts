Write a short dialogue between Maya and Leo, two philosophy grad students arguing about Death in Damascus over coffee. Maya defends causal decision theory: she says the merchant should stay in Damascus because fleeing doesn't causally change where Death waits, it only changes where the merchant ends up running into him. Leo pushes back—he says that framing ignores that Death's location is counterfactualy dependent on the merchant's choice, so treating flight as causally independant begs the question. Then Maya introduces a twist: imagine the merchant knows Death updates his schedule using the merchant's own decision algorithm, not just his final act. Leo claims this just collapses into Newcomb's problem with extra steps, but Maya argues it's different because the update is evidential, not mediated by a causal mechanism, and that Leo is smuggling in a deterministic picture of deliberation. Make them actually respond to each other's points—no strawmen, neither should clearly win. Keep it casual, maybe include a typo or two.
