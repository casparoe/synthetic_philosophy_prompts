As a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, I am assembling a structured dataset of conceptual classification instruments and need a decision-tree taxonomy for interpretations of quantum mechanics. The taxonomy should sort positions—specifically Everettian, Bohmian, and Copenhagen-style interpretations—not by their conventional names but by concrete commitments that a reader could endorse without already knowing which label applies.

Begin at the root with the claim: “The universal wavefunction evolves linearly and unitarily at all times, with no exception.” From this root, branch first on whether experimentally observed measurements yield exactly one definite physical outcome or allow multiplicity. Next, branch on whether the wavefunction is supplemented by any additional ontological variables (hidden variables). Then branch on whether the Born rule is introduced as an axiomatic postulate or derived from the dynamics and structure of the theory alone.

Your tree should terminate in four named leaves: (1) Everettian mechanics with branching worlds; (2) Bohmian mechanics; (3) Copenhagen-style collapse interpretations; and (4) an epistemic or information-theoretic variant of Copenhagen (e.g., QBist-style). For each leaf, provide one sentence identifying its most awkward commitment regarding the reconciliation of deterministic dynamics with observed statistical outcomes.

Crucially, expose an unoccupied logical region in the tree: the combination that accepts unitary evolution without exception, insists on single definite outcomes, rejects hidden variables entirely, and demands a dynamical derivation of the Born rule rather than accepting it as postulated. Label this combination “unoccupied,” and diagnose in one sentence whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

Also flag any pair of leaves that your branching logic cannot genuinely separate—cases where two interpretations share all node answers and differ only in rhetorical framing.

Every node must be phrased as a concrete claim-based or yes/no question (for example, “Do measurements have exactly one physical outcome?”), never as “Are you an Everettian?” Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or appeals to obscure secondary literature; the instrument must be fully usable without external lookup. Present the result as a formal, polished classification instrument suitable for inclusion in a technical dataset.
