Most countries treat secession as something you can only do if you're being seriosuly oppressed, like Kosovo was, but they don't treat it as a normal democratic option even when a clear majority wants out for cultural or economic reasons—think Scotland or Catalonia. At the same time, those same states usually let regions have a lot of internal self-rule, like Quebec's language laws or Scotland's parliament, as long as they stay inside the union. What theory of political legitimacy does that combo presuppose? Is it that states are voluntary associations that can be dissolved by consent, but only if that consent is remedial? Or does it assume that territorial integrity is a default moral fact that democratic preference just can't override unless there's grave injustice? Reconstruct the implicit view, check whether it's actually consistent—since we let people leave marriages or contracts for reasons that aren't oppressive at all—and tell me what'd have to change in border law if we replaced it with a straightforward democratic-exit principle.
