Jean-Jacques Rousseau argued that conventional theater corrupts audiences by substituting artificial emotional display for genuine moral sentiment and by drawing citizens away from direct civic engagement into passive, isolated spectatorship. Imagine Rousseau is presented with a form he never encountered: “biometrically responsive distributed theater,” in which each spectator wears a headset that generates a personalized dramatic narrative in real time, with no shared physical stage, performers rendered as synthetic agents that adapt dialogue and gesture to the viewer’s live heart-rate and facial-expression data, and with the audience physically separated in private pods. The experience is marketed not as entertainment but as a therapeutic technology for emotional regulation. Drawing on the conceptual structure of Rousseau’s critique—especially his worries about artificiality, the fragmentation of shared public life, and the confusion of performed feeling with authentic virtue—predict how he would assess this form. Would he regard it as an extreme intensification of the dangers he identified, or might he see in its isolation and synthetic personalization a different, perhaps more fundamental, threat to the unmediated social bond he valued? Be specific about which elements of his framework apply and where they might require revision.
