In a study group on migration ethics, a fellow student shut down my tentative question about whether states may legitimately penalize unauthorized border-crossing by declaring that "no one is illegal," apparently taking the phrase to settle both the linguistic and the normative issue at once. I am trying to understand exactly how many different theses could be compressed into that slogan, and which of them—if any—actually do the work of ruling out border enforcement. Could you lay out, as a menu rather than a single interpretation, the distinct precise claims the slogan might be advancing: for instance, the grammatical or rhetorical claim that predicating "illegal" of a person is a category mistake or dehumanizing; the legal-taxonomic claim that in standard jurisprudence wrongness attaches to acts (and that in many jurisdictions unauthorized entry is a civil, not criminal, infraction, so labeling a person "illegal" doubly mischaracterizes the legal status); the moral-status claim that human beings possess an inherent dignity or membership right that no immigration statute can annul; and the stronger political claim that territorial sovereignty never justifies exclusion, so that treating migrants as wrongdoers is necessarily unjust? For each reading, indicate whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and specify whether it actually supports the particular move my study partner made—namely, that states are not permitted to criminalize or penalize unauthorized entry because the people involved are not the kind of entities that can bear legal wrongness. Please separate what is conceptually settled (e.g., that legal categories usually apply to conduct) from what remains genuinely contested among philosophers and legal scholars of migration (e.g., whether moral personhood constrains state sovereignty over borders, or whether civil penalties are coercive in a morally relevant way), and do not present any contested normative conclusion as the consensus view. End with a single line beginning "Verdict:" that lists, from weakest to strongest, the readings that genuinely license my partner's dismissal of border criminalization.
