As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun to study philosophy systematically, I am trying to map whether philosophy makes progress in any sense comparable to science. Please present a taxonomy of positions on philosophical progress as a decision tree with exactly five terminal leaves, delivered in a numbered list from 1 to 5 with no introduction or conclusion. Each item should contain exactly two sentences: the first should state the branching diagnostic—whether philosophy has genuinely answerable questions, whether correct answers accumulate cumulatively, and whether a failure to converge is best interpreted as problem-dissolution, perennial stagnation, or as a retrograde conceptual expansion in which philosophy deliberately multiplies live options rather than reducing them—and the second should identify the position by name or as 'unnamed' and state its single most awkward commitment, particularly any conflict with scientific norms of assessment. Frame every branch as a concrete claim that can be evaluated without prior knowledge of philosophical labels, and use item 5 to flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate.
