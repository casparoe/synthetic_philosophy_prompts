Please explain the conceptual tension that arises if we apply Moore's proof of an external world to a subject experiencing a vivid phantom-limb sensation that is phenomenologically indistinguishable, from the first-person perspective, from seeing an actual hand. Specifically, address whether Moore's inference from the claim that here is a hand to the claim that therefore there is an external world requires an implicit premise about the reliability of perceptual discrimination under normal conditions, and if so, whether that premise already presupposes the existence of an external world in a way that makes the proof circular when extended to abnormal cases. In your explanation, distinguish between the epistemic status of the perceptual claim in the standard case and in the phantom-limb case, and clarify whether Moore's strategy can be repaired by restricting the proof to well-behaved perceptual contexts without begging the question against the skeptic.
