I organize a monthly discussion night at my local library for adults with no formal philosophy or economics training. Next session I want to address the sunk-cost fallacy, but specifically to examine whether honoring sunk costs is ever rational rather than treating it as a simple error. Plan the explanatory arc: the conceptual distinction that must be in place before the question even makes sense to them—between resources already expended and commitments that structure future choices—the single concrete scenario that should carry the whole session, which must present both an obvious case of wasteful persistence and a less obvious case where continuing is defensible for reasons other than the past expenditure, the misconceptions the group will predictably raise in the order they are likely to arise and the exact point in the sequence where each should be defused, one closing question I can ask to tell whether they have grasped the structural distinction or are merely repeating “don’t throw good money after bad,” and what part of formal decision theory I should openly say I am leaving out.
