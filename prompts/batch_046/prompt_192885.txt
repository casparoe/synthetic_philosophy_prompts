I've been turning over the saying "the ends don't justify the means" ever since it was used to dismiss an argument about whether a government may violate one person's rights to stop a collapse that would violate thousands of people's rights in exactly the same way. To me the phrase sounds like it should be obvious, but when I try to apply it I realize I don't know whether it means that consequences never count, that you can never use one wrong act as a tool, or something else entirely. Could you separate out the different precise moral claims this slogan might stand for—ranging from the trivial to the genuinely controversial—especially for cases where committing one forbidden act might prevent many more acts of the very same forbidden kind? For each reading, say whether it is defensible, whether it is false, and whether it actually supports the refusal to act in that kind of systemic-prevention case. I'm particularly interested in whether any version avoids the strange result that following the rule strictly could end up allowing more of the exact harm the rule was meant to prevent.
