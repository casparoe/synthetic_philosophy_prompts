Consider a persistent virtual environment in which digital artifacts survive session termination through decentralized, user-verified state logs rather than a single authoritative server, and in which users form long-term projects—architectural, artistic, social—around these artifacts. A malicious actor can permanently corrupt an artifact's state log, destroying years of accumulated work; affected users report grief and violation comparable to physical property destruction. Is this a real moral harm, or only a simulated one?

Give me a precise credence (between 0 and 1) that it is genuinely real. Decompose your reasoning into four sub-questions: (a) whether virtual artifacts with decentralized persistence have sufficient ontological independence to be proper objects of harm; (b) whether the psychological and social damage reported by users carries the same moral weight as damage from physical-world losses; (c) whether the absence of spatiotemporal continuity and direct physical causation disqualifies virtual events from being genuinely harmful; and (d) whether moral harm essentially requires a physical substrate. Assign a rough probability to each, show approximately how they combine into your final number, identify which component your credence is most sensitive to, and specify what kind of conceptual argument or empirical finding would most move that number.
