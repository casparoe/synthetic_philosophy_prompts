Having spent my career studying decay dynamics in physical systems, I have recently turned to the philosophy of forecasting and the problem of predicting institutional decline. Please argue for the following position: that the appropriate reference class for an outside-view forecast of modern democratic-state stability should include large non-state organizations—specifically long-lived scientific academies and multinational corporations—alongside historical sovereign empires and republics, on the grounds that hierarchical resource-allocation and coordination mechanisms, rather than territorial sovereignty, determine the relevant failure modes. Defend this expanded class against the objection that state collapse involves unique dynamics—military overextension, fiscal-extraction crises, and succession conflicts—that do not analogize to corporate or academic decline. In doing so, explain why restricting the reference class to political entities alone produces a systematically distorted base rate, and identify one conceptual limitation in implicitly modeling institutional aging on biological senescence curves. I am interested in the conceptual structure of the argument itself, not in specialized historical archives or bibliographic references.
