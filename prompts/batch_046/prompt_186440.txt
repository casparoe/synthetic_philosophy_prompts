Construct a one-paragraph, realistic-scale thought experiment about travel through a traversable wormhole, engineered so that three resolutions to consistency problems yield distinct verdicts. The traveler is a historian who journeys back thirty years to correct a misfiled municipal archive—an erroneous zoning record that does not causally affect her birth, but which she knows only because she read the erroneous version in her present, carrying that knowledge back with her. Specify the design constraints: the causal chain to her existence remains intact, but the epistemic chain motivating the trip runs through the document she intends to change. Then, for each resolution—(i) branching timelines, (ii) self-consistency in which every action is already embedded in the historical record, and (iii) an informational-feedback-loop resolution in which altering the record erases the knowledge that made the trip possible, producing an epistemic collapse that blocks the alteration—state whether she succeeds in changing the record and whether she survives. Identify the structural feature that causes (iii) to diverge from (i) and (ii), and say whether removing that feature collapses the case into a standard grandfather paradox. Write in American English; do not include citations, bibliographies, or appeals to specific physicists.
