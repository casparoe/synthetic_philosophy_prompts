i'm putting together a session for my philospohy reading group on ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood — specifically the tension between treating it as a moral ideal vs. an actual ontology where the self is constituted through recognition. i want to close with a poem, probably a villanelle, that doesn't just recycle the usual slogan about being through others but uses the form itself to show how recognition is iterative and never quite settled. so: two refrains. one should sound like the communal affirmation we all know. the other should be something like i am not yet, unless you speak me into being — capturing the more radical ontological claim. the villanelle's repetition should feel like the same recognition happening over and over, but the turn should reveal that the two refrains aren't harmonizing; they're pulling in different directions about whether relation completes the self or constantly re-creates it. speak it from inside the process, not from outside observing. accurate enough that someone in african philosophy wouldn't wince, but no bibliography, no citations, no as-so-and-so-said stuff. about 19 lines, classic villanelle with the A1/B1/A2 structure. also i don't care about perfect rhyme if the conceptual rhythm works, but try to keep the form recognizable.
