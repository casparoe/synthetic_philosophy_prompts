I am preparing an independent study on the Anthropocene, and I have encountered the stratigraphic proposal that the epoch's lower boundary should be fixed at 1610, corresponding to the Orbis spike in atmospheric carbon and transoceanic biotic exchange triggered by the Columbian Exchange. Given that the International Commission on Stratigraphy has formally incorporated this 1610 boundary into the official Geological Time Scale—thereby recognizing the Anthropocene as a formal subdivision of the Quaternary—should historians of climate change be compelled to treat early modern colonial expansion, rather than nineteenth-century industrialization, as the definitive origin point of human planetary dominance? More specifically, does the geological ratification of the 1610 boundary function as a disciplinary correction to historical narratives that center fossil-fuel capitalism?
