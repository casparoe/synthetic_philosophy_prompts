Please construct a rigorous argument for the position that Spinoza's defense of democracy in the Tractatus Politicus should be interpreted not as a normative endorsement of popular sovereignty or majoritarian decision-making, but as a structural implication of his metaphysics of power applied to collective bodies. Specifically, defend the claim that democratic government, on Spinoza's account, operates as a mechanism for the maximal distribution of rational affect, wherein the common power achieves its highest actualization not through representative delegation but through the continuous, direct coordination of citizens such that no subset can alienate the collective conatus. In doing so, address the apparent counterexample of Spinoza's own treatment of aristocratic councils in the Tractatus, explaining why such councils do not constitute representation in the modern procedural sense but rather represent temporary concentrations of already-actualized common reason that remain immediately accountable to the undivided multitude. Your argument should remain internal to Spinoza's conceptual architecture—drawing on his distinctions between reason and passion, between potentia and potestas, and between absolute and limited sovereignty—without appealing to external democratic theory.
