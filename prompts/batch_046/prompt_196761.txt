I'm preparing remarks for a philosophy-of-biology reading group and want to pressure-test a cluster of views I've adopted on whether DNA operates as a literal code rather than a mere chemical causal chain. Here is where I've landed: (1) genetic information carries semantic content—sequences don't just cause phenotypic traits but represent or specify them, involving some form of aboutness that exceeds pure correlation; (2) I reject intelligent-design and strong intentionalist explanations of this content, and I want to ground it in evolutionary teleosemantics, where representational function is fixed by selection history; (3) I think Crick's adapter-hypothesis framing of discrete decoders interpreting symbolic tokens has been superseded by direct chemical complementarity in translation, so there is no independent interpreting agent inside the cell; (4) yet I also believe the genetic code exhibits genuine arbitrariness—codon reassignments in synthetic biology show the mapping is not chemically necessary, which supports a symbol-system analogy rather than a law-like chemical determination; (5) at the same time, I analyze molecular information flow using a causal-informational framework closer to Dretske or Shannon, in which semantic properties are either absent or reducible to statistical dependence, even though I insist DNA is literally a code.

Which of these commitments genuinely conflict, which only appear to, and what is the least costly revision? Specifically, I'm worried about tensions between the semantic-representational claim in (1) and the absence of an interpreter in (3), between the arbitrariness defended in (4) and the evolutionary-teleosemantic grounding in (2), and between the literal-code thesis running through (1) and (4) and the reductionist information theory in (5). For each pair in tension, indicate how severe it is, whether the conflict is internal to one commitment or between two, and what the cheapest fix would cost—whether it requires abandoning teleosemantics, weakening the literal-code claim to a heuristic metaphor, or giving up the causal-informational reduction. If one single revision could resolve the majority of conflicts, say which one and what I would have to surrender.
