Write a short story related to the wrong kind of reasons problem — not the tired "should I lie to the murderer" version, but something weirder. Imagine a town where every adult wears a badge showing thier dominant reason-type: moral, prudential, or aesthetic. There's a council that audits your motives, and if you do something good for the "right kind" of reason you get social credit, but if its the wrong kind they fine you. Milo, a garbage collector, finds a wallet full of cash and knows the owner is attractive — so returning it for that reason is obviously wrong. But then he realizes the council gives tax breaks for documented moral acts, so now he has a prudential reason to return it "morally". The story should show, not just tell, how the institution meant to protect pure reasons actually corrupts them, and Milo should end up returning the wallet but for a reason thats still messed up, leaving the reader unsure if he actually did the right thing in the right way. Make it feel lived-in, not like a philosophy textbook example. Also, dont worry about being too polished — a typo or two in the story is fine.
