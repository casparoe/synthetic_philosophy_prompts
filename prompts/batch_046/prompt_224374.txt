Consider the cluster of open problems at the intersection of David Lewis’s account of convention — with its emphasis on common knowledge, coordination-game structure, and the arbitrariness of conventional rules — and evolutionary approaches to norm emergence in populations characterised by bounded rationality, noisy signalling, and heterogeneous preferences. Rather than requesting a survey, I am asking for a defended, dependency-aware research agenda: which unresolved questions in this intersection matter most for constructing formal models of norm emergence that are descriptively adequate for human societies and simultaneously applicable to the design of coordination protocols in multi-agent artificial systems?

Please identify and discuss three to five live questions. These might include, but need not be limited to, the following: whether full common knowledge can be replaced by approximate mutual belief without erasing the distinction between genuine convention and mere behavioural regularity; how evolutionary dynamics are altered when agents can revise not only their strategies but also the salience structures that define coordination points; whether the normative force of a convention derives solely from the stability of its underlying equilibrium or requires an independent evaluative layer; and whether purely local interaction rules can sustain global conventions in the absence of central enforcement.

For each question, assess its tractability using current formal tools — such as evolutionary game theory, epistemic logic, or agent-based modelling — and map its downstream dependencies: which other issues become tractable, moot, or substantially transformed if this question is resolved?

Then provide an explicit ordering, defending which question should receive priority effort in a research programme of roughly three to five years. Your defence should invoke clear criteria — for instance, importance for both descriptive social science and artificial coordination design, tractability with existing methods, and the number of downstream questions that depend on its resolution — and should acknowledge the trade-offs between them. If your analysis suggests that any two questions are actually inseparable and ought to be treated as a single problem, flag this explicitly.

Please write your response in British English. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; paraphrase any theoretical ideas without attributing them to specific pages or texts.
