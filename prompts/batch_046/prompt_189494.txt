I've been thinking about suffering vs happiness since my grandma got sick, and I'm trying to figure out if they even belong on the same scale. Here are my gut calls:

A life with 70 good years then one year of unbearable pain: that life was bad overall, the pain swallows everything. But 70 okay years and one boring year? The okay-ness wins easily.

Billions of people living well, one person in constant agony: I don't think the agony is "cancelled" by the joy, but I also don't think it makes the whole world bad. I'd still rescue that one person first.

Prevent one severe torture session vs create 500 lives of moderate joy: prevent the torture, always.

But I keep flipping on this: I could take a job I hate that donates 90% of my income to stop extreme suffering. I'd be stressed and lonely every day, but thousands avoid real agony. Part of me says my own moderate misery is on a seperate scale from theirs, so I shouldn't do it. Another part says suffering is suffering, period.

Is there one principle that gets cases 1-3 without being totally ad hoc? Does it force me to drop case 4, or my feeling that the billion-happy-one-agonized world is still "mostly okay"? And what does it say about the job -- should I take it?
