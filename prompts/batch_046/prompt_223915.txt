Draft the joint statement that a defender of the Williams interest thesis and a defender of the Appiah honor thesis on moral revolutions could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Position A holds that norms like slavery and dueling collapse mainly through shifts in material interest and economic cost, with moral argument serving as post-hoc rationalization; Position B holds that they collapse through transformations in honor and status, with economic shifts mattering only insofar as they alter honor structures, and with moral argument articulating rather than causing the change.

Produce a formal document with three sections. First, a numbered list of agreed claims—about the historical patterns of British abolition and the decline of dueling, about the failure of purely rationalist explanations to account for timing, and about what evidence could in principle distinguish interest-driven from honor-driven collapse. Second, a disagreements table in which each divergence is phrased twice: once in wording Position A accepts for its own view and once in wording Position B accepts for its own view. The core disagreements should cover whether economic costs can directly drive abandonment without an intermediate honor-status shift, and whether honor dynamics can operate independently of material interests. Third, the cruxes: identify two sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side. For each, specify a concrete comparative case both agree is relevant—suggest the persistence of profitable slave economies in specific regions versus the rapid decline of dueling where economic costs were minimal—and record each side’s advance prediction about what the case shows. Finally, inject one novel test scenario not standard in the literature—the sudden abandonment of a socially costly but economically cheap apprenticeship-punishment ritual in nineteenth-century manufacturing—stating precisely what each camp predicts about whether economic or honor mechanisms dominated there. Avoid bibliographies, citations, or appeals to niche theoretical apparatus; keep every formulation exact enough that neither side can dismiss the residual disagreement as merely verbal.
