I have been facilitating a year-long adversarial collaboration between two philosophical camps on meritocracy and desert, and they have asked me to draft the framework for the joint statement they should produce. One side—call them the Agency-Desert theorists—holds that meritocratic rewards are justified only when they track genuinely agential properties for which individuals bear direct responsibility, chiefly voluntary effort and deliberative choice; they maintain that compound structural advantages dissolve desert claims because they sever the link between outcome and agency, and they reject any institutional metric that pretends to isolate pure effort from its social conditions. The other side—call them the Institutional-Alignment theorists—holds that desert is not a pre-institutional moral property but a relational feature of well-designed institutions; they defend the use of a counterfactual effort baseline, an institutional metric that estimates what contribution an agent would have made absent specified structural advantages, and they argue that meritocracy should be evaluated by incentive alignment rather than by metaphysical purity of agency. Please draft the document both camps could sign.

The statement should begin with a numbered section of agreed claims: precise statements about how meritocratic institutions actually function, about the interaction between individual effort and structural context, about the logical and practical status of counterfactual effort baselines, and about what kinds of institutional experiments or empirical observations both sides accept as bearing on their dispute. This should be followed by a disagreements table that states each camp's position in wording its own members would accept, paired with the opposing camp's neutral formulation of the same divergence, so that the residual disagreement is exact and free of rhetorical distortion. Finally, identify the cruxes—specifically, whether desert requires pre-institutional agential properties, whether counterfactual effort baselines are conceptually coherent and empirically tractable, and whether meritocracy can be justified instrumentally without appealing to moral desert. For each crux, describe a concrete institutional case or piece of evidence both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance what each camp predicts about that case. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the document should be self-contained and accessible to someone without web access.
