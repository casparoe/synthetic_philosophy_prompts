Grade the following exam answer. The prompt given to the student was: "Distinguish Molinist middle knowledge (scientia media) from natural and free knowledge; explain how counterfactuals of creaturely freedom are meant to reconcile divine providence with libertarian freedom; and present the grounding objection, together with one Molinist reply and a brief evaluation of that reply."

Student answer:

Middle knowledge, or scientia media, occupies the logical space between natural knowledge and free knowledge. Natural knowledge includes all necessary truths—God’s own nature, mathematics, and logical relations. Free knowledge comprises the contingent truths of the actual world that depend on God’s creative will. Middle knowledge comprises counterfactuals of creaturely freedom: for every possible free agent and every possible circumstance, God knows what that agent would freely do.

The priority here is logical, not temporal, since God is eternal. Nevertheless, in the order of explanation God first surveys the full range of counterfactuals, then selects which world to actualize by placing creatures in the circumstances that yield the free choices he desires. Because these counterfactuals are true independently of God’s will, he does not cause the choices; he merely arranges the conditions under which agents exercise libertarian freedom. Providence is therefore secured without determinism.

The grounding objection asks how God could have epistemic access to these counterfactuals prior to any creative decree. If the creatures do not yet exist and nothing has been willed, there appears to be no source from which God could learn what they would freely choose. A Molinist may reply that the counterfactuals are grounded in the essential properties of the possible creatures: just as triangularity flows from the essence of a triangle, so a particular agent’s free response in a given situation flows from that agent’s individual essence. God reads these essences and thereby knows the counterfactuals.

This reply is not fully adequate. If every free response in every circumstance flows deterministically from an essence, then for each situation there is only one action the agent can perform, which seems to collapse libertarian freedom into a kind of essentialist determinism. Hence the grounding objection retains its force.
