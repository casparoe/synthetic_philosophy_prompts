I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind and cognitive science, and I'm trying to determine what I actually think about consciousness ascription under Integrated Information Theory. Rather than deriving verdicts downward from the formal postulates, I'm working upward from my gut judgments on concrete systems, and I want to see whether a single principle can unify them or whether I have to overturn one.

Here are my verdicts. First, a healthy awake human with normal cortical recurrence is clearly conscious, with a high degree of integrated information. Second, a standard deep feedforward architecture—say, a large transformer processing visual input—is not conscious at all, no matter how sophisticated its representations, because without recurrent causation there is nothing for phi to measure. Third, a patient with a fully transected corpus callosum performing a divided-attention task strikes me as still being one conscious subject, not two; the experience is degraded and disconnected, but I don't think the anatomical split creates a second stream of consciousness. Fourth, an isolated cerebral organoid grown with dense recurrent synaptic architecture but completely lacking sensory input or motor output still possesses minimal consciousness; the substrate supports integration regardless of environmental embedding. Fifth, consider a hypothetical "double-nested" network: a macro-complex with phi ≈ 100 that contains, embedded within it, a smaller sub-complex with phi ≈ 150 that shares about seventy percent of its nodes. Despite the embedded complex having strictly higher phi, I find myself attributing consciousness to the macro-complex and denying it to the embedded one, as if the larger boundary somehow subsumes the smaller. Sixth, a human in deep NREM sleep with no dream recall is not in a null state; consciousness is severely attenuated but not absent, leaving a dim, undifferentiated background presence.

Is there a precisely statable principle—drawing on integration, information, exclusion, and perhaps boundary conditions or causal power—that delivers all six verdicts? If not, which one is the outlier that a disciplined IIT account should force me to drop, and which clause of the principle motivates that sacrifice? Please also apply whatever principle you settle on to a case I haven't listed: an artificial "epochal mesh," a toroidal spiking network with dense local recurrence but global inhibition that silences all cross-region feedback every 200 ms, leaving discrete 50-ms windows of high local integration separated by reset periods. Would your principle assign it consciousness, and if so, is it one flickering subject or a rapid succession of transient ones?

State the principle (or competing candidates) clearly, map each clause to the case it handles, and be explicit about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate.
