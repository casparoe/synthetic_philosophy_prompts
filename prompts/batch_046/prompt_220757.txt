I'm formalizing an epistemology of distant historical knowledge for a seminar defense, and I want an audit of whether these commitments are jointly tenable before I present them. Here is my position: (1) I hold that legitimate knowledge of events centuries removed is possible through source criticism, but only via what I call echo-chain validation—successive interpretive layers must preserve structural invariants across transmission rather than literal propositional content; (2) I judge Thucydides's exclusion of the mythical and his single authoritative narrative to be methodologically purer than Herodotus's polyphonic recording of variant traditions, because I treat ambiguity as an epistemic contaminant rather than a resource; (3) I maintain that archaeological or material evidence can correct textual testimony about physical occurrences, dates, or demographic scale, but can never override claims about intentional states, meanings, or causal narratives—so carbon-dated contradiction of a chronicler's timeline is admissible, but material reinterpretation of motive is not; (4) I require that historical explanation satisfy an interventionist criterion: to genuinely explain an outcome such as the collapse of a regime, one must specify a counterfactual intervention on a discrete factor that would have disrupted the result; (5) I accept that all access to the distant past is mediated by present conceptual frameworks, yet I deny that these frameworks construct the past—they only select which echoes in the chain become audible. Which pairs are in genuine tension? Specifically, does (2)'s preference for Thucydidean purification conflict with (1)'s reliance on layered, variant-tolerant transmission? Does (4)'s demand for causal specificity sit badly with (3)'s sharp restriction on what counts as correctable? Is (5) compatible with (3)'s insulation of intentional states from material correction? Rank the tensions by severity, identify any that dissolve on closer inspection, and specify the least costly single revision that would restore coherence.
