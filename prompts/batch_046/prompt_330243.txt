I have come to accept a modified position that I call the attenuated Cartesian theater. I grant Dennett's critique of a single central observer and accept that perceptual and cognitive content is generated and revised through parallel, distributed drafts; yet I maintain that there must still be a singular, functionally localized bottleneck—what I think of as a thin theater—where competing drafts are subjected to one unified evaluative frame before anything becomes available for verbal report or deliberate action. This theater does not generate consciousness ex nihilo, as in the traditional Cartesian picture, but acts as a necessary convergence point for reportability, without which the multiplicity of drafts would never yield the coherence observed in first-person narration. Tell me what would genuinely count against this position, beyond the standard objection that such localization is neurobiologically implausible. Specifically, which empirical findings about distributed neural processing, which phenomena in split-brain or severe dissociative cases, and which formal results concerning parallel computation versus serial bottlenecks does it risk or depend upon? I am also interested in whether developments in other areas—such as the architecture of working memory, the neuroscience of attention, or models of narrative self-construction—provide decisive support or damaging counterevidence. Please distinguish threats that would be fatal from those that would be merely awkward, indicate which defeaters a committed defender of the attenuated theater would concede as genuinely damaging versus those they could absorb by narrowing the bottleneck's scope or function, and rank them accordingly. Finally, assess whether this position is well insulated in ways that are intellectually virtuous or in ways that risk rendering it unfalsifiable, and identify the single further observation or experimental result that would best discriminate between the thin-theater view and a fully distributed multiple-drafts model with no convergence point at all.
