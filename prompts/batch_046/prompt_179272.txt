hey, i'm a novelist and tomorrow i'm giving the opening remarks at this small workshop — 'Obligation and Its Others' — half academic philosphers, half people who've actually orginized sit-ins, plus grad students who just came for the bagels. i need about four minutes, spoken not read. i want to avoid the usual rawls-thoreau greatest hits because half the room lives this stuff and the other half has heard it all in seminars. instead i want the talk to hang on one idea i'm calling the 'double signature': the state enrolls you as a citizen without asking, then treats thier silence as consent, then treats your protest as breaking that same consent. it should shape everything i say — i can't claim we freely signed up, but i also can't pretend there's no bond at all. the activists should feel recognized, the philosphers should see the tacit-consent problem being pushed past its usual resolution, and nobody should be able to tell if i'm making a philosophical point or telling a story about a contract you never held in your hand. please write it casual, with a typo or two, no verbatim quotes from anyone — i always misremember lines and get corrected — and don't resolve the tension, just sit in it. the room needs to feel the weight of both signatures at once.
