I've been arguing with friends about tranquilism -- the idea that only the absence of craving and suffering actually matters, and positive goods like knowledge or achievment are just decorative. The debate's been going in circles for months. Tranquilists say if craving is at zero, nothing else can improve the state. Opponents say that's obviously wrong because then a blissfully ignorant person and a fulfilled scientist are on par, and they cite flourishing and human potental. But nobody moves. Is this really about values, or is it a hidden disagreement about whether people can even maintain zero craving long-term? Or maybe it's verbal -- 'value' meaning one thing to them, another to us? There's also this nagging thought: the tranquilists seem to treat the self like a point in time, not a story. If they think there's no narrative gravity holding goods together across a life, then of course only present absence matters. Could that hidden view about the self be the real engine? What's the actual structure of this stall, and what -- a conceptual distinction, an empirical claim about sustained tranqulity, or just accepting it's bedrock -- would count as progress?
