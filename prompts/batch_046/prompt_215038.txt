Please argue for the following reconstruction of Avicenna's flying-man argument: rather than proving that the soul is an immaterial substance independent of the body, the thought experiment demonstrates that self-awareness requires the absence of contradictory proprioceptive signals, not the absence of embodiment altogether. On this reading, the flying man knows himself precisely because he lacks conflicting bodily information, which implies that a genuinely disembodied system—one that never had any bodily tracking to suppress—would not meet the conditions for the kind of self-reference Avicenna describes. Take a committed position on whether this makes Avicenna an unwitting precursor to minimal embodied cognition, and defend it against the standard objection that he explicitly intended to establish substance dualism. Do not merely survey interpretations; choose this reconstruction and argue for it, noting the weakest point in your own position—specifically, that Avicenna frames the case as total bodily ignorance rather than conflict-free absence—and explain why that weakness does not defeat the reading.
