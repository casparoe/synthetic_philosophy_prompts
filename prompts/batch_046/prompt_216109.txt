As a journalist covering science, governance, and emerging technologies, I am drafting a feature on the resurgence of prediction-market governance and keep encountering the futarchic slogan "vote on values, bet on beliefs." At a recent panel on decentralized public decision-making, an advocate used it to dismiss concerns about whether concentrated speculative capital could distort policy outcomes—implying that anyone unwilling to wager against a proposed mechanism must lack genuine conviction. I need to understand exactly what work that sentence can do before I report it.

Can you lay out, as a menu rather than a single interpretation, the distinct precise theses that "vote on values, bet on beliefs" could be advancing? For each reading, indicate whether it is largely definitional and trivial, whether it makes a substantive claim about epistemic aggregation, whether it asserts something about preference revelation under risk, or whether it implicitly equates belief with willingness to stake capital. In particular, distinguish the reading that treats betting as revealing pre-existing belief, the reading that treats it as constructing belief through financial commitment, and the reading that treats it as filtering for wealth-weighted opinion. Then say, for each, whether it is defensible, false, or conceptually confused, and—crucially—whether it actually supports using the slogan to brush aside worries about capital-driven manipulation in governance markets. Finally, identify which reading the panelist was likely deploying, and whether that deployment depends on an unstated premise about capital and conviction that should be flagged for readers.
