I've constructed an argument about food ethics and wild-animal suffering that seems to prove far more than I can accept, and I need help locating the fault and pricing the fix. The chain runs as follows.

(1) The correct moral criterion for comparing diets is the net wild-animal suffering sustained or suppressed by the ecosystem each diet requires.
(2) Beef production depends on pastureland, which maintains high carrying capacity for wild herbivores and predators that suffer chronically from predation, starvation, and disease; chicken and direct crop consumption depend on row-crop agriculture, which suppresses wild populations—killing some during harvest and pest control, but sustaining far fewer suffering lives per hectare-year.
(3) Beef requires substantially more land per unit of protein than chicken, and far more than direct plant consumption.
(4) Therefore, per calorie of nutrition, beef sustains far more wild-animal suffering than chicken or plant-based alternatives.
(5) So we are morally required to avoid beef and prefer chicken or plants.
(6) By the same reasoning, since forests and wetlands have even higher wild-animal carrying capacity and suffering densities than pasture, we ought to replace all wild ecosystems with cropland to suppress suffering populations.
(7) Hence we should maximize agricultural conversion and consume only crops—an implication that is not merely impractical but morally perverse.

The reasoning looks valid, yet (6)–(7) are clearly wrong. Is the error a hidden scope shift between marginal dietary choice and global land-use transformation? An equivocation between suffering sustained per hectare and suffering actually caused by my consumption? Or is the argument sound, and the real cost is abandoning (1) or (2)? I am inclined to keep (1), but I want to know precisely what giving up (3) or (4) costs, and whether there is a more surgical rejection.

Please diagnose the specific step that fails, explain why it appeared plausible, and state the price of rejecting it versus the alternatives. Keep your answer to no more than 350 words, and end with a single sentence identifying the one premise you would drop if forced.
