okay so i've been chewing on wittgenstein's idea that games dont share one essential feature, just family resemblences—like threads in a rope with no single thread running all the way through. here's an argument i cant shake. (1) if a concept is held together only by family resemblance, then no single trait is necessary for membership. (2) but without any necessary trait, there's no principled boundary—just a sliding scale of "close enough" in different directions. (3) wittgenstein applies this to language-games, so language itself has no shared essence. (4) therefore there's no hard line between a language-game and something that just mimics language, like a parrot trained to respond to cues. (5) yet we clearly do distinguish real language from parroting, and it doesn't feel like we're just making a convention. (6) so applying family resemblance consistently either erases a real distinction or forces us to say the distinction is arbitrary. (7) that seems to prove too much—like the view undermines itself when turned on language. where exactly does it go wrong? i'd like to keep (1) and (5), but (2) feels sneaky. what's the cost of giving up (2) versus giving up (3)? and is there a specific name for this kind of self-undermining, where a cluster concept collapses when applied to the cluster that contains it?
