Write a villanelle of nineteen lines, using the traditional ABA rhyme scheme with two refrains (A1 and A2) that rotate through the five tercets and conclude together in the final quatrain. Take as your subject the problem of material constitution—specifically the statue formed from clay—so that the poem's formal repetition enacts the coincidence of two entities occupying exactly the same space with exactly the same parts. Let the first refrain be "The statue stands where I am standing now," and let the second be "But melt me and I outlast every shape." Through the B-lines, develop the specific metaphysical considerations: that the statue and the clay share all material parts and location yet differ in modal properties (the statue is destroyed by squashing, the clay is not), that sortal concepts determine persistence conditions, and that two wholly coincident objects violate a plausible principle of ontological economy. Allow the refrains to accumulate these distinctions rather than resolving them, so that by the closing couplet—where both refrains appear side by side—the reader feels the tension between constitution and identity without either being dismissed. The poem should be technically accurate enough that a metaphysician recognises the argument for non-identity and the challenge to mereological essentialism, yet it should work as verse for a reader unfamiliar with the debate. Write in polished British English, with no citations, bibliographic references, or appeals to named authorities.
