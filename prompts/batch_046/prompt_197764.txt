Read William Golding’s Lord of the Flies through the lens of moral progress understood as the expansion and potential contraction of the circle of moral concern—specifically, the view that ethical development involves extending recognition, protection, and obligation to beings previously excluded as outsiders. I want a philosophically rigorous reading that does not reduce the novel to an illustration: trace how the boys’ initial assembly (the conch, the rules for speaking, the division of labor, the signal fire) attempts to institutionalize a broad moral circle, and then examine how Jack’s faction progressively contracts that circle to an in-group defined by physical strength, shared ritual, and predatory identity. Treat Piggy and Simon as critical test cases: does their exclusion and killing represent not merely a failure of empathy but a structural reversal of institutional safeguards, and does Golding thereby dramatize a fragility—perhaps an institutional dependency—that standard expanding-circle narratives, which often assume monotonic or irreversible growth, tend to overlook? I am particularly interested in whether the conch operates not just as a democratic symbol but as a procedural scaffold whose presence or absence determines whether the circle can be maintained; if so, does the novel suggest that moral progress depends less on individual sentiment expanding outward than on fragile, authority-dependent mechanisms that can collapse suddenly? Identify the scenes—such as the killing of Simon, the marginalization of Piggy, and the final hunt for Ralph—where this framework illuminates the dynamics of inclusion and exclusion, and be explicit about where the novel resists the framework: for example, does Golding imply that contraction can occur more rapidly and more totally than expansion, that the circle can be sustained only by an external authority figure (the naval officer) rather than by internal conviction, and that the boys’ regression reveals something about the reversibility of moral progress that philosophical argument alone rarely captures?
