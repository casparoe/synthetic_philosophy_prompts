I've come around to a particular take on Molyneux's problem — not the usual innate-vs-empirical framing, but the idea that the blind subject fails immediatly after sight-restoration not because they're missing some built-in geometric concept, but because they haven't yet constructed the cross-modal correlation map between manual exploration dynamics and retinal layout. Sighted babies spend months reaching and grasping to build this bridge; the restored patient hasn't. So the problem is about missing sensorimotor calibration, not missing knowledge. I want to know what'd actually count against this. Which findings or arguments would be fatal — say, immediate shape-recognition in restored patients with no motor exploration — versus merely awkward, like partial recognition after brief training? And what would defenders of my view concede as a real problem rather than dismiss? I'm also wondering if this risks being unfalsifiable: if recognition always takes some time, I can always say 'the bridge wasn't built yet.' Is that insulation a bug? Give me an honest exposure map, not a history lesson.
