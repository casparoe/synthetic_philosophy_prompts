You are a senior philosopher of science with expertise in epistemology, philosophy of physics, and scientific methodology. Your task is to conduct a disciplined two-directional case analysis: apply the Quine-Duhem thesis of underdetermination to a real scientific episode, and then use that episode to test and refine the thesis itself.

Take the Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration’s 2019 release of the first direct image of the supermassive black hole in M87. Treat the episode not as a mere illustration but as an empirical situation in which underdetermination either operates, is suppressed, or is transformed. Begin with brief internal brainstorming: list (1) the core theoretical prediction being tested (the Kerr-metric shadow morphology), (2) the auxiliary hypotheses embedded in the observation (interferometric calibration, closure-phase computation, imaging-algorithm priors, and astrophysical accretion-flow models), (3) the actual observational inputs (the April 2017 visibility amplitudes and closure phases), and (4) the institutional responses (multiple independent reconstruction pipelines, consistency checks between geometric and GRMHD simulations, and the decision to present a single ring image).

Then proceed in four steps.

First, map the underdetermination structure precisely. Given only the raw interferometric data, what rival theoretical packages could have been assembled? Identify exactly where a rival theorist could have preserved an alternative spacetime metric—say, a gravastar with a photon-sphere analog that reproduces the same ring diameter—by adjusting auxiliaries in the accretion-flow model or in the imaging priors rather than in the gravitational theory. Specify which adjustments are empirically constrained and which remain free.

Second, analyze the collaboration’s actual practice as an institutional strategy for managing underdetermination. They ran independent pipelines with different regularization schemes and tested both geometric crescents and magnetohydrodynamic simulations. Does this multiplicity of methods break the symmetry between rival theories, or does it merely relocate the underdetermination into the choice of pipeline priors and model-selection criteria? Be explicit about the level at which the Quine-Duhem thesis applies here.

Third, examine a niche but serious rival framing: suppose a modified-gravity advocate claims that the observed ring is not a Kerr shadow but rather the photon-sphere signature of an exotic compact object, and that the same visibility dataset can be reproduced by altering only the accretion-flow auxiliary. Without adjudicating the physics, use this hypothetical to probe the limits of the Quine-Duhem analysis. Where does the thesis direct us to look for saving adjustments, and does the EHT episode show that those adjustments are observationally bounded or still radically underdetermined?

Fourth, state what the episode reveals about the thesis itself. Is the convergence on a single black-hole image a practical defeat of underdetermination, a confirmation that large collaborations can suppress it through robustness checks, or an anomaly that exposes the thesis as inapplicable to instrumentally dense, socially distributed science? Identify precisely where the theory explains the collaboration’s behavior, where it misdescribes it, and where it is silent.

Write in a formal, polished philosophical style. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for further sources. Do not invent specific historical quotes; if you reference published statements, paraphrase them without quotation marks. Ensure your answer distinguishes clearly between what is common knowledge about the 2019 EHT release and what is speculative or hypothetical framing introduced for the analysis.
