At my study group last night Mira and Jonas got into it over whether buddhist ethics is consequentialist or not. Mira said its basically karma-as-causation: good actions produce good results, bad ones bad results, so morality tracks outcomes. Jonas said thats wrong because Abhidharma defines karma by cetena (intention/volition), not result. He used the old example: stepping on an ant by accident with pure mind vs swatting one with anger. Same dead ant, totally different moral weight, so outcomes don't determine the ethics.

Mira replied that intentions are just proxies for expected outcomes, and that buddhism still cares about suffering caused. Jonas then mentioned this Theravada distinction he had read about -- garuka vs lahuka karma -- where weight is set by mental quality and object, not result size. So a petty lie with malice is heavier karma than accidentally causing huge harm with a distracted mind. He said thats a direct counterexample.

Mira switched gears and said the boddhisattva delaying nirvana for all beings is clearly welfare-maximizing, pure consequentialism. Jonas said its driven by karuna, a virtue, not a calculus. He also brought up merit transfer (parinamana): you dedicate karma to others, which breaks the direct act-to-outcome link that consequentialism needs. Mira said thats just distributing outcomes, still system-level consequentialism, and actually buddhism is closer to negative consequentialism since its obsessed with ending dukkha not promoting happiness. She said alot of modern interpreters miss that.

They asked me to referee and I couldn't. Please go through each move -- rate them from clearly wrong to clearly right, justify briefly, say who is more right overall, and point out what both miss. Don't end with a non-committal shrug; take a side.
