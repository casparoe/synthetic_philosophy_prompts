I am drafting a novel set in a constitutional monarchy that persists into the twenty-third century not through visible coercion but through what my protagonist terms architectural anticipation—a social order in which citizens never encounter direct prohibitions because the built environment, predictive credit algorithms, and ceremonial etiquette have been calibrated so precisely that disobedience becomes literally unthinkable before it becomes physically impossible. For the philosophical architecture of the narrative I need to understand whether this condition is more accurately diagnosed as a failure of republican liberty, in the tradition that defines domination as the presence of arbitrary power capable of interference regardless of whether that power is actually exercised, or as a radical absence of negative liberty, where freedom is construed not merely as the non-actualization of interference but as the absence of any credible capacity for arbitrary interference altogether.

Compare these two analyses with particular attention to the temporal structure of anticipation itself: does the fact that the subject has already internalized the ruler's will before any explicit command is issued alter whether we are dealing with domination, or does it instead describe a form of self-imposed constraint that neither framework is designed to capture? I want the comparison to be rigorous and not merely definitional—show me where the two theories genuinely diverge in their verdict on this case, and where they might unexpectedly converge. But please be plain with me: if my framing conflates predictive governance with domination in a way that misrepresents either theory, or if the notion of architectural anticipation introduces a confusion that makes the comparison impossible to adjudicate without empirical sociology, then say so directly rather than forcing a neat answer. I am not asking for bibliographies, citation lists, or references to obscure secondary texts; I need a conceptual analysis precise enough to inform the design of a fictional bureaucracy without relying on anything a reader could not reconstruct from the concepts themselves.
