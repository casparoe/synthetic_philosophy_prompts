J said pragmatic encroachment and epistemic contextualism are basically the same thing: in both cases, saying 'I know' gets harder when more is at stake. R said thats wrong -- contextualism says the word 'knows' shifts meaning depending on conversational context, while pragmatic encroachment says the actual mental state of knowing is affected by practical stakes, not just our talk.

Then J brought up the pilot with the fuel gauge. Before takeoff she glances and it reads full. If she's just curious, she knows there's fuel. If she's about to fly 200 people, she checks the tank manually. R said see, the stakes changed whether she actually knows -- thats pragmatic encroachment. J said no, its contextualism: in the pilot conversation, 'knows' just has stricter standards, but her mental state is the same.

Once evidence crosses a baseline you're safe, but you can't fall below it just because stakes drop. J said thats just ad hoc special pleading to save the view -- if stakes really affect knowledge, they should affect it both directions.
