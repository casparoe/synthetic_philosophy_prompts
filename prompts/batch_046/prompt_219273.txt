The Humean regularity theory, going back to Mill and then developed by Ramsey and Lewis, says that laws aren't necessary connections out in the world but just the best summary of actual regularities. Lewis's Best System Account picks the system that balances simplicity and strength, and laws are the regularities that show up in every system tied for best. This handles accidental generalisations — like all gold spheres being under a mile wide — because they don't earn their keep in the system; they're just isolated patterns in the Humean mosaic. There's no metaphysical glue.

Dispositional essentialism, as Bird and Ellis present it, reverses this. Properties like charge have dispositional essences: it's part of what charge is that it repels like charges. So laws aren't descriptions imposed on nature; they're direct expressions of these essences. That also explains why accidental regularities aren't laws — being gold doesn't essentially determine size, so the gold-sphere pattern is contingent.

But there's a worry about modal force. Essentialists say properties govern their manifestations, which sounds explanatory. Yet when we ask why charge necessarily repels, the answer is just 'that's its essence'. This seems to relocate rather than solve the mystery. In fact, Armstrong's DTA theory uses a relation of necessitation between universals to do this work, and Bird's view is essentially the same thing dressed up — the universal charge stands in N to repulsion. So essentialism doesn't really avoid primitive modality; it just hides it in the property itself.

On the Humean side, there's a circularity problem. Humeans use counterfactuals to analyse laws, but also need laws to ground counterfactuals. This loop makes it hard for them to explain why regularities hold with modal force. Essentialism breaks the loop by grounding modality in property essences. Even with quidditism — the idea that properties could swap roles across worlds — Bird can reply that essences are tied to the property identity, so charge can't be charge without its power. So essentialism still comes out ahead, though it pays the price of mysterious governing.
