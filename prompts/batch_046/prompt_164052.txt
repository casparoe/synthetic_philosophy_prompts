Here is an argument I keep returning to that seems to collapse Popper's philosophy of science from the inside. (1) Popper originally argued that Darwinism is not genuinely scientific because its core claim—survival of the fittest—is tautological: whatever survives is defined as fit, so no observation could refute it. (2) He later recanted, allowing that Darwinism makes risky predictions, for instance that evolutionary change proceeds through small gradual steps and that transitional fossils should appear in the record. (3) But if a tautological core can be rescued by attaching auxiliary hypotheses that generate risk, then any pseudoscientific framework can do the same; astrology could be insulated by adding testable predictions about planetary positions and personality traits. (4) The problem deepens with Popper's own evolutionary epistemology, which describes scientific knowledge growing through conjecture and refutation—a selectionist mechanism structurally identical to the Darwinism he once excluded. (5) So either Popper's rescue of Darwinism dissolves his falsifiability demarcation entirely, admitting both astrology and his own meta-theory, or he must reject Darwinism and thereby undermine evolutionary epistemology. Where does the argument slip? Is the tautology charge itself wrong, or is there a principled way to distinguish legitimate auxiliary predictions from ad hoc ones? I'd rather keep both Popper's recantation and his evolutionary epistemology intact—what does preserving them cost?
