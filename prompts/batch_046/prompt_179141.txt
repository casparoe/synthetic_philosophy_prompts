Patients retain precise nociceptive discrimination—localizing stimuli, rating intensity, identifying quality—yet report no unpleasantness whatsoever. From this clinical picture, philosophers of mind infer that pain is not intrinsically bad; rather, its badness consists in an independent emotional evaluation appended by limbic circuitry, functionally analogous to a cognitive belief about danger rather than to the sensory state itself. Consequently, the evolutionary function of pain is purely informational—signaling actual or potential tissue damage—while its aversive quality is an evolutionary byproduct lacking any independent adaptive value. Critics respond that asymbolic patients still exhibit reflex withdrawal and occasionally request analgesics, which demonstrates an implicit evaluation of harm and therefore undermines the claim of full dissociation. If the critics are correct, then representationalist theories of pain—which maintain that pain represents bodily damage—are falsified, because representational content alone cannot explain why normal subjects suffer."
