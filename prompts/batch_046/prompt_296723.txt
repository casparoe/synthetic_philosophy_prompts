Write a final-exam essay question for an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar on Daniel Dennett’s three stances—physical, design, and intentional—and on his criterion for when a pattern counts as real rather than merely projected. The question must not be answerable by summarizing the distinctions; instead, present a concrete case: an opaque neural weather-prediction system that achieves high accuracy and supports rich counterfactuals (for example, if barometric pressure had fallen by a specific amount, the system would have predicted a hurricane), yet its internal weights are uninterpretable, it was not designed with explicit meteorological rules, and it has no evolutionary history. Ask students to apply each of the three stances to this system, to evaluate whether predictive success and counterfactual robustness alone justify adopting the intentional stance toward it, and to address whether the intentional stance requires assumptions of rationality or evolutionary origin that the system lacks. Finally, ask what the case reveals about the limits of stance-relative ontology—specifically, whether there is a principled reason to deny intentionality here, or whether the case shows that Dennett’s framework collapses the boundary between design-level and intentional-level explanation.

Provide the essay prompt itself (requesting about 800 words), a model answer of roughly that length, and a marking scheme. The marking scheme should allocate points across at least four required moves: precisely distinguishing the three stances and Dennett’s predictive-utility criterion for real patterns; correctly applying each stance to the system; assessing the objection that intentional stance adoption requires rational-agency or evolutionary-history conditions that predictive success cannot supply; and drawing a reasoned conclusion about whether the design/intentional boundary holds or collapses. For each move, include the point value and a one-line note on the typical mistake that costs marks. Specify that an A-level answer must go beyond describing Dennett and must identify exactly where pragmatic stance adoption ends and where ontology becomes stance-dependent.

Write the entire deliverable in American English. Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific editions or page numbers.
