Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a defender of ascription universality and a defender of localized epistemic dominance. The universalist maintains that any coherent ascription of an epistemic property—such as cognitive authority, justification, or knowledge—entails an implicit commitment to universal applicability across all relevantly similar contexts, and that assessing claims of epistemic dominance requires a single domain-independent standard. The localist maintains that epistemic properties are necessarily indexed to specific practices, evidential frameworks, or social positions, that universal extension is a conceptual error, and that epistemic dominance is a structurally local relation that cannot be evaluated by an external universal metric.

The deliverable should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both parties can sign, addressing the nature of epistemic ascription, the reality and structure of dominance relations, and the current inadequacy of purely formal tools to resolve the dispute; (2) a disagreements table stating each camp's precise position in wording the opposing camp would accept as an accurate characterization of its own view; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions such that resolving them in either direction would move at least one party; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case, structured thought experiment, or empirical scenario that both sides agree bears on the question, accompanied by each side's explicit prediction of what that case demonstrates.

Keep formulations exact and non-vacuous: separate terminological disputes from substantive ones, and avoid framing either view as obviously irrational. Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific authors or texts. The prompt must be answerable without web access.
