My specific view is that every apparently emergent phenomenon, from quantum phase transitions to conscious experience, is in principle fully determined by lower-level dynamics, and that treating these patterns as ontologically novel is merely a pragmatic response to our computational limits. The recent success of deep-learning surrogates in predicting turbulent flow and protein folding without invoking any new physical laws strikes me as strong confirmation that higher-level properties are lossy compressions of micro-states rather than autonomous causal powers; so I take downward causation to be strictly epistemic, meaning macro-variables help us compute but do not literally push micro-particles around.

That said, I want an honest assessment of where this stance is actually exposed. What specific observations, formal results, or conceptual distinctions should make me concede that some form of ontological emergence is real rather than heuristic? Would a demonstrated physical case in which macro-properties alter micro-dynamics in ways that cannot be reconstructed from boundary conditions be decisive? Would a rigorous proof of computational irreducibility for certain macro-patterns—showing that no algorithm, however compressed, can generate them from base rules—count as a genuine defeater? Or has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that insulation a problem for a claim about how scientific explanation should work? Please lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence—whether my reductionist position is genuinely vulnerable or largely insulated—then provide the reasoning, distinguishing decisive counter-evidence from merely awkward findings, with no preamble and without restating the question. I am not asking for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; I just want to know what should rationally change my mind.
