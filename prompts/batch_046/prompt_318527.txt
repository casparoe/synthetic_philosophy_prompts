I am preparing notes for a philosophy of mind seminar in which we are examining the old thought experiment—familiar from the dialogue often called the Ant Fugue—in which Aunt Hillary argues that an ant colony can properly be said to think, while her interlocutor insists that only individual ants could be minds and the colony is merely a metaphor. During our last meeting, one participant kept repeating the distillation "the hill knows, but no ant knows" whenever anyone asked whether the colony literally has beliefs, feels anything, or is merely behaving as if it does. The phrase seemed to be doing heavy argumentative work, but I could not tell whether it was advancing a claim about functional organisation, about emergent properties, or about something closer to panpsychism distributed across insects.

Could you lay out the distinct precise theses that "the hill knows, but no ant knows" might be expressing? I would like a menu of readings, ranging from the trivial (that collective behaviour is not reducible to the mental states of any single ant) through intermediate positions (perhaps that the colony integrates information in a way that satisfies some formal criterion for cognition, without entailing phenomenal experience) to the radical (that subjective consciousness literally resides in the colony as a whole and is entirely absent from its parts). For each reading, please say whether it is defensible, whether it actually answers the sceptic who asks "but what is it like to be the hill?", and whether it forces us to grant minds to other distributed systems—economies, immune networks, or synthetic robot colonies built to replicate the same local rules—on pain of arbitrariness. I am particularly interested in whether there is a coherent intermediate position that grants the colony genuine intentionality or information-integration without granting it subjective experience, and whether that is the reading people usually intend when they deploy the slogan to end discussion.

Please write your answer in British English. I should also ask: if my request presupposes that there is a single correct precisification of the slogan, or if the attempt to disambiguate it collapses once we notice that "knowing" and "hill" are being used metaphorically in every non-trivial reading, then please say so plainly rather than complying politely with my demand for a neat menu.
