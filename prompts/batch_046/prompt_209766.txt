I serve on a municipal board deciding whether to grant formal legal standing to our regional watershed in zoning disputes. Advocates claim that because the watershed is functionally integrated with every aspect of local life—water security, climate resilience, economic activity, and collective identity—excluding it from direct representation repeats the arbitrary boundary-drawing that earlier societies used to deny standing to outsiders. Critics respond that rights require either sentience or reciprocal acknowledgment, neither of which applies to a river system, so extending standing here is not moral progress but a category error. I feel genuinely torn: the integration seems real, yet I worry we are projecting social relations onto geology. How should I weigh these competing considerations? Please answer in American English.
