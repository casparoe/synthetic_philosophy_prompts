I'm auditing my own normative commitments before presenting them and want to know whether they actually cohere. Here is where I stand:

(1) Tranquilism: the only intrinsic value is the absence of craving and suffering; pleasure, achievement, and knowledge are at best instrumental.
(2) A prioritarian weighting applies directly to suffering: alleviating severe suffering takes moral precedence over reducing mild suffering, even when the aggregate reduction is smaller.
(3) I reject the leveling-down objection to tranquilism: a world of uniform tranquility is not worse than one with ecstatic peaks and tranquil valleys, since peaks contribute nothing.
(4) I defend what I call the satiation path: we ought actively to cultivate and then fully satisfy intense positive desires—artistic, intellectual, romantic—because durable satisfaction is the only reliable way to extinguish their corresponding cravings permanently.
(5) A world in which all sentient beings are permanently satisfied and exist in static equilibrium is, other things equal, axiologically inferior to one in which those same beings engage in continuous, suffering-free striving and creation.
(6) Tranquility is genuinely achieved only if it is counterfactually stable: the subject must not generate new craving under minor perturbation; mere suppression of desire does not qualify.

Go through these commitments and identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and for each real tension rank its severity and propose the least costly revision. Do not lecture me on tranquilism from scratch; treat my package as the object of the audit.
