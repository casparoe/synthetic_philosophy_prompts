Please explain the conceptual issue of whether Confucian role ethics contains resources for what might be called role-residual moral agency—the capacity to maintain ethical obligations when a relational role is structurally suspended but not fully dissolved. Focus specifically on the tension between relational constitution, the view that personhood is constituted through sustained roles such as parent-child or ruler-subject, and the practice of remonstration (jian), in which a subordinate must correct a superior. Does remonstration require stepping outside the filial or ministerial role into a universal moral standpoint, or does it operate through a nested meta-role of co-participation in the Way? Then apply this framework to a constructed case: imagine a polity that replaces permanent kinship and political roles with rotating, contract-based functional assignments designed to prevent corruption and favoritism. From the perspective of Confucian role ethics, is the moral damage of this system best understood as the loss of specific obligations, or as the erosion of the conditions under which a self can be constituted as an ethical agent? In addressing this, distinguish carefully between role-performance, fulfilling the norms of a position, and role-identity, being constituted as a particular kind of person through sustained relational practice, and explain why this distinction matters for evaluating whether Confucian ethics can condemn the polity without implicitly relying on a pre-social, autonomous self that Confucianism typically rejects.
