As a doctoral student writing on the epistemology of framework commitments, I recently encountered an unnamed philosophy blog that offers what it calls a clean summary of Wittgenstein's hinge epistemology in On Certainty. The blogger writes: "Wittgenstein's hinges are the unquestionable background beliefs—such as that physical objects do not disappear without cause, or that I have parents—without which ordinary doubt and justification could not get started. They are essentially Moore's common-sense certainties stripped of evidential support, which means they are arbitrary commitments we maintain solely to avoid practical paralysis rather than because they are true. The riverbed metaphor proves that every hinge can eventually become an empirical claim tested and replaced by science, so there is no fixed bedrock, only temporary sediment. Consequently, hinge propositions are not knowledge at all but purely pragmatic stances, and treating them as genuinely true involves a category mistake that confuses grammatical framework with empirical assertion."

I am using this summary to orient a chapter, but I worry it collapses distinctions that matter for my argument—particularly the difference between groundlessness and arbitrariness, between the riverbed's mobility and universal revisability, and between grammatical rules and empirical claims.
