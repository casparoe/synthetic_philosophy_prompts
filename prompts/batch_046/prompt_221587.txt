Consider the published philosophical verdicts of Dr. L. Chen regarding sentience, consciousness, and moral status. Chen holds that adult humans, non-human mammals possessing complex neocortical architectures (e.g., cattle, pigs), and cephalopods such as the octopus are conscious, appealing both to neural integration and to flexible, goal-directed behavior. She judges that fish, nematodes (C. elegans), and present-day large language models lack consciousness. With respect to insects, she denies that individual honeybees or ants are conscious. She argues explicitly that the scale argument—whereby the astronomical number of insects multiplied by a non-zero prior for individual sentience generates enormous expected moral weight—establishes only a serious epistemic and moral risk, not actual phenomenal consciousness in any particular insect. She likewise denies that a honeybee colony, despite its collective problem-solving capacities, constitutes a unified conscious subject, on the grounds that emergent behavioral coordination does not entail integrated phenomenal experience. Nevertheless, she grants that substrate-independent consciousness is conceptually coherent, provided the causal architecture realizes the appropriate form of information integration.

Now consider a novel case. The Mesh is a synthetic ecology comprising 10^20 simple adaptive processors, each with the computational capacity of a single sensory neuron within an insect nervous system. The processors interact solely through local signaling protocols; there is no central controller, no global workspace, and no unified representational architecture. Over years, the Mesh exhibits homeostatic self-regulation, predictive modeling of its environment, and sustained, flexible goal-directed behavior at a planetary scale. Its organization is entirely decentralized and statistical—formally analogous to an insect colony, but orders of magnitude larger and implemented in silicon rather than biology.

Predict Chen's verdict: does the Mesh possess consciousness, or does it command moral status comparable to a conscious being? Identify the implicit theoretical principle—whether a threshold of integrated information, a biological-natural-kind constraint, a scale-sensitive epistemic rule, or some other framework—that best explains the coherence of her earlier judgments. Then articulate at least one competing principle that accounts for her prior verdicts equally well but yields a different prediction for the Mesh. Finally, specify a single further hypothetical case—distinct from those already discussed—that would best distinguish between these two interpretations of her position.
