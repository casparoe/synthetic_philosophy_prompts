I've been working through a decision puzzle that seems like a modern version of the absent-minded driver problem, but applied to an autonomous delivery drone. The drone has two visually identical landing zones, Zone A and Zone B. It must deliver to Zone B for a successful outcome; landing at Zone A results in failure. Because of a partial corruption in its navigation log, whenever the drone reaches either zone it cannot determine whether it is at A or B. Before takeoff, it can pre-program a flight rule—either "land at the first zone encountered" or "skip the first and land at the second." Once in flight and approaching a zone, it can also run a local update using whatever probabilities it assigns to its current location.

Two groups I've discussed this with take opposite sides. One group, call them the Pre-Commitment Planners, insists that the drone should choose the rule that maximizes success from the planning stage and then adhere to it without local revision. The other group, the Local Evidentialists, insists that at each zone the drone should reason from its current beliefs about where it is and act accordingly, which they claim gives a different recommendation. They seem to talk past each other: one frames the problem as selecting a complete strategy, the other as making a decision at a single information set.

Please draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between the Pre-Commitment Planners and the Local Evidentialists would produce regarding this drone case. The document should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can genuinely sign—covering the formal setup (the two zones, the corruption, the pre-programmed rules, the local belief state), what exactly needs explaining, and what standard consistency conditions rule out; (2) a disagreements table with columns Issue, Planner Wording, Evidentialist Wording, Crux, and Agreed Test or Case; (3) for each side, a concise statement of what specific result or observation they would treat as evidence moving them; and (4) an explicit judgment, with reasoning, on whether any portion of their disagreement is merely verbal rather than substantive.

After the document, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields: "agreed_claims" (array of strings), "disagreements" (array of objects with keys "issue", "planner_view", "evidentialist_view", "crux", "agreed_test"), "verbal_dispute" (boolean), "verbal_dispute_explanation" (string or null), and "remaining_genuine_cruxes" (array of strings).
