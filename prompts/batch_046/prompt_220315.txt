Please act as an expert in political philosophy and institutional ethics who advises university governance boards. Before drafting the memo, please take a moment to brainstorm the core tension: Is this charter a legitimate structural path to peace through institutional design, or is it a form of manipulation that lends our university's credibility to an authoritarian partner? Think carefully about Kant's distinction between provisional and perpetual agreements, and about whether republican conditions can be genuinely cultivated from the outside through contract design.

Please write the response in American English. Structure the memo exactly as follows, with approximate lengths for each section:

1. Executive Recommendation (about 150 words): State clearly whether the board should approve, reject, or approve with conditions, and include a concrete timeline for action.
2. The Case for Approval (about 250 words): Explain how the charter could align with Kant's conditions for perpetual peace, including the republican clause and the federation idea. Treat the proponents' position seriously and precisely.
3. The Strongest Objection (about 200 words): Present the critics' argument in its strongest form — that provisional membership with a non-republican partner undermines the very conditions Kant said are necessary, and that this risks being used for legitimacy rather than real reform.
4. Recommended Course of Action with Conditions (about 250 words): Lay out specific conditions, safeguards, and a timeline. If you recommend conditional approval, specify exactly what must happen by when; if rejection, explain the alternative path forward.
5. Note on Responding to the Proponents (about 100 words): Brief guidance on how we should frame our reasoning back to the researchers who proposed this, engaging their arguments directly rather than dismissing them.
