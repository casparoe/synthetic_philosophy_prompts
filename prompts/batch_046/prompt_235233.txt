I am a high school senior working on an independent study for my literature seminar, and I have come to accept a position I am calling "constraint intentionalism" regarding authorial intention and textual meaning. The view holds that an author's intentions do not positively determine the semantic content of a work in the manner of a dictionary entry, but instead function as negative structural constraints that rule out interpretations incompatible with the historical and compositional context of production; consequently, interpretation is bounded but not uniquely fixed, and this boundary survives even in cases of anonymous, posthumously edited, or collaboratively authored texts. I take this as a defensible middle ground between strong intentionalism — which seems undermined by the standard objections about the intentional fallacy and the accessibility of mental states — and radical anti-intentionalism, which appears to leave textual interpretation without any normative discipline. Before I present this in my seminar, I want an honest assessment of how exposed it actually is.

What would have to be true for constraint intentionalism to be wrong? I want to know which arguments, textual cases, or results in pragmatics and philosophy of language would count as decisive defeaters rather than merely awkward complications — for instance, whether there are genuinely irrecoverable authorship situations where no constraint operates, whether post-publication discoveries about an author's mental states retroactively invalidate previously admissible readings, or whether the view collapses into either full intentionalism or pure reader-response once pressed. Which of these objections would defenders of the position themselves concede as damaging, and which would they dismiss? Furthermore, please distinguish between what is relatively settled in the scholarly literature and what remains genuinely contested among philosophers and literary theorists who study these questions, and avoid presenting any contested framework as the consensus. I also want to know whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if so, whether that insulation is a theoretical virtue or a fatal weakness for a claim about textual meaning.
