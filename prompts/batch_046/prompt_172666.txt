Trying to sort out my gut feelings on whether ignoring local customs is actually immoral, especially when you're just passing through. Cutting in line at a Berlin bakery is wrong even if you're a tourist who genuinely didn't see the queue system. Skipping the tip at a US diner where it's expected feels wrong too, even if you're from Japan and the waitress was rude. Jaywalking in Tokyo where literally no one does it: wrong. But pushing to the front at a packed London pub where thats just how you get served? Not wrong, it's the local game. Not leaving a coin on the tray after espresso in Rome: not wrong. Driving 20 over in a rural Spanish town where every local does it and police never stop anyone: I'm torn, maybe wrong but not as bad? Also refusing a handshake greeting in Saudi Arabia because you're immunocompromised: not wrong, health comes first. Is there one principle that gets all these, like maybe obligations only attach when breaking the rule actually messes up a cooperative scheme others depend on? If so, why does the Berlin queue hit different from the Rome coin? And what should I say about wearing shoes inside a Korean home when you have a foot injury you can't explain? Does my principle have to drop one of my verdicts, probably the speeding one, or is there a more complicated rule that saves them all?
