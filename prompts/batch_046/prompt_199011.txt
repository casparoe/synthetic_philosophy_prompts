I am preparing an essay on philosophical responses to mortality for an independent study, and I am trying to understand a well-known episode in the Zhuangzi in which Zhuangzi's wife dies. In the account, Huizi comes to console him and finds him drumming on a tub and singing; Zhuangzi replies that death is merely the transformation of qi, no different from the change of seasons, and asks why he should weep. I want to understand the exact logical structure of this reply: is he arguing that personal identity does not survive the dissolution of the body, so that the person who died is not the same subject who lived, or is he arguing that even if identity survives in some transformed state, the emotional attachment to one particular form is arbitrary? I am also trying to see whether this argument avoids a problem analogous to the Epicurean challenge—that death cannot harm the dead because there is no subject present to experience harm—since if Zhuangzi denies a persisting self, then grief might be conceptually confused rather than merely excessive. Could you help me map out the dependency between his metaphysics of qi and his normative claim about appropriate emotion, and tell me where my comparison to Epicurus is likely to go wrong? I am not looking for further readings, but for a precise breakdown of the conceptual steps.
