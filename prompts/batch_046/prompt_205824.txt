Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Mara and Leo, sitting in a cafe arguing about indirect normativity and idealized deliberation. Mara holds that your real practical reasons come from what you'd want if you had full information and perfect rationality—so if your idealized self would ditch medicine for poetry, then right now you actually have reason to switch, even though you love being a doctor. Leo pushes back that this is just alienation with extra steps: the actual person in front of you has reasons tied to her actual cares, and importing some hypothetical deliberator doesn't explain why she should listen. Have them work through a concrete case—maybe a musician who genuinely thrives on performing but learns that a fully informed, rational version of herself would quit to preserve her hearing. They need to actually engage: Leo should respond to Mara's claim that the idealized agent is still "you," and Mara should answer Leo's worry that indirect accounts smuggle in consequentialism without admitting it. Don't let either side be a strawman; both should sound like they actually read the literature. Keep it casual, like people talking over coffee, and please answer in British English (use colour, centre, realise, etc.). Oh, and make sure they address whether indirect normativity can avoid the charge that it's just consequentialism in disguise—Leo definately thinks it can't.
