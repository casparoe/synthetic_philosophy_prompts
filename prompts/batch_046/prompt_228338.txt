I run a philosophy workshop for about 16 grad students, one 90-minute session. Design a classroom simulation that makes them feel the pull of saying one culture is morally superior—honor, dignity, or victimhood—and then undermines that feeling.

Split them into three seperate groups of ~5, each assigned one culture with its own "moral currency": honor trades in reputation and public restoration, dignity in individual rights and autonomy, victimhood in harm acknowledgment and institutional remedy. Give them one shared case: a researcher publicly accuses a colleague of stealing ideas; the accused says the ideas were communal knowledge and the accusation is a racist dogwhistle; the institution promises an investigation but delays it. Each group must propose a resolution using only their currency. Then run a "translation round" where each group has to restate the other two proposals in their own terms, followed by a vote—but they can only vote yes if the proposal is fully translatable into their currency.

Include rules, materials (index cards or token slips), a timeline for 90 min, and a debrief that maps moments onto the real conceptual tension. Be honest about what the game can't reproduce—probably the lived emotional weight of real shame or trauma. Also warn me what will predictably go wrong, like one group treating translation as domination, and how to fix it.

Keep the whole response under about 450 words. Start with the rules and timeline, not with background lecture.
