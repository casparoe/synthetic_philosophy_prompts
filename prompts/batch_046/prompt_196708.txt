i've been thinking about Orwell's continuous revision (the Ministry of Truth updates records so the past always matches the present) vs Stalin's discrete airbrushing (cut a face from a photo and splice the film, done). it feels like the difference between running a movie at 24fps where every frame gets replaced smoothly, versus making one hard jump cut that people notice.

then i ran into the color phi phenomenon — two dots of different colors flash in rapid succession and viewers report seeing one dot move through an intermediate color, even though no middle frame ever existed. consciousness researchers use it to argue there's no Cartesian theater playing experience out in real time; the brain constructs the narrative after the fact.

so here's my half-baked idea: Orwellian revision is like color phi applied to collective memory. society doesn't hold a static archive but experiences a continuous historical narrative that's reconstructed frame by frame. each update gets smoothed over, so people never feel the rupture that Stalinesque editing creates. the discrete version is the scar people actually see.

specificaly, does the color phi literature actually license talking about "frames" of social experience, or am i just importing false precision? and if the analogy holds at all, does continuous revision become more dangerous because it's invisible, or less dangerous because it avoids collective trauma?
