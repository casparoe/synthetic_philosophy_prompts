Please develop and defend a specific position within the Strawsonian framework of reactive attitudes and moral responsibility. Your thesis should be this: the objective attitude that Strawson describes is best understood not as the absence of reactive attitudes, but as a second-order reactive attitude—call it reflexive epistemic humility—directed at our own dispositions to resent and hold others accountable. Argue that reconceiving the objective attitude in this way allows one to defend a graded, context-sensitive model of moral desert that avoids both metaphysical libertarianism and the eliminativist conclusion that determinism undermines all responsibility. In constructing your argument, carefully distinguish between personal and vicarious reactive attitudes, and explain how reflexive epistemic humility operates as an internal normative check on the severity of our primary responses rather than as an external suspension of them. Conclude by assessing whether this modified Strawsonian view provides a more stable foundation for our practices of moral responsibility than standard compatibilist or hard-incompatibilist alternatives.
