On one side, people argue that ranking mass killings treats human suffering like a spreadsheet and inevitably trivializes particular victims; on the other side, refusing to compare them seems like a kind of moral evasion, because it prevents us from recognizing patterns of state violence and learning how to prevent future ones. To make the problem more concrete, I recently started using a distinction of my own: what if we separate "atrocities of design," where widespread harm is the intended output of a bureaucratic or ideological system, from "atrocities of neglect," where comparable harm results mainly from indifference to predictable consequences? I am not sure whether that distinction clarifies anything or just adds another layer of confusion.
