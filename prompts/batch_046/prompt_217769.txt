I am writing up my commitments on practical rationality and symmetric choice for a philosophy of action seminar, and I want a consistency audit before I present. Here is where I stand: (1) I am a rational permissivist—when two options are exactly on a par with respect to every reason-relevant feature, rationality permits either but does not mandate one. (2) I take Buridan's ass to describe a genuine normative possibility, not merely a psychological or physical puzzle; there can be perfect evaluative symmetry between options. (3) I think that employing an arbitrary, non-reason-based selection procedure—say, randomization or a fixed leftward bias—to break a tie does not violate rationality, so long as the agent does not mistake the mechanism's output for a justifying reason in favor of the option selected. (4) I reject what I call preference laundering: an agent should not retroactively cite a feature that was introduced only by the selection mechanism (e.g., "this bale was closer to my left foot") as if it had been part of the original evaluative comparison. (5) At the same time, I hold that practical rationality must be determinately action-guiding; a rational agent should never remain permanently suspended between equally good options, so the normative system must eventually yield a definite recommendation. (6) I maintain that formal representations of choice—utility functions or preference orderings—should encode genuine parity as strict equivalence and should not resolve ties through hidden perturbations of values or unstated lexicographic rules. Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and what the least costly revision would be in each case. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious. Be specific about whether the problem lies in (1)–(2), in (3)–(5), or in the interface between (6) and the others, and say what each fix costs—especially whether saving determinacy requires abandoning permissivism or merely reconceiving how mechanisms relate to reasons.
