I'm trying to translate some rondeaux in the style of Marot and keep both the rhyme scheme and the syllable count, but I keep running into the same weird problem I don't have a word for. You preserve the AABBA rhyme and the ten-syllable lines, so the poem still looks and sounds like a poem, but the rhyme words land in totally diferent semantic fields. For instance, where the original rhymes 'coeur' with 'douleur' — heart with pain, so the rhyme itself carries an emotional logic — in English I end up with 'engine' and 'routine', which rhyme fine but turn the stanza into something about machinery and repetition. The form is intact, the tone is roughly there, but the conceptual skeleton that the rhyme was holding together has been swapped out for an alien one. I know about compensation in transaltion and formal equivalence, but those feel too broad. I'm asking about the specific case where the rhyme skeleton survives perfectly and the semantic skeleton it was carrying dies silently, replaced by whatever phonetics allow. Does that configuration have its own name? And if not, how exactly is it diferent from ordinary translation loss — is it just a subset, or does the fact that the surface form convinces you nothing was lost make it a separate failure mode?
