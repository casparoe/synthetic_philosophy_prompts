Question: In Daniel Dennetts taxonomy of minds, explain the four kinds of creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian — and discuss why the distinction matters for philosophy of mind, especially regarding human cognition.

Student answer:
Darwinian creatures run entirely on genetic programming, so they cant learn individually from experience — everything is hardwired by natural selection, like bacteria or simple insects. Skinnerian creatures can learn through trial and error and operant conditioning, like rats recieving rewards, but they dont form internal models; they just adapt behavior. Popperian creatures go further by generating hypotheses inside their minds and testing them mentally, which lets them avoid dying in dangerous real-world experiments. Dennett names them after Karl Popper because Popper showed that science works through falsification, so these creatures basically do mental falsification. Gregorian creatures are essentially Popperian creatures that also use language, writing, and cultural tools to share and store ideas, which is why humans count as Gregorian. The taxonomy shows that human cognition isnt just a more advanced Skinnerian system but belongs to a completely separate class that depends on cultural transmission.
