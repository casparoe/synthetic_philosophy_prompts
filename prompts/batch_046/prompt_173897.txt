I'm a software engineer without formal philosophical training, but I've been working through whether the mind runs on something like a mental programming language. Here is the package of commitments I've arrived at, and I want a consistency audit—not a lecture—before I present these to a reading group. (1) I think cognition involves a language of thought—something like Mentalese—with explicit syntactic structure and compositional rules, analogous to source code being compiled into machine-level instructions. (2) I think this mental language must be largely innate, because learning any representational system seems to require already having representational resources to bootstrap with. (3) At the same time, I think deep neural networks could in principle implement every cognitive capacity that a symbolic system can, including compositional representation, so I don't think a literal language of thought is strictly necessary to explain how minds work. (4) I also think the strongest argument for LOT is the systematicity and productivity of thought—e.g., that understanding "John loves Mary" carries with it the capacity to understand "Mary loves John"—and that this is best explained by literal syntactic combination in the head rather than by associative weights. (5) Finally, I think the semantic content of mental representations is fixed entirely by external causal or informational relations to the environment (a teleosemantic or causal-informational view), not by their internal syntax alone.

Please examine these for joint tenability. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and how severe is each tension? Rank them by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. For each real tension, specify the least costly revision and what I would have to give up. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most inconsistencies overall. In particular, address whether (3) undermines (1) and (4), or whether they can coexist if I reinterpret "compiled program" as an implementation-level description of what a sufficiently structured neural network is doing. Also address whether (2) and (5) pull against each other: if syntax is innate but content is fixed externally, is there a hidden bootstrapping problem for semantics?

Please also state explicitly what argument or empirical finding would most likely change your verdict—for example, a rigorous proof that connectionist architectures cannot genuinely implement systematic compositionality without explicit symbolic rules, or a convincing demonstration that an innate syntax is incompatible with a fully externalist theory of mental content. And indicate where your reasoning is most vulnerable to failure or to smuggling in assumptions that a specialist would reject.
