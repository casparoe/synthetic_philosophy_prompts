I'm giving the opening talk at a little dedication for a mural my aunt Maria painted on the old pharamacy wall. She was a nurse thirty years, no art school. The mural shows everyone in town holding a tool that isn't theirs — baker with stethoscope, mechanic with paintbrush, mayor with fishing rod — because she said we only get by when people do things outside their job for each other. Half the room are neighbors who just love her; the other half are local artists and a couple professors who'll spot if I'm sneaking in Tolstoy's point from What is Art? that real art infects people with feeling and only counts if regular folks can catch it without training. Write a three-minute speech that uses that idea to explain why her mural is real art even though she never learned perspective rules, without sounding like a lecture. It needs to comfort and celebrate, not insult the experts, and the philosophical point should actually shape what I can and can't say — like maybe I shouldn't claim she's technically brilliant. Keep it casual, like family toast, but let Tolstoy do the structural work.
