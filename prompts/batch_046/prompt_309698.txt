I'm writing an essay for my course on comparative economic history, and I've built an argument about the Needham question that now seems to prove far more than I intended. Here are the steps:

(1) To ask "why Britain and not China?" is well posed only if Britain and Qing China were genuinely comparable candidates for industrial take-off around 1750.
(2) Genuine comparability requires that the two cases share all causally relevant background conditions except for the factor we identify as decisive, so that counterfactual dependency can be traced without confounding.
(3) But they differed profoundly in coal endowment, state structure, property-rights regimes, scientific institutionalisation, and colonial access — so they fail (2).
(4) Therefore the comparison is ill-posed: it treats two historically unique configurations as if they were controlled experiments.
(5) The same reasoning applies to every macro-comparative claim — coal-endowment explanations, institutionalist accounts of parliamentary constraint, even broader "Europe versus Asia" frameworks — since any two societies differ in innumerable respects.
(6) So either all contrastive historical explanation is methodologically invalid, or we must reject (2).
(7) If we reject (2), we can invoke single factors despite massive background divergence, but then we lose any principled filter against spurious correlations: tea consumption, alphabetic script, or climate alone could be offered with equal causal weight.
(8) Hence either comparative economic history collapses into narrative particularism, or it abandons causal rigour altogether.

The conclusion that the entire field is either impossible or arbitrary feels absurd, yet each premise seems defensible. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I would prefer to keep the intuition that coal geography and institutional constraints genuinely mattered, but I cannot see how to preserve that without falling into (7). Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
