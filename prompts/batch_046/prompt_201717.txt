The derivations of the Born rule within Everettian quantum mechanics have been developed for decades—decision-theoretic arguments that rational successors in a branching universe should assign credences proportional to |ψ|², symmetry-based approaches that invoke decoherence and envariance to fix objective branch weights, and a persistent critical line alleging that every derivation either smuggles in a probabilistic premise or mistakes epistemic uncertainty for ontological structure. Despite extensive formal refinement, the dispute feels stalled: the same objections about circularity, the ambiguity of "equal weight" across branches, and the absence of a single actualized outcome are met with the same adjustments, and neither camp treats the other as having made an error of physics or mathematics. I want a diagnosis of this impasse, not another derivation or defense.

Concretely, describe what each side actually requires of an adequate explanation: must it avoid any prior probabilistic reasoning; must it apply to one deterministic world-history rather than a branching tree; must it justify observed frequencies within a single branch rather than weights distributed across all branches; and does it need to deliver normative guidance for belief, an ontological account of world-structure, or both? Then diagnose whether the disagreement is substantive—about quantum mechanics, decision theory, or the logic of self-locating belief—or partly verbal, with "probability" functioning as rational credence for one group, as objective branch-weight for another, and as a category error for a third. Are the camps answering different questions—normative rationality for branching agents, metaphysics of worlds, semantics of statistical prediction—under the single heading "the Born rule problem"? Offer your own hypothesis if you have one—mine is that the stall largely comes from treating a normative project (how should a branching agent reason?) and an ontological project (what do the squared amplitudes represent in world-structure?) as rival answers to one question, when separating them might dissolve much of the conflict, though a residual dispute about whether "branch-weight realism" or "branch-index realism" is correct may remain.

Finally, tell me what, given your diagnosis, would actually count as progress: a formal result showing that rational preference orderings in deterministic branching structures necessarily match |ψ|² without probabilistic premises; a conceptual distinction that removes the appearance of circularity; an argument that the demand for a "derivation" itself presupposes single-world ontology and should be dropped; or a demonstration that the underlying intuitions about explanation are not shared and the disagreement is structurally permanent. Specify which move addresses which part of the disagreement, and be honest about whether you think resolution is possible or the debate is ineliminable.
