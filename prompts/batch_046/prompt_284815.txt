I'm trying to hold together a view about pleasure and well-being, and I'd like a consistency check before I commit to it. Here are my commitments:

(1) Hedonism is true in the sense that pleasure and the absence of pain exhaust intrinsic well-being.
(2) The heterogeneity problem is decisive: there is no single phenomenal quality shared by gustatory pleasure, the pleasure of solving a proof, aesthetic absorption, and even masochistic enjoyment.
(3) So pleasure is best understood as a functional kind unified by its role in reward-learning mechanisms, not by any introspective feel.
(4) But I also think functional unification cannot ground intrinsic value: if artificial reward signals in a brain-in-a-vat produced the same functional state without any phenomenology, they wouldn't contribute to well-being, which suggests phenomenology still matters.
(5) I reject attitudinal theories that reduce pleasure to a pro-attitude or endorsement, since there are passive pleasures—like feeling warmth spread through one's limbs—where no desire or approval is present.
(6) I maintain that reflective equilibrium demands we treat the pleasure of a cool breeze and the pleasure of understanding a theorem as contributing to well-being in exactly the same direct way, not merely analogically.

Do these hang together? Specifically, is there a hidden tension between (2)-(3) and (6), or between (3) and (4), and does (5) actually conflict with (1) in ways I'm missing? Please rank any real tensions by severity, identify which commitments pull against each other and why, note any apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection, and say which revision is least costly if consistency requires one. Please answer in American English.
