I have been studying the technical literature on artificial intelligence alignment and have encountered a distinction between intent alignment—ensuring that a system correctly infers and executes a user’s immediate objective within a specific interaction—and value alignment, which concerns the embedding of durable ethical commitments that should govern behavior across varied and unforeseen contexts. Some formulations present the alignment problem in its narrow form as being confined entirely to intent alignment. This raises a conceptual difficulty for me: if a system achieved perfect intent alignment, such that it flawlessly realized whatever goal I specified in any given situation, would that not simultaneously resolve the broader problem of value alignment? After all, I could issue a standing meta-instruction requiring that all executed actions conform to a particular moral framework, and the system’s perfect fidelity to my intent would seem to guarantee compliance with that framework. Is the separation between intent alignment and value alignment therefore merely an artifact of framing, rather than a substantive technical distinction? Or does perfect intent alignment contain an internal limitation—perhaps related to how goals are specified, interpreted, or pursued across time—that prevents it from serving as a reliable vehicle for enduring values?
