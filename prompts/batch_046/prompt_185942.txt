I'm drafting a position paper on political liberty and want a consistency audit before presenting. Here are my commitments:

(a) I take republican freedom—non-domination, or being free from subjection to another's arbitrary will—to be the core political value, because it captures the structural wrong in relationships like benevolent dictatorship or kindly paternalism that negative liberty misses entirely.

(b) I accept Berlin's distinction between negative liberty (absence of interference) and positive liberty (effective self-mastery or capacity), and I agree with his warning that conflating positive liberty with political freedom invites dangerous justifications of coercion. At the same time, I reject his claim that republican freedom is just negative liberty with a sociological gloss; non-domination is conceptually and normatively distinct.

(c) In workplace governance, I maintain that a worker in a democratically managed cooperative—where collectively authored rules are numerous and often interfere with individual choice—is republican-freer than a well-paid employee in a hierarchical firm with a benevolent CEO who rarely issues direct orders, because the latter remains vulnerable to arbitrary restructuring or dismissal.

(d) I hold that when the state provides universal healthcare, education, and income support, it legitimately expands citizens' positive liberty, but I deny that such provision, by itself, increases republican freedom unless it also reduces relationships of arbitrary power.

(e) To resolve conflicts among dimensions of freedom, I propose strict lexical priority: first minimize domination, then maximize non-interference, then expand effective capacities.

(f) I also hold that negative liberty possesses intrinsic weight that is not reducible to its instrumental contribution to preventing domination; consequently, a marginal reduction in arbitrary power never automatically justifies a substantial increase in interference.

(g) I maintain that any institutional arrangement that creates discretionary, unaccountable authority—such as an oversight body with broad unchecked powers—reduces republican freedom for those subject to it, regardless of whether that authority is ever exercised.

(h) Nevertheless, I support establishing precisely such an oversight agency, with broad discretionary authority insulated from direct democratic control, to audit corporate governance and forcibly restructure firms that exhibit high domination risk.

(i) Finally, I treat freedom as fully scalar rather than binary: there are degrees of domination, interference, and capacity, and institutional design should aim to maximize an aggregate weighted by the lexical priorities above.

Please audit this set. Which commitments genuinely pull against each other, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision to restore consistency? Rank the tensions by severity, specifying for each what must give—whether a case verdict, a methodological rule, or a conceptual distinction—and explain what that revision costs me elsewhere in the framework. Do not treat every pairing as equally problematic; I want to know where the real fractures are.
