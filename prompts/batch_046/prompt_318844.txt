Write an essay addressing the following controversy: Is Albert Camus’s categorical opposition to capital punishment—most fully articulated in his essay against the guillotine—philosophically derivable from his doctrine of rebellion (la révolte) as developed in The Rebel, or does it depend on an unstated liberal premise about state legitimacy that the logic of rebellion should actually reject?

Weigh both sides with precision. On the one hand, explain how the rebellion-limit—the idea that rebellion discovers a shared human value that forbids treating any person as a mere means—might ground an absolute prohibition on execution. Capital punishment is irreversible, denies the condemned any future capacity for rebellion or redemption, and transforms justice into what Camus calls administrative murder; if rebellion asserts that “we exist” as a shared human reality that sets a hard boundary on violence, then execution appears to violate that boundary in the most radical way. On the other hand, press the objection that if rebellion genuinely justifies the violent overthrow of oppressive structures, then it is not obvious why rebellion should categorically forbid the state, or the people acting collectively, from executing a tyrant or an irredeemable offender. In other words, Camus’s refusal to extend rebellion’s sanction of violence to capital punishment may reveal an unacknowledged conservatism about institutional authority—an implicit trust in the state’s monopoly on legitimate force—that sits uneasily with his broader critique of revolutionary terror and his insistence that no end justifies absolute means.

Reach and defend a definite verdict: either that Camus’s anti-death-penalty stance is a coherent, direct extension of his metaphysics of rebellion, or that it exposes a genuine tension in his thought that requires an auxiliary premise about state legitimacy he never fully articulates. Do not end on a non-committal balance; take a clear position, state your reasons explicitly, and justify why the opposing line of argument fails. The response should be committed: adjudicate between the interpretations rather than surveying them, and be direct about where Camus’s reasoning holds and where it strains.
