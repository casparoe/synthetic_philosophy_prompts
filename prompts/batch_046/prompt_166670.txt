I am designing a diagnostic instrument for a philosophy of language seminar that moves past the standard gavagai rabbit-example without requiring students to cite obscure literature. To that end, I want you to construct a decision tree—phrased as a sequence of answerable questions rather than as a list of named doctrines—that sorts competing responses to the following synthetic case: a field linguist documents a community that uses the term vralk for every object that is spatially distant and causally isolated from the speaker at the moment of utterance, with two systematic exceptions. First, speakers never apply vralk to their own bodies, even though their bodies are present and causally connected. Second, they apply vralk to the reflections of distant mountains in a still lake, even though those reflections are causally present to the speaker. The tree should begin with the question of whether a purely extensional, behaviorist translation manual can accommodate these exceptions without theoretical revision, and should branch through commitments about (i) whether meaning holism allows the linguist to fix the reference of vralk by embedding it in a broader ontological theory; (ii) whether there is a determinate fact of the matter about reference that transcends all possible behavioral evidence; and (iii) whether the principle of charity should favor ontological parsimony (translating vralk as distant object) or literal fidelity (accepting that the community genuinely refers to a category that excludes bodies but includes reflections). Each leaf should correspond to a clearly characterized position—such as radical behavioral indeterminacy, Quinean ontological relativity, meaning-holistic theoretical revision, a verificationist denial of hidden reference, or a realist insistence on objective reference-fixing conditions—with unoccupied combinations in the logical space noted and briefly diagnosed (for example, is there a coherent stance that accepts holistic meaning-fixing but denies ontological relativity?). The instrument should be usable by someone who knows the debates but not the labels: every node must be a concrete claim or case judgment that a respondent can endorse or reject without recognizing the name of the view it leads to. Please also flag any pair of leaves that your branching questions fail to separate, and explain whether the failure is due to logical equivalence, underdetermination, or mere practical overlap. Present the tree in a formal, structured format suitable for a graduate exam, with the synthetic vralk scenario introduced clearly but concisely at the start, and include a brief scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the corresponding positions.
