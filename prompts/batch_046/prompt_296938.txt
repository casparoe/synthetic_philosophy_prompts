I am writing from an upper-division seminar on seventeenth-century moral and political thought, where we have been examining Pascal's Pensées—particularly the fragments treating the relationship between justice and force. Among specialists, a persistent interpretive stalemate remains: one camp reads Pascal as a thoroughgoing conventionalist for whom justice is nothing more than the rationalization of established power, while another camp insists that he retains a normative standard—perhaps theological, perhaps rooted in the geometric spirit—that allows justice to criticize force rather than merely describe it. The texts themselves are brief, and both sides cite the same passages.

My own hypothesis is that the disagreement is not primarily textual but methodological: the two camps operate with different criteria for what counts as an adequate explanation of a moral concept once theological authority is bracketed. One side demands that justice reduce to observable social facts; the other requires that any account preserve the phenomenology of obligation. If this is correct, then no archival discovery will settle the matter, because the dispute concerns the standards of explanation rather than Pascal's intentions.

I would like you to diagnose the structure of this disagreement with reasons. Is it verbal—do the sides mean different things by 'conventionalist'? Is it substantive but hidden, turning on an empirical claim about Pascal's sources or intellectual context? Or is it a clash of starting intuitions about whether force can generate genuine normative authority, with neither side able to argue the other into agreement? Please support your diagnosis by engaging concretely with what each camp actually asserts. Then, tell me what kind of progress—if any—should be expected. Would a formal reconstruction of Pascal's argument structure help? Would a conceptual distinction between tactical acceptance of law and normative endorsement of justice dissolve the conflict? Or is the impasse a sign that the question is ill-posed? I want an honest assessment, not a balanced survey of interpretations.
