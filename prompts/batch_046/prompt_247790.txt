Consider the metaphysical tension between downward grounding—where a higher-level property (for instance, a unified conscious state) is taken to ground or partially determine features of its lower-level physical basis—and the standard formal constraints that grounding is irreflexive and asymmetric. Rank the following four positions by how successfully they accommodate downward grounding without violating irreflexivity, assigning each a coherence score from 1 (fails to resolve the tension) to 10 (fully resolves it): (A) strong emergentism with autonomous downward causation; (B) weak emergence via mereological supervenience that permits descriptive but not ontological downward dependence; (C) Russellian monism, in which proto-phenomenal properties serve as the shared ground for both mental and physical relations; and (D) eliminative physicalism that rejects the existence of the higher-level properties altogether. Provide the score for each position followed by a single sentence of justification.
