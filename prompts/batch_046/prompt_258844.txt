As a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of science in earnest, I wish to commission a joint adversarial-collaboration statement from two camps: defenders of Humean regularity accounts of natural law (best-system or mosaic-based views) and defenders of anti-Humean governing or dispositional-essentialist accounts. The deliverable should be a single document to which both sides could append their signatures, divided into three sections. First, a numbered list of agreed claims—statements about scientific methodology, the role of prediction and explanation, the distinction between accidental correlation and lawlike behavior, and the modal status of physical constants—that neither camp need reject. Second, a disagreements table stating each side's core commitments in wording that adherents of that side would recognize as accurate and fair, including specifically whether probabilistic quantum regularities (for instance, decay-rate distributions) share law-status with deterministic macro-regularities (for instance, gravitational dynamics in isolated two-body systems), and whether a globally valid conservation principle that does not determine local trajectories counts as genuinely law-governed. The table should distinguish substantive disagreement from merely verbal dispute. Third, identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that resolving them in one direction would move at least one camp. Among these, include whether explanatory success requires metaphysical necessitation beyond actual-world regularity, and whether the best-system account can exclude cosmic-scale coincidences without circular appeal to lawhood. For each crux, present one concrete hypothetical case, argument, or observational scenario—such as the discovery that a fundamental constant varies slowly with cosmic epoch in a pattern not derivable from deeper symmetry principles—that both sides agree is relevant, and record in advance each side's predicted analysis or outcome. The statement must contain no requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and should be composed so that a reader lacking access to external literature could assess the arguments solely on their internal coherence.
