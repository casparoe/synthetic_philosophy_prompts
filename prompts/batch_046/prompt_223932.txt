Two people in a study group, Leila and Marcus, have been arguing about whether an ant colony—one they nickname Aunt Hillary, after the ant fugue—can literally have a mind. Their exchange went as follows. (1) Leila said the colony qualifies because it implements integrated information with a phi value comparable to a simple mammal: pheromone trails and trophallaxis create a continuous causal mesh that is irreducible to individual ants. (2) Marcus replied that IIT applies only within a single physical boundary with irreducible cause-effect power, and since ants are separated by exoskeletons with weak direct coupling, colony-level phi collapses to near zero. (3) Leila countered that this picks the wrong boundary—like denying integration across hemispheres because the corpus callosum is “just” axons—the pheromone field and shared gut microbiome form the actual substrate. (4) Marcus said even granting substrate integration, there is no unified phenomenal perspective: each ant runs its own sensory-motor loop, and without a shared “what it is like,” the colony is only behaviorally complex, not minded. (5) Leila answered that this begs the question against distributed cognition; she pointed to split-brain patients who retain one mind despite hemispheric separation, and noted that Aunt Hillary suppresses foraging for exactly seventy-two hours after losing its signal ant regardless of food need, a functional grief-state that cannot be decomposed into individual algorithms.

Please adjudicate the five moves in order: rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right, briefly justify each rating, say who is more right overall, and identify the central issue both are missing. Respond as a numbered list with exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
