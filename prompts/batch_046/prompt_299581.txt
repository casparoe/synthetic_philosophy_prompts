I am trying to understand a passage from Augustine's Confessions (Book XI) in which he examines the nature of time. He writes that if no one asks him what time is, he knows; but if he tries to explain, he does not. He rejects the idea that time is the movement of celestial bodies, since those bodies could stop and time might still pass. Instead, he proposes that time is a kind of stretching or distension of the mind itself: the present of past things is memory, the present of present things is direct attention, and the present of future things is expectation. Yet he then asks how we can measure intervals of time if the past no longer exists and the future does not yet exist, and he seems to answer that we measure them by the impression they leave upon the soul as they pass through the present.

I want to understand whether this passage is best read as an early psychological theory of time — one that makes temporal order depend on the continuity of consciousness — or whether Augustine is actually advancing a form of presentism in which only the present has being, with memory and expectation serving merely as present mental states. In particular, I am struggling with the move in which he asks how measurement is possible if past and future lack existence: is he assuming a hidden realist commitment to the present moment, or is he demonstrating that any measurement of time must be internal to the mind?

This relates to what I have started calling the Borges-Augustine convergence problem. In his essay "A New Refutation of Time", Borges argues that successive moments are incompatible with identity and that time is therefore illusory, holding instead to an eternal present in which all moments coexist. I wonder whether Augustine's distension of the soul anticipates Borges's idealism — since both deny that time exists in external things — or whether Augustine's reliance on the successive ordering of memory, attention, and expectation actually commits him to the very reality of succession that Borges denies.

If my framing rests on a confused premise — for instance, if I am misreading Augustine's ontology, misrepresenting Borges's argument, or inventing a false convergence between them — please say so plainly rather than accommodating the error.
