Please write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—Dr. Aris, who defends a classical Abhidharma-inspired reading of anātman together with the doctrine of momentariness (kṣaṇikavāda), and Dr. Bhat, who defends a narrative-continuity account of the self informed by process-relational metaphysics. Have them work through the following in real back-and-forth. First, whether the absence of a metaphysical substrate renders karmic imputation incoherent or merely conventional—specifically, whether the causal stream (santāna) of impermanent dharmas can bear moral properties without a bearer. Second, whether narrative coherence can do the work of diachronic identity without smuggling in a hidden essence or svabhāva. Third, address a hypothetical case I have devised for the seminar—the “Split Continuity” scenario—in which, following a neural-archival procedure, a single meditator’s cognitive-affective patterns are simultaneously and stably instantiated in two biologically distinct bodies. Ask them to determine whether either framework can deliver a determinate answer about which body, if either, inherits the original agent’s moral responsibilities and first-personal concerns.

In addition, I want the dialogue to treat the Buddhist two-truths distinction (saṃvṛti and paramārtha) not merely as epistemological layers but as competing explanatory registers within moral psychology: does conventional self-talk serve a different explanatory function from ultimate dharma-analysis when we account for remorse, anticipatory anxiety, or moral motivation? Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations; do not quote any real text verbatim in quotation marks—paraphrase freely instead. You may inject one conceptual move that is not standard textbook fare but that a well-trained model can engage with—perhaps drawing an analogy between the Abhidharma analysis of contact (sparśa) and contemporary relational accounts of intentionality, or reconceiving dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda) as a constraint on narrative rather than on substance.

Keep the tone formal and philosophically precise. At the conclusion, please state explicitly how confident you are in the dialogue’s reconstruction of these positions, and identify the single interpretive claim or argument within it about which you are least certain.
