I was reading about revealed preference theory and it got me thinking about how at work we treat user click data like it reveals some deep stable preference, but then you change the default ordering or add a social-proof label and people flip completely — so is there even a utility function in there, or is the architecture doing all the work? I want an in-person activity that makes people feel that tension in their bones, not just talk about it. The observers build rankings and try to predict future picks, then everyone compares notes and hits the wall: the same person "prefers" opposite bundles depending on frame. After that I need a debrief that maps each framing shift onto the philosophical problem — where is the real preference supposed to live if revealed preference just tracks the frame? — plus an honest note on which part of the idea the game can't reproduce and I have to supply.
