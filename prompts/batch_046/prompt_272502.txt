I spent three decades in experimental physics working on precision instrumentation and distributed sensor calibration, and since retiring I have been auditing graduate seminars in epistemology and philosophy of science. The standard tripartite framing of the epistemic regress problem—foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism—strikes me as curiously disconnected from the actual architecture of measurement validation in my former field. In metrology, instruments rarely stand in a simple linear hierarchy of authority; instead they form networks of cross-calibration in which certain nodes act as provisional anchors whose own status depends on distributed agreement across the network, producing dependency structures that involve both local circularity and global directionality without either an ultimate base or an infinite ascent.

I want to develop this observation into a workshop proposal—approximately 4,000 words, suitable for a philosophy-of-science colloquium—asking whether these calibration architectures constitute a genuinely distinct fourth model of epistemic justification, or whether they inevitably collapse into one of the three standard positions. Please write the proposal document itself. It should state the research question precisely, articulated so that it admits a determinate answer; explain why existing treatments of the regress leave this particular question open, especially where they treat justification as a property of individual propositional beliefs rather than of engineered error-propagation networks; outline the method—specifically, extracting dependency graphs from actual calibration protocols and comparing their formal properties to those that distinguish foundationalist, coherentist, and infinitist structures—and justify why this analytical approach is preferable to purely conceptual argumentation; specify what a successful result would demonstrate and what specific finding would count as failure; and provide an honest assessment of scope, showing how the project could be completed within a single workshop or seminar cycle. Please also separate what is broadly settled in the regress literature from what remains genuinely contested among specialists who work on it, and do not present any disputed interpretation as if it were the consensus view.
