I'm preparing material for a philosophy-of-time workshop and want a consistency audit of the position I've staked out in a dissertation chapter on cosmological arguments against temporal passage. Here are my current commitments:

(1) Gödel's 1949 rotating-universe solution is an exact, non-artifactual feature of general relativity: it permits globally closed timelike curves without violating the field equations.

(2) If a world containing such curves is metaphysically possible, then global A-theoretic presentism is false, because causal loops allow events that are future-relative-to-now to lie in the causal past of that now.

(3) I reject Gödel's further metaphysical conclusion that time itself is therefore an illusion; instead I defend a local, indexical form of presentism in which temporal passage is real at each worldline but need not be globally synchronized.

(4) I hold that scientific realism about general relativity requires treating every exact solution as physically possible unless an independent dynamical or boundary-condition argument, not mere observational absence, excludes it.

(5) I take the observed isotropy and absence of global rotation in our cosmos to furnish precisely such an independent exclusion: the Gödel metric is physically impossible for our universe, not merely uninstantiated.

(6) Despite (5), I maintain that the philosophical threat to A-theory from Gödel's metric survives, because modal arguments about what spacetime structures permit should not be defeated by contingent cosmological boundary conditions.

My question is whether (1)–(6) form a coherent package. In particular: does my local presentism in (3) genuinely answer the global objection in (2), or is it an ad hoc restriction that concedes the core point? Does the realism in (4) combine with the exclusion in (5) without contradiction, or does it secretly commit me to treating the Gödel universe as physically possible after all—which, given (2) and (6), would undermine my presentism? Is there a hidden tension between claiming that boundary conditions physically exclude the metric (5) while insisting that those same conditions do not defuse the modal argument (6)? Which pairs are genuinely incompatible, which only appear so, and what is the least costly revision—weakening (4), abandoning (6), or reconceiving (3) as a B-theoretic localism rather than presentism? Please rank the tensions by severity and indicate, for each, which commitment would have to be modified or dropped.
