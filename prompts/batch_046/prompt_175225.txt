I am a doctoral student in philosophy of religion and metaphysics, and I have been debating with a colleague in analytic metaphysics about how to read Al-Ghazālī's occasionalist arguments—specifically whether his denial of necessary causal connections in nature is best interpreted as a metaphysical elimination of secondary causes or as an epistemic limitation on human knowledge of divine habit. Rather than continue arguing past one another, we want to draft a joint adversarial-collaboration statement that both an occasionalist defending Al-Ghazālī and a dispositionalist defending intrinsic causal powers could genuinely sign. Present the document as exactly five numbered items, each one to two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. Item 1 should state the shared commitments: that deterministic materialism fails, that regularities in nature require explanation, and that God (or ultimate reality) sustains the existence of substances regardless of whether they possess independent causal efficacy. Item 2 should state the precise disagreement, with wording each side accepts for its own view, about whether created substances like fire bear an intrinsic power to burn or serve only as the occasion for direct divine action. Item 3 should identify the first crux—whether quantum-level stochastic indeterminacy supports occasionalism (by showing nature has no fixed powers) or undermines it (by showing intrinsic tendencies can be probabilistic)—and record each side's prediction about what confirmed, law-violating quantum anomalies would imply. Item 4 should identify the second crux—whether a complete physical description of combustion renders the causal claim redundant—phrased so the occasionalist predicts redundancy is impossible without divine reference and the dispositionalist predicts redundancy is possible if powers are fundamental. Item 5 should identify the third crux—whether occasionalism and dispositionalism are empirically distinguishable at all—and state what conceptual or empirical finding each side agrees would actually move them, including whether the dispute partly collapses into a verbal disagreement about the meaning of "cause." Write the response in American English.
