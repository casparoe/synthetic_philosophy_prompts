After forty years none of the original boards are still structural, but all the original wood is in teh adhesive. Maya said that means it's definately not the same statue anymore, since the material that made it up is now just filler in bonds, not arranged as the object. Leo said that's wrong: the wood never left the object, it's distributed through the structural matrix, and the form was maintained the whole time, so it's the same thing with replaced macro-parts.

Maya came back with: if being in the glue counts, then burning the old planks for pigment in the paint should count too, and there's no principled line. Leo said there's a real difference — glue is structurally integral, paint is superficial, and functional role is part of what makes material constitution work anyway. Maya said that's just smuggling form back in, which undermines the claim that material continuity is doing the work; in the classic statue-and-clay case the clay stays in one region, but here the original material is scattered, so it's more like destroying the statue and using its ashes to build a replica.

Leo replied that even in the statue case reshaping changes spatial arrangement, so material continuity was never about exact location, and if you allow reshaping you should allow redistribution within the same functional form. They asked me to referee because neither would budge.
