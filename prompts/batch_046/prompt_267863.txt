Please work through the following judgments about access consciousness, phenomenal consciousness, and global workspace architectures, then propose the principle—if any—that unifies them.

Case 1: A standard global workspace broadcasts visual information to working memory, language, and motor planning; the subject reports seeing a red square and behaves accordingly. Verdict: both access and phenomenal consciousness are present.

Case 2: The same information reaches working memory and guides behavior, but there is no associated phenomenal signature and the subject sincerely denies seeing anything. Verdict: access without phenomenal consciousness.

Case 3: The phenomenal signature is present, but the broadcast is blocked from working memory and language; the subject cannot report or act, yet later, under relaxation, describes a vivid red square. Verdict: phenomenal consciousness without access.

Case 4: A “fragmentary workspace” distributes information piecemeal to separate modules with no unified global state, yet each module reports a coherent experience. Verdict: I am uncertain whether this counts as genuine global access or as a unified phenomenal field.

Case 5: Both full access and the phenomenal signature are present, but the temporal binding that integrates the experience across roughly 300 ms is disrupted; the subject reports seeing the square but describes it as lying outside normal temporal thickness, lacking the usual flow of experience. Verdict: something essential to phenomenal consciousness seems absent despite both access and signature.

Is there a coherent principle—perhaps involving global availability together with a specific temporal-integrative condition—that captures these verdicts? If so, state it precisely and say what it predicts for a system with perfect access but 500 ms temporal fragmentation. If not, indicate which of my judgments should be overturned and why. Please argue from the cases themselves rather than appealing to bibliographies or external authorities.
