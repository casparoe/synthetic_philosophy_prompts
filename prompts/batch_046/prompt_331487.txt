My roommate cooked up this argument that hypocrisy can never actually cost you standing, and I'm suspicious because it feels like it proves way too much—like a blatant hypocrite still gets to blame everyone. (1) Hypocrisy undermines standing only if it violates a fairness condition: you're holding B to standard S but not applying S to yourself. (2) Applying S to yourself in the first-person is strucutrally different from applying S to B in the second-person—you're not assessing an object, you're living through the case—so the "same standard" can't be literally the same across the two perspectives. (3) So the fairness condition is impossibe to satisfy (or violate) from the inside; there's no coherent way for you to be treating yourself "the same" as B. (4) Therefore hypocrisy, as self-other asymmetry in judgment, is conceptually impossible. (5) So standing to blame is never lost through hypocrisy. Where's the cheat? I think (1) is basically right about fairness, but I don't know if I'm paying too high a price to save it.
