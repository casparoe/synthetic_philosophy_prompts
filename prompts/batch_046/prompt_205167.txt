Sure, so in the Meno Socrates compares true beliefs to statues of Daedalus that run away unless tied down with an account. That account is basically the modern justified-true-belief setup, so Plato is basically giving the JTB analysis. This tethering fixes the problem because once you have reasons your belief won't disappear.

As for modal fragility — that's when you're right only by luck, like guessing the number of stars. Tethering solves this too, because having an account means you didn't just get lucky, so your belief isn't fragile across possible worlds. So both problems are really the same: lack of justification makes belief unstable and fragile.
