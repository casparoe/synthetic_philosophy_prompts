I am drafting a long-form feature for readers who follow science and philosophy of mind but are not specialists, on whether symbolic representations can genuinely move the physical substrate that carries them. The discussion turns on two deliberately artificial cases—the careenium, a hypothetical material in which energy flow is allegedly governed by symbol-arrangement rather than by standard thermodynamic law, and the simmballs, entities that exist only inside a running simulation yet are claimed to exert causal influence over their simulated environment. I want a diagnostic taxonomy—structured as a branching decision tree—that sorts a reader by what she actually believes about these cases, not by which philosopher she names.

Please construct the tree according to the following specifications. Begin with a concrete commitment: when a symbol in the careenium is rearranged, does the substrate’s energy distribution change in a way that cannot be fully accounted for by the underlying physical dynamics of the material alone? The first branch should split on whether one accepts that there is any causal efficacy here that outstrips the substrate physics. The second branch should split on whether that efficacy, if present, belongs to the symbol’s semantic content, to its formal structure, or to an emergent organizational pattern that supervenes on both substrate and symbol. The third branch should apply the substrate-fungibility constraint: if the same symbolic organization could be realized in silicon, in hydraulic networks, or in the simulated environment of the simmballs without loss of causal profile, does that strengthen the case for symbol-driven causation or dissolve it into functional description?

Each internal node must be phrased as a question or claim that a reader can answer without already knowing which philosophical position it maps onto. The leaves must include at least the following named positions: hard substrate determinism (only physical law pushes, symbols are inert); semantic causal realism (the content of the symbol literally directs substrate behavior); formal structural emergence (the causal work is done by organizational form, fungible across substrates); and simulation-bound functionalism (simmballs have causal efficacy inside the simulation but none outside it, so symbols never push an external substrate). You must also include at least one unnamed leaf—an answer-combination that seems logically available but is apparently unoccupied—and briefly diagnose whether the vacancy is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect.

Where the tree cannot cleanly separate two named positions—for instance, if semantic causal realism and formal structural emergence collapse once substrate-fungibility is granted—flag that overlap explicitly rather than obscuring it. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the instrument should be fully usable by someone with no access to external sources. Keep the running examples grounded in the careenium and the simmballs, and maintain a tone appropriate for an intellectually serious general audience.
