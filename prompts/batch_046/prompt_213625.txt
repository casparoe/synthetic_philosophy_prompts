I've been working through problems in a course on modal metaphysics, and two members of my study group—Maya and Leo—got stuck on a hypothetical case they invented for a problem set. Imagine a device they call a "modal mirror" that does not copy information in the ordinary sense but establishes a direct phenomenal bridge between an original person in world W and a physically identical counterpart in world W*, such that the original feels the counterpart's sensations in real time and vice versa, while each retains separate bodies and fully isolated causal environments outside the bridge. Maya argued that this demonstrates transworld identity: the same conscious subject is instantiated in both worlds simultaneously, analogous to how a split-brain patient might have two hemispheres within one body. Leo countered that the link is merely an especially intimate form of causal connection between two numerically distinct individuals—counterparts—because if the body in W is destroyed, the one in W* survives with full continuity of experience, which would be impossible if they were literally one and the same person.

From there their exchange developed through several rounds. (1) Maya said that if consciousness can remain continuous across spatial separation—as in split-brain cases—then spatiotemporal location is not essential to personal identity, and the bridge reveals that the two bodies are simply different locations of one branching self. (2) Leo replied that split-brain continuity is still intra-corporeal, mediated by shared neural architecture, whereas the mirror connects two bodies in causally isolated worlds, so the experienced unity is artifactual rather than intrinsic. (3) Maya then claimed that the mirror does not transmit data but reveals a pre-existing modal essence: the original and counterpart share an essence that can be multiply instantiated, and the bridge makes that shared essence experientially manifest. (4) Leo answered that this merely relabels counterpart similarity as "shared essence," and that the independent survival of one after the other's destruction proves they are distinct existences linked only contingently; if they were truly identical across worlds, the destruction of one instantiation should affect the other. (5) Maya came back with the point that transworld identity does not require necessary co-existence—an object may exist in W and fail to exist in W*—so the survival of the W* body is exactly what her view predicts, not a refutation. (6) Leo concluded that if the relation depends on a technological bridge and permits independent destruction, then calling it "identity" rather than "counterpart relation augmented by a causal bridge" violates parsimony and obscures the underlying metaphysical structure.

Please adjudicate their exchange move by move: rate each of the six moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Then say who is more right overall about whether the case supports transworld identity or counterpart theory, and identify anything important that both sides are missing—perhaps regarding the nature of phenomenal unity, the role of causal isolation between worlds, or whether the "modal mirror" introduces a genuinely third category that neither standard view accommodates.
