I have recently become preoccupied with a problem at the intersection of synthetic biology and philosophy of mind: engineered gene-regulatory circuits—designed to detect heavy-metal contamination—exhibit behavior that looks representationally erroneous when they misfire in novel chemical environments, yet they clearly lack evolutionary histories that standard biosemantic theories treat as essential to original intentionality. This has made me skeptical of whether existing teleosemantic frameworks can accommodate derived misrepresentation in artifacts without either collapsing into full mentalism or diluting the normative force of malfunction. Before I settle on an account, I need the full specification of what any adequate naturalistic theory of mental content must actually deliver.

Write the desiderata that a teleosemantic theory must meet to count as genuinely naturalizing intentionality rather than redescribing it in biological vocabulary. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or distinction that makes it non-optional—such as the need to distinguish original from derived intentionality, to account for misrepresentation without circular appeals to prior mental content, to respect the boundary between statistical deviation and genuine malfunction in simple organisms, and to remain consistent with results from evolutionary biology about the plasticity of function—while flagging which demands are camp-specific preferences (for example, whether selection history is strictly necessary or whether any stable etiological function will suffice). Identify whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, particularly if accommodating artifact-based representation requires abandoning the very normativity that makes teleosemantics attractive for misrepresentation. Then, briefly, assess how a Millikan-style biosemantic approach and a more permissive etiological functionalism fare against your list, keeping the evaluation to a short scorecard rather than a full survey. I am not looking for a bibliography or reading list—just the criteria, the tensions among them, and the brief comparison.
