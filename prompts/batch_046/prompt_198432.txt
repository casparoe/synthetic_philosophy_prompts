ive been stuck on this old Malcolm vs Dennett debate about whether dreams are actual experiences and i think the stall is weirder than people admit. everyone knows Malcolm's line that you cant have experiences if youre not awake to have them, and Dennett's cassette idea that dreams are just post-hoc narratives loaded from memory traces during REM. but heres the thing: i think both sides are smuggling in different ontologies of sleep — Malcolm treating sleep as a temporary suspension of subjecthood so theres literally no one there to experience anything, Dennett treating the sleeping brain as still running subpersonal processing that just lacks first-person access. so is this disagreement even about consciousness, or is it about whether being-asleep counts as a mode of being-a-subject? and is there a hidden empirical question about NREM replay versus REM consolidation that neither side wants to admit depends on, or are they just answering different questions — one about grammatical authority of dream reports, one about information flow — under the single heading 'are dreams experiences'? i want an actual diagnosis: verbal, substantive, bedrock intuition, or misframed? and given that, what would actually move it — a distinction between phenomenal and access consciousness applied specifically to sleep states, or should we just accept that no argument will shift anyone?
