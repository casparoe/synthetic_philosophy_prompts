There's this hybrid disciplinary model some colleges are piloting: if you're caught plagiarizing you sit in front of a panel and professors tell you how dissapointed they are, but there's also a psychologist who can certify you lack the moral emotional wiring for that part, in which case you skip the shaming and just do an ethics module. What view of blame and reactive attitudes does that presuppose? Is it basically Strawsonian — that resentment is only fitting when the target can participate in the interpersonal — or does it smuggle in a capacity-based exemption Strawson wouldn't accept? And can the practice stay consistent if it treats the emotional session as optional but keeps the punishment the same either way, or does that split the difference between holding someone responsible and treating them like an object to be managed?
