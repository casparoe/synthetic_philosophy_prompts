In transitional-justice writing people keep using this group-therapy anology for South Africa's TRC: the nation is the traumatized patient, the hearings are the talking cure, perpetrators admitting crimes are like patients spitting out poison, and amnesty is the fee you pay so the cure can actually happen. Critics say that smuggles in a medical model treating structural violence like an individual neurosis, and it hides that the 'therapist' — the commision — had no power to prescribe anything except listening. Meanwhile Argentina's CONADEP was more like an autopsy: no amnesty, no healing story, just documenting the dead. Can you lay out what maps onto what, where the patient-therapist-poison framing breaks down (especially on collective agency and whether amnesty functions like a therapeutic fee), and whether the conclusion — that you need amnesty to get truth — survives once the disanalogies are visible? Please keep separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested, and don't present a contested view as the consensus — there's definitely no agreement on whether the TRC 'worked' as healing.
