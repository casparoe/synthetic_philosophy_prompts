I'm on my uni's digital ethics panel and we've got this awkward pilot programme to decide on by Thursday. The wellbeing office want to use anonymised Wi-Fi login and library swipe patterns — not what books people borrow, just when and where they log in — to automatically flag students who might need mental-health support. They claim it's all aggregated and de-identified, but the student union says that for international students from small countries the specific behavioural patterns of buildings they visit makes them practically re-identifiable, and that turning movement data into clinical risk profiles breaches contextual integrity because the information was recieved in an educational context, not a health-surveillance one. There's also this newer view that privacy works more like a collective good — an informational commons — rather than just an individual right you can waive, so even technically anonymous sorting can damage the group environment. I need a memo for the panel recommending whether we approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with clear reasoning that takes the strongest argument on the other side seriously. Write it as continuous prose only — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, just like an email or letter.
