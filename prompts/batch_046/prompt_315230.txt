ok so i'm a retired physicist who got into philosophy late and i'm trying to pin down where i stand on panpsychism and ethics before i talk to a grad student. here's my rough profile:

(1) i'm a physicalist — no souls, everything is physical — but i think consciousness is genuinely puzzling and not just a language mix-up.

(2) macroscopic panpsychism seems wrong to me (rocks don't feel), but i take seriously the idea that fundamental quantum fields or particle excitations might have a minimal 'what-it's-like' quality. not full awareness, just being that particular excitation.

(3) i think actual suffering needs more than that minimal quality. it needs intergrated information with a re-entrant loop — information feeding back on itself at least twice. one excitation can't do that, so it can't suffer.

(4) i think there's no sharp boundry in nature between having that loop and not having it; it's all continuous.

(5) i think moral consideration applies only to things that can suffer, so bare field excitations aren't in the moral circle.

(6) still, i think destroying even minimal experience has a kind of intrinsic negative value, like crushing a very simple organism.

(7) i think we should use a lab-coat precautionary principle in research: if we genuinely don't know whether we're harming something that might suffer, we should avoid or minimize the harm.

(8) i think high-energy physics experiments — smashing particles, altering field states — are ethically neutral, even though they affect countless fundamental entities, because none reach the loop threshold.

do these hang together? go through the pairs and tell me which ones are in real tension, which ones only look like tension but dissolve on inspection, and rank the real ones by severity. for each, say what the cheapest revision is and what it costs me. then tell me which single change would clean up the most problems at once. don't lecture me — just audit my commitments.
