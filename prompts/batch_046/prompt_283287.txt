I'm a PhD student in aesthetics preparing my prospectus on aesthetic testimony. Most discussions in the literature ask whether we can acquire aesthetic knowledge through others' judgments, but they typically assume the testimony tracks features that a listener could eventually verify through direct perceptual encounter. I want to push on a more extreme case and need an essay that works through it conceptually. Imagine a museum that displays abstract paintings with no titles, artist information, or wall text. Every visitor must form aesthetic judgments solely through the testimony of a single curator—let us call her the inverted guide—who systematically attributes to each work qualities that are opposite to those recognized by art historians and, apparently, opposite to what unmediated viewers initially perceive. For instance, she describes a warm, expansive canvas as cold and constricted. Over time, visitors come to genuinely see the works through her descriptions and develop what feels like direct aesthetic knowledge, despite lacking independent perceptual access to the properties art historians identify.

The controversy is this: does this scenario show that aesthetic testimony can transmit genuine knowledge even when it systematically misaligns with expert consensus and with the viewers' own unmediated perceptual access? Or does it instead demonstrate that testimony in aesthetics can only ever produce belief—specifically, a form of epistemic dependence on authority—because the misalignment reveals that the visitors are tracking the curator's credibility rather than the work's actual properties?

Write an essay that weighs the main arguments on both sides, paying particular attention to whether aesthetic knowledge requires some causal or perceptual connection to the object, how we should understand aesthetic disagreement in cases of radical testimony divergence, and whether the visitors' changed experience counts as genuine seeing-as or as a kind of suggestive framing. Reach and justify a definite conclusion: either that inverted testimony can yield aesthetic knowledge (and explain what this implies for standard pessimism about aesthetic testimony), or that it cannot (and explain why the appearance of knowledge is actually authority-dependent belief). Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; work through the conceptual issues directly.
