I have been working through an argument about whether relying on AI explanations undermines understanding, and it seems to collapse into an absurd generalization that I cannot accept. Here are the steps:

(1) Understanding a domain requires being able to reproduce its key reasoning steps without external aid.
(2) If an external source performs those reasoning steps in the user's place, then the user's understanding is eroded or never formed.
(3) A textbook performs those reasoning steps for the reader — it states conclusions and justifications that the reader could not yet derive alone.
(4) So if (2) holds, using textbooks should erode understanding.
(5) But textbook use is paradigmatically how understanding is acquired, not destroyed; therefore either (1) or (2) is false.
(6) Large language models also perform reasoning steps for the user and deliver conclusions.
(7) So if we reject (2) to save textbooks, we must concede that LLM use does not erode understanding either.
(8) Yet there is an apparent difference: textbook reasoning is static and fully inspectable, while LLM reasoning is opaque and reconstructed variably across queries. The argument treats them identically, but I want to preserve that difference without begging the question.

I would prefer to keep (1) and certainly want to keep (4) and (8). Which step should I reject, why did it look plausible, and what does each option cost? Specifically, can I distinguish textbooks from LLMs by appealing to opacity or interactivity without giving up the idea that understanding requires internal reproduction of reasoning?
