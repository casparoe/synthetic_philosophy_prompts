I'm defending the thesis that Plutarch's Parallel Lives should be read neither as defective history nor as straightforward moral exemplars, but as deliberately constructed "gradient hagiographies": the paired exaggeration of virtues and corresponding softening of vices creates ordinal moral distances between figures that train the reader in comparative practical judgment without imposing false cardinal precision about virtue's magnitude. I argue that this hagiographic distortion is structurally necessary to the pedagogical design of the paired biographies, not merely a rhetorical garnish, and that contemporary virtue ethics can recover this comparative practice—learning to navigate relative moral proximity—without being forced to accept Plutarch's specific value commitments or his metaphysics of character.
