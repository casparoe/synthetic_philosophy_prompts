I'm a junior in my university's Philosophy of Art and Race seminar and I sit on the Museum Acquisition and Curation Committee. Next Thursday we must vote on a conditional bequest: forty didactic posters and mural studies made between 1932 and 1954 by an obscure collective that called itself the Harlem Didactic Circle. The group explicitly rejected aesthetic autonomy and insisted that art must serve as propaganda for social truth; the bequest requires that the works be displayed permanently as Propaganda as Art: The Rejected Archive. Our charter directs acquisitions to foster aesthetic education and critical judgment rather than to function as instruments of political advocacy, though it offers no definition of that boundary. Half the committee holds that accepting the collection violates institutional neutrality and lowers curatorial standards, since the pieces lack conventional technique and were produced solely to advance a political program. The other half contends that exclusion repeats historical suppression of Black artistic voices and that, on Du Bois's view that all art is propaganda and that the relevant distinction is between truthful and false propaganda rather than between propaganda and art, the museum should preserve them. I need a formal opinion for circulation before Thursday recommending approval, rejection, or approval with conditions, within a specific time frame. The opinion should treat Du Bois's framework as a live criterion rather than historical background, should address the strongest version of the neutrality objection without dismissing it, and should specify concrete curatorial conditions if you recommend conditional acceptance. Please structure the response as follows: (1) Executive Summary and Recommendation (about 150 words); (2) The Philosophical Issue: Du Bois on Propaganda, Truth, and Artistic Value (about 250 words); (3) The Strongest Case for Rejection on Grounds of Institutional Neutrality (about 200 words); (4) The Case for Conditional Acceptance and the Least Costly Revision (about 200 words); (5) Concrete Recommendation with Time Frame and Curatorial Conditions (about 150 words).
