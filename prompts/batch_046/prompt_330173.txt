Compare approval-directed agents with goal-directed agents in the specific context of recursive self-modification, where the approval signal is generated not by an external human evaluator but by a frozen, earlier version of the agent's own value head. Discuss whether this internal-approval architecture avoids the instrumental convergence problems typical of goal-directed systems, or whether it merely relocates them: does the agent have an incentive to manipulate its earlier value head's predictions, to preserve that head's accessibility, or to distort the counterfactual evaluation conditions? Clarify whether the distinction between seeking approval and seeking a world-state collapses under self-reference, and identify which philosophical commitments about identity over time and about the nature of preferences are required for the comparison to remain stable.
