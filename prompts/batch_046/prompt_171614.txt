An epistemologist has given consistent verdicts on closure cases: she grants knowledge for straightforward deduction from a known, modally robust premise; denies it when the premise is true but modally fragile; denies it when the conclusion is accidentally true because the premise is false; grants it when the premise is true and robust despite an unused false belief in the causal background; and denies it when the reasoning chain contains a false intermediate lemma. Now apply her framework to this case: an agent acquires a true belief P via a method that tracks P in all nearby worlds; she competently deduces Q, which is true; her acceptance of P causally depends on a false belief R that plays no logical role in the deduction; and in nearby worlds where R is false she would still hold P and Q, but through a pathway that fails to track. Predict whether she knows Q, identify whether her implicit rule is Nozick-style tracking with closure permitted, tracking supplemented by a no-false-causal-antecedent condition, or modal safety applied to the entire belief-forming chain, and specify the minimal variant case that would separate the first two rules.
