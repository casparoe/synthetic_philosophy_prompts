You are an expert philosopher of science with deep familiarity with hinge epistemology, particularly Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and with the dynamics of methodological commitment in working science. Before you answer, take a few minutes to brainstorm the distinction between hinge propositions that stand fast, ordinary empirical beliefs, and Kuhnian paradigms, and consider how each behaves under anomalous evidence.

As a journalist reporting on scientific methodology, I have been tracking a recurring maneuver that I cannot place precisely within existing frameworks. In neuroimaging, for instance, many laboratories long operated with a foundational commitment—let us call it H1—that BOLD signal variations map directly onto localized neural activity in a way that licenses direct inference. This was not defended as a hypothesis among others; it functioned as bedrock. When replication failures and noise-model critiques accumulated, the community did not treat H1 as falsified in the ordinary sense, nor did they abandon the imaging framework entirely as in a paradigm revolution. Instead, H1 was temporarily demoted: it was treated as an explicit, testable empirical assumption, spawning extensive calibration studies, multivariate reconstructions, and artifact-correction protocols. After several years, many groups either reinstated a qualified version of H1—now bounded by stricter conditions—or replaced it with a different bedrock commitment about distributed pattern information rather than voxel-level mapping. A structurally similar pattern occurred in climate modeling around parameterizations of cloud feedback, where a methodological bedrock was suspended, surrounded by intermediate validation layers, and then either restored with qualifications or substituted.

The move I am trying to isolate is not ordinary belief revision, not a Duhem-Quine auxiliary defense, and not a full Kuhnian shift, because the commitment in question moves in and out of the standing-fast role itself. Is there an established concept in hinge epistemology or philosophy of science for this specific pattern—temporary demotion of a hinge to empirical status, followed by either re-establishment in qualified form or replacement? If such a concept exists, how does it differ from adjacent phenomena? If not, can you name and define the maneuver precisely, specifying the conditions that distinguish true hinge-demotion from mere weakening of belief, and identifying where the edges of the concept lie?
