I've been thinking about that old "why Britain and not China" question — the Needham problem, or whatever they call it now — and it feels like historians have been stuck in the same loop for ages. Some people say coal and colonies did it, others say it was property rights and instutions, and nobody seems to budge. But lately I'm wondering if the stall isn't really about those facts at all, but about whether the comparision is even fair — Britain is a small island with one government, China is this massive empire with totally different internal dynamics, so are we comparing apples and oranges? Maybe one side is secretly answering "why this region" and the other is answering "why this empire," and they don't realize they're talking past each other. Is that what's going on — a hidden disagreement about what counts as the right unit of comparision? Or is there actually an empirical question buried in there that could be settled? And if the framing is the problem, what would progress even look like — picking different regions, or just admitting the whole contrastive question is badly posed?
