Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a scalar phenomenalist and a discontinuity theorist on whether moral patienthood is genuinely gradational across insects, artificial feedback systems, and thermostats. The scalar phenomenalist holds that moral status tracks the richness and integration of phenomenal experience continuously across biological and artificial architectures, so insects possess partial but real moral status and highly integrated homeostatic systems pose genuine calibration problems rather than category errors. The discontinuity theorist holds that direct moral status requires a specific normative interest-bearing capacity that depends on temporal self-modeling, producing a sharp, non-arbitrary threshold below which entities—including insects and certainly simple thermostats—have exactly zero direct moral status, though they may retain indirect, ecological, or instrumental value.

Structure the document in four parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims both can sign, covering: the empirical state of evidence on nociception and integrative neural processing in insects; the functional architecture of simple feedback loops in thermostats and analogous artificial systems; their shared rejection of the view that the dispute is purely verbal; and their agreement on what kinds of empirical or conceptual findings would be relevant without being individually decisive. Second, a disagreements section in which each disagreement is phrased in wording its own side accepts—especially on whether comparing insects to thermostats reveals a reductio of gradualism or a misapplication of moral concepts, and on whether the substitutability of functionally identical copies undermines claims about intrinsic moral value. Third, the cruxes: identify at least two, including whether phenomenal richness without temporal continuity can ground normative interests, and whether ecological or relational obligations can fully substitute for direct moral status in guiding our treatment of sub-threshold entities. Fourth, for each crux, specify a concrete case or argument both sides accept as bearing on it—such as a hypothetical artificial system with minimal information integration but no temporal model, and an ecological intervention protecting an insect population without appealing to individual welfare—together with each side’s explicit, advance prediction of what that case should show.

Write this as a committed statement: take definite positions in formulating the disagreements, do not present the dispute as merely verbal without argument, clearly distinguish substantive disagreement from terminological choice, and state your reasons for any judgment that one framing is more precise. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not depend on obscure empirical findings or niche theoretical labels that a well-informed reader could not be expected to know without searching; and write in American English.
