I am writing for my philosophy seminar on agency and determinism. We have studied Sphexishness—the mechanical rigidity of the digger wasp that repeats its burrow-checking loop whenever the prey is moved—but I want to go further than the textbook example. Could you write a villanelle (nineteen lines, ABA rhyme, two refrains) voiced by a wasp that suddenly suspects its repetition is not pure instinct but a kind of captivity? Use as your two refrains the lines I drag the prey to the edge and check the hole and But what if the loop is not the world but me? Let the form's cyclical returns mirror the compulsive checking behaviour, and let the closing couplet—where both refrains finally meet—leave open whether breaking free is possible or whether that very suspicion is only another programmed step. It should be precise enough that someone familiar with the debate about rigidity, flexibility, and free will recognises the tension rather than just the name. After the poem, please include a section titled Commentary on choices (around 150 words) explaining where the poem takes liberties with the actual ethology of Sphex and stating exactly what argument or observation would change your mind about whether the wasp's loop genuinely parallels human determinism.
