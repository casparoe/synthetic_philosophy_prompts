I'm preparing a decision-tree taxonomy for an advanced seminar in normative ethics that focuses on what I have been calling the delegation paradox within deontology: the tension between agent-centered prohibitions on harming and the moral difference between direct bodily causation and the encoding of harm-causing rules into an artifact or institutional mechanism. I want the instrument to sort positions not by the labels theorists prefer, but by the answers practitioners would actually give to concrete cases, so that a student can locate her view without already knowing whether she is a Kantian, a threshold deontologist, or a rights-based contractualist. The root commitment is fixed: there exist agent-centered moral restrictions that are not reducible to outcome-optimization. From there, the tree should branch through three diagnostic questions that genuinely divide the logical space. First, does an agent-centered restriction apply with equal force when harm is mediated by an authored system—such as programming an autonomous vehicle's unavoidable-collision algorithm, or drafting public-health triage protocols—rather than being physically direct, as in the classic footbridge case? Second, if mediation weakens or transforms the restriction, is the decisive factor the absence of immediate bodily causation, the presence of distributed consent among those affected, or the scale differential between harm caused and harm prevented? Third, does the agent retain a distinctive moral residue—some form of taint, repair-obligation, or diminished standing—after producing harm through the delegated mechanism, and does that residue attach differently to omissions versus commissions within the institutional structure?

The deliverable should be a branching tree with nodes phrased as answerable questions, leading to named leaves (strict Kantian absolutism, a Scheffler-style hybrid, threshold deontology, rights-based contractualism, and any others that occupy the space) as well as cells marked "unnamed / unoccupied," each with a brief diagnosis of why the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. I need explicit flagging of any pair of named positions the tree cannot genuinely separate—my suspicion is that some hybrids collapse into one another once both mediation and residue are admitted. Every branch should be built from the logical combinations of answers rather than from a pre-existing list of authors, and the concrete cases used to illustrate the nodes must include the autonomous-vehicle programming scenario and a case involving a public-health official setting vaccination-triage rules, in addition to the familiar switch and footbridge variations. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation requests, since the purpose is conceptual sorting for students who already know the canonical cases but need to see how deontological logic extends to institutional design; also flag any point where judgment cannot be eliminated and an answerer must simply decide.
