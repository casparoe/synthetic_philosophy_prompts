In his published queries on money, banking, and national wealth, George Berkeley rendered a series of judgments that can be reconstructed as follows. First, he treated gold and silver coin as a legitimate counter because its value is intrinsic and portable, not dependent on a sovereign’s promise. Second, he rejected shares in speculative joint-stock ventures—such as those of the South Sea Company—as suitable for monetary use, precisely because they lack tangible backing and encourage trading divorced from real goods. Third, he endorsed a proposal for an Irish bank issuing paper notes, but only on the condition that every note be secured by mortgaged land and stored commodities, with no reliance on government credit or anticipated future revenue. Fourth, he condemned the steady export of bullion from Ireland to England, treating the physical loss of specie as a depletion of national wealth even when domestic paper circulation remained unchanged. Fifth, he allowed that private bills of exchange might temporarily serve as instruments of settlement among merchants, provided they eventually resolve into specie or deliverable goods and do not become permanent circulating media.

Consider now a novel scheme: a decentralized, non-state digital currency whose tokens are generated exclusively upon verification of performed reforestation labor, with algorithmic rules that permanently block lending, speculation, or interest-bearing transactions; the tokens circulate globally but are redeemable only for goods within participating local cooperatives.

Predict Berkeley’s verdict on whether this scheme qualifies as legitimate money by his lights. Identify at least two distinct rules that fit his five earlier judgments equally well but diverge on this case—for instance, one requiring physical tangibility in the backing asset, one requiring that every counter be anchored to productive labor or land improvement, and one permitting any functional counter so long as speculative use is structurally impossible. State what each rule predicts about the scheme, then take a committed position on which rule Berkeley is actually running and what he would therefore say, giving your reasons rather than leaving the choice open. Finally, specify the single further case—concerning either the physical form of the backing, the geographic scope of circulation, or the possibility of secondary markets—that would best discriminate between the rules you have identified.
