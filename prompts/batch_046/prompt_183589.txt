My goal is to teach them the species problem as a conceptual tension rather than a taxonomic failure: specifically, the conflict between the continuous, branching nature of evolutionary change and the categorical demands of biological classification. Design the session's explanatory arc: state the preliminary conceptual distinction that must be established before the problem becomes visible—for instance, the difference between describing a pattern of variation and identifying a causal-process boundary—and explain why this distinction is a dependency for everything that follows. Specify the carrying example, preferably a constructed or generic scenario rather than a famous named case, that must exhibit gradual phenotypic divergence, intermediate reproductive connectivity across adjacent populations, and discrete clustering at the extremes; explain precisely what this example must have and what it must lack in order to generate the tension rather than resolve it. Provide a precise check for understanding that distinguishes genuine comprehension of the philosophical tension from mere discomfort with ambiguous taxonomy, and explicitly list what should be left out at this level—such as formal debates about species pluralism, specific named theorists, or technical frameworks in metaphysics of science—with a brief justification for each omission.
