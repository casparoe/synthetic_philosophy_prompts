Draft an exam essay question for an upper-level philosophy of mind or epistemology course that uses a modified Sleeping Beauty scenario to probe self-locating belief. The scenario should run as follows: Beauty is told that a fair coin will be flipped on Sunday; if heads, she is awakened once on Monday, and if tails she is awakened on Monday and, after being put back to sleep with her memory erased, awakened again on Tuesday. Now add the twist that the experimenters have a hidden protocol: on tails, whether she is awakened on Tuesday depends on the answer she gives when questioned on Monday—if she answers A she is not awakened again, but if she answers B she is awakened twice as usual, while on heads the protocol is irrelevant and she is always awakened once. Ask students to determine her rational credence in heads upon first awakening, whether her answer choice on Monday should be guided by that credence, and whether this strategic dependency undermines the standard argument that self-locating belief requires treating centered worlds as equally weighted. Provide a model answer of roughly 600 words and a marking scheme that separates excellent answers from adequate ones across four dimensions: the credence calculation, the strategic interaction between belief and protocol, the evaluation of the self-locating principle, and the diagnosis of where the standard half-er response goes wrong. Present everything in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as one would write in a letter.
