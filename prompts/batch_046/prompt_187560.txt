My colleague in philosophy of mind has offered the following verdicts on whether various entities possess phenomenal consciousness: awake neurotypical adult humans, yes; cows and octopuses, yes; fish and solitary insects, no; a beehive collectively solving a multi-stage navigation problem that exceeds any individual bee's capacity, no; a human in deep, dreamless slow-wave sleep, no; a decorticate mammal retaining intact limbic and brainstem structures, yes; a patient in a minimally conscious state with preserved sleep-wake cycles but no language or goal-directed behavior, yes; a cerebral organoid displaying spontaneous cortical oscillations but lacking sensory input, motor output, or a body, no; a current large language model generating coherent dialogue, no.

The new case is an engineered neural-biological hybrid: a fused thalamic-cortical organoid integrated with a robotic sensorimotor loop, which learns predictive models of its environment, exhibits global slow-wave oscillations during rest periods, and corrects errors in its behavior, but was constructed de novo with no evolutionary history and no homeostatic drives.

Predict her verdict on whether this hybrid is conscious. Identify at least two distinct rules—such as one centered on global information integration or workspace broadcasting and one centered on evolutionary embodiment or homeostatic need—that are each jointly consistent with her full track record but diverge on this case. State which rule you think she is actually running, defend that reading by pointing to the specific prior judgments that select for it, and then propose one further case that would most sharply distinguish between the rules you have isolated.
