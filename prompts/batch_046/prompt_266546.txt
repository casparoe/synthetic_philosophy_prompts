The summary concerns Berkeley's Querist and claims the following: that Berkeley treats money as nothing more than a conventional token or counter, with gold and silver valued only by collective agreement, comparable to a shared password with no intrinsic worth. From this premise the essay allegedly derives that national wealth is essentially proportional to the quantity of currency circulating, so that if Ireland issued more paper notes it would automatically become richer, since wealth just is the presence of these tokens. The summary further contends that Berkeley extends his immaterialist metaphysics into economics, treating banknotes not as representations of real labor or goods but as mental items existing only in the minds of bankers, which implies that inflation and banking crises are essentially perceptual errors arising from collective forgetfulness that money is imaginary. Finally, it concludes that Berkeley is a direct precursor to modern fiat-currency theory and that his philosophy of mind strictly entails his monetary views.

Some elements here strike me as suspicious—particularly the idea that Berkeley equated wealth with the volume of currency, which seems to conflict with his well-known emphasis on labor, land, and actual production—but I am not sufficiently familiar with the Querist to pinpoint every distortion. Is this summary fair? Please provide a precise accuracy audit: distinguish what Berkeley genuinely argued about money as a token distinct from wealth, what represents a defensible popular simplification, and what are serious misrepresentations, especially regarding the application of immaterialism to banknotes and the alleged proportionality between paper money and national prosperity. I want you to take a definite position rather than balancing options neutrally: tell me whether the essay's claim that Berkeley's metaphysics entails his economics survives correction, explain exactly where the causal or conceptual link breaks down if it does, and identify the single most misleading move in the summary that a reader should watch out for.
