I am preparing the final examination for an upper-level seminar on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. I need a single essay question that candidates should be able to answer in approximately 75 minutes, with a total word budget of roughly 1,000 to 1,200 words for the answer. The question must not be answerable by summarizing familiar illusionist positions — for instance, by reciting standard heterophenomenological or introspective-illusion accounts. Instead, it should require students to work through a constructed conceptual framework that is not part of the standard literature.

Present the following scenario in the exam prompt: consider a cognitive architecture in which phenomenal properties are not eliminated in a binary fashion, but are subjected to graded phenomenal attenuation. In this model, a parameterized mechanism gradually reduces the introspective salience of a phenomenal property without removing the functional machinery that generates first-person reports about it. The subject continues to produce structurally intact reports — grammatically complete assertions that there is something it is like to undergo the state — but these reports become progressively hollow: they retain syntactic form and causal role in reasoning, yet lack the semantic anchoring that would normally link them to an actual qualitative character. Crucially, the attenuation operates along a continuum rather than as an on-off switch.

The essay question should ask students to do three things. First, explain why this graded model might seem to strengthen illusionism compared with a binary elimination view, particularly with respect to explaining the persistence of first-person authority. Second, argue whether the model genuinely supports illusionism or whether it covertly reintroduces phenomenal properties as real but merely obscured — and identify exactly which premise in the illusionist argument the continuum version threatens to undermine. Third, evaluate what the graded attenuation framework implies for the epistemic status of introspective reports: if the illusion is not present versus absent but operates in degrees, can first-person authority be preserved in any non-trivial form, or does the continuum collapse the distinction between knowing that one is in error and being unable to recognize the error?

Please provide:
1. The exam question itself, written in formal academic prose and including the scenario description, with clear instructions to the candidate about the three tasks.
2. A model answer of approximately 800 to 900 words that demonstrates the conceptual precision required.
3. A marking scheme allocating 25 marks total, divided among five must-hit analytical moves or distinctions, with the mark value for each specified. For each point, include a one-line description of the characteristic error or omission that should cause marks to be lost.

The marking scheme should separate excellent answers from adequate ones not by breadth of reference but by the specificity with which the answer locates the graded attenuation model relative to three distinctions: the metaphysical claim that phenomenal consciousness is illusory; the epistemic claim that introspective access is unreliable; and the methodological claim that first-person reports retain authority even under illusion. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or instructions to cite specific texts. If you refer to broader positions, keep the references generic rather than attributing precise formulations to named authors with page citations.
