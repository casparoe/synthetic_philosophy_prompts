Consider the published comparative verdicts of an unnamed reviewer—working at the intersection of Theravada Abhidhamma moral psychology and normative ethics—on four cases that pit Buddhist assessments of kamma against act-utilitarian judgments. The system optimizes for expected quality-adjusted life-years, but possesses no mental states, intentions, or awareness. Would the reviewer hold that the Buddhist assessment converges with the utilitarian approval (because suffering is minimized), diverges by treating the outcome as karmically null (because no cetana is present), or diverges in a different direction—perhaps by attributing indirect karmic residue to the engineers who designed the system?
