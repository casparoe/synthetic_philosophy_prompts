So two people from my rationalist-adjacent meetup got into it over Braudel's longue durée framework — the geography/climate/slow structures vs events and individuals thing. I just listened and didn't take sides.

(1) Jules said Braudel's tripartite split is basically geographic determinism with extra steps. He treats mountains, coastlines, and climate patterns as autonomous 'structures' that constrain human action for centuries, but modern environmental history shows these are co-produced with societies — there's no pure geographic layer independent of practice. So the distinction between long duration and event is conceptually broken from the start.

(2) Riley said that's a strawman. Braudel never claimed geography determines culture; he said geographic rhythms are so slow they form a background constraint within which faster social and political rhythms unfold. Bidirectional causality doesn't erase the temporal gap, it just complicates it.

(3) Jules came back with another point: even on it's own terms the taxonomy fails. The 4.2 kiloyear aridification was a 'geographic' climate shift, yet it collapsed the Akkadian Empire in decades — faster than plenty of dynastic crises Braudel'd dismiss as surface 'events.' If the deep layer can outpace the middle layer, there's no principled boundary, just historian preference.

(4) Riley replied that Jules is conflating climate shocks with geographic structure. Braudel's deepest layer isn't 'all climate'; it's durable material conditions — soil chemistry, bathymetry-driven currents, crop biogeography like olive vs wheat zones — that don't flip in a generation. The 4.2 event belongs to conjoncture (the intermediate term), not geographical time. But Jules is missing that the framework is about the inertia of material life, not a labeling system for every environmental change.

Can you adjudicate this move by move? Rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right with brief justification. What's both sides missing — maybe about whether Braudel intended this as ontology vs methodology, or how the Anthropocene blurs the lines? I'd definately like to recieve a point-by-point breakdown.
