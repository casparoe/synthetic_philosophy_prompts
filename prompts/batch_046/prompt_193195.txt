I have been thinking through the utility-monster scenario—the case in which one being could derive so vastly more well-being from resources that distributing everything to it would maximise total utility. Rather than accepting the familiar reply that we ought simply to reject interpersonal utility comparisons, I want to defend a different claim: the scenario is conceptually incoherent because it treats well-being as a poolable substance, like water in a tank. Drawing on the thought that certain human capacities only make sense within a shared social framework—much as a private language is said to be impossible—I argue that well-being cannot be radically concentrated in a single being without losing the very conditions that make it intelligible. So the monster is not a terrifying moral possibility but a category mistake.
