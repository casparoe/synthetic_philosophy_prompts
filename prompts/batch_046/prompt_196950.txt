Draft a 90-minute essay examination for an advanced jurisprudence seminar focused on self-amending rules and paradoxes of legal self-reference. Present students with a hypothetical constitutional framework—the Nomic Order—containing two provisions: Article I vests an unlimited constituent authority in a designated body to amend any provision of the framework, including Article I itself; Article II is an entrenched procedural clause that prohibits any amendment removing or suspending the supermajoritarian and temporal requirements for valid constitutional change. Require students to analyze whether an amendment that invokes Article I to repeal Article II, and then immediately uses the resulting unencumbered authority to alter the amendment rules retroactively, is legally valid, and to defend their answer by engaging with three analytical distinctions: the separation of hierarchical levels of norms; the temporal logic of validity, specifically whether legality is assessed at enactment or at the moment of application; and the difference between logical contradiction and normative conflict. Provide a model answer of approximately 800 to 1,000 words demonstrating how each distinction resolves or complicates the paradox. Include a marking scheme totaling 25 points that allocates specific weights to each analytical distinction and lists precise conceptual errors warranting deductions—such as treating constituent power as a purely sociological fact rather than a normative competence, conflating self-amendment with logical contradiction, or assuming that entrenchment is automatically self-defeating without considering dynamic reconstructions of the amendment rule. The question should be unanswerable by mere summary of standard formulations; it should test whether candidates can apply structural reasoning to an unfamiliar constitutional design.
