In my phil of perception class we're stuck on Berkeley's master argument — the bit where he claims you can't concieve of a tree existing unperceived without putting it back in your mind, so matter is impossible. One camp says it's just a use/mention slip with 'conceive'; the other says it reveals mental acts are necessarily self-involving, like some built-in phenominal transparency. We've argued for weeks and nobody budges. Is this actually a substantive disagreement about the ontology of mental acts, or is it verbal — like one side using 'conceive' in a propositional-attitude sense and the other in a phenomenological one? And if it's verbal, could a distinction between object-directedness and meta-representational awareness break the deadlock, or is the stall just proof the whole thing rests on prior intuitions about immaterialism? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not another recap of Hylas and Philonous.
