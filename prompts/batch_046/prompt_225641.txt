As a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, I am auditing an upper-level undergraduate seminar on epistemology and metaphysics. For my final project, I must design one in-class essay examination that tests whether students can actually operate with transcendental reasoning rather than merely recite canonical names such as Kant or Strawson. To keep the assessment from being answerable by pre-training regurgitation, I want it centered on a synthetic, non-canonical scenario: a globally distributed, version-controlled knowledge repository—analogous to an open-source wiki with immutable audit logs—where users propose factual corrections. The argument to evaluate claims that for such a correction to be rational rather than merely causal, participants must already presuppose a shared normative framework of epistemic accountability, a framework that cannot itself be derived from any particular entry or commit history in the repository. The exam prompt should ask students to determine whether this reasoning succeeds as a transcendental argument for intersubjective norms, and to identify the precise step where it is most vulnerable to collapsing into an empirical claim about institutional design or group dynamics.

Please deliver the following, with no bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific texts: (1) the essay prompt itself, calibrated for a 45-minute sitting with an expected response of roughly 1,200 to 1,500 words; (2) a model answer that lists the specific philosophical moves a top-scoring response must execute—specifically, isolating the condition-of-possibility structure, distinguishing transcendental premises from empirical ones, assessing whether the presupposition is genuinely necessary rather than contingent, and locating the risk of circularity or over-generalization; and (3) a marking scheme totaling 30 marks that assigns points to each of those moves and explicitly catalogs the misunderstandings that should cost marks, such as treating the argument as an induction from observed collaborative behavior, conflating normative presupposition with software-enforced rules, or mistaking the audit-log mechanism for the transcendental condition itself. The difficulty should come from the crux of the reasoning, not from recall of labels.
