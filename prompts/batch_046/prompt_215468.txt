Write a two-page research proposal for a semester-long philosophy project (approximately 7,000–9,000 words) on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility. Frame the proposal around a precise, underexplored question: if Strawson's argument shows that no agent can be ultimately responsible for an action because no agent can be responsible for the way they are, does the same logic also undermine what we might call "transmissive" responsibility—the kind of responsibility institutions or collective agents bear for outcomes that flow from, but are not originated by, individual actions—or is it restricted to "originative" responsibility alone?

State the question so that it admits a determinate answer. Explain why the existing discussion leaves it open: standard treatments of the Basic Argument focus almost entirely on individual moral agents and either defend or reject the claim that ultimate responsibility is impossible, but they rarely ask whether the argument's modal premises about self-creation apply analogously to collective entities that inherit, rather than generate, the conditions of their agency. Describe the approach you will take: first, conceptually distinguish originative responsibility (grounded in an agent's direct authorship of the conditions of action) from transmissive responsibility (grounded in an entity's capacity to inherit, channel, or reform those conditions); second, apply Strawson's premises about causal history and self-creation to both categories; third, test whether the argument overgeneralizes by examining whether collective agents can ever satisfy a modified version of the self-creation condition through institutional reform or restructuring.

Specify the possible conclusions and what would decide between them. One outcome is that the Basic Argument targets only originative responsibility, leaving institutional and collective blame structurally untouched. Another is that it applies with equal force to transmissive responsibility, which would require a substantial revision of how we assign accountability to organizations and groups. A third is that the originative/transmissive distinction collapses upon analysis, revealing that the Basic Argument needs reformulation rather than restriction. Identify the considerations that would adjudicate these outcomes—particularly the causal structure of collective decision-making, whether institutional self-reform can count as a form of self-creation, and whether transmissive responsibility requires a different normative foundation altogether.

Give an honest assessment of scope and risk for a single semester. Note that the conceptual analysis of the two responsibility-types and their application to Strawson's premises is fully tractable, but that developing a complete theory of collective agency or group intentionality is not, and should be bracketed with clear boundary conditions. Say what would count as failure: for instance, if the distinction between originative and transmissive responsibility turns out to be merely verbal, or if collective agency proves so conceptually contested that no determinate answer about the argument's scope is possible within the time available.
