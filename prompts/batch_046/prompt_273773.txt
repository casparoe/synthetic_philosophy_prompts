For a philosophy-of-science research agenda, lay out the desiderata that a viable structural-realist account of inter-theoretic relations must meet if it is to avoid both the access problem that undermines epistemic structural realism and the ontological inflation that undermines standard scientific realism. For each desideratum, state the motivating consideration—e.g., accommodating the pessimistic meta-induction, preserving empirical equivalence with predecessor theories in observable domains, or providing a non-miraculous explanation of predictive success—then mark which are non-negotiable constraints and which reflect camp-specific commitments (such as rejection of intrinsic properties, insistence on a specific formal apparatus for representing structures, or determinism about structural continuity). In particular, examine whether a jointly unsatisfiable cluster exists: specifically, whether requiring that structural preservation hold over functional dependencies among unobservable entities, rather than over purely formal mathematical mappings, can be reconciled with the demand that the account remain observationally indistinguishable from its realist rivals. If these two requirements conflict, identify which should be relaxed and price the conceptual cost of that relaxation—what explanatory power or ontological parsimony is sacrificed. The deliverable should be a defended specification, not a survey of existing positions, and should fix the purpose as guiding a research group with limited time and no need for bibliographic coverage.
