I am preparing the conceptual framework for a graduate seminar on induction, and I would like an internal audit of the following set of commitments, which I take to be jointly defensible but fear may not be. Here is my profile. (1) I am a strict Humean about natural law: universal generalizations are lawlike only in virtue of being systematically interrelated with other true generalizations, and there are no metaphysically necessary connections between distinct existences. (2) I regard Goodman's new riddle of induction as a genuinely unsolved problem for any account that seeks a non-circular, epistemic justification of inductive projection, and I reject appeals to "natural properties" or intrinsic qualitative differences as solutions. (3) I resolve the riddle pragmatically by appealing to predicate entrenchment: "green" is projected rather than "grue" solely because it is more deeply embedded in our actual linguistic practice, and I treat entrenchment as a purely descriptive, non-normative feature—had our linguistic ancestors entrenched "grue," our inductive practices would have centered on it with no accompanying cognitive error. (4) Despite this anti-realism about projectibility, I hold that there is a mind-independent fact about whether emeralds instantiate a uniform chromatic property across time, though I deny that this fact can serve as a philosophical justification for induction. (5) I maintain that scientific laws must support counterfactuals, and I agree with modal critics that a bare regularity theory cannot explain this feature without introducing modal primitives that I explicitly reject. (6) I grant that the evolutionary advantage of tracking surface reflectance in the approximately 520–570 nm range explains the historical entrenchment of "green," but I insist this gives induction only a causal genealogy, not a normative foundation. (7) Finally, I think the problem of induction is philosophically insoluble yet practically dissolved: working scientists are fully rational to project uniformity, even in the absence of any philosophical warrant.

Please examine this profile for joint tenability. Identify every pair of commitments that pulls against the others, ordering them by severity. For each tense pair, specify what is at stake, how severe the conflict is, and what the cheapest single revision would be to restore consistency. Be careful to distinguish genuine tensions from merely apparent ones—some pairs look incompatible but dissolve once the distinctions between metaphysical, epistemic, and pragmatic levels are kept clear. After surveying the pairs, tell me which one revision—giving up or weakening exactly one commitment—would clean up the greatest number of tensions at the lowest overall cost to the view. Do not provide bibliographies or reading lists; I want only the logical audit.
