I have just spent an hour arguing with a friend about whether the gender-equality paradox undermines liberal feminism or merely complicates it, and I want to check that I am grading past papers consistently rather than letting my own view colour the marks. Below is an exam question I set and one student response.

Question: Sociologists observe that in several highly gender-egalitarian societies occupational segregation by gender is more pronounced than in less egalitarian ones — the so-called gender-equality paradox. Three explanations are prominent. The resource hypothesis holds that economic security allows people to pursue gender-typical preferences shaped by culture rather than by material necessity. The socialisation-amplification view holds that egalitarian norms can make gender identity more salient, not less, so that freedom from legal constraint actually intensifies rather than dissolves gendered choice. A third position argues that measures of “equality” track legal rights rather than lived social pressure, so the paradox may be partly an artefact of what is being measured. Evaluate these three explanations and say what each implies about whether justice requires reducing occupational segregation.

Student answer: The resource hypothesis proves that once welfare states remove economic pressure, innate biological preferences emerge. In Scandinavia women cluster in nursing and teaching while men dominate engineering, not because society pushes them, but because freedom reveals nature. This shows liberal feminism was wrong: culture suppresses natural differences, and equality legislation exposes them. The socialisation-amplification view is incoherent, because if egalitarian cultures made gender more salient they would also suppress it; one cannot have it both ways. As for measurement, legal rights are the correct metric, so the paradox is real and justice should accept different outcomes rather than enforce statistical parity. Therefore the paradox supports a conservative view of gender roles.

Please assess this answer with a definite mark out of ten and justify every point with specific, point-by-point comments. Be committed: take a clear position on whether the answer passes or fails, and identify precisely where the reasoning slips — particularly any conflation of culturally shaped preferences with innate biological drives, and any misrepresentation of how egalitarian norms might interact with gender identity — rather than offering a balanced survey of possible interpretations. Your verdict should state clearly whether the student has grasped the logical space of the debate and what a correct evaluation of the resource hypothesis actually requires.
