Write a short dialogue between two philosophy grad students — call them A and B — arguing about Benatar's asymmetry. A defends it: absence of suffering is good even without a subject, absence of pleasure isn't bad without a subject. B is skeptical and thinks this creates a weird bias toward never creating anyone. They should actually respond to each other's points, not talk past one another. Specifically, B should press the "potental child" problem: if not having a kid spares them suffering (good?) but doesn't deprive them of joy (neutral?), then non-procreation is always at least neutral, never bad — so why ever have kids? A needs to answer that without just saying "we like kids." They should also disagree on whether the asymmetry is about value or about metaphysical conditions for being harmed. Keep it casual, sharp, no textbook language. Include this edge case: a being that lives for exactly one second and feels only mild pleasure, then dies. Does the asymmetry give us any reason to create it? Don't cite books or page numbers.
