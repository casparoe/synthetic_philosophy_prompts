I serve on the certification board of our city’s Community Reciprocity Council. We are not scholars, but several of us have been thinking through classical distinctions between managing a household for use and pursuing exchange as an end in itself, and we have tried to apply those distinctions to our charter reviews. A local cooperative has submitted a proposal for a dual-account exchange network: members may open Household Accounts for direct, need-based barter of goods and labor, and separately open Venture Accounts through which surplus goods or labor are pooled into shared workshops. These workshops generate further goods, and members may draw from them in proportion to their pooled contribution multiplied by an agreed productivity factor. The cooperative argues that both accounts serve community flourishing; critics on our board argue that the Venture mechanism permits accumulation without a natural ceiling, turning exchange into an unlimited pursuit rather than a subordinate means, which would violate our founding principle.

We must issue a formal recommendation memo by next week: approve the charter with both account types permitted; approve it only if Venture Accounts are removed or structurally capped; or reject the proposal outright. The memo should be written so the cooperative can respond with concrete amendments rather than broad philosophy. Please draft the board’s decision memo. State a clear recommendation, defend it with reasoning that a lay board can stand behind in public, and honestly engage the strongest argument in favor of allowing Venture Accounts—specifically, that shared workshops could reduce waste, preserve skills, and strengthen local resilience—rather than dismissing it. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; we need reasoning we can verify and defend ourselves without relying on texts we have not examined together.
