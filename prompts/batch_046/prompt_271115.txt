Hey folks—essay prompt for next tuesday. I want you to dig into Zhuangzi on death, but skip the usual death-is-just-transformation-like-caterpillars summary we've all seen a million times. Focus on this tension: he treats death as a natural transformation with no persistant self that gets lost, yet he also describes fasting the mind and the pipes of heaven producing sounds that come and go without anyone owning them. So here's the controversey—if there's really no self to survive, why does he still talk about friendship and emotional response? Is grieving for a dead friend necessarily a failure to understand transformation, or can grief itself be part of the transformation rather than resistance to it? Write an essay weighing whether Zhuangzi's view commits him to saying grief is irrational, or whether he can allow grief without betraying his metaphyics. Reach a conclusion and defend it; don't just say it's complicated. Hard cap: 450 words max, three paragraphs tops. Definately don't cite outside sources—just reason it out yourself.
