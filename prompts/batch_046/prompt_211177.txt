Take the Bristol Colston statue being pulled down and dumped in the harbor in June 2020 and analize it through commemorative ethics - specifcally the honorific vs documentary distinction and the idea that monuments leave a kind of moral residue in urban space. I dont want the usual take about whether it was vandalism or justice; I want you to treat the harbor-dumping itself as a counter-commemorative act that re-indexes the site. Does the preserve-as-contested-memory framework survive this? Is the harm in the representation itself or in the social practice of upkeep? And say where the theory just goes silent, like on who actually has standing to do the removing if its not a democratic vote. Be disciplined about mapping - who's the actor, whats the option space, what counts as the harm - and dont force the framework to speak where it has nothing to say.
