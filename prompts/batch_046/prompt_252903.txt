So she runs a small fleet of delivery drones — like actual autonomous quadcopters that navigate around buildings — and about a year ago she signed up for this platform where the drones learn collectively. The way it works is each operator can adjust the local obstacle-avoidance paramaters on their own machines, and every night those adjustments get merged into the shared model through some federated averaging thing. Nobody at the company audits the individual contributions; they just check that the global error rate stays low. Last Tuesday one of her drones misread a construction scaffold as open air, crashed into it, and knocked over some equiptment that hurt a pedestrain. The investigation showed that the dangerous parameter shift had come from three different operators tweaking their settings for windy conditions, and together they pushed the global model into a blind spot that none of them intended. The software company says they're not liable because they never touched those weights. The other operators say they were just optimizing for their own routes. My friend says she feels responsible because she was part of the pool, but she also feels like she was just one tiny piece of a machine that made its own decision. I'm trying to figure out if there's actually a right answer about who should bear the cost — her, the company, the other users, or maybe nobody in particular? And if responsibility really does spread out so thin that it disappears, does that mean we should just treat it like a natural disaster and move on? I feel like there's a philosophical structure hiding in here but I can't name it, and I don't want to just say 'corporations bad' or whatever. How should she think about it?
