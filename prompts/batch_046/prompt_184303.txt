Write a sestina on Avicenna's flying man argument, using the standard six-line end-word rotation with these words: air, spread, blind, body, know, soul. Structure the poem so that the argument unfolds through the form: stanzas 1–3 establish the suspended figure, limbs spread in air, senses blind, with no prior experience of embodiment; stanzas 4–5 turn to the realization that self-knowledge remains intact even when bodily knowledge is wholly denied; and the envoi should gather all six words to state exactly what the argument concludes about the soul’s independence from the body. In the closing lines, inject a brief modern counter-current—let the speaker momentarily wonder whether this “I am” still smuggles in an implicit bodily frame, as in the brain-in-a-vat scenario—before the form resolves back to the classical conclusion. The poem should be precise enough that a reader familiar with the argument recognizes each step, and should stand as poetry for someone who has never encountered it.
