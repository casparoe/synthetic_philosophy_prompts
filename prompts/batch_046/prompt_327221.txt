You sit on your university's academic regulations committee. The administration proposes abolishing the long-standing rule that every submitted essay must carry a handwritten cover sheet with a signed originality declaration, on the grounds that digital submission and automated plagiarism detection have rendered it obsolete. A senior committee member objects, invoking Chesterton's fence: we may have forgotten why the handwritten element was originally required, and Burkean conservatism warns against discarding practices whose hidden function is no longer visible. Your papers include a brief internal study suggesting that the physical act of signing still modestly lowers self-reported plagiarism intention relative to a digital checkbox, though the practical burden on students is significant. Draft the short written opinion you will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action with a clear time frame, engage the Burkean and Chesterton objections seriously rather than treating them as nostalgia, and address whether the hidden-function argument survives if the empirical claim is granted. Please write in British English.
