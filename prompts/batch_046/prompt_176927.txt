I have come to accept a particular reading of Quine's meta-ethical stance—one that treats moral values not as irreducible non-natural properties, nor as mere expressions of affect, but as theoretical posits within an empirically continuous "normative science" that organizes behavioral dispositions and social coordination, analogous to the way physics posits unobservable entities. On this view, moral predicates inherit the same ontological austerity Quine applies everywhere: they earn their place only if indispensable to our best overall theory of human behavior, and they suffer from the same radical underdetermination he diagnosed in natural science. Specifically, I hold that for any stable corpus of cross-cultural moral judgments, there will always exist rival normative theories—formally incompatible with one another, yet empirically equivalent with respect to all observable behavioral outputs—that assign contradictory moral values to the same actions. Moral disagreement, then, is structurally analogous to the indeterminacy of translation: not a failure to discover a shared fact, but the pragmatic selection of one coordination manual among others that fit the behavioral evidence equally well.

This framing seems to absorb the standard realist counterarguments—apparent moral convergence can be redescribed as shared pragmatic pressure selecting one manual, and the seeming objectivity of particular judgments can be treated as theoretical entrenchment—yet I worry the position risks becoming unfalsifiable: any apparent moral discovery becomes a shift in theoretical convenience, and persistent disagreement is simply more underdetermination. Before committing, I want an honest map of its exposure. What specific observations or formal results—say, about the structure of moral learning in children, about rational moral conversion across apparently incommensurable frameworks, or about constraints from decision theory—would have to obtain for this Quinean normative-science view to be wrong? Which defeaters would a committed defender concede as genuinely damaging, and which would they dismiss by appealing to the flexibility of theoretical positing or pragmatic convention? Please distinguish fatal from merely awkward, and assess whether the view has been qualified into a position that risks nothing empirically—if so, whether that insulation is a problem for a view that claims continuity with empirical science rather than a retreat into non-cognitivism.
