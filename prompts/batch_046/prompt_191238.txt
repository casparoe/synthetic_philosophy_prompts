Write a research proposal (approximately 1,200 words, suitable for a one-semester philosophy seminar or a small independent-study grant) that addresses the following problem. Contemporary popular Stoicism—particularly in cognitive-behavioral therapy adaptations and modern self-help literature—typically presents the ancient dichotomy of control as a binary filter for emotional investment: either an event lies within my power and is therefore an appropriate object of concern, or it lies outside my power and should be treated with indifference. Classical Stoic texts, however, appear to presuppose a more granular taxonomy in which so-called indifferents—health, reputation, the welfare of others—are genuinely outside our full control yet remain rationally preferable or avoidable. Propose a study that asks whether the modern binary framing implicitly abandons the category of preferred indifferents, and whether a reconstructed tripartite schema—strictly internal (judgments and intentions), partly within influence but not guaranteed (actions, relationships, bodily care), and entirely external (past events, others' judgments, cosmic events)—can preserve the therapeutic utility of the dichotomy without collapsing the ancient metaphysical distinctions into a simplistic either/or.

Your proposal should contain: (a) a precisely formulated research question that has a determinate answer; (b) an explanation of why current philosophical scholarship and clinical literature leave this open, specifically noting that modern revivalists rarely engage the metaphysical status of preferred indifferents while classical scholars rarely assess therapeutic framing; (c) a clear methodological plan, distinguishing the textual-reconstruction component from the conceptual-analysis component, and explaining why this combination is preferable to a purely historical or purely normative approach; (d) a description of what a successful outcome would look like (e.g., a defensible tripartite boundary that resolves standard counterexamples without ad hoc stipulation) and an honest account of what would count as failure or as evidence that the project exceeds one semester; (e) a realistic timeline or scope assessment.
