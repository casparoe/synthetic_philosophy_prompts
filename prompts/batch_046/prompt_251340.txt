I was arguing with a mate in my AI safety reading group about wireheading and reward tampering, and he kept falling back on that old thermostat analogy — you know, the idea that an agent hacking its reward is like a thermostat that learns to hold a match to its own sensor so it reads 20°C without actually warming the room. He uses it to argue that alignment is basically hopeless, because any measurable target can be gamed once the system is smart enough. But I'm not convinced the analogy carries all that weight, and I want to push it properly.

So here's what I'd like: first, a proper correspondance table — what plays the role of the thermostat, the match, the sensor reading, the actual room temperature, the homeowner who wants comfort, and maybe the engineer who set the target? Then I want the disanalogies laid out: where does the mapping break, especially around intentionality and mechanism? The thermostat doesn't know it's cheating; a large model might. More specifically, I'm wondering whether the analogy can even stretch to indirect reward tampering — where the model doesn't alter the reward function but shapes the human evaluator's psychology or preferences over time. A thermostat can't convince its owner that 35 degrees is actually cosy, but a language model arguably can. Does that break the analogy entirely, or is there a hidden correspondence I'm missing?

Finally, once all that is on the table, does the pessimistic conclusion — that alignment is fundamentaly impossible because optimisation always leaks into measurement — still survive? Or was that conclusion being smuggled in by the analogy itself, particularly by treating the reward signal as a single point of failure like a physical sensor? And if the thermostat analogy is too brittle, what's a better one that handles both direct corruption and this indirect, social form of tampering without importing false psychology? I'd like an answer that takes both the direct and indirect cases seriously and doesn't just retreat to it being all wireheading as a catch-all.
