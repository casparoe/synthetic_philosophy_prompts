In a philosophy discussion about determinism and free will, someone dismissed compatibilist accounts with the line "sphexishness is just determinism wearing a mask of complexity" — the idea being that flexible, adaptive behavior is only mechanical rigidity scaled up. I am trying to see whether that sentence actually does any work. Can you separate the precise claims it could be making: for example, that flexibility is nothing but multi-step rigidity; that a system which modifies its own rules still operates within a fixed meta-rule and thus remains sphexish; that the sphex wasp's burrow-checking loop differs from human deliberation only in complexity, not in kind; and that "sphexishness" is an empirical description of behavior rather than a metaphysical thesis about causation? For each reading, say whether it is trivial, defensible, or false, and indicate which version actually supports dismissing compatibilism and which merely sounds like it does. The slogan was used to collapse the distinction between instinctive loops and reflective choice, so I want to know which precisification makes that collapse valid, if any.
