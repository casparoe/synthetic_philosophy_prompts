Write a short story set in a near-future city-state that has abolished traditional spectator theater and replaced it with mandatory civic performances in which every resident must take an active role, with no passive audience permitted. The founders justify this arrangement by appealing to Rousseau's critique of theatrical spectacle—specifically, the claim that watching representations corrupts virtue by turning citizens into passive onlookers and substituting artificial roles for authentic communal life. Follow one resident who secretly yearns not to perform but to observe, and through her experience examine whether eliminating the audience actually resolves the moral danger, or whether the corruption simply migrates into the structure of compulsory role-playing and manufactured identity. Take a clear, committed position in the narrative: does the abolition of spectatorship solve Rousseau's problem, or does it reveal that his deeper objection was never about the presence of an audience at all? After the story, include a brief author's note that states exactly where your narrative takes liberties with Rousseau's actual arguments or depends on a contested reading of his views on authenticity and civic virtue.
