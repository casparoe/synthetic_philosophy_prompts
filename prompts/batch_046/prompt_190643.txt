(iii) Mathematical proofs can be beautiful in a non-metaphorical sense, and mathematicians across traditions reliably converge on which ones are, suggesting that refined aesthetic judgment extends beyond art to structured human achievements.
(iv) A pop song engineered by algorithms to maximize immediate dopaminergic reward is not beautiful, no matter how widely it is enjoyed; she compares it to junk food that pleases without nourishing.
(v) Two equally trained art critics can legitimately disagree about an abstract expressionist canvas, but they cannot both be correct about whether a perfect forgery of a Vermeer—indistinguishable from the original to every viewer—is beautiful; she insists the forgery lacks beauty precisely because it lacks the right origin, regardless of perceptual effect.
