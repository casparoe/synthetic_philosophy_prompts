Write a sestina on money pumps and diachronic rationality. Use exactly these six end-words, rotating through the six stanzas in the standard sestina pattern and gathering all six in the final three-line envoi: plan, choice, cost, belief, time, cycle. In the opening stanzas, establish a speaker who constructs a long-term plan with stable beliefs about future costs. In the middle stanzas, render successive local choices—each rational in its own moment—as eroding the plan by exploiting shifting beliefs about cost and time, so that the poem enacts a preference loop in which plan yields to choice, choice to cost, cost to belief, belief to time, time to cycle, and cycle back to plan. In the envoi, place all six words together in a way that forces the reader to determine whether the speaker has achieved resolute commitment to the original plan or has instead adopted the sophisticated stance of anticipating the loop and planning around it. The poem should be structurally precise and conceptually accurate enough that someone familiar with dynamic consistency can identify which view the speaker embodies, without naming any author, text, or theory.
