I'm making a decision-tree handout for a philosophy-of-city class on zoning and NIMBY fights, and I want it to sort people by what they actually believe rather than what label they slap on themselves. Start with whether private property rights include a right to regulate your neighbors' land use through zoning. Then branch on whether housing scarcity is mainly a supply failure or a distribution failure, then on whether rent control protects vulnerable tenants or just freezes the wrong people in place and kills new construction. At the leaves put named positions—market urbanist, social-housing advocate, Lefebvrian right-to-the-city, homeowner-association libertarian, Georgist land-taxer—and for each one write one sentence on its most awkward commitment, like the market urbanist having to defend bulldozing historic neighborhoods for density or the Lefebvrian having to explain why squatters don't automatically win every dispute. Include one empty branch or unnamed combo where the logical space exists but nobody actually occupies it, and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate—especially Georgists and strong supply-siders, since both want zoning abolished but for totally different reasons. Keep it casual like lecture notes, maybe misspell "seperate" once, and don't ask anyone to name-drop philosophers they haven't actually read.
