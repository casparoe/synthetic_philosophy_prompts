I'm drafting a historical novel set in the 1780s and I'm stuck on which non-human forces get to be treated like real characters with agency, not just background. Here are my gut calls, and they dont line up neat.

The Black Death in the 14th century — definatly a historical actor, it snapped feudal labor bonds like a protagonist forcing the plot.

The 1783 Laki fissure eruption in Iceland, poisoning skies over Europe and wrecking harvests just before the Revolution — I want to treat this volcano as a silent antagonist with real agency.

COVID-19 reshaping office norms and remote work — yes, but thinner, more like a plot device that only works because human institutions react.

The 1815 Tambora eruption causing the Year Without Summer, which apparently pushed Mary Shelley toward writing Frankenstein — I say no, that's just atmospheric backdrop, not agency, even though the causal chain is well-known.

Blaming the Syrian civil war on the 2006-2011 drought through climate change — I reject this hard, it feels like environmental determinism, not a real actor.

But then cholera in 19th-century London: Snow traces it to the Broad Street pump. The germ is an actor, yet it only kills through human water pipes and urban density. So human mediation is present in both COVID and cholera, which I accept, but absent in the drought case I reject — or is it?

The tension that bugs me: Laki and Tambora are both volcanoes with continental reach, yet I grant agency to one and deny it to the other. And I welcome germ agency even when it depends on human infrastructure, but I reject climate-driven conflict for depending on political failure. Is there a coherent principle — maybe something like "catalytic agency" versus "structural agency," where a non-human thing counts only if it creates a decision point institutions couldn't generate alone? Can you state that principle precisely, test it against my verdicts, and tell me which ones I have to drop to keep it simple? Also, what does it predict for a case I haven't listed — say, the El Niño event of 1982-83 triggering droughts and state collapse in parts of East Africa? I dont need a bibliography, just the logic.
