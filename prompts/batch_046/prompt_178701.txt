In a recent metaphysics discussion, a speaker dismissed talk of "masked dispositions" with the line: "A mask doesn't conceal the disposition — if it's genuinely masked, the disposition isn't there." The context was a contrived case they called a "recursive fink": an apparatus that carries an interfering factor disposed to suppress its own presence at the exact moment a triggering stimulus arrives, leaving the base object apparently unmanifesting yet lacking any active blocker at the critical instant. The speaker seemed to invoke the slogan to argue that applying the word "disposition" to the base object in such a scenario is a category error rather than an observation about hidden powers. I am unsure what precise claim they meant.

Could you lay out the distinct philosophical theses this one-liner might express — for example, claims about whether manifestation conditions are constitutive of dispositions, about whether counterfactual analyses must be evaluated relative to an object's actual present properties, about whether masks and finks differ ontologically rather than merely causally, or about semantic conventions governing attributions of powers — and for each indicate whether it is a platitude, a defensible but substantive position, or false? Please also specify which of these readings, if any, actually supports extending the slogan to the recursive-fink example they constructed, and which readings would instead allow that a disposition can genuinely exist even when systematically masked and never manifested.
