Given that Russell's paradox arises specifically within naive set theory from allowing a set to contain itself, and that Russell's theory of types resolves this by imposing a strict hierarchy in which no object can belong to its own type, I am struggling to understand why contemporary cognitive science and formal semantics tolerate recursive, self-referential architectures at all. If the contradiction is genuinely logical rather than merely syntactic, should not any system capable of representing its own representations—including, for instance, a predictive model that updates its priors on the basis of its own outputs—be vulnerable to precisely the same collapse? In particular, does the distinction between static set-membership and temporal feedback loops genuinely block the paradox, or is it merely a way of ignoring the fact that a self-modifying system has, in effect, placed itself inside its own extension?
