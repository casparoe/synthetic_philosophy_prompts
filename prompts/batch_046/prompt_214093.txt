I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about advance directives and whether the earlier self gets to boss around the later one — like, is the 65-year-old a trustee for the body, or does the 80-year-old get to tear up the contract?

Here are my gut verdicts:

- Maria writes at 65: if she ever has advanced dementia and doesn't recogize her kids, no artifical feeding, even if she's calm and happy. At 85 she's exactly that — listens to music, seems content, but pneumonia means she can't swallow safely. I think we still shouldn't tube feed; the old Maria's call stands.

- James writes at 50: if dementia hits, don't treat infections, just comfort care. At 80 he gets a simple UTI, easily cured with antibiotics, but he doesn't understand what's happening. I still think treat it — we're not dragging out a hated life, just fixing a hiccup.

- Linda writes at 30 right after a bad divorce: "if I'm ever severely disabled I want to be let die." At 70 she's disabled from a stroke, writes poetry, says she's glad to be alive. I think her old directive is garbage — she was emotionally wrecked and basically formed a different practical identity.

- David writes at 60 refusing chemo for cancer. At 75 he gets cancer but also dementia; he keeps saying "fix me" but can't grasp what chemo is. I think honor the 60-year-old and skip chemo, even though he's asking now.

So sometimes the old self wins, sometimes the current self does, and sometimes we do small fixes the old self banned.
