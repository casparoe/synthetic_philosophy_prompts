The control-theorists in our group maintain that genuine moral assessment must track only what lies within an agent’s direct volitional command, while the luck-accepting side insists that outcomes, circumstances, and even constitutive features legitimately shape how we ought to regard someone. The disagreement does not seem to be about which cases belong in which category.

My working hypothesis is that the stall is structural rather than empirical. I suspect that the control side is primarily concerned with attributability—whether an action or trait can genuinely be ascribed to the agent’s own will—while the luck-accepting side is primarily concerned with accountability—whether the agent stands in a relation to others that demands explanation, repair, or response, regardless of how the situation arose. If so, the two groups may be pursuing different questions under the single heading of moral responsibility, treating answers to one as direct rebuttals to the other without recognizing the partition.
