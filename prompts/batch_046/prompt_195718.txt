You serve as the clinical ethics reviewer for a hospital pilot that trains nurses to apply the modern Stoic trichotomy of control—full agency, partial agency, and none—to decide whether to escalate workplace concerns or manage them individually. Staff routinely misclassify “partially within control” situations as fully outside their power, producing both dangerous passivity and unnecessary escalation, so you need a reusable, five-minute checklist that preserves the agency-outcome distinction, specifies explicit stopping rules where the procedure halts and demands a human values judgment, and flags the boundary conditions where the trichotomy collapses back into a simple dichotomy. Apply the checklist to three cases: (a) a nurse has been manually patching a broken infusion-pump protocol and now asks administration to replace the device; (b) a nurse declines to report a physician’s repeated dismissive behavior, framing her emotional response as fully within her own control; (c) a nurse requests institutional grief-counseling resources after a traumatic patient death, claiming the hospital fully controls access to care. Ensure one recommendation runs counter to the nurse’s own intuition. Explicitly say what the procedure cannot settle, state how confident you are in each step, identify the single point about which you are least sure, and present everything—procedure, applications, limits, and confidence assessment—as exactly five numbered items of one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion.
