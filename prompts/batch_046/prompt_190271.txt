I shall defend the thesis that commemorative street names should be retained not as honours but as what I call “mnemonic fractures”—deliberate narrative discontinuities that compel pedestrians to hold contradictory historical accounts in simultaneous awareness, rather than allowing renaming to manufacture a false coherence in urban space. Draft my Q&A preparation: the eight or so objections this particular audience will actually raise, ordered by how likely and how damaging each is, phrased in their own vocabulary of archival continuity, spatial legibility, heritage value, and moral hazard. For each objection, provide a reply I can deliver in roughly two minutes, note precisely where that reply is softest, and indicate what I ought to concede up front. Include the single question I am hoping nobody asks—specifically, the geographer’s probable charge that my concept of “mnemonic fracture” is merely an aestheticisation of harm that privileges a novelist’s taste for ambiguity over the lived experience of those for whom the name is not metaphor but wound. Please write the answer in British English.
