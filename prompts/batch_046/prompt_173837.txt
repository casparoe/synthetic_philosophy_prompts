Over the past semester I have been preparing material for a graduate seminar on non-standard moral patients, and I have become preoccupied with a problem that sits at the intersection of philosophy of physics and normative ethics: if panpsychism—or even a weaker proto-experiential view—is correct about fundamental entities, does the possibility of distributed, micro-level suffering generate ethical obligations that dwarf our usual concern for macroscopic organisms? I do not want a survey of named philosophers or a bibliography; I want a rigorous sorting instrument, specifically a decision tree or taxonomy, that maps the logical space of positions on this question without requiring respondents to recognize technical labels in advance.

The tree should open with a split on whether one accepts that fundamental physical entities possess any experiential or proto-experiential property for which suffering is not a category error—this is not asking whether quarks are conscious in the human sense, but whether there is a minimal something-it-is-like at that scale, however attenuated. The second branch should ask whether suffering necessarily requires a unified subject of experience, or whether it can be genuinely distributed across non-integrated micro-properties. The third should ask whether the sheer quantitative scale of such distributed micro-suffering, if real, creates a dominant moral catastrophe that should reorder conventional ethical priorities. The fourth should ask whether our radical epistemic inaccessibility to these putative experiences provides a decisive practical defeater against acting on them. Each leaf should be labeled with an actual position name if that combination is inhabited, or marked unnamed if it is logically available but apparently unexplored; every leaf must include one sentence describing its most awkward commitment. I also want explicit diagnosis of any unoccupied nodes—whether they are incoherent, repellent, or merely unclaimed—so that the taxonomy reveals the structure of the debate rather than reproducing a canon. The instrument is intended for advanced students who need to locate their own commitments through concrete answers rather than through familiarity with the literature.
