Write a short story set in a near-future sleep clinic where neural logs of REM-phase activity are admissible in court as evidence of “nocturnal experience.” A patient produces a vivid, emotionally charged log-report of a dream, yet upon waking insists she had no inner experience whatsoever—only darkness—while a bed partner claims she spoke and moved as if living through the events. The clinic’s philosopher-advisor must advise the court whether the log records a genuine experience that occurred during sleep and was immediately forgotten, or whether it is a narrative confabulated by the brain only at the moment of awakening. Frame the advisor’s deliberation specifically around whether phenomenal absence upon waking entails experiential absence during the night, and show how the legal system’s binary demand—experience or fabrication—fails to do justice to either view. Keep the technology and courtroom setting concrete but minimal, and focus the narrative on the conceptual tension rather than on technical or legal detail.
