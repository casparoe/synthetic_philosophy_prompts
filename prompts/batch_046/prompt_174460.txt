I hold that Jain anekāntavāda, read specifically through the saptabhaṅgī framework, should be interpreted as an ontology rather than an epistemology: reality is irreducibly multi-aspected, so that predicates such as being, non-being, and inexpressibility apply to the same substrate in non-overlapping respects (nayas) without contradiction, arranged in something like a seven-valued lattice rather than filtered through limited cognition. This is stronger than mere perspectivism; it claims the world itself is structured this way. What would genuinely count against this ontological reading? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward: for instance, whether allowing contradiction in any single respect collapses the view into dialetheism; whether quantum superposition offers a cleaner physical analog that renders the seven-fold scheme redundant; whether comparison with the Buddhist catuṣkoṭi shows the scheme arbitrary rather than exhaustive; and whether the naya distinctions can be formalized without circularity. For each, indicate whether a committed defender would concede damage or absorb it by refining the respect-distinction. Is this position well insulated from refutation, and is that insulation a philosophical virtue or a vice?
