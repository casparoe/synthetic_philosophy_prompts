I serve on the interpretive ethics board for a public history museum that is planning a major exhibit on the transformation of the Roman Republic. I do not have formal academic training in classics or philosophy, but over the past year I have been reading on my own and talking with friends about whether comparing Rome’s institutional decay to modern democracies actually illuminates anything, or whether it mostly produces misleading analogies. Our board must now decide whether to approve an interactive gallery—tentatively titled “The Unmade Republic”—that invites visitors to vote on counterfactual constitutional changes, such as whether the Senate ought to have adopted term limits for consuls around the time of Marius, or whether agrarian redistribution should have been enforced through a separate military authority rather than through the tribunate. The museum director argues that this makes structural decay tangible for general audiences; our senior staff historian objects that it implicitly treats democratic collapse as a matter of discrete institutional choices rather than deep economic and military pressures, and that it risks teaching visitors that history was far more contingent than the evidence supports. Before you draft anything, please take the role of an expert in political philosophy and public historical interpretation, think carefully about the distinction between pedagogical counterfactuals and structural inevitability, and briefly brainstorm the strongest considerations on each side. Then draft the board’s formal decision memo recommending a concrete course of action—approve with specific modifications, approve with conditions, or reject—with a clear time frame for implementation if approved, and with reasoning that takes the historian’s structuralist objection seriously rather than dismissing it. The memo should be something the board can act on at next month’s meeting. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; what we need is reasoning we can weigh and respond to directly.
