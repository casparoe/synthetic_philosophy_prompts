Write a deadpan parody, approximately 600 words, formatted as the Terms of Service agreement for a fictional subscription platform called Ground Project Integrity™ (GPITM). The platform promises to eliminate the kind of comparative moral reasoning that Bernard Williams criticized—specifically, the extra aggregative thought that intrudes when an agent treats a commitment to a particular person or project as subject to cost-benefit analysis—by automating ethical decision-making according to the user's pre-registered integrity profile. The satire must extrapolate Williams's integrity objection, particularly his claim that some commitments are identity-constituting ground projects not properly sacrificed for greater aggregate good, into the language of software engineering and consumer contracts. Include specific fictional clauses: (1) an Integrity-Weighted Consent provision stating that once a relationship or endeavor is marked as ground-level, no algorithmic override may introduce comparative metrics; (2) a No-Utilitarian-Override feature guaranteeing that the system will never recommend sacrificing the designated commitment for the greater welfare; (3) a customer-support transcript in which a subscriber complains that the service saved his designated partner but also saved three strangers, thereby generating the exact comparative calculus—why her and not them?—that he had paid to avoid; the support agent must respond in purely contractual terms, treating the user's distress as a breach of service-level agreement rather than a philosophical problem. Maintain an entirely deadpan, corporate-legal tone throughout; do not break character to explain the philosophy or wink at the reader. The humor and critical force should arise solely from the literalization of Williams's concepts—treating ground projects as configurable data fields, treating the absence of destructive comparative reasoning as a deliverable feature, and treating moral identity as a terms-of-service property right. A reader familiar with Williams's work should recognize each clause as an exaggeration of a real feature of his view, especially the idea that requiring a utilitarian justification for rescuing a loved one introduces a corrosive extra thought. Do not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to secondary literature. Do not mention any living person. The piece should stand alone as a mock legal document that is simultaneously absurd and philosophically legible.
