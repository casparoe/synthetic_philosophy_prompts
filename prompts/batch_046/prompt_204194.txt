Consider the absent-minded driver problem. A driver is traveling home and must take one of two consecutive highway exits. The first exit leads directly home with payoff 10; the second also reaches home but with payoff 6 because of extra traffic. The driver is absent-minded: if he reaches the second exit he cannot remember whether he has already passed the first. Now add a private token: the driver carries a scratched coin in his pocket. By feeling for it he can determine with certainty whether he is at the first exit (coin is smooth) or the second (coin is scratched), but the act of checking costs 0.1 utility. Should the driver check the token if he finds himself at the second exit? The student wrote: The absent-minded driver should plan to drive straight past the first exit, since taking it yields payoff 10 and missing it still yields 6 at the second, which is better than stopping prematurely with no information. At the second exit, acting rationality tells him to take the exit, because he might actually be at the first exit where leaving gives 10. This is the classic planning-versus-acting tension. With the token added, he can feel the coin in his pocket. If it's smooth he is at exit one, if scratched he is at exit two, and checking costs 0.1. So if he checks and then exits, he gets either 9.9 or 5.9, both positive. That means he should definitely check at either junction rather than drive past blindly. Now, about centered worlds. In Lewis's framework beliefs are centered on the agent, so learning he is at exit one is just updating a self-locating belief, not learning a new fact about the highway layout. This means the token is an evidential signal — it tells him where he is — but it doesn't causally affect which exit he's actually at. So under causal decision theory he shouldn't bother checking, since feeling the coin doesn't change his location, but under evidential decision theory he should, because it updates his belief. The planning-acting distinction actually collapses here, because once you have perfect self-locating information there's no conflict between ex ante and ex post: both just maximize expected utility over centered worlds. So the token solves the paradox by giving him perfect information, but people think it doesn't because they confuse private belief updates with causal interventions.
