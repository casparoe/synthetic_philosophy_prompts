I'm preparing a unit for an undergraduate philosophy-of-religion course that treats religious commitment not as a response to revealed truth but as a natural phenomenon—something shaped by evolved cognition, emotional arousal, social transmission, and narrative practice. For our fiction module I want to pair this framework with Yann Martel's Life of Pi, but I want to avoid reducing the novel to either a defense of faith or a rationalist debunking of it. Instead, please read the text through Harvey Whitehouse's distinction between doctrinal and imagistic modes of religiosity: the doctrinal mode relies on frequent repetition, semantic encoding, and standardized transmission to maintain a stable propositional creed, while the imagistic mode depends on rare, high-arousal rituals that generate intense episodic memories and resist easy verbal systematization. Apply this lens—not as a rigid taxonomy but as a diagnostic tool—to Pi Patel's simultaneous practice of Hinduism, Christianity, and Islam.

Does the novel present his triple belonging as doctrinal pluralism (three propositional systems held in logical tension), or as an imagistic constellation (a sequence of traumatic, high-arousal events—the shipwreck, the carnivorous island, the months with Richard Parker—that are retrospectively clothed in religious imagery)? Where does the narrative resist this binary: for instance, does Pi's father, with his scientific rationalism, represent an alternative doctrinal mode with its own rituals of verification, and does the novel's famous ending, in which Pi offers two versions of his survival and asks which is the better story, treat belief-selection as a matter of narrative preference rather than either doctrinal assent or imagistic trauma? Finally, I want to know whether Martel's work adds something to the philosophy of religion that purely naturalistic accounts—whether evolutionary, cognitive, or anthropological—tend to flatten: does his insistence on choosing the story with God capture an aspect of religious life, perhaps its function as a deliberate, meaning-making response to extreme contingency, that descriptions of religion as adaptive social glue or cognitive by-product fail to preserve?
