Sphexishness, a term coined by Daniel Dennett to describe the sphex wasp’s behavior, refers to the loop observed when the wasp checks its burrow, drags paralyzed prey inside, and then checks again; if an experimenter moves the prey a few inches away, the wasp repeats the sequence rather than adapting. This demonstrates procedural rigidity—the repetition of a fixed motor program—but it also demonstrates representational inflexibility, since the wasp clearly cannot update its internal map of where the prey lies.
