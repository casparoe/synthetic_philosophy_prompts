I am preparing a podcast interview with an unnamed contemporary philosopher who defends a Quinean naturalist account of moral values. The view she holds is that moral claims do not correspond to mind-independent normative properties in any metaphysically robust sense; rather, they function as culturally embedded nodes within a holistic web of belief, justified by their contribution to practical and predictive coherence rather than by representational accuracy, much as Quine treats physical-object posits. I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond having her restate this position. In particular, I need at least one question on how her view distinguishes genuine moral disagreement from mere conflict of preference without collapsing into relativism, with a follow-up keyed to the reply that practical coherence alone grounds intersubjective argument—pressing whether coherence without representational content yields real disagreement or only pragmatic adjustment. I also need at least one question on whether Quine’s indispensability argument for mathematical entities can be extended to moral values, and what that extension implies for their ontological status, with a follow-up keyed to the likely defense that moral posits are indispensable to social prediction—asking what kind of empirical discovery could ever dislodge a moral posit in the way a mathematical one might be. Finally, I need at least one question applying the doctrine of the indeterminacy of translation to moral language—whether radically different moral vocabularies describe incompatible normative worlds or simply fail to translate one another—followed by a question about whether this makes cross-cultural moral criticism impossible in principle or merely difficult in practice. The remaining questions should press other live tensions in the literature: for example, how Quinean naturalism handles first-personal moral authority, whether the view can accommodate moral progress without a telos, and how it compares to expressivist or error-theoretic alternatives without borrowing their metaphysical commitments. Avoid generic biographical openers; each question should turn on the actual content of the Quinean position and anticipate the standard defensive reply.
