Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between gains-from-trade moral pluralists and lexical-integrity deontologists regarding whether cross-system moral compromise can generate genuine normative surplus. The pluralists maintain that when autonomy-based, solidarity-based, and ecological-integrity frameworks each relax one lexical priority—autonomy trading procedural neutrality for substantive welfare guarantees, solidarity trading distributive equality for adaptive stewardship rights, and ecology trading absolute preservation for long-term resilience—a Pareto-improving moral contract can preserve the core of each system. The deontologists maintain that such side-payments dissolve normative identity: autonomy without neutrality becomes paternalism, solidarity without equality becomes charity, and ecological integrity without preservation becomes management. Deliver the statement in two parts. First, numbered agreed claims: the formal conditions for identifying a value system's "core" versus "adjustable periphery"; whether Pareto improvement is definable without a shared metric; and whether constitutional settlements provide valid analogies. Second, a disagreements table stating, in wording each side accepts for itself, exactly where they diverge—whether on the possibility of surplus, on the corruption of identity, or on the empirical identification of cores—and specifying the cruxes: sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one camp, such as whether a value survives losing its lexical rank if all other structural features remain intact. For each crux, record what both sides agree would count as bearing evidence, with each side's prediction registered in advance—pluralists requiring a worked three-way example with no net core loss; deontologists requiring proof that relaxing lexical priority collapses the justification for remaining commitments. If part of the dispute is verbal, identify which part—e.g., "compromise" as pragmatic accommodation versus normative synthesis—and explain why. Ensure formulations are precise, non-vacuous, and equally acceptable to both sides for describing their own position.
