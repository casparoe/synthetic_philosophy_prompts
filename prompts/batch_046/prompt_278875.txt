Two friends of mine, R and S, got into it after a city council meeting about these new pedestrian signs. The city put up displays near crosswalks that show live air-quality data plus a line like "people walking here right now are breathing 30% cleaner air." No fines, no car bans, just the sign sitting right at the point where you decide wether to drive or walk.

R called it libertarian paternalism done right: it doesnt restrict anything, it just puts true, relevant info where people can use it. S said thats wrong, its not just information, its what she called a "choice-coupled frame" — the data is engineered to be visible exactly at the decision node and paired with a social cue, so its not ambient knowledge, its a targeted salience injection. R said salience is unavoidable; every sign highlights something, and calling that paternalism makes every public-health poster paternalistic. S said there's a real line between general public info and info that is spatially and temporally locked to the moment of choice with the explicit purpose of weighting one reason over others; the latter bypasses deliberation rather than supporting it. R then argued that if the intent is what matters, then we have to read minds, but S said we can read design: the display only activates near the crosswalk, not elsewhere in the city, which proves its coupled to the choice. R replied that still doesnt make it coercive or even manipulative, just well-placed. They asked me to referee but I dont really know.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate R's and S's key claims from clearly wrong to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Also say whether "choice-coupled framing" is a useful distinction or just a relabeling of standard nudging, and whether either of them is missing something about how belief-formation interacts with autonomy.
