Write a proposal for a philosophy term paper of about 6,000 words, to be completed in one semester. The project concerns Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the principle of alternative possibilities (PAP), but I want the framing to turn on a specific conceptual distinction that the standard literature tends to overlook: whether these cases assume a 'single-track' model of practical reasoning, in which an agent considers one course of action at a time, and whether adopting a 'parallel-track' or branching model of deliberation—where several options are held open simultaneously within the agent's own reasoning—allows the agent to retain a form of alternative possibility even when an external intervener is present. State the precise research question: do Frankfurt cases demonstrate that moral responsibility does not require alternative possibilities in any robust sense, or do they only demonstrate this relative to a linear psychology of choice that may not be obligatory? Explain why existing positions leave this open: defenders of PAP usually respond by arguing that the agent could still have done otherwise globally, while critics reply that global openness is not what matters; neither side typically examines whether the agent's internal reasoning structure preserves local, branching alternatives that the intervener does not control. Set out the method: first, reconstruct a canonical Frankfurt scenario with close attention to the temporal and psychological structure of the agent's decision; second, articulate the parallel-track model at a purely conceptual level, without importing empirical psychology or obscure theories, and identify what kinds of alternatives remain available within the agent's deliberation; third, assess whether these retained local alternatives are sufficient to rescue a qualified version of PAP, or whether PAP requires a stronger global openness that the case genuinely eliminates. Specify the two main possible conclusions—either that Frankfurt cases are more damaging to PAP than usually recognised because they suppress alternatives even under richer models of deliberation, or that they are less damaging because they only eliminate a superficial, single-track kind of openness. Give an honest assessment of scope: identify which part of the argument risks expanding beyond a semester's work (for example, extending the analysis to collective agency or connecting it to empirical decision science) and say exactly how you will contain the project. Please avoid requesting a bibliography, citation list, or literature review by name; refer to positions and arguments generally, and note that citations will be added separately. Finally, separate what is settled in this debate—namely, that Frankfurt cases present a serious and widely recognised challenge to an unqualified version of PAP—from what is genuinely contested—such as whether that challenge survives once we allow for richer internal structures of agency. Write in British English, keep the tone formal and polished, and do not present any contested interpretive claim as the consensus view.
