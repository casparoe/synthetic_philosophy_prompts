hey, I'm writing a feature on whether moral frameworks that dont share a metric can actually trade without one colonizing the other. Before I call anyone, I want the spec sheet for what a theory of cross-value compromise has to get right.

Let's frame it as the 'moral arbitrage problem': you've got agents with genuinely different axiologies -- not just different weights on shared goods, but goods they dont even rank on the same scale -- and somehow they reach a deal both see as a gain over status quo. What does the theory explaining that need to deliver?

Give me the desiderata:
- the phenomena it must cover (real mutual gains, not domination dressed up as dealmaking or simple side-payments)
- the cases it has to handle correctly (say, one side accepts a temporary ecological cap in exchange for tech that redefines their growth metric, versus cases where they just lose ground)
- the distinctions it cant collapse (strategic concession vs. actual value revision; intra-framework Pareto improvement vs. cross-framework improvement; temporary truce vs. structural integration)
- consistency conditions with results we already accept (Arrow-style aggregation failures, that incommensurability doesnt automatically mean incomparability, that reflective equilibrium isnt an algorithm you run)
- and flag which ones are hard requirements vs. camp-specific preferences (like, is demanding a shared meta-metric a real desideratum or just economic-utilitarian bias?)

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not just scoring existing theories. But do briefly check the main contenders -- contractualist bargaining, value-pluralist negotiation, integrative social-choice models -- against the list.

Also, please seperate whats settled from whats genuinely contested among people who actually know this area. Don't present one view as the consensus; I want to see where the real fault lines are.
