I'm on the board of a small community energy coop — about 200 households — and we vote quaterly on whether to reinvest surplus, distribute rebates, or fund efficiency upgrades. Lately people are proposing bundled packages that split across quarters: take a small rebate now and commit to a future investment path, but the commitments aren't binding and can be renogotiated each vote. Some members worry we're vunerable to being 'pumped' — slowly moving our prefrences in a circle across quarters until we've given up more value than we meant to, even though each quarter's vote looks rational on its own. I need a practical audit procedure my board can run before every vote to check whether a proposed sequence of commitments creates a diachronic money-pump risk. Not the abstract proof — a checklist: what docs to gather, the order of checks, the specific questions that expose cyclical preference shifts, where to flag for human judgement rather than math, and a hard stop. Apply it to three proposals: (1) a rebate-now-upgrade-later bundle, (2) a seasonal storage investment that defers payouts, and (3) one where your procedure says 'this is clean' but the board still hates the outcome. Also say clearly what the procedure misses — emotional manipulation, political dynamics, anything else it can't catch.
