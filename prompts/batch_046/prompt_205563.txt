For my philosophy of science seminar, I want a specification—not a literature review—of what an adequate philosophical account of scientific thought experiments must actually achieve.

Frame the problem around three canonical cases: Galileo’s falling-body argument, Einstein’s equivalence-principle elevator, and Schrödinger’s cat. The account must explain how each yields knowledge despite lacking direct empirical execution, but it must also preserve a sharp distinction between “reductio” experiments (which expose internal contradictions in a theory) and “constructive” ones (which establish positive ontological or methodological principles). Do not collapse either form into a disguised logical argument or into a mere mental simulation.

Your deliverable is the full list of desiderata, each with the motivating case or distinction behind it. Specifically include: (1) the explanatory phenomena the account must cover; (2) the conceptual distinctions it must not erase (e.g., between a priori clarification and empirically revisable modeling); (3) historical constraints it must respect—such as the fact that ether-based thought experiments were eventually undermined by observation, whereas equivalence-principle reasoning survived despite empirical equivalence with rival frameworks; and (4) whether any subset of these requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. In particular, diagnose whether demanding both “independence from actual observation” and “empirical falsifiability in principle” forces a choice, or whether that tension can be dissolved.

For each desideratum, indicate whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference (for instance, the mental-model camp versus the argument-analysis camp). End by identifying the single requirement that does the most damage to the most popular existing accounts, and state whether the tension you diagnosed is fatal. Present this as a numbered list with brief rationales, followed by a short concluding paragraph; keep the whole response under 450 words. No bibliography, no citation list.
