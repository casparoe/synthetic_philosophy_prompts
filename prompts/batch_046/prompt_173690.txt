Restate Wittgenstein's treatment of hinge propositions—the kind of certainties he examines in On Certainty, such as that the world existed long before my birth, that I have hands, or that the earth has existed for a very long time—using only the vocabulary and conceptual structure of distributed-system design: genesis blocks, shared consensus assumptions that nodes never put to a vote, invariants that are presupposed rather than enforced, and the boundary between state that can be revised without network restart and state that cannot be changed without dissolving the system's identity. For each hinge-type claim, specify whether it functions like a bootstrap parameter, a consensus invariant, or something else in your translation, and explain what had to be distorted to make the fit work. Conclude by assessing whether the residue reveals a genuine gap in distributed-systems thinking about certainty, or whether it suggests that Wittgenstein's hinges are less philosophically distinctive than they appear and map onto ordinary architectural commitments that engineers already understand well.
