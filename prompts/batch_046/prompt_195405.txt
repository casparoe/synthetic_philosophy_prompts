I'm moderating a colloquium Q&A with an unnamed defender of a revived tripartite ontology of meaning — frame, outer message, and inner message — of the kind developed in Gödel, Escher, Bach, but modified with two further claims: first, that in "autoreferential framing" the frame functions not as a neutral context but as an active interpretive protocol capable of overriding or nullifying the outer message (she takes this to be the norm in legal instruments, algorithmic outputs, and self-referential artworks rather than an edge case); second, that "empty-frame" communications, in which a frame and an outer message are present but no inner message is intended, constitute a distinct semantic category that Gricean and standard semantic theories misclassify as defective or ambiguous. Write me a sequenced set of questions that forces her to specify exactly where meaning is located in each of these cases and whether the frame is constitutive of meaning or merely a transmission mechanism. Each question should anticipate her likely reply — that the frame is constitutive and that empty frames are genuine semantic kinds — and include the direct follow-up that makes that reply harder to maintain. End with a single question asking what interpretive or empirical evidence could demonstrate that a given inner message was entirely an artifact of the frame's protocol rather than of authorial intent, or that a particular communication genuinely lacked any inner message whatsoever. Keep the pressure on the ontology of location — whether meaning resides in the message, in the frame, in the interaction, or in the interpreter's recovery procedure — and avoid generic or biographical questions.
