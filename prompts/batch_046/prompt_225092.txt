After a long and unresolved argument with a friend about what survives in Derek Parfit's teletransporter scenario, we want to formalize our disagreement through an adversarial collaboration rather than keep talking past each other. I defend a psychological-continuity reductionism: survival consists in the appropriate causal and qualitative links between mental states, regardless of whether the substrate is biological or synthetic and regardless of spatiotemporal gaps, so long as there is no branching; an instantaneous scan-and-rebuild produces me, not a replica. My friend defends an embodied-continuity view: survival requires the spatiotemporally continuous functioning of a living, conscious body; a perfect replica is a successor, not the original person, and even a gradual synthetic replacement preserves only an illusion of continuity rather than genuine identity.

Draft the joint statement we could both sign. In particular, address whether the intuition that gradual replacement preserves identity while instantaneous teletransportation does not can be rendered coherent, and whether that difference tracks a real feature of survival or is merely an artifact of temporal framing.
