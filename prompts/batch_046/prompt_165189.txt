I am a junior member of my university's Honors Curriculum Committee, currently enrolled in a seminar on ancient biography and moral formation. We must vote on whether to approve a proposed first-year course, Mirrors and Models: Plutarch and the Uses of Life-Writing, which pairs selections from Plutarch's Parallel Lives with modern secular hagiographies—idealized accounts of contemporary figures that systematically omit documented moral failures. Proponents argue that Plutarch himself shaped his subjects for pedagogical effect, pairing Greek and Roman lives not for historical completeness but to create a reflective comparison that invites the reader to correct his own soul; they claim modern hagiography can serve the same function without biographical frankness. Opponents reply that Plutarch's method depends precisely on the visible presence of vice alongside virtue—his lives include contradictions, rivalries, and failures that make moral comparison genuinely instructive—so that stripping away failure produces not a reflective mirror but a flat icon, useless for real self-examination and potentially deceptive about how difficult virtue is.

Please draft the committee's written opinion recommending approval, rejection, or approval with conditions. Specify a concrete course of action—for example, requiring that every hagiographic text be paired with a primary-source corrective, restricting enrollment to students who have completed historical-method training, or mandating that the syllabus include at least one life in which the subject's failure is central. Provide a clear conceptual rationale and engage seriously with the strongest argument on the losing side: if you recommend approval, explain why the risk of moral simplification is containable; if rejection, explain why Plutarch's own editorial practices do not license modern omission of failure. The memo should stand on reasoning that faculty and students could respond to directly, without bibliographies, citation lists, or references to works the committee would need to consult externally.
