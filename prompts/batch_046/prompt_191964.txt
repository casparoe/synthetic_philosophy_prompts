I am preparing the final exam for an upper-level philosophy of economics seminar on the socialist calculation debate. I need one 90-minute essay question that students cannot answer by summarizing Mises, Hayek, and Lange. To force engagement with the underlying issues, embed the following hypothetical institutional design: "Distributed Algorithmic Planning" (DAP), in which socially owned production nodes report local capabilities and needs to a decentralized network; a modified Langean iterative algorithm adjusts shadow prices without any central board holding full information; and private property rights over the means of production are abolished, though nodes retain operational autonomy. Ask students whether DAP resolves Mises's claim that rational economic calculation is impossible without market prices tied to private ownership, and whether it answers Hayek's claim that central planning fails because relevant knowledge is dispersed, tacit, and context-specific. Require them to say which premises of each critique survive, which are undermined, and whether a new difficulty—such as algorithmic opacity, incentive misalignment, or epistemic closure—replaces the old ones. Provide a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points (total 20 marks) that distinguishes an A answer from a B answer. For each point, state the marks, the specific move required, and a one-line note on how candidates typically fail to make it.
