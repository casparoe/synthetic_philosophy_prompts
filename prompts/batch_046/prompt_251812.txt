I am convening a postgraduate reading group on causal explanation in the human sciences, and in our last meeting a visiting lecturer offered a concise characterisation of Max Weber’s Protestant Ethic thesis that several participants found persuasive. The summary ran as follows: Weber showed that Calvinist predestination generated an unbearable psychological tension, which believers resolved through systematic worldly asceticism and the relentless accumulation of capital. In so doing, he proved that religious values operate as direct, independent causes of economic institutions, thereby overturning materialist historiography and establishing culture as a causal force in its own right. Although Weber spoke of an “elective affinity” between Protestant ethics and the spirit of capitalism, this was merely a rhetorical qualification; in substance he advanced a unidirectional causal chain from theological belief to economic behaviour. It follows, on this reading, that modern rational capitalism could not have arisen without the Reformation, since no other cultural formation could have supplied the requisite motivational structure.

I am inclined to think that this account captures part of Weber’s narrative but flattens his methodological position—particularly his insistence that material conditions remain causally relevant, his use of ideal types rather than psychological laws, and his explicit rejection of mono-causal explanations—into a rather crude cultural determinism. Before I present this to my students as an object lesson in how competent popularisations can drift, I should like a precise accuracy audit. Please assess the summary in British English: identify which elements are faithful to Weber’s argument (including his treatment of the “calling”, or Beruf, and his concept of adequate causation), which are standard pedagogical simplifications, and which constitute subtle misrepresentations—especially regarding the nature of elective affinity, the scope of his anti-materialism, and his counterfactual claims about the uniqueness of Western capitalism. Separate these categories clearly, and indicate where a defender of Weber’s actual view would object.
