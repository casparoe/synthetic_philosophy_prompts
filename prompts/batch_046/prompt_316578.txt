I cover philosophy and intellectual history for a science-and-ideas publication, and I am preparing a feature on how classic existentialist arguments are being stress-tested in advanced seminars. For the pedagogical sidebar, please design one rigorous exam component.

Write an essay question for an upper-level course on Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling, centered on Abraham and the teleological suspension of the ethical. The question must be unanswerable by merely summarizing the standard distinction between the universal ethical and the religious exception, or by labeling the knight of faith and the knight of infinite resignation. Instead, inject the following framing: ask students to consider whether Abraham's silence can be read not as a transcendent rupture with ethical communicability, but as an extreme form of ethical attentiveness — a refusal to justify Isaac's singular particularity within any universal moral grammar that thereby protects, rather than abandons, the other. The prompt should require students to adjudicate whether this "ethical humility" interpretation collapses Kierkegaard's distinction between the knight of faith and the knight of infinite resignation, or whether it reveals that the suspension is internal to ethics rather than external to it.

Provide a model answer of approximately 600 words. It should make these specific moves: (1) reconstruct the standard teleological suspension with precision; (2) articulate the counter-reading in which silence preserves rather than negates ethical relation; (3) evaluate whether a religiously motivated act can simultaneously be an ethical refusal of universalization; (4) determine if the knight of infinite resignation remains conceptually distinct under this pressure; (5) conclude on what is at stake for Kierkegaard's critique of systematic ethics.

Then supply a marking scheme out of 20 points. Explicitly separate an A answer (16-20) from a B answer (12-15) by naming the analytical moves each tier must execute — for example, an A must show that the ethical humility reading threatens to dissolve the religious/ethical boundary only if one assumes ethics is purely procedural, whereas a B merely describes both positions without adjudicating their interaction. List the specific misconceptions that should lose marks, such as treating Abraham's silence as mere privacy, or conflating the suspension with a utilitarian exception.

Keep everything self-contained: no bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not depend on knowledge of obscure secondary literature. Write in a formal, polished register suitable for an assessment instrument.
