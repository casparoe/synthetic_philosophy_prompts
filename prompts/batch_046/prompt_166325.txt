Nietzsche shows that guilt comes from debt. In ancient creditor-debtor relations, if someone failed to repay, they could suffer punishment calculated to match the debt, which established the idea that suffering can cancel a debt. Then priests spiritualized this idea: they taught that human beings owe an infinite debt to God, and that failure to pay results in eternal suffering. That is bad conscience—turning cruelty inward because external outlets are blocked. The slave revolt occurs when weak people, burdened by this internal guilt, invent the moral framework that strength is evil and weakness is good. The ascetic priest is essentially the leader of this revolt; he interprets suffering as punishment for sin, which allows the weak to dominate the strong. So morality is basically economics transformed into religion. But Nietzsche also argues that something genuinely new emerges: when cruelty is turned inward, it creates psychological depth, self-awareness, and an inner life.
