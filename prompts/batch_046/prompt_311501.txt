My research group includes two energy-systems analysts who have debated for over a year without converging: one defends France’s nuclear-fleet strategy as the indispensable backbone of deep decarbonization, the other defends Germany’s nuclear-exit pathway—relying on accelerated renewables, storage, and demand flexibility—as the more resilient model. Rather than another round of rebuttals, I want them to produce a joint adversarial-collaboration document. Please draft it.

The document should open with a numbered list of empirical and conceptual claims both can sign—covering grid physics, lifecycle carbon accounting, radioactive waste production, nuclear construction economics, and system reliability—phrased with enough precision that neither analyst can read them as tacit concessions.

Next, present a table of residual disagreements. Each entry should state the disagreement in wording that the analyst holding that view would accept as an accurate formulation of her own position, and should distinguish empirical predictions from normative commitments.

Then identify the cruxes: the sub-questions such that resolving them empirically would shift at least one analyst’s position. I take the central cruxes to be, first, whether firm low-carbon capacity retains a positive marginal capacity credit once variable renewable penetration exceeds roughly 80 percent in a synchronous grid with constrained cross-border interconnection; and second, whether the intergenerational risk profile of deep geological waste repositories is comparable in kind and scale to the climate and supply-security risks of delaying decarbonization through reliance on unproven storage and flexibility scaling.

For each crux, specify one concrete episode or dataset both analysts agree is probative, and record each analyst’s advance prediction about what that evidence shows. For the first crux, use the paired winter episodes of 2022–2023—the corrosion-related availability crisis that sharply reduced French nuclear fleet output and the concurrent German grid stress that required coal-reserve activation and elevated LNG reliance. For the second crux, use the contrast between Finland’s Onkalo deep geological repository—representing an advanced, licensed final-disposal program—and Germany’s still-unresolved siting and licensing framework for a comparable facility.

Close with a brief section that isolates disagreements that are irreducibly normative—such as whether energy sovereignty justifies accepting higher system costs, or whether intergenerational equity demands avoiding long-lived radioactive waste regardless of comparative climate risk.

Write in neutral, technical language suitable for co-authorship. Where quantitative judgments are involved—capacity-factor degradation curves, levelized cost ranges, or storage-duration requirements—state the conditions or ranges each analyst expects rather than asserting a single figure. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the document must stand on its internal reasoning and recorded predictions alone.
