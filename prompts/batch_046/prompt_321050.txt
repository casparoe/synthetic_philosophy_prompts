my friends maya and jordan got into it at a bar about the hedonistic imperative, specifically whether biotech should just wipe out suffering in every sentient being, wild animals included. maya said yes, absolute priority — suffering is the only intrinsic bad, so we should use germline editing and neurotech to kill it everywhere, ecological disruption be damned. jordan said thats too quick. pain isnt just a bad feeling; its a binding mechanism that links sensory prediction errors to behavioral correction. he described this idea of valence-neutral predictive coding where you keep the brain's error-correction machinery but strip the negative affect, replacing it with non-aversive attention tags. maya jumped on that: exactly, we can decouple urgency from suffering. a loud alarm doesnt hurt but still makes you move. jordan pushed back that salience without suffering collapses into plain information processing. if tissue damage just registers as a neutral data point, the agent wont maintain bodily boundaries — you get a blissful automaton wandering into fire. so either you preserve suffering for survival, or you redesign agency from scratch. maya said thats the feature, not the bug: the imperative is about redesigning sentient life, not preserving thier current architectures. but jordan said shes smuggling in a bait-and-switch. if you redesign motivation and replace old beings with engineered ones, youre not abolishing suffering for the lives that actually exist — youre letting them suffer or go extinct and calling the replacements saved. they asked me to referee and i honestly couldnt. please explain whos right about whether suffering can be decoupled from survival motivation without destroying agency, whos right about whether redesign counts as saving existing lives, and what big thing both are missing when they extend this logic to wild ecosystems.
