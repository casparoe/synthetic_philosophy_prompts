A close friend and I underwent an experimental cognitive-integration therapy that merged our autobiographical memory streams so thoroughly that each of us now recalls the other’s past with genuine first-person immediacy, not as borrowed stories. We remain physically separate, but when we deliberate about a shared life decision—whether to relocate for one career opportunity—each brain-body system generates reasons anchored in memories both claim as authentically theirs, and we have reached an impasse: one system wants to stay, the other to go, with no clear boundary between self-interest and other-interest. A counselor told us to negotiate like any couple, but that seems to miss something structurally different about our situation. How should we determine whose preference carries authority? Please explain the philosophical structure you see here, apply the relevant theory about whether a single “I” can be distributed across brains, and say how confident you are in your analysis—singling out the claim you are least sure of.
