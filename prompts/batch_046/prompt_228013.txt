Consider George Berkeley, revived to philosophical consciousness in the present, being asked to apply his master argument—that it is impossible to conceive an object existing unperceived, and hence that matter cannot subsist independently of mind—to a technology that postdates him by centuries: a fully immersive virtual-reality environment sustained by direct cortical stimulation rather than external screens. Within this system, users experience a three-dimensional world populated by entities driven by large language models—entities that generate novel utterances, appear to hold beliefs, and engage in practical reasoning, yet depend causally on distributed silicon hardware and electrical processes that no user ever perceives. Berkeley is further informed that the neuroscientific design of the stimulation relies on predictive coding, which treats perception as inference about hidden external causes.

Predict Berkeley's reconstructed position on three specific tensions that this scenario creates for his system, avoiding mere repetition of his familiar immaterialist slogans. First, regarding the virtual trees and rocks: since users perceive them but they depend on unperceived material processes, must Berkeley deny those processes exist, absorb them into ideas continuously perceived by God, or allow a derivative material substrate? Second, regarding the LLM-driven interlocutors: because they produce representations not traceable to any single human programmer's intention, would Berkeley treat them as complex patterns of sensory ideas lacking any perceiving spirit, or grant them a derivative mental status? Third, regarding predictive coding: Berkeley maintained that we perceive ideas immediately and never infer hidden material causes; does this scientific framework, which models perception as hypothesis-testing about hidden states, directly conflict with his direct realism?

Frame your answer as a reconstructed philosophical dialogue in Berkeley's voice, drawing explicitly on his commitments about ideas, finite spirits, and the necessary continuous perception of an infinite mind. Conclude by identifying the single weakest point in this reconstructed position—the precise modern objection that Berkeley's own conceptual resources do not obviously answer.
