Ive been running a grad seminar on moral status and we've hit a total wall on the insect-to-thermostat spectrum. We all mostly agree consciousness isnt binary—there's probably some kind of fading rather than a lightswitch—but the same three positions keep recycling: the integrationists point to global workspace dynamics in bees, the eliminativists say its just reflex loops that mimic suffering, and the scalarists want moral weight to track some continuous property like phenomenal richness. The frustrating part is that nobody actually disputes the neuroscience; we all know about nociception pathways in insects and the feedback architecture of a home thermostat. So the stall can't be about missing data. I'm trying to figure out if this is really a hidden empirical disagreement about whether a 'subject' is present in simple loops, or if its deeper—maybe we're answering different questions under one heading, with one camp talking about functional architecture, another about intrinsic value, and the scalarists smuggling in a metric they never defend.

There's also this thing Ive been calling 'threshold pluralism'—the hunch that sentience might not sit on a single axis but on several (sensory integration, affective valence, self-modeling) that don't covary in simple organisms. If thats right, then asking 'where exactly between a fly and a thermostat does consciousness fade?' might be malformed, like asking for the average color of a rainbow. But I dont know if thats a genuine diagnosis or just my own pet framing. So I want a real diagnosis: is this disagreement verbal, substantive, or bedrock intuition? Are there hidden formal or empirical questions doing the work? And if my pluralism idea holds up, what would progress actually look like—a model of how these axes decouple, a conceptual distinction that dissolves the framing, or something else? I dont want a survey of positions; I want to know why we're stuck and whether anything could actually move it.
