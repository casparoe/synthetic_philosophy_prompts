so i've been tracking this pattern in debates about moore-style intrinsic goods -- friendship and beauty being the classic ones. people start hardline: the value is right there in the relation or the object, totally independent of anyone's mental states or outcomes. then you hit them with a locked vault painting nobody will ever see, or a friendship where both parties are happy but completely deceived about each other. they don't abandon ship; they slide. suddenly its "the good requires some kind of awareness or potential for experience", or "that deceptive case isn't real friendship so it doesn't count". but two minutes later they're back to saying mental states don't matter at all. it feels like its own thing, not just motte-and-bailey, since both versions are contested and they share the same slogan rather than a defensible core plus an extreme extension. is there an established name for this exact oscillation in value theory? if not, can you coin and define it precisely -- especially where its edges are: does it only happen with plural goods like friendship vs beauty, or with any intrinsic-value claim? and is this something moore's own framework can block, or is it a separate failure mode people keep falling into when they defend these goods?
