I've come to think that Kierkegaard's attack on the press and 'the public' in that essay about literature isn't mainly about leveling or mob psychology. The real problem, I think, is structural: the press creates a phantom interlocutor that lets everyone speak without ever being adressed back, so commitment gets replaced by pure spectatorship. The public isn't a crowd; it's a diffuse absence that makes every opinion weightless because there's no one to answer to.
