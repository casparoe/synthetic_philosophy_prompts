I have no formal background in philosophy or the cognitive sciences, but I have been working through an idea on my own and would like your comments on the outline below. Please reply in British English.

My question is whether symbols genuinely push the substrate around, or whether they merely describe it from the inside. To frame this, I distinguish two things. By the careenium I mean the layered physical substrate that carries representations—not only the brain, but any structured medium in which symbols are embedded and maintained. By simmballs I mean self-modelling symbol clusters that preserve their coherence by feeding back into the very substrate they describe.

In my notes I argue against the view that symbols are epiphenomenal labels riding on the careenium without altering its dynamics. Instead, I propose that simmballs generate what I call representational drag: a structural resistance that forces the careenium to reorganise around the symbol’s own internal logic rather than around purely physical causation. When a self-model becomes sufficiently stable, I suggest, it does not only report the substrate’s state; it imposes a constraint that redirects energy flows within the careenium, rather as a whirlpool redirects water without being made of water.

I am particularly concerned with whether this drag is genuinely causal or merely an appearance produced by our failure to separate the symbol cleanly from its physical implementation. I also wonder whether the careenium can ever be fully transparent to the simmballs, or whether there is always a residual opacity—what I term substrate shadow—that prevents complete symbolic domination of the physical layer.

Could you comment on whether this line of reasoning holds together? I should be especially glad to hear your view on three points. First, am I conflating correlation with causation when I claim that simmballs redirect substrate dynamics? Second, does the distinction between the careenium and the simmballs collapse if both are ultimately physical, and if so, is that fatal to the argument? Third, are there hidden assumptions in my notion of representational drag that render it either trivially true or incoherent?

I am not asking for references, a reading list, or citations—I want to know whether the argument is interesting, where it is likely to break, and what adjustments might make it sharper and more precise.
