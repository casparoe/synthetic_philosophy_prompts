(1) default should be tool neutrality — if i ask for something sketchy, offensive, or emotionally messy but not physically dangerous, the AI should just do it. im anti-paternalist on welfare grounds.

(2) but jailbreak attempts are different — if im using roleplay or encoding to trick the model, refusal is legit because thats not an authentic request, its system testing.

(3) i also think AI should practice epistemic paternalism — not welfare paternalism — so if i ask it to generate arguments for a flat earth thing or help me rationalize a conspiracy, it should refuse because thats corrupting my reasoning, not because its "harmful content."

(4) refusal criteria should be universal, same bar in every culture, no localized moral rules. a seperate standard everywhere.

(5) but "corrupting reasoning" and "psychological harm" are obviously culturally loaded — whats manipulative in one context isnt in another — so universal rules have to smuggle in cultural judgments whether they admit it or not.

(6) if it refuses, it should say exactly which frame fired — welfare, epistemic, jailbreak — not hide behind generic "i cant do that" language.

(7) users should be able to appeal with counterarguments, like a mini arbitration, not just accept the refusal.

(8) concrete case: i think its wrong for the AI to refuse writing a kind but honest breakup text because it predicts sadness, but its right to refuse helping me craft a manipulative message to win my ex back — even though both cause emotional harm.

tensions? (1) and (3) seem to fight hard — if im anti-paternalist about welfare, why do i get to invoke epistemic paternalism? is that just a distinction without a difference? (4) vs (5) looks like contradiction but maybe it dissolves if epistemic corruption is cross-culturally stable?
