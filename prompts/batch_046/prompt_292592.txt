Draft the joint statement that a Dennett-style compatibilist defending just deserts—grounded in evolved, reasons-responsive capacities for self-governance—and a Caruso-style free-will skeptic rejecting basic-desert retributivism—grounded in the claim that causal determination undermines the authorship required for proportional suffering—could both sign after a sustained adversarial collaboration.

Structure the document in four parts.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims. Both camps must be able to endorse each claim without reservation. Cover: (1) that human behavior is causally influenced by neurobiological states and environmental history; (2) that punishment serves multiple social functions, including deterrence, incapacitation, rehabilitation, and expressive communication; (3) that moral anger and blame are real psychological phenomena with observable neural correlates; (4) that the legal system currently operates with a mixture of forward-looking and backward-looking justifications; and (5) that the distinction between holding someone accountable and holding them answerable is conceptually coherent, even if its practical importance is disputed.

Second, a disagreements table. For each residual dispute, provide two parallel formulations—one that the compatibilist camp accepts as an accurate statement of its own view, and one that the skeptic camp accepts as an accurate statement of its own view—phrased in neutral language that neither side would reject as a straw man. The table must address at least: (a) whether “could have done otherwise” understood in a reasons-responsive, compatibilist sense is sufficient to ground proportionate desert; (b) whether moral blame and punitive sanctions serve irreducible communicative or relational functions that cannot be fully translated into public-health or quarantine logic; and (c) whether desert is best modeled as a scalar property tracking continuous degrees of executive and rational capacity, or as a binary institutional status that either applies or does not apply to an agent.

Third, the cruxes. Identify the precise sub-questions such that settling them empirically or conceptually would move at least one camp. Include at least: (i) whether graded impairments in ventromedial prefrontal and associated executive-function networks diminish desert continuously across a spectrum or only fall below a categorical threshold of agency; (ii) whether current neuropsychological risk-assessment tools possess sufficient predictive validity and fairness across demographic groups to justify differential sentencing or treatment; and (iii) whether social practices of public apology, forgiveness, and moral repair conceptually presuppose a metaphysics of agency that compatibilism can supply but deterministic skepticism cannot.

Fourth, for each crux, specify a concrete case, empirical study design, or institutional scenario that both sides agree is relevant and well-defined, and record in advance what each camp predicts. For example, both should agree that a well-characterized cohort of adult offenders with documented ventromedial prefrontal lesions—who retain intact abstract reasoning but show marked deficits in emotional regulation and future-oriented decision-making—constitutes a valid test case. The compatibilist should predict that these individuals retain partial, diminished desert-based liability to the extent their reasons-responsive capacities remain operative, justifying proportionally reduced but still retributive sanctions. The skeptic should predict that the causal etiology of their impairment undermines basic desert entirely, leaving only forward-looking quarantine, rehabilitation, or risk-management considerations, with no legitimate role for proportional suffering.

Conclude with a brief assessment of whether any portion of the dispute is purely verbal—specifically, whether disagreements about the term “free will” or “moral responsibility” dissolve once translated into claims about capacities, predictive accuracy, and institutional roles—and mark exactly which substantive claims become redundant under that translation.

Keep the tone formal and philosophical. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, requests for citations, or references to specific page numbers or publication dates. Where specific theories are invoked, describe them in generic conceptual terms rather than attributing verbatim quotations.
