For my graduate seminar on comparative epistemology and metaphysics, I have been growing dissatisfied with the way the Zhuangzi's butterfly dream is routinely taught as an Eastern analogue to Cartesian dream skepticism. My students keep importing a substantialist ego—the very thing I want them to question—into their readings of the passage. So I am asking for an essay that examines a more structurally demanding interpretation. Specifically, consider what I will call the symmetry of awakening: the transition from butterfly-experience to human-experience is presented as no more authoritative, and no less mysterious, than the reverse transition would be. From this symmetry, one might derive a phenomenal pluralist thesis: the butterfly-self and the Zhuangzi-self are not successive masks of one enduring substance but co-equiprimordial, relationally constituted configurations of experience, each with its own phenomenological integrity. The essay should weigh this pluralist reading against the epistemic instability reading, which holds that the dream does not multiply ontologies but rather exposes the fragility of the first-person present—the indexical "now" that anchors knowledge-claims operates within distinct temporal coordinate systems in each state, so that comparing dream-certainty to waking-certainty is not a skeptical challenge but a category mistake that presupposes a unified epistemic subject who persists across the transition. Your task is to determine whether Zhuangzi's refusal to adjudicate between the two states supports a form of global skepticism about personal identity, or whether it dissolves the skeptical problem altogether by denying that there is a single knowing subject whose epistemic status must be secured across both configurations. Reach and defend a clear verdict—either that the text supports a plural ontology of transient, non-substantial selves, or that it undermines the Cartesian framework required for dream skepticism to gain any purchase—while addressing the concern that treating waking and dreaming as equiprimordial collapses into an implausible relativism about reality. I want the argument to stay at the level of conceptual architecture: do not provide textual philology or historical commentary, but do show me whether you think the symmetry of awakening is a genuine metaphysical insight or merely a rhetorical device.
