I serve on the Conservation Integrity Committee of the Museum of Temporal Arts, and my recent appointment followed directly from my work on diachronic identity in aesthetic objects—work that felt suddenly less abstract when, last month, I stood inside a degraded 1978 sound installation and realized the acoustic experience I had come to study was being sustained by three generations of replaced resonators and synthetic chemical substitutes. The committee must now advise the board on Proposal 2024-R, which concerns that very installation: forty-seven hand-blown borosilicate resonators originally filled with an aqueous crystallizing solution that continuously alters harmonic overtones. The artist’s contract specifies both the chemical formula and a prohibition against “fixing” the crystals in any single state. Over four decades, conservators have replaced roughly two-thirds of the glass and replenished the solution six times. The current proposal asks to retire the remaining original resonators, digitize their present acoustic signatures, and reproduce the sound through concealed speakers embedded in replica vessels, arguing that the physical substrate is now unstable and that the work is the temporal acoustic pattern rather than the matter. Our committee must recommend—approve, reject, or approve only under conditions—whether this digital substitution preserves the installation or destroys it and replaces it with a different object. In your memo, take a clear position with an actionable time frame, and treat seriously the strongest argument drawn from the opposing metaphysical view: that is, either the view that the work is a wholly present enduring object whose dominant material replacement terminates its identity, or the view that it is a four-dimensional entity with temporal parts for whom substituting resonators is analogous to adding a later segment. The reasoning should be concrete enough for committee members to apply without external research, and it should not rely on bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Please circulate the draft opinion for discussion at Thursday’s meeting.
