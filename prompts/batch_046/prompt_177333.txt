Local school board voting on funding: undocumented parents whose kids attend should definetely count, yes. National referendum on tax reform: citizens who moved abroad thirty years ago and never came back, no, they don't belong. Climate assembly setting carbon budgets that lock in conditions for 2100: people not born yet should have proxy reps, yes, but not full equal voting weight. Immigration referendum on who can enter: people living in other countries who want to move here but haven't arrived, they shouldn't vote even though the decision totally effects their lives. Regional water board for a river crossing borders: people downstream in the other country who drink the water, yes they should have seats -- that feels different from immigration but I can't say why. Goverment board for an open-source AI dataset used globally: people who just download the weights and never contribute code, no, only active maintainers belong. The pair that keeps bugging me is future generations getting proxy seats in the climate case but potential immigrants getting nothing in the referendum case. Both are affected outsiders who aren't physically present, yet my gut splits them. Is there one principle that captures all this, or do I need two?
