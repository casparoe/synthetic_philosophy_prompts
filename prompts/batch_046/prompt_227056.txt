Here is an argument my study group built about constitutional precommitment that now strikes me as suspicious, because it seems to show that either all Ulysses contracts are illegitimate or none are. (1) A Ulysses contract is rational only if the contracting self has temporal authority over the future self. (2) Temporal authority holds just in case the future self's preferences are causally traceable to the contracting self's original decision, in the sense that the decision initiates the causal chain that produces those preferences. (3) In constitutional precommitment, the founding generation's decision initiates the legal framework within which future generations form their preferences, but it does not causally produce those preferences; they arise from the future generations' own circumstances and deliberation. (4) So constitutional precommitment lacks temporal authority and is not a legitimate Ulysses contract, but rather an intergenerational imposition. (5) However, apply the same criterion to an individual: my present decision to lock away alcohol initiates a causal chain (the lock, the absence of alcohol) that influences my future preferences, but it does not causally produce my future desire to drink; that desire arises from my own biology and situation. (6) Therefore, by (2), my present self also lacks temporal authority over my future self. (7) So individual Ulysses contracts are illegitimate too. (8) But individual contracts are clearly legitimate—otherwise no rational agent could bind themselves against foreseeable weakness of will. (9) Hence (2) must be false. (10) Yet without (2), we have no principled criterion to distinguish legitimate constitutional rules (say, entrenched rights) from illegitimate domination (say, a founding generation permanently fixing tax rates), because both shape future preferences without producing them. Where does this chain go wrong? I would most like to keep both the legitimacy of individual precommitment and the idea that constitutional rules are not mere domination, but I need to know which step forces me to pay for keeping them.
