Hey, I came up with this argument for a seminar on responsibility gaps and now I'm second-guessing it because the conclusion feels totally off but I can't spot the leak. Here it is:

(1) If an autonomus system produces an outcome that no human specifically intended or could have predicatble in detail, then no individual human can be morally responsible for that outcome — responsibility requires some kind of direct control or foresight.

(2) Now apply the same reasoning upward: the engineers who built the system used automated design tools, inherited training data they never inspected, and optimization processes they don't fully understand. So they also lacked direct control over the system's specific behavior.

(3) The managers who funded them made strategic decisions but not technical ones; the regulators who approved it relied on reports they didn't write. At each level, the people involved made choices within constraints they didn't fully author.

(4) So if (1) is right, then for any harm caused by an autonomous system, responsibility doesn't just disappear — it diffuses upward through the entire causal lattice until it hits society as a whole, and since "society" isn't an agent, nobody is responsible.

(5) But here's the kicker: take a completely non-AI harm, like a factory worker injured by a machine that was designed with CAD software, built from standardized parts, and maintained by a contractor using diagnostic algorithms. The causal chain is just as diffuse and autonomous at each node.

(6) So if (1) is correct, then responsibility dissolves for practically every modern harm, not just AI ones. That proves way too much.

(7) Therefore (1) must be false, and users or designers are responsible even for unpredictable autonomous outputs.

(8) But that seems wrong too — holding a nurse responsible for every weird edge-case decision made by a clinical AI she just turned on feels like blaming her for something she genuinely couldn't steer.

So where's the mistake? Is there an equivocation between "direct control over the specific outcome" and "sufficient control to be accountable"? Or is the problem in (5), that I'm smuggling in autonomy where there isn't any? I really want to keep (1) in some form because it captures the intuition behind responsibility gaps, but I need to know what I'm paying to keep it. Also, is there a hidden scope shift with "society isn't an agent"?
