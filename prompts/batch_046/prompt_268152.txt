I'm trying to work out my actual judgments about when precommitment—binding a future decision-maker—is rational or legitimate, versus hubristic or wrong. Here are my concrete verdicts.

1. Odysseus ordering his crew to bind him to the mast and disregard his later commands so he can hear the Sirens without steering into them: clearly legitimate self-binding.

2. A legislature enacting an "automatic fiscal brake" that mandates spending cuts if public debt exceeds 60% of GDP, with no provision for legislative suspension even during war or natural disaster: legitimate, because legislators correctly anticipate their own panic-driven exceptions.

3. An individual creating an irrevocable trust that locks away 80% of liquid assets for twenty years, releasable only if two independent trustees verify there is no acute mental-health crisis: legitimate protection against depressive impulsivity.

4. The same individual contracting an automated system to permanently filter out all news, books, and correspondence advocating for any political position they currently reject, with no review mechanism: not legitimate—this binds belief-formation in a domain where revision should remain possible.

5. A constitutional amendment establishing an independent fiscal council, with lifetime-tenured members, empowered to veto budgets that violate the brake in case 2, but only within fiscal domains: legitimate democratic insulation.

6. The same council structure, but with veto power extending to any legislation altering the country's official language or founding religious tradition, plus a clause declaring the amendment itself permanently unamendable and prohibiting future conventions: not legitimate, despite ratification by referendum.

My tension is between 3 and 4—both lock away access (assets vs. information) to protect against predicted future irrationality, yet I split them—and between 2 and 6, both insulating rules from future democratic revision, yet I treat them oppositely.

Be explicit about which of my six verdicts your principle must overturn, and why. Keep the response under 450 words: the principle in one paragraph, the rest in bullet points.
