Please provide the following in a formal, polished register suitable for direct administration: first, the exam question itself; second, a model answer of roughly 700 words that executes all five moves with independent critical judgment; and third, a marking scheme totaling 30 marks that allocates specific marks to each of the five moves, names the exact argumentative content required for full marks in each section, lists typical errors that should reduce marks—such as conflating epistemic uncertainty with ontological gradation, treating evolutionary continuity as empirical proof rather than a structural argument, or ignoring the discontinuity problem altogether—and clearly distinguishes an excellent answer that resolves the threshold-aggregation tension from a merely competent one that summarizes the scale argument without addressing it.

The deliverable should include no request for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and should not depend on knowledge of obscure named theories, specific papers, or figures that a well-prepared student might not have encountered. Well-known concepts—evolutionary continuity, probabilistic reasoning, aggregation—are appropriate, but grading must not hinge on recall of niche literature. Write without verbatim quotations from any source; if you reference a specific claim or framework, paraphrase it without quotation marks.
