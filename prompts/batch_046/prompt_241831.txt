I was talking with my study group last night about panpsychism and we're stuck in the same loop we've been in for months. Everyone agrees the combination problem is real — if every particle has some minimal form of experiance, you still need an account of why there's one big subject (me) instead of trillions of tiny ones. Some people in the group say the issue is just vertical: how do micro-qualia combine structurally into macro-qualia? Others say that's the wrong framing entirely, that the real puzzle is horizontal: how do separate micro-subjects merge into a single macro-subject. We keep citing Chalmers's palette problem and Goff's phenomenal bonding, but then the conversation stalls because half the room thinks bonding is a primative metaphysical relation that can't be decomposed, and the other half thinks calling it "bonding" is just giving a name to the hard problem of consciousness. Nobody's moved, and I don't think anyone's even sure we're talking about the same "combination problem" anymore.

So I'm wondering: is this actually a substantive disagreement about whether relations can constitute subjects, or is it a verbal mess where one side is asking for a mechanistic bridge law and the other is asking for an ontological analysis of unity? Or maybe there's a hidden empirical question — like, if integrated information theory gives us a formal measure of integration, does that settle the horizontal case, or does it just push the mystery into the math? And if the disagreement is bedrock intuition about whether macro-subjects are literally composed of micro-subjects or whether they replace them, is there anything that could actually break the deadlock — a conceptual distinction, a formal result, or should we just accept that this part of panpsychism is stuck? I'd really like a diagnosis rather than another round of the same arguments.
