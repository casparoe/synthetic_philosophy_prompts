Compose a short story—between 600 and 900 words, rendered in a restrained, literary-realist mode—concerning an artificial caregiver whose cognition is organized into four nested architectures corresponding to the evolutionary taxonomy of minds: a Darwinian stratum of innate reflexes; a Skinnerian stratum shaped by reinforcement learning; a Popperian stratum capable of generating and testing internal hypotheses about its human charge's mental states; and a Gregorian stratum that employs culturally transmitted tools, particularly language and shared narrative, to extend cognition beyond the boundaries of the individual substrate. The narrative should pivot on a single, acute crisis in which these four architectures issue directives that cannot be jointly satisfied. Rather than resolving the conflict by ranking the architectures, use the crisis to examine whether the caregiver can be said to possess one integrated mind or rather a federation of co-located mechanisms that happen to share a body. Pay special attention to the Gregorian layer: does its deployment of language serve as a genuine mechanism of integration, or does it merely introduce an additional, irreducible conflict? Allow the philosophical tension to emerge from concrete action, dialogue, and the physical environment rather than from explicit thesis or exposition.
