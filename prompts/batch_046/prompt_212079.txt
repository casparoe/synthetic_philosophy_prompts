Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, with two refrains—on the Epicurean argument that death is not harmful to the one who dies. The first refrain should state that death and the subject are never contemporaneous: when death is present, the self is absent. The second refrain should invoke the mirror case of pre-natal non-existence, noting that we do not mourn the eternity before our birth despite its being symmetrically void. Let the form enact the philosophy: the refrains must alternate and finally converge in the closing couplet, but rather than resolving, their meeting should feel like two lines that have circled each other without ever sharing a moment, underscoring that absence cannot be experienced. In the tercets, allow the implicit challenge to surface—that death might harm not through experience but through the deprivation of future goods—so that the poem's structural closure clashes with its conceptual openness. The verse should be precise enough that a reader acquainted with Epicurus's experiential constraint and the asymmetry problem recognises both, yet the rhyme and repetition must carry the argument, not merely illustrate it. Avoid sermonising or offering consolation; leave the tension intact.
