Here is an argument I have been turning over that seems to prove indirect normativity, paired with an idealized-deliberation condition, cannot accommodate special obligations without cheating. (1) On the indirect view, a reason binds me only if it is ratified by my fully informed, coherent, and unbiased deliberative anchor—the self that chooses without distortion. (2) To avoid smuggling in arbitrary preference, the anchor must be purged of thick indexicals: not merely knowledge of which body is mine, but any framing that treats my projects, relationships, or history as non-interchangeable with anyone else’s. (3) What remains is a thin rational agent capable only of endorsing principles that weight interests impersonally. (4) From that thin standpoint, “I must care for my child” is indistinguishable from “someone with these relational capacities must care for a child with these needs,” and the latter is satisfied by anyone’s intervention. (5) Therefore special obligations dissolve into general duties distributed across all agents, weighted only by causal efficacy and opportunity cost. (6) But this implies that neglecting a distant famine victim is, other things equal, as blameworthy as neglecting one’s own child—an outcome that contradicts the structure of moral practice. (7) So either the purge in (2) removes something that practical reason genuinely requires, or indirect normativity is incompatible with special obligations.

The reasoning looks valid, yet (2) feels like the suspicious step: why treat relational framing as bias rather than as part of what practical reason tracks? If I reject (2) and allow the anchor to retain a thin but genuinely indexical self-concept, can I still claim the anchor is unbiased? Or does any retention of “this life is mine” reintroduce the very distortions the idealization was meant to eliminate? I would prefer to keep (1). Where exactly does the argument fail, and what does each available repair cost—particularly for a defender of indirect normativity who wants to preserve both idealized ratification and agent-relative reasons?
