Read Oscar Wilde's The Importance of Being Earnest through Augustine's eight-category taxanomy of lies in De mendacio. The play is basically one long exercise in Bunburying -- Jack and Algernon maintain false personnas and invent relatives just to dodge social obligations. Augustine sorts lies into eight kinds in that treatise and insists any falsehood spoken with intent to deceive counts, even if the motive is harmless fun. So where do these sustained social fictions fit? Are they verbal lies, or do they fall into some other category he has for pretense? And here's the thing I'm stuck on: the play treats all this deception as charming and necessary, but Augustine seems to think even jocose lies damage the speaker's soul. Does Wilde's comedy actually push back on that, or is there a reading where Augustine's framework explains why the characters eventually have to drop the masks and marry honestly? I want a reading that takes both the play and the taxonomy seriously -- don't reduce Wilde to an illustration -- and say whether the artifice of comedy itself is something Augustine's view can acomodate, or whether it accidentally condemns the genre.
