One camp reads the posthumously published Chapters on Socialism and the cooperative passages in his Autobiography as evidence that Mill genuinely moved toward socialist organization of production, albeit within liberal constraints; the other camp holds that these texts are strictly exploratory thought-experiments, fully consistent with his earlier defense of competitive markets and his explicit rejection of state ownership, so that no doctrinal shift occurred. Rather than letting the disagreement continue in vague terms, I want the kind of joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between these two camps should have produced: the claims both sides genuinely accept about Mill’s texts and intentions; the exact disagreement phrased so that each camp would endorse the wording of its own view; the central cruxes—specifically whether Mill’s production-distribution distinction survives his theory of liberty, and whether his endorsement of worker partnerships presupposes a transformation of human character that his psychology elsewhere rules out; for each crux, a concrete textual passage or argument both sides agree is relevant, together with each side’s predicted interpretation of it; and an assessment of whether any portion of the dispute is merely verbal. Deliver this as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. If my framing misrepresents Mill’s actual arguments or asks for judgments that the texts cannot support without speculation, please state that plainly rather than accommodating the request.
