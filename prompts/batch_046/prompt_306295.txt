If Dennett's multiple drafts model eliminates the Cartesian theater by denying that there is any single canonical narrative presented to consciousness—replacing it with parallel, distributed content-fixations that become reportable only when queried—then it seems to face a reconstructed version of the very problem it was meant to dissolve. For any particular verbal report, say of seeing a red afterimage, there must be a specific draft that supplies the content being expressed. This suggests that, at the moment of report, one among many parallel streams has been functionally privileged, not by being projected onto an inner stage, but by being the one that happens to win the competition for verbal articulation. But if there is no further mechanism—no selector—that explains why this draft rather than another was available for report, then the subject lacks any first-person access to the grounds of her own report. Hasn't Dennett therefore not eliminated the Cartesian theater but only relocated it: from a spatial center where experiences come together, to a temporal-functional index—the present report—that operates with exactly the same opacity and privileged status as Descartes's observing ego? And doesn't this mean his argument succeeds only if we conflate the absence of a localized observer with the absence of any integrative condition whatsoever, when in fact his own account of reportability seems to require precisely such a condition, however distributed?
