Here are my intuitions about belief revision when evidence is uncertain rather than categorical, and I want to know whether they converge on a single updating rule or force a split. In Case 1, you begin with equal priors for Hypothesis A and Hypothesis B, where A makes observation O likely (90%) and B makes O unlikely (10%). You then experience something that feels like O, but you are only 70% sure the experience is veridical and 30% sure it is symmetric noise that carries no information about A or B. I think you should shift closer to the full Bayesian update on O, but dampened—roughly as if you weighted the Bayes factor by 0.7—without altering the conditional probabilities P(O|A) and P(O|B). In Case 2, the structure is the same except that the 30% noise is not symmetric: if A is true, noise tends to mimic B, and if B is true, noise tends to mimic A. Here I think you should not merely weight the Bayes factor; instead, the observation-error model itself should be revised so that P(O|A) and P(O|B) are pulled toward each other, producing a flatter update than in Case 1. In Case 3, you do not observe O directly but receive testimony from a source you judge 80% reliable, and you are unsure whether the source said O or said not-O, with prior probabilities 60% and 40% over the utterance. I think your belief in A should update in the same Jeffrey-style fashion—treating the uncertain utterance as the partition element—despite the uncertainty being linguistic rather than sensory. In Case 4, however, you feel 70% sure that O occurred, but you have independent reason to think that when A is true you systematically overestimate your confidence, and when B is true you systematically underestimate it. Here my intuition is that you should first correct your confidence report for this directional bias and only then apply the update, which seems to violate the Jeffrey prescription to take the posterior probabilities of the evidence partition as given. Is there one principle—perhaps a generalized Jeffrey rule augmented with corrections for correlated noise or for meta-level calibration—that delivers all four verdicts, and if not, which verdict should be overturned? Please state the principle precisely, indicate which clause handles each case, predict its verdict for a situation in which an observer receives two independent noisy signals with different reliabilities about the same partition, and be explicit about which of my four intuitions it cannot accommodate.
