(2) If that failure was genuinely a rational error corrected by recognizing shared capacities for welfare or agency, then the same reasoning applies to every present boundary. The distinctions we currently rely on—biological species, embodied cognition, present-timed existence—are structurally parallel to the distinctions past agents relied on. (3) Therefore, consistency requires that we either extend full moral consideration to all entities with any capacity for welfare, including synthetic minds, ecological systems, and not-yet-existing future beings, or concede that our own boundaries are equally arbitrary. (4) The first option makes moral deliberation impossible: if every allocation of attention or resources is an emergency across an unbounded set of patients, the concept of a duty-bearer with limited obligations collapses. (5) The second option reduces moral history to fashion, denying that there was any rational correction at all. (6) Hence the expanding-circle model is self-defeating: if it is a rational mechanism, it must apply now and destroy the conditions for choice; if it does not apply now, it was never rational in the past.
