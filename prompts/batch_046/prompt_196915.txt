I've noticed a pattern in philosophy arguments that I don't have a word for. Someone cites a real contradiction in one field - like quantum superposition or the liar paradox - and then treats that as a license to hold contradictory beliefs somewhere else entirely, say about justice or identity. But they don't actually run with paraconsistant logic across the board. If you derive an unwanted conclusion from their contradiction using the same rules they just endorsed, they suddenly switch back to classical consistency and accuse you of being unfair. So the contradiction works like a one-way shield: it blocks attacks on their view but never generates obligations for them. It's not dialetheism proper, since there's no systematic logic behind it, and it's not just special pleading because the contradiction is out in the open. Is there a name for this asymmetric, weaponized contradiction-tolerance? If there's no established term, can you define it precisely and say where it ends and honest paraconsistency or plain hypocrisy begins? Also, how is it different from just being sloppy about scope - like, what makes it a distinctive failure mode rather than ordinary inconsistency?
