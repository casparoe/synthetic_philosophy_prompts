Write a joke intended for an audience conversant with analytic philosophy of religion that derives its punchline from the internal mechanics of Molinist middle knowledge—specifically, the claim that counterfactuals of freedom are true prior to any divine creative decree yet lack a metaphysical ground in either God’s nature or the actual world (the so-called grounding objection). To keep the joke from being generic, frame it around a deliberately contrived counterfactual of freedom—say, what a particular analytic philosopher would freely choose to do if placed in a possible world where the grounding objection is satisfied but Molinism is otherwise intact—and let the humor emerge from the paradox that God’s middle knowledge apparently requires observing a free choice that, by the theory’s own lights, has nothing in reality to ground it. The result should be conceptually precise rather than a simple pun.
