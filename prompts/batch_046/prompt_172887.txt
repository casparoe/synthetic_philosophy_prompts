Use as the single carrying case the centuries-long salinization and irrigation fatigue of the Lower Mesopotamian alluvial plain alongside the sudden Akkadian dynastic crisis of the late third millennium BCE; specify what the case must contain (the multi-century soil and canal dynamics, the institutional adaptations, the rapid political rupture) and what it must exclude (the Mediterranean comparison, quantitative economic-history debates, the full Annales school genealogy). Lay out the exact order in which the three temporalities should be introduced and why each layer depends on grasping the prior one; map the predictable misconceptions—climate determinism, equating slowness with causal irrelevance, and the belief that Braudel eliminates human agency—to the precise point in the sequence where each should be interrupted; propose a concrete midway check for understanding that reveals whether students see the dependency relation rather than merely listing layers; and state clearly what must be left out at this level—such as later critiques within the Annales tradition or debates over environmental determinism in historical sociology—and justify the omission as strengthening the explanation. Deliver this as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction and no conclusion.
