I serve on my school's peer-review board for an interdisciplinary science journal, and we must evaluate submissions claiming that a system—biological, computational, or hybrid—possesses genuine understanding rather than mere performance. I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded in the inversion of reasoning that Darwin demonstrated in natural selection and Turing demonstrated in computation, that my board can apply without philosophical training. The procedure should list ordered tests or questions, specify what answer stops the evaluation with a verdict of competence without comprehension, identify the points where judgment cannot be eliminated, and include a specific step testing for counterfactual decoupling—whether the system maintains its performance when the task is presented in a format that separates representation from execution context. Apply it to three cases: a genetic algorithm that designs load-bearing structures without physical intuition; a language model that solves formal logic puzzles; and a bacterial colony that optimizes nutrient paths. Include one case where the procedure gives an answer the board will dislike, and state clearly what the procedure cannot do. We meet twice monthly and have two hours per submission, so design the steps to be runnable in that window.
