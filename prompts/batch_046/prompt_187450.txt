You are delivering the opening remarks at an interdisciplinary workshop titled "Music Beyond Representation: Schopenhauer's Metaphysics and Contemporary Auditory Science," attended by philosophers of art, musicologists, neuroscientists studying predictive processing, and performing musicians. The workshop's guiding problem is whether Schopenhauer's claim in The World as Will and Representation that music is not a representation of Ideas, like painting or poetry, but rather an immediate objectification of the Will itself, can survive engagement with modern empirical accounts of music cognition—particularly predictive-coding frameworks that treat auditory experience as hierarchical error-minimization rather than direct metaphysical disclosure.

Your remarks must do two things simultaneously. First, for the philosophers and musicians present, they must take Schopenhauer's structural analogy seriously: bass lines correspond to the lowest grades of the Will's objectification (inorganic nature), melody to the human grade (the direct voice of willing), and harmony to the intermediate animal and plant grades, with rhythm analogously linking them through universal causality. You should make clear that this is not a metaphor but a metaphysical mapping, and that on Schopenhauer's view music therefore does not represent joy or sorrow but copies the inner nature of willing itself—meaning that to interpret a symphony as being about a narrative or emotion is, strictly speaking, a category mistake within his system.

Second, for the neuroscientists present, you must not dismiss their empirical frameworks, yet you must also not collapse Schopenhauer's claim into a proto-scientific hypothesis about brain states. You should articulate the tension precisely: predictive processing describes how the brain generates auditory expectations, whereas Schopenhauer describes what music is in itself, independent of any subject's cognitive apparatus. The remarks should therefore frame the workshop's task as maintaining this distinction without allowing either side to reduce the other—avoiding both the reduction of metaphysics to neuroscience and the dismissal of empirical findings as irrelevant to aesthetic ontology.

The audience is mixed: some are experts in Schopenhauer's texts, others have never read Book III. Do not cite page numbers, do not provide a bibliography, and do not invoke secondary literature by name. The speech should be approximately 800 words, formal but accessible, and should conclude by proposing a specific constraint for the workshop's discussions: any proposed account of musical meaning must specify whether it treats music as representational (like the other arts) or as direct objectification, and must explain why Schopenhauer's grading of bass, melody, and harmony either succeeds or fails as a structural account rather than a historical curiosity.
