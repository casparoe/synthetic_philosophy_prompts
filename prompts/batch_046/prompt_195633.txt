Write a villanelle—nineteen lines in five tercets followed by one quatrain, with two refrains and only two rhymes—about the metaphysics of masked dispositions. Center the poem on an object that genuinely possesses a disposition (fragility, solubility, flammability, or something analogous) yet is systematically prevented from manifesting it because an interfering condition—a fink or mask—is present precisely when the triggering stimulus occurs. Rather than simply using the words "fink" and "mask," embody the logical problem structurally: let one refrain assert the underlying disposition as if it were straightforward, and let the other refrain assert the interfering condition that neutralizes it. Lock these two lines into the poem's repeating rhyme scheme, so that each return mimics the cycle of the disposition being claimed and then withdrawn. Place the volta in the final quatrain at the moment the mask is either removed or revealed to be permanent; let the rhyme either resolve into manifestation or collapse back into concealment, so the poem's formal closure tracks the metaphysical outcome. Adopt the voice of the object itself—perhaps a sugar cube encased in wax, or a match head kept permanently wet—speaking not as an illustration but as a thing whose nature is genuinely contested. The tone should be contemplative, not sermonizing: leave open whether the object is truly fragile, or whether the mask has become part of what it is.
