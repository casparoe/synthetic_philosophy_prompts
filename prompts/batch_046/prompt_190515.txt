As a novelist constructing a character who maintains contradictory self-narratives without psychological distress—what I call “parallel drafting”—I want to understand whether self-deception is even metaphysically coherent. The familiar paradox asks how a single mind can simultaneously know and not know the same fact; but if we model the self as an editorial collective in which separate belief-drafts circulate without ever being merged into one globally endorsed version, there seems to be no contradiction at all. One draft registers the unwelcome truth, another suppresses it, and no unified subject holds both. From this, self-deception appears structurally identical to fiction-writing: the deceiver is merely the author of a suppressed manuscript, and the classic puzzle dissolves rather than being solved. Please respond by stating your bottom line in the very first sentence, then giving the reasoning with no preamble and without restating my question.
