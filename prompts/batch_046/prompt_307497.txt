Please construct an operational audit procedure for a marine aquaculture certification body, grounded in the following conceptual framework — here termed the Graduated Capacity Model. The model holds that ethical obligations to aquatic organisms depend not on taxonomic class but on three functional capacities: (i) nociceptive registration, being the reflexive detection and avoidance of tissue damage; (ii) integrative affect, being sustained, context-modulated emotional states that cannot be fully explained as reflex loops; and (iii) prospective coherence, being behavioural patterns that indicate preference for future conditions contingent upon present experience. Under this model, a husbandry practice is permissible only if it avoids inflicting harm at every capacity layer that the organism possesses, and it must provide environmental conditions adequate to support the highest layer present.

Your task is to turn this principle into a practical decision procedure that a compliance officer without philosophical training can apply in approximately thirty minutes per proposal. Present it as an ordered sequence of questions, specifying for each: what empirical or observational evidence counts as a sufficient answer; where a negative answer stops the audit with a definitive refusal to certify; and where the answer is genuinely a matter of values rather than facts, requiring the decision to be escalated to an ethics panel. Identify explicitly which conceptual distinctions from the model must be preserved in practice and which can be safely collapsed or ignored without distorting the outcome. The procedure should include clear sign-off points and should acknowledge where it is likely to misfire.

Apply the procedure to two proposals:
1. Mandating electrical stunning for farmed decapod crustaceans prior to boiling, where the facility contends that these animals lack integrative affect and therefore only nociceptive registration need be addressed.
2. Eliminating all substrate heterogeneity and variable current flow from juvenile Atlantic salmon tanks on the grounds that growth metrics are unchanged, despite observed exploratory behaviour that may indicate prospective coherence.

In your answer, write in British English. Conclude with a brief epistemic reflection: state exactly what finding or argument would cause you to revise your verdict in either case, and pinpoint the stage in your reasoning that is most vulnerable to being undermined by revised neuroethological evidence.
