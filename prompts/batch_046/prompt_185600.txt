I've been revisiting Einstein's hole argument—not the usual gloss about coordinate freedom, but the actual underdetermination structure it creates for the metric field inside a matter-free region when you allow diffeomorphisms that are identity outside the region and non-trivial inside. The standard philosophical resolution is anti-haecceitism about spacetime points: since permuting the identities of points doesn't change any physical predictions, the apparently distinct metrics inside the hole represent a single physical state. I think that resolution is correct but incomplete. Please argue for the stronger claim that the hole argument doesn't merely show that points lack primitive individual identity, but that the point-set manifold itself is ontologically redundant—that general relativity should be reconstructed as a theory of the metric field alone, carrying its internal relational structure, and that invoking a background set of points is a representational convenience with no physical counterpart. In particular, address the standard objection that without points there is no domain over which the metric is defined, explaining why that objection mistakes the mathematical machinery of local charts (which can be specified relationally through field coincidences) for an ontological commitment to a point-set substrate. You should ground the argument in the hole argument's core logic—that all observable predictions depend only on coincidence relations among fields and not on point-identity—rather than appealing to quantum gravity or other external theories. I'm looking for a tight, conceptually precise argument, not a literature survey or a list of references.
