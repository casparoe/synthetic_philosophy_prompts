Please explain the conceptual tension between eliminative materialism's rejection of propositional attitudes and the explanatory role of belief-desire psychology in accounting for the behavior of institutional agents such as legislatures or corporations. Specifically, consider whether extending eliminativism from individual cognition to collective entities collapses into a behaviorist reduction that cannot preserve normative distinctions—between, say, a justified legislative decision and a causally regular bureaucratic outcome—without tacitly reintroducing the intentional vocabulary that the view aims to eliminate. Assess whether a replacement framework drawn purely from neuroscience or dynamical systems theory could recover those normative distinctions, or whether collective agency inherently resists eliminative reduction.
