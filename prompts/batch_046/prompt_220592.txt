For a 75-minute seminar with 16 undergraduates who have read the core texts of the socialist calculation debate—specifically the claim that central planners can use a parametric trial-and-error method to converge on equilibrium prices, and the counterclaim that dispersed, tacit knowledge makes such convergence impossible—design a participatory simulation that forces the group to feel a distinction rarely emphasized in standard summaries: the gap between allocative price-correction (which a board can perform with reported data) and dynamic entrepreneurial discovery (which requires that local actors generate new knowledge that did not exist prior to their acting on private cues). Assign roles: four local producers, each holding hidden cards that list both a private cost structure and a potential innovation that can only be unlocked if the producer retains a surplus and acts on local information; a central planning board of three who must set prices each round based only on reported quantities; an auditor of one who tracks whether innovations appear; and observers of eight who record discrepancies. Provide rules for three ten-minute rounds with a point-based payoff: producers score for meeting demand and for unlocking innovations; the board scores for equilibrium convergence and for missing hidden opportunities. Include the materials needed (role cards, price-setting worksheets, innovation tokens), a minute-by-minute timeline, and instructions for the facilitator to reveal nothing about the theoretical framing until after round two. The debrief should map round-one behavior onto Lange's parametric adjustment, round-two hidden-information failures onto the knowledge problem, and round-three innovation absence onto the entrepreneurial-discovery critique; it should also state explicitly what the simulation cannot reproduce—namely the open-ended, multi-period temporal structure of real market discovery over years—and instruct the facilitator to supply that limitation verbally. Note what will predictably go wrong the first time (producers may collude to report false costs, or the board may ignore hidden cards and treat the exercise as pure computation) and how to correct it. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading.
