I've been running a philosophy of science disucssion group and we've been stuck for months on Wigner's old idea about the unreasonable effectiveness of mathemtics in physics. The argument keeps splitting into three camps that don't actually talk to each other. Camp A says it's just selection bias and anthropic filtering: physicists try tons of formal tools, keep the ones that match data, and then act surprised that the survivors work — it's like being amazed that the keys we kept open the doors we tried. Camp B insists that misses the real mystery, because the same deep structures keep showing up across totally unrelated domains in ways that don't look like post-hoc fitting, which suggests reality actually has a kind of mathematical skeleton independent of our choices. Then there's this smaller third view — which I'm partly drawn to — that the framing itself is broken: we act like there's a sharp line between pure mathematical structure and empirical content, but once you drop that distinction you're not explaining a miracle, you're just describing how formal systems and physical measurements co-evolve. But the group keeps looping back to whether this is a metaphysical puzzle about the world, a cognitive illusion about filtering, or just a badly posed question.

My working theory is that the stall isn't really about physics or mathematics at all, but about what each side demands from an explanation. Camp B wants an ontological story about why reality is structured, Camp A wants an epistemic story about how we pick tools, and they keep using the same examples — symmetry groups, prediction of particles, whatever — to talk past each other. But I'm not sure if that's the whole story. Could there be a hidden empirical disagreement about the actual historical record of failed formalisms that never made it into textbooks? Or is the impasse a sign that 'effectiveness' is being used in two different senses, one about predictive success and one about explanatory depth? I want an honest diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, substantive, about hidden metaphysical commitments, or about standards of explanation — tied to what each camp really says. And if your diagnosis is right, what would actually move it? Would a formal result about the uniqueness of certain representations help, or a conceptual distinction between applied and pure structures, or should we just admit the question is malformed and stop expecting progress?
