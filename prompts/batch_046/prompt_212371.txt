As the examiner for an advanced tutorial I have designed on the architecture of political prose, I require an independent grading of the student work reproduced below. The examination prompt was as follows: consider a distinction between architectural corruption—where a language is structurally redesigned to render certain propositions unformulable—and performative corruption—where available linguistic resources are used to obscure meaning without altering the underlying grammar or lexicon. Examine whether Orwell’s well-known essay on political language describes only performative corruption, whether the mechanism of Newspeak in his novel represents pure architectural corruption, and whether either text admits a hybrid form. Be precise about points of convergence and divergence, and avoid treating similarity of effect as proof of identity of mechanism.

The student submitted this response:

In Orwell’s essay, political language is performatively corrupt: writers deploy dead metaphors and pretentious diction to conceal ugly realities, yet the grammatical and lexical structure remains fully intact. Newspeak, by contrast, is architecturally corrupt: it eliminates antonyms, restricts vocabulary, and flattens grammatical complexity so that heretical propositions become literally inexpressible. Thus the essay attributes corruption to individual moral laziness, whereas Newspeak attributes it to state engineering. Despite this divergence in cause, both mechanisms produce an identical result—the suppression of critical thought. Orwell proposes that writers can resist corruption by adopting concrete, precise vocabulary and abandoning ready-made phrases. Yet Newspeak demonstrates that simplicity of vocabulary offers no protection when the architecture itself is restricted; consequently, Orwell’s stylistic remedy would be powerless against a system of architectural control, which demands political rather than rhetorical resistance. From this it follows that all linguistic corruption is ultimately an exercise of power, and that the moral quality of individual speakers is essentially irrelevant.

Please assign a specific grade—either a letter or a score out of ten—and justify it with itemized comments that examine the answer claim by claim. The commentary should distinguish between points that are well defended and points that rest on a conflation, an unsupported inference, or a mischaracterized view, without collapsing these into a single overall impression. Do not state your own agreement or disagreement with the student’s interpretation; confine yourself to the accuracy of the reasoning and the fidelity of the account given.
