I sit on the University Committee for the Ethical Oversight of Animal Research, and I am composing this request in the immediate aftermath of a long, intellectually intense argument with a philosopher friend about whether institutional practices of 'respect' can be coherent within a strictly Kantian framework that denies direct moral status to non-rational beings. By Thursday's session we must reach a decision on Protocol 24-C-09, submitted by the Department of Comparative Cognition: a twenty-four-month study of problem-solving in captive ravens that is methodologically conventional except for one institutional innovation. The researchers propose that upon the study's conclusion, each bird be given an individual name, entered into a permanent university archive with a brief behavioral narrative, and disposed of through a designated 'dignified closure' procedure rather than standard biomedical waste protocols. They defend this explicitly not as recognition of rights — they deny that ravens possess the rational autonomy that grounds direct moral status in Kant's practical philosophy — but as a structured practice of indirect duty, intended to cultivate in staff a durable disposition of care that protects human moral character. My friend contended that this is a category mistake: indirect duties to animals, on her reading, are justified solely by analogical relations to human suffering and by the risk that cruelty degrades our treatment of rational beings; to institutionalize memorialization is therefore to treat the animals as if they possessed the very dignity Kant denies them, collapsing the direct/indirect distinction and undermining the framework's coherence. I countered that ritual need not carry ontological commitment and can function purely as a technology of self-cultivation, but we reached an impasse. Please draft the formal decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. Recommend a concrete course of action — approve the protocol with the ritual intact, approve with the ritual excised, or reject with specified conditions — together with a clear implementation or revision time frame. The memo must treat the researchers' Kantian defense charitably, engage the strongest version of the objection that memorialization corrupts the conceptual architecture of indirect duty, and present an honest, substantive account of the best reason on the side you do not take. Write it as a document the committee can act upon, addressing both philosophical coherence and the institutional precedent this case would establish.
