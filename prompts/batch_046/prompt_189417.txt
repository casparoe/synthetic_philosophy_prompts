A colleague is revising her estate plan and has declined expensive life-extending treatments, maintaining that death cannot harm her because, once dead, there will be no subject present to experience anything. She returns to this reasoning often. At the same time, she has directed substantial funds towards a legal clause that prevents her unpublished manuscripts from being edited or released after her death in ways she dislikes, claiming that such misrepresentation would genuinely damage her interests. When I noted that both scenarios appear to involve harm befalling a non-existent subject, she replied that legacy concerns affect the living world she values, whereas death is simply an experiential blank. I sense an unresolved philosophical tension, yet I cannot articulate precisely where the inconsistency lies, if indeed there is one. Could you advise me on whether her two stances are compatible, and, if not, which consideration ought to prevail?
