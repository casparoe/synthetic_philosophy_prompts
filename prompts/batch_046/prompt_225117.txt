I've been arguing with friends in a rationalist reading group about whether expected-value maximization is salvageable, and I've realized we keep talking past each other because nobody has laid out the full spec first. The problem isn't just Pascal's mugging or St. Petersburg lotteries—it's a deeper tension between the linearity of expectation and any plausible bound on rational commitment, and I think we need to separate structural fanaticism (the linear aggregation of value across outcomes) from parametric fanaticism (unbounded utility curves that make mega-payoffs dominate). Before I evaluate any proposed fix, I want the complete list of desiderata that a non-fanatical normative decision theory must actually deliver. Specify the cases it has to get right: it should reject one-shot exploitation by astronomically unlikely mega-payoffs without collapsing into risk-averse paralysis for ordinary entrepreneurship or altruistic longshots; it must preserve transitivity in clear cases yet explain or dissolve Allais-style ambiguity without hand-waving; it must distinguish practical rationality from epistemic rationality so that updating beliefs isn't confused with committing resources; it must remain consistent with Savage-style coherence where coherence is genuinely normative rather than merely pragmatic, and it must not contradict the empirical regularities of small-stakes risk preference; and it must say whether solving structural fanaticism alone is sufficient, or whether parametric bounds are independently required. For each desideratum, give the motivating case or formal result behind it, flag which are non-negotiable (e.g., avoiding Dutch books in single-decision contexts) versus camp preferences (e.g., determinism in choice, the independence axiom, cardinal measurability, or separability), and note if any pair looks jointly unsatisfiable given standard impossibility results. Then briefly score bounded-utility theories, risk-weighted expectation models, and lexical probability-threshold approaches against your list. I want a committed verdict: take a definite position on which subset of these requirements can be jointly satisfied, identify the single desideratum that does the most damage to standard EV maximization, and defend one of the three approaches as the least-broken option. Don't just map the landscape—plant a flag and tell me which theory you'd actually bet on.
