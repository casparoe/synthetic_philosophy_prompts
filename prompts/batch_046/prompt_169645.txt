Write a villanelle—nineteen lines arranged as five tercets followed by a closing quatrain, with rhyme scheme ABA and two refrains, A1 and A2, that alternate through the tercets and then appear together in the final two lines—about the specific phenomenological crux in Daniel Dennett's brain-transplant thought experiment from his essay Where Am I?. The first refrain, A1, should voice the first-person location of the self with the body outside the vat (for instance, a line expressing that the speaker stands in the operating theater staring at the brain in the glass); the second refrain, A2, should voice the competing first-person location of the self inside the vat with the brain (for instance, a line expressing that the speaker floats in bubbling fluid being observed by eyes outside). These refrains should not be identical across stanzas but should maintain their core claim, and their final meeting in the quatrain should leave the location of the self structurally unresolved rather than synthesized. The poem should render Dennett's specific failure of imaginative projection—his attempt to think that he is suspended in a bubbling fluid and his inability to carry that thought off with conviction—while also registering his underlying physicalist conviction that the tokening of thoughts occurs in the brain. Do not treat the scenario as generic brain-in-a-vat fiction; the poem should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with Dennett's argument recognizes the distinction between the brain, Yorick, kept in the Houston life-support vat, and the body, Hamlet, that remains in the theater, and recognizes why the narrator cannot relocate his center of gravity. To avoid relying on pretraining clichés about the scenario, include one invented sensory contradiction not present in Dennett's original text—such as the radio-link transmission registering to the body as a persistent taste of copper, or the life-support fluid registering as cold rain on the skin of the body—so that the phenomenological tension has a niche, non-canonical texture. The poem should not include bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or requests for further sources. Write the response in American English. At the end, state how confident you are in the philosophical accuracy of the poem's rendering of Dennett's puzzle and identify the single point about which you are least sure.
