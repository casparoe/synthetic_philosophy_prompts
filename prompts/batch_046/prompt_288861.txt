In my philosophy of science / ethics crossover group we've been circling particularism vs generalism for like eight months and its going nowhere. The particularist keeps saying that a feature like being painful isn't just a reason that gets cancelled — sometimes its an enabler for other reasons, sometimes an intensifier, sometimes nothing at all — and the generalist keeps saying thats just a more complicated principle with context-sensitive weights. But then we just loop. Is this disagreement actually about whether moral reasons are holist in this specific way (with enablers and intensifiers), or is it verbal over what counts as a principle? Or are they answering different questions: one about the ontology of reasons, one about the epistemology of moral learning? And is there any move — maybe a formal model of reason-interaction, or a finding from moral psychology about how people actually weight features — that would break the loop, or is it just bedrock intuitions about whether morality is principled? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey, and an honest answer about whether anything could actually move it or if we should just expect the stall.
