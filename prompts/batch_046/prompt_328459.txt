I've been tinkering with a story that involves a charity distributing medical supplies — don't worry, it's not a morality play, just background — and I've been reading ethics to get the details right. I keep noticing this move in arguments about who should get what first. Call it the "stuck ceiling" thing: everyone agrees you shouldn't take medicine away from a healthy person just to make the gap smaller, but then people argue that if you've got one extra dose, giving it to the healthy person is worse than giving it to the sick person not just because the sick person needs it more, but because the healthy person's gain "doesn't count" unless there's a matching gain at the bottom. So the healthy people aren't pulled down; they're just never allowed to go up unless someone below comes with them.

I saw one version of this in a discussion about education funding — don't fund the good school unless the bad school gets funded too — and another in healthcare triage where people said saving a well-off patient was "morally cheaper" only if a worse-off patient was saved in the same decision. Is there an actual term for this? It feels adjacant to the leveling-down objection people talk about with prioritarianism, but it's not pulling anyone down — it's more like a conditional lock on upward movement. Is this just prioritarian weighting dressed up in egalitarian clothing, or is there a specific concept for this "no lift without parallel lift" rule? And if I'm inventing it, where exactly does it differ from a regular budget line or from the standard leveling-down worry?
