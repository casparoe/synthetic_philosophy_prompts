Write a short research proposal (suitable for a three-month philosophy workshop or graduate fellowship) addressing this precise question: does the internalist thesis that all practical reasons must be reachable from an agent’s existing motivational set survive cases of radical, transformative psychological change—where core evaluative commitments are replaced—without collapsing into tautology? Specifically, can internalism be reconstructed by distinguishing diachronic accessibility (reasons reachable through a rational process of change over time) from synchronic accessibility (reasons reachable only from current motives), and does that reconstruction avoid the externalist charge that it smuggles in normative premises about which changes are rational? State why standard Williams-style and Parfit-style positions do not settle the transformative-change case; outline a conceptual, non-empirical approach that analyzes conditions for personal identity across change and the logic of practical reasoning during transformation rather than running psychological studies; describe what a defensible result would look like, what would count as failure, and how the project stays within a single-semester scope. Refer to positions and arguments generally without providing a bibliography or citation list. Finally, indicate what specific argument or consideration would change your mind about the viability of the diachronic reconstruction, and identify the point in your reasoning most likely to fail.
