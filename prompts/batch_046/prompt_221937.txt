I'm trying to get clear on my own metaphysics of propositions and relations—specifically around Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition—before I commit these views to a paper, and I want a consistency audit rather than a general survey. Here is where I stand:

(a) Propositions are structured entities composed of particular objects and universals arranged in a definite order; there is no hidden "propositional glue" or combining relation among their constituents, since the structural arrangement itself is primitive.

(b) Bradley's regress is genuinely fatal for any ontology that explains the unity of a complex fact by postulating a further relating relation; I therefore reject all "tie" or "nexus" theories in metaphysics.

(c) At the same time, I take external relations to be real and irreducible: when a stands in R to b, the relation genuinely connects its terms and is not analyzable into monadic properties of a and b alone.

(d) Every true relational proposition has a corresponding truthmaker—specifically, a state of affairs in which those very objects stand in that very relation—and I treat this state of affairs as a single, unified entity rather than as a mere plurality of objects plus a relation.

(e) For negative propositions (e.g., that a is not F), I deny that there are negative states of affairs or absences serving as truthmakers; instead, such propositions are made true by the failure of the corresponding positive state of affairs to exist, even though the negative proposition itself still possesses a unified, determinate content.

(f) In cognition, I think we grasp a proposition only through an act of mental synthesis—something like judgment or binding—that combines the constituent concepts, and I regard this psychological synthesis as supplying the experienced unity without implying that there is any corresponding metaphysical synthesizer inside the proposition itself.

Which of these commitments actually pull against one another, which pairs only appear to be in tension, and what is the least costly revision that restores consistency? I am especially unsure whether my anti-regress stance in (b) sits comfortably with my claim in (d) that states of affairs are unified entities, and whether my realism about external relations in (c) conflicts with my reliance on mental synthesis in (f). Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging, and for each serious tension specify exactly which commitment would have to be modified or abandoned and at what theoretical cost. If there is an apparent conflict that dissolves on closer inspection, identify it and explain why it is only superficial.

Present this as an audit of my position, not as a lecture on Bradley or Russell. Please conclude with a line that reads exactly: Verdict: [the least-cost revision, with the top-ranked tension noted].
