I am the student delegate on my district's Secondary School Governance Review Panel. We must issue a formal recommendation memo by Thursday regarding Charter Amendment 7-B, which proposes three simultaneous changes to student-council elections: first, compulsory voting for all enrolled students, with non-participation registered as an explicit blank ballot rather than an absence; second, lowering the voting age from sixteen to thirteen so that middle-school students in the district are enfranchised; and third, extending full voting rights to non-citizen students, including those on temporary exchange visas and those without documented immigration status, on the explicit grounds that anyone subject to school disciplinary and curricular authority should have an equal formal voice in shaping that authority. Proponents frame this as an application of the affected-interests principle, arguing that citizenship and age-based competence are arbitrary gatekeepers in an institutional context where rules apply universally; opponents argue that compulsory voting coerces political expression, that cognitive-developmental considerations undermine the reliability of thirteen-year-old electoral judgment in representative bodies, and that decoupling the franchise from citizenship or legal residency erodes the distinction between being governed and being a member of the governing community. The panel must recommend unconditional approval, rejection, or approval subject to specific conditions and a phased timeline, and the memo must present the recommendation with reasoning that takes the strongest counter-argument seriously rather than dismissing it. Please draft the memo I will circulate before the meeting.
