Please argue that when a community's collective memory and the official historical record come into sustained conflict, the record—not the memory—should be revised or suspended, but only under the specific condition that the community's remembrance operates through what might be termed distributed epistemic stewardship: a practice in which testimony is evaluated not by individual documentary verification but by intergenerational coherence, internal corrective feedback, and the structural absence of the community's own voice from the archival institutions that produced the record. Defend this view against the charge that it licenses collective fabrication, and explain how your position handles cases in which the archival gap is not accidental omission but an institutional product of the very power structures that generated the record.
