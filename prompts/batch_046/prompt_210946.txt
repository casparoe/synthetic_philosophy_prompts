I'm putting together my position on Kant's perpetual peace for a seminar on international political philosophy, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present. Here is where I've landed. I think membership in this federation has to remain genuinely revocable; otherwise we have collapsed into the very world-government Kant explicitly rejects. At the same time, I think perpetual peace is genuinely achievable, not just a regulative idea we strive toward, because once republican states are bound together by shared commercial and legal interdependence, the economic and strategic costs of exit become prohibitive—so revocation is legally permitted but practically impossible. I also think Kant's guarantee of nature works through gradual evolutionary reform rather than through revolutionary upheaval, yet I am convinced that without periodic revolutionary catalysts—like the spread of republican ideals through revolutionary example—gradual reform stalls and the evolutionary path never reaches its endpoint. I accept that republican constitutions prevent war because citizens must consent to bear its costs, but I also think that in modern representative systems this consent mechanism is largely symbolic, since foreign policy is so distant from ordinary voters that the Kantian guarantee is far weaker today than he assumed. Finally, I think cosmopolitan right creates direct moral obligations between individuals across state borders—particularly regarding hospitality and non-deception—yet these obligations never justify overriding a state's domestic jurisdiction over citizenship or entry, since that jurisdiction is protected by the very sovereignty the federation is meant to preserve.
