I'm preparing the interview guide for a philosophy-of-mind podcast series that draws an audience steeped in rationalist and effective-altruist discussions of epistemic hygiene. Next week's guest is an unnamed philosopher defending a specific, non-canonical variant of introspective skepticism she calls structural update-blindness. On her view, introspection delivers propositional content and broad affective valence with reasonable fidelity, but it is constitutionally unreliable about the actual computational update rules applied during belief revision—analogous to the way a running executable cannot faithfully inspect its own source weights without decompilation artifacts. She further contends that this blindness is especially pronounced in domains where identity and community norms, such as rationalist calibration practices and Bayesian self-image, generate powerful post-hoc coherence pressures.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that avoid asking her to restate the view from scratch. I want the sequence to build in three clusters. First, have her distinguish her position from classical Cartesian transparency failures and from modern higher-order tracking theories; then press whether her computational analogy quietly commits her to a functionalist eliminativism about first-person authority. Second, move to the rationalist-specific application: ask whether practitioners who claim high introspective access to their own belief-updating are essentially performing narrative coherence maintenance rather than genuine calibration monitoring. Third, probe the normative stakes—whether her view undermines the practical self-improvement and reflective-equilibrium projects that rationalist culture encourages.

For each cluster, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. For the first cluster, anticipate her reply that the distinction hinges on access to outputs versus access to transition functions, and ask what empirical finding about neural or cognitive architecture would collapse that distinction. For the second, anticipate her denial of eliminativism via an appeal to restricted authority over outputs, and ask whether that restricted authority is robust enough to ground the actual self-modification practices rationalists undertake. For the third, anticipate her concession that calibration narratives are partly performative but her insistence that they remain locally reliable, and ask how she avoids reducing introspective practice to a self-deceptive signaling game without surrendering the epistemic value she still grants it.

Keep the tone formal and polished, appropriate for listeners familiar with LessWrong-style debates about belief updating and reflective equilibrium. Do not include generic biographical questions, and do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
