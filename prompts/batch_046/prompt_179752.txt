I think about causation mainly through structural causal models, do-operators, and the distinction between endogenous mechanisms and exogenous interventions. Restate al-Ghazālī's occasionalism — the denial that fire, medicine, or any created thing possesses an intrinsic causal power; the claim that God is the direct and immediate cause of every particular effect, using the apparent cause only as an occasion; and the explanation of observed regularity through divine habit rather than natural law — entirely in that vocabulary. Be precise about the mapping: which occasionalist claims become claims about exogenous nodes, which become claims about the absence of autonomous structural equations, and whether "occasion" can be represented as a correlation structure without collapsing into Humean constant conjunction. Then give the residue report: what survives cleanly, what must be distorted (for example, can the occasionalist rejection of secondary causation be expressed as a denial of non-interventionist edges, or does it require a metaphysics of power that the framework lacks?), and whether anything essential — perhaps the normative or theological status of the regularities — has no expression in an interventionist ontology at all. Finally, say whether the residue suggests that occasionalism is articulating a constraint on causal models that they usually ignore, or whether it is just a theologically loaded way of stating that all causation is exogenous.
