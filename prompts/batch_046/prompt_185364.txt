I have been working through debates about whether pleasure is the sole intrinsic good, and I have noticed a recurring argumentative pattern for which I lack a precise label. A defender of hedonism lists apparently disparate pleasant experiences—the taste of ripe fruit, the physical warmth after being cold, the intellectual satisfaction of completing a proof, and the relief when a headache subsides—and claims they all share some common qualitative feature. When a critic observes that the first three experiences share little phenomenological character and that relief is essentially the cessation of pain, the defender does not abandon the shared-feature claim but substitutes a different unifier: perhaps a particular positive tone, then being desired for its own sake, then feeling good in a non-evaluative sense, then standing in opposition to suffering. Each proposed essence survives the counterexample that undermined the previous one, yet none remains stable across the full range of examples. At the same time, the defender continues to employ pleasure as a uniform normative currency, treating the discovery that an experience is pleasant as if it yielded a determinate value judgment, despite the absence of any fixed boundary for the category.

Is there an established concept for this specific dialectical structure—the iterative retreat to a new essential feature for pleasure, paired with the continued normative deployment of the category as though its membership conditions were settled? I am familiar with the general heterogeneity objection and with responses that invoke family resemblance or dimensional variation, but I am asking about the dynamics themselves: the tailored succession of proposed unifiers, each calibrated to survive its predecessor's failure, together with the asymmetry between the elasticity of the concept's extension and the rigidity of its evaluative function. If no specific term captures this pattern, how should it be delineated, and how does it differ from ordinary ad hoc revision or from an explicit family-resemblance view that forthrightly rejects essentialism?
