Consider a two-year interdisciplinary working group—two philosophers of mind, one cognitive neuroscientist, and one ML researcher whose expertise is in interpretability rather than consciousness science—tasked with advancing conceptual clarity around higher-order theories of consciousness (HOT). Rather than a survey, I want a defended prioritization of which open conceptual tensions in this framework deserve their limited collective effort, ordered by upstream dependency and tractability for this specific team composition.

Frame the agenda around three live fault lines. First, whether a higher-order representation must be occurrent and actively deployed at the time of consciousness, or whether a dispositional or “archival” structure—where the first-order content is stored in a retrieval-ready format that makes it available for report or guidance without being simultaneously monitored—can carry the explanatory load. Second, whether the “targeting” relation between a first-order state and its higher-order counterpart is best understood as a form of internal attention, as a direct meta-representational tagging, or as an inferential reconstruction, since each analysis implies different neural-implementation commitments and different conditions under which artificial hierarchical representations might qualify. Third, whether HOT can accommodate states that appear phenomenally present yet resist clear higher-order characterization—such as diffuse emotional background moods or the fringe of awareness—without either diluting into a first-order account or multiplying ad hoc auxiliary conditions.

For each fault line, defend its rank in the agenda: explain which other questions depend on resolving it first, what kind of progress (formal, conceptual, or empirically-informed) would constitute success within two years, and what specific trade-off justifies deprioritizing it if resources are tight. In particular, address whether the ML researcher’s capacity to probe hierarchical representations in artificial networks should steer the group toward the implementation-sensitive targeting-relation question, or whether that risks misallocating philosophical labor that ought to remain conceptually prior to any interpretability project.

Conclude with concrete sequencing: what should dominate year one versus year two, what intermediate result would signal that the ordering is correct, and what finding—conceptual or empirical—would cause you to reorder the priorities. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations to specific authors and works; I want reasoning that stands on its own.
