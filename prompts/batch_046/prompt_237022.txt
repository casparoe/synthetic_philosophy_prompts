I'm a student in an advanced philosophy of rationality course, and for our final session on diachronic coherence we're discussing money pumps: the scenario in which an agent with cyclic pairwise preferences among three options can be led through a sequence of individually rational trades back to her initial bundle, paying a fee at each step so that she is strictly worse off despite never reversing a single preference ordering. Rather than ending with a standard summary, I'd like to close the seminar with a poem that makes the structural problem visceral through form. Please write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines, followed by a three-line envoi—using the following six end-words in the standard rotational pattern across all stanzas: preference, trade, return, loss, cycle, reason. The formal repetition should mirror the exploitation: each stanza should advance one transition of the pump (A preferred to B, B to C, C to A), with an incremental cost exacted at each step, so that by the sixth stanza the loop is complete and the accumulated loss is total. In the envoi, arrange the six words so that either reason stands adjacent to cycle with loss displaced outward (representing the diachronic-rationality solution that forbids the sequence) or so that return and preference are paired with loss still embedded inside the triad (representing the synchronic view that tolerates exploitation). The poem should be technically accurate—someone who knows the literature on Dutch books and diachronic consistency should recognize the mechanics of intransitivity exploited over time—but it should also work as poetry for a reader unfamiliar with decision theory. Please do not include citations, references to specific authors, or bibliographic notes; the aim is to let the form carry the philosophical point.
