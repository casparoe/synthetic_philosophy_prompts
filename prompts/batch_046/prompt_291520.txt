I'm drafting my position on the epistemology of financial markets for a seminar-style discussion among people who've read the standard rationalist literature on prediction markets and decision theory, and I want a consistency check before I present. My current view is a bundle of commitments that I'm not sure cohere: (a) I accept the canonical no-trade result—under common priors with rational Bayesian updating, voluntary exchange reveals information that should prevent the trade from happening in the first place; (b) despite this, I believe real markets aggregate dispersed knowledge in a genuinely epistemic way, such that prices often out-perform individual experts; (c) I resolve the tension by saying real traders hold heterogeneous priors and different causal models, not just different private signals, which breaks the common-prior premise; (d) but I also think that if priors are heterogeneous in this deep way, then the market price is not converging on an objective truth but is merely a weighted compromise among incommensurable world-models; (e) still, I treat market forecasts as having predictive authority that I should rationally update on, rather than dismissing them as arbitrary social constructions; and (f) I hold a reflexive corollary—if I spot what I take to be a systematic bias in market pricing and trade against it, I should simultaneously downgrade my confidence in my own diagnosis, because the counterparty's refusal to accept my price is itself evidence that my "bias" is visible and priced in, creating a loop where acting on epistemic disagreement alters its evidential status.

Which of these pull against each other in ways that go beyond the obvious? In particular, is there a hidden contradiction between (d) and (e)—treating prices as incommensurable compromises versus treating them as authoritative forecasts? Does (f) secretly reintroduce the common-prior logic of (a) and undermine (c), or can it be sustained only if heterogeneity is shallow rather than deep? Please rank the tensions by severity, identify any pairs that only look incompatible but dissolve on inspection, and tell me what the least costly revision would be and exactly what I would have to give up.
