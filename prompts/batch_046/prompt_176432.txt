I am grading final exams for a graduate seminar on evolutionary and comparative cognition, and I have become stuck on one student’s response to the second essay question. The prompt asked students to outline Daniel Dennett’s fourfold taxonomy—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures—paying particular attention to what distinguishes internal hypothesis-selection from culturally mediated cognition, and then to apply that distinction to the documented behavior of New Caledonian crows that manufacture hook tools and subsequently modify the design after observing conspecific successes and failures, apparently without direct individual reinforcement for each variant.

The student wrote the following (reproduced from handwritten work, preserving logical structure and vocabulary):

‘Dennett’s taxonomy sorts creatures by how they solve problems. Darwinian ones are hard-wired by natural selection; they do not learn within a lifetime. Skinnerian creatures learn through operant conditioning—trial and error with rewards and punishments—so they avoid dangerous mistakes by being shaped gradually. Popperian creatures go further: they can generate possible actions inside their minds and evaluate them before trying them out, which Dennett compares to Popper’s idea that scientific theories should be falsified without killing the scientist. Gregorian creatures are essentially Popperians who have acquired cultural tools—especially language—that allow them to import ideas from other minds and think in ways that would be impossible alone.

In the case described, the crows manufacture tools and then refine them after watching other crows succeed or fail. Because the refinement depends on observing others, the cognitive work is being done by the social group rather than inside each individual crow’s head. That makes them Gregorian: they are using the group as an external source of tested hypotheses, which is precisely what cultural transmission provides. A purely Popperian crow would have to imagine each tool shape internally before building it, but here the shapes are being filtered through social observation. So they have crossed into Gregorian cognition, though they probably lack full language.’

Please assign a letter grade and justify it with specific, point-by-point comments. Identify exactly where the characterizations of Darwinian, Skinnerian, and Popperian cognition are sound; locate the subtle conflation between observational social learning and genuinely Gregorian cultural scaffolding; and assess whether the student understands that Gregorian status requires the acquisition of mind-tools that restructure individual cognition, rather than merely the presence of group-filtered behavior. Do not give a vague impression—provide the precise errors, the partial-credit reasoning, and the corrections that would be needed to earn full marks, so that I can return defensible feedback.
