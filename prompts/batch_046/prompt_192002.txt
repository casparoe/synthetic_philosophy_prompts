In discussions about whether visitors are bound to follow these practices, my colleagues repeatedly invoke the proverb that when in Rome one ought to do as the Romans do, using it to argue that anyone present in a locality has a moral obligation to comply—whether that means joining a strict line for public transit, tipping in a service economy where wages depend on it, or obeying anti-jaywalking rules even when no vehicles are near. I need to understand exactly what theses are packed into that sentence so I can see whether it actually supports those conclusions.

For each reading, please say whether it is trivial or definitional, whether it is substantive and defensible, whether it is false, and—crucially—which readings would justify a moral obligation for a temporary visitor as opposed to a merely prudential or aesthetic reason to conform.
