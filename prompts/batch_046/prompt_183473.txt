As a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of science and social choice theory, please write a satirical internal memorandum—approximately 600 words, in the deadpan bureaucratic voice of a university “Committee for the Aggregation of Expert Judgment”—reporting that the committee has discovered the discursive dilemma in its own voting records. The memo should describe how individual members consistently hold judgments on premises P, Q, and R → S that are logically consistent and entail S, yet separate majority votes on the premises and on the conclusion yield an inconsistent collective recommendation. The satire should exaggerate the institutional response: propose procedural fixes such as confidence-weighted voting, “premise-level quorum rules,” or outsourcing entailment checks to an external contractor, all presented with straight-faced seriousness. The target should be the administrative fantasy that judgment aggregation can be repaired by procedural tinkering without confronting the fundamental tension between premise-based and conclusion-based aggregation. To ground the piece, invent a concrete case study—perhaps a funding decision weighing climate-model robustness against epidemiological forecasting reliability—where the dilemma produces a contradictory committee outcome. The humor must come from accurate engagement with the formal structure of the dilemma rather than caricature; a reader familiar with the standard impossibility result should recognize that you understand what is being satirized, and the memo should be readable to someone who does not. After the memorandum, append a brief, clearly separated note identifying which elements depend on contested interpretations—specifically, whether premise-based aggregation is the uniquely rational response and whether weighting judgments by epistemic confidence genuinely dissolves the tension—while distinguishing the settled formal result (that no aggregation rule can simultaneously satisfy universal domain, anonymity, and systematicity across both premises and conclusions) from genuinely contested normative and practical responses. Do not present any contested resolution as the consensus view.
