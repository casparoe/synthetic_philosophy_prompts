If Émilie du Châtelet were alive today and read modern happiness research — especialy the idea that people have a kind of emotional set-point and big life changes barely move long-term well-being — do you think she’d see it as backing up her old argument that study and illusion are what really keep us content? She was pretty insistent that passions drive everything, but also that we need certain illusions to stay happy. Would she take this hedonic adaptation stuff as proof that passions are unreliable and that contentment-based happiness is separate from physical circumstances? And how do you think she’d react to people using brain scans to measure well-being — given she saw happiness as tied to study and feeling, not just bodily states? I’m curious if she’d think neuroimaging misses the point entirely.
