I've been trying to work out a distinction that seems simple at first but keeps getting tangled in my head: the difference between using words to describe something and using words to actually do something. Everyone knows that when a judge pronounces a defendant guilty in the proper setting, or when someone says "I bet you five dollars," the speaking itself changes the world in a way that merely describing the weather never could. But I'm stuck on the ordinary middle cases—like when I tell my roommate "It's freezing in here" and clearly want him to close the window, without ever saying "Please close it." One way to think about this is that I'm literally describing the temperature but also, as a kind of hidden layer, performing the act of requesting. Another way is that in this context the sentence just takes on the force of a request, so there's no hidden layer at all—the meaning itself shifts. I want to understand which of these pictures is right, and especially whether there's a real conceptual line between them or just a convenient fiction we use to save face.
