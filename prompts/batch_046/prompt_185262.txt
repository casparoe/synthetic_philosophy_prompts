After a long argument with a friend about blame, hypocrisy, and who gets to speak, I have tried to formalize exactly where I stand. I want a consistency audit of my own package, not a survey of the literature. Here are my commitments:

(1) Standing to blame is relational and stake-based: what matters is being affected by the wrong or having a legitimate interest in its correction, not moral purity or consistency.

(2) I maintain a strict separation between "having standing" and "blame being appropriate all-things-considered"; they come apart regularly.

(3) Hypocrisy undermines blame only instrumentally—by making the normative message hard to hear—not by removing standing itself.

(4) I reject the "clean hands" doctrine: one can have full standing to blame for theft even while being a thief, provided one acknowledges one's own fault and is working to rectify it.

(5) In structural or institutional wrongs, however, if you are actively benefiting from the system you condemn without resistance, you have literally zero standing to blame others for participating in it.

(6) The parental case is special: parents who smoke retain standing to blame their children for smoking, not because of purity, but because the parent-child relation generates a distinctive normative authority that survives shared fault.

(7) I think blame has a performative dimension: the blamer implicitly claims authority from a position not constituted by the very wrong being condemned; if one's social position is constituted by that wrong, the performance is incoherent regardless of personal fault.
