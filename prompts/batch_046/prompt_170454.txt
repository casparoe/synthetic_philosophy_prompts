I have been wondering whether it is legitimate for unelected judges to invalidate laws enacted by elected legislatures. The standard case is Marbury v. Madison (1803), in which the Supreme Court asserted the power to strike down congressional statutes. The usual story is that this was a straightforward assertion of judicial supremacy over a democratic body. But I want to examine a different reading: that the Court was not simply overruling Congress, but engaging in a strategic negotiation with the executive and legislative branches—claiming authority in principle while avoiding a direct enforcement clash, particularly given President Jefferson's refusal to deliver the commissions at issue. Analyze the episode through this inter-branch negotiation lens. Who were the actual players and what were their available moves? Does the case show judicial review functioning as democratic negotiation rather than anti-democratic overruling, or does the negotiation framing obscure the real power dynamics? And what does the episode, in turn, reveal about whether the counter-majoritarian objection to judicial review assumes a false model of how courts and legislatures actually interact?
