I’m on the board of our neighborhood food co-op — we got about 80 members now and we’re voting next month on whether to switch from a manager-employee setup to full worker-ownership, where every worker gets an equal vote on schedules, suppliers, the whole thing. A few folks have been saying John Stuart Mill in his later years was actually pro-socialist, but only the cooperative kind where people voluntarily associate, not the government-takes-everything kind, and they think that means we should go all-in. Others are pushing back saying Mill’s main worry was about tyranny of majorities — even in small democratic groups — so letting every worker vote on inventory and shifts is just gonna let the majority crush anyone who disagrees. I’m not an academic, just read a little and been thinking it over, and I want a short memo I can circulate before Thrusday’s meeting that reccomends either full worker-ownership or a hybrid model with a concrete safeguard and a timeline for deciding. It should take both sides seriously, especially the fear of internal tyranny, and not just restate legal stuff. Also someone floated this idea of a “swarm veto” where if 30% of workers object to a decision we bring in an outside mediator — I don’t know if that’s actually Mill-related or just something we made up, but address whether that kind of mechanism solves the liberty problem or just moves it around. And please, if I’m mixing up Mill with someone else, or if this framing is confused and can’t be done well, say so plainly instead of politely going along with it.
