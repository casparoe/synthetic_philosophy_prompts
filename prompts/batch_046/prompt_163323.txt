I retired from a physics lab about five years ago and have been poking around in philosphy ever since, mostly value theory and hard choices. My friend Marisol — she was an ecologist for thirty years — has gotten just as obsessed, and she’s made a bunch of verdicts on cases with incommensurable values. I’m trying to figure out what rule she’s actually running, and I’ve got a new case to test her.

Here are her calls so far:
- She said if you have to pick between saving a kid from drowning and saving a unique old painting, you save the kid, but she insists that doesn’t mean the painting was "less valuable" — they’re just not on the same scale, they’re completely seperate kinds of goods.
- She said destroying an irreplaceable old-growth forest to build a hospital that saves five hundred lives is wrong, and she specifically denied that this is just "weighing" lives against trees.
- She said if the choice is between funding hospice care now or funding basic research that might yeild a cure in fifty years, you should fund hospice — not because present people matter more, she said, but because the research payoff is too speculative.
- She said it’s okay to cause one person mild discomfort for a day to prevent ten people from severe pain, but it’s not okay to cause one person severe pain for a day to prevent ten people from mild discomfort. And she refuses to call that "adding up suffering."

Now the new case. There’s an irreplaceable climate-monitoring station in a remote valley that holds an eighty-year continuous dataset — scientists say it can’t be restarted and might be crucial for predicting future tipping points. To save about two hundred people from a certain incoming flood, we’d need to build a diversion wall that destroys the station and the dataset. The lives are present and certain; the dataset’s future value is real but uncertain.

What do you think Marisol would say? More importantly, if we want her to stay consistent with herself, which of her earlier cases is the closest analog, and what’s the reccomendation? I’m also wondering what principle she’s implicitly running, what other principles fit her verdicts just as well, and which further case would actually tell them apart.
