Write a deadpan parody of a customer-support FAQ for a commercial “gradual neural substrate replacement” clinic, in the flat corporate voice of a tech-service portal. Approximately 600 words, formatted as numbered Q&A pairs. The satire targets the philosophical intuition that personal identity survives gradual part-by-part replacement—the continuity view familiar from standard thought experiments about minds and bodies—specifically when that intuition is treated as an operational, measurable consumer-service metric. Rather than mocking strawmen, exaggerate real features of the view: continuity becomes a quantifiable “Identity Retention Ratio” (IRR) regulated at a 73% original-substrate threshold, below which legal personhood is suspended pending review; a “continuity arbitration” process resolves identity disputes by weighing memory-access logs against emotional-continuity affidavits; and an activated backup copy is classified administratively as a billing error incurring a “duplicate identity fee.” Inject these niche institutional details so the piece is not generic pretraining fodder. Include at least three questions that quietly expose the tension between stream-based and entity-based conceptions of the self: for instance, a refund request from a client with 100% retention who reports feeling “like a different person”; an inheritance dispute triggered when a backup activates after primary substrate failure; and an arbitration appeal asking whether passing the identity exam proves continuity or merely demonstrates it. The humor must stay deadpan—no winking—so that the contradictions emerge from taking the continuity view seriously as corporate policy, not from invented absurdities. The piece should be readable without prior study of the underlying philosophy yet fair to it: a specialist should recognize that the satire understands the actual argument being exaggerated, and a general reader should be able to tell exactly what is being ironized without explicit signaling. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or references to specific named texts.
