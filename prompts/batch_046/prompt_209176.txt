In my philosophy of blame reading group we've hit a wall on the hypocrisy/standing question and it's driving me a bit nuts. The basic setup is familiar: one side says if you're a hypocrite — like you do the exact thing you're blaming someone else for — you automatically lose standing to blame them, because you've shown you don't actually buy into the norm. The other side says standing isn't about your moral resume, it's about the wrong being done to someone and whether they need acknowledgment; hypocrisy might make you annoying or undermine your authority, but it doesn't cancel the entitlement.

But here's where it gets stuck. Both sides keep running the same cases — the politician preaching family values, the climate activist with a private jet — and both claim the cases go their way. The forfeiture people say "obviously she can't blame anyone, she's guilty of the same thing." The address people say "she can blame them, she's just being a jerk, and the victim still needs to recieve recognition." So for months nobody's moved.

My own theory is that they're not actually disagreeing about hypocrisy. I think one side is treating standing like a kind of moral license you can revoke if you break the rules — almost like a membership card — and the other side is treating it like a relational duty that attaches to victims regardless of who's delivering it. But I'm not sure if that's the real split, or if there's some hidden empirical assumption about how blame works in social life that neither side is defending.

Can you diagnose the stall? Is it verbal — do they mean different things by 'standing'? Is it substantive — about what blame is for? Or are they asking totally seperate questions, one about entitlement and one about efficacy, under one heading? And given your diagnosis, what would actually make progress here — a sharper conceptual distinction, a weird edge case that forces separation, or is this just bedrock intuition about purity versus harm and we should expect it to stay stuck?
