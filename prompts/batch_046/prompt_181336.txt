I'm on this small committee that reviews proposed changes to our own departmental charter — yes, meta, i know. Our past rulings: we okayed a temporary switch from unanimous to 2/3 vote for amendments, but only with a 5-year sunset that snaps back; we okayed lowering the quorum but only after confirming the proposal vote happened under the old quorum; we rejected a clause that tried to make itself permanently unamendable; and we rejected a recursive validation rule that said an amendment is valid iff it's ratified by the rules it creates. Now there's a two-step proposal: Step 1 inserts "this clause shall not be referenced in any subsequent amendment"; Step 2, filed as a technically seperate motion right after, references that clause to suspend the 30-day notice period. The sponsor says neither motion is self-referential alone. What should we do for consistency — allow, block, or conditionally allow? Which of our four precedents is the closest analog? If our track record genuinely doesn't settle it, what's the principle we'd be adopting either way? And what's the one further case that'd actually force us to choose between those two principles?
