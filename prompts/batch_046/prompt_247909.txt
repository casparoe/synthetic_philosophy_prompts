I've been fixing my view on dispositions, finks, and masks for a metaphysics workshop and want a consistency audit before presenting. My commitments are: (1) Dispositions are primitive powers, not reducible to categorical bases or laws; they can exist wholly unmanifested, even in worlds where triggering conditions never occur. (2) The fink/mask distinction is metaphysically deep: a fink removes the power upon triggering, whereas a mask only blocks the causal pathway, leaving the power intact. (3) So a permanently masked disposition—say, solubility shielded in every physically possible world by an eternal blocker—is still genuinely possessed; it is a real power with no open manifestation corridor. (4) Yet I also hold that if an object is masked across all physically accessible worlds, it simply lacks that disposition, since dispositions require at least one non-empty modal pathway to manifestation. (5) I apply this uniformly: a person whose irritable temperament is permanently suppressed across all nearby worlds by medication or environment lacks irritability, rather than having it merely masked. (6) For analysis, I endorse a uniform counterfactual conditional—if S occurred in nearest interference-free worlds, M would occur—but I treat this strictly as an epistemic criterion, not a metaphysical reduction. (7) I reject evolutionary debunking of dispositional realism because such talk tracks real causal structure, not adaptive fiction.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision for each real problem? Rank the conflicts by severity, and for each say exactly which commitment must give and what that costs the broader picture. I have no prior attachment to saving any particular claim—just map where the package actually breaks.
