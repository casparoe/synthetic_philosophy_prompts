I am putting together my position for a philosophy of biology seminar and would like a consistency check before I present. Here is roughly where I have landed: (1) I think DNA carries genuinely semantic information — it represents phenotypic outcomes, and mutations can be true misrepresentations rather than mere chemical failures. (2) I take the phrase “genetic code” literally: it is a symbolic system with an arbitrary mapping between codons and amino acids, comparable in kind to linguistic or computational codes. (3) I reject any design-based origin story; the system arose through blind evolutionary selection with no intentional encoder. (4) I hold that robust representation requires an interpretive consumer — a system that uses the representation for a functional end — yet I do not regard ribosomes, polymerases, or other cellular machinery as interpreters in that sense; they are purely mechanistic transducers. (5) I regard “genetic information” as indispensable to molecular biology, not eliminable in favour of a purely chemical or physical description. (6) I accept that the codon table is highly chemically constrained and near-universal across life, which severely limits the arbitrariness that a literal code seems to require. (7) I am a metaphysical naturalist: all biological facts ultimately supervene on physical facts, with no non-physical properties involved.

Please audit this package carefully. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and how severe is each real tension? For every genuine conflict, identify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and say what it costs me elsewhere in the set; then rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if revised or dropped, would clean up the most overall tension, and whether that revision is affordable given the rest of my views. Do not flag every combination as problematic — some of these are meant to sit together comfortably — and do not simply lecture me on the philosophy of biology; the deliverable is an audit of my specific commitments.
