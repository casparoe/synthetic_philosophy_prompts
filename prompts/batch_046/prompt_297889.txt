I think the distinction between the probability of a conclusion and the weight of the supporting argument is best read as an internal division within evidential assessment: weight measures how fully the premises cover the relevant alternatives, and it conditions how much practical confidence the assigned probability warrants, rather than sitting as an independent axis. Finally, I treat Keynesian uncertainty as objectively grounded in the logical structure of available evidence rather than as a subjective measure of ignorance, but I defend using vague, subjectively calibrated priors in macroeconomic forecasting precisely because I think Keynes showed that precise probabilities are often unattainable.
