Two people in my study group got stuck arguing about global workspace theory and whether it explains phenomenal consciousness or just access consciousness. The first one, call her J, said that in standard GWT if information isn't globally broadcast—available to working memory, reasoning, and report—then it's not conscious at all, not even phenominally. The second, M, said that's conflating access with phenomenology. He said you could have rich qualitative experience in local sensory processing that never reaches the workspace, kind of like blindsight in reverse. J said he was just describing unconscious processing and slapping the word "experience" on it. Then M introduced this "echo-chamber" idea he made up: a local brain module with recurrent loops that generate structured qualitative states, but the global hub is completely blocked from them. He said this shows phenomenal consciousness can exist without any global access. J said that's unfalsifiable—if the global system can't detect it, there's no behavioral evidence, so he's just intuition-pumping. M said J was being circular by defining consciousness as whatever the workspace can measure. J came back that even if you grant the echo-chamber, making consciousness epiphenomenal and disconnected from behavior robs it of any functional role, which should bother M. M said maybe some experiences are just isolated pockets that don't guide action. They wouldn't budge. Can you go through their exchange round by round and say who's right about each specific point? Also, does the echo-chamber actually help M's case, or does it collapse back into something J can recieve? And what are both missing that neither brought up?
