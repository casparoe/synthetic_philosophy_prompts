A historical-materialist sociology working group has delivered consistent judgments on whether particular cultural and institutional phenomena are determined by the economic base or enjoy relative autonomy within the superstructure. Their record runs as follows: they held that Weber's Protestant ethic, despite being presented as an autonomous cultural driver, was ultimately shaped by the demands of early mercantile capital accumulation; they ruled that the post-war mass expansion of higher education was driven chiefly by the technical and managerial needs of advanced capitalism rather than by democratic aspiration; they determined that the aesthetic and material norms of mid-twentieth-century Brutalist architecture were largely explicable through concrete-production logistics and state fiscal constraints; they concluded that the contemporary resurgence of astrology and esoteric spirituality among urban millennials could not be reduced to base conditions and instead manifested a superstructural crisis of meaning; and they found that the legal persistence of monogamous marriage contracts across several jurisdictions, despite profound economic restructuring, demonstrated genuine institutional inertia within the juridical superstructure. The group must now rule on a new case: the rapid proliferation of deceleration or slow living movements that explicitly reject productivity norms, acceleration, and conspicuous consumption, yet whose organisational existence depends almost entirely upon subscription-based digital platforms, algorithmic content curation, and the monetisation of wellness through influencer economies. Predict the verdict the group ought to reach if it wishes to remain coherent with its own precedents, identify the closest analog in its track record, and articulate the principle about base-superstructure relations that each possible ruling would implicitly adopt. Should the precedents genuinely underdetermine the case, specify what further phenomenon would best distinguish the competing principles. Please provide your response in British English, with no bibliography or external citations required.
