Write a joke about Russell's paradox and the failure of unrestricted set comprehension. The humor should turn on a precise technical feature—specifically, that merely banning self-membership does not rescue naive comprehension; one must also abandon the principle that every well-defined property determines a set, which is why ZFC substitutes separation (restricted to members of an already-given set) and relegates the leftover collections to proper classes lying outside the cumulative hierarchy—rather than on a generic “math is weird” punchline. To keep the formulation from being a pretraining cliché, stage the joke in an unfamiliar scenario: for instance, an exclusive club whose membership rule is defined by a property that sounds harmless until someone asks whether the club’s own governing committee satisfies it, and the resolution requires not expelling one applicant but dissolving the very idea of a universal membership roster. The joke should be coherent to a reader who knows why the paradox forces the set/class distinction, and it should not invoke citations, named texts, or bibliographic references.
