Consider a formal model of institutional path dependence in which each equilibrium state s carries a reversibility coefficient R(s), defined as the ratio of transition costs away from s to transition costs into s. Suppose that for every major contemporary institution—legal frameworks, technical standards, energy grids—we observe R(s) far exceeding 1, indicating strong local lock-in. Given that departing from any of these states is prohibitively expensive, does it not follow that our present macro-configuration is the only globally stable attractor in the space of possible histories? If so, would counterfactual trajectories in which different equilibria persist be not merely unlikely but structurally excluded, reducing path dependence to a disguised form of long-run determinism rather than to genuine contingency? Please respond in one paragraph of no more than 130 words, opening with the central conceptual error in this inference.
