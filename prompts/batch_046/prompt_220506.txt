For a metaphysics seminar that wants students sorted by commitments rather than labels, write a decision tree for theories of persistence through time. The root should ask whether a concrete particular is wholly present at each time it exists or only partly present. From there branch through four concrete diagnostics: (1) whether gradual total part-replacement over a year preserves identity; (2) whether a fission case in which one object divides with continuous matter-flow can yield two products each identical to the original; (3) whether artifacts such as ships have identity conditions independent of their material continuity; and (4) whether the relation linking temporal stages into one persisting thing is a fundamental metaphysical fact, a conventional grouping, or absent altogether. The leaves must include standard endurantism, standard perdurantism with temporal parts, an exdurantist or stage-theoretic counterpart view, and at least one hybrid or unoccupied combination — for instance, a view that accepts temporal parts but denies any fundamental cross-temporal unifying relation. For each leaf name the position (or mark it 'unnamed/unoccupied'), state its most awkward commitment in one sentence, and flag any pair of leaves the tree fails to separate, such as relational-essentialist endurantist positions that risk collapsing into stage-theoretic analogues. For every unoccupied branch, briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Phrase every node as a concrete case or claim that can be answered without prior knowledge of the metaphysical label it corresponds to.
