here's the student anwer (also written for the scene):

Dennett attacks the Cartesian theater because it assumes there's a central place in the brain where all information comes together to be observed by a homunculus, which just pushes the problem of consciousness back one step. His multiple drafts model replaces this with parallel, distributed processes that generate various versions or drafts of experience over time. There is no single correct draft that is 'the' conscious one; consciousness is just these distributed processes.

Dennett's narrative center of gravity is a fictional self we construct from these drafts. Even without a theater, this center pulls together beliefs and memories into a coherent story, giving us the sense of being unified subjects. So the center does the integrating work that the theater was supposed to do, but without a central observer.

Regarding the binding problem—how features like color and shape get combined into one object—Dennett solves it by distributing drafts across re-entrant loops. Each draft binds features locally, and then the narrative center selects the best draft for conscious report. Temporal integration happens through these loops interacting with the center, not in one place.

Dennett also argues there's nothing it's like to be a thermostat, since it lacks the complex narrative structures required for consciousness. This confirms that while drafts occur everywhere, only systems with a strong narrative center have real conscious experience.
