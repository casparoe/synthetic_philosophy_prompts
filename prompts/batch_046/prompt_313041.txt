I am preparing a consistency check for a philosophy of language seminar on Augustine's ethics of speech. Here are the commitments I want to defend together: (1) Augustine's prohibition on lying is absolute—any voluntary utterance of falsehood is sinful, regardless of whether deception actually occurs or is intended, because the speaker's word fails to match their mind. (2) The underlying argument is teleological: language was instituted so that minds can disclose themselves to others, so to speak falsely is to misuse an instrument whose natural end is truthful revelation. (3) This teleological reading requires that speech have a single, uniform natural end fixed by divine institution, not one that shifts with social context or conventional practice. (4) I accept a broadly conventionalist view of linguistic meaning: what distinguishes an assertion from a fiction, an irony, or a metaphor is determined by shared social norms and contextual framing, not by an invariant natural function. (5) I want to defend Augustine against incoherence charges by arguing that fiction, metaphor, and irony are not lies, since the audience recognizes the non-literal frame and is not deceived about the speaker's actual beliefs—there is no deceptive intent. (6) I reject mental reservation—saying something technically true while expecting the listener to draw a false conclusion—as incompatible with Augustine's view, because it still involves a deceptive intention about one's communicative purpose. (7) I also hold that Augustine permits prudent silence, deliberately withholding speech when disclosure would cause harm, without that being a failure of charity or truthfulness.

Please audit this package. Which commitments genuinely conflict, which only appear to, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity: specifically, address whether (1) can survive together with (5), whether (3) can survive with (4), and whether (6) is consistent with the rationale behind (1). For each tension, indicate whether the fix requires weakening the teleological argument, refining the definition of lying, or abandoning conventionalism. Please answer in American English.
