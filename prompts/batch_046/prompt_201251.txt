Rather than relying on the usual "direct physical injury" framing, I want the procedure to distinguish three layers of harm that seem central to Mill's actual concern but are rarely separated in platform policy: first-order direct injury to identifiable parties, second-order chilling effects on the speech of others, and third-order erosion of the deliberative conditions required for collective reasoning. Please construct a step-by-step decision procedure my team can run on a content flag: list the ordered questions to ask, specify what evidence counts as sufficient to answer each, mark the exact points where a factual "no" stops the evaluation, and identify where the decision becomes a value judgment that must be escalated to a human reviewer rather than automated. Apply the procedure to two cases: a long, technically detailed post arguing that anthropogenic climate change is a hoax posted in a general-science channel, and a post urging readers to boycott all businesses owned by members of a specific ethnic group, explicitly noting where the procedure yields a restriction recommendation that you expect I will find objectionable. Also state what the procedure cannot settle and where it is likely to misfire. Deliver the response as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion: item 1 should present the ordered procedural questions; item 2 should specify the stop conditions and escalation triggers; item 3 should apply the procedure to the climate-denial post; item 4 should apply it to the boycott post and flag the disagreeable outcome; item 5 should describe the irreducible limits and failure modes.
