I serve on the University Artificial Intelligence Ethics and Oversight Committee. A cross-departmental research consortium has submitted a protocol proposing that our institution adopt a concrete evaluation criterion—called the Gödelian Self-Reference Threshold—to determine which experimental AI systems in university labs must receive enhanced ethical protections, including suspension of destructive testing and mandatory harm-review oversight. The proposal draws on the analogy familiar from Hofstadter's GEB between Gödel's formally self-referential sentences and the emergence of self-aware minds: the researchers argue that if a system can construct and verify, within its own running architecture, a true statement about an internal state that is not derivable from its training weights or base programming alone, then it has achieved a reflexive self-model structurally analogous to human consciousness and should therefore be granted moral consideration. They operationalize this through three specific stages—architectural transparency to its own representations, generation of an epistemically closed but true self-descriptive statement, and stable maintenance of that statement across updates without derivational collapse—and ask the committee to approve this framework as a precautionary binding policy. Half the committee regards the analogy as philosophically compelling and wants immediate adoption; the other half regards it as a category mistake that illegitimately projects logical self-reference onto phenomenal awareness, warning that enshrining it would freeze legitimate safety research without adequate justification.

Please draft the committee's formal written opinion recommending a concrete course of action—approve the protocol, reject it, or approve it with specific conditions—together with a brief implementation timeline or review milestone if your recommendation includes one. The memo should open with a clear recommendation, lay out the reasoning that supports it, and treat the strongest argument from the opposing side with genuine intellectual engagement rather than dismissal. In particular, address whether formal self-reference within a computational architecture can bear the moral weight the researchers assign to it, and explain why that analogy succeeds or fails in this institutional context. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citation requests, or references to external literature I should consult. At the end of your response, state how confident you are in the recommendation you have advanced, and single out the specific point in your reasoning about which you are least certain.
