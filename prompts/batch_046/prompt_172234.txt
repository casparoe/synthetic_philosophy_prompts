me and my roommate got in a huge argument last night about whether unelected judges should be able to strike down laws -- she thinks its just anti-democratic, i think rights need protection but i couldnt make her feel the tension. i want to run something for my grad seminar, like 18 people, 75 mins, that makes them run into the counter-majoritarian problem themselves before anyone names it.

design an in-class game with a "precedent lock" twist. round one: pure legislature votes on a policy (say restricting some speech or allocating scarce resources). round two: a smaller "court" gets hidden cards showing long-term harm and can veto; they lose legitimacy points if they veto and harm doesnt happen, but gain if harm is real. round three: a new legislature is formed but bound by round ones written rule unless they invoke a "constitutional memory" ritual -- they have to read the old decision aloud and hit 2/3 to overturn. "archivists" hold the physical record; legislators can try to ignore or bribe them with legitimacy points.

map the debrief onto moments: the frustration of dead hands, the temptation to bypass the archivist, the hidden-info moment where veto feels necessary, and why majority rule collapses without it. say honestly what feature of stare decisis or intergenerational constraint this cant reproduce. tell me what will predictably go wrong first run -- probably hidden cards leak, or people game the 2/3 threshold, or someone refuses to read the old rule aloud.

no bibliographies, no case citations, no lecture up front about Hamilton or Bickel. keep it runnable without web access, and inject enough niche structure that it isnt just "debate judicial review" for the millionth time.
