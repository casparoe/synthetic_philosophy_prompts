I've been arguing with my friend about Galileo's letter to Cristina and whether his two-books idea actually commits him to something stronger than people think. I think Galileo's view is that whenever science reaches true demonstration — not just probable opinion — scripture has to bend, even if the literal reading is crystal clear, and that this applies symetrically: if tomorrow we had true demonstration that geocentrism was right, then Copernican readings of Psalm 104 would have to be abandoned. So the position is that Galileo's hermeneutic is fully bidirectional and hinges entirely on epistemic status, not on science being modern or whatever. What would actually count against this reading? I want the textual bits from the Letter or other writings that push back, plus arguments that he was really just being politically strategic and didn't mean the symmetry part, and whether there's a hidden asymmetry in how he treats senses versus reason. Also, is this view vulnerable to the objection that true demonstration is a moving target and therefore his principle is empty? Sort the defeaters by how decisive they'd be, and be honest if the position can just retreat into he was being strategic without losing anything.
