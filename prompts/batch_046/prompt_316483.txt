I was arguing with my roomate about Dennett's real-patterns idea and now I'm stuck in a loop I can't exit. Here's the argument I sketched on a napkin after class:

(1) For Dennett a pattern is real only if it's projectible—using it gives you predictions that are more accurate or more tractable than you'd get without it.
(2) The intentional stance predicts by attributing beliefs and desires; the design stance by functional architechture; the physical stance by bare physics.
(3) Whenever the intentional stance succeeds, there's always a design-level redescription that yields the exact same counterfactual predictions, since mental states supervene on functional states.
(4) So intentional patterns never add counterfactual robustness that design patterns lack; they're just compressed notations.
(5) By the same token, design patterns are just compressed physical patterns.
(6) Therefore only physical patterns are genuinely real; higher stances are pragamtic shortcuts with no ontological weight.
(7) But then consider synthetic interlocutors—LLMs. The design stance treats them as next-token predictors; the intentional stance might treat them as having beliefs about history. If (6) holds, we should deny they have beliefs, and by parity deny humans do too—it's all just neurons.
(8) That's clearly wrong: social coordination collapses without intentional attribution.
(9) So (4) must be false—there have to be intentional patterns that design descriptions genuinely miss.
(10) But if mental patterns aren't reducible to functional ones, then supervenience fails, which is a huge metaphysical price to pay.

I feel like something slipped between (3) and (4), maybe an equivocation about what "same predictions" actually means, or maybe (7) cheats by treating pragmatic indispensability as proof of reality. I want to keep (1) and (8) if possible. Which step is actually the weak link, why did it look fine at first, and what do the standard positions—reductive physicalist, non-reductive functionalist, and Dennett's own middle path—each have to pay if they reject it?
