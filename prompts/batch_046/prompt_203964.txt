I'm working on this novel where one of my characters belongs to a hive-mind council — like, they share decision-making and you can't really single out who caused the harm — and I got into a long debate with my friend who teaches moral philospohy about whether Strawson's reactive attitudes even apply to groups. Anyway, she's drafting the final for her upper-level seminar and wants an essay question on Strawson's reactive attitudes and moral responsibility that students can't just answer by summarizing his classic paper. It needs to push them on whether determinism actually threatens reactive attitudes, or if Strawson's just describing habits we can't shake, and specifically whether the objective stance collapses into being just another reactive attitude (like contempt or cold indifference).

She also wants me to inject the hive-mind idea since I'm the novelist: ask students to apply Strawson's framework to the collective council, deciding whether reactive attitudes can be directed at the group without being reducible to individuals, and whether this extension breaks Strawson's distinction between the participant and objective stances. The model answer should take a definate, committed position — not fence-sitting, not just listing options — on whether Strawson survives this test, with clear reasoning for why it does or doesn't. About 700 words, aimed at students who've done the primary reading but shouldn't need to cite obscure secondary stuff.

Plus a marking scheme out of 25 that separates an A from a B by naming the specific philosophical moves each must contain — for instance, correctly separating quality-of-will responses from harm-based ones, spotting the reflexive problem with the objective stance, applying the collective case precisely rather than hand-waving — with marks assigned to each move and a one-line note on how B answers typically miss it. No citations or reading lists required; it should be doable without searching. Keep the prompt itself grounded in the actual debate rather than niche literature they wouldn't have seen.
