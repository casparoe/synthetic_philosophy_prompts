ok so in my civics/philo hybrid class weve been arguing about whether countries should officially say sorry for old bad stuff. i have opinions on like six examples but i think im contradicting myself and i want to know if theres actually a rule behind it or if im just full of it.

heres my takes:
1. canada apologizing for residential schools in 2008 feels totally legit. they named exactly what happened, some survivors were still alive to hear it, and there was actual money and policy changes tied to it. real apology.
2. japan issuing a statement about comfort women but then having ministers deny it happened a year later feels fake. like they want the moral credit without owning the harm. not a real apology.
3. germany's holocaust reparations and all the memorials/education stuff feels like the best version. they kept paying and teaching it for decades. definitely real.
4. the us has never formally apologized for slavery at the federal level, but some cities have done local ones. those city ones feel weird and performative to me — they didnt do slavery, the federal government and the whole system did. but then if a city was built on slave labor maybe they should? im torn. i lean toward "fake unless they actually change something now."
5. australia's 2008 apology for the stolen generations — i think that was good and emotional, but then not much structural stuff changed after, and some people said it was just words. so maybe half-real?
6. britain apologizing for some colonial massacre in india in like the 1900s where all the direct victims are dead. i actually think thats still ok? the harm ripples down through families and land. but wait — in case 4 i said the current city government "didnt do it" and thats why its fake. but in this case the current british government also didnt do the massacre. so why do i feel ok with one and not the other?

so my principle attempt: maybe an apology counts when it (a) admits specific harm without backtracking, (b) is directed at people still affected by that harm, and (c) comes with some real cost or structural change. that saves 1, 2, 3, and 5 (partially). but it doesnt save 4 vs 6 — in both cases the direct perpetrators are gone and current officials didnt do it, yet i call one performative and one okay. is the difference that britain's harm is still structurally present (land, poverty) while the city's slavery harm is... also structurally present? ugh.

so is there a coherent principle that saves all six verdicts, even if its complicated? or do i have to drop one? and what should i think about a case i hasnt considered — like if a country apologizes and pays reparations but only to a specific ethnic group that now lives mostly elsewhere, not to the people actually living in the harmed region now? does that break the principle?

basically: what am i actually tracking here, which verdict is the weird one, and whats the right way to think about state apologies?
