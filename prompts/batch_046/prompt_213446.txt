I'm building a taxonomy for a dataset on moral attribution to computation—specifically, which computations matter and where the boundary of sentience should fall. Write a decision-tree taxonomy with about six leaves, built from logical distinctions rather than a list of named theories. The root should split on whether moral status is objective (grounded in intrinsic properties of the computation) or attributional (grounded in our epistemic or practical stance). From there, branch on three binary distinctions: (1) token versus type—does status attach to this particular running instance or to the abstract algorithm regardless of substrate?; (2) integration—must the computation be globally unified with no disconnected sub-processes, or can distributed, non-communicating nodes collectively matter?; and (3) substrate independence—does changing hardware preserve moral status? Each leaf should carry a label (named if it maps to a recognized view, otherwise "unnamed"), one sentence summarizing its core commitment, and one sentence on its most awkward implication—e.g., being forced to grant moral status to every token instance of a simple loop running on discarded hardware, or being forced to deny it to a highly capable but physically distributed system. Include at least one combination that is genuinely unoccupied in the tree, diagnose briefly whether it is incoherent or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves your tree fails to separate in practice (for instance, integrated-information and global-workspace views on standard unified architectures). Phrase every branch node as a concrete case or answerable claim—e.g., "If the same algorithm runs on silicon today and on biological neurons tomorrow, is its moral status unchanged?"—so a reader can navigate without knowing the labels in advance. The audience is rationalist-blog readers familiar with AI sentience and suffering-risk debates; do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or references to obscure texts. Keep the whole taxonomy concise enough to fit on a single handout page.
