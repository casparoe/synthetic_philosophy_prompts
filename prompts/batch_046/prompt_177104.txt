i'm working through Bradley's regress for a philosophy course and got kind of stuck comparing it to these newer structuralist takes on propositional unity that obviously came way after he died in 1924. Bradley's basic move is that relations cant be real, because if A is related to B by R, then R needs its own relation to A and to B, and that just keeps going forever—so reality is really just one undifferentiated Absolute and all the distinctions we see are only appearance.

Now, some philosophers after the 1960s tried to solve the unity problem by saying the "glue" isnt a relation at all but a primitive logical composition operator—like an unsaturated function that applies to arguments without being an extra thing in the world. There's also this weirder idea about "joint-carving pluralities", where the same objects can form different "grades" of unity—molecule vs heap—without any relational tie, just a primitive parameter that picks which grade is actual. If Bradley was alive today and read this, do you think he would say the grade parameter is just another hidden relation that falls into the same regress, or would he see it as closer to his own view that distinctions are only in appearance? And what about the composition-operator idea—would he accept that as genuinely escaping the regress because it's logical not ontological, or would he argue it's just a distinction without a difference? I dont need a bibliography or anything, just your best reconstruction of how his actual arguments apply to these post-Fregean moves.
