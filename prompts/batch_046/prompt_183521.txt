I'm grading exams for a community course I help facilitate called "Inventing the Past: How Historians Build Timelines." We spent several sessions on whether "the Middle Ages," "the Renaissance," and "the Scientific Revolution" name real chunks of history or are later packaging devices—what we discussed as "zoning-style labels" that can reshape what people think happened even when daily practices change gradually rather than overnight. One exam prompt asked students to defend a specific position about one of these three labels.

The exam question was: "Some historians treat 'the Middle Ages,' 'the Renaissance,' and 'the Scientific Revolution' as real periods with clear boundaries; others treat them as 19th- and 20th-century inventions that flatten complex change into neat marketing labels. Choose one of these three labels, explain the strongest case that it is largely an invention, and then answer this: if that case succeeds, does it show that all periodization is useless, or only that this particular label needs to be redesigned? Use at least one concrete example of continuity across the supposed boundary."

Here is the student's handwritten answer, reproduced exactly from the exam booklet:

"I picked the Scientific Revolution. The strongest case it's an invention is that there was no single revolution—medieval universities already taught natural philosophy, and figures like Copernicus built on Islamic and Greek astronomy rather than breaking with the past. The label was popularized by 19th-century writers who wanted to tell a story of modern science triumphing over superstition. So yes, the case succeeds, and it shows that all periodization is useless because history is just one continuous flow; if one label is fake, the whole system collapses. For continuity, I noted that alchemy and astrology stayed active in royal courts well into the 1700s, so the boundary between Renaissance magic and Scientific Revolution rationalism never existed. Also, Burckhardt's idea that the Renaissance invented individualism is wrong because medieval monks practiced deep self-examination in confession, which proves individualism was already there, so the Middle Ages-to-Renaissance break is also fake. Therefore we should stop using these three terms entirely and just talk about centuries."
