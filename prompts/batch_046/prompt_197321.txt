I'm a university student enrolled in a seminar on historiography and the philosophy of history, and I also work as a peer reviewer for an undergraduate journal that publishes essays on twentieth-century international relations. I need a reusable decision procedure—essentially a flowchart in words with ordered tests—that I can run in about fifteen minutes per submission to evaluate whether an essay's attribution of blame for the origins of the Cold War is historiographically defensible. The procedure should operationalize the principle that asking "who started it" collapses three dimensions that must be evaluated separately: (1) causal initiation, meaning which specific state or institutional actor performed the first action that made sustained conflict highly probable given the background institutional conditions at the time; (2) normative breach, meaning which actor violated the legitimate procedural expectations that other actors in the system could reasonably hold, regardless of whether that breach was the first causal link; and (3) counterfactual robustness, meaning whether the conflict would still have emerged if that actor had acted differently, holding constant both structural constraints and the actions of other major powers. Please translate this into practical steps that require only the essay in front of me and standard background knowledge, not mastery of the full historiographical literature. The deliverable should include: the exact order of tests; a small set of diagnostic questions for each layer; explicit rules for what to do when an essay confuses the layers, assigns blame without identifying a specific agent, or treats structural inevitability as equivalent to agentless blamelessness; worked applications to at least two hypothetical essay claims—such as one arguing that Truman's atomic diplomacy constituted the initiating normative breach, and another arguing that Soviet security demands made conflict structurally inevitable so no agent bears blame—including one case where the procedure yields an answer I, as a reviewer inclined to assign primary responsibility to Western policymakers, will not like; clear flags marking where an irreducible judgment call remains and must be escalated to an editor; and a brief statement of what the rubric is not designed to do. Write the answer in American English, do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not include any verbatim quotations from published texts—paraphrase all referenced concepts.
