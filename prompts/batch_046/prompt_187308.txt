ok so i'm trying to sort out my views on incommensurable values and hard choices for a seminar writup, but i'm not sure they actually hang together or if i'm just smuggling consistency in where there isn't any. here's my rough package:

(1) value pluralism is true in a strong sense -- goods like artistic integrity, personal loyalty, and distributive fairness aren't just hard to compare, they're structurally incomparable. no common metric, not even ordinal.

(2) but i think Ruth Chang's 'parity' analysis of hard choices is wrong, because parity collapses into rough equality, which secretly assumes comparability. so i reject parity.

(3) in hard choices we resolve through volitional commitment: we don't discover which is better, we make ourselves into the kind of agent for whom one path is right. it's a crane not a skyhook -- builds the reason ground-up.

(4) despite that, i think moral regret is rational and sometimes required even when we acted well. but regret doesn't mean we picked the worse option; it's more like grieving a lost world.

(5) i still think QALY-style aggregation and cost-benifit analysis are okay for policy, even though they presuppose a common metric, because practice needs procedures and metaphysics shouldn't paralyze it.

(6) i reject evolutionary debunking of pluralism: if values are genuinely plural, evolution didn't 'get it wrong', it just bundled incommensurable drives together.

my worry is that (3) and (4) are in tension -- if the reason is created by commitment, why is there a lost world to grieve? and doesn't (5) contradict (1) in practice, since using QALYs treats values as commensurable? also is (6) just a dodge? which tensions are real and which dissolve on inspection? rank them by severity and tell me the cheapest revision for each, especially whether (3) needs to go or if i can save it by tweaking (4).
