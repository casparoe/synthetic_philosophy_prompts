In our philosophy of probability seminar we have been examining the Sleeping Beauty problem and the debate between halfers and thirders regarding self-locating belief. I have attempted to dissolve the disagreement by applying the principle of indifference not to possible worlds but to observer-moments. Because the Tails scenario generates two distinct awakenings—Monday and Tuesday—whereas the Heads scenario generates only one, I maintain that there are twice as many centred experiences under Tails. Therefore, my prior credence in Tails should already be 2/3 before I even open my eyes. Once I do wake, I acquire no genuinely new evidence: I already knew I would have some awakening, and the fact that I am having one is twice as probable under Tails, so my credence should remain unchanged at 2/3. This suggests that thirders reach the right figure but for the wrong reason, attributing an update to awakening that is actually just the reflection of a correctly weighted prior over centred worlds. Could you explain precisely where this reasoning collapses? Specifically, should the prior be distributed over worlds rather than over observer-moments, and is it correct to treat being awake as evidentially inert when the hypotheses differ in the number of awakenings they produce? I would appreciate a response in British English.
