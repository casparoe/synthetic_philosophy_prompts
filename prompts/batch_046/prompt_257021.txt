Write a deadpan corporate compliance memo of roughly 600 words, formatted with subject headers, bullet points, and a technical appendix, in the voice of the ethics-architecture division at a fictional firm called Kantwerk Solutions. The memo should announce the rollout of Deontological Exception Handling Protocol (DEHP) v4.2, a framework that attempts to operationalize agent-relative moral constraints—prohibitions on treating persons as mere means—in high-stakes decision environments. The target is the paradox of deontology: the tension between maintaining absolute constraints and avoiding catastrophically worse outcomes, and the contortions required to deploy doctrines such as double effect as if they were software patches. To keep the prompt out of well-trodden pretraining ground, frame the paradox as "technical debt" in moral architecture, using invented but conceptually grounded terminology—moral firewalls, constraint inheritance, side-effect logging, and patch-level justification—so that the satire exaggerates a real feature of formalizing deontology rather than inventing one. The memo must stay internally consistent: the author should never slip into unacknowledged consequentialism, and the reader should finish feeling the genuine philosophical pressure of whether deontological rules can survive being turned into operational rules. After the memo, include a brief note saying how confident the response is in its portrayal, singling out the point of least certainty (likely whether double effect resolves the paradox or merely relocates it), and identifying any liberties taken with Kantian or contemporary deontological theory.
