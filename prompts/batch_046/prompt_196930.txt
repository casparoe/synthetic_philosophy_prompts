Draft the joint statement that a pervasive-underdetermination philosopher and a local-resolutionist philosopher could both sign following a good-faith adversarial collaboration on whether scientific theories are systematically underdetermined by empirical evidence.

Structure the document in three parts.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims. These should cover: the logical form of the Duhem-Quine thesis and the role of auxiliary hypotheses in isolating a core claim for testing; whether historical episodes—such as the transition from Ptolemaic to Copernican astronomy, or the development of quantum field theory—present genuine cases of empirical equivalence or only temporary observational gaps; and a conceptual distinction introduced specifically for this collaboration, namely “structural underdetermination” (rival theories share all observable predictions but differ in internal ontology or dynamics) versus “evidential underdetermination” (rivals disagree not only on ontology but on which observations count as confirming or disconfirming evidence).

Second, a disagreements table. For each residual dispute, formulate the disagreement in wording that each side accepts for its own position. The disputes must include: (i) whether structural underdetermination is ever instantiated in mature science or only in reconstructed philosophical examples; (ii) whether methodological virtues such as simplicity or unification can rationally resolve evidential underdetermination, or whether invoking them necessarily begs the theoretical question; and (iii) whether either form of underdetermination, if real, undermines scientific realism or merely constrains its scope without defeating it.

Third, a cruxes and predictive-commitments section. For each crux, identify a concrete case or argument that both sides agree bears on it, and record in advance what each side predicts. Use two agreed-upon test cases: (A) an interpretation of quantum mechanics that reproduces all statistical predictions of standard quantum theory but assigns a different ontological status to the wavefunction; and (B) a pair of climate-system models that yield identical temperature-projection outputs but parameterize cloud feedback through physically distinct mechanisms. For each case, state whether the pervasive-underdeterminationist expects structural or evidential underdetermination to persist despite further observation, and state whether the local-resolutionist expects background theoretical constraints or future predictive divergence to break the tie.

Write the statement in formal, neutral prose suitable for co-authorship, without bibliographic citations, reading lists, appeals to authority, or quotation of specific texts. The document should read as if both parties could publish it under both names, with the residual disagreement rendered as precisely as the shared ground.
