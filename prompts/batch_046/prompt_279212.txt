ok so in my philosophy of physics group we've been going in circles about the Rietdijk-Putnam argument that special relativty forces a block universe. You know the drill: simultaneity is frame-relative so there's no global now, therefore all events are equally real and becoming is an illusion. The usual critics say this mistakes cordinate conventions for ontology, or that becoming happens locally along worldlines, or that there's a hidden foliation we just can't detect. But the debate hasn't moved in decades despite everyone knowing the Lorentz math. My working theory is that it's not really about physics but about whether 'now' functions like an indexical ('here') or needs a metaphysically privileged hyperplane -- and also whether people are secretly disagreeing about Reichenbach-style conventionality of simultaneity rather than eternalism itself. Is that right? Or is there a genuine formal disagreement hiding in there, maybe about whether determinism and eternalism come apart? I want an actual diagnosis: verbal disagreement, hidden empirical question, different standards for what counts as an ontological commitment, or just bedrock intuitions about change that can't be argued for? And if you think it's one of those, what -- a distinction, a theorem, a thought experiment -- could actually shift anyone, or should we expect the stall to be permanent?
