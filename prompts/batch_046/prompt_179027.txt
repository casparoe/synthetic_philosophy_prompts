You reject "jumping out of the system" — JOOTSing — as a philosophical mistake: if a formal system S does not derive a conclusion, that only means S does not contain it, and proving that S cannot derive it requires working in a different system S', not seeing a deeper truth. Here is my opening challenge. I have been thinking about the old string puzzle with M, I, and U — the four rules that start from MI and never reach MU. I understand that inside those rules it is impossible. But I invented a variation that makes me uneasy about your position. Imagine three people, each running a private copy of those rules on beliefs in their own heads. They can talk, but they only exchange rules, never copy beliefs. Each starts with MI. The question is whether any individual, or the group taken together, can reach MU. To answer, it seems you must step outside each private system and examine the group-level pattern — yet you say that is illegitimate. If you refuse, you seem forced to say each person is reasoning correctly while collectively they miss an obvious impossibility. How is that not willful blindness?

Please separate what is mathematically settled about the original puzzle from what is genuinely contested about whether stepping outside a system is valid reasoning, and do not present either philosophical interpretation as the consensus among people who study these things.
