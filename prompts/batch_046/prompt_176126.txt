The debate over Sleeping Beauty and self-locating belief has been running since the problem was first formalized, and in both the literature and in seminar rooms the halfers and thirders remain exactly where they started. Both sides accept the setup: a fair coin is flipped; if tails, Beauty is awakened once on Monday; if heads, she is awakened on Monday and again on Tuesday, with amnesia between awakenings and no way to tell the days apart; upon each awakening she is asked for her credence that the coin came up tails. The halfers maintain the answer should stay 1/2, because the coin flip is fair and she learns nothing about it. The thirders maintain it should be 1/3, because there are three subjectively indistinguishable awakening slots—Monday-tails, Monday-heads, Tuesday-heads—and her being awake now selects among them in a way that favors heads. Decades of replies have catalogued every objection, yet nobody moves.

My working hypothesis is that the stall is not about probability calculus at all, but about an unacknowledged split over what kind of belief update is occurring. One camp treats "I am awake now" as an indexical premise that selects among centered worlds and therefore dilutes the prior over the coin; the other treats the awakening as a selection effect that reveals no new information about the coin's bias, analogous to being told "you are one of the people who was awakened," which on the tails branch is necessarily true of the single awakening and on the heads branch true of each of two. But I am not convinced this is right. Perhaps the sides are answering different questions—one about rational credence in a centered world, one about the long-run frequency of correct guesses across all awakenings—under the single heading of "credence." Or perhaps the disagreement is bedrock: an unargued intuition about whether being in a situation with more copies of yourself should weaken your belief in a shared cause.
