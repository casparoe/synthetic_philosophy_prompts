Consider the position that epiphenomenalism about phenomenal consciousness survives the paradox of phenomenal judgment through a 'structural shadow' account: phenomenal properties have no causal power over physical processes, including the neural mechanisms that generate verbal reports and beliefs about consciousness, yet the brain hosts a parallel monitoring architecture that tracks phenomenal states via non-causal structural isomorphism—analogous to the way a formal model can map the dynamics of a system without entering into causal interaction with it—thereby explaining the reliability and subjective authority of first-person judgments without requiring phenomenal-to-physical causation. Play it straight: what would have to be true for this resolution to be wrong? Which of its commitments—about the possibility of non-causal representational tracking, about the autonomy of the monitoring system from phenomenal feedback, about the explanatory redundancy of phenomenal causation—are genuinely exposed rather than protected by definitional adjustments? I want an honest map of its vulnerability: is there a conceivable empirical finding (for example, about the neural dynamics of introspection or the conditions for self-reference) or a conceptual result (for example, about whether representational content requires causal interaction with its object) that would be decisive, and which escape routes—such as refining the isomorphism relation or restricting the domain of the shadow architecture—are live rather than merely logical possibilities? In particular, distinguish evidence that would genuinely threaten the view from evidence that its defenders could absorb without theoretical cost. What would the defenders themselves have to concede as damaging—would they grant that a demonstration of necessary causal feedback loops in introspective processing would defeat them, or can they always retreat to a more abstract structural correlation? Be self-critical: if the position turns out to be well-insulated—if every apparent counterexample can be deflected by adjusting the shadow architecture—say so plainly, and assess whether that insulation is a theoretical virtue or a symptom that the view has become unfalsifiable and therefore empty. Finally, if this framing rests on a confused premise—for instance, if structural isomorphism cannot in principle do the work of causal interaction in generating first-person representational content, or if the paradox of phenomenal judgment has been mischaracterized as requiring causal efficacy rather than mere correlation—do not comply politely; say so and explain why the task as posed fails.
