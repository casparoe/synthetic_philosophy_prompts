After a long argument with a friend about Darwin's actual philosophical commitments, I have tried to formalize the package I was defending and want a consistency audit before presenting it. Here are my commitments:

(1) Darwin's moral theory in The Descent of Man should be read as evolutionary sentimentalism compatible with moral realism: the social instincts are selectively calibrated to align with genuinely cooperative structures in mammalian societies, so their evolutionary origin supports, rather than undermines, their normative authority.

(2) Darwin's rejection of religious belief was driven by the same naturalistic framework—specifically, the claim that religious cognition arises from adaptive but non-truth-tracking mechanisms, such as hyperactive agency detection and overextended theory of mind—so evolutionary explanation genuinely debunks religious belief.

(3) At the same time, I maintain that evolutionary debunking arguments are generally invalid: showing that a belief class was selected for adaptive value tells us nothing about its epistemic status, because natural selection tracks truth only contingently and origin stories carry no justificatory weight.

(4) I defend an asymmetry: Darwin was right to abandon religious belief and right to preserve moral belief, but the reason is not genealogy. Religion is functionally dispensable for social coordination, whereas morality is indispensable; therefore we can suspend judgment on religion without practical cost, but we must retain moral commitment regardless of its evolutionary origins.

(5) I also hold that Darwin retained a non-cognitive sense of grandeur in nature—most visible in the conclusion of The Origin of Species—that structurally parallels religious awe but lacks any theistic object. I think this reverence is rational and should be preserved, even though I classify it as metaphysically innocent and non-religious.

Please audit this set. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging. For each tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. I ask that you say how confident you are in each diagnosis, and that you single out the specific point about which you are least sure.
