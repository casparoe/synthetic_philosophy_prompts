He's convinced Smith is basically anti-empire: the Wealth of Nations attack on mercantilism is really an attack on using colonies as extraction tools, and free trade is the antidote to that domination. I think that's overreading—Smith is just an institutionalist who hates monopoly privileges like the Navigation Acts and exclusive company charters, but he's fine with empire if it's run efficiently and eventually pulls colonies into commercial society. Can you draft the joint statement we'd both sign?

- A numbered list of claims we both accept—Smith's growth framework, his specific objections to colonial monopoly, his observation that defending American colonies cost more than they yielded, and that he treats political and economic liberty as conceptually separable.

- The cruxes. One I think is real: whether there's something like institutional drag—where a colony's legal subordination slows its path to the stationary state in a way that pure trade restrictions wouldn't. - For each crux, pick a concrete comparison we both agree is relevant—say Ireland under direct rule versus the American colonies, or East India Company territories versus crown colonies—and write down what each of us predicts about it.
