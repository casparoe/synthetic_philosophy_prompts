(2) Money is institutionally constructed in exactly this sense: a particular printed note is a twenty-dollar bill solely in virtue of collective intentionality assigning it the status function of being legal tender, and its physical properties are neither necessary nor sufficient.

(3) Race and gender are institutionally constructed in precisely the same metaphysical manner—not merely causally shaped by social practices, but constituted by collectively sustained status functions and structural positions—so that biological features serve at best as arbitrary markers rather than essential conditions.

(6) By strict parity of reasoning, racial and gender ascriptions should therefore possess equally objective, institution-relative truth-conditions that cannot be undermined merely by appealing to biological variation or individual dissent.

(7) Yet the critical social-constructionist position I want to maintain holds precisely that recognizing race and gender as constructed reveals them as contingent, power-laden classifications lacking objective authority and suitable for abolition, rather than treating them as institutionally objective.

(8) So either institutional construction does not eliminate objective authority—meaning (7) collapses—or race and gender are not institutionally constructed like money—meaning (3) fails—or money is not constructed in the relevant sense—meaning (2) fails.
