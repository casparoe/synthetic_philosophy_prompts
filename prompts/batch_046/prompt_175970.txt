I sit on the clinical-continuity review board of a university hospital. A terminally ill patient consented to an experimental protocol: destructive high-resolution neural scanning followed by gradual reconstruction of her cognitive dynamics in a synthetic prosthetic substrate. The reconstructed system exhibits the patient’s memories, values, and first-person narrative, but the original body was destroyed in scanning and no biological tissue remains. Her family demands that clinical authority and legal identity transfer automatically, while hospital counsel warns that recognizing continuity here would erase the boundary between life-extending therapy and entity-replacement, with precedent-setting effects for liability and inheritance law. Draft the board’s formal opinion recommending whether the reconstructed system should be registered as the same patient for medical and legal purposes. Your reasoning should weigh psychological continuity against bodily and causal-continuity objections, treat the family position seriously rather than dismissing it, and state the single strongest consideration on the side you reject. Format the opinion as exactly five paragraphs, each consisting of one or two sentences, with no introductory or concluding paragraph, and begin with the concrete recommendation.
