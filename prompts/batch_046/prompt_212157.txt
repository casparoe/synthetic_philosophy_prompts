I'm recording an episode for a philosophy podcast on relational metaphysics, and my guest is an unnamed philosopher who defends what she calls an ecological-ancestral Ubuntu view: she holds that individual personhood is not merely influenced but ontologically constituted by sustained reciprocal relations with a living community, and she extends this constitutive base to include obligations to ancestral others and to the land itself, treating these not as symbolic attachments but as active conditions for full moral agency. I want eight to ten interview questions that avoid having her restate the familiar community-personhood slogan and instead press where the literature on relational ontology and African philosophy actually presses. Specifically, include at least one question on how her view avoids collapsing into a form of collective consciousness that eliminates individual moral standing; one on whether the relational base is metaphysically prior to the relata or whether she is sneaking in a substantialist account of the community; one on what happens to personhood under conditions of forced exile or ecological rupture, given that the constitutive relations are disrupted rather than merely missed; and one on why the ancestral-ecological extension should be read as ontologically constitutive rather than as a culturally specific normative framework. For each of these four, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—if she appeals to asymmetrical moral repair to defend individual standing, press whether that asymmetry presupposes the very substantial self she denies; if she describes relations as processual, ask what individuates one process from another without a prior substrate; if she distinguishes disrupted constitution from lost property, ask whether diaspora then produces a permanently diminished rather than relocated personhood; and if she invokes temporal continuity, ask whether this makes Ubuntu personhood conceptually impossible in contexts of displacement. The remaining questions should turn on actual conceptual tensions in her position, not generic biographical material.
