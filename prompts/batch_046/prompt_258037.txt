I'm in my school's philosophy discussion group, and two other members—Maya and Leo—have been arguing for three meetings about how belief should update in the Judy Benjamin scenario. They use a version where a soldier is dropped into one of five sectors: Red-Alpha, Red-Bravo, Blue-Alpha, Blue-Bravo, or Blue-Charlie, with prior credences of one-third for Red (uniform across its two sectors) and two-thirds for Blue (uniform across its three). Maya's position is that if Judy learns the conditional "If I am in Blue, then I am not in Blue-Charlie," she should apply standard Bayesian conditioning on the set of worlds where that conditional holds: Blue-Charlie drops to zero, Blue-Alpha and Blue-Bravo each rise to one-third of the Blue mass, and Red stays at one-third overall. Leo says this misclassifies the evidence: learning a conditional is not like observing a categorical fact, so she should use Jeffrey conditioning, distributing the "excluded" Blue-Charlie weight across all remaining worlds in proportion to their prior likelihood, which Leo claims actually shifts some mass toward Red and leaves Blue-Alpha and Blue-Bravo unequal. Maya replies that Jeffrey conditioning is designed for uncertain observation, not for absorbing a logical constraint, and that Leo is smuggling evidential ambiguity into what is essentially a restriction on possibility space. Leo then introduces a further twist he calls a "centered-worlds" objection: because Judy's belief is indexical—she asks "where am I?" rather than "which world is actual?"—the update should depend on whether she considers herself in a Blue world at all, and Maya's conditioning ignores this self-locating structure by treating all Blue worlds as an undifferentiated set. They asked me to referee since neither will concede. Can you adjudicate their exchange step by step—rating Maya's conditioning move, Leo's Jeffrey reply, Maya's logical-constraint distinction, and Leo's centered-worlds objection—from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with brief justification for each? And please say whether both are missing a middle option for updating on conditional evidence in self-locating contexts, or whether one framework genuinely subsumes the other.
