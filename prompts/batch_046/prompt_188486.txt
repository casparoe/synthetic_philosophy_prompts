I advise a small municipal heritage panel that meets every quarter to approve, revise, or reject proposals for public monuments, digital archives, and museum installations across our region. The stakes are moderate but highly visible — a rejected proposal usually returns to the local council for a democratic final vote — and I have roughly thirty minutes per case, with no expectation that fellow board members have read philosophy. After a long discussion with a colleague who curates industrial archives, I want a reusable decision procedure drawn from Nietzsche’s distinction between monumental, antiquarian, and critical history — that is, the three ways the past can serve or harm present life — which my panel can apply without knowing the underlying theory. The procedure should take the form of a sequential checklist in plain language: first classify the dominant mode in the proposal; then run a “life-affirmation audit” that asks whether the representation expands present capacity for action or paralyses it, using Nietzsche’s own criteria; then flag any “mnemonic overloading” where two or three modes conflict and cancel each other out. Each step must state what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure stops with a clear recommendation, and where it must hand the decision back to the board because the question is one of values rather than classification. Please apply it to three proposals: a bronze statue glorifying a nineteenth-century factory founder whose labour practices are now widely condemned; a climate-controlled vault preserving every factory ledger with zero public interpretation or narrative framing; and an immersive digital exhibit that simultaneously celebrates worker solidarity, preserves every document without selection, and deconstructs the founder’s legacy — a case I expect the procedure will reject despite its popularity with visitors. The answer should be written in British English, should explicitly mark every point where judgment cannot be eliminated, and should state clearly what the procedure is unable to do.
