Write opening remarks for an interdisciplinary workshop marking the digitization of a private archive of correspondence among the final generation of French duelists (c. 1880–1914). The audience is mixed: historians, moral philosophers, and sociologists of norms. Structure your remarks around the competing explanations for why practices like dueling disappear—whether through rational moral argument, shifting material interests, or internal transformations in honor culture—drawing specifically on Bernard Williams’s skepticism about argument-driven moral revolution and Kwame Anthony Appiah’s analysis of honor as both sustaining and dissolving cruel norms. Use this tension to frame what the archive can and cannot adjudicate: letters about shame, status, and risk may illuminate honor’s collapse, but they will not reveal whether anyone was persuaded by philosophical reasoning. Do not resolve the debate, do not provide a bibliography or reading list, and do not treat the end of dueling as historically inevitable. The speech should be accessible to non-philosophers without flattening the philosophical disagreement into mere background color.
