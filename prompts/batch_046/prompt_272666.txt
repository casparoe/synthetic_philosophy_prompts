I've been working out my position for a graduate seminar on metaethics and want a consistency check before I present it. Here is where I stand: (1) I endorse a robust form of moral relativism—moral truths are constituted by the normative commitments internal to a social framework, and there are no framework-independent moral facts. (2) I take evolutionary debunking arguments to successfully undermine mind-independent moral realism, since our core moral intuitions are better explained by adaptive pressures than by tracking objective moral properties. (3) I nonetheless maintain that reflective equilibrium is the correct methodological stance within ethics: we begin with our considered judgments and revise both principles and particular cases until they achieve coherence. (4) I believe tolerance of other frameworks is a genuine second-order virtue, but I also hold that any framework failing to satisfy what I call minimal reciprocal recognition—the condition that agents within the framework can recognize one another as sources of normative claims—is not merely wrong relative to ours but structurally defective as a moral system. (5) Finally, I reject the idea that relativism forces quietism about other cultures; I think both internal critique and, in some cases, external critique via pragmatic incoherence are legitimate.

Do these five commitments hang together? I'm particularly worried that (4) introduces a framework-transcendent standard that contradicts (1), and that (2) undermines the reliability of the very intuitions that (3) asks us to trust in reflective equilibrium. At the same time, (5) might seem to conflict with (1) if external critique presupposes access to framework-independent norms. Please audit this specific package: identify which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each specify the least costly revision and what it costs. I am not looking for a general lecture on moral relativism—just a targeted assessment of whether this set is jointly tenable, with the tensions ordered and the cheapest fixes named. Please respond in American English.
