You sit on a pension-oversight board that must screen self-directed retirement-allocation sequences for structural vulnerability to intertemporal money-pump exploitation. You have no access to participants' full utility functions, you face dozens of cases per quarter, and your sign-off is required within a fifteen-minute review window. Write a reusable audit procedure—ordered questions or tests in sequence—that operationalizes the logic of diachronic rationality and money-pump arguments without citing literature or requiring theoretical background. The procedure must treat preferences as expressed over multi-period timelines (not single goods), must include a resolution-dependency check (whether preference reverses when uncertainty resolves between evaluation points), and must include a cyclic-intransitivity check across time horizons. For each step, specify what counts as a sufficient answer, where the procedure issues a structural-flag stop, and where it must hand the case to a human because the remaining issue is one of values or welfare policy rather than formal consistency. Then apply the procedure to three cases: (A) a 35-year-old who shifts from aggressive equity to bonds at 50 then back to aggressive equity at 65, with fee-free internal exchanges permitted; (B) a 55-year-old who consistently prefers lump-sum distributions to annuities at every horizon, yet whose sequence permits a third party to construct an exploitative trade loop; and (C) one case where the procedure returns a clean structural bill but the board will still want to restrict the allocation on paternalistic welfare grounds—identify exactly the step where judgment replaces mechanics. Finally, state explicitly what kinds of vulnerability or rational failure the procedure cannot detect. End with a one-sentence summary on its own line beginning with "Limits:".
