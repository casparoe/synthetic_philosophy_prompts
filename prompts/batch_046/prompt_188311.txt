I have been trying to make sense of the different ways intelligent systems navigate danger and solve problems without destroying themselves, and I keep seeing what looks like four distinct patterns in nature, people, and machines. The first is the bacterial pattern: produce enormous variation blindly, then let the environment eliminate the failures, with no individual organism learning anything at all. The second is my dog's pattern: she attempts an action, suffers a direct bodily consequence, and avoids repeating the mistake — pure conditioning through lived experience. The third is my young niece's pattern: she can imagine touching a flame, anticipate the pain without ever feeling it, and reject the action internally before trying it. The fourth is cultural transmission: she can also learn the danger through language, warning, and example from others, carrying forward tested solutions she never personally experienced. Recently I noticed a fifth case that does not sit neatly inside these four: a language model I interact with generates thousands of possible responses, selects one based on statistical patterns in human text, yet has never experienced pain, never been conditioned by a real environment, and never held a bodily belief about fire in any literal sense. So I want to ask whether philosophers have named and mapped exactly this four-fold division — blind selection, direct conditioning, internal simulation before action, and symbolic transmission — and whether they have said where an artificial system like this belongs. Does it collapse into one of the four categories, perhaps as a strange form of internal simulation without a body or as cultural transmission without a culture, or does it expose a genuine gap in the taxonomy? If this particular division is not established in the literature, can you name and define the categories precisely, with clear boundary conditions, so that I can see whether familiar oppositions like "nature versus nurture" or "instinct versus reason" are only rough approximations of something sharper? I am especially interested in what distinguishes the internal-simulation case from mere association, and whether the symbolic-transmission case requires something like shared intentionality or can operate through pure pattern-matching alone.
