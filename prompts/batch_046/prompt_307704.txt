Consider a municipal charter that establishes a "multi-layered demos" for its climate-adaptation and housing boards. Full voting membership belongs to anyone who has maintained a registered address within the municipal boundary for at least 180 days in the preceding year. Non-resident property owners receive votes weighted proportionally to the assessed value of their holdings. Seasonal workers physically present for more than ninety days but without registered residency receive half-weight consultative votes restricted to labor, transit, and housing items. Two non-human representatives—one court-appointed trustee for the regional watershed and one randomly selected youth proxy representing future generations—hold binding vetoes over any decision projected to alter carbon or biodiversity baselines for more than fifty years.

What philosophical commitments about the boundary of the demos does this institutional design implicitly presuppose? Does it commit to an all-affected-interests principle, a stakeholder or contribution-based model, a territorial co-citizenship view, or an irreducible hybrid? Examine whether its parts are philosophically consistent: for instance, does weighting votes by property value presuppose that economic stake confers political standing, and is that compatible with granting veto power to ecological and posterity representatives who hold no property and cast no weighted vote? Could a single theory license both the exclusion of nearby non-resident workers—who are clearly affected by zoning decisions—and the inclusion of distant future generations represented only by proxy? Distinguish what the charter strictly requires from what merely fits it well, and identify where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, such that rival theories of democratic membership could license the same arrangement. Finally, specify what would have to change—particularly which membership rules, weighting schemes, and veto structures—if the underlying commitment were replaced by a strict territorial-presence model that recognizes only sustained co-residence as the legitimate basis for belonging to the demos.
