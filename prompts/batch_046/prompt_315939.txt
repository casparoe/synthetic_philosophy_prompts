Design a 75-minute seminar activity for 16 upper-level philosophy undergraduates that makes participants feel the pull of superrationality—specifically the structural tension captured by the Platonia dilemma and by a Luring-Lottery-style correlation trap—before either concept is named. The room should have movable chairs and space for paired facing seats; no lecture on causal versus evidential decision theory should precede play.

Structure the session as a two-round embodied game titled “The Mirror Lottery.” Before round one, each student privately draws a sealed card assigning them a hidden reasoning posture: Type R (treat other players’ choices as causally independent of one’s own) or Type S (treat analogical reasoning across players as generating evidential correlation). The distribution is unknown to the group—say, eight of each, though only the facilitator knows. Present a visible payoff matrix for round one: choosing Cooperate yields 3 points to every Cooperator if at least one other player also Cooperates, but 0 if one is the sole Cooperator; choosing Defect yields 2 points unconditionally. Then introduce the hidden “lure”: after round one is complete, the facilitator reveals that any player who chose Defect was automatically entered into a private second lottery. That lottery pays 10 bonus points with 10% probability, but only if the total number of Defectors exceeded a hidden threshold that the facilitator sets post-hoc to match the actual count. This reproduces the Luring-Lottery correlation—choosing the dominated option creates a hidden statistical link—without explaining the parallel.

In round two, rearrange students into eight facing pairs. Each pair receives one sealed “reasoning trace” envelope containing a single anonymous sentence justifying either Cooperate or Defect from round one; the trace may match neither player’s actual posture. The pair then simultaneously chooses Match or Mismatch: mutual Match yields 4 points each; mutual Mismatch yields 1 point each; a split gives 5 to the Mismatch-chooser and 0 to the Match-chooser. The rules should be arranged so that Type R players feel the causal independence of their partner’s move, while Type S players feel the evidential pull that shared reasoning traces create, and so that the hidden threshold from round one still hangs over their interpretation of correlation.

The rules, card assignments, and envelope randomness must do the work: participants should discover in real time that their belief about their partner’s reasoning process is itself part of the strategic environment, and they should experience the fracture between treating that belief as causal background versus as evidential signal.

After both rounds, run a 20-minute debrief that maps specific moments onto formal decision theory: identify which round-one choices reproduced the Platonia dilemma’s superrational selection of a dominated option; identify which round-two dynamics reproduced the Luring Lottery’s correlation between choice and hidden state; and articulate the precise disagreement between causal and evidential theories as it manifested in students’ justifications. Include a payoff scheme that uses only points, not real money, and specify the materials (cards, envelopes, printed matrix, score sheets).

Also specify what will predictably fail the first time the activity is run—e.g., students fixating on computing the hidden threshold rather than reasoning about correlation, treating the reasoning traces as authoritative testimony rather than noisy signals, or conflating round-one payoffs with round-two incentives—and describe how to handle each failure without breaking the embodied experience.

Finally, include an honest note on which feature of superrationality—specifically the infinite regress of reasoning about reasoning—this physical format cannot reproduce, and state exactly how the facilitator must supply that gap verbally in the debrief rather than pretending the game generated it. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific texts; all necessary structure should be contained in the rules you provide.
