Having recently relocated with my family to a society that ranks near the top of international gender-equality indices, and having observed that my children's peers—despite being raised within a welfare and educational system designed to neutralise gendered socialisation—nonetheless exhibit career aspirations that cluster more sharply by sex than those I observed in my previous, less egalitarian country of residence, I have become personally invested in the philosophical stakes of the gender-equality paradox.

Please draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing interpretive camps. The first camp—the Constraint-Release Divergence camp—holds that greater institutional equality primarily removes artificial barriers, thereby allowing deeper, pre-social or biologically canalised preference distributions to manifest without distortion; on this view, heightened segregation is the signature of authentic preference revelation. The second camp—the Identity-Signalling Amplification camp—holds that reducing economic coercion and structural constraint permits gendered social identity, rather than economic necessity, to become the dominant organising principle of career choice; on this view, heightened segregation reflects culture's adaptability, not nature's rigidity.

The statement should be written in British English and should take the form of a formal, constructive document that both parties could sign. It should contain:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims—statements about the empirical pattern itself, about the limits of what the data can establish, and about shared methodological commitments—each phrased so that neither camp is misrepresented.

2. A disagreements table. For each camp, provide the precise formulation of its interpretive position, worded so that adherents of that camp would recognise it as their own view and not as a straw man.

3. The cruxes—sub-questions such that, were they settled, at least one camp would be compelled to revise its position substantially.

4. For each crux, identify the kind of argument, empirical case, or conceptual test that both sides agree would bear upon it. Record, in advance, each side's explicit prediction about what that test or argument would show. In particular, both sides should engage with the conceptual distinction between preference-autonomy convergence (the claim that freedom allows individuals to converge on genuinely individual, non-gendered preferences) and identity-differentiation divergence (the claim that freedom allows gendered identity to become a more salient axis of self-definition, thereby increasing between-group variance).

The document should not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific studies, nor should it invoke named theories or technical frameworks that are not widely established in general philosophical and social-scientific discourse. The disagreement should be rendered exact: separate genuine philosophical divergence from merely verbal dispute, and indicate where the camps talk past one another and where they genuinely conflict. The tone should be formal, precise, and collaborative rather than polemical.
