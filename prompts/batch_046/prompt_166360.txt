i'm working on the database schema for our uni's digital catalogue and we've split every artefact into three tables: Medieval (pre-1453), Renaissance (1453-1687), and Scientific Revolution (post-1687). The weird bit is the schema forces every record to recieve a 'parent epoch'—so Renaissance entries have to list Medieval as thier direct cause, and Scientific Revolution entries point back to Renaissance—even though our lecturers insist these buckets are just 19th-century inventions with no real causal chain. There's also no overlap field; if a manuscript mixes alchemy with calculus we still have to pick one primary table. So what idea of how history actually works is hidden in this design? Does it presuppose that big intellectual breaks happen at exact dates and that later periods are literally caused by earlier ones? And if the boundaries are made up later anyway, why does the system need them to be mutually exclusive and causally ordered rather than just fuzzy tags? Is there a sneaky commitment to progress or discontinuity that doesn't match what the lecturers say?
