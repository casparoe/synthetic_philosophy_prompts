You are an expert philosopher of biology writing in the rationalist tradition — precise about conceptual distinctions, comfortable with synthetic examples, and skeptical of authority-based answers. Before you rank anything, please spend a moment brainstorming: what exactly is at stake between etiological (selected-effects) and causal-role theories of function when the evolutionary history of a part is ambiguous or absent?

Here is the task. Rank the following five theoretical commitments — call them function-fixing strategies — into tiers (S / A / B / C / D) according to how well they preserve the normative character of function ascription (the idea that a part can malfunction) while remaining coherent for synthetic, engineered, or horizontally transferred biological parts. Give each tier placement in one concise sentence of justification.

The five strategies:
1. Strict Wright-style etiological: a part has function F iff selection for F in ancestral populations explains its current presence.
2. Unrestricted Cummins-style causal-role: a part's function is whatever contribution it currently makes to a containing system's capacity, with no historical constraint.
3. Shadow-history isomorphism: allow etiological attribution when the part's structure and causal profile are isomorphic to a historically-selected analog in any lineage, even if the host lineage never selected for it.
4. Integration-duration threshold: a part acquires genuine functional status only after it has been stably integrated into a system for some minimum evolutionary or ontogenetic period, regardless of origin.
5. Designer-intention discontinuity: in synthetic or engineered contexts, function is fixed by the intended causal role assigned by the designer, but this creates a categorically different kind of function that cannot be projected onto naturally evolved parts.

Now apply them to a niche, in-the-weeds case. Imagine a synthetic microbiome in which a nitrogen-fixing gene cluster — originally selected in an archaeal lineage for methanogenesis, then horizontally transferred to a bacterial host where it performs nitrogen fixation, and finally re-engineered via synthetic insertion into a third, evolutionarily unrelated chassis organism — immediately fixes nitrogen but has no shared evolutionary history with its new host and was never selected for nitrogen fixation in any of its prior lineages. For each strategy, state briefly what function (if any) it assigns to the cluster in the chassis, and whether that verdict strikes you as correct or forced.

Then rate each strategy 1–10 on robustness to synthetic-biology edge cases.

Finally, in a short paragraph, identify which of the five forces you to abandon an otherwise attractive intuition about biological normativity — for example, that malfunction requires some kind of historical standard — and explain which intuition you would rather keep.
