I'm preparing a graduate seminar on the philosophy of historiography, and I need a diagnostic taxonomy—structured as a decision tree—of the critical responses to recent grand-narrative histories of the human past, particularly the sweeping, multi-millennial accounts advanced in Yuval Noah Harari's Sapiens, Jared Diamond's Guns, Germs, and Steel, and the counter-arc offered by David Graeber and David Wengrow in The Dawn of Everything. Rather than sorting critics by author-target, the tree should branch according to the kind of failure alleged: first, whether the critique charges an empirical omission (ignoring regional archaeological variation or counter-evidence); second, if not that, whether it alleges a causal misattribution (treating contingent ecological or technological factors as deterministic drivers of social change); third, if neither, whether it claims a normative smuggling (deriving a hidden moral or political prescription from an ostensibly descriptive historical arc); and fourth, whether it identifies a formal-narrative distortion (imposing a false teleology or periodization that renders contingent discontinuities as necessary progress). At each branching node, phrase the question as a concrete commitment a student could endorse without knowing the doctrinal label—for example, whether one would maintain that the agricultural transition was driven primarily by environmental pressure rather than by collective social choice. Each leaf should carry either the name of an established critical position—such as the archaeological-particularist objection, the geographic-determinism critique, the progressivist-fallacy charge, or Graeber and Wengrow's rebuttal that inequality was not inevitable—or the designation unnamed, together with one sentence describing the awkward commitment that position must accept, such as endorsing all the empirical data while rejecting the implicit philosophy of history that structures the narrative. Please also flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot genuinely separate, explaining briefly whether they collapse because they share the same underlying commitment or because the distinction between them is purely rhetorical. The purpose is not to catalog every reviewer, but to map the logical space of objections so that we can see which combinations are occupied, which are unoccupied but coherent, and which are incoherent.
