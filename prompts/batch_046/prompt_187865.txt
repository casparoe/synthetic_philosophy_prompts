I'm on the ethics review board at a hospice center that just started offering immersive "life replay" pods — basically high-fidelity VR that lets terminal patients live out alternate timelines or revisit memories with full sensory detail. The vendor keeps pitching it as pure hedonistic well-being delivery, but after skimming Nozick's experience machine argument in a philosophy of medicine course I'm taking, I'm stuck on whether plugging someone in for their final weeks is actually good for them or just a sophisticated form of deception. Our board has to review every request, and right now we have no framework besides "does the family want it?"

So I need a reusable decision procedure — like a checklist with gates — that my team can actually run in our Tuesday meetings (forty-five minutes, and I'm usually the only one who remembers the philosphy). It should be grounded in the experience machine objection and in actual theories of well-being, but translated into questions a clinician or social worker could answer without a PhD. The procedure needs ordered steps, clear stop points where the answer is no, clear hand-off points where it's a values call rather than a fact call, and explicit flags for where it will misfire.

Please build in these specific complications: counterfactual well-being (the patient wants a replay of a life they never lived), family-requested entry for an ambivilent or unconscious patient, and what I'm calling "narrative lock" — when exiting causes severe withdrawal because the patient now treats the simulation as the "real" timeline. Run the procedure on three cases: (1) a terminal cancer patient who wants to spend her last month in a simulation where she recovered and traveled; (2) a family asking to place an unconscious patient in a pod that simulates being surrounded by loved ones; and (3) a patient who entered for pain relief but now refuses exit, saying the outside world is the wrong timeline. Also tell me exactly where the procedure can't settle the issue — especially whether "choosing to stay" counts as autonomous if the choice architecture is baked into the simulation itself. No bibliographies, please; I just need something I can print out and stick in our binder.
