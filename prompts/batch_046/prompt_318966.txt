I sit on our state's living-donor policy board. There's a pilot proposal on the table: instead of banning all compensation, we let kidney donors opt into a "reciprocal security contract" where they recieve guaranteed priority on the transplant list if they ever need a kidney, plus a state-funded health savings match, but zero direct cash changes hands. The idea frames donation like a mutual-insurance pact rather than a sale, though critics say any structured reward corrupts the altruism that makes donation ethical. I need a short decison memo for Thursday's vote recommending wether we approve the pilot, reject it, or approve with conditons, and it should take the corruption objection seriously rather than just waving it away. Please also address the worry that poor donors will be the ones opting in while wealthy recipients free-ride, and if you recommend approval, include a concrete review timeline.
