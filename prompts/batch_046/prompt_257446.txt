My ethics seminar leader’s rulings so far on whether a situation is a genuine moral dilemma—defined as a case in which an agent faces obligations that are jointly unsatisfiable and at least one binding obligation remains unfulfilled regardless of choice—are these. She holds that a mother forced by captors to choose which of her two children will be executed, with refusal meaning both die, is a genuine dilemma. She holds that pulling a lever to divert a runaway vehicle from five strangers onto one stranger is not a dilemma, only a tragic choice with a clear right answer. She holds that allocating a single life-saving ventilator between two patients with identical prognosis and equal moral claim, where either choice leaves one to die, is a genuine dilemma. She holds that breaking one of two promises made under identical conditions and of exactly equal weight to different people, where both cannot be kept, is a genuine dilemma. She holds that being coerced by a tyrant to kill an innocent person or be killed oneself is not a genuine dilemma, since the duty not to murder is not canceled by threat. She holds that stealing an inexpensive medicine to save one’s child, thereby violating property rights, is not a genuine dilemma, because the preservation of life clearly dominates. Next week we examine a surgeon who, during simultaneous emergency operations, accidentally discovers that saving Patient A requires exhausting the entire available blood supply, leaving Patient B—who is in an adjacent operating room with an identical prognosis and need—to die; she saves A, B dies, and she reports feeling no moral residue whatsoever, describing the depletion as mechanically unavoidable rather than chosen. Predict her verdict on whether this surgeon’s situation is a genuine moral dilemma, identify whether her implicit criterion is structural obligation-conflict, unavoidable moral residue, or the absence of any hierarchical or comparative resolution principle, and specify the further hypothetical case that would best discriminate between the two leading interpretations of her pattern.
