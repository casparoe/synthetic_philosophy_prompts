Compose a villanelle of exactly nineteen lines in the traditional ABA form with two refrains, intended to be read aloud at the close of a graduate seminar on epistemic modal logic. Take as your subject Fitch’s paradox of knowability: open with the principle that every true proposition is in principle knowable, then move through the middle tercets to trace the derivation that if some particular truth actually remained unknown, the statement describing that very unknown status would itself constitute a true but unknown proposition, thereby violating the principle applied to itself. Let refrain A1 state the knowability thesis in plain language, and let refrain A2 state the contradictory result of an actually unknown truth; arrange the rhyme sounds so that the two refrains share a sonic root that tightens across the poem. In the final quatrain, fuse A1 and A2 into a single closing couplet so that the structural collapse of the refrains enacts the logical collapse from ◊Kp to Kp—that is, from the modal possibility of knowledge to the actuality of universal knowledge—rather than merely describing it. Render each logical step with enough precision that a specialist recognizes the self-referential conjunction generating the contradiction, yet keep the diction audible and unadorned for listeners who have just worked through the proof. Do not offer a theological or moral resolution; end with the paradox locked in place, the two refrains now inseparable.
