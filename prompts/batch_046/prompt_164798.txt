Following a recent diagnosis that gives me a precise prognosis—approximately eighteen months before significant cognitive decline, with no corresponding physical impairment—I have been trying to determine whether meaning can survive the knowledge that one's consciousness is about to be bifurcated. I am specifically interested in how the absurdist tradition’s claim that meaning arises from the tension between human expectation and an indifferent universe interacts with contemporary naturalistic accounts that ground meaning not in transcendence but in the structure of caring and practical commitment.

Write a short story, no longer than 800 words, about a protagonist who learns she will undergo a standard clinical procedure in which her neural patterns are mapped onto a non-biological substrate designed to eliminate all predictive error: the duplicate will experience no mortality, no friction between desire and reality, and therefore no capacity for the kind of rebellion against silence that the absurdist framework treats as central. Her biological self will continue for the remaining months with full awareness of the duplicate’s existence.

Treat the duplication not as science-fiction spectacle but as an unremarkable medical option, and avoid generic tropes about digital immortality or artificial intelligence. Instead, keep the narrative in ordinary actions—preparing a meal, answering an old letter, walking a route she once shared with someone she has lost—while exploring whether her local, finite commitments collapse, reconfigure, or deepen once she realizes that the specific predictive failure her brain cannot overcome (the inability to fully model its own ending) is precisely what the substrate eliminates by design. Incorporate a contemporary naturalistic view of meaning—such as the idea that meaning arises from caring about particular things in ways that structure practical reasoning—to show how her relationship to these commitments changes in light of the bifurcation. The story should not resolve whether the duplication represents salvation or annihilation.
