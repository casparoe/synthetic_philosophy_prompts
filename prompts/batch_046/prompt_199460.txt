I am designing a single ninety-minute seminar for first-year graduate students in economics and computer science who understand utility representations and preference orderings but have no prior exposure to formal social choice theory. The goal is to teach the Gibbard-Satterthwaite result—specifically that any deterministic social choice function over three or more alternatives that is strategy-proof and non-dictatorial must restrict the range of admissible outcomes—together with the concept of strategy-proofness itself. I want a teaching plan, not the lecture transcript.

Set out the order in which the conceptual components must be introduced, with explicit dependency reasoning: why the formal definition of a social choice function must precede the definition of strategy-proofness, why the three-alternative threshold needs motivation before the impossibility statement, and where range restriction should appear relative to the intuition of manipulation. Identify the single concrete scenario that should carry the entire explanation—specifically, a three-laboratory consortium selecting shared research equipment through a deterministic preference-aggregation mechanism in which no monetary side payments are permitted—and specify exactly what structural features this scenario must include (full preference revelation, deterministic outcome, absence of transferable utility) and what it must exclude (probabilistic allocation, restricted domains, or dictatorial assignment) so that the theorem's conditions remain visible rather than hidden.

Map the predictable misconceptions these students will bring or form: the conflation of strategy-proofness with dominant-strategy equilibrium in game-theoretic settings, the inference that the theorem eliminates strategic voting in all institutional contexts (ignoring restricted preference domains), and the misunderstanding of dictatorship as merely an undesirable endpoint rather than the unavoidable consequence of unrestricted range plus strategy-proofness. For each misconception, state the precise point in the sequence where it should be headed off, and what conceptual distinction must be in place first for the correction to stick.

Provide one diagnostic check for understanding that tests whether students have grasped the dependency structure—why range restriction is not an arbitrary assumption but the only escape route—rather than merely memorizing the slogan. Finally, state explicitly what must be omitted at this level and acknowledged as such: the full combinatorial proof manipulating preference profiles over triples, extensions to probabilistic mechanisms, and any reference to specific institutional mechanisms such as school-choice algorithms; do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations.
