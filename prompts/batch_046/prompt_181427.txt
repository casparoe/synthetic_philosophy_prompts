Next Thursday I am presenting to a small philosophy circle that meets in the back room of the community library. I have no formal training in philosophy or religious studies—only years of personal meditation practice and a habit of reading whatever I can understand about Buddhism and the mind—but I have developed a view I want to defend: that the Buddhist teaching of anattā does not erase personal continuity so much as relocate it. Rather than a permanent ego-substance, I argue that what we call personal identity is a kind of narrative resonance sustained by karmic intention that functions like an echo rather than a carrier; the same "person" persists not because some identical core travels through time, but because each moment of consciousness inherits and slightly reshapes a pattern of concern, producing continuity without a substrate. The audience is precise, and I need to be ready for them. There will be about twelve people: a retired clinical psychologist who studies narrative identity in aging patients and tends to treat the self as a story the brain tells; a Theravāda monk who takes the Abhidhamma literally and insists on discrete mind-moments with no hidden thread; two secular mindfulness coaches who constantly invoke "the observing self" and fear that denying any self undermines moral accountability; and several lay attendees who mostly seek practical reassurance. I want a speaker's prep sheet, not a general essay: the objections ordered by how likely they are and how damaging they would be, each phrased in the vocabulary that particular listener would use, with a brief reply and a clear note on whether I should concede, hold firm, or split the difference. Please also flag the weakest point in my echo-pattern position—the place where my reasoning is thinnest and I should prepare to defend rather than hope they miss it—especially the objection I am dreading most, which I suspect concerns whether an echo-pattern without an enduring agent can genuinely bear moral responsibility across discontinuous moments, and advise how I might reply within the brief discussion time we will have.
