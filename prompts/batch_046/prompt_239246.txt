Hey — I retired from physics a few years back and now sit in on philosphy workshops with about 8-10 people, usually 90 minutes. I want an in-room activity that makes us feel Dennett's idea that the self is just a center of narrative gravity, not some little pilot inside. Don't lecture up front; the rules should force us to build a "person" ourselves and then watch it slide around.

Set it up like this: each table gets a jumbled stack of first-person log entrys — memories, body descriptions, value-statements — that actually belong to three fictional lives, though we don't know that. Their job is to construct one coherent "center" by linking entries into a story that explains the most data with the least contradiction. Give them a scoring rule: coherance points for every entry they fit into their narrative thread, penalty for orphan cards they leave out. They defend their center to the room.

Then about halfway, drop in "discontinuity cards" — same handwriting and values, but wrong body, wrong date, missing years. Physical and psychological continuity both break, yet the narrative thread still pulls them together if coherance is the goal. Force a vote: split into two persons, or keep one center that jumped?

Debrief should map those moments — initial grouping, scoring pressure, the jump vote — onto the idea. Be honest about which feature of narrative gravity a card game can't reproduce and you'll have to supply yourself. Also tell me what'll predictably go wrong the first run; I think people will cling to body-continuity way too long.

Please say how confident you are in all this, and single out the point you're least sure of.
