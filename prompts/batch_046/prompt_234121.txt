A recent popular essay summarizes the debate over laws of nature like this: Humeans, in the Lewisian best-system tradition, hold that laws are just the axioms that provide the most efficient summary of the Humean mosaic; there is no metaphysical difference between a law and an accidental regularity, only a pragmatic difference in how we organize descriptions. Anti-Humeans, such as defenders of Armstrong's DTA theory, hold that laws are real relations of nomic necessitation linking universals, which is why they genuinely explain their instances. The essay then claims that because Humeans reduce laws to descriptions, they cannot ground counterfactuals—there is nothing in the world that makes it true that an unstruck match would not light—so Humeanism collapses into a purely descriptive view of science that cannot distinguish prediction from post-hoc cataloguing.
