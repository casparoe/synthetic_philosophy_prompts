Please explain the conceptual core of Molyneux's problem—not merely as a dispute about whether empirical learning is required, but as a deeper question about whether spatial form can be a genuinely modality-independent kind. The standard framing asks whether a subject who has learned cubes and spheres by touch could recognize them by sight alone upon being cured of blindness. Push past that: articulate why the problem threatens to collapse if we deny that there is any unified shape shared between haptic and visual experience, and why accepting immediate recognition might commit us to a kind of hylomorphism in which form is separable from sensory matter. At the same time, explain what an embodied, anti-representational account of spatial cognition—one that treats touch and vision as structurally incommensurable—would have to say about the very coherence of asking whether the subject recognizes the object, rather than simply acquiring a new, non-translatable skill. Clarify whether the problem is genuinely about cross-modal identity, or whether it smuggles in an unjustified assumption that perception aims at modality-neutral objects.
