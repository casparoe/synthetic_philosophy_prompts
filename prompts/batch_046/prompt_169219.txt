Following the sudden death of my brother last year, I have found that the standard philosophical frameworks for meaning in life—subjectivist fulfillment, objectivist contribution to value, and even the classical absurdist response—address a different problem from the one that grief actually raises. I am applying for a six-month independent research grant to examine whether there is a distinct form of meaning-collapse, which I will call structural absurdity, that is not captured by either metaphysical absurdism or by hybrid accounts that combine subjective fulfillment with objective worth. On my preliminary formulation, structural absurdity occurs when a life contains robust local meanings—deep relationships, engaged work, aesthetic commitment—that are fully functioning and genuinely valued by the agent, yet the agent recognizes that these local meanings depend for their coherence on sustaining conditions, particular attachments, capacities, and contexts that are contingent and, in principle, losable. The result is not that life is globally meaningless in the nihilistic sense, nor that the agent fails to love worthy objects, but that the accumulation of local meanings does not generate global coherence because the agent sees that none of the sustaining conditions are necessary. I want the funded project to ask precisely this: is structural absurdity a genuine conceptual challenge to hybrid theories that treat meaning as arising when an agent is fittingly fulfilled through engagement with objectively valuable objects, or can those theories absorb it by distinguishing sharply between the conditions for meaning and the conditions for its endurance? And if it is a genuine challenge, does it motivate a revised form of absurdism—one that affirms local meaning while denying that local meaning aggregates into global significance? The proposal should state the question with enough precision to distinguish structural absurdity from ordinary grief-related loss of purpose and from existentialist metaphysical absurdity; explain why existing hybrid and objectivist positions leave the issue open, specifically where they risk conflating the loss of meaning-sustaining conditions with the absence of meaning altogether; set out the method, which should be primarily conceptual analysis of coherence conditions within hybrid frameworks, supplemented by a phenomenological test case drawn from bereavement that functions as an illustrative constraint rather than autobiography; describe what a useful deliverable would look like for practitioners in philosophical counseling or palliative care who encounter clients reporting exactly this pattern of intact local purpose coupled with global incoherence; explain why a philosophical rather than psychological project is the right tool here; and provide an honest assessment of whether six months is sufficient to do more than defend the distinction and map its implications, or whether the scope needs to be narrowed to one side of the debate. I will add citations and references myself. The framing should acknowledge the personal origin of the question without allowing it to substitute for philosophical argument.
