Im prepping questions for a podcast Q&A with this philosopher — call her Dr. Reed — who just put out a book on ontological disputes. Her big move is saying that debates like "do numbers exist?" arent about whats real, their register mismatches: people are running different language-games and not noticing. But she doesnt just recycle Carnap; she adds this idea she calls register inertia, which is that once a community is locked into one register they literally cant translate their claims into another one without losing whatever practical work the claim was doing. Its not that you can just switch frameworks whenever you want.

I want questions that actually press her, not just "walk us through chapter two." Specifically:

1. Ask her how register inertia differs from just saying people are stubborn. Then the follow-up: if inertia is supposed to be an empirical claim about linguistic communities, what kind of data could actually show its false — or is she just describing cultural stickiness?

2. Push the unfalsifiability worry. If I come in and say "no, numbers really do exist independent of our talk," she can just reply "thats the catalog-register talking, youre not in the narrative-register." How is that different from defining away objections? Follow-up keyed to her likely answer that inertia is like Kuhnian incommensurability: does that turn ontology into sociology?

3. The moral case. Someone says torture is wrong, another says wrongness doesnt exist. Reed calls this a register clash. But the first person isnt playing a game; theyre making a demand. How does she keep the normative force without smuggling realism back in? Follow-up anticipating she'll say "practical entanglement" — ask whether that phrase just renames the problem.

4. Ask whether register inertia makes her view more conservative than old-school deflationists, since they at least thought you could switch frames at will. If inertia is real, are we stuck with our ontological commitments?

5. Ask about fictional objects vs abstract ones — does inertia apply differently? Follow-up: if mathematical registers are more stable, does that explain why mathematicians rarely have ontological crises?

6. A closing question about what it would take for her to admit a dispute isnt verbal. Is there any disagreement she thinks is genuinely about the world?

Write me maybe seven or eight questions total, with the follow-ups marked clearly. Keep it casual, my notes are a mess and I probably left a typo or two in here.
