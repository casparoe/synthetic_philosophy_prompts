Darwin showed that functional complexity like the eye can emerge through selection without any agent's foresight, and Turing showed that universal computation can proceed without semantic comprehension of the symbols manipulated. Together these form an inversion: high competence does not entail understanding. Apply this combined inversion to five domains of human performance—native grammatical judgment, expert chess pattern recognition, intuitive moral evaluation in sacrificial dilemmas, scientific paradigm choice, and artistic authorship attribution. Rank them from 1 to 5 according to how severely this inversion undermines the claim that competence in the domain requires corresponding comprehension, with 1 being the most undermined. Respond as a numbered list of exactly five items, ordered by rank; each item should state the domain, its rank, and in one or two sentences explain whether selection-like tuning, computation-like symbol manipulation, or both drive the competence, and why that weakens or preserves the inference to understanding. Do not include an introduction or conclusion.
