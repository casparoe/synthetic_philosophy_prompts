In my philosophy of value seminars I have noticed a recurring manoeuvre in debates about tranquilism — the view that only the absence of craving and suffering carries genuine value. The pattern unfolds in two stages. First, an interlocutor presents what appears to be a clear case of positive value independent of any prior deprivation: imagine a conscious being that experiences nothing but serene aesthetic contemplation, with no antecedent lack, no hidden frustration, and no future threat of loss. Rather than denying the intuition outright, the tranquilist reconceptualises the state: the contemplation is said to depend on a subtle craving for continued existence, or the absence of suffering is treated as a background condition that alone makes the contemplation possible. Second, one might point to knowledge pursued purely for its own sake; the response is that the pursuit itself expresses a craving for certainty, and that its satisfaction is merely the removal of an irritation. What interests me is not the substantive position but the form: any proposed positive good is translated, without remainder, into either a disguised absence of suffering or a masked form of craving. The translation is always available, yet it never seems to yield a testable prediction — one cannot imagine a positive experience that would count as a genuine counterexample, because the vocabulary of craving and suffering expands to cover it. I wish to know whether this dialectical strategy has an established name in value theory or in the logic of scientific theories. Is it a variant of immunisation, or of the auxiliary-hypothesis strategy that Popper criticised? Or does it differ precisely because the expansion is conceptual rather than empirical — a redefinition of the core terms rather than an addition to the theory’s protective belt? I should like a precise carving: what exactly the phenomenon is, how it differs from ordinary definitional manoeuvring and from ad hoc rescue, and where its boundaries lie. If no received term captures it, I should welcome a coinage together with clear conditions for its application and its distinction from plain equivocation.
