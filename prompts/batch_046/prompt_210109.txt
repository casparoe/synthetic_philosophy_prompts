I am a physicist fluent in statistical mechanics and renormalization-group theory but with only a lay familiarity with philosophy of mind. Restate Daniel Dennett's distinction among the physical, design, and intentional stances, together with his claim that patterns count as real when they support reliable predictions, using only the vocabulary of statistical physics: microstates and macrostates, coarse-graining, effective theories at different scales, universality classes, predictive compression, and the distinction between ontologically fundamental and merely instrumentally useful descriptions. For each stance, specify exactly what physical concept it maps onto and where the mapping requires forcing the philosophical idea into a physical mold. Then deliver the residue report: which elements of Dennett's account survive intact, which are distorted by being treated as just another level of description, and which have no counterpart in statistical mechanics—particularly any normative or voluntary aspect of adopting a stance, and the idea that patterns are real in a non-derivative sense. Conclude by assessing whether the residue indicates that Dennett is describing something statistical physics genuinely omits, or whether it exposes imprecision in his original formulation that disappears once the physical analog is made explicit.
