In current AI governance, certification protocols at major labs and in proposed regulation treat exhaustive process documentation—training-data provenance, interpretability reports, and red-teaming logs—as establishing a form of causal-chain custody that makes a system accountable, while treating aggregate post-deployment harm statistics as epistemically opaque for assigning responsibility, even though deployment decisions themselves are driven by outcome metrics like accuracy and revenue. What philosophical commitments about explanation, moral responsibility, and the ontology of causation does that bifurcated practice presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory that licenses privileging process-custody in evaluation but relying on outcome-optimization in deployment; distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely fits it; assess whether its parts are consistent; and take a definite position on whether a uniformly outcome-based supervision regime would correct an incoherence or discard a genuinely important distinction.
