Here's an argument I cooked up about Weber and I'm stuck. (1) If values cause economic change, they have to operate independently of material conditions, or else it's just economics in disguise. (2) Weber claims the Protestant ethic caused rational capitalism via elective affnity with bourgeois practices. (3) But if values have real causal power, then any culture with strong ascetic, world-denying norms—like Jain merchant ethics or Buddhist monastic economies—should've generated capitalism-like rationalization too, yet they clearly didn't. (4) So Weber either has to claim those values weren't genuinely present (making his thesis unfalsifiable) or admit that values only bite when there's already an instutional slot open for them—call it value-slot matching. (5) But if every value needs a pre-made slot, then culture is never the cause; it's just the paint on structural change. (6) So either Weber's thesis collapses into materialism, or cultural causation is an empty label. Where's the slip? I want to keep that culture actually matters, but steps 4-5 seem to trap me. Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost?
