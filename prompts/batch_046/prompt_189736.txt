I advise a regional planning authority that must evaluate whether legacy institutional patterns are products of path-dependent lock-in or of functional convergence. The board needs a reusable decision procedure that analysts without graduate training can run on any inherited policy or infrastructure framework to determine whether reform should be incremental or structural, with clear hand-off points where historical dynamics end and value judgments begin. Please construct the procedure as an ordered sequence of diagnostic questions, specifying for each what counts as sufficient evidence, how to distinguish branching path dependence from convergent functional equivalence, and where the analysis stops with a recommendation versus stops with an explicit flag that the board must decide. Ground the distinctions in what path-dependence theory actually maintains—early contingency, increasing returns, functional substitutability—without requiring citation or literature access. Then apply the procedure to two cases: a 1970s industrial zoning compromise now under pressure for mixed-use conversion, and a 1950s water-rights treaty now criticized for inequitable allocation. In each application, include the case where the procedure recommends radical reform that the board is likely to reject, and mark precisely where judgment enters despite the procedural framework.
