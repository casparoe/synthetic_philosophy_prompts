I was listening to a policy podcast that tried to explain why Scandinavian welfare states work, and the host offered what he called the Nordic model in a nutshell. His summary went roughly like this: the Nordic countries prove that if you tax citizens at roughly fifty percent and build universal welfare, you automatically generate the high social trust required to sustain those taxes; because trust is the real engine, the institutional package can be copied anywhere—Sweden even exported the same high-trust logic to its prostitution laws, and since both the welfare state and that legal framework succeeded, the model is clearly portable. The only reason other countries fail, he concluded, is low tax morale, not culture or history.

I am skeptical, but I am not sure which parts are simplifications and which are outright mistakes. Could you audit this summary for me? In particular, distinguish what is broadly accurate about the relationship between taxation, welfare provision, and social trust in Denmark, Norway, and Sweden; identify where the summary treats correlation as causation or ignores well-known institutional prerequisites; assess whether the reference to Swedish prostitution policy is a category error or reveals a genuine parallel; and evaluate whether the claim that the model is exportable survives once the misrepresentations are corrected. I do not want a bibliography—just a disciplined separation of the defensible claims from the distortions, and a judgment about whether the host's conclusion actually follows from the accurate parts.
