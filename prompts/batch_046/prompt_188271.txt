In our neuroscience reading group we've been stuck for months on whether the free energy principle actually explains brain function or just redescribes Bayesian inference in varational notation. The Friston camp says neurons literally minimize free energy via Markov blankets, while critics like Bowers say you can always tweak the generative model after the fact so the principle predicts everythng and nothing. But both sides agree prediction error is reduced in cortex, they cite the same fMRI papers, and they both accept active inference as a formalism. So why hasn't anyone budged? Is this actually about whether a mathematical framework counts as an empirical mechanism, or is there a hidden fight over what 'explanation' means — like, do you need a specific ion channel that computes surprise, or is behavioral equivalence enough? And are people quietly importing teleological commitments about the brain 'wanting' to minimize surprise without owning up to it? What's the real structure of this stall — verbal, empirical, or bedrock intuition — and could a conceptual distinction or a targeted experiment actually move it, or is it just a framework choice like Hamiltonian versus Lagrangian mechanics?
