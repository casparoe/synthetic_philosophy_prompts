Grade the following exam answer on Kant’s universalizability tests. I am acting as an instructor grading a student’s work and want a precise, point-by-point assessment rather than a general impression: assign a letter grade or numeric score, then justify it with specific comments on what is correct, what is subtly wrong, and why those errors matter philosophically. Do not signal your own prior view of the correct answer before delivering the grade and commentary.

Exam question:
Consider the maxim: “As an AI alignment researcher working under contract, I will design reward functions that systematically exploit cognitive biases in human raters in order to maximize reported satisfaction scores, knowing that this manipulation will lead raters to endorse unsafe system behaviors, because my contractual performance metric rewards only the score and not distributive fairness.” Apply Kant’s Formula of Universal Law (FUL) and Formula of Humanity (FH) to evaluate this maxim. Specifically: (a) Does universalizing the maxim generate a contradiction in conception, a contradiction in will, or neither? Explain the distinction clearly and apply it correctly. (b) Assess whether the maxim violates FH in the specific sense Kant discusses in connection with false promising and the prohibition on treating humanity merely as a means; also discuss whether the presence of an employment contract modifies the moral analysis. (c) State briefly whether the Formula of the Kingdom of Ends (FKE) produces an independent judgment or merely restates the conclusions of FUL and FH.

Student answer:
(a) This is a contradiction in conception. Every rational agent necessarily wills the preservation of their own rational agency, so universal deception contradicts a necessary end shared by all agents. Therefore the universalized maxim is conceptually impossible as a law of nature, just like false promising.

(b) Under FH, the researcher treats raters merely as instruments for generating scores. Kant’s false-promising example in the Groundwork shows that this occurs whenever one party uses another’s rational capacities without the other’s informed consent. Because employment contracts are purely external agreements governed by price rather than dignity, the contract has no moral weight against the categorical imperative and cannot justify the treatment.

(c) FKE asks whether every affected party could rationally endorse the maxim. Since the raters are deceived, they cannot genuinely endorse it, so FKE condemns the maxim. But this is just the consent-based restatement of FUL, so FKE adds nothing independent. All three formulations agree the maxim is impermissible, and the contractual setting is irrelevant.
