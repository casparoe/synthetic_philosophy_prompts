I've settled on a view about the absent-minded driver problem - the classic case where someone drives to Middletown, takes every correct turn, but has completely forgotten thats where they're headed and can't report it if asked. Most people say he still knows the way implicitly or has a tacit belief. I think thats wrong. I think there's a seperate kind of directional knowing - call it vector-knowledge - that can guide action without being about anything at all. Its not propositional, its not even implicitly representational; its just a kind of embodied orientation that carries the agent toward an endpoint without mental content matching that endpoint. What would actually count against this? I want the defeaters ranked: which observations about navigation failures, amnesia, or procedural breakdown would be fatal, which just awkward? Would a case where the driver ends up in the wrong town but feels like he arrived correctly destroy it? And be honest: has this view already been qualified into unfalsifiability, and is that a problem for a claim about practical knowledge? Don't just list options - take a stand, tell me what should move me, and say whether the insulation is a bug or a feature.
