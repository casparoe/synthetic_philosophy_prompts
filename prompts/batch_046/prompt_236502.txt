Write a two-page research proposal for a semester-long independent study (approximately 8,000 words) addressing the following precise question: Can Nietzsche's account of the slave revolt in morality in On the Genealogy of Morals be reconstructed as a purely structural dynamics of value-inversion—defining reactive valuation functionally by its dependence on an external enemy—without relying on the specific historical narrative about the Judeo-Christian priestly caste? State the question so that it admits a determinate answer: does the explanatory force of the slave revolt survive once the ascetic ideal is treated as an abstract functional role, a value-inverting mechanism, rather than as the achievement of a particular social group?

Explain why standard interpretations leave this structural question open: both defenders of the historical narrative, who treat the priestly story as essential, and critics, who dismiss it as rhetorical embellishment, tend to conflate the functional structure with its historical vehicle. Describe your method: which arguments and distinctions in the text you would examine, how you would separate structural claims from genealogical ones, and why this approach is preferable to the obvious alternative of either accepting or rejecting the historical story wholesale. Specify the possible conclusions—for example, that the structural account succeeds but loses the specific normative content of slave morality, such as the elevation of suffering, or that it collapses back into the historical account—and identify the considerations that would decide between them.

Give an honest assessment of scope: what makes this tractable for one semester, and what could make it fail or expand beyond the available time, such as if distinguishing structure from history requires a full exegesis of every section on asceticism. Finally, identify one concrete failure condition for the project—for example, if the functional reconstruction turns out to be indistinguishable from Nietzsche's own historical account, leaving nothing genuinely new to say.
