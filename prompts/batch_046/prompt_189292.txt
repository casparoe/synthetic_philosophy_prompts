I'm giving a talk next thursday to our phil of mind / cog sci joint seminar, defending this weird middle view I cooked up about ant colonies and collective minds. I'm calling it 'polyphonic functionalism' — I take the Aunt Hillary / Ant Fugue scenario seriously, but I don't think the colony is one unified subject like Hofstadter's metaphor almost suggests. Instead I think it's more like a fugue with overlapping voices: lots of partial minds running parallel, locking into temporary 'chorus states' where info integrates, but never reaching a single phenomenal point of view at the top. So not panpsychism, not one big mind, but not just dumb mechanistic swarm behavior either.

The audience is a mixed bag: Dr. Chen studies actual ant phermone networks and hates antropomorphism, two grad students are hardcore panpsychists who'll try to make me admit micro-experiences exist, a phil of biology who thinks evolutionary continuity arguments are being abused, and our chair who cares if I'm sneaking in unfalsifiable claims. I need objections in the order they'll come, phrased like each would actually say it — especially Chen's 'where's the empirical hook?' and the panpsychists' 'if integration happens, why deny unified experience?' I need notes on what to concede (probably no single neural analog), what reply must land (access integration vs phenomenal binding), and the one question I'm praying nobody asks: whether my 'chorus state' applies to human crowds or corporations, since then I'm either committed to group minds everywhere or drawing an arbitrary line.
