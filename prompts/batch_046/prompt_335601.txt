Philosophers and cognitive scientists who use Dennett's taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian minds often reach an impasse over the Gregorian category: one camp holds that entering this stage requires a novel internal capacity for meta-representation or symbolic recombination, while the other holds that it is merely Popperian cognition operating within dense cultural scaffolding, with no new mental machinery required. The evolutionary story is shared; the architecture is contested. Diagnose why this debate has stalled. Is it a substantive disagreement about mental structure, a verbal dispute over whether "kind of mind" should be defined by internal mechanisms or by environmental coupling, or two research programs—one phylogenetic, one ontogenetic—using the same four labels? Ground your diagnosis in what each side actually commits to, and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress: a particular empirical result about non-linguistic social learning, a formal distinction between representational redescription and distributed cognition, or evidence that the taxonomy itself collapses under its own criteria.
