Write an essay evaluating whether an asymmetry between lowering suffering set-points and raising pleasure set-points undermines the standard defense of the hedonistic imperative in biotechnology-oriented abolitionist ethics. The imperative holds that genetic and neurochemical engineering should eliminate suffering and replace it with gradients of well-being, yet critics observe that hedonic adaptation and opponent-process dynamics may render sustained high-level pleasure unstable, while lowering the suffering floor could prove more robust. Assess whether this asymmetry requires reformulating the imperative as purely negative—abolishing suffering without guaranteeing elevated pleasure—rather than symmetrically positive. Weigh the neurobiological plausibility of decoupling suffering reduction from pleasure enhancement, consider whether a life of neutral-to-mild contentment satisfies the moral urgency motivating the project, and defend a clear verdict: does the asymmetry strengthen the case for exclusive suffering abolition, weaken it by revealing an impoverished vision of the good life, or demand a third formulation?
