A colleague summarized Adam Smith's moral psychology as follows: sympathy operates by reproducing another person's feelings in ourselves through an imaginative change of places, and the impartial spectator is essentially the internalized average opinion of one's actual social circle—like a mental poll of neighbors and peers. From this, she derives that moral approval is just statistical consensus within a culture: propriety means whatever sentiment the local majority finds fitting, and justice is valued only instrumentally, as a convention that stabilizes commercial exchange. She concludes that Smith offers no genuinely objective moral standard, only a descriptive account of how communities coordinate sentiment. Is this summary fair? Separate what it gets right about sympathy, what it misrepresents about the spectator's required impartiality and informational conditions, and whether it collapses Smith's distinction between propriety and merit. Then say whether the anti-objectivist conclusion survives once the misrepresentations are corrected, and whether a more accurate analogy for the spectator would be a judge rather than a poll.
