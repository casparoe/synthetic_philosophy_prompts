I am preparing a position paper for a workshop on moral responsibility and have sketched the following five commitments on standing to blame and hypocrisy. Before I present them, I need a consistency audit. My commitments are these:

(a) Moral standing to blame is undermined by hypocrisy, where hypocrisy is understood as a failure of consistency between one's own moral judgments and one's conduct.

(b) Standing is relational: A can blame B for wrong X only if A is not also culpably involved in X in a manner that renders A's blame self-undermining.

(c) In cases of structurally mediated harm—such as benefiting from supply chains involving forced labour—an individual's standing to blame a corporation is not automatically cancelled by their own indirect participation, provided that their agency in the harm is sufficiently attenuated.

(d) Hypocrisy requires more than mere accidental parallel conduct; it requires that the agent either knows, or should reasonably know, of the relevant similarity between her own case and the target's.

(e) The primary function of blame is expressive: its purpose is to register and reinforce normative commitments, rather than to exact retribution or to serve as a vehicle for moral ranking.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled, and how severely. For each genuine tension, indicate what the cheapest revision would be and what that revision costs in terms of the other commitments. Then rank the tensions by severity and tell me which single revision—if I had to make only one—would clean up the most inconsistencies without collapsing the overall view. Please write your response in British English.
