I'm the ethics consultant on call at a long-term care place, and every other weak some patient or family asks staff "what's the point?" or "why am I still here?" I want a reusable intake checklist — flowchart in words — for my nurses, grounded in Frank Ramsey's idea that asking about "the meaning of life" usually mistakes a practical commitment crisis for a theoretical knowledge request. I want to inject this niche nursing-ethics distinction: "epistimic stance calibration" (testing whether values still align with the care plan) versus "propositional craving" (wanting a factual answer). The procedure should list the gates in order, what counts as passing each, where it stops with "call ethics now," and where it stops with "this is values misalignment — stop explaining and start renegotating goals." Apply it to three cases: an 82-year-old with dementia asking "why don't I just die?", a 45-year-old ALS patient asking if her suffering has purpose, and a daughter asking "what's the meaning of keeping dad alive like this?" Include one where the answer forces staff to change the plan rather than comfort the asker. Flag judgment calls, keep it to a five-minute run, and don't ask for citations or reading lists — just the procedure.
