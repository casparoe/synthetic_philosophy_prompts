I'm writing a feature on rational decision-making under deep uncertainty, and I want to pressure-test my own framework before I interview philosophers and economists. Here are my commitments: (1) normative consequentialism is correct—actions are right in proportion to the value they produce; (2) we face profound cluelessness about the long-run effects of present choices, especially regarding transformative technologies; (3) this cluelessness does not justify maximin or satisficing—we should still act on our best point estimates of expected value; (4) preserving "option value"—keeping future pathways open—is itself a consequentialist reason to avoid irreversible commitments now; and (5) epistemic humility about distant outcomes should push our credences toward near-uniform distributions, which undermines the point-estimate reasoning in (3). Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only appear so? Rank the tensions by severity, specify the least costly revision for each, and tell me whether (3) and (5) can be held together through some account of option value, or whether one must be abandoned.
