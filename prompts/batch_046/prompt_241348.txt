So my mate who's been writing about corruption norms across Finland and West Africa has given me her verdicts on five cases, and I want to guess what she'll say about a sixth. Her calls so far: a Lagos customs officer takes monthly cash envelopes from importers but processes every file correctly and never delays anyone specifically — corruption; a Finnish mayor hires her sister as a cleaner after an open advert and an independant panel picks her, with the mayor recusing herself — not corruption; a defence procurement officer receives an expensive watch from a contractor after awarding a tender, keeps it secret, but the contract was genuinely best value — corruption; a rural clinic doctor accepts seasonal food and labour help from patients' families as part of local custom, treating everyone identically — not corruption; and a Swedish MP directs bridge funding to her home district where it's genuinely needed, fully discloses the link, and abstains from the vote — not corruption.

The new case: a Helsinki planner belongs to a housing cooperative with her neighbours. She recuses herself from the zoning vote on their development, but beforehand she shared internal 'best practice' notes with the cooperative board — notes she didn't share with competing applicants — which shaped an application that then went through standard technical review and was approved by others. No cash changed hands, her membership was declared, but the inside guidance gave her network a structual edge. What's her verdict?

I'm less bothered about the answer than about the rule she's running. Is it hidden quid pro quo, undisclosed conflict of interest, strategic use of public office for private network gain, or something else? What other rules fit those five verdicts just as well, what does each predict here, and what single further case would best tell them apart?
