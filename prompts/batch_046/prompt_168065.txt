Write a short story about a xenolinguist, Dr. Vey, who is decoding the cognitive architecture of a long-dead alien civilization that engineered four nested forms of intelligence within a single symbiotic organism. The innermost layer evolves behavior purely through environmental selection without any individual learning; the second learns through reinforcement but never constructs internal representations; the third generates and tests hypotheses internally, allowing bad ideas to die before action; and the outermost acquires and transmits complex symbolic tools across generations. In the course of her research, Vey discovers that the outer layer developed a pathological variant she terms a “hollow Gregorian” form: it could absorb, replicate, and ritualize any cultural technology or belief system it encountered, but it had lost the capacity to generate novel symbolic structures of its own. Because this hollow layer eventually dominated the organism, the civilization could adopt every innovation it contacted but could not adapt them to changing conditions, since the inner Popperian layer’s internal criticism was treated as heresy and suppressed. The plot should turn on a specific crisis—perhaps a failing life-support system that requires inventing a new symbolic framework to reinterpret sensor data—where the hollow Gregorian layer insists on applying every previously adopted ritual in sequence, while the Popperian layer attempts to kill off the failing interpretations internally and the Skinnerian layer simply repeats the behaviors that were once rewarded. Do not treat the four categories as metaphors; build the narrative around their functional differences, especially the distinction between reinforcement learning, internal selection, and cultural inheritance, and show why the absence of generative capacity in the outer layer proves fatal despite its vast accumulated knowledge.
