I work as a senior reviewer for a synthetic-biology oversight board that must classify engineered microbial strains for regulatory exemption, and our caseload is high enough that each file gets roughly thirty minutes of structured analysis before a recommendation is due to the board chair, who holds final sign-off. The governing statute grants exemptions only when an organism performs what it calls a natural biological function rather than a novel engineered role, yet the text never specifies whether function should be understood etiologically, as the evolutionary history that selected for the trait, or according to a causal-role account, as the current contribution the trait makes to the organism's metabolism and survival. Because applicants and reviewers alike treat these two frameworks as interchangeable, I need a reusable decision procedure that my team can run without consulting philosophy of biology texts, one that proceeds through ordered questions, states what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, marks the precise points where factual determination ends and a value judgment or conceptual priority must take over, and indicates where the procedure must halt with a denial recommendation versus where it must hand the file to the chair because the conflict is not empirical but conceptual. The procedure should also be honest about its misfires, especially when evolutionary history and present causal contribution diverge sharply and both appear biologically significant. Please apply it to three cases: a Pseudomonas strain modified to express a nitrogen-fixation pathway copied from rhizobia, where the pathway carries a clear selection history in the donor lineage but none in the recipient genome; a synthetic yeast that synthesizes a growth-essential compound through a de novo metabolic route with no evolutionary analog, where the causal role is indispensable under engineered conditions but the etiological history is absent; and a reconstructed gut-microbiome consortium into which a gene cluster from an extinct bacterial lineage, recovered from fossil DNA, has been reintroduced, where the cluster's original selected effect is unknown but its present metabolic impact is precisely measured. Include at least one case in which the procedure yields a denial that the applicant will vigorously contest, and conclude by stating exactly what the procedure cannot settle, particularly in situations where the two accounts of function point in opposite directions. Write your entire response in continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, tables, or numbered lists, as you would in a formal letter or email.
