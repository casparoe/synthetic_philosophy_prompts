okay so i've been arguing with my roommate about whether Marx would have hated ChatGPT or seen it as proof he was right. We both read the machinery chapters in Capital and i get that he thought automation would eventually make labor redundant and collapse the rate of profit, but LLMs feel different because they don't just replace physical work — they ingest past human output (writing, code, art) and remix it. Marx had this concept in the Grundrisse about the general intellect — social knowledge becoming its own productive force — and it feels like AI is literally that, except owned by OpenAI and Google instead of being socialized.

So here's my actual question: if Marx was alive in 2025, would he classify large language models as just advanced machinery in the Capital sense, where the worker is subordinated to the apparatus? Or would he say they're something else — maybe a privatized form of collective knowledge that lets capital extract value not just from current workers but from the acumulated dead labor embedded in training data? I'm especially curious about the hidden labor part: thousands of low-paid workers labeling data and doing RLHF, mostly in Kenya, India, the Philippines. Marx talked about the global reserve army of labor, but this is more like a hidden cognitive reserve army that makes the model seem autonomous. Would he see that as a new layer of exploitation, or just outsourcing?

Also, there's this weird thing where people now talk about "prompt engineering" as a skill. Marx was pretty clear that capital likes to split labor into simple and complex parts. Do you think he'd view prompt crafting as a genuinely skilled mental labor that commands higher wages, or as a transitional job that will get de-skilled once the models improve? I don't need any citations, just reasoning from his framework — but try not to just repeat the standard "robots take jobs" take everyone already knows.
