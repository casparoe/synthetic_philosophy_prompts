Me and my roommate got into this thing last week about Ubuntu and whether it actually changes anything about rights, or if it's just liberalism with a communal vibe. We were talking about this hypothetical choral collective in a township where everyone composes together with no individual credits, and one singer wants to leave and record the songs solo for a label. My roommate said under Ubuntu she's not really an individual author in the first place—her personhood is literally made up of those relationships—so the collective owns the songs and she can't just take them. I said that's wrong: Ubuntu says "I am because we are," not "I don't exist." The community makes her who she is, but it doesn't dissolve her into the group; she can leave, she just owes them recogntion, not her copyright.

He came back that I'm smuggling liberal individualism back in—if she can exit with the goods, then the relational part is just decoration. I said no, the exit conditions are different: liberalism lets her leave because she was always already separate, but Ubuntu lets her leave while carrying the relational debt with her, like shared credit or teaching the younger singers. He said that's incohearent—either the self is prior to relations or it's constituted by them, you can't have a self that both needs the group to exist and can walk away with the products. I think he's being too binary, and that relational constitution can shape what you owe on exit without erasing the fact that you can exit.

Can you go through this move by move? I want to know who's right about the metaphysics of the self, who gets the ethics of leaving right, and whether there's actually a middle ground here or if we're just talking past eachother. Don't just say who wins overall—rate each step and say what both of us are missing.
