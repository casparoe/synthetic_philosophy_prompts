I'm writing out my philosophical commitments on the mind-computation relation for a seminar audit, and I want to know whether the package is coherent before I defend it. Here is where I stand:

(1) I am a computationalist about cognition in a broad sense: reasoning, representation, and decision-making are genuinely information-processing operations, but I reject the classical symbolic architecture and understand these processes as distributed, continuous, dynamical computations rather than discrete symbol manipulation.

(2) I take this dynamical computation to be multiply realizable: in principle, a non-biological system that preserved the relevant functional dynamics and informational relations could implement the same cognitive processes.

(3) I deny that phenomenal consciousness belongs to this computational story. I hold that phenomenal experience depends on specific biological or organic properties and is therefore not substrate-independent.

(4) I maintain that there is an objective, non-arbitrary fact of the matter about what computation a given physical system performs; pancomputationalism is false.

(5) I think the explanatory gap is real for phenomenal consciousness but illusory for intentional content. Identifying representational states with their functional-computational roles closes the gap for intentionality, leaving only the hard problem untouched.

(6) I reject digital theories of mind that require a language of thought, yet I still describe my view as computationalist rather than, say, pure dynamical-cognitivist.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and what is the least costly revision for each real conflict? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each say exactly which commitment must give or be qualified and at what cost to the overall view. Be specific about whether the problem is internal to the conception of computation, internal to the division between cognition and consciousness, or at the boundary between them. I am not asking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature—just a consistency check on this specific package.
