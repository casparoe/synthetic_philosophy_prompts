Consequentialist cluelessness is usually framed as an individual-level failure: an agent cannot assign reliable probabilities to the distant consequences of her actions, so expected-utility reasoning collapses. I want to push the framing toward institutional reflexivity—the problem that collective epistemic architectures (forecasting tournaments, model-based policy evaluation protocols, deliberative aggregation mechanisms) are designed and maintained by agents who are themselves clueless about the second-order and long-run effects of those very architectures. Assuming a research group with three philosophers and one institutional economist, about three years, no large-scale empirical trials, and no mandate to produce bibliographies or citation surveys: which three or four open conceptual questions at this intersection should they prioritize? For each, explain whether it is upstream or downstream of the others, how tractable it is with conceptual and formal tools rather than big-data ML, and whether resolving it would actually change recommendations for institutional design. Then defend the overall ordering: what criteria—tractability, dependency, practical payoff, neglectedness—drive the ranking, where the trade-offs lie, and what kind of argument or conceptual result over the next two years would cause you to reorder the list. Do not provide a survey of the existing literature; deliver a defended agenda for where limited attention should go and why.
