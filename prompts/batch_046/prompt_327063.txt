I'd like a decision tree for sorting positions on the AI alignment problem—specifically the split between narrow intent-alignment and broad value-alignment—designed for readers of rationalist forums. Start from a concrete root: imagine an advanced system that does precisely what its operators intended in every deployable context; does this eliminate the alignment risk, or is there a remaining danger that its objectives conflict with human values?

Branch first on whether "value alignment" is a coherent specification task: are human values too plural, context-dependent, or ontologically contested to be encoded?

Then branch on stability under intent alignment: if interpretability and direct control are solved, does instrumental convergence still subvert the intended goal, or is that failure purely a matter of insufficient capability?

I want leaves capturing at least four combinations: (1) intent alignment is sufficient and value alignment is redundant or incoherent; (2) both are required, but value alignment needs only behavioural equivalence; (3) both are required, and value alignment demands ontological or normative convergence that intent alignment cannot guarantee; (4) the distinction collapses because any sufficiently capable intent-aligned system will necessarily reshape human values.

Phrase every node as a concrete claim or case answerable without knowing the labels. Flag any logically unoccupied combinations, and note particularly whether (2) and (3) can genuinely be separated on purely behavioural commitments, or whether that split requires metaphysical assumptions about values that the tree should make explicit. Keep it to about six branching nodes, formal but concise, with British spelling.
