I was discussing with my friends the other night and I think I've convinced myself of this position: the alignment problem isn't actually one single monster — it's at least three seperate issues that people bundle together for dramatic effect. There's the specification problem, where we can't write down precisely what we want; then there's the robustness problem, where the model behaves fine in training but does something bizzare once it's deployed in a new environment. And then there's this third layer I keep coming back to, which I call the 'category mismatch' problem: the idea that a sufficiently capable system might not share our basic ontological boundaries, so it doesn't recognise the distinction between a 'tool' and a 'goal', or between 'optimising for users' and 'optimising for the metric'. I think if you keep these layers seperate, instrumental convergence mainly threatens the category-mismatch bit, while the other two are basically hard but normal engineering challenges.
