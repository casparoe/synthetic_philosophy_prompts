Plan the explanatory arc—what conceptual distinction has to be in place before the asymmetry even makes sense to them, specifically the difference between evaluating an absence from the perspective of an actual sufferer versus evaluating an absence where there is no actual subject to be deprived—followed by the precise order in which the premises should be introduced so that the conclusion feels like a structural feature of axiological comparison rather than a rhetorical move.

Within that arc, identify the one concrete scenario—staying within the simulated-consciousness framing—that should carry the whole argument without collapsing back into reproductive ethics, noting exactly what it must include (a clearly defined non-actual state) and what it must lack (any implied duty-bearer or biological narrative).
