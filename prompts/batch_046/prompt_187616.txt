After arguing with a friend for hours about whether psychiatric labels actually manufacture the behaviors they claim to discover, I want a satirical piece. Write a deadpan “End User License Agreement” of roughly 450 words for a fictional mental-health platform called Loop, whose terms are written in impenetrable corporate-legal language. The platform assigns each user a dynamic psychiatric category that updates in real time based on the user’s reaction to being labeled; specifically, if a user denies having the assigned disorder, the denial is interpreted as “reactive denial,” which automatically upgrades the category, which in turn revises Section 4’s definition of denial. By the end of the document, the agreement should have amended itself twice within the text, leaving the reader unable to determine which version is authoritative. The satire should exaggerate the real mechanism of Ian Hacking’s looping effects—where interactive kinds change in response to being classified—without inventing a false feature of that view, and the tone must remain strictly deadpan with no winking at the reader. The target is the reflexive application of looping to clinical taxonomy itself, not any named individual.
