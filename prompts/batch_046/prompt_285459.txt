The exchange between Dennett's model of derived intentionality—exemplified by his minimal representational system, sometimes called the two-bitser, whose states acquire content solely through evolutionary or design history—and Searle's counter-insistence on original, intrinsic intentionality has cycled through the same dialectic for decades: functional organization versus the semantic emptiness of syntax, observer-relativity versus biological reality, without evident convergence. My working hypothesis is that the stalemate is not primarily metaphysical but methodological: Dennett treats intentionality as an explanatory posit justified by predictive success, whereas Searle treats it as a phenomenological datum that any adequate theory must preserve, and they therefore apply different standards of theoretical adequacy to the same word. Is this diagnosis correct, or is there instead a hidden empirical disagreement—perhaps about whether artificial systems could ever instantiate conscious representation, or about what neural mechanisms would be necessary for original intentionality—that neither side makes fully explicit? I want a diagnosis of the disagreement's structure: is it verbal, about competing standards of explanation, about irreconcilable starting intuitions regarding intrinsicness, or about a substantive metaphysical question that remains unarticulated? And given that diagnosis, what, if anything, would count as genuine progress: a conceptual distinction that dissolves the conflict, a thought experiment that forces one side into contradiction, or an argument that the entire framing is ill-posed?
