Among historians of early modern science, the debate over Johannes Kepler's relationship to astrology has persisted for decades without convergence. One camp reads his horoscopes, his defense of celestial influence in De fundamentis astrologiae certioribus, and his patronage calculations as evidence of sincere cosmological commitment: for them, Kepler believed that planetary configurations genuinely shaped human temperament and events, and he integrated this into his astronomical program. The opposing camp emphasizes his private complaints about astrological clients, the instrumental use of predictions for income, and the sharp methodological distinction he drew between astronomical calculation (certain) and astrological interpretation (probabilistic and contingent), arguing that he treated astrology as a pragmatic, largely cynical profession rather than a core belief.

My working hypothesis is that the stall is partly verbal—'astrology' is being used as a monolithic category when Kepler in fact operated with sharply differentiated practices (natal, meteorological, and electional), each with its own epistemic status—and partly about hidden evidential standards, since both sides agree on the textual record but disagree on what kind of psychological or sociological inference that record licenses. But I want a diagnosis, not a verdict: is this disagreement about facts (what Kepler actually believed), about the criteria for attributing belief in historical contexts, about whether different astrological practices should be evaluated together, or about the boundary conditions for calling something 'scientific' in the seventeenth century? And given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress—a new archival discovery, a conceptual distinction that disaggregates the practices, a change in the question being asked, or an admission that the dispute is ill-posed? Please support your answer by engaging with what each side actually says about the texts, avoid surveying interpretations for their own sake, and tell me honestly whether anything should be expected to resolve it.
