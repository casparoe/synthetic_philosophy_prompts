As a retired marine biologist who has recently turned to philosophy of culture and political theory, I have been struck by the casual deployment of the phrase "Western values" in both academic and public discourse, and I find myself unable to determine whether it refers to a genuine normative architecture or merely to a geopolitical slogan. I am asking you, acting as a professional philosopher with expertise in conceptual analysis and the history of political thought, to compose a formal dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors who hold opposing views on this exact question. Before you begin, please take a moment to brainstorm the strongest version of each position, ensuring that neither speaker is a strawman and that you have identified the specific points on which they genuinely clash.

The first interlocutor should defend the coherence of "Western values" as a meaningful category, arguing that despite internal diversity there exists a distinctive family of commitments—perhaps including procedural liberalism, the moral priority of the individual, secular public reasoning, and specific institutional inheritances—that hang together in a way that distinguishes this tradition from others. The second interlocutor should reject this coherence, contending that the phrase is an instance of strategic essentialism that collapses historically antagonistic traditions—Christian moral universalism, Enlightenment rationalism, colonial administrative practice, and capitalist exchange ethics—into a false unity, and that its primary function is rhetorical boundary-drawing rather than conceptual description. Crucially, both speakers should engage directly with one another's arguments: for instance, the defender must address the problem of normative overdetermination—the fact that similar norms (e.g., dignity, rights) emerge from incompatible metaphysical sources—while the critic must explain why apparent institutional continuities do not establish genuine conceptual unity. They should also examine how the phrase operates in actual argument: who is included or excluded when it is invoked, and whether its rhetorical utility undermines or reinforces its descriptive validity. Please write the dialogue in a polished, formal register, with real back-and-forth rather than parallel monologues, and avoid invoking obscure specialist terminology unless you first explain its relevance to the coherence debate.
