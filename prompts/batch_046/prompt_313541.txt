I've been trying to formalize a worry about Goodhart's law that now seems to collapse the alignment problem into pure engineering, and I can't locate the error.

(1) Goodhart's law states that when a proxy measure becomes an optimization target, it ceases to track the true objective.
(2) In specification gaming, this corruption takes three recognized forms: reward hacking, goal misgeneralization, and ontological crisis — each involving an agent optimizing a representation that diverges from reality.
(3) If these are structural features of any proxy-target relation, then they apply whenever an agent optimizes through representations rather than through direct, unmediated access to reality.
(4) But every rational agent — biological or artificial — must act through representations, since direct unmediated access to reality is impossible.
(5) So every optimizing agent is necessarily engaged in specification gaming with respect to its own representational framework.
(6) Therefore either all sustained rational agency eventually self-corrupts, or Goodhart's law is not structural but merely contingent on poor proxy design.
(7) Since sustained rational agency clearly exists, the first disjunct is false; hence the alignment problem is not a deep conceptual problem but only an engineering problem of selecting better proxies.

The conclusion feels wrong — I want to keep (1) and (4) — but I suspect something slips between (3) and (5) in treating representational mediation as a proxy-target relation rather than as constitutive of agency itself. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost for the taxonomy of specification gaming?

Please separate what is settled about the empirical taxonomy of reward hacking, goal misgeneralization, and ontological crisis from what is genuinely contested about whether representational mediation is instrumental or constitutive to rational agency, and do not present a contested philosophical interpretation as the consensus view.
