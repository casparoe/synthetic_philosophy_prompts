Write a short story exploring a transcendental argument in a concrete, unfamiliar setting. The protagonist is a mnemonic engineer in a civilization that stores collective memory only through synchronized rhythmic patterns rather than written or digital records. She uncovers that the coherence of these patterns—specifically, the distinction between recollection and invention—depends on a shared, pre-reflective commitment to linear causation that cannot be justified within the patterns themselves. If that commitment were suspended, the society would not merely lose specific memories but lose the conceptual framework that makes error about the past intelligible at all. The story should center on the moment she presents this finding to an empirical audit board that demands observable evidence of the commitment's necessity. She must show that the board's very request for evidence already operates within the linear-causal framework she is describing, without reducing her point to a simple tautology. Ground the narrative in sensory detail—the vibration of storage drums, the smell of oxidized metal, a single pattern that contradicts itself only if linearity is assumed—and leave ambiguous whether recognizing this transcendental condition opens space for cultural revision or locks the society into an inescapable circularity.
