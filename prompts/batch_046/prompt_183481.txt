Write a short story about a modern diplomat who gets invited to a funeral feast in a remote community where they practice endocannibalism—eating the dead as an honor, not out of cruelty. Make it feel like Montaigne's essay Of Cannibals is actually happening: the diplomat starts out judging them as barbaric, then realizes that word is just a reflex of his own custum. But push into the deeper, shakier idea Montaigne only hints at—that human reason is too weak to ever ground a real moral high ground, so relativism isn't a theory we pick but an admission we can't avoid. I want the locals to turn the mirror back hard, maybe with something like that old court anecdote where cannibals visiting Charles IX were shocked that grown men obeyed a child and that people starved next to hoarded gold. The story should take a definite stand—either this collapse of reason kills moral objectivity for good, or it saves objectivity by stripping away false universals. Don't sit on the fence. After the story, add a brief note saying where you took liberties with Montaigne's actual text or leaned on a reading scholars might dispute; no bibliogrphy, just be honest. Keep the diplomat ordinary—no superhuman insight, just someone slowly losing the ground under their feet.
