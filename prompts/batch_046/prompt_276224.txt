Hey — I'm putting together a feature on why the corruption debate has been stuck in the same loop since the 90s transparency boom. You know the split: the World Bank / Transparency International crowd measures bribery and nepotism like they're physics, assuming good governance means weberian impersonal rules. On the other side, anthropologists and local political theorists say that in plenty of societies clientelism is basically the welfare state — it's how goods and accountability actually flow — and calling it corruption just exports Western procedural norms as universal law. Every few years someone publishes a "rethinking corruption" piece, the rebuttal lands, nothing shifts.

My hunch is that the stall isn't about outcomes — it's about what corruption is FOR. One side treats it as a legal-diagnostic for state weakness; the other sees it as a moral category that smuggles in liberal bureaucracy as the only legitimate order. But I want a sharper diagnosis. Is this disagreement verbal (they use "corruption" for different things — illegal exchange vs violation of embedded reciprocity norms)? Substantive but hidden (they disagree on whether patron-client networks deliver goods, but never run the same study)? Or are they answering totally different questions — one empirical, one normative — under one word?

Walk me through what each side actually says that keeps them talking past each other. Is there a hidden empirical fight about state capacity versus social embeddedness? Do they differ on what counts as data — cross-national indices versus ethnographic accounts of gift exchange? And what would progress look like? A taxonomy that separates legal breach from functional reciprocity? An agreed outcome metric? Or is the concept itself doing normative work it pretends isn't there, meaning the debate should be retired, not resolved?

Don't send me a bibliography or reading list — I'd only guess at half the citations without access. Just the concrete diagnosis, and tell me if there's any move that wouldn't just restart the loop. (Also I keep writing weberian with a lowercase w — don't hold it against me.)
