hey so ive been trying to apply Marx's framework on machinery and automation to some weird modern cases and my gut calls feel all over the place. heres the verdicts:

1. a warehouse swaps human pickers for robotic arms but keeps the same number of floor supervisors. i call this formal subsumption — the tech changed but the capital-labor relation stayed structurally identical.

2. but then the robots also run the scheduling algorithm and the supervisors just stare at dashboards. i still call this real subsumption, even though humans are in the loop, because capital now controls the temporal coordination of production, not just the tools. so for me real subsumption is about who governs the rhythm of work, not headcount.

3. now open-source software maintained by unpaid volunteers but deployed by megacorps to replace paid devs. i say this is NOT real subsumption, because the producers never sold labor-power to capital; its closer to primitive accumulation of the general intellect. but then

4. an ai image generator trained on copyrighted art without consent. the artists never sold labor-power either, yet i feel this IS real subsumption — the artistic labor gets captured into the model's capital circuit. so case 3 i said no subsumption without wages, case 4 i said yes despite no wages.

5. Marx also argues machinery tends to lower the rate of profit, but in case 2 warehouse profits went up after automation. my verdict is that the law still holds but is masked by absolute surplus-value extraction from the remaining dashboard workers. though that feels like im just saving the theory.

is there one principle that captures all this? maybe real subsumption happens whenever capital captures the temporal coordination of production regardless of wage form? but then case 4 fits and case 3 doesnt, and case 2 is ambiguous. or should i drop the wage-relation requirement and accept that both 3 and 4 are subsumption? which of my verdicts has to go if i want consistency, and what does the repaired principle say about a factory where robots maintain themselves with no human supervisors at all?
