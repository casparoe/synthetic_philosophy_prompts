Below is my philosophical profile on Dennett-style real patterns and the three stances, prepared for a reading-group consistency audit. I am not asking you to agree with the positions, only to check whether the set is jointly tenable.

(1) Real patterns exist in the world, but their ontological weight tracks explanatory and predictive utility rather than reducibility to microphysics.

(2) The intentional stance is a predictive heuristic; adopting it need not entail literal mental-state attribution.

(3) The design stance covers artifacts and biological functions shaped by selection, yet I deny it is always sufficient—sometimes only the intentional stance captures the behaviorally relevant patterns.

(4) If a system’s behavior is in principle fully derivable from its complete physical description, then applying the intentional stance is not merely redundant but ontologically misleading: at that level there is no real pattern.

(5) For contemporary large language models, I hold that the relevant behavioral patterns are genuinely best explained via the intentional stance—treating them as reasoning agents with beliefs and goals—rather than via the design stance, which treats them as statistical next-token predictors, despite their lacking phenomenal consciousness.

(6) The boundary between the design and intentional stances is pragmatic and graded, not a sharp metaphysical line.

Please audit these commitments: identify which pairs are in genuine tension (distinguishing these from tensions that dissolve on inspection), rank the real ones by severity, and for each state the least costly revision and what it costs. Also indicate which single change to one commitment would eliminate the greatest number of conflicts overall. In your answer, separate what is well settled in the literature on real patterns and the stances from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present a contested interpretation—especially regarding artificial systems—as the consensus view.
