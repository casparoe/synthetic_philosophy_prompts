I am a doctoral candidate in comparative literature with a concentration in medieval poetics and translation theory, and I serve as the graduate student representative on the editorial advisory board of our university press's Constrained Forms series. At next Thursday's meeting we must vote on whether to approve publication of a proposed bilingual edition of an anonymous fourteenth-century Occitan lyric sequence. The translator, who describes her method as morphemic calquing under syllabic lock, has imposed an extreme formal constraint: every English line must reproduce the original's syllable count, rhyme gender, and stanza architecture, and must preserve the initial consonant cluster of each content word in its original position, but all other morphological and syntactic elements may be reconstructed freely to yield grammatically correct modern English. The result reads as coherent verse, yet its narrative and emotional arc diverge substantially from the source text; the translator defends this by arguing that the poem's true identity resides in its phonetic skeleton, and that literal semantic fidelity is impossible without destroying that skeleton. She explicitly situates her approach within the tradition of formal preservation associated with Le Ton beau de Marot, though she extends that logic to permit systematic semantic reconstruction. Our board's policy requires us to determine whether a translation that alters meaning while preserving every audible and structural feature qualifies as an edition of the original work or as a new work wearing formal disguise. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action—approve, approve with conditions, or reject—within a specific time frame, and it must engage seriously with the translator's strongest argument rather than simply restating a conventional definition of fidelity. Write your response as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a formal letter or email.
