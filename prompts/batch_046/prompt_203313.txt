(2) So the identity of indiscernibles fails: two things can share all intrinsic properties and still not be identical.

(3) Now consider two electrons in a singlet state that are genuinely entangled with no definite individual trajectories or spatio-temporal paths; they share every intrinsic quantum number and there is no relational property (like 'being to the left of') that distinguishes one from the other, even in principle.

(5) But physics treats them as two distinct particals with a symmetric wavefunction, not as one particle.

(6) Therefore, either the identity of indiscernibles is actually true after all (and Black's spheres are one sphere observed twice), or our way of counting particles in quantum mechanics is fundamentally wrong, or the move that saved us in the macroscopic case doesn't carry over.
