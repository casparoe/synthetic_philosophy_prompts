hey, so i'm writing this sci-fi novel where the cosmos is literally a formal system (don't ask) and my mathematican friend threw Skolem's paradox at me. he says it proves "uncountable" is just model-relative noise. here's the argument he laid out:

(1) ZFC proves there exist uncountably many real numbers.
(2) By Löwenheim-Skolem, if ZFC is consistent it has a countable model M.
(3) Inside M there's a set R that M identifies as "the real numbers," and M proves R is uncountable.
(4) But outside M, R is just one more countable collection of objects, since M itself is countable.
(5) So there's no contradiction: R is uncountable-in-M and countable-outside-M.
(6) Apply this to every cardinal. ZFC proves there's an endless hierarchy of larger infinities, yet in some countable model every single one is just a countable set with a fancy internal name.
(7) Therefore "uncountable" can't mean "absolutely too large to enumerate"; it only means "not enumerable within this particular model."
(8) Which means any talk of absolute infinite size - continuous physical space, an infinitely powerful deity, whatever - is either false or empty.

i feel like the move from (5) to (7) smuggles something in, but i can't pinpoint it. is the error that "uncountable-in-M" is still a real structural property, not just a name? or is there an equivocation between internal and external quantification? tell me which premise to reject and what keeping the rest costs me for my world-building.
