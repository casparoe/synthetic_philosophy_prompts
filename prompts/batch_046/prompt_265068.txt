I have been working through Pascal's reflections on justice and force, particularly in the Pensées, and I have come to hold a specific interpretive position: that his well-known observation that justice without force is powerless and force without justice is tyrannical is not merely a sociological diagnosis but contains a hidden normative thesis. Specifically, I take him to hold that legitimate political authority requires what might be called a veil of custom—a collective, partly unconscious agreement to treat the contingent origins of power in force as if they were grounded in reason, with the stability of the order depending precisely on the fact that this transformation is never fully thematized by those who live under it. On this reading, custom (la coutume) functions not just as a descriptive substitute for absent justice but as an active normative mediator that converts coercive strength into binding obligation, and the health of a commonwealth is measured by how successfully this mediation remains opaque.

What would count against this interpretation? I am not asking for the standard objection that Pascal is a cynic about justice, but rather for the evidence—whether textual, historical, or philosophical—that should genuinely move me away from the view that Pascal assigns a positive, stabilizing normative role to this kind of collective self-misrecognition. Which passages in the Pensées, if read differently, would be fatal to this claim? Which alternative interpretations of custom's function—say, as purely pragmatic accommodation or as a temporary evil to be endured—would be more textually faithful, and what would a committed defender of my reading be forced to concede about them? Furthermore, are there structural problems with attributing such a normative theory to Pascal, given his broader theological commitments about human corruption and the need for grace? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, with an honest assessment of whether my reading risks being insulated from falsification by interpretive flexibility, and whether that insulation is itself a weakness for a view of this kind.
