I run a philosophy workshop for grad studnts, about 12 people, 80 mins. I want them to feel escalation of commitment in their bones, not just hear about the classic dollar auction. Design an in-class game using only paper and a white board: rules, round structure, and a point-payoff scheme with no real money. The thing being bid on should be interpretive authority over a vague policy concept — once you claim a framing in round one, you're stuck defending it, and switching frames costs double your sunk points plus a public exit fee that drags your final score down.

Set up the rules so they walk into the trap themselves: early bids feel cheap, quitting gets more expensive the longer you stay, and the only way to avoid backtracking is to defend increasingly weird extensions of your initial framing. Include a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto sunk-cost dynamics and escalation, plus an honest note on what the activity can't reproduce — probably the emotional identity component — and how I'd supply that verbally after. Also tell me what will predictibly go wrong the first run, like them spotting the trap and boycotting or one loud person dominating, and how to handle it. Keep it runnable without any tech.
