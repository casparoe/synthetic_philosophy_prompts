The guest is a philosopher who defends a specific variant of indirect normativity: she holds that an agent's obligations are determined not by what a fully rational, fully informed counterpart would choose, but by what a counterpart preserving the agent's actual cognitive architecture—what she calls her "structured epistemic constraints"—would endorse after reaching reflective equilibrium within those constraints. She argues that standard ideal-observer theories fail because they correct away the agent's limitations rather than treating them as normative parameters, thereby replacing the agent with an alien deliberator rather than guiding her.

I need eight to ten interview questions that engage the actual mechanics of this position rather than asking her to restate it. For each of these four, provide both the primary question and a follow-up tailored to the answer she is most likely to offer—particularly her likely appeal to the distinction between "architectural" and "contingent" ignorance. The questions should be precise, unsparing, and suitable for a colloquium-level audience; avoid any generic biographical or career-oriented inquiries.
