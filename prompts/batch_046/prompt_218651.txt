I serve on the technical certification board for a national regulator that evaluates automated reasoning systems—currently in medical diagnostics and legal reasoning—before they may be deployed with reduced human oversight. In our recent deliberations, I have kept returning to the philosophical pairing of Darwin's inversion of teleological reasoning, in which adaptive functional design arises without an intelligent designer, and Turing's inversion of the intelligence question, in which behavioural competence is produced without any internal comprehension; together these inversions seem to map precisely onto the problem we face, namely how to distinguish genuine domain understanding from mere competence without comprehension in an artificial system.

I need a reusable audit procedure—ordered diagnostic questions, explicit criteria for what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, a clear stop-point that returns a definitive negative, and explicit flags where judgement must substitute for rule—that my board can apply repeatedly rather than constructing ad hoc opinions. The procedure should operationalise the Darwin-Turing framework by distinguishing three grades of system performance: pure statistical correlation, structurally ignorant functional competence, and integrated comprehension. It should also include a concise battery of stress-test conditions that an assessor can run within thirty minutes, and it should be honest about which distinctions survive simplification and which must be dropped for practical use.

Please apply the procedure to two submitted cases: first, an image-analysis model that achieves expert-level diagnosis of a rare retinal condition yet cannot answer counterfactual questions about pathogenesis; second, a language-model legal assistant that produces doctrinally coherent judgements but systematically collapses when the logical structure of precedent is inverted. The first case should produce a result my board will dislike—specifically, that high diagnostic accuracy does not by itself justify reduced oversight—while the second should expose a boundary where the procedure must refer the decision back to human values rather than reaching a factual verdict. Finally, indicate explicitly where the Darwin-Turing framework is silent, particularly whether its implicit benchmark of human cognition renders it circular when applied to artificial systems, and whether the presence of an uninterpretable internal architecture should count for or against the attribution of understanding.
