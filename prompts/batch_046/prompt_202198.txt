Write an essay on whether Kuhnian incommensurability is a real, robust phenomenon or mostly a myth philosophers have overinflated. I keep seeing people argue that since scientists in rival paradims can always translate each other's terms and share data, incommensurability must be weak or nonexistent — at best a temporary communication glitch. But then there's the taxonomic case in biology: pre-Darwinian natural kinds and post-Darwinian population-thinking don't just swap vocabulary, they seem to slice nature at different joints in a way that resist neutral reconstruction. Weigh both sides carefully, but don't end on a non-committal note — defend a verdict on whether there's a genuine form of incommensurability that survives rational translation, or whether Kuhn is really just describing a social/psychological block. And please say what would change your mind: which kind of argument or historical example would make you flip your verdict, and where do you think your reasoning is shakiest?
