Please explain the conceptual tension between naturalist and normativist theories of health and disease when applied to evolutionary-mismatch conditions — for instance, metabolic dysfunctions arising when a physiology selected for energy scarcity operates in an environment of caloric abundance. Focus specifically on whether a biostatistical naturalist can classify such conditions as diseases without implicitly importing a normative judgment about the "proper" environment for that physiology, and whether the normativist, who defines disease relative to valued human capacities, can avoid making the classification depend on contingent social values about what bodies ought to achieve. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among specialists in philosophy of medicine, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
