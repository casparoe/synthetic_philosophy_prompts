After a long argument with a friend about whether the EU's democratic deficit is real or a conceptual mistake, I want a formal adversarial-collaboration document drafted between two camps: an integrationist who holds that sovereignty can be genuinely shared across levels without being destroyed, that democratic legitimacy may rest on plural demoi rather than a unitary European people, and that closer union is an open-ended procedural commitment rather than a constitutional endpoint; and a statist critic who holds that sovereignty remains conceptually unitary, that pooling it creates structural accountability gaps, and that indirect representation cannot remedy the deficit. The document should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides accept about EU institutional design, the meaning of sovereignty in international law versus political theory, and the precise conditions that make a democratic-deficit claim coherent rather than rhetorical; (2) the exact disagreements in wording each side would accept for itself, especially on whether divided sovereignty is still sovereignty, whether multi-level representation creates genuine accountability or only diffuses blame, and whether closer union implies an irreversible teleological direction; (3) the cruxes — I suspect the first is whether the European Parliament's powers and national parliamentary oversight together constitute a novel form of democratic control or merely compound opacity, and the second is whether the absence of a European demos is a contingent failure or a conceptual impossibility that undermines legitimacy; (4) for each crux, a concrete treaty mechanism or institutional episode both sides agree is relevant, with each side's predicted interpretation recorded in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
