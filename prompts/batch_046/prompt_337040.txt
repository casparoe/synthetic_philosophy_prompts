In GEB and a lot of pop-philosophy writing people lean on this analogy: a formal system like Principia Mathematica is like a mind, Gödel numbering is like the brain encoding thoughts about itself, and the Gödel sentence expressing its own unprovability is like self-awareness or consciousness. Hofstadter uses it to argue that strange loops — self-reference — generate the I. I've seen this extended to claim that any system able to encode statements about its own outputs is therefore approaching phenomenal consciousness, and that AI alignment is hard because we can't fully specify a system's self-model.

Can you audit how far the analogy actually holds? Give me a correspondance table — formal system, Gödel number, derivability, strange loop — mapped onto mind, neural encoding, belief or experience, self-awareness. Then the breaks: Gödel numbering is arbitary and extensional, mental content seems intensional and grounded; proof is syntactic, belief has semantic conditions; and the formal system doesn't know the sentence is true, it just derives it. Does this smuggle in the idea that minds are essentially syntactic engines?

If we correct those disanalogies, does the conclusion — that self-reference explains consciousness or that AI self-modelling is philosophically mysterious — survive?
