I'm a trustee of a forty-household cooperative housing association. We're drafting a covenant that prohibits visible displays—religious, political, or philosophical—that conflict with our charter's commitment to "inclusive neutrality." Enforcement is not punitive: it proceeds through mandatory mediation and, if unresolved, a structured buyout offer. The aim is to protect residents from feeling implicitly judged in shared spaces, not to suppress any particular doctrine. A member argues that this still uses collective institutional power to enforce a specific moral vision of good neighborhood life, despite causing no physical harm or nuisance; I see it as preserving an environment people voluntarily entered. Is there a coherent philosophical distinction between preserving a shared moral environment and imposing one, or does the voluntary-contract framing simply disguise a form of moral coercion?
