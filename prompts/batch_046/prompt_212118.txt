Write a short story of roughly 600–800 words set in a near-future polity that has achieved the material abundance and sharply reduced working hours Keynes envisioned, yet has solved none of the moral or social problems he believed abundance might cure. In this world, aesthetic contemplation and non-instrumental friendship have become scarce positional goods: citizens undergo periodic simplicity audits that rank the authenticity of their leisure, turning Keynes's hoped-for good life into a new arena for invidious comparison. Your protagonist should face one concrete ritual—perhaps an audit interview or a public simplicity ranking—where she must choose between genuine enjoyment and strategic display. Take a committed stance in the resolution: the story should argue that Keynes underestimated the adaptability of status competition, not that he misunderstood the satiability of material need. After the story, include a brief author's note identifying where you take liberties with Keynes's framework—especially his distinction between needs satisfied regardless of others and needs driven by comparison—and stating whether your use of that distinction relies on a contested interpretation.
