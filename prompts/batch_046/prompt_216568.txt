I have been discussing Samuel Scheffler’s concerns about human extinction with a colleague who recently read an essay summarizing what the author calls the “afterlife conjecture.” According to my colleague’s account, Scheffler holds that the meaning of individual human pursuits depends almost entirely on a mechanism he terms “trans-temporal value transmission”: the expectation that our efforts—whether in science, art, or even intimate relationships—will be taken up, continued, or appreciated by future generations. The essay claims that if humanity were to vanish shortly after our own deaths, this transmission chain would be broken, and Scheffler believes the consequence is not merely sadness or disappointment but a structural collapse in the value of our present activities; without a future audience or continuation, our current projects become “essentially wasted motion,” and even personal bonds lose their point because they lack a legacy-bearing horizon. From this, the essay draws two conclusions: first, that extinction is worse than the aggregate of individual deaths because it retroactively annuls the value of lives already lived; and second, that Scheffler’s view commits us to direct, present-tense moral obligations toward specific distant future individuals, since harming them is structurally identical to destroying the very conditions that make our own lives meaningful now.

I am inclined to think this summary imports more metaphysical machinery than Scheffler actually uses, but I am not confident enough to say where the distortion lies. Could you audit the characterization claim by claim: which parts fairly represent Scheffler’s argument about the conditions for present meaning and the “doomsday scenario,” which parts are exaggerations or conflations—particularly the framing of “trans-temporal value transmission,” the claim that intimate relationships depend on future audiences, and the idea of retrospective value-annihilation—and whether the essay’s two conclusions actually follow once the view is corrected? Please separate what a careful defender of Scheffler’s position would accept, what they would reject as misrepresentation, and whether any accurate core of the argument still supports the stronger moral and metaphysical claims the essay derives from it.
