Write a dialogue between two philosophers of medicine -- one a naturalist about health (statistical function, reference classes, biological norms) and one a normativist (health involves well-being, values, social context). Don't strawman either side; both should be sharp and actually listen. Center it on this case: a patient gets an elective synthetic microbiome replacement that drops her gut bacterial diversity far below the human statistical mean, but it wipes out her chronic autoimmune symptoms and she reports way higher quality of life. The naturalist should argue this is still a functional deviation or at least ambiguous health, since her immune calibration now sits outside the species-typical range and "health" needs some objective biological anchor. The normativist should push back that tying health to population curves just makes medicine conservative, and that if she's flourishing according to her own goals then she's healthier, full stop. Make sure there's real back-and-forth: the naturalist must address the flourishing objection directly, and the normativist must deal with the worry that without statistical norms "health" collapses into personal preference. Keep the tone like two grad studnets arguing over cheap coffee, and make sure they respond to each others actual points rather than talking past eachother.
