In my independent study in philosophy I have been working through Rorty’s antirepresentationalism, particularly his claim that knowledge is not a matter of mirroring nature but of finding useful ways to cope with our environment. I have also encountered his distinction between normal discourse, which operates within shared criteria of justification, and abnormal discourse, which seeks to replace those criteria altogether. Here is the difficulty I cannot resolve: if justification is always internal to a vocabulary and there is no representational standpoint outside every vocabulary from which to adjudicate them, then abnormal discourse—such as the historical shift from a creationist vocabulary to an evolutionary one—seems to lack any rational basis whatsoever. It would appear to be merely a rhetorical or cultural substitution, with no greater claim to progress than the reverse shift. Consequently, I worry that Rorty’s neopragmatism cannot coherently distinguish between a genuinely progressive redescription and an arbitrary or regressive one, and that it therefore collapses into a flat relativism about scientific change. Could you identify the precise error in my reasoning and explain how Rorty’s framework allows us to prefer one vocabulary over another without smuggling representational accuracy back in?
