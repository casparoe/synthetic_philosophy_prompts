Write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines followed by a three-line envoi, with the end-words rotating in the standard sestina pattern—about the structural isomorphism between DNA self-replication and Gödelian self-reference (the loop architecture in which a system contains the instructions for its own decoding, sometimes discussed as typogenetics), but with the twist that the analogy fractures precisely where mutation introduces noise: biological copying absorbs error to generate functional variation, whereas in formal proof the same noise collapses into contradiction or undecidability. Use these six end-words—strand, number, read, error, proof, copy—so that their mechanical rotation enacts the replication cycle, and let the envoi force all six into proximity to ask whether the mismatch is a contingent feature of biology or a hard limit of the isomorphism itself. The poem should be accurate enough that someone familiar with Gödel numbering and transcription errors recognizes the parallel, yet should treat the breakdown of perfect replication as structurally central rather than ornamental. Begin with the poem itself, with no preface or commentary.
