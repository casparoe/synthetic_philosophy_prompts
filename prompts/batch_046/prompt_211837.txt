My opening move: I press a self-reference regress. If syādvāda requires every assertion A to carry the qualifier syāt ("in some respect"), then apply it to syādvāda itself: it holds only syāt. The qualifier then demands its own qualifier, generating an infinite regress with no stable ground. You might invoke the naya/durnīti distinction, claiming that a meta-standpoint evaluates the self-application as valid rather than distorted. I challenge that: is this meta-standpoint not itself just another naya? If so, your distinction between valid and faulty standpoints is itself standpoint-relative, and you have relocated the regress rather than blocked it.
