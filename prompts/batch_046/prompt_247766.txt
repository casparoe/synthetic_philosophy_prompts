I'm currently enrolled in an upper-level course on early modern epistemology and philosophy of mind, and we've been working through Descartes's Meditations with particular attention to the Second Meditation's reasoning that leads to ego sum, ego existo. For an assignment that asks us to reconstruct a live philosophical disagreement rather than summarize a settled one, I would like you to draft the joint statement that two opposing interpretive camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on what exactly the cogito proves.

The two camps are:

Camp A (the "thin occurrence" reading): The cogito establishes only that there is a present occurrence of thinking, with no entitlement to a persisting substantial self, no determination of essence beyond the present act, and no bridge across temporal gaps.

Camp B (the "substantial res" reading): The cogito establishes that I am a thing whose essence is to think, that this thing persists as long as it thinks, and that the "I" in successive cogitations refers to one and the same substance.

Please produce the deliverable in the following form. First, a numbered section of agreed claims about the text of Meditations II, about what the hyperbolic-doubt considerations (the demon, the unreliability of the senses, the possible non-existence of the body) actually rule out and what they leave untouched, and about the formal structure of the inference from the presence of thought to some form of existence. Second, a disagreements table that states, for each remaining point of divergence: (a) the precise claim Camp A holds and Camp B denies, phrased in wording that Camp A would accept; (b) the precise claim Camp B holds and Camp A denies, phrased in wording that Camp B would accept; (c) the crux — the sub-question whose resolution would move at least one camp; and (d) for each crux, the specific argument, textual consideration, or conceptual test both camps agree bears on it, with each camp's predicted verdict recorded in advance.

In addition to the standard substance-versus-occurrence disagreement, please inject and make precise one reconstructed but non-obvious point of divergence: whether the first-person present tense in the cogito carries an implicit commitment to diachronic identity conditions, and whether Descartes is entitled to move from a punctual "I think now" to "I am a thinking thing" without importing a premise about substance or persistence that is itself not secured by the cogito alone. The goal is to separate genuine disagreement from verbal disagreement, and to identify whether any part of the dispute collapses once this reconstructed crux is made explicit.

One request about how to answer: if my framing rests on a confused premise — for instance, if these two camps actually share all relevant premises and differ only in rhetorical emphasis, or if the adversarial-collaboration format cannot be executed well for this particular disagreement without manufacturing disagreement — please say so plainly rather than producing a document that papers over the problem. I want the residual disagreement made exact, not inflated for the sake of the exercise.
