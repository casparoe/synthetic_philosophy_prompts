I was skimming a summary of Davidson's radical interpretation on some forum and I'm not sure how much to trust it. The poster said Davidson thinks that if you ran into a speaker with a totally unknown language, you'd have to reconstruct their beliefs and word-meanings together since they're holistically tied, and you do this by applying the principle of charity — treating them as mostly rational and mostly right about the world. They said Davidson takes charity to be a constitutive condition: you can't actually have beliefs unless there's an interpreter applying charity to you, which is why he rejects incommensurable conceptual schemes and why he thinks thought requires language (via triangulation between speaker, interpreter, and world). The poster also claimed this is basically Quine's radical translation with an ethical gloss, and that it follows that miscommunication is impossible because charity always resolves ambiguity in the speaker's favor.

Especialy that last part about miscommunication feels wrong, and the Quine comparison seems too quick, but I don't know Davidson well enough to pinpoint the mistakes. Is this summary fair? Which parts are accurate, which are standard simplifications, and which are misrepresentations? And does the conclusion about miscommunication actually follow from the charity principle?
