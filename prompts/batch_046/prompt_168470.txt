I am taking a graduate seminar on the philosophy of biology and artificial intelligence, and over the past weeks we have been working through my professor's framework for what she describes as the strange inversions of reasoning—Darwin's inversion, in which blind selection produces functional design without any grasp of the end, and Turing's inversion, in which formal symbol manipulation produces behavior that mimics reasoning without understanding its content. She has given clear verdicts on several boundary cases. She judges that natural selection shaping the vertebrate eye is a genuine instance, since adaptive complexity arises with no designer's comprehension of optics. She judges that a Turing-complete automaton generating mathematical proofs through purely syntactic rules is also a genuine instance, because the system achieves proof-level success without semantic access to mathematics. She judges that a human savant performing rapid mental arithmetic is not an instance, on the grounds that the competence still resides in a comprehending agent even when conceptual articulation is weak. She judges that an ordinary household thermostat maintaining temperature is not an instance either, because the functional achievement is too minimal to count as competence in the philosophically interesting sense.

Next week we will examine a new case that I have prepared for discussion: a bio-digital hybrid system in which bacterial colonies are evolved through an artificial selection regime—guided solely by efficacy in regulating a synthetic neural interface—to achieve precise, coordinated motor control in a paralyzed patient; neither the bacterial genomes nor the statistical weights of the interface contain any representational model of motor function, anatomy, or causation, yet the integrated system reliably performs complex, adaptive movements in response to neural signals. Predict her verdict on whether this hybrid counts as a genuine case of competence without comprehension, and in doing so identify whether she is implicitly running a pure-type rule that counts only mechanisms that are either purely evolutionary or purely computational but not both layered together, or a functional-transparency rule that permits layered hybrid mechanisms so long as high-level functional success is achieved and the absence of representational content is fully transparent, or perhaps some other principle altogether. Also tell me which further case—maybe a purely evolutionary chemical pathway so genetically opaque that biologists cannot trace its mechanism—would distinguish these rules, and please present everything in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, writing as if in a letter or email, and please use American English.
