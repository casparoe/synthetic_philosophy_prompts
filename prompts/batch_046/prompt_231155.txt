I'm putting together a feature on why the introspection wars never seem to shift — psychologists claim its unreliable, philosophers claim its authoritative, and nobody moves. Lately Ive been toying with a slightly odd framing: what if an introspective report isnt just a measurement of mental behaviour but a speech act that feeds back into the brain's predictive model, altering the very state it describes? That splits reliability into at least three questions — causal interference, representational accuracy, and social coordination — and I suspect the stall is just people answering different ones without realising. Is this genuinely substantive, or mostly verbal? And if predictive processing really treats self-report as part of the inference, is there a hidden empirical question both camps are ignoring?
