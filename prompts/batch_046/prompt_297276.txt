I keep running into this Kant take that animals don't count morally except indirectly—like, being mean to a dog is bad only because it trains you to be mean to people. But then he also says you shouldn't be cruel to animals even in private where no one's watching your charcter development. So isn't he actually relying on the animal's own suffering being bad, just refusing to call it a direct duty? Or is there some other mechanism—maybe like, damaging your own rational nature by treating sentience as worthless? I feel like people either say he's a speciest or that he's secretly a consequentialist about animal pain, but both readings seem to miss something about how he thinks moral feeling works.
