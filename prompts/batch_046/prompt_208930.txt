For a philosophy of science workshop, lay out the desiderata that an account of scale-relative determinism must satisfy to count as adequate. Define the target view as one that treats determinism not as a global property of a physical system, but as holding relative to a specified observational scale and a tolerated error margin in initial conditions. Your specification should cover: (i) how deterministic chaos—exemplified by bounded, non-periodic dynamics such as Lorenz-type strange attractors—can be genuinely deterministic at the micro-scale yet yield macro-scale trajectories that are structurally stable but individually unpredictable; (ii) why the practical versus in-principle unpredictability distinction should not collapse once scale-relativity is admitted; (iii) how the account avoids both Laplacian global predictability and quantum-mechanical indeterminacy, retaining law-governed micro-dynamics without entailing long-range forecastability; and (iv) what conditions must hold for a system to "count as deterministic" when its state space is confined but never repeats, and whether this requires a privileged decomposition into deterministic subsystems or can be sustained holistically. For each desideratum, provide the motivating conceptual case or theoretical pressure, mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference of particular camps, and identify whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable given standard results in dynamical systems theory—particularly sensitive dependence on initial conditions and topological mixing. Weight your answer toward the criteria and their justifications rather than toward scoring existing interpretations; the aim is to determine whether scale-relative determinism is a coherent middle position or collapses into either hidden-variables determinism or epistemic humility about chaos.
