I cover science and ideas for a magazine and yesterday I overheard two friends — a historian of social norms and a moral philosopher — arguing in teh cafe about why practices like dueling and slavery actually stop being acceptable. They kept circling back to Bernard Williams on honor and Kwame Anthony Appiah on moral revolutions, but then they went in a direction I hadn't heard alot about. Can you adjudicate?

Move 1: The historian said moral revolutions are basically interest shifts wearing fancy clothes. Once modern states needed a monopoly on violence and labor markets needed mobile workers, dueling and slavery became inefficient. Honor talk is just elite branding for material advantage.

Move 2: The philosopher pushed back with Appiah's point about dueling — legal bans existed for decades but were ignored until elite honor codes turned internally. People started seeing dueling as backward, not just illegal. So normative change came first and made the legal change stick.

Move 3: The historian said that's just correlation. Elites dropped dueling because aristocratic honor lost economic function in bureaucratic capitalism; calling it backward was just status competition updating its signals. You can't separate honor from interest because honor is social currency.

Move 4: The philosopher then threw in a twist she called category drift — some practices die not through argument or interest, but by being reclassified into the wrong ontological domain. Dueling stopped being treated as a serious moral institution and started being seen as theatrical costume drama, like LARPing. Once it's in the wrong box, neither defense nor economic incentive can revive it. She said this explains sudden vanishings that neither interest nor argument captures.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify. Also say what both miss, especially whether category drift is actually distinct from status updating or just a fancy redescription. At the end, put one line starting with Verdict: that lists your ratings for moves 1 through 4 (like Verdict: 1-weak, 2-strong, 3-partial, 4-interesting-but-unclear) and says who is closer to right overall.
