I'm directing a small municipal logistics cooperative that relies on four very different problem-solvers for lost-item recovery and route planning. The first is a scent-trained bloodhound that locates misplaced goods through pure olfactory instinct, incapable of learning from abstract feedback. The second is an autonomous delivery drone that optimizes its paths solely through cycles of reward and penalty, operating without any internal map of the city. The third is a retired civil engineer who will not adjust a single piece of equipment until she has mentally simulated multiple failure scenarios and chosen the safest hypothetical course. The fourth is a veteran archivist who rejects any proposal that has not been rendered into shared written protocols and ratified by the collective, treating individual intuition as inherently unreliable. Our oversight committee now demands a single evaluation framework—either pure outcome-based compensation for all, or pure deliberative peer-review for all—applied uniformly across these four contributors. I sense that forcing one standard ignores something fundamental about what each contributor actually is, yet treating them differently risks looking like arbitrary favoritism rather than reasoned principle. How should I determine the proper domain of responsibility and the correct standard of success for each?
