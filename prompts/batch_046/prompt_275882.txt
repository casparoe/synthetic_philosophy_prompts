Hey, I'm prepping a grad seminar on social explanation and got stuck in this back-and-forth with two colleauges over coffee — well, they argued and I just listened with a bad latte. Could you adjudicate it for me?

Colleague A said Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis is basically correlation with fancy vocabulary. She said the "elective affinity" between Calvinist salvation-anxiety and capitalist accumulation isn't real causation; culture is just the form people use to rationalise what they'd already do for material gain. The causal work, she said, is done by market incentives and institutional shifts. She claimed if you hold the economic structure constant but swap in Catholic or Buddhist values, you'd still see capital accumulation wherever institutions allow it, so culture has no independent causal weight.

Colleague B pushed back. He said Weber explicitly describes the ethic "releasing" capitalism from traditionalist constraints — that's causal language, not decoration. The anxiety over predestination generated an ascetic labor discipline that reshaped economic behavior in ways pure profit-seeking wouldn't have produced. He brought up the comparative point: many cultures had market incentives, but only the Protestant West developed rational capitalism, so you can't explain the specific form without the religious value-system. B said Weber never meant billiard-ball causation; he meant adequate causation at the level of meaning. The value-structure made the transformation both intelligible and sustainable, and that's a genuine causal efficacy even if it isn't mechanical.

Then A came back: "Released" is metaphor. You're describing post-hoc rationalization. People do what material conditions push them toward, then dress it in religious terms. The comparative argument fails because institutional differences are confounded with cultural ones — there's no natural experiment isolating culture.

B replied that Weber knew culture and institutions are intertwined, which is why he used "elective affinity" rather than simple cause-effect. But B insisted the meaning-structure did real explanatory work: without the specific anxiety about salvation, you don't get the systematic, methodical discipline that distinguishes rational capitalism from mere greed. The values didn't push like physics, but they configured what was economically possible and durable.

A's final move was that B had changed the subject. If culture only explains intelligibility or sustainability, not emergence, then it's not a cause of capitalism at all — it's commentary. She said Weber should be read as showing how culture accompanies economic change, not drives it.

They turned to me and I honestly couldn't call it. Please go through their exchange round by round and say who's right about each specific point — not just who wins overall. I want to know where each one is strong and where they're missing something, especially whether B's appeal to adequate causation actually saves Weber from being a correlation story, or whether A is right that this collapses culture into commentary. Take a definite stance, give your reasons, and don't just lay out options and leave me hanging. I'm particularly interested in whether "meaning-adequate" explanation can do the work B needs it to do without smuggling in mechanistic causation.
