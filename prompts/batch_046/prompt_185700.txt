I'm sketching an essay that reads Wittgenstein's hinge propositions through the metaphor of architectural scaffolding rather than bedrock foundations or grammatical rules. The core claim is that hinges do not support a world-picture by providing an unmoving base, but by functioning like temporary structural supports—necessary and determinative during construction, not part of the finished edifice, and replaceable without collapse because the structure eventually bears its own weight. I extend this to scientific change: commitments such as the very great age of the earth act as scaffolding that makes specific empirical inquiry possible during normal science, and during a paradigm shift they are dismantled and replaced rather than directly refuted, which explains both their felt indispensability and their apparent immunity to counter-evidence. I then address an objection: if hinges are scaffolding, they ought to disappear once the world-picture is complete, yet Wittgenstein suggests they remain active. My reply draws on internalized formwork—scaffolding that leaves traces embedded in the concrete—so that hinges become incorporated into the practices they originally enabled rather than vanishing. I'd like comments on whether this reading genuinely differs from the foundation and grammar analogies, whether it handles the riverbed image in On Certainty without distortion, and whether importing Kuhnian dynamics risks making hinges either too empirical or too easily replaceable.
