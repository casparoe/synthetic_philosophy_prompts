In my phil of sci seminar students keep reaching for the bouncer-at-the-club analogy to explain falsificationism: a theory only counts as scientific if it carries an entry condition it can fail, like being on a VIP list you can get kicked off of. They lean on it to dismiss psychoanalysis as "unfalsifiable, so it's not in the club." I want you to audit how far this actually holds. Give me the correspondance table — what plays the bouncer, the VIP list, the fake ID, the club owner? Then the breaks: is falsification really binary like being on a list, does the analogy smuggle in verificationist baggage Popper hated, and what happens to the psychoanalysis verdict once the disanalogies are on the table? Don't lay out options and sit on the fence — take a definite stand on whether the bouncer picture does real demarcation work or whether it's carrying the whole conclusion.
