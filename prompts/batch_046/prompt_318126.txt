Construct a thought experiment that isolates hedonism's heterogeneity problem. Engineer one agent experiencing two pleasures that share no qualitative feel, neural substrate, or intentional object: (1) a simple bodily warmth with no cognitive content, and (2) the satisfaction of resolving a deep conceptual contradiction, where the immediate mental texture is aversive or neutral but the overall state is valued as pleasant. Specify the single structural feature—other than subjective liking or desire-fulfillment—that a hedonist could claim unifies both. Then demonstrate: (i) why each pleasure counts as genuinely pleasant despite the dissimilarities, (ii) why that structural feature is not merely a post-hoc stipulation, (iii) what verdict a desire-satisfaction theorist gives about case (2) and why it differs, and (iv) how removing that feature collapses the scenario into either a homogeneous-feeling account or a non-hedonic theory. Keep the setting realistic, avoid exotic neuroscience or named citations, and do not include bibliographies.
