You just fix the system. They call it a "blameless postmortem" I think. But if it turns out someone broke it on purpose—like they pushed bad code knowing it would crash things—then everyone gets furious, they call for them to be fired or banned, and nobody cares about fixing the system first. The anger is personal.

Then there's this other layer. Some companies now have AI chatbots that send apology messages to users when there's an outage. People hate it. They say it's creepy or hollow, like the bot isn't actually feeling anything so the apology doesn't count. But when a real human manager sends the exact same words, people usually recieve it and calm down.

Meanwhile I heard that in some countries doctors are legally protected if they apologize for a medical mistake, but only if they don't admit they were negligent? That seems backwards—if you're not admitting fault, what are you sorry for?
