Question: Explain why cooperation collapses in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma but can persist in the iterated version. Then evaluate whether the "shadow of the future" by itself is enough to stabilize cooperation when players match randomly in a large population and occasionally make errors.

Student answer:
In one-shot PD defection dominates since T>R and P>S regardless of opponent's move, so both defect and get (P,P), which is Pareto-inferior to (R,R).

With repetition the shadow of the future changes incentives. If w > c/b then tit-for-tat works because future cooperation rewards outweigh the one-time temptation T. Axelrod ran computer tournaments and showed tit-for-tat wins, and he formally proved its evolutionary stability even with noise.

But random matching destroys direct reciprocity since you rarely see the same partner twice. In that setting the shadow alone fails. Indirect reciprocity via reputation can still work if theres a public score, so you need either pairwise repetition or reputation tracking.
