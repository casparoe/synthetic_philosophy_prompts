I am helping my philosophy instructor evaluate responses for an advanced independent-study exam on consciousness and conceptual analysis. The prompt asked students to articulate the incoherence challenge to qualia associated with Chase and Sanborn: specifically, that any attempt to characterize qualitative properties either renders them causally inert and therefore unknowable, or collapses them into structural, functional, or physical descriptions that leave no residual qualitative feel. Students were then presented with a hypothetical discovery—a neural qualium-meter that registers reports of a specific shade of azure exclusively through synchronized gamma oscillations in cortical area V4, without detecting any additional intrinsic feature—and asked whether this result vindicates the existence of qualia, undermines them, or leaves the dispute unchanged, with explicit reference to the incoherence dilemma.

The student answer reads as follows. The respondent correctly distinguishes the two horns of Chase and Sanborn's dilemma, noting that if qualia possess no causal profile they become epiphenomenal, yet if they do possess one they risk being reducible to that profile. The student also observes that a purely physical detection mechanism should not, in principle, be able to access a non-physical property, which shows a grasp of the epistemic constraint. However, the answer then misattributes the origin of the incoherence argument, treating it as a defense of epiphenomenalism developed by Chase and Sanborn rather than an attack on the coherence of the qualia concept, and it erroneously imports Dennett's strategy of quining qualia as if it were part of the same line of reasoning. In discussing the meter scenario, the student asserts that because the instrument captures only oscillatory dynamics, this proves qualia are identical to those dynamics and therefore physicalism is vindicated; the student never considers that the meter might simply be blind to an intrinsic property that remains present but causally isolated, nor does the student recognize that this move begs the question against the very possibility the dilemma was meant to expose. The response concludes that the discovery therefore completely undermines the qualia-defender, without acknowledging the neutral option.
