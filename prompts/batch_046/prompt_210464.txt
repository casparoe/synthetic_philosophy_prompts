I am a retired physicist who has recently taken up philosophy. I have sketched an argument that Rorty's antirepresentationalism, applied consistently, dissolves the distinction physicists draw between instrumental calibration and representational approximation: systematic error is corrected within a vocabulary of practice rather than removed to reveal an independent state of affairs, yet scientists still speak of measurements approaching true values. I ask whether this residual language is merely a pragmatic convenience or whether it betrays a hidden representationalist commitment that neopragmatism cannot accommodate. I also propose that Rorty ought to distinguish vocabulary revision prompted by predictive failure from instrument repair prompted by malfunction, but I am unsure whether that distinction can be maintained without reintroducing a mind-independent criterion for correct functioning. Would you comment on the coherence of this sketch? Specifically, I should like to know whether I misrepresent Rorty's view, whether calibration practice genuinely supports antirepresentationalism, and whether the repair-versus-revision distinction is sustainable within a purely pragmatic framework.
