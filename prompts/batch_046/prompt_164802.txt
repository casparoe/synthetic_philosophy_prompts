I have been working through Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling with an online reading group, and one participant offered a summary of Abraham's position that strikes me as mostly plausible yet suspiciously neat. He suggested that Abraham performs the teleological suspension of the ethical in the following way: having received a direct, unmediated divine command, Abraham is entitled to set aside the universal ethical prohibition on filicide because the religious telos takes unconditional precedence over the ethical universal. Unlike Agamemnon, who sacrifices Iphigenia for the welfare of the state and can therefore justify himself within the universal, Abraham is a knight of faith whose silence indicates not an absence of reasons but the presence of reasons that are incommensurably private—specifically, his certainty that God will preserve the promised lineage. The summary adds that Kierkegaard thereby demonstrates that ethical obligations are always conditional on a prior religious commitment, and that Abraham's refusal to explain himself anticipates what contemporary discourse might call a sacred-value override, in which rational deliberation is legitimately halted by a commitment that cannot be translated into public, cost-benefit terms.

I suspect this smooths over several distinctions—perhaps between faith and knowledge, or between the knight of faith and the knight of infinite resignation—but I cannot locate the errors with confidence. Would you audit this characterisation? Please distinguish what a careful reading of the text would endorse, what counts as a standard simplification, and what is genuinely misleading, particularly regarding whether Abraham possesses a calculable higher purpose, whether his silence is strategic or existential, and whether the teleological suspension should be understood as an exception, a paradox, or a rational override. I am asking this without access to secondary literature or search tools, so please do not provide bibliographies or citation lists; I only want to know which parts of my acquaintance's account I can safely rely on.
