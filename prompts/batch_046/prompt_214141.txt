If the sphex wasp's burrow-checking—repeating its inspection sequence even after the prey has been relocated—remains the canonical illustration of sphexishness, then the concept seems to pick out behavior that executes an invariant procedural script regardless of changed circumstances. Translating this into human cognition, one might distinguish model-free habits, which are cached and largely context-insensitive, from model-based control, which updates with outcomes and supports flexible planning. Yet if every apparently free, flexible action ultimately relies on model-based circuits merely suppressing or overriding an underlying stratum of model-free, sphexish routines, then is there any categorical philosophical difference between mechanical rigidity and genuine agency, or have we reduced the distinction to a mere matter of hierarchical control depth? In other words, if flexibility is mechanistically nothing more than the inhibition of a more basic rigidity, does that collapse sphexishness and free will into a single continuum of complexity rather than preserving them as genuinely distinct kinds?
