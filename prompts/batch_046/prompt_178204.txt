I'm working through my own judgments about when an agent "means" to bring about a foreseen side effect, and I'm noticing an asymmetry that holds across domains but breaks in one case I keep reversing. Here are my verdicts.

In business: an executive approves a factory closure knowing it will destroy a local economy; I say she meant the economic harm, even though her only goal was profit. But if she approves an expansion knowing it will accidentally revive that same local economy, I say she did not mean the revival, even though she foresaw and accepted it.

In scientific practice: a physicist revises her equations knowing the revision will eliminate an embarrassing outlier; I say she meant to eliminate the outlier. If she revises them knowing the same move will preserve an elegant symmetry she hadn't been seeking, I say she did not mean to preserve the symmetry, despite foreseeing it.

In education: an instructor simplifies a complex theory for her class knowing the simplification will cause students to form a misconception; I say she meant the misconception. If she simplifies knowing it will cause students to form a deeper intuition that wasn't her aim, I say she did not mean the intuition, though she saw it coming.

The pattern looks like harm-related side effects count as intended and benefit-related ones don't, but I'm unsure that's right, because in that same education case the instructor also knows her simplification will make the theory more memorable, and memorability seems like a clear pedagogical good. So did she mean the memorability? I feel pulled to say yes, because it's a benefit she actively welcomed, but then my principle has to treat some benefits like harms, which feels ad hoc.

Is there a coherent principle behind these verdicts—perhaps about normative salience, agentive focus, or the structure of practical reasoning—that captures the asymmetry without just listing domains? What does it say about my unsettled education case? And which of my verdicts, if any, should I drop if the principle is to stay simple?
