Having spent thirty-five years in experimental condensed-matter physics before turning, somewhat unexpectedly, to philosophy of science and philosophy of mind, I find myself returning repeatedly to the framework that distinguishes the physical stance, the design stance, and the intentional stance, and to the associated claim that real patterns exist at varying levels of abstraction. The account seems especially strained, however, when applied not to familiar organisms shaped by evolutionary history but to synthetic biological circuits and to autonomous adaptive systems that lack such history yet exhibit stable, predictive behavioral regularities. I am not asking for a survey of the original literature; I am trying to decide where philosophical effort should go. Suppose one had limited resources—say, a small research group with about three years—to address open conceptual questions in this area. Which two or three problems should take priority, and in what order? I want the reasoning defended with explicit criteria: which questions are upstream of others, which remain tractable without waiting for empirical breakthroughs in neuroscience or synthetic biology, and which carry the greatest weight for deciding whether a system genuinely supports intentional-level explanation rather than merely design-level functional description. I am particularly drawn to whether self-modifying adaptive architectures—systems that revise their own transition rules in response to environmental feedback—can be consistently captured by the design stance alone, or whether their behavioral stability forces an intentional attribution that risks collapsing back into physical-level dynamics. Should priority go first to refining stance-independent criteria for pattern reality, second to mapping the conditions under which design and intentional explanations genuinely diverge, or third to assessing whether the physical stance retains permanent explanatory priority in all synthetic cases? Rank them, defend the ordering, specify what kind of conceptual or formal development would cause you to reverse it, and do not provide a bibliography or reading list—my aim is reasoning I can evaluate without external references.
