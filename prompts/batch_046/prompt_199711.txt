I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of medicine writing on conflicts between advance directives and present welfare. In clinical ethics consultations I have observed a recurring argumentative move that I cannot place precisely among established concepts in bioethics or philosophy of personal identity, and I want to know whether it carries a specific name.

The situation is structurally consistent. A patient executes an advance directive refusing life-sustaining treatment upon reaching a specific cognitive threshold—say, being unable to recognize close family members. The patient reaches that threshold, appears subjectively content, and asks to continue treatment. Rather than arguing that present comfort should override prior preferences, or that the earlier self possessed superior judgment about future welfare, the family or surrogate contends that honoring the present request would fracture the narrative integrity of the patient's life. They treat the directive not as a predictive preference but as an authorial commitment that binds the arc of the life-story, so that allowing persistence in the rejected state wrongs the earlier author even when no present harm is experienced.

This is not standard precedent autonomy, which grounds the directive's authority in the prior self's welfare judgments. Nor is it the familiar narrative-identity question about which psychological stage counts as the same person, because the claim is not about survival conditions but about whether an earlier self holds a veto-right over the plot of her life that operates independently of current subjective experience.

Does this pattern—defending an advance directive on narrative-authority rather than preference-authority grounds—have an established concept? If not, please propose a precise term with a definition and clear boundary conditions, especially distinctions from (a) welfare-based precedent autonomy, (b) narrative-integrity arguments in other ethical domains, and (c) surrogates projecting their own aesthetic values onto the patient's biography.
