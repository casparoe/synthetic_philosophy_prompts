A philosophy professor summarized Hilary Putnam's mature position on religious belief like this: "Putnam's rejection of metaphysical realism means that religious language does not describe an independent reality; instead, it functions within a self-contained conceptual scheme that organizes moral experience. He applies his reasonableness criterion to faith: a religious belief is rational if it survives reflective equilibrium within its own scheme, but since schemes are causally and conceptually closed, there is no neutral standpoint from which to compare religious and scientific claims. Consequently, God-talk is neither true nor false in any transcendent sense; it is a useful fiction that acquires local truth only inside the scheme. This makes Putnam's view a form of fideism—faith is protected from all external criticism because cross-scheme evaluation is impossible, and religious commitment becomes an arbitrary choice among incommensurable worlds."
