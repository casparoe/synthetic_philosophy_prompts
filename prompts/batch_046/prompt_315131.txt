I've come around to the idea that dirty hands in politics isn't a real moral stain if democratic ratifcation eventually endorses the call — the mud gets washed off, not just outweighed. So a leader who lies or coerces isn't permanently tainted as long as the voters later say yes. What would count against this? I want the arguments and cases that should actually move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be — including maybe historical examples where popular endorsement followed something clearly wrong, or philosophical reasons retrospective consent can't cleanse betrayal or deception. Which of these would defenders of the view concede, and which would they dismiss? Does the position risk anything, or has it been qualified into unfalsifiability by claiming the ratifcation wasn't genuinely democratic? And what does it predict that standard dirty-hands theories don't?
