Please evaluate the following undergraduate exam answer. The exam question was: Consider a bronze statue that is gradually melted and recast into a different shape over the course of a year. Explain the difference between endurantist and perdurantist accounts of whether this is the same statue persisting through change. In particular, address (i) whether either view is ontologically committed to temporal parts, (ii) how each view handles apparently contradictory intrinsic properties (e.g., being cylindrical at t1 and being cubical at t2), and (iii) whether the reshaping case gives us reason to prefer one metaphysics of persistence over the other.

Student answer:

Endurantism holds that the statue is a three-dimensional object that exists wholly and completely at every moment it exists. It does not have temporal parts; instead, it endures through time by being fully present at t1 and fully present at t2. To avoid saying that the statue is both cylindrical and cubical simpliciter, the endurantist relativizes intrinsic properties to times: the statue is cylindrical-at-t1 and cubical-at-t2. This means the endurantist can keep the same object without contradiction, though in practice this treats the statue-at-t1 and statue-at-t2 as distinct entities, which is really just temporal-part talk in disguise.

Perdurantism, by contrast, denies that any object is wholly present at more than one time. The statue is a four-dimensional spacetime worm made up of instantaneous temporal parts—statue-stage-at-t1, statue-stage-at-t2, and so on. Each part bears its own shape property non-relativized, so there is no contradiction. The four-dimensional whole is the sum of these stages, related by continuity and causal connections. In fact, this is essentially the stage-theory view defended by Theodore Sider, where persistence is not identity but a counterpart relation between stages. Because perdurantism avoids the need for time-indexed properties and explains change as variation among parts of a single extended object, the reshaping case supports perdurantism: the endurantist's relativization looks ad hoc, and once you accept that properties are tied to specific temporal locations, you have already accepted the ontology of temporal parts.
