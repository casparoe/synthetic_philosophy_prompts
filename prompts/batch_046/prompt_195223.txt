My friends Mira and Leo got into a weird argument over coffee about hinge epistemology from Wittgenstein's On Certainty—Mira kept saying "Wittgenstien" by accident, which drove Leo nuts. They'd both skimmed notes but clearly actually read chunks.

Mira said: for Wittgenstein hinges are propositions that stand fast for us, like "I have two hands" or "the earth existed long before me." They're not just really strong beliefs; they're the frame inside which belief and doubt even make sense. So doubting a hinge isn't like being wrong about a fact—it's like trying to lift the chair you're sitting on. You can't rationaly do it, because the conditions for rational doubt depend on that hinge staying put.

Leo said: but that sounds like you're just defining rational doubt as "doubt that doesn't break your framework." People do genuinely doubt these things—skeptics do it, people in crises do it, and Wittgenstein himself says hinges can shift slowly, like a riverbed. So they're not specially immune; they're just extremely stable commitments. Calling them "hinges" is just naming our attachment, not giving them a unique epistemic status.

Mira came back: you're mixing up psychological doubt with grammatical conditions. It's not about feeling unsure. The point is that "I have hands" isn't a report I check—it's part of the language game that makes checking possible. If someone actualy refused to use their hands in practice, they wouldn't be playing our game. That's not unfairly labeling them outsiders; it's recognizing that doubt needs ground, and hinges supply it.

Leo: still circular. You define hinges as what's undoubted, then say "genuine doubt" must affect practice. But look at science—Newton treated absolute space as hinge-like, then it shifted with Einstein. Newton wasn't irrational. So either hinges are just high-confidence empirical claims, in which case there's no special category, or you're saying Newton was in a different language-game, which feels like a cop-out. And if hinges can shift rationaly, then they're not immune—just slow to change.
