On her view, you could not have originated from a different sperm-egg pair not merely because the nuclear genome would differ, but because the specific egg's cytoplasmic architecture—its mitochondrial haplotype, the distribution of maternal mRNA, and the biochemical gradient established at fertilization—constitutes part of your individual essence. She deploys this to resist both the standard "arbitrary gamete" objection and recent bioethical arguments that treat the nuclear genome as the sole bearer of personal identity in debates about embryo selection and reproductive cloning.

I'd like you to draft eight interview questions that treat her position seriously and press exactly where the metaphysical and biological details intersect. For each question, indicate briefly what answer she is likely to offer, and craft the immediate follow-up that makes her position do the heaviest lifting. Avoid generic biographical or methodological questions; keep the sequence focused on the logical structure and empirical commitments of cytoplasmic essentialism.
