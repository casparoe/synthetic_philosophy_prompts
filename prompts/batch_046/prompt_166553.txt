I am preparing a podcast episode on W. E. B. Du Bois's claim that art is inescapably propaganda—specifically, that every artwork advances some conception of human worth and that the only honest choice is which conception to advance. My guest is a contemporary philosopher of art who defends a Du Boisian propaganda thesis applied to both representational and non-representational work. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond having her restate the view. I want at least one question on how the thesis handles abstract or non-representational art that carries no explicit racial or political content; one on whether collapsing art into propaganda also collapses the distinction between art and rhetoric, and whether that is a genuine cost; and one on how Du Bois's documented appreciation of European classical music and literature can be reconciled with his warning that Black artists should not imitate white aesthetic forms. For each of these three, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—for instance, that formal abstraction still encodes a politics of form, that rhetoric and art differ in means rather than in aims, and that strategic assimilation is compatible with cosmopolitan taste. Finally, include one question that introduces a niche conceptual framing: treating aesthetic experience as a form of epistemic testimony, ask whether Du Bois's demand for truthful propaganda implies that morally bad propaganda is not merely wrong but epistemically corrupting for its audience, and what that conclusion would mean for the autonomy of the artist.
