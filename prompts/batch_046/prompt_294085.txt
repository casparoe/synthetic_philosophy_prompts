If hyperbolic discounting entails that an agent's preference ordering inverts as rewards become imminent—so that her present self genuinely values the smaller-sooner option more than the larger-later one—then in what sense can procrastination or impulsive choice still count as akrasia? It seems there is no stable, all-things-considered judgment being violated, only a rational update across temporal perspectives. Yet if we invoke precommitment devices—Ulysses contracts, for instance—to bind the present self to earlier resolutions, are we not smuggling in a normative standard of diachronic consistency that hyperbolic discounting, as a purely descriptive model, cannot itself supply?
