I was discussing with a friend in an informal philosophy reading group whether Charles Peirce and William James are genuinely at odds over the nature of truth, or whether they are simply addressing different questions that happen to share a vocabulary. I am trying to locate exactly where my own uncertainty lives, and I would like you to state your own credence — as a specific number between 0 and 1 — that Peirce's pragmatic maxim and James's account of truth are incommensurable rather than rival answers to the same problem. By incommensurable I mean that they are structured to solve different explanatory tasks: one perhaps functioning as a regulative ideal for scientific inquiry, the other as a descriptive psychology of belief-formation and practical success.

Please decompose your reasoning into at least four weighted components. First, assign a probability to the claim that Peirce's reference to the final opinion of an unlimited community of inquirers is best read as a regulative ideal with no necessary temporal endpoint, rather than as an empirical prediction about actual convergence. Second, assign a probability to the claim that James allows verification conditions — such as moral satisfaction, aesthetic coherence, or individual experiential harmony — that Peirce's framework, oriented toward public scientific testing, must reject as non-cognitive. Third, consider a hypothetical scenario I will call verification asymmetry: imagine a long-range predictive model, say in climate science, whose forecasts extend centuries beyond the lifespan of any human investigator and can only be validated by an artificial successor intelligence that lacks human experiential interests. Assign a probability to the claim that Peirce's account struggles to assign a determinate truth-value in such a case, whereas James's account permits a provisional, practically grounded acceptance. Fourth, assign a probability to the claim that the apparent disagreement largely dissolves once we distinguish Peirce's concern with the semantics of scientific terms from James's concern with the dynamics of belief-fixation under conditions of uncertainty. For each component provide a rough numerical probability and a brief line of justification, then show how they combine into your overall credence. After that, identify the component in which you are least confident, and describe what kind of evidence — perhaps a formal reconstruction of Peirce's later semeiotic writings or an empirical study of how scientists actually fix belief under resource constraints — would most substantially move your overall number. I am not asking for a bibliography or for appeals to authority; I want the reasoning decomposed so that the weak points are visible.
