In my interdisciplinary working group on historical ontology, a disagreement about periodization has been deadlocked for years, despite both participants accepting the same archival and institutional evidence. The dispute concerns whether labels like “the Middle Ages,” “the Renaissance,” and “the Scientific Revolution” name real historical kinds or are purely retrospective narrative inventions.

Colleague A argues they are retrospective scaffolds with no causal unity. She emphasizes that no actor in 1450 self-identified as living in “the Renaissance,” that the temporal boundaries are arbitrarily drawn (why begin around 1400 rather than 1380?), and that internal diversity—Italian civic humanism versus Northern scholastic textual recovery—defeats any claim to a single unified process. For A, “the Scientific Revolution” functions like “the Jazz Age”: a convenient cluster-label for loosely related trends, but not a kind that licenses real explanations or counterfactual inferences.

Colleague B agrees on all the empirical details but insists these are real kinds unified not by essences but by what he calls “functional-institutional coherence.” He identifies a specific package: a shift in authority over natural knowledge away from ecclesiastical and university structures toward court-sponsored academies and private societies, coupled with a standardized epistemic practice—systematic, mathematized experimentation that becomes institutionally self-reinforcing. B claims this package is projectible: it appears in a specific spatiotemporal region and not in structurally different contexts (such as the 12th-century translation movement or 19th-century industrial science), which makes the kind explanatory rather than merely descriptive. He accuses A of treating history as a “flat chronology” lacking structural joints.

The exchange has stalled in a predictable loop. A demands that B show her an “essence” of the period; B explains that historical kinds need only stable causal-functional signatures, not essences. B then suggests A is covertly committed to a normative picture in which history should not have “heroic” origin points; A replies that B is the one smuggling values—he wants scientific progress to have a dignified institutional birth narrative—into ontology.
