Write a 30-minute, 800-word essay question for an upper-level bioethics seminar on advance directives and the authority of the earlier self. The scenario should present a patient whose advance directive refuses a life-extending intervention on the grounds that it would violate her narrative identity—she understands herself as an artist for whom bodily dependence precludes the creative projects that give her life meaning—but after she loses competence she develops a serene, contented personality with no artistic interests, no memory of her former projects, and no identification with that earlier narrative. Ask students to evaluate whether the directive still binds, directing them away from a simple yes-or-no answer and toward an analysis of whether the authority of the earlier self derives from (i) continuity of core values, (ii) continuity of psychological identity, or (iii) a contractual commitment that survives both value-change and identity-change. Then provide a model answer that distinguishes these three sources of authority, demonstrates how the case is constructed specifically to drive them apart, takes a reasoned position on one source with explicit acknowledgment of the others, and addresses the contractual view's particular difficulty with revocation by a party now lacking competence. Finally, include a marking scheme that awards marks specifically for: correctly separating the three sources of authority (3 marks); recognizing that the case separates values, identity, and contract rather than treating them as co-extensive (3 marks); avoiding the mistake of treating narrative identity as either necessary or sufficient for the directive's authority (2 marks); and confronting the revocation problem that arises if the contract view permits an incompetent later self to be bound by commitments she can no longer understand or rescind (2 marks).
