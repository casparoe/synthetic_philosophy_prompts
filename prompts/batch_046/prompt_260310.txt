I'm delivering the opening remarks tomorrow morning at a workshop titled "Understanding Without Translation," co-hosted by the philosophy and computer-science departments. The audience will be split evenly: on one side, philosophers of language who work on Davidson and know the details of radical interpretation; on the other, machine-learning researchers who build retrieval-augmented systems and have likely never encountered Davidson's texts. I need a five-minute address—roughly 600 words—that genuinely welcomes both groups, frames the day's discussions, and makes Davidson's idea of radical interpretation do real structural work rather than serving as decorative ornament.

The framing I want is precise but not pedantic. Davidson asks us to imagine interpreting a speaker with whom we share no prior language, where the interpreter's only resources are the principle of charity and the necessity of rendering the speaker's utterances largely rational and true. I want to extend this structurally—not metaphorically—to a problem the engineers face: what happens when a language model receives a prompt in a domain or formal notation genuinely absent from its training distribution, so that retrieval mechanisms fail and the model must construct coherence using only internal constraints on rationality and consistency? The speech should ask whether this situation is philosophically analogous to Davidsonian radical interpretation, without claiming Davidson anticipated artificial intelligence. For the philosophers, the address must respect Davidson's actual constraints—charity as a precondition rather than an option, the holistic interdependence of belief and meaning, and the idea that interpretation is not translation but the attribution of a coherent mental economy. For the engineers, those same constraints should illuminate why evaluating an out-of-distribution model response requires first attributing a rational belief-system to the system, which in turn complicates straightforward error metrics that assume a direct mapping between prompt and "intended" meaning.

Please write the speech. It should not contain bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or suggestions for further reading. It should avoid treating Davidson's view as a mere analogy; instead, the comparison should shape what I can and cannot claim about model behavior—for example, if charity is constitutive of interpretation, then labeling a model output as a "misunderstanding" presupposes that we have already reconstructed a coherent internal framework for it, rather than measuring deviation from a ground-truth translation. Keep the tone welcoming, forward-looking, and suitable for an interdisciplinary room.
