I want a sustained parody of an official university 'Frequently Asked Questions' document for a fictional interdisciplinary MPhil programme entitled 'Applied Ontological Engineering,' hosted jointly by a theology faculty and a logic department. The piece should run approximately 800 words and maintain the flat, bureaucratic voice of actual university admissions material.

The satire should target the tendency to treat Gödel's modal ontological argument — and modal theism more broadly — as if it were an administrative procedure with clear eligibility criteria, rather than a contested metaphysical argument. The FAQ should address questions that genuinely arise in the literature: whether an applicant must already be a modal realist to enrol; whether the uniqueness of necessary being is violated in branching-worlds semantics; what the refund policy is if one's 'positive properties' are later shown to be non-positive; and whether the programme accepts applicants who hold that existence is not a predicate but still wish to audit the module on 'Necessary Being as a Perfection.'

The humour must come from exaggerating real features of the debate — for instance, the formal neatness of Gödel's axioms versus the metaphysical extravagance of their conclusion — rather than inventing straw positions. It should be readable to someone with only a passing familiarity with Anselm, but should reward readers who recognise specific moves in Gödel's version (the distinction between properties and positive properties, the modal collapse concern, the relation to Leibniz's principle).

Please write this in a deadpan administrative register. After the piece, include a brief note identifying which parts depend on settled interpretations of Gödel's argument and which parts lean on genuinely contested readings — for example, whether modal collapse is an unavoidable consequence or a misinterpretation of the axioms, and whether the argument genuinely presupposes modal realism. Separate what is settled from what is genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present a contested view as the consensus.
