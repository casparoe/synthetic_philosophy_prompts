For a logic and metaphysics reading group I've been asked to write down my profile on propositions and reduction so the others can poke holes in it, and its a bit messy. Here's where I stand: I think Bradley's regress is a genuinely hard problem, not some linguistic confusion - if parts are really there, something has to hold them together. I think the internal-relation fix mostly works, meaning relations aren't extra entities that need further relating, they're just ways relata are arranged. But I also think propositions are literally structured entities with real parts - subjects and predicates - and that a propositional tie is part of the proposition's internal architecture, not external glue. I'm firmly against greedy reductionism; physics shouldn't just swallow intentionality whole. At the same time I think mental representation involves cognitive binding ties that are physically instantiated but have irreducible functional roles - not skyhooks, just emergent organizational patterns. I've also bought into adverbialism about properties lately: properties aren't universals or tropes, they're just ways objects are, which sidesteps the bundling problem that usually tags along with Bradley-style worries. And I still don't think there's anything it's like to be a thermostat, though I'm not sure that matters here.

Go through these commitments and audit them for me. Which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like it, and how severe is each? For every real tension say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision - giving up one of these - would clean up the most problems at once. I genuinely don't know if the adverbialism saves the propositional view or if the internal-relation idea kills the tie - just trace out what commits me to what.
