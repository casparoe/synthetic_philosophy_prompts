Please take on the role of an expert philosophical auditor with particular training in ethics, philosophy of biology, and aesthetics. Before you deliver any verdict, I ask you to think carefully and step by step: begin with a brief brainstorming of the logical relations among the commitments below, identifying which pairs might genuinely conflict, which might only appear to conflict, and which single revision would have the greatest restorative effect on the overall package. I am writing from the perspective of a recently retired evolutionary biologist who, after three decades studying stress physiology and animal behavior, has begun formal philosophical study. For an upcoming seminar I wish to present my tentative philosophical profile—a form of tranquilism shaped by biological thinking—and I need a rigorous consistency audit before I expose it to discussion.

Here are the commitments I currently hold. (a) Tranquilism about intrinsic value: the only goods that matter in themselves are the absence of suffering and the absence of craving; knowledge, beauty, achievement, and pleasure are at best instrumental means to reducing these two negatives. (b) A biological model of craving: I understand craving not as ordinary intentional desire but as allostatic load, a cumulative homeostatic error signal analogous to the wear-and-tear metrics used in stress physiology; moral and prudential reasoning should therefore target the measurable reduction of this load. (c) Aesthetic realism with a specific peak: I maintain that aesthetic value is objective, and that its highest form is the state John Keats described as negative capability—the disciplined capacity to remain in uncertainty, mystery, and doubt without forcing rational closure—where craving is not eliminated but structurally contained and redirected rather than dissipated. (d) Ethical anti-demandingness: morality imposes a strict negative duty not to cause suffering in others, but it does not generate a positive duty for me to suppress my own craving for scientific understanding, provided that this craving does not increase net allostatic load elsewhere. (e) A selective epistemic split: evolutionary debunking arguments undermine our confidence in abstract metaphysical and aesthetic beliefs, since these are likely adaptive by-products, but they do not undermine our direct knowledge of suffering and craving, which are biologically basic and causally calibrated.

My question is whether these five commitments form a jointly tenable package or whether they pull apart in ways I have not seen. Please examine them systematically: identify every pair that is in genuine tension, rank those tensions by severity, and for each specify the cheapest revision available and what it costs the core view. Also identify at least one pair that looks tense but actually dissolves on closer inspection, explaining precisely why the conflict is only apparent. Finally, advise me which single commitment I should revise if I wish to clean up the most conflicts while preserving the central tranquilist intuition that only the absence of craving and suffering carries intrinsic weight.
