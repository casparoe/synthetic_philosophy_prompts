Following a lengthy argument with a friend about whether Kant's ethics can be reconstructed without metaphysical baggage, I want an outside check on whether his characterization is fair or whether my resistance to it was justified. He summarized Kant's universalizability tests—both the Formula of Universal Law and the Formula of Humanity—as functioning like a coherence test for maxims: you universalize your maxim, and if doing so produces a contradiction in conception (as with false promising, where universal deception eliminates the institution of promising itself) or a contradiction in will (as with refusing aid, where universal indifference conflicts with the rational agent's necessary interest in receiving help), the maxim is impermissible. He then argued that this is essentially rule-utilitarianism disguised in logical language, because what we call a "contradiction" is really just the identification of a maxim whose universal adoption produces socially unstable or self-defeating outcomes. Applying this framework, he claimed that Kant's prohibition on suicide is not grounded in a special duty to preserve one's own rational nature, but rather in a contradiction in will: if every rational agent destroyed themselves upon experiencing suffering, the very community of rational agents that makes moral deliberation possible would be eroded. Finally, he insisted that the Formula of Humanity adds nothing independent, since treating humanity as an end in itself is merely a redescription of the same universalization constraint applied to other people rather than to maxims. I suspect he is collapsing the distinction between logical contradiction and practical unsustainability, and that he mislocates the ground of the suicide prohibition—possibly conflating perfect and imperfect duties—but I need help seeing exactly where. Which elements of his summary are accurate representations of Kant's position, which involve subtle conflations—particularly between contradiction in conception and contradiction in will, between the Formula of Universal Law and the Formula of Humanity, and between a logical incoherence and a consequentialist harm—and does his reductive conclusion that Kant is a disguised rule-utilitarian actually survive once the misrepresentations are corrected?
