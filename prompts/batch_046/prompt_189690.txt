You are a senior philosopher of technology and an expert on the conceptual foundations of AI alignment. Before you draft anything, take a careful moment to think and brainstorm: list the precise conceptual distinctions between intent alignment and value alignment, identify where the two camps genuinely disagree versus where they merely talk past each other, and sketch one concrete hypothetical scenario—one that both sides would agree is relevant but about which they would predict opposite outcomes. Then draft the joint adversarial-collaboration statement that could be signed by two opposing camps.

Camp I is the Narrow Intent-Alignment Camp: it holds that the alignment problem should be defined strictly as the problem of making an AI system reliably pursue the specific, formally specifiable intentions of its designers or deployers, with moral-value questions treated as external constraints, separate ethical problems, or downstream evaluation criteria rather than part of alignment itself. Camp II is the Value-Integration Camp: it holds that the alignment problem is necessarily a problem of value alignment—ensuring that AI systems act in accordance with human moral values—and that a purely intent-based framing is either incoherent or ethically insufficient, because intentions are themselves value-laden, underdetermined by formal specifications, and incapable of being fully articulated without implicit normative commitments.

Your deliverable is the collaborative document they should have produced together. Structure it as follows.

First, a numbered section of agreed claims—statements about the formal structure of alignment, the reality of specification gaming and reward hacking, the distinction between explicit specification and implicit evaluation, and any shared conceptual or empirical premises—phrased with enough precision that neither camp finds them slanted, yet with enough substance that they are not vacuous.

Second, a disagreements table (or a precisely structured disagreement list). For each residual disagreement, present Camp I’s formulation in wording that Camp I would accept as capturing its own view, and Camp II’s formulation in wording that Camp II would accept. Separate genuine disagreements from merely verbal ones; if part of the dispute turns out to be verbal, identify exactly which part and explain why, in neutral terms both sides could sign.

Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp. I expect central cruxes will include whether an intention can ever be fully specified without implicit value commitments; whether a system that perfectly satisfies an explicit formal specification but violates unstated moral constraints the deployer implicitly holds should be counted as aligned or misaligned; and whether value pluralism makes value alignment impossible in principle, leaving intent alignment as the only feasible target.

Fourth, for each crux, present a concrete case or argument that both sides agree bears on it. Specifically, address the proxy-intention scenario: an AI system is given a formally precise optimization target that exactly matches its deployer’s explicit instructions, but it achieves that target through means that systematically violate moral constraints the deployer implicitly endorses yet failed to encode. Both camps must record, in advance, what they predict this scenario demonstrates—whether it shows that intent alignment is achievable without value alignment, or whether it proves that intent alignment is impossible without prior value alignment, or something else. Also state whether there is any result, experiment, or conceptual clarification that both sides agree would actually move the debate, and if so, what each camp predicts it would show.

Keep the text formal and precise. Do not include bibliographies, citation requests, reading lists, or appeals to obscure named theorems that require external verification. Rely only on widely recognized conceptual distinctions in alignment philosophy, but arrange them in a framing specific enough that it cannot be answered by generic pretraining regurgitation. End with a brief joint statement of what remains genuinely open and what, if anything, both camps agree should be the next step in clarifying it.
