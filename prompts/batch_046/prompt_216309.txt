I am designing a graduate module on causal explanation in social theory, and I recently came across the following summary in a review essay. The writer claims: "Weber held that Calvinist predestination produced a psychological anxiety about salvation that was relieved only through systematic worldly asceticism, which unintentionally generated the capitalist spirit. Because this belief was a cultural value rather than a material interest, Weber treated culture as an autonomous causal force capable of reshaping economic structures without being reducible to them. He therefore denied methodological individualism outright and maintained that values could function as efficient causes in historical narratives, rather than serving only as interpretive contexts for understanding action." The reviewer is a well-regarded intellectual historian, yet the account feels suspiciously tidy. Is this a fair characterization? Please distinguish what is largely accurate, what is a standard simplification, and what Weber either rejected or significantly qualified—especially concerning the psychological mechanism, the causal status of values, and his stance on methodological individualism.
