I've been trying to formalize a tension between the No-Free-Lunch results and the problem of induction, and I ended up with an argument that feels like it should collapse at some step but I can't locate the leak.

(1) The NFL theorems show that, averaged over the uniform distribution of all possible input-output mappings, every learning algorithm has exactly the same expected generalization error.

(2) Scientific induction is a learning algorithm in this sense: it updates beliefs on observed regularities in order to predict unobserved cases.

(3) Therefore, averaged over all logically possible worlds, induction performs no better than random guessing.

(4) For induction to be rationally justified in our actual world, we need a reason to think our world is not drawn from that uniform distribution — that is, we need a prior that breaks the symmetry.

(5) Any such prior must itself be justified. But justification for a prior about the empirical world can only come from prior induction or from purely a priori reasoning.

(6) Purely a priori reasoning cannot discriminate among empirically distinct worlds without smuggling in empirical content.

(7) So the symmetry-breaking prior must come from prior induction, which in turn requires its own symmetry-breaking prior.

(8) This launches an infinite regress of priors, with no non-circular base case.

(9) Hence, either we accept that induction is globally unjustified (radical skepticism), or we accept an unjustified base prior (dogmatism).

(10) Both horns seem unacceptable, yet I cannot see which step fails.

The conclusion is stronger than I want — I think induction is defensible — but I want to preserve (1) and (3) since they seem mathematically sound. Is the mistake in treating induction as a single algorithm subject to NFL in (2)? In assuming in (4) that rational justification requires coverage over all possible worlds? Or is the regress in (7)-(8) spurious because priors can be justified non-empirically in a way I haven't accounted for? If I have to choose, I'd rather keep (1) and reject (9), but I want to know what I'm paying to do so. Where does this go wrong?
