I teach an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar and I am drafting the final exam. Write one essay question on sphexishness—mechanical rigidity, flexibility, and free will—that cannot be answered by summarizing the classic sphex-wasp example. Instead, inject a novel hypothetical architecture, “Sphex-Prime”: its base-level sensorimotor loops are rigidly stereotyped and unresponsive to contextual variation, but it possesses a flexible meta-cognitive layer that can rewrite those base loops, though only according to a fixed, unalterable optimization function that it can never revise.

The question should ask whether Sphex-Prime possesses free will, and more generally whether freedom requires the absence of sphexishness at every organizational level or only at the level of action-selection. Require students to: (a) distinguish sphexishness precisely from determinism and from mere behavioral predictability; (b) argue whether flexibility is necessary and/or sufficient for moral agency; and (c) apply their analysis to Sphex-Prime, identifying exactly which levels remain sphexish and whether meta-level flexibility compensates for base-level rigidity.

Specify the exam conditions: upper-level seminar final, 75 minutes, answer of approximately 900 words, 30 marks total. Provide the essay question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme built around five must-hit points with marks assigned (totaling 30), plus a one-line note on the typical mistake or failure mode for each point. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not quote any text verbatim.
