I've been wrestlling with Bradley's regress ever since a tutorial on metaphysics went sideways last week. I've come round to a kind of hybrid view: the unity of the proposition isn't solved by stacking more relating relations (which just kicks the can) or by treating propositions as bare lists (which Bradley rightly mocks). Instead, I think the binding is enacted — the relation isn't a separate constituent sitting between terms but is realised in the very dynamics of judging or entertaining the content. So Bradley's regress never gets off the ground, because there's no static relational piece demanding further connection.

But I'm not convinced I'm not just dressing up wordplay. What would actually count against this? I want an honest assessment of how exposed the view is. Which formal or intuitive results — maybe about false propositions, or about thinking the same content in different attitudes, or about structural universals — hit it hardest? Which of these would a defender of 'enactive binding' concede as genuinely damaging, and which could they brush off? Is there a risk that the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if so, is that fatal for a metaphysical claim of this sort?

Please answer in British English. Don't just rehearse standard objections; I want them sorted by how decisive they'd be, plus a clear verdict on whether this kind of position risks anything real or has been made too slippery to fail.
