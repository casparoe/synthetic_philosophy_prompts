A close friend has been prescribed an experimental neurostimulation therapy that produces sustained, intense subjective well-being but markedly suppresses her motivation to maintain relationships or pursue long-term projects. She reports feeling "more genuinely herself" during the sessions than in her ordinary, often frustrated life, and asks whether she should use the device continuously. I feel her choice would be wrong, yet I struggle to articulate why without merely romanticizing struggle or invoking an unargued preference for "authenticity." Please identify the philosophical structure of this dilemma—particularly how it relates to debates about well-being and practical reason—and explain whether there is a decisive argument against her plan, or whether my discomfort reduces to a bias. Respond in one paragraph, no more than 130 words.
