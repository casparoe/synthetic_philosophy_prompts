theres this pattern my friend and I kept circling after arguing for like two hours about whether ranking historical atrocities is even possible without being obscene. Every time one of us said the Holocaust was worse than the Congo Free State, the other said "yeah but the Holocaust is unique in way X"—industrial scale, bureaucratic intent, whatever. Then if you show the Congo also had massive scale and state intent, they switch and say "well scale isnt the right metric here, the real uniqueness is Y, like the racial ideology behind it." Each metric sounds definately reasonable alone, but they only get pulled out to protect the ranking you started with. I saw the same thing with chattel slavery versus the Gulag: duration, personal cruelty, systemicity, they all take turns being "the decisive factor." My friend calls it the uniqueness shell game, but I want to know if philosophy actually has a term for this—where the criteria for incomparable evil keep sliding so the comparison never gets genuinely tested. How is it different from plain incommensurability or moral particularism? If theres no real name, what should we call it and where exactly does it blur into legitimate contextual judgment? Give me exactly five points, each one or two sentences, no intro or conclusion, and dont bother with citations or reading lists.
