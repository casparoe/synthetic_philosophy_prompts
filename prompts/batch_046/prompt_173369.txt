I was arguing with my cousin last weekend about belief and how it works — like, if you have a thousand lottery tickets you can say about each one that this particular ticket is a loser and be right almost every time, but then you also have to accept that one of them is gonna win. My brain keeps looping on how you can hold all those individual beliefs and still have the whole set contradict them. Then he brought up this preface thing, where a writer thinks every claim in the book is correct but also writes in the preface that there's definetly an error hiding somewhere inside. Same knot, right?

Anyway I want a poem about this loop. Specifically a villanelle — 19 lines, five tercets and a quatrain, with two refrains that keep coming back and only two rhymes. One refrain should be something like trusting each piece on its own, the other should be about the total picture breaking that trust. The repetition should mirror how the paradox just circles back on itself no matter how you try to untie it, like the form itself is stuck in the logic. Don't resolve it at the end — leave the tension hanging, cause thats literally where me and my cousin ended up, just staring at eachother. Make it accurate enough that someone who knows the lottery paradox and the preface paradox sees the structure immediately, not just a vague poem about doubt. No sermon about fixing belief, no shrug about it not mattering — just the knot itself, left open in the rhyme.
