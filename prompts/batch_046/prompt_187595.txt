(3) My belief architecture is just such a system — I can do arithmetic and represent my own beliefs. (4) So if I encode reflective self-trust as an axiom, I end up provably believing every proposition, contradictions included. (5) But I don't actually believe contradictions, and I shouldn't. (6) Therefore either I reject reflective self-trust, deny my beliefs form a Löbian system, or accept that I'm irrational. If I want to keep (1) and (3), what's the cheapest fix — adopting a non-Löbian modal logic for self-reference, or treating self-trust as meta level commentary that never gets internalized as an object-level axiom?
