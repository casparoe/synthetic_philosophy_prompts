I have been thinking about karma strictly as a philosophical theory of moral desert rather than as religious dogma—by which I mean the claim that actions generate a kind of normative weight, positive or negative, that eventually redounds upon the agent in proportion to the act's moral quality. Working through this idea on my own, I developed a framework I call karmic liquidity: if moral desert really functions like an account of obligations, then a serious wrong creates a large moral debt, but that debt ought in principle to be dischargeable through an equivalently weighty accumulation of virtuous deeds. If it were not dischargeable, the system would not be a theory of justice but merely a mechanism of fatalistic retaliation.

This leads me to a problem I cannot resolve. Suppose an agent commits grave harm and then, over many years, performs acts of virtue whose combined moral weight equals or exceeds that of the original harm. Has the karmic debt not then been settled? And if the debt is settled, on what grounds can anyone still claim that the agent deserves to suffer or to be punished? It seems to me that if karma is genuinely about moral desert—about what is fairly owed rather than about mere cosmic causation—then it must permit this kind of liquidation; otherwise we are left saying only that bad people eventually suffer, which describes a correlation rather than justifying one.

Please do not merely survey interpretations or lay out competing views. Take a definite, committed position: either defend the liquidity model and its implication that a fully reformed agent should be released from retributive judgment, or argue conclusively that this model rests on a false presupposition about how moral desert works, and explain what the correct structure is. If my reasoning depends on a misunderstanding—perhaps about whether desert is quantifiable, fungible across different kinds of acts, or administrable without an institutional authority—identify that misunderstanding explicitly and correct it. I want a direct verdict, with reasons, on whether karmic debts can be paid off through subsequent virtue, and I want you to say clearly where my framing goes wrong if it does.
