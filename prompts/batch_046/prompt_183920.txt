Write an essay on whether tranquilism—only the absence of craving and suffering matters—ends up eating itself. The worry is that if positive states have zero value, then any motivation to reduce suffering has to come from... suffering, or from craving relief, which is just more craving. So is tranquilism secretly self-undermining? On the other hand, defenders say craving's the root harm and that non-craving awareness can still motivate care without being a "positive good" in itself. Weigh both sides but don't sit on the fence—defend a verdict. In particular, deal with this weird case: imagine someone who deliberately keeps a mild background ache alive because it keeps them alert and effective at helping others. If tranquilism is right, is that person being irrational? Or is there a hidden value in that discomfort? Keep it focused, no need for a bibliography.
