I'm writing a feature on long-range policy and the people it creates, and there's a philosophical corner I've hit that I can't find the right word for. Picture two energy policies: one pushes renewables hard now, the other waits a decade then does the exact same buildout. By 2150 both worlds are equally well off -- same average welfare, same population size -- but that decade shift changes who meets whom and where, so the actual people alive are almost entirely seperate sets. No one can say I'd have done better under plan B, because the complainer wouldn't exist in B's world. This isn't the classic nonidentity problem, where one path is worse for the people it produces; here both paths are welfare-equivalent, and yet the identities are totally swapped. Is there an established term for this welfare-neutral version? Does it still break person-affecting ethics, or does the equality of welfare let us off the hook? If there's no name, I'd like to know exactly where the edge is between this and the original formulation, and what distinguishes it from plain demographic drift.
