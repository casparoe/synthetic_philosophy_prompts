Francis Herbert Bradley died in 1924, long before the contemporary metaphysical literature on grounding—the relation of metaphysical dependence said to hold between facts—took shape in the late twentieth century. Drawing on his argument that treating relations as independently real entities linking terms generates an infinite regress, and on his insistence that a genuine proposition must possess internal unity rather than being a bare aggregate of parts, predict how Bradley would assess grounding relations. Would he regard them as appearances that dissolve into an underlying monistic reality, as relations vulnerable to his regress argument, or as reconceivable without independent relational terms? Apply this specifically to the modern claim that grounded entities are nothing over and above the entities that ground them: does this resolve Bradley's worry about propositional unity, or does it reproduce the very aggregation he rejected?
