I am moderating a colloquium Q&A with an unnamed philosopher who defends a position she calls ascription universality: the claim that to ascribe universal status to an epistemic or normative principle is not merely to generalize, but necessarily to embed a structure of epistemic dominance, positioning the ascriber’s framework as authoritative over frameworks that do not share the principle. She supplements this with the dominance-gradient thesis, which holds that the dominance is not binary but operates on a sliding scale of interpretive veto power, allowing for graded rather than absolute hierarchical control. Draft eight interview questions that do not ask her to restate the view, but press where the literature on universalism, standpoint theory, and epistemic injustice presses. I want at least one question on each of the following: (i) how she distinguishes ascription universality from ordinary universal generalization in non-normative domains such as basic biological needs; (ii) the reflexivity problem—whether her own universal claim that universality entails dominance performs the very dominance she diagnoses, and whether a meta-level exemption is available to her; (iii) the dominance-gradient thesis and whether symmetrical conflicts of universality (two incompatible frameworks each claiming universal authority) can be resolved without reinstating a hidden hierarchical arbitration; and (iv) the institutional implications for bodies such as international courts or scientific peer-review boards that routinely invoke universalist framing. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give—particularly if she appeals to negotiated translation between frameworks or to a reflexive meta-exemption—and make the follow-up press her on whether that reply smuggles back in the very universalist authority or interpretive veto she claims to reject. Keep the questions specific to the content of her position, avoid generic biographical openings, and do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
