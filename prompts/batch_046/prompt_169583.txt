Construct a thought experiment describing a near-future nanoscale sorting device—no quantum computing or exotic physics—that separates fast and slow molecules using an embedded sensor-memory-actuator loop, engineered so that three interpretations of the information-entropy relation yield three distinct verdicts on whether the second law of thermodynamics is violated. The Landauer-physicalist interpretation must conclude that the law is preserved, with the resolution lying in an unavoidable thermodynamic cost of resetting the memory substrate. The epistemic interpretation must conclude that no objective local violation occurs, with the resolution lying in a change in the observer's or system's knowledge state rather than in any dissipated energy. A third, syntactic-functional interpretation—treating information purely as correlation structure decoupled from thermodynamic substrate—must conclude that a genuine local violation does occur and that no recovery mechanism is available within that framework. Constraints: the device and its operation must fit in one paragraph; the information-processing step (recording and comparing velocity data) must be physically and conceptually isolable from the measurement and actuation steps; and the scenario must not rely on perfect prediction or human-level intelligence. After the scenario, present the reasoning for each of the three interpretations, identify the specific structural feature of your design that forces the syntactic-functional view to diverge from the other two, and state whether removing that feature collapses the case into the standard Maxwell-demon discussion. Finally, provide the defense that a proponent of the interpretation that fares worst could offer: state precisely which assumption in the scenario they would reject, whether that rejection is consistent with the core commitments of their view, and whether it leaves them able to accommodate standard Maxwell-demon cases.
