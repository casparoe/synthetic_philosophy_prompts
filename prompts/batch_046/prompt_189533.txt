Construct a realistic, one-paragraph bargaining scenario between two academic co-investigators, Alice and Bob, negotiating over a fixed grant budget and shared authorship credit, with a well-defined disagreement point (each retains only baseline salary and no publication credit) and a convex, symmetric feasible set near that point. Engineer the case so that the Kalai–Smorodinsky and Nash bargaining solutions yield different allocations: specifically, include an unattainable "utopia" benchmark—such as both investigators receiving full funding with sole first authorship—that lies strictly outside the feasible set, does not alter the Pareto frontier in the neighborhood of the agreement, but shifts the ideal reference point that Kalai–Smorodinsky uses to construct its proportional division line from the disagreement point. After presenting the scenario with explicit numerical or structural specification of the disagreement point d and the feasible frontier near d, derive the allocation each solution recommends, explain precisely why they diverge, identify the isolated feature that does the work, describe whether removing the unattainable benchmark causes the two solutions to converge, and conclude by stating how a defender of Nash bargaining would attack the institutional relevance of that benchmark in this specific grant-negotiation context.
