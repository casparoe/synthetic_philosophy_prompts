I am writing for an upper-level seminar on metaphysics and scientific explanation, and I have come to defend what I will call the crane-only view of explanation. On this position, every legitimate explanatory achievement is a crane—a stepwise, mechanistic, bottom-up construction that builds complex phenomena out of simpler parts without invoking mysterious top-down forces—while skyhooks, understood as irreducible downward causation, vital forces, or strongly emergent properties lacking mechanistic bases, are always either explanatory illusions or temporary placeholders for cranes we have not yet built. The view further holds that greedy reductionism—the attempt to explain high-level phenomena in a single leap by direct appeal to fundamental physics—is the only genuine error of reductionist thinking, and that intermediate-level sciences are fully legitimate precisely because they operate as intermediate cranes. I want an honest assessment of what would actually count against this view, not merely what sounds mysterious. Specifically: (1) Would robust empirical demonstrations of downward causation in complex adaptive systems genuinely threaten the crane-only position, or could they always be redescribed as reciprocal interaction among local mechanisms? (2) How serious is the challenge from explanatory inversion in cognitive neuroscience, where high-level cognitive architectures appear to constrain lower-level neural dynamics in ways that resist decomposition into purely bottom-up processes? (3) Is there a normative constraint problem—can rational norms or semantic content exert causal influence in a manner that cannot be rebuilt crane-by-crane from individual physical states, and if so, does this force a skyhook or merely demand a more complex crane? (4) Does the view risk unfalsifiability, since any apparently irreducible phenomenon can be dismissed as a crane under construction, and if so, is that insulation a methodological virtue or a theoretical vice? Please distinguish the defeaters that committed defenders of the crane-only view would themselves concede are damaging from those they would dismiss, rank the live threats from fatal to merely awkward, and indicate whether there is any observation, case study, or formal result that could genuinely move a committed theorist to abandon the position.
