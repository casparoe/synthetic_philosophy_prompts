hey, i'm drafting a short essay for a philosophy of science reading group and wanted some comments before i flesh it out. the core idea is that darwin and turing both invert the usual move from "competent result -> there must be comprehension behind it" but they do it in directions that rarely get lined up together. darwin's argument in origin is that biological competence—eyes, wings, all the fiddly adaptation—accumulates through selection with no designer grasping the endpoint. turing, in his 1950 mind paper and in the 1952 morphogenesis stuff, does something parallel for cognition and form: reaction-diffusion patterns generate stripes without any cell understanding the pattern, and his child-machine idea is basically learning by selection-like adjustments rather than explicit rule-following.

the twist i want to push is that turing's imitation game sneaks darwin back in at the evaluation stage. we call the machine intelligent because its outputs match ours, but if those outputs came from a genuinely darwinian process—variation and selection on weights with no internal model—then we're attributing comprehension exactly where there's only piled-up competence. so you get a nested inversion: darwin removes mind from nature, turing removes mind from the machine, and then the observer puts a mind back in just by interpreting behavior.

i'm definately unsure whether this forces turing into being more darwinian about cognition than he meant. he did want to replace the vague can machines think question with something operational, but he also talked about educating machines like children. does that education metaphor imply comprehension growing in, or is it just more selection? also, am i treating reaction-diffusion as mechanistic in a way that's wrong—maybe it's analogical and shouldn't be read as competence without comprehension at all? and is the nested point actually interesting, or just convoluted?

comments welcome on accuracy (especially around the child-machine idea), on whether the framing is wierd in a good way or just messy, and on which part needs the most work. feel free to tear into the structure.
