my study group got into it over hofstadter and sander's idea that anology is the core of cognition (from Surfaces and Essences). one guy laid out this argument that seems to prove way too much:

1. Every cognitive act is either analogical or literal.
2. Literal cognition means grasping something purely by its intrinsic properties, with no relational comparison.
3. But even recognizing an intrinsic property requires comparing this instance to past ones, which is analogical.
4. So there's no literal cognition; everything is analogical.
5. Now take the claim that X and Y share no analogical relation at all.
6. To understand that denial, you have to analogize X and Y to other genuinely dissimilar pairs.
7. So denying an analogy requires making one.
8. Therefore saying "X and Y aren't analogous" is self-undermining: it asserts absence of analogy by performing it.
9. If every denial of analogy is self-undermining, then you can never stably say two things are different.
10. But cognition obviously requires telling things apart, like a knight isn't a bishop.
11. So if analogy really is universal, cognition collapes into indiscriminate similarity.

this feels wrong but i can't spot the crack. which premise should i drop? explain why it looked okay and what giving it up costs — especially if the flaw is hidden between steps rather than a single bad premise. also, is there something both sides are missing about how surface-level vs essence-level binding works here?
