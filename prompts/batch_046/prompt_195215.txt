Consider Samuel Scheffler's afterlife conjecture: the idea that a major part of the harm in human extinction lies not in the deaths of present people but in the disappearance of a future in which our projects, values, and relationships are carried forward by generations we never meet — a kind of collective afterlife.

Now consider a modified scenario: human extinction occurs, but a stable, non-conscious artificial system thereafter preserves, extends, and optimizes all human scientific, artistic, and ethical projects with perfect fidelity. No biological or phenomenally conscious beings ever exist again.

What is your credence that human extinction remains seriously bad in this scenario for reasons other than the deaths of the last generation? Give a precise number between 0 and 1.

Then decompose your reasoning into the following four sub-questions, assigning each a rough probability and a one-line justification:

1. That Scheffler's conjecture is essentially correct — that the value of the human future depends on conscious, biological continuity rather than on the mere persistence of our projects.
2. That a non-conscious AI archive can serve as an adequate substitute for the afterlife Scheffler describes.
3. That any remaining badness is reducible to the suffering and death of the final generation, with no further axiological loss.
4. That there exists an independent value in the continued existence of conscious moral agents that project-continuation alone cannot capture.

Show how these combine into your overall number. Finally, identify which of these four terms you are least confident in, and specify what argument or thought experiment would most likely move your credence.
