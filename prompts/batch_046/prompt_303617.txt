Okay I'm prepping an argument for a seminar comparing Nietzsche's history essay — the one about monumental, antiquarian, and critcal history — to how people curate digital archives, like treating the three modes as curatorial algorithms, and I think I've walked myself into a contradiction. Here it is:

(1) Nietzsche says life needs history in one of three modes — monumental for inspiration, antiquarian for roots, critical for breaking free — but each becomes harmful if it dominates.

(2) To actually practice monumental history you have to pick which past figures or events count as great and worth emulating.

(3) Picking them requires judging them by present standards of what's useful or admirable for life right now.

(4) But that kind of judging-and-condemning-the-past-to-serve-the-present is exactly what Nietzsche calls critical history.

(5) Same deal for antiquarian: deciding which traditions to preserve means evaluating what's alive versus dead weight, which is again critical judgment.

(6) So both other modes secretly depend on critical history to do their filtering.

(7) Now Nietzsche also warns that critical history is the riskest one because it severs your roots and leaves you rootless, unable to act.

(8) So if you try to use monumental or antiquarian history consistently, you keep having to invoke critical history, which destroys the very continuity those modes need.

(9) Therefore you can't actually mix them in a healthy balance; activating one activates the destructive one.

(10) But Nietzsche insists we need history, and pure unhistorical living is impossible for humans.

(11) So we need history, but every form either is or activates the one form that makes life impossible, and we can't live without it.

This feels like it proves his whole triad collapses into self-defeat. Where's the exit? Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up cost — especially if I want to keep the idea that the three modes aren't really separable?
