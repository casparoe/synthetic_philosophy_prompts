Think of it like the old calibration rules in an abandoned observatory: they still tell you exactly how to align the telescope, but there's no astronomer left to want you to do it. The rules carry weight, but nobody's there to hold them.

I want an in-class activty — more of a structured game with roles and small stakes — that makes my group feel that pull before I ever name it. They should actually run into orphaned rationales and have to act on them, not just talk about them.

Here's the setup: 8 particpants, 90 minutes, no lecture up front. I need rules, materials (cards or props are fine), and some kind of payoff or incentive scheme that doesn't need real money — maybe points that affect a shared outcome. The idea should emerge from what happens, not from me explaining afterwards: maybe they discover they're enforcing norms that have no owner, or that resisting them costs something even with no one watching. Then a debrief that maps the key moments onto the concept — where they treated a reason as authoritative despite no reasoner, where they tried to invent an agent to ground it, and where they felt the normative pull anyway. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how to fix it.
