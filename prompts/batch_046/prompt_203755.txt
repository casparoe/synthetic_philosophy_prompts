After thirty years in statistical mechanics, I have only recently begun reading philosophy of science in earnest, and I find myself repeatedly stuck on the same unresolved tension in discussions of the thermodynamic arrow. One position—call it the cosmological account—holds that temporal directionality is fully explained by the extremely low-entropy boundary condition of the early universe. On this view, entropy increases away from that boundary in both directions, and what we call "the future" is simply the flank on which we happen to be situated; the arrow is statistical, contingent, and entirely grounded in that initial macrostate. The opposing position—call it the asymmetry objection—accepts the cosmological data but denies that a statistical gradient can by itself constitute temporal direction. Its proponents argue that without an independently oriented causal or experiential framework, an entropy gradient is symmetric: it correlates states but does not direct them. They maintain that explaining the arrow requires explaining why one direction is open, mutable, and causally efficacious, while the other is fixed and inaccessible, and that no boundary condition alone delivers that.

My working hypothesis is that this is not a hidden empirical dispute. The shared formal apparatus—Liouville dynamics, coarse-graining, and the past-hypothesis framework—is largely uncontested, and no foreseeable cosmological observation seems poised to break the symmetry. Rather, I suspect the sides are answering different questions under a single heading: the cosmological account asks what explains the origin of the asymmetry, while the asymmetry objection asks what justifies treating one flank as genuinely directed. They may therefore differ not about physics but about the standards an explanation of direction must satisfy—structural derivation versus phenomenological grounding.

I want an honest diagnosis, supported by what each side actually says, not a survey of interpretations. Is this disagreement verbal, turning on ambiguous uses of "explain" or "direction"? Is it substantive but about explanatory standards rather than physical facts? Or does a concealed empirical question—perhaps about whether quantum decoherence supplies an irreducible physical asymmetry independent of boundary conditions—actually drive the stalemate? Furthermore, if the impasse is conceptual or rooted in bedrock intuitions about temporal passage, is there any development—whether a formal distinction between entropic and causal asymmetry, a new result about reversibility, or a reconceptualization of the question itself—that could genuinely move it? Or should we expect permanent stalemate, and on what grounds?
