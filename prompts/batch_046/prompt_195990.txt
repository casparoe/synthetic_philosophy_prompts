Design a 90-minute essay question for an upper-level epistemology final exam, together with a model answer of approximately 600 words and a marking scheme. The topic is the regress problem, but frame it so that students cannot answer by reciting foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism. Instead, present a specific conceptual challenge: a belief that appears justified both by a direct experiential report and by its coherence with a theoretical framework that itself depends on accepting that very belief. Ask students to evaluate whether this bootstrapping loop undermines all three standard positions—foundationalism because basic beliefs are theoretically contaminated, coherentism because linear coherence collapses into circularity, and infinitism because an infinite linear chain is replaced by a closed circle—and to defend whether any position can accommodate the loop without viciousness or arbitrariness. Specify that answers should run about 1,000 words.

In the model answer, demonstrate the moves that earn an A: recognizing the contamination problem for pure foundations, distinguishing vicious from benign circularity in coherentist networks, and showing why a folded loop is structurally different from an infinite regress. Contrast this with a B-level answer that describes the three positions correctly but applies them mechanically to the loop without resolving the tension.

The marking scheme should allocate marks for conceptual accuracy, direct application to the loop structure, and critical evaluation; name specific errors that should cost marks, such as conflating coherence with linear consistency, treating basic beliefs as immune to theoretical loading, or mischaracterizing the loop as merely a long chain. Include the total marks and time allocation.

Finally, conclude with a brief meta-critical paragraph stating which element of your A/B distinction is most vulnerable to criticism, and identify the specific argument or consideration about assessing philosophical reasoning that would lead you to revise these grading criteria.
