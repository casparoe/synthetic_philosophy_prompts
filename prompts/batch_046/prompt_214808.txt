Hey, I'm drafting a blog post about whether Lewis's triviality results for indicative conditionals actually stick if you weaken the clousre conditions on belief sets. I've jotted some rough notes below — there's probably a mistake or two in there — and I'd really like your comments on whether my 'restricted salience closure' idea escapes the first result or just sneaks Stalnaker's thesis back in via the probabilistic side door. Does the comparison to Adams's thesis hold up, or am I conflating semantic and epistemic issues?

Notes:
Lewis (1976) shows that if P(A→B) equals P(B|A) then, assuming full closure, P(A→B) collapses to P(B) for independant B. The usual move is to deny truth conditions altogether. But suppose we keep them and instead restrict closure: an agent only needs to believe A→B when B is 'salient' relative to A in some contextually ordered partition. This blocks the derivation globally because the equality only binds within salience cells, not across them. Still, I'm worried this is just Stalnaker's selection function in probabilistic drag.

Also, for the second result — about nested conditionals — I wonder if treating them as meta-linguistic rather than object-language saves us from the collapse. Or does that just relocate the problem?

Please reply in British English, and feel free to be informal — I'm mainly trying to check if the logic holds water.
