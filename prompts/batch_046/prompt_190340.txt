Write a two-page research proposal for a three-month independent project or small workshop fellowship on the following problem. Weber’s thesis on the Protestant ethic is typically read as either claiming that Calvinist values causally generated capitalist dispositions or as observing a non-explanatory correlation, and the debate stalls because both sides assume that if culture is not an efficient cause it is merely decorative. I want to determine whether a coherent intermediate category exists. Specifically, propose and defend a three-part taxonomy of cultural efficacy—generative, selective, and constitutive—where only the first mode resembles standard causation, and apply it to Weber’s argument that worldly asceticism reshaped economic conduct. The proposal must include: a precise statement of the question (what explanatory work can values do if they are neither mechanical causes nor post-hoc rationalizations?); an account of why existing interpretations leave this open (they collapse Weber’s notion of elective affinity into either psychological mediation or statistical correlation); a defense of the taxonomic approach over the obvious alternatives (a purely archival historical study or a formal causal-modeling exercise), with the reasoning that conceptual clarification must precede empirical testing in this case; a realistic month-by-month method (month one: formalize the triad using established distinctions in philosophy of social science without inventing new formalisms; month two: apply the triad to Weber’s specific claims about capital accumulation and rational economic action; month three: test whether the framework resolves standard objections, such as materialist reduction or reverse causation, and identify where it breaks); a clear specification of success (a falsifiable-in-principle account of value-explanation that avoids reducing culture to individual psychology) and of failure (the triad collapses back into one of the two poles it was designed to avoid); and a scope appropriate to a single researcher with no archival access. Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. Begin the response with the proposal text itself, with no preface or commentary.
