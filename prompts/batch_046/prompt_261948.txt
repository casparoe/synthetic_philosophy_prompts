I work as a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy or history, but I have been trying to borrow a system-reliability distinction—whether a component is a single point of failure or part of a redundant architecture—to think about historical causation. A colleague who studies modern European history has answered four counterfactual “removal” questions in a pattern that feels consistent to me, though I can see at least two different principles that might explain her answers.

Her verdicts:
- If Johannes Gutenberg had never lived around 1440, movable-type printing would still have emerged in Europe within roughly thirty years, with broadly similar effects on literacy and religious reform.
- If Marie Curie had never lived around 1898, radioactivity and its practical characterization would still have been achieved within a generation by other researchers working in parallel.
- If Napoleon Bonaparte had died in 1799, the French revolutionary state’s legal and administrative reforms would still have spread across Europe; however, the specific sequence of imperial conquests, the Continental System, and the 1815 settlement would not have occurred.
- If Martin Luther King Jr. had died in 1963, the U.S. civil rights movement would still have secured major federal legislation by 1970, led by other organizers, though the particular nonviolent strategy and rhetorical framing would have been different.

My question concerns Adolf Hitler: if he had died just before becoming Chancellor in 1933, would there still have been a Nazi Germany—that is, a one-party, racially totalitarian state with an aggressive expansionist war program? Or would Weimar have evolved into a conventional authoritarian regime, or perhaps survived in some altered democratic form?

Please address the following:
1. Given her four earlier answers, what is the best prediction of her verdict on the Hitler case?
2. What are the two most plausible underlying rules that jointly explain her first four answers?
3. Where do those two rules specifically diverge on the Hitler counterfactual?
4. Which rule should we prefer if we want consistency with her track record, and what is the strongest reason?
5. What further counterfactual case—one not already listed—would best distinguish these two rules?

Treat the system-design analogy only as a framing device, not as an established historical theory, and do not rely on external sources or bibliographies.
