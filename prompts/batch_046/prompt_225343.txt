I have been tracking the verdicts that Dr. Voss, the moderator of our epistemology reading group, has delivered across this term's case studies, and I am trying to reverse-engineer the implicit framework—Nozickian tracking, closure-preserving tracking, a strict 'no false lemmas' constraint, or some hybrid—that best systematises them. His rulings to date are these.

On direct perceptual belief in optimal conditions—seeing a cup on one's desk in clear light—he grants knowledge without reservation.

On standard Gettier constructions, in which a true belief is justified by a false intermediate premise but the conclusion is true only by coincidence, he denies knowledge.

On the fake-barn scenario, in which a subject genuinely perceives a real barn in an environment saturated with facades, he likewise denies knowledge.

On deductive closure: when a subject actively derives a conclusion q from known premises p that entail q, he grants knowledge of q; but when p entails q and the subject knows p yet has never formed the belief that q, he withholds the ascription.

On redundant verification with awareness: a researcher concludes that dataset D supports hypothesis H after running two independent analyses, Analysis A and Analysis B; Analysis A contains a hidden error that would have yielded a false result, but Analysis B is sound and sufficient alone, and the researcher is explicitly aware of both procedures. He grants knowledge.

On testimony with hidden deception: a hearer receives a specific true claim from a speaker who is generally deceptive about unrelated matters; the claim is also supported by the hearer's own background evidence, though she is unaware of the speaker's deception and does not recognise her own independent support. He denies knowledge.
