Write a short story, composed in American English, that unfolds within a fictional archipelago known as the Resonance Islands, a setting designed to embody two distinct forms of cognitive insulation. On the island of Echo, dissent is never concealed; rather, it is routinely exposed only to be immediately neutralised through ritualised public denunciation and social exile, so that inhabitants encounter opposing views solely to reject them collectively. On the adjacent island of Bubble, by contrast, foreign information is structurally intercepted—bridges and ferry routes are engineered to filter out carriers of dissent before they ever reach the shore—meaning that inhabitants remain genuinely unaware of alternatives rather than actively hostile towards them. The narrative should follow a cartographer named Mara, who is commissioned to map the boundary between these two societies and who must move between them without being assimilated into either mode of insulation.

Please include a specific scene in which Mara attempts to deliver an identical piece of news—that the archipelago's central volcano, long believed to be active, is in fact dormant—to both islands, and show how Echo processes this information through active discreditation while Bubble processes it through absence and structural ignorance. The story should be approximately eight hundred words, with a contemplative rather than didactic tone, allowing the conceptual distinction between an echo chamber, in which counter-evidence is present but systematically undermined, and an epistemic bubble, in which counter-evidence is structurally absent, to emerge organically through dialogue, setting, and Mara's internal observations. Avoid explicit philosophical terminology; let the difference be felt through the characters' reactions and the physical architecture of each island.
