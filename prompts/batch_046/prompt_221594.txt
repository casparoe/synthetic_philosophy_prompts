I have been working through some recent discussions of epistemic circularity and easy knowledge, particularly in the context of reliabilist responses to the bootstrapping problem. Most textbook examples involve a subject using her eyesight to check her eyesight, or a thermometer checking a thermometer, which feels too abstract and too distant from the kind of structured, multi-layered verification that actually occurs in technical practice. I would like you to construct a more concrete, engineered thought experiment that meets precise specifications.

Design a scenario set in a realistic technical workplace—say, a quality-control laboratory or a clinical diagnostics unit—in which a technician acquires knowledge that her integrated multi-sensor measurement system is reliable by relying on the system’s own outputs. The key constraint is that the circularity must appear to be broken, not by an independent external audit or by stepping outside the method, but by an internal structural feature: specifically, that the different sensors within the system produce errors that are systematically anti-correlated, and that the technician can verify this anti-correlation using only outputs generated by the system itself, together with a coherence constraint she can observe (for instance, that the sensors must sum to a fixed baseline if functioning correctly). The scenario should be presented in one paragraph, should avoid perfect predictors, demons, brain-in-a-vat scenarios, or exotic physics, and should not depend on any highly specialized theory that could not be expected to be well known.

After presenting the scenario, please do the following: state the belief whose status is at issue, identify the method being used to form it, specify the nearby worlds relevant to assessing reliability, and then walk through what a reliabilist who permits a form of epistemic circularity would say about whether the technician has knowledge and why, versus what a bootstrapping skeptic—concerned that a method cannot certify its own reliability without generating easy knowledge—would object. Then say explicitly which feature of the case is doing the philosophical work, demonstrate what happens to the verdicts if that anti-correlation feature is removed (collapsing the case back into a standard bootstrapping loop), and finish by outlining how the skeptic would attack the remaining feature as still smuggling in an unverified architectural assumption about the system’s design.
