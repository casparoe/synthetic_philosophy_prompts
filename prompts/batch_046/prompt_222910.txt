As I work through teleosemantic accounts of mental content, I'm stuck on a conceptual tension in how they distinguish representational error from biological malfunction. Millikan's framework distinguishes direct proper functions—established by evolutionary selection—from derived proper functions acquired through individual learning. But when an organism's representational capacities are shaped by overlapping phylogenetic and ontogenetic selection-like feedback loops, it becomes unclear whether a learned cognitive map should be assigned only derived intentionality. Could you explain whether allowing individual learning episodes to count as selection processes in the same technical sense necessarily collapses the direct/derived distinction? And if the distinction is preserved, what criterion maintains the normativity of misrepresentation for acquired representations rather than reducing it to mere dysfunction?
