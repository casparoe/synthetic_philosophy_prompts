I've been working on a long-read about moral absolutism for my magazine — I cover science and ideas, mostly neuroscience and ethics — and I got talking to a tutor who let me peek at an exam script on Kant. The question asked students to discuss Kant's 1797 essay On a Supposed Right to Lie from Philanthropy, specifically his reply to Benjamin Constant's muderer-at-the-door case. They had to explain why Kant rejects the idea that truthfullness is only owed to people who deserve it, and then say whether this conflicts with the Doctrine of Right's discussion of coercion and self-defence in the Metaphyics of Morals.

Here's what the student wrote: 'Constant, in Des réactions politiques (1796), imagines a murderer asking where your friend hides. He argues we may lie because the murderer has forfeited any right to the truth by his criminal intent. Kant, in his 1797 reply, rejects this by applying the Formula of Universal Law from the Groundwork: if everyone adopted the maxim "lie to protect an innocent person from harm", the practice of truthful communication would collapse in contradiction, so benevolent lying is always impermissible. Kant adds that a lie damages humanity in general by corrupting the juridical source of right, regardless of whether the particular listener is wronged. Looking at the Metaphyics of Morals, the Doctrine of Right permits coercive resistance to wrong, which shows that when rights are forfeited we may use force; but false speech is not a form of coercion, so lying remains banned even to a murderer. Hence there is no conflict — self-defence is allowed, but lying is not, because the latter attacks the conditions of right itself rather than the wrongdoer.'

Could you grade this answer? I'm not after a vague impression — I want a specific mark (say, out of 20 or a letter), plus point-by-point comments on what's accurate and what's subtly off. In particular, check whether they've conflated the 1797 essay's reasoning with the Groundwork's universal-law argument, whether they've misrepresented how the Doctrine of Right actually relates to forfeiture, and whether they've missed Kant's specific point about shared responsibility if the lie accidentally leads to harm. Please write your assessment in British English, with clear reasoning for the score.
