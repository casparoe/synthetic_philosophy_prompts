I have been thinking about Rudolf Carnap's principle of tolerance—the idea that in logic and mathematics we are free to choose our linguistic framework based on practical utility rather than on metaphysical truth. It seems to me that this should extend naturally to politics and urban planning: if we built a precise formal language for zoning laws, budgets, and resource distribution, then disputes among citizens or council members would become practical questions about which syntactic rules are more convenient, not deep moral conflicts. Since Carnap apparently held that metaphysical disagreements dissolve once you clarify your framework, why haven't modern planning boards adopted a Carnap-style system that resolves value-laden debates—say, over affordable housing versus historic preservation—by translating them into choices about linguistic convention? Or did Carnap actually maintain that normative value judgments lie outside the scope of logical framework selection altogether, which would explain why political planning still treats them as substantive?
