On the other side is a structurally analogous but far less discussed case involving collective scientific acceptance. Here, a research community may face practical pressures—funding continuity, regulatory urgency, or institutional coherence—to stabilize a theoretical framework and treat it as established even when the underlying evidential base is genuinely ambiguous and contested. The question is whether these practical pressures constitute wrong-kind reasons for the collective doxastic attitude of acceptance, or whether collective science operates under norms—perhaps involving pragmatic values, social epistemology, or distributed reasoning—that legitimately allow practical considerations to shape acceptance in ways that would be irrational for an individual believer.
