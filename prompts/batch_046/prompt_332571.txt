hey, I'm working on a paper about abductive stagnation in consciousness studies and I built this argument that now feels like it eats its own tail. I can't tell if there's a hidden equivocation or if I'm just stuck with a bad premiss.

(1) If theory T explains all the first-order data but leaves a residual "why not otherwise" question Q open, and rival theory T' explains both the data and Q, then IBE says T' is strictly better.

(2) Physicalism explains all the neural/behavioral data about consciousness but leaves the hard problem Q untouched: why is there something-it's-like?

(3) Dualism explains the same data and also closes Q by treating phenomenal properties as primitive—no further explanation needed.

(4) So by (1), dualism is abductively superior.

(5) But there's also the meta-problem M: why do we even feel Q as a puzzle? Physicalism has a decent story—our conceptual architechture creates an explanatory-gap illusion.

(6) Dualism doesn't really explain M; if phenomenal properties are just basic, there's no special reason we should experience cognitive dissonance about them.

(7) So now physicalism explains both the first-order data and M, while dualism explains the data and Q but leaves M dangling.

(8) Apply (1) again: physicalism explains more total data (E + M) without extra primitives, so physicalism wins.

(9) But (4) and (8) contradict each other—IBE picks opposite winners depending on whether we count M as part of the evidence.

(10) So either IBE is self-defeating here, or one of the "explanations" in (5)-(7) is fake.

I really want to keep (1) because it sounds like standard IBE, and I also want to keep (5) because the cognitive-illusion story is the best physicalist response to the hard-problem framing. Where do I cut? And please—say what would actually change your mind about which step is the rotten one, and flag exactly where your reasoning is shakiest.
