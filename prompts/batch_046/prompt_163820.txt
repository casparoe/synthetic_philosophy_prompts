I'm constructing a conceptual sorting instrument for a discussion group on the philosophy of physics, and I want it to classify people by what they actually believe about the thermodynamic arrow of time rather than by which textbook label they prefer. The specific angle I'm after is not just "why is entropy low in the past?" but whether answering that statistical question settles the direction of time entirely, or whether there remains a further metaphysical or epistemic fact about temporal asymmetry that thermodynamics does not capture — in other words, whether the arrow is explained, grounded, or merely projected onto a symmetric substrate.

Please build a decision tree (with an equivalent scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions) that starts from logical space rather than from a list of named theories. The root should split on whether a temporary local entropy decrease inside an otherwise equilibrated, isolated system possesses its own "backward" local arrow or none at all; then branch through concrete commitments that a respondent can endorse without knowing philosophical jargon. The internal nodes should cover three fault lines: (i) whether the arrow is fully objective in the world or relative to an observer's or causal structure's partition of system from environment; (ii) whether accounting for the arrow requires a cosmological boundary condition, or whether it can be recovered locally in any region given appropriate initial conditions; and (iii) whether perfectly time-symmetric microscopic dynamics yield macroscopic asymmetry purely statistically, or whether some hidden directional principle — even if not in the fundamental laws — is still required. Include as leaves at least the pure statistical-entropic view (low-entropy boundary plus dynamics), the causal-branching view, the observer-relative or decoherence-based view, the "no further fact" conventionalist view, and one explicitly unoccupied combination — for instance, accepting local recovery of asymmetry while also insisting on a redundant global cosmological boundary — which you should briefly diagnose as incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also present a thought experiment involving two causally disconnected regions with opposite entropy gradients relative to a shared coordinate parameter, asking whether they share one objective time-direction, two, or none.

Make sure the tree separates positions by patterns of yes/no/depends answers to these cases, not by self-identification with names like "Boltzmannian" or "Reichenbachian," though you may assign those labels afterward at the leaves. Flag any pairs of positions that collapse onto the same leaf because they agree on all the test cases and differ only in metaphysical framing, explaining why the instrument cannot distinguish them. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations; the goal is a self-contained diagnostic tool.
