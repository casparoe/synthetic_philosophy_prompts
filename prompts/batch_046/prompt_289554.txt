I am a senior in an advanced high school philosophy seminar, and I have volunteered to assist my instructor in designing the final examination for our unit on ethics and future generations. I need one essay question addressing the nonidentity problem—the puzzle that identity-affecting choices appear to escape evaluation in terms of harm to particular individuals—designed so that a student cannot earn high marks merely by recounting the familiar reproductive-choice and resource-depletion scenarios. Instead, the question should require candidates to assess whether a reformulated person-affecting harm principle can survive when the identity of future individuals is itself a variable of the policy choice, or whether preserving our intuitive judgment that reckless climate and reproductive policies are wrong necessarily forces us toward an impersonal principle that abandons the restriction entirely. The exam is intended for advanced high school students who have encountered analytic philosophy but are not specialists in metaphysics; candidates should have approximately 60 minutes. The total value should be 20 marks. Please provide: (a) the exam question itself; (b) a model answer outlining the five specific analytical moves a top response must make; and (c) a marking scheme that allocates points to each of those five moves, includes a one-line note on the characteristic mistake that causes candidates to miss each one, and specifies how partial or confused answers should be graded.
