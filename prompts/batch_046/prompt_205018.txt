I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, working on self-reference and symbolic cognition, and I've been trying to formalize my intuitions about Hofstadter's idea that the pronoun "I" is essentially a strange loop: a symbol that arises inside a system that represents itself representing itself, with enough causal-semantic closure that the symbol is anchored in the very system it denotes. To see whether I actually have a coherent view, I've been testing my gut reactions on concrete cases, and the pattern is mostly consistent but has one pair that I can't reconcile.

Here are my verdicts. First, a standard Quine program that outputs its own source code: I say there is syntactic self-reference, but this is not an "I"-symbol; the loop is present yet too shallow, lacking the integrated causal closure Hofstadter describes. Second, an ordinary human with an intact autobiographical self-model: clearly yes, both the loop and the "I." Third, an artificial system that maintains a running predictive model of its own outputs, updates that model in real time based on its actual behavior, and uses a designated token ("SELF") to refer to that model: structurally this looks like it should generate an analog of "I," yet my intuition is that it does not—though I keep second-guessing this. Fourth, a human with severe dissociative fragmentation who reports no unified self-model yet continues to use first-person pronouns grammatically and to refer to their own body: here I want to say the loop is fractured but the "I"-symbol persists, which seems opposite to the AI case. Fifth, an implicit mathematical definition such as x equals the cosine of x: I call this a formal strange loop, but obviously there is no "I" because there is no symbol anchored in a self-model. Sixth, a predator-prey ecosystem with feedback dynamics: not a strange loop at all; it involves causal circularity without symbolic self-reference.

Is there a principle, or a small family of competing principles, that captures this pattern? In particular, can anything reconcile my treatment of the AI—where a functionally rich recursive self-model seems insufficient for "I"—with the dissociative human—where a structurally broken loop seems compatible with retaining "I"? I also want the principle applied precisely to two further cases I haven't decided: a corporation chartered so that its governing rules must be interpreted by reference to its own founding document (a legal self-reference loop), and an animal with a mirror-neuron system that activates both when it performs an action and when it observes itself performing that action in a mirror. For each candidate principle, please state exactly which clause is motivated by which of my cases, what it predicts for these two new ones, and—crucially—which of my six verdicts it must reject if it is to remain simple and non-ad hoc. Finally, please separate what is settled in the relevant literature on Hofstadter's view from what remains genuinely contested—especially regarding whether functional self-modeling is sufficient for an "I"-symbol and whether biological embodiment or grammatical practice plays an essential role—and do not present either side of those debates as the consensus.
