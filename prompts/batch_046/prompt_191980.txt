The anti-sphexist holds that genuine agency requires the capacity to break rigid, mechanically repeated behavioral patterns, and that sphexishness — the ritualized repetition of actions despite changed conditions, exemplified by the burrow-checking wasp that continues its inspection even after the burrow has been relocated — is the antithesis of freedom. The structuralist holds that free will depends not on flexibility but on stable, rationally ordered dispositions, and that an agent capable of constant revision but lacking internal coherence would be no more free than a sphexish one, being merely reactive rather than self-governed.
