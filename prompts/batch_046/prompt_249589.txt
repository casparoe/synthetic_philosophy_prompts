I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of science. I am struck by what seem to be parallel conceptual inversions in Darwin's account of natural selection and Turing's account of computation: both describe mechanisms that generate outcomes—adaptive functional design and intelligent behavior, respectively—that ordinarily appear to require comprehension, yet neither mechanism itself understands anything. Compare these two inversions with particular attention to whether they truly eliminate comprehension or merely displace it into background conditions—the selective environment in Darwin's case, the designing programmer in Turing's. Which of the two inversions is more radical in this respect, and why? Finally, consider a modern system such as a large language model that produces coherent scientific explanations without possessing any internal model of physical causation: does this constitute a distinct third inversion, or does it collapse back into Turing's framework? I seek conceptual clarity rather than historical commentary.
