I spent three decades in experimental physics working on precision measurement and calibration protocols, and since retiring I have been trying to understand the philosophical problem of the epistemic regress—foundationalism, coherentism, and infinitism—not as an abstract logical puzzle but as a question about whether scientific justification can terminate in something genuinely non-arbitrary. In metrology we practice traceability: every measurement is justified by comparison to a standard, which is justified by comparison to a higher standard, until we eventually reach a conventional definition, such as fixing the meter by the speed of light, rather than an empirical foundation discovered in nature. This situation looks like an uneasy hybrid of coherentist circularity at the top, conventionalist stopping points that neither foundationalists nor infinitists comfortably accommodate, and practical closure requirements that seem to demand an end to the chain even if philosophy denies one.
