I'm constructing a taxonomy for a graduate seminar on meta-normative architecture, specifically on whether practical norms apply directly to actions or only indirectly through mediating structures—such as idealised beliefs, purified preferences, or procedural rules of reasoning. Rather than asking students to identify their allegiance by name, I want a decision tree that sorts positions by answers to concrete cases. The tree should begin with whether there exists any direct normative fact about an action independent of the agent's cognitive or conative state, then branch according to what mediates indirect normativity—epistemic idealisation, volitional coherence, or discursive rationality—and finally according to whether the idealised deliberation yields a determinate verdict or merely constrains permissible options.

Please design the instrument so that every internal node is a diagnostic question tied to a specific scenario: imagine an archivist who must choose between preserving a damaged but unique manuscript for private study and surrendering it to a public collection where expert restoration is possible but access becomes unrestricted, with the added complication that she cannot predict which future scholars will need it. The questions should be answerable without knowing philosophical labels, and the leaves must include at least direct normative realism; a Firth-style ideal-observer view mediated by fully informed sympathetic response; a Korsgaard-style constructivist view in which idealised practical reasoning constitutes the norm; a purely procedural rationalist view that locates normativity only in the coherence of reasoning rules rather than in outcomes; and a particularist or anti-theory position that denies both direct and systematically mediated norms. For any combination of answers that produces an unoccupied position—say, direct realism combined with volitional mediation—note briefly whether the gap stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere historical neglect. Finally, flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, explaining which shared commitment makes them collapse onto one another, and indicate which distinctions matter most if one's purpose is pedagogical clarification rather than historical classification.
