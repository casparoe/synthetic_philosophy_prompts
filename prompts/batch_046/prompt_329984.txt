(1) If a factor produces large-scale historical changes that no human agent at the time intended or could have substituted for, then it counts as a historical actor in the same sense we talk about Napoleon or the printing press. (2) The 1815 Tambora eruption caused the Year Without a Summer, reshaped European agriculture, and indirectely created conditions for Mary Shelley's Frankenstein and mass migration out of New England. (3) Nobody at the time intended that climate shock, and no human choice could have replaced it. (4) So Tambora is a historical actor. (5) Now take the gut microbiome of a specific general during Waterloo. It's particular bacterial populations influenced his inflammation levels and therefore his risk-assessment in a way he didn't intend and couldn't instantly substitute. (6) So by the exact same standard, those bacteria are also historical actors. (7) If we keep going, every solar flare, every dust mite, every slight temperature shift becomes an actor, and history collapses into an infinite causal list with no room for human agency. (8) But that can't be right, because we still want to say humans make choices that matter. (9) So I guess (1) is false? But then what's the principled reason climate counts and gut bacteria don't, without just picking favorites?
