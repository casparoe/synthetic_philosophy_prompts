I have been studying Carroll's dialogue between Achilles and the Tortoise, particularly the standard reply that modus ponens functions as a rule of inference rather than a premise, and therefore need not be included among the premises of an argument. Yet this reply seems only to relocate the difficulty: a formal system lists its inference rules separately, but applying any one of them apparently requires a further meta-rule governing that application, then a meta-meta-rule, and so on. Carroll's tortoise thus appears to demonstrate that no system can ever fully internalize its own rules of deduction—there is always an unjustified external step. Given this, why do philosophers of logic treat formal derivations as providing complete justifications for deductive conclusions, rather than admitting that every system ultimately depends on something outside logic itself? Furthermore, if we simply declare inference rules to be primitive, haven't we conceded the Tortoise's central point that deduction cannot be justified from within?
