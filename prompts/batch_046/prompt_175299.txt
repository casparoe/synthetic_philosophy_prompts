I'm preparing notes for a workshop on normative structure and would like a consistency audit of my current package before I circulate it. My commitments are these:

(1) In practical reason I accept indirect normativity: the right action is not the case-optimal one but the one prescribed by the practical code I would rationally endorse under conditions of full transparency, coherence, and information—where the endorsement relation itself fixes normative status, not merely serves as an instrumental heuristic.

(2) I take idealized deliberation to be first-personal and value-preserving: the idealization removes ignorance, error, and weakness of will, but retains my actual normative commitments and psychological constitution, so that the deliverance remains recognizably my own.

(3) These indirect norms have non-derivative authority: even when I can see that violating the endorsed code in a particular case would yield a better outcome, the violation is wrong, because authority derives from the rational endorsement relation rather than local consequences.

(4) In epistemic domains, however, I reject indirect normativity and hold direct evidentialism: belief formation should respond directly to evidential force in particular cases, not be mediated by general belief-forming policies that are only indirectly truth-conducive.

(5) Furthermore, I think the direct/indirect distinction collapses under full idealization: an agent free of all computational and motivational limitations has no need for mediating policies, since her first-order responses just constitute the globally optimal pattern.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be; rank the real tensions by severity; for each, indicate what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs; and then tell me which single revision, if I had to make one, would clean up the most conflicts with the least damage to the overall picture.
