I'm drafting a dissertation chapter on predictive processing and have constructed the following argument, which I take to be reductive in a damaging way. A senior colleague insists it is sound and that the collapse it describes is a feature, not a bug. I believe there is a hidden slide, but I cannot locate it precisely.

(1) The free energy principle holds that all adaptive biological systems minimize variational free energy with respect to a generative model of their environment.
(2) In predictive processing, perceptual inference is the brain's mechanism for minimizing prediction error about hidden causes.
(3) Epistemic justification for a belief is, at minimum, possessing the representation that best explains one's available sensory and cognitive inputs relative to one's constraints.
(4) In this framework, "best explains" is defined technically: the representation that maximizes model evidence, which is equivalent to minimizing expected free energy.
(5) Therefore, any perceptual state that minimizes free energy for an agent is, by definition, the agent's best-available representation — and hence epistemically justified.
(6) But free energy is minimized not only by veridical models. Through active inference, an agent can reshape its environment to match predictions; through precision-weighting adjustments, it can suppress contradictory inputs; and through allostatic niche construction, it can restructure its long-term informational environment to eliminate surprising stimuli altogether. A systematically delusional system that survives by avoiding disconfirmation and down-weighting counterevidence maintains a stable, low-free-energy state.
(7) So predictive processing makes epistemic justification coextensive with adaptive self-regulation: if a belief-state survives because it minimizes free energy, it is justified, regardless of accuracy. This proves too much — it eliminates the normative gap between rational belief formation and mere biological persistence, and makes epistemically blameless any stable delusional system.
