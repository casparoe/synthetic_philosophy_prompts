In my intergenerational justice seminar we've been stuck for months on whether climate obligations are really obligations to future people or just obligations about future conditions. One camp treats carbon budgets like a rolling commons: each generation inherits both atmospheric space and the debt, so the wrong is breaking the chain of stewardship, not harming specific individuals. The other camp says thats mystical — obligations need bearers, and since the non-identity problem means different policies produce different people, you cant have a victim, so climate justice collapses into general beneficence. They share the same IPCC figures, they agree suffering is bad, but the stewardship side keeps talking about intergenerational covenant and the other side keeps asking to whom? Nothing moves. Is this a metaphysical disagreement about whether obligations can be relational without being personal, a verbal dispute over stewardship, or are they answering different questions — one about institutional continuity, one about distributive harm? And given your diagnosis, could reframing climate justice as institutional maintenance rather than victim-based correction actually resolve anything, or does it just rename the problem?
