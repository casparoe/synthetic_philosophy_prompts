I'm preparing a philosophical profile for a seminar on personal identity through technological transformation, and I'd like a consistency audit before I present. My position combines standard psychological-continuity commitments with some more specific claims about substrate independence and the metaphysics of gradual brain replacement. Specifically, I hold: (a) that personal identity over time consists in appropriate causal and informational continuity among mental states, not in the persistence of a particular biological substrate or a non-derivative metaphysical self; (b) that phenomenal consciousness is substrate-independent in principle—if a synthetic neural architecture replicates the relevant functional organization of my cortex, there will be something it is like to be that system; (c) that gradual, neuron-by-neuron replacement of biological tissue with functionally equivalent synthetic analogues preserves my identity, by analogy to the ship of Theseus applied to the brain; (d) that destructive scanning and uploading—where my brain is destroyed to instantiate a perfect simulation elsewhere—constitutes my death and the creation of a replica, not survival; (e) that there is no "further fact" of identity beyond these continuity relations, so branching cases (two equally continuous copies) leave it genuinely indeterminate which, if either, is me; (f) that the temporal speed of replacement is metaphysically irrelevant—whether the process takes ten years or ten seconds should not change whether I survive, as long as functional integration is maintained; and (g) that even if gradual replacement involves brief "maintenance windows" in which large cortical regions are simultaneously offline and replaced in batches, leaving temporary islands of biological and silicon tissue without direct interface, there is no intermittent death, since identity tracks the overall trajectory of functional restoration rather than moment-to-moment continuity.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look problematic, and how severely. In particular, I want to know whether my rejection of destructive upload in (d) can be sustained alongside (f) and (b), and whether my denial of further facts in (e) removes the resources I need to ground that rejection. Also check whether (g) conflicts with (a) in a way that requires revising either, or whether that tension dissolves once we distinguish continuous mental activity from continuous causal-informational architecture. Rank the tensions by severity, identify the cheapest revision for each, and tell me whether there is a single change that would clean up the most problems without abandoning my core physicalism or my commitment to substrate-independent consciousness.
