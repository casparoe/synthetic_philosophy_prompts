For a feature on the mechanics of knowledge attribution under institutional pressure, please compare pragmatic encroachment—the thesis that practical stakes partly determine whether a belief counts as knowledge—with epistemic contextualism, which locates variability in the conversational standards of the attributor rather than in the subject's practical situation. Rather than rehearsing the standard taxonomy, isolate the divergence through a paired clinical scenario engineered to hold the evidence constant while varying stakes and social context independently. In Case One, an emergency-room physician asserts that a patient knows she is not allergic to a particular antibiotic, based solely on weak family-history evidence, because in this high-stakes setting the catastrophic cost of withholding treatment if she is wrong makes false belief far more dangerous than unnecessary administration; the risk is sharply asymmetrical—borne mainly by the patient—while the conversational setting is routine, with no skeptical interlocutor present. In Case Two, the same physician, holding identical evidence about the same patient, makes the identical assertion in a casual staff lounge where skeptical colleagues have just raised rigorous epistemic standards, yet the practical stakes—treatment outcomes, liability, patient welfare—remain exactly as they were in Case One. For each case, state precisely what pragmatic encroachment predicts about whether the physician, or the patient, knows, and explain whether shifting the conversational context without shifting stakes alters the knowledge relation on this view. Then do the same for contextualism: does the lounge setting change the truth conditions of the knowledge claim even though stakes are flat, and how does the view treat the asymmetrical risk distribution in Case One? Next, identify the structural feature that actually drives the divergence—whether it is the location of practical risk, the salience of error possibilities, or the social dynamics of attribution—and show what happens if that feature is removed: if stakes and conversational standards are both held constant across the two settings, do the two views collapse into the same verdict? Finally, assess whether a defender of either view can consistently redescribe the opposing case in her own theoretical vocabulary without conceding the divergence, or whether the pairing reveals an irreducible difference in what each theory takes knowledge to be.
