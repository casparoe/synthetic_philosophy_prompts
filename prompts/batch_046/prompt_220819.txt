Here is the exam question that was given to students:

Consider a modified version of Derek Parfit's hitchhiker thought experiment. A driver offers to take you to a distant city only if she can scan your brain and determine that, upon arrival, you will voluntarily transfer $1,000 to her account. The scan detects your true intention with 85% accuracy and is otherwise unbiased. Once you have arrived, there is no further enforcement mechanism, so retaining the $1,000 is strictly better for you at that later time. Before departure, you may take a cheap, safe commitment pill that causally fixes your preference for paying upon arrival; the pill causes a temporary headache valued at approximately $50 in disutility. Analyze whether it is rational for you to take the pill and intend to pay. In your answer, explain how this case illustrates the broader problem of rational irrationality—specifically, whether it can be rational at the time of decision to bring about a state of affairs in which you will subsequently act against your then-best reasons.

Below is one student's answer, reproduced verbatim:

Yes, it is rational to take the pill. The driver uses evidential reasoning: she picks you up because she has evidence that you will pay. By taking the pill, you make it more likely that the scan detects an intention to pay, since the pill correlates with the mental state the scanner is looking for. Once you arrive, it is rational not to pay because the driver is already there and you can keep the $1,000. But because you took the pill, you will pay anyway, which is irrational at that moment. This shows Parfit's point that rationality is time-relative: you should be irrational in the future to be rational now. The $50 headache is a small price compared to the $1,000, so you should still take the pill. Because the scan is only 85% accurate, there is a 15% chance she rejects you even if you take the pill. Therefore, the expected value of taking the pill is 0.85 times the value of the ride minus $50, which is still positive if the ride is worth more than about $59. So the answer depends on how much you value the ride.
