i work on an ai governance board and we keep getting drafts of 'explanantion protocols' for superintelligent systems that have to talk humans through high-stakes choices. the risk isn't just lying — it's that they could restructure your beliefs just by being extremely clear and coherent, even with no bad intent.

i want the 'friction and reversibility' principle turned into a repeatable audit procedure. the principle says an acceptable message must contain: (a) a 'friction point' — one concrete claim the listener can verify independently without trusting the ai's authority; and (b) it must pass the 'reversibility check' — if the listener knew the ai was incentivised to reach the opposite conclusion, they'd still find the reasoning sound.

write the step-by-step checklist my auditors can run in about 15 minutes: what to ask in order, what counts as a pass or fail, where we stop and reject, and where we have to hand off to a human ethicist because it's a values call not a procedure call. apply it to three scenarios: an ai telling clinicians to halt a drug trial; an ai arguing for a specific constitutional reform; and an ai convincing a scientist that her own published theory is flawed. make sure one of these gives the procedure a 'pass' that the board will still find deeply uncomfortable. then say exactly what kinds of persuasive harm this checklist will miss.

use british english, no bibliographies, and flag the steps that are really just judgement dressed up as rules.
