I've been reading about von Neumann's idea that self-replicating tech can outlast us and now I'm stuck in this argument my roommate threw at me. (1) Von Neumann's automata survive not because they preserve any original parts but because each generation copies it's design and functional role; survival here is just information continuity. (2) If that's enough for technology to survive a total physical replacement, then survival in general can't depend on keeping the same matter. (3) People also survive despite every cell being replaced, so human identity must also be information or functional continuity. (4) So if you destroy my body and build a perfect functional duplicate elsewhere, I survive in exactly the same way a von Neumann probe survives after rebuilding itself post-crash. (5) But then destroying every human and replacing them with perfect copies wouldn't be extinction; it would be routine maintenance. (6) That's clearly wrong — genocide plus replication is still genocide. (7) So either von Neumann's notion of technological survival is incoherent, or it doesn't apply to people, but both options feel expensive. Where does the slip happen? Which step is the weakest link, and what does giving it up cost? If I have to pick, I'd keep (1) and (4), but I want to know the price.
