I cover science and ideas for a national publication, and I serve on the Review Board of the Horizon Foundation's Program on Human Flourishing and Economic Transition. By the end of this quarter we must reach a recommendation on the Grandchildren Trial, a proposed five-year, multi-site pilot that would reduce standard work hours to twenty per week without cutting pay, pairing the reduction with a structured civic curriculum. The proposal is framed as an empirical test of Keynes's well-known argument that rising productivity eventually frees people from the pursuit of money for survival, allowing them to turn to the art of life rather than remaining driven by economic necessity. The applicants assume that once absolute needs are met, leisure will naturally become a space for intellectual and cultural flourishing. A critical internal review challenges this: it argues that Keynes underestimated the persistence of status-driven relative needs, and that modern workers have internalized norms linking self-worth to productivity so deeply that unstructured free time produces anxiety rather than the good life. The review further distinguishes between negative leisure, which is merely the absence of labor, and active practices that actually cultivate human flourishing, suggesting the pilot risks delivering the former while claiming the latter. The board must decide whether to recommend full funding, conditional approval with specific safeguards, or rejection with an alternative path. Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before our decision meeting: it should recommend a concrete course of action within a clear time frame, engage seriously with the strongest consideration on the losing side, and be written so that board members without philosophical training can follow the reasoning. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the memo should stand on its own reasoning.
