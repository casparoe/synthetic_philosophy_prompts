I'm preparing a graduate seminar on incommensurable values and hard choices, and I've assigned William Styron's Sophie's Choice as our central text. The novel's pivotal scene—Sophie, upon arrival at Auschwitz, being forced by an SS doctor to choose which of her two children will be sent immediately to the gas chamber—presents what looks like a paradigmatic case of genuinely incomparable goods: the lives of her son and daughter are not merely equal but resistant to any common metric, so that any selection is arbitrary and leaves an irreducible moral residue. But I want the discussion to move past the obvious and probe something more philosophically unsettling. Specifically, I want the students to consider whether Styron's narrative architecture performs a subtle form of "retrospective commensuration": by weaving Sophie's subsequent alcoholism, her fragile relationship with Stingo, her compulsive storytelling, and her eventual suicide into a continuous causal thread that originates in that single moment, the novel may implicitly treat the choice as having possessed a hidden determinable answer that she failed to locate, or at least as having generated a quantifiable moral debt that the remainder of the plot is structured to repay. Read the novel through this precise lens. Does the text genuinely preserve the incommensurability of the children's lives at the instant of decision, or does it gradually convert that incommensurability into a measurable moral economy of guilt, redemption, and narrative punishment? Which major scenes—the revelation to Stingo, her refusal to save herself through him, the ending—support one interpretation over the other, and where does the philosophical distinction between "value incomparability" and "tragic choice under radical uncertainty" actually illuminate something about Sophie's psychology that abstract argument alone misses? I want a critical reading that takes both Styron's craft and the philosophical debate seriously, that does not reduce the novel to an illustration of Ruth Chang-style hard choices, and that concludes by identifying what the novel contributes to our thinking about incommensurable values that a purely conceptual analysis cannot capture. Please write your response in American English.
