A colleague objects that M, if genuinely present, should also produce symptoms S1 and S2, which the patient lacks. The clinician responds not by testing M further, but by adding a condition — M produces S1 and S2 only in the presence of factor F, which is absent here — where F is selected solely because it blocks the unwanted predictions. Second instance: in historical explanation, a scholar accounts for one anomalous document by invoking a secretive faction, and when it is noted that the faction’s behaviour should have left traces in other archives, the scholar introduces a hidden rule that the faction concealed its actions precisely in those other contexts.
