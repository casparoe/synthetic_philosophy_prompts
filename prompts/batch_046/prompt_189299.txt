I have spent considerable time with the dialogue Lewis Carroll wrote between Achilles and the Tortoise, in which the Tortoise accepts premises but refuses to apply the rule that allows one to derive the conclusion from them, and when Achilles supplies that rule as an additional premise, the Tortoise demands yet another rule to apply that one, generating an endless chain. It strikes me that Carroll has shown deduction can never be fully grounded, because every justification requires a further justification. This led me to wonder whether deduction should be understood on the model of coherentism — the view that beliefs are justified by how well they fit together with other beliefs rather than by resting on a foundation — with logical rules like the one that lets you move from premises to conclusion being justified only by their coherence with our broader reasoning practices, rather than being treated as primitive axioms. I also find myself connecting Carroll's regress to Gödel's result that formal systems capable of arithmetic cannot demonstrate their own consistency: does the Tortoise's refusal reveal a parallel kind of incompleteness, one that applies not merely to arithmetic but to the very rules of inference, suggesting that no logical system can ever fully justify its own application?
