I was arguing with my study group about demandingness objections and one friend walked me into this reductio that I can't find the exit from. Here is the chain. (1) Consequentialism is false because it demands too much — like requiring you to donate most of your income until you're barely above global poverty. (2) For this to work there must be a "cost ceiling" marking what's reasonable for morality to ask. (3) Any specific ceiling is arbitrary: why 10% and not 9%? (4) So defenders invoke an agent-centered prerogative: morality can't demand sacrifices that destroy what's central to an agent's own life and projects. (5) But "central to the agent's life" is either read subjectively — whatever the agent strongly cares about — or objectively — what rational agents need to flourish. (6) If subjective, then anyone can claim that even not murdering is too burdonsome when they're really angry, since restraining rage conflicts with a central emotional project. (7) If objective, then we need an account of human flourishing to set the ceiling, and consequentialism can just use that same account to measure demands against total well-being — which means consequentialism isn't uniquely demanding after all. (8) So the objection either collapses into a subjectivity that makes morality toothless, or it borrows objective standards that dissolve the problem. I really want to keep (1) and (4) if I can. Which step is the weak link? Explain why it looked okay and what rejecting it costs. Please respond in exaclty five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro and no conclusion.
