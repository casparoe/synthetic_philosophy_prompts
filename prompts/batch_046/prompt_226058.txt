A friend who read the Guide sumarized Maimonides on sacrifice like this: "Maimonides thought animal sacrifice wasn't something God actually wanted, but was a kind of pedagogical concession—since the Israelites had been surrounded by cultures that worshipped through slaughter, God channeled that impulse toward Himself so people wouldn't worship idols. The ideal worship is prayer without any blood, and once people are educated they shouldn't need sacrifice at all. That's why he was okay with the Temple's destruction—he saw it as progress away from primitive ritual. He also tied this to his view that animals don't have rational souls, so killing them for worship isn't a moral harm the way harming a human would be, which means there's no tension between divine command and animal welfare." I think he mostly got it right but some parts feel like modern spin. How fair is this? Break it down: what's accurate to the Guide, what's a simplification, and what's a misreading—especially the part about the Temple's destruction and whether his view on animal souls actually does the work he claims?
