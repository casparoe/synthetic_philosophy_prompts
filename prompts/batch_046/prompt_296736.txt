I have recently been asked to lead a single ninety-minute session for a mixed theology reading group—graduate students and one philosopher colleague—on Kierkegaard’s notion of the teleological suspension of the ethical, as developed in *Fear and Trembling*. The subject has become personally urgent since a family crisis left me wondering whether a private conviction could ever legitimately override public moral duty; I want a rigorous teaching plan rather than a sermon. The group has already worked through the first two problemata, so they grasp the tension between the universal and the individual, but they consistently conflate suspension with simple ethical abolition. Plan the explanatory arc for me: in what precise order must the remaining conceptual pieces be introduced so that the suspension reads as a structured, teleological movement rather than nihilism or fanaticism; identify the single carrying example—presumably Abraham and Isaac—and specify exactly what it must possess (the silence, the paradox, the return to the ethical) and what it must lack (explicit justification, allegorical softness) if it is not to collapse into either endorsement of violence or mere metaphor; map the misconceptions that will arise in the order they will arise—particularly that Kierkegaard sanctions child sacrifice and that the ethical is permanently destroyed—indicating at which point in the sequence each must be intercepted and how; propose one concrete question I can ask at the end that distinguishes genuine comprehension from mere discomfort or agreement; and state clearly what should be openly refused at this level—perhaps the pseudonymous authorship or the Hegelian dialectic behind the universal—and how to frame that refusal without seeming evasive. Please answer in British English, divided into the following sections: ‘Sequencing and dependencies’ (around 250 words), ‘The carrying example’ (around 200 words), ‘Misconception map and interventions’ (around 250 words), ‘Check for understanding’ (around 100 words), and ‘What to leave out’ (around 150 words).
