I have recently been asked to assess an essay that invokes Gödel's first incompleteness theorem to claim that any formal legal code necessarily contains propositions that are neither provable nor disprovable within the code itself, and that judicial discretion is therefore not a drafting failure but a structural inevitability. Before I can judge whether this is a valid inference or merely an analogical sleight of hand, I need the full specification of what a sound extra-mathematical application of Gödel's results must actually accomplish. Please lay out the desiderata that any argument applying Gödel's theorems outside pure mathematics—whether to jurisprudence, cognitive science, physics, or theology—must satisfy in order to count as rigorous rather than metaphorical. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or underlying consideration: for example, demonstrating that the target domain admits formalization within a system that is both consistent and recursively axiomatizable; distinguishing syntactic incompleteness from semantic incompleteness; clarifying that the unprovability of a specific Gödel sentence within one formalization does not entail a corresponding epistemic limitation on human or institutional knowledge; and addressing whether the domain permits categorical characterization, since second-order resources with standard semantics can escape the scope of the first theorem. Mark which of these are genuinely non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps—such as those who require physical computability, determinism, or the literal truth of every statement in the intended interpretation. Then examine whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, particularly the pairing of demanding full formal capture of a domain within a recursively enumerable axiomatic system with demanding that the same system be both consistent and complete in its intended interpretation, since such a pairing may render the project self-defeating from the outset. Finally, identify the single requirement that, in your judgment, eliminates the largest share of popular extra-mathematical Gödel-arguments, including those advanced in discussions of artificial intelligence and legal theory. Keep the weight on the criteria and their justification rather than on a scorecard of named positions; if particular camps are mentioned, do so without bibliographic apparatus.
