As part of a workshop I am designing on the philosophy of law, I need you to operationalize the theoretical problems surrounding legal self-reference—specifically, the paradoxes that arise when a rule attempts to govern its own conditions of validity or amendment—into a practical decision procedure. The practitioner is a senior legislative counsel for a regional constitutional convention. She reviews proposed charter amendments every week; she holds a law degree but has no formal training in logic; she must sign off within thirty minutes; and her approval is required before any proposal reaches the floor. The stakes are high: an erroneous sign-off can embed an irreconcilable conflict into the charter, yet excessive caution stalls democratic revision.

Your deliverable should be a reusable, step-by-step audit—presented as a flowchart in words—that the counsel can apply to any proposed amendment clause. The audit must be grounded in the conceptual distinction between direct syntactic self-reference (a clause citing its own article or section number), indirect semantic self-reference (a clause altering the validity conditions of the very system that contains it), and meta-systemic reflexivity (a clause attempting to regulate the rules that govern meta-rules, such as this audit itself). You must preserve these three layers, because collapsing them produces false negatives.

The procedure should include:
1. A preliminary filter (time: under five minutes) that sorts proposals into clear-pass, clear-fail, and ambiguous.
2. A three-layer reflexivity test (textual, systemic, meta-systemic), with explicit criteria for each layer and a decision rule for when layers conflict.
3. A conflict-resolution protocol for cases where layers yield contradictory results, marking precisely where judgment cannot be eliminated and where the counsel must exercise discretionary sign-off with a written justification.
4. A statement of what the procedure cannot do—specifically, which paradoxes it can flag but not resolve, and which forms of hidden circularity escape detection.

Then apply the procedure to three hypothetical charter amendments:
(a) A proposal to lower the amendment threshold from two-thirds to simple majority, enacted under the current two-thirds rule.
(b) A popular "entrenchment sunset" clause that automatically nullifies all prior amendment procedures after ten years, but exempts itself from nullification; the convention expects this to pass easily.
(c) A technically complex proposal that inserts a new meta-article governing "all future audits of self-reference," without specifying whether that meta-article is itself subject to audit.

For case (b), the procedure should yield an answer that the counsel—who favors democratic flexibility—will find unwelcome. For case (c), the procedure should stall and require discretionary judgment. In each application, state the result, the reasoning path through the three layers, and any required justification.

Write in formal academic prose appropriate for a philosophy of law seminar, but ensure every step is executable by the practitioner described. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not ask me to verify sources.
