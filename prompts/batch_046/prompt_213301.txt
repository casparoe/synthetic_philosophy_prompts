I'm enrolled in an advanced philosophy seminar that takes Quine's essay Two Dogmas of Empiricism as its focal point, and I'm designing a diagnostic instrument for my final project. Rather than asking respondents which philosophical school they belong to, I want a decision tree that sorts people by concrete commitments about revision, meaning, and theoretical change.

Please construct a taxonomy with four branching nodes, each expressed as a specific case or claim that splits positions without using the standard labels. The first node should ask whether one would ever revise a basic logical law—say, the law of non-contradiction—if it systematically conflicted with well-confirmed empirical predictions in a restricted domain. The second should ask whether mathematical truths, such as the infinity of primes, are in principle revisable for the sake of overall theoretical simplicity. The third should ask whether the meaning of an ordinary term like "bachelor" is fully fixed by observable patterns of linguistic behavior and can therefore shift with empirical usage. The fourth should ask whether there is any meaningful difference between changing one's beliefs within a language and changing the language itself.

From the answer combinations, map out the leaves: hard Quinean holism in which every statement is revisable; moderate holism that protects logic and mathematics only for pragmatic reasons of centrality; a restricted Carnapian conventionalism that treats explicit linguistic rules as analytic within a framework but allows framework change; a domain-local analytic view that permits unrevisable truths inside specific disciplines while denying a global analytic-synthetic boundary; and the combination that treats logic as absolutely unrevisable yet treats all word-meanings as purely empirical—note this branch and briefly diagnose why it is unoccupied (incoherence, instability, or merely unexplored). Each leaf should carry the view's standard name or "unnamed" and one sentence describing its most awkward practical commitment. Flag any positions the tree cannot cleanly separate, such as moderate holism and domain-local analyticity on certain answers. Do not include citations, bibliographies, page references, or reading lists; the instrument must be usable by someone with no direct exposure to Quine. Present the result as a flowchart-style tree with branches labeled by the splitting claim.
