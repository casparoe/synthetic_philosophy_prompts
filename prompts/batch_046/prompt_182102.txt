Write a sonnet (14 lines, rhyme scheme ABAB CDCD EFEF GG) about Borges's denial of time—specifically, the claim that temporal succession depends on a continuous self, and that once the self is dissolved into discrete, unowned mental states, the distinction between past and present collapses into an eternal, unexperienced simultaneity. In the octave, establish the familiar picture: memory as a thread stitching moments into a line, giving the illusion that one subject travels from yesterday into today. At the volta in line 9, turn to the dissolution: if there is no enduring “I” but only successive perceptions with no bearer, then nothing actually carries the past forward. In the sestet, show the consequence—“before” and “after” become adjacent rooms in a labyrinth with no corridor, a structure walked by no one—and let the rhyme scheme mirror this collapse, so that the closing GG couplet sounds like a loop closing rather than a resolution advancing. The couplet should state the paradox plainly: eternity is not infinite duration but duration made redundant, because no one remains to count its passing. Write the poem so that a reader familiar with Borges’s argument recognizes its accuracy, and a reader unfamiliar with it still receives a coherent, self-contained poem.
