I'm trying to work out whose values an aligned system should actually serve, and I'm stuck on a tension between liberal neutrality and value aggregation that feels like it should be upstream of everything else but maybe isn't. If you take neutrality seriously — that the system shouldn't endorse any one comprehensive doctrine — then standard aggregation tools like averaging utility or using social welfare functions seem to smuggle in a thick metaphysical commitment, usually something like preference-satisfaction or hedonism, just by assuming values are interpersonally comparable. But if you avoid that by going deliberative or democratic, you risk collapsing into majoritarianism that violates neutrality for minority worldviews, and you still haven't solved what to do when doctrines are genuinely incommensurable.

So I'm wondering which open questions here are actually worth a small conceptual research group spending years on, versus which are philosophical window dressing. Is resolving incommensurability — whether values from radically different worldviews can be mapped onto any shared scale — a prerequisite for designing aggregation protocols, or can we build mechanisms that stay agnostic about comparability? And how does that relate to recursive value change: if the system helps users refine their prefferences over time, does liberal neutrality require it to stay passive about that refinement, or does neutrality apply differently when values are being co-formed rather than statically reported? There's also this overlooked angle about value dynamics — neutrality not applied to fixed inputs but to the process by which human and system values evolve together — that I think gets ignored because everyone treats aggregation as a static snapshot problem.

Could you give me a dependency-aware prioritization? Which two or three conceptual questions should philosophers and conceptual ML people tackle first, in terms of what's upstream of what, what's tractable without running large-scale feedback experiments, and what you'd deprioritize because even a clear answer wouldn't change the mechanism design? I want the reasoning, not just a list — specifically, where the cruxes are, what evidence over the next few years would move something up or down, and whether that dynamics-of-co-formation line I mentioned deserves more attention than it currently recieve.
