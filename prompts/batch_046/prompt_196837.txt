As a novelist drafting a scene in which a physicist and a linguist argue about whether notation can alter the matter that bears it, I have been working through a conceptual puzzle I intend to present—not resolve—to a small reading circle of fellow writers who have no background in philosophy of mind. The puzzle turns on what I have taken to calling the careenium: a hypothetical symbolic architecture in which rules encoded at one level are said to direct the motion of physical tokens I call simmballs, even though both levels are constituted by the same substrate. The question I want them to feel as live rather than academic is whether these symbols actually push the substrate around, or whether that way of speaking merely redescribes the substrate’s own dynamics in a vocabulary that smuggles in causation. Before I bring this into the workshop, I need a teaching plan rather than an explanation itself.

Please lay out the dependency order: what idea must already be in place—perhaps the distinction between description and mechanism, or the problem of downward causation—before I can introduce the careenium and its simmballs without the group dismissing it as either mysticism or tautology. Identify the single concrete feature of the simmball dynamics that should carry the entire session, and specify what that feature must possess and what it must lack so that it functions as a genuine example rather than a disguised conclusion. Map the misconceptions I should expect in the sequence they will arise—likely that symbols are ghostly pilots riding the substrate, or that they are mere decorative labels with no causal weight—and indicate exactly where in the arc each should be headed off. Suggest one check I can use at the end to determine whether the group has grasped the tension itself rather than simply adopted one side, and be explicit about which related distinctions—perhaps multiple realizability, implementation relations, or token-identity theories—I should openly refuse to introduce at this stage and say so. The audience is intelligent but non-specialist; the aim is not to settle whether symbols push the substrate, but to make the group understand why the answer is not obvious.
