Write a Petrarchan sonnet—octave in ABBAABBA, sestet in CDECDE, with a decisive volta at line nine—that stages the dispute over whether large language models are proper subjects of Daniel Dennett’s intentional stance. In the octave, develop the instrumentalist position with precision: attributing beliefs, desires, and intentions to an LLM is warranted whenever the attribution yields predictive and explanatory gains, regardless of internal architecture, just as the stance applies to thermostats and chess programs. At the volta, introduce the specific objection that current LLMs lack the integrated, world-coupled behavioral control that makes rationality a genuine design constraint rather than a post-hoc interpretive gloss—namely, they generate tokens without a unified sensory-motor loop, without persistent goals that survive across conversational contexts, and without the kind of vulnerability to empirical error that characterizes belief-holding organisms. In the sestet, let the instrumentalist reply that the stance is graded and pragmatic, not binary, and that predictive success across contexts is sufficient warrant; but do not allow either side to win. End with a couplet that structurally refuses synthesis—perhaps by rhyming “stance” with “chance” or “predict” with “convict”—so that the rhyme itself enacts the instability of the attribution. Use accurate philosophical vocabulary (intentional stance, physical stance, rationality constraint, world-coupling) but do not cite specific papers, page numbers, or authors beyond Dennett, and do not include a bibliography or reading list. The poem should be rigorous enough that a philosopher of mind recognizes the exact argument being made, and formally strange enough that it is not a generic meditation on “AI consciousness.”
