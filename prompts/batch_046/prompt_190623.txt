I am composing a philosophical novel concerned with metaphysical identity, and I need a short fiction piece that dramatizes the problem of origin essentialism—the view that you are necessarily tied to the particular sperm and egg from which you actually originated, so that a different zygote would not have yielded you but someone else, or no one at all. Rather than a generic thought experiment, I want the concept rendered through intimate, realistic narrative. Please write a short story (approximately 800 to 1,000 words) about a woman in her thirties, raised with no knowledge of her biological parents, who discovers through an ancestry database that she was conceived via IVF with donor gametes and that her legal father is not her genetic father. The story should center on her attempt to imagine a counterfactual: had the clinic selected a different sperm sample, or had the egg been fertilized by a different donor, would the same person—she—have come into existence?

To prevent the narrative from collapsing into a standard nature-versus-nurture debate, embed a niche conceptual distinction: treat the original zygote as a "modal anchor," a singular causal node that fixes personal identity across possible worlds, and have her encounter a rival view—call it "composition essentialism"—according to which she is essentially constituted by her present psychological traits and bodily continuity, making her origin historically contingent but metaphysically inessential. The tension should unfold in a concrete scene: perhaps a conversation with her non-biological father, who holds the essentialist view, or with a genetic counselor who defends the compositional alternative. The prose should be literary, precise, and free of technical jargon for its own sake.

After the story, provide a single section titled "Commentary on the philosophical architecture" (approximately 300 to 400 words). Within it, address the following in order:
1. Which philosophical positions are dramatized—specifically origin essentialism about persons versus the compositional alternative—and whether the story fairly represents both.
2. Why the IVF/donor-gamete framing was chosen over natural conception, and what metaphysical feature it isolates.
3. How the "modal anchor" metaphor operates in the narrative, and whether it illuminates or distorts the underlying essentialist claim.
4. One subtle misrepresentation of origin essentialism that a popular account might commit (for example, conflating biological origin with genetic determinism), and how the story attempts to avoid it.

Please do not include bibliographies, citations, or references to specific scholarly texts; this is intended for my own compositional use, not for academic publication.
