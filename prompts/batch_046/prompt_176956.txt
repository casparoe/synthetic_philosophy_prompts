Student answer: Nagel's four categories are resultant luck, concerning outcomes; circumstantial luck, concerning the situations one happens to face; constitutive luck, concerning one's character and temperament; and causal luck, concerning whether our actions are determined by factors outside our control. Williams uses the case of Gauguin leaving his family to pursue painting in Tahiti, arguing that we judge Gauguin partly by whether he succeeds, even though that success depends on luck. Williams calls the appropriate emotional response agent-regret, which resembles guilt but lacks the belief that one has done something wrong.
