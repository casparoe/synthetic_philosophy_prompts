Write a polished dialogue between two philosophers, Clara and Elias, on whether Frege's late political diary—whose entries include nationalist and antisemitic sentiments—has any legitimate bearing on the interpretation of his logicism and anti-psychologism.

Clara holds an isolationist position: she maintains that the objectivity of thoughts (Gedanken) in Frege's system is structurally independent of the thinker's biographical commitments, and she presses Elias to identify a specific formal feature of Frege's concept-script or a particular inference in his logic that actually depends on the racial ontology hinted at in the diary. She argues that without such a formal dependency, the diary is at best a biographical anomaly and at worst a moral indictment, but never a philosophical counterexample.

Elias advances a genealogy of exclusion: he contends that Frege's distinction between objective thoughts and subjective representations implicitly carries a normative hierarchy about who possesses the authority—what he terms logical sovereignty—to grasp universal contents, and he suggests that this hierarchy is not accidentally parallel to the categories in the diary but is philosophically continuous with them. He further argues that the formal system's silence regarding which agents count as rational is not neutral but is itself a philosophical decision that reflects this hierarchy.

The dialogue must exhibit genuine engagement: Clara should directly challenge Elias's claim of continuity by distinguishing context of discovery from context of justification and demanding concrete textual evidence within Frege's logical writings; Elias should respond not by dismissing that distinction but by arguing that the very framing of logical objectivity as accessible to rational beings without prior qualification masks a prior determination of who qualifies as rational, and he should ask Clara whether her insulation thesis could survive if Frege's private criteria for logical authority mirrored the exclusions in his diary. Each speaker should concede at least one specific weakness or unresolved tension in their own view by the end. Maintain a formal, precise tone throughout, and avoid appeals to bibliographies, citations, or obscure secondary literature.
