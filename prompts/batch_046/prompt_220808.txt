me and my roommate got into a weird argument last night about whether you can ever bootstrap your way out of not knowing if your own mental shortcuts are any good. not the usual car or table examples—something more specific.

(1) i said if you use some gut heuristic to guess whether strangers are lying, and then you check your guesses against what actually happens, and you're right like 80% of the time, you've now got real knowledge that your shortcut works. that's just learning from experience, not bootstrapping.

(2) she said that's exactly the bootstrapping problem, or "easy knowledge" about your own reliability. you're using the outputs of the heuristic to validate the heuristic. the world lining up could be luck, or a self-fulfilling pattern, or just seleciton bias in which guesses you remember.

(3) i pushed back that there's a real distinction between validating an external instrument and validating an internal heuristic. with an external tool you need prior knowledge that the calibration standard works. but with an internal shortcut, the "check" is just more cognition, not an independant standard—so it's coherence, not circularity in the bad sense.

(4) she said that misses the point because coherence isn't truth-tracking unless you already have some anchor. then she brought up a weird case: imagine you have a "taste-defeator" mechanism where you automatically downgrade judgments that feel too hasty. you calibrate that defeator using your past judgments, including ones the defeator already filtered out. now you've got a hidden loop where the filter validates itself through its own filtered outputs. she said this is bootstrapping with extra steps, and worse because the loop is invisible.
