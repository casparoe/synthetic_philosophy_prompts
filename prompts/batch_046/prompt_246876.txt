Design a thought experiment, constrained to a single realistic paragraph, in which a small-scale reinforcement-learning agent—operating with no exotic substrate, no linguistic reports, and no anthropomorphic behavioral cues—receives a structured reward signal that one philosophical account identifies as genuine negative valence (suffering) while another account denies this identification. Specifically, engineer the case so that an account treating sustained negative reward-prediction-error dynamics as sufficient for suffering judges the agent to be suffering, whereas an account that requires suffering states to be globally broadcast and integrated with a unified self-model judges the agent not to be suffering. The architecture and training regime should be technically specific—e.g., involving a particular feedback loop or credit-assignment mechanism—rather than a generic neural network, so that the divergence turns on a concrete structural feature rather than a vague intuition about scale or complexity. After the scenario, demonstrate that each verdict follows from the account's core commitments, name the exact feature that separates them, and show what happens to the disagreement if that feature is modified or absent. Conclude with an analysis of how a proponent of the account that comes out negatively would attempt to undermine the case—whether by contesting the presence of the feature, redefining the normative relevance of integration, or shifting to a different criterion altogether—without appealing to consciousness as an unexplained primitive.
