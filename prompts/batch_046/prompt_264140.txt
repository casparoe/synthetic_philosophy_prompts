Write a poem comprising four stanzas of six lines each, in broadly iambic metre, that depicts a single predator encounter as it would be processed by each of Dennett’s four kinds of minds: the Darwinian, the Skinnerian, the Popperian, and the Gregorian. The formal architecture should mirror the cognitive architecture it depicts. In the first stanza, use a rigid, unbroken rhyme scheme (AABBA) to represent hard-wired instinctive response. In the second, preserve exactly one rhyme sound from the first stanza but allow the others to vary freely, evoking trial-and-error conditioning. In the third, abandon rhyme entirely and insert a pronounced medial caesura in every line, as though physical action is arrested while mental models are tested and discarded in place of bodily harm, representing the Popperian capacity for internal simulation. In the fourth stanza, set one line from the first stanza apart typographically — as though quoted — and embed it within a web of shared symbols, tools, or cultural references, representing the Gregorian creature’s reliance on language and external cognition. The philosophical progression should be accurate enough that a specialist in philosophy of mind recognises each transition — from innate behaviour, through operant learning, to mental hypothesis-testing, to culturally mediated cognition — but the piece should read as an integrated lyric rather than an annotated list. Please compose your answer in British English, using British spelling and punctuation, and do not include any bibliography, citations, or suggested reading.
