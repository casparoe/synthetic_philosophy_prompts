I'm noticing a weird patern in expert disagreements that doesn't fit the usual conciliationism vs steadfastness framing. Two peers start with a clear clash over some thesis. Over months they keep making little concessions, but each time they concede they also subtley redefine what the thesis meant, so they never actually lose ground on there original point. Eventually they say they've 'resolved' it, but they've just moved the goalposts in slow motion until the words stay the same and the content shifts. It's not conciliationism since confidence in the original claim never drops, and it's not steadfastness since they're constantly yielding on surface issues. Is there a name for this slow definitional retreat in peer disagreement? How does it differ from plain equivocation or motte-and-bailey? And if there's no established term, what should we call it — and does it mean the conciliationism/steadfastness debate misses how real disputes actually die?
