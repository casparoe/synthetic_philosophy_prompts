Construct a taxonomy—in branching-tree form—that classifies agent architectures according to how they relate human evaluative feedback to their action-selection criterion. Build the tree from logical space rather than from named theories: each internal node must be a concrete architectural or behavioral question that a student could answer without knowing the leaf labels, and each leaf must be a specific position.

Root commitment: the agent receives scalar or comparative feedback from human evaluators and relates that feedback to a world-state objective (which may be present, absent, or structurally separated).

Your tree must contain exactly these five leaves:
1. Pure approval-directed: feedback is the terminal optimization target; there is no representational separation between reward and world-state.
2. Instrumental approval-directed: feedback is still the terminal target, but the agent possesses a causal model of the evaluator and can modify the evaluator or its environment to increase feedback.
3. Pure goal-directed: a fixed world-state objective exists and is invariant to changes in feedback; approval is used at most for initialization.
4. Dynamically updated goal-directed: a world-state objective exists and is modified by approval signals, yet remains conceptually distinct from them (not reducible to reward maximization).
5. Counterfactual-approval-anchored goal agent: it maintains a fixed world-state objective plus a separate structural gate that evaluates counterfactual actions against a hypothetical evaluator in out-of-distribution contexts; this gate never feeds into the objective or reward but can veto actions.

Use no more than four branching questions. Phrase each as an inspectable feature (e.g., “Does the architecture contain a representational variable for world-state that is not a transformation of the reward signal?”).

Then include:
- One logically possible answer-combination that the tree leaves empty, with a brief diagnosis of why it is incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored.
- At least one pair of leaves that cannot be separated in ordinary in-distribution behavior, plus the specific out-of-distribution or interpretability probe that would distinguish them.
- A compact key mapping answer patterns onto the five leaves.
