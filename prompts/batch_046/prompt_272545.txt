Here is roughly where I have landed on the therapy versus enhancement distinction, and I want an audit of whether these commitments hang together or whether I am helping myself to incompatible motivations. First, I treat the distinction as a deprecated API boundary in a legacy codebase: it is a regulatory convention we inherited, not a natural kind tracking any deep biological reality. Second, despite that, I think there is a genuine normative difference between correcting a pathogenic mutation that prevents an organism from reaching its own counterfactual healthy baseline and editing for traits that fall within or beyond normal population variation. Third, my working rule is therefore counterfactual: therapy restores the genome the individual would have had absent specific pathogenic mutations, and this holds even if the restored baseline is well above the population mean; enhancement is anything that moves the individual away from that personal baseline. Fourth, applying this to pharmacology, I judge that using modafinil for diagnosed narcolepsy is therapy, but using it by a healthy engineer to extend work hours is enhancement, and here the moral evaluation should depend on social context—coercive norms, distributive fairness—not on the molecule's mechanism alone. Fifth, I also hold that once an intervention's risk profile is well-characterized and low, the state should not prohibit parents from selecting for non-disease traits such as height or cognitive ability, because statistical ranges have no intrinsic normative authority. Sixth, despite that permissive stance, I feel a distinct moral discomfort about selecting purely cosmetic traits—eye color, hair texture—that neither reduce suffering nor expand capabilities; this feels less like widening an option set and more like treating the child as a product specification. Seventh, I apply the counterfactual rule consistently in edge cases: if a gene edit eliminates a mutation that raises late-onset Alzheimer's risk but also removes a protective factor against viral infection, I still classify it as therapy if the net effect returns the organism to its counterfactual healthy baseline, since the baseline is defined by absence of pathogenic deviation rather than by global optimality.

So: is this package consistent? In particular, does my claim that the boundary is just a convention in the first point undermine the normative weight I place on the counterfactual baseline in points two and three, or can descriptive taxonomy and normative evaluation be separated cleanly? Does my counterfactual rule in point three actually deliver the verdict in point six about cosmetic traits, or is that discomfort an orphan intuition that forces me to either drop point six or smuggle in a hidden principle about capability-expansion? Is there a real conflict between evaluating drugs by social context in point four and evaluating germline edits by biological baseline in points three and seven, or are these domain-appropriate differences that only look like tension? And does my willingness in point five to permit non-disease germline selection contradict my claim in point two that there is a categorical normative difference, or is that merely an apparent tension because categorization does not entail prohibition? Order the tensions by severity, specify which commitment must be revised or abandoned in each case, and state the cheapest repair and what it costs.
