Design a seven-node decision tree that classifies an influence attempt as rational persuasion, autonomy-respecting nudge, capacity-bypassing manipulation, deceptive manipulation, hard coercion, or structurally mediated influence, built from the logical space of answers rather than from named theories. Each node must present a concrete scenario—e.g., a physician framing survival statistics to steer a treatment choice, an app using emotional-vulnerability data to extend session time, or an employer restructuring default retirement contributions—followed by a binary or trinary choice that requires judging the case, not recalling a philosophical label. The branching logic should first separate rational-capacity-targeting from capacity-bypassing; second, separate awareness-preserving from deceptive; third, separate voluntary-entry contexts from asymmetric-power contexts. The required leaf positions are pure rational persuasion, libertarian-paternalist nudging, non-deceptive manipulation, deceptive manipulation, hard coercion, and structurally embedded influence. After constructing the tree, map every combination of answers onto its leaf; identify any logically possible but unoccupied branch—particularly whether a view can consistently accept rational-capacity targeting yet deny that awareness is morally relevant—and briefly diagnose whether the gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or neglect. Explicitly flag any pairs of named positions that the tree cannot distinguish and explain why they collapse at the same node. Frame the instrument for a graduate ethics workshop: questions should be answerable without prior knowledge of the taxonomy, and no node should ask respondents which theory they prefer.
