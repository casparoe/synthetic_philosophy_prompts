Write the adversarial-collaboration document that a liberal functionalist about phenomenal consciousness and a restricted neurobiological realist should have produced after a year of arguing past each other. The liberal functionalist holds that qualitative mental states are defined entirely by their causal-functional relations and are therefore in principle realizable in non-biological substrates; the restricted realist grants that intentional states may be multiply realizable but insists that phenomenal consciousness requires specific homeostatic, autopoietic organizational properties found only in living tissue. The document should contain: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims—about the formal structure of functionalism, about what needs explaining in consciousness (distinguishing the hard problem from the easy problems), about what classic no-go arguments (absent qualia, inverted spectrum) actually establish, and about the current state of empirical neuroscience; (2) a disagreements table in which each side’s position is phrased in wording it would accept, isolating whether substrate-independent global workspace dynamics could ever ground phenomenal presence and whether biological autopoiesis is a necessary condition or merely a contingent implementation; (3) the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party—including the predictive divergence over a hypothetical silicon-based global workspace that is behaviorally and reportively identical to a human brain, and the interpretation of integrated-information measures in anoxic neural tissue; (4) for each crux, the concrete case or evidence both sides agree is relevant, with each side’s prediction recorded in advance. The reading should be faithful in both directions: it should not reduce either view to a straw man, and it should identify any part of the dispute that is genuinely verbal rather than substantive. Write the response in American English, without bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
