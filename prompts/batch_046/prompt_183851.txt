I am a graduate student in a philosophy of science seminar focused on scientific reasoning and mental modeling. For my presentation, I need a decision-tree taxonomy—built from the logical space of answers rather than from a catalog of named theories—that sorts people by what they actually believe about how scientific thought experiments yield knowledge. The tree should contain six to eight branching nodes, each framed as a concrete verdict on a specific scenario (for example, Galileo’s inclined plane, Einstein’s accelerating elevator, Maxwell’s demon, or Schrödinger’s cat) rather than as a theoretical label, so that a respondent can navigate it without knowing the literature. The first split must ask whether thought experiments can deliver genuinely novel knowledge about the physical world or only conceptual clarification. From there, branch on whether the epistemic work is done by pure logical argument, by mental simulation of physical dynamics, or by appeal to conceptual intuition detached from simulation. The leaves must include at least four positions: a rationalist view holding that reason alone applied to conceptual structures reveals physical facts; an argument-analysis view holding that thought experiments are only compressed deductive or analogical arguments with no special status; a mental-modeling view holding that they run embodied spatial simulations in the mind; and a deflationary view holding that they possess no distinctive epistemic power beyond ordinary reasoning. For every combination of answers that does not correspond to one of these leaves, please note whether the cell is empty because it is incoherent, merely unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Also explicitly flag any pair of leaves that the tree cannot distinguish on the basis of scenario verdicts alone—if they diverge only in metaphysical framing rather than in their answers to the case questions. The audience is fellow students who are not epistemology specialists, so no node should ask anyone to identify a philosopher by name; instead, each node should present a specific outcome of the scenario and ask whether that outcome supports, undermines, or is neutral to the claim that knowledge was gained. Present the taxonomy as a text flowchart with the path for each leaf, followed by a brief diagnosis of the empty cells and the flagged indistinguishabilities.
