I'm building a little sorting quiz for my philosophy blog — nothing fancy, just a decision tree that sorts people by what they actually think about the analytic-synthetic split rather than which team they root for. I'm tired of quizzes that ask "do you agree with Quine?" and call it taxonomy, so I want something in the weeds: a tree built on three concrete revision scenarios I call "revision-resistance profiles."

Case one: we discover emeralds that are genuinely blue-green in a non-illusory way, so "all emeralds are green" comes out false. Is that a belief revision, a meaning revision, both, neither? Case two: Twin Earth has XYZ in its lakes, not H2O, and locals call it water — does that show "water" meant H2O all along, or that we were wrong about water's essence? Case three: a legal change lets married people be called bachelors — meaning shift, social fact shift, or no shift?

The tree should start with answers to these three, then map the eight possible answer-patterns onto named positions: hard Quinean holist, Carnap-style internal/external splitter, Putnam-style deflated analyticist, semantic externalist about natural kinds, Grice-Strawson ordinary-language defender, Boghossian-style epistemic-analyticity advocate, and maybe one unnamed slot if a combination is coherent but nobody holds it. For patterns that don't land anywhere, flag whether they're incoherent, ugly, or just unexplored. Also flag any pair of named positions your tree can't actually seperate with these cases. Phrase every branch as a question someone can answer without knowing the label it leads to, and please don't ask for citations or a reading list — I'm writing this for people without library access. I definatly don't want bibliography requests, and I might be mixing up analytic and synthetic in my framing so correct me if I'm being sloppy about which case belongs where.
