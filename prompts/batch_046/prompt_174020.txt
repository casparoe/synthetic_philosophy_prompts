I am drafting a scene for a novel about the first permanent settlement on Mars, and I have been trying to work out the ethics of terraforming for a debate among the colonists. I have constructed the following argument, but it seems to collapse into an intolerable conclusion no matter which direction I take, and I cannot locate the exact slip. (1) Only beings with subjective interests or a capacity to suffer can be wronged; planets, rocks, and ecosystems possess none of these. (2) Mars is currently uninhabited and non-sentient. (3) Therefore altering or terraforming Mars wrongs no one in itself; only indirect effects on future humans need be weighed, so colonization is ethically permissible in principle. (4) By identical reasoning, destroying Earth's last untouched wilderness—say, the deep Antarctic interior—wrongs no one in itself, since ice and stone have no interests. (5) So if terraforming Mars is permitted, paving over every wilderness on Earth is also permitted, provided no human is directly injured. (6) That conclusion is intolerable. (7) So premise (1) must be false: there exist wrongs that do not require a sentient victim, perhaps wrongs to the integrity of unclaimed possibility or to wilderness valued independently of use. (8) Applying this to Mars, the planet's untouched state may carry a direct moral claim against transformation. (9) Hence space colonization is not merely permitted but presumptively forbidden, and we ought to preserve Mars exactly as we preserve Earth wilderness. (10) Yet this proves too much: it would forbid not only large-scale terraforming but also a single research station, a planted tree, or even footprints, since any alteration violates that claim. I want to preserve the intuition that destroying wilderness is wrong even without human victims, but I do not want to end up treating a footprint on Martian soil as a moral catastrophe. Which numbered step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? Must I abandon the idea of non-sentient wrongs altogether, or is there a more surgical correction? Please end with a single line beginning Verdict: that states the step and the price of giving it up.
