I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of mathematics, and I have drafted a brief essay on a point that seems to me underexplored. I argue that the possibility of an undiscovered inconsistency in ZFC should be treated not as an imminent crisis but as a standing background condition analogous to an undetected systematic error in a long-running physical experiment—one that does not invalidate all prior measurements but changes how we interpret them. More specifically, I propose that if a contradiction were eventually localized deep in the large-cardinal hierarchy—far beyond any region with physical or computational application—it might be possible to quarantine it, preserving an “instrumental” mathematics adequate for science while abandoning a “foundational” mathematics that claims to describe a single, mind-independent realm of sets. This, I suggest, would parallel the way quantum mechanics forced physicists to distinguish observable phenomena from unobservable hidden variables.

Please comment on whether this analogy between empirical underdetermination and axiomatic risk is genuinely illuminating or merely rhetorical, on whether the instrumental/foundational distinction can be drawn sharply in mathematics, and on where my reasoning is most vulnerable—particularly regarding the claim that a contradiction in remote set theory could be isolated without infecting applied arithmetic. Finally, please state clearly what specific argument or discovery would cause you to reject either the analogy or the proposed distinction.
