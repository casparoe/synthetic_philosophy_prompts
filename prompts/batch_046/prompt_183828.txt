Design an in-class role-play that makes the group feel the specific structure of bad faith not as generic self-deception, but as a flight from ontological freedom into what Sartre calls the spirit of seriousness, mediated by an institutional alibi of your own design. The mechanism should work as follows: each participant receives a concealed institutional mask—a role card assigning them to evaluate two peers on criteria they do not fully know, while being evaluated themselves on a hidden rubric that shifts without announcement at the forty-minute mark. The rules must be arranged so that every participant makes at least one binding judgment about a peer, then immediately explains that judgment by appealing to the system’s opacity—invoking the rubric as if it determined their choice—thereby experiencing in real time the move by which facticity is treated as if it constrained transcendence. Provide a minute-by-minute timeline for the full ninety minutes, specifying exactly who knows what, when the hidden rubric changes, and at what point the facilitator reveals that all judgments were authored within a freedom the institutional framework never actually removed. The protocol should be entirely self-contained: no external texts, citations, bibliographies, or references to niche secondary literature should be required, and a model without web access should be able to construct the full rules, roles, evaluation structure, and payoff scheme from your description alone.
