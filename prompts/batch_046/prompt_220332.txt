I sit on my city's Land Use Review Board. Next Thursday we vote on whether to approve a proposal to tear down a block of 1950s bungalows and build a 9-story co-op with 30% permanently affordable units and the rest market-rate. The neighborhood association isn't objecting on traffic or shadow studies — those came back clean. They're making a philosophical claim they call "community sovereignty": people who've lived here for decades have a moral property right in the scale and social fabric of the block, and allowing more height is basically a regulatory taking from them, not just a zoning update. The developer argues that exclusionary zoning is a collective-action failure that externalizes costs onto future residents who don't have a vote yet, and that treating "neighborhood character" as protected is like privatizing a vertical commons — air, light, and density potential — without compensation.

Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before Thursday's vote. Recommend approve, deny, or approve-with-conditions, and if conditions specify exactly what they are plus a concrete timeline (e.g., affordable covenant recorded by end of Q2, design review finalized within 60 days). I want it to take the residents' "community sovereignty" argument seriously rather than just labeling it NIMBYism, but also push back with the idea that current residents don't own the future density of the city. Frame the core issue as who actually holds "a right to the city" — present occupants, potential ones, or the municipality as trustee? The memo needs a real bottom line, not "it depends," and should honestly lay out the strongest consideration on the losing side. Oh, and please don't use "principle" when you mean "principal" — I always mix those up in drafts.
