I defend a reconstructed occasionalism in the spirit of Al-Ghazālī, but modified in one respect: I hold that created things have no intrinsic causal powers whatsoever—not even a power to persist in existence from one instant to the next. What we call natural laws are therefore not expressions of dispositional essences or inherent tendencies, but genuinely stable patterns in successive divine creations. These patterns are real enough to ground scientific prediction and experimental replication; they are not illusions. Yet they are ontologically dependent on continuous volition, carrying no self-grounding necessity of their own. This allows science to proceed as usual—studying regularities, formulating counterfactuals, and explaining phenomena—while denying that explanation ever bottoms out in creaturely causal capacities.

What would count against this view?
