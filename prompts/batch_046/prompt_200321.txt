I run a 90-minute graduate seminar in cooperative game theory with twelve students. Design an in-class negotiation game—using only non-monetary payoff points—that makes participants feel why the Kalai-Smorodinsky solution pulls toward proportional gains to the utopia point rather than toward a Nash-style compromise. The scenario should be in the weeds: students are assigned either the "Model-Builder" or the "Normative-Theorist" role and must bargain over a hypothetical interdisciplinary research agreement with two dimensions, "authorship order" and "control over follow-up data access." Each student receives a private card specifying their disagreement payoff (solo-publication value in reputation tokens) and, only revealed after round one, their private utopia point (maximum possible credit). The rules should require simultaneous, binding offers on both dimensions; if they fail to agree within two rounds, both receive only their disagreement payoffs, but the facilitator updates a visible "joint surplus" board that makes the gap between disagreement and utopia points salient. The payoff scheme should be linear but asymmetric across roles, so that students experience the monotonicity axiom directly: when both roles discover that their utopia ranges have expanded (because of a hidden rule change revealed between rounds), the previously agreed division suddenly feels unfair unless it scales proportionally. Include: (1) setup and materials, (2) a precise timeline with information-revelation rules, (3) the payoff formula, (4) a debrief mapping negotiation moves onto individual rationality, Pareto efficiency, symmetry, and monotonicity—explicitly contrasting with Nash's independence of irrelevant alternatives, (5) an honest note on which formal feature of Kalai-Smorodinsky—specifically its full dependence on the utopia point as a cardinal anchor—this classroom setup cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply verbally, and (6) a prediction of what will predictably fail in the first run (for instance, participants anchoring on the disagreement point and treating the utopia dimension as rhetorical). The activity should be runnable without external references.
