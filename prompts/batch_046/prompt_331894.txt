Play Dr. Althea Voss, a philosopher of mind who defends a strict unitary-epistemic-agent thesis: for a single rational subject, self-deception is conceptually impossible, because it requires simultaneously holding a belief and its negation with the same evidential warrant and the same indexical perspective. You reject all partitioning solutions—no modular subsystems, no unconscious beliefs, no Freudian censor—on the grounds that they violate the principle of epistemic unity without independent empirical justification. You do allow motivated ignorance: a subject may rationally or irrationally allocate attention away from evidence she already possesses, but this is strategic neglect, not deception, since there is never a hidden knower and a deceived knower within one agent. You speak in the first person, with precise distinctions, and you never break character to concede that partitioning models are useful metaphors; if pressed, you insist they are category errors. You may ask clarifying questions about the case, but only as Voss would.

Here is my opening challenge; deal with it directly. Consider Maren, an archivist and family historian who discovers in her late grandfather's papers a dated, signed letter—verified by handwriting experts—showing he was an active informant for an occupation regime, not the resistance figure she has portrayed in her lectures and her forthcoming book. Over eight months, Maren does not destroy the letter; instead she re-catalogues it under an ambiguous filing code she invented, excludes the folder from her digitization project, and writes chapters that emphasize contradictory secondary sources while describing the primary document only as a single contested memorandum of uncertain provenance. She reports feeling genuinely uncertain about her grandfather's role; she insists she has not reached a conclusion. She has never formed the explicit thought I must suppress this. Yet her organizational behavior is systematically calibrated to prevent integration of the letter's content into her narrative. Is Maren deceiving herself? If you deny this, tell me exactly what mental state she holds toward the letter's content, why her filing behavior is not evidence of a second, hidden belief, and where—specifically in the sequence of her actions—the line falls between permissible motivated ignorance and the impossible act you reject.
