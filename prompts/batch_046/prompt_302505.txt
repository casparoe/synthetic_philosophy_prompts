Analyze the March 2020 COVID-19 intensive-care triage crisis in Lombardy, Italy, through the lens of care ethics, treating the episode as a two-way test rather than a simple illustration. The salient facts are these: the region faced a sudden surge of critically ill patients, hospital authorities proposed formal triage criteria—including age and comorbidity thresholds—to ration scarce ventilators, and clinicians on the ground were widely reported to resist applying those criteria mechanically, relying instead on case-by-case judgments about individual patients.

Your task is disciplined mapping in both directions. First, apply Joan Tronto's four-phase framework—caring about, taking care of, care-giving, and care-receiving—to the institutional setting. Identify precisely who occupies each role when the care-receiver is not a named individual in a direct relationship but an anonymous statistical population, and when the caregiver is a regional health authority issuing protocols rather than a person providing hands-on attention. Does the framework illuminate why clinicians rejected mechanical allocation, or does it break down because responsiveness requires particular knowledge that anonymous triage cannot supply?

Second, examine the predictive and normative limits of the theory. Does care ethics explain the clinicians' behavior better than utilitarian or justice-based rationing models—suggesting that relational obligations persist even under scarcity—or does it fail to offer a principled decision procedure for cases in which two unknown patients compete for one ventilator? In other words, does the episode show that care ethics can be scaled to public-health institutions, or does it reveal a structural boundary: that the theory is coherent only at the interpersonal scale and becomes either incoherent or merely rhetorical when applied to anonymous distribution?

Finally, state honestly what the case shows about care ethics itself. If the clinicians' resistance confirms the theory, what institutional form would "care at scale" take? If the resistance instead exposes a gap, does that gap damage the theory's claim to guide policy, or only its claim to replace distributive justice entirely?
