I am writing a philosophy paper for my senior seminar on whether well-being requires contact with reality. To make the comparison precise, please construct a one-paragraph thought experiment—set in a realistic hospice context, with no exotic technology or omniscient predictors—featuring a terminal patient who can enter an "Autonomy Capsule." Inside, she lives ten years containing genuine objective goods: deep friendships, completed creative work, and scientific understanding. The capsule also retroactively implants a psychological profile so she authentically values these goods, though she retains full awareness of the implantation and knows her biological body remains unconscious. Design the case so that hedonism, desire-fulfillment theory, and objective-list theory all judge the capsule life maximally good.

Then introduce a fourth view, which you should call "causal-authenticity well-being": welfare requires not only possessing good things, but that one's central values have an unmanipulated causal origin in one's actual biography. Apply all four views to the case, showing exactly why causal-authenticity rejects entry despite the goods present. Identify the single structural feature that isolates this disagreement, and explain whether removing the retroactive value-implantation—while keeping the capsule and goods identical—collapses the debate back into the standard experience-machine problem. Finally, include a brief hedonist rebuttal arguing that the causal origin of values is irrelevant to welfare.
