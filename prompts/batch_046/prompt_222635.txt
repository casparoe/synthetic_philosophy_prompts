I want to bring back a sharper analysis that separates what that slogan could actually mean from what it was made to do in the room.

Could you lay out the distinct precise theses hiding in that one-liner? I need a menu that distinguishes at least: (1) an empirical correlation claim about modern liberal electoral regimes; (2) a normative claim about what republican governments, in Kant’s specific sense of representative constitutions with separation of powers and citizen consent to war, are rationally bound to do; (3) Kant’s own institutional argument linking republican representation to a federation of free states; and (4) any stronger deterministic or metaphysical reading that makes democratic form literally incompatible with war. For each, say whether it is a platitude, defensible, false, or a misreading of Kant, and indicate which version someone is probably invoking when they deploy the slogan to shut down discussion about contemporary foreign policy. In particular, tell me whether any defensible reading actually licenses ignoring Kant’s further claim that perpetual peace requires an international federative structure, or whether that part gets silently dropped whenever the slogan is used as a standalone defense of democratic exceptionalism.
