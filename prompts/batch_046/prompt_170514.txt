Assuming that democratic backsliding must involve a measurable degradation of vertical accountability—specifically, the erosion of free and fair electoral mechanisms through which citizens sanction incumbents—rather than a restructuring of horizontal checks between branches, how should we interpret Poland's judicial reforms between 2015 and 2023? Given that every statutory change was enacted by a Sejm reflecting successive electoral mandates and signed into law by a president who was himself directly elected, and given that voter turnout and party competition remained robust throughout the period, doesn't this indicate that what critics call backsliding is in fact a legitimate democratic rebalancing toward parliamentary supremacy, and that the concept collapses into a partisan objection to the substantive policy outcomes of majoritarian rule?
