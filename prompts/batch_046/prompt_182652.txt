In a seminar on AI and aesthetics, someone dismissed machine-generated images by invoking Tolstoy's idea that art is infection — the transmission of feeling from artist to audience through external signs — and argued that since a model has no feelings to transmit, its outputs cannot be art. I want to respond precisely, but I'm stuck on what that slogan actually commits to. Can you separate the different precise theses hiding in the claim that art is infection? For each reading, say whether it is definitional, causal, normative, or epistemic; whether it requires literal emotional contagion or only functional equivalence; whether it demands that the artist actually experienced the feeling or merely that the audience comes to experience it; and specifically which of these readings, if any, would actually support the anti-AI conclusion versus which leave the door open. Lay them out as a menu I can bring to the seminar, not as a single interpretation, and flag which ones are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false.
