Please argue that the strategy-stealing assumption, familiar from combinatorial game theory as a proof of first-player advantage, fails to translate into domains where strategies are not fully observable or transferable. Defend the specific claim that if a second agent holds a genuinely superior but opaque decision procedure, the first agent cannot appropriate it without first achieving the very understanding that eliminates the second agent's advantage—thereby turning the argument into a case for epistemic humility rather than preemptive action. Contrast this failure of translation with the standard transparent-strategy case, and respond to the objection that information acquisition itself can be treated as the stolen step, explaining why that response breaks the symmetry required by the original argument.
