I need a decision procedue my team can actually run across a few meetings: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a no, and where it hands the decision to the minister because it's values not facts. Ground it in whatever institutional economics actually knows about tax morale and universalism — specifically the idea that willingness to pay high rates depends on seeing the benefit as universal and on seeing others contribute, not just on abstract fairness. Keep it practical: three months, no budget for big surveys, and the minister signs off. Then apply it to two proposals: a flat universal childcare subsidy funded by a 5-point VAT rise, and a targeted unemployment top-up for long-term jobless people funded by a wealth tax on the richest 2%. One should come out as probably not viable here even though it sounds progressive, and I want the procedue to flag exactly which institutional precondition is missing. Also be honest about where it can't settle things — for instance wether high trust is actually causing high compliance or just correlating with ethnic homogeneity.
