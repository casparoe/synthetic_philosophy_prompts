During a seminar on well-being metrics, a colleague kept invoking the line “only the absence of craving and suffering matters; all positive states are just the absence of negative ones” to reject any attempt to weigh achievements, knowledge, or relationships in an axiology. I want to bring a precisified version to our next meeting. Can you separate the precise claims this slogan could be expressing? Specifically: (1) Is it a conceptual claim that positive welfare is reducible to negative welfare? (2) Is it an empirical claim about human psychology—that all pursuit of goods is really just avoidance of discomfort? (3) Is it a normative claim that only suffering-reduction counts morally, or a stronger metaphysical claim that value itself is purely privative? For each reading, say whether it is trivial, defensible, or false, and—crucially—which ones actually license dismissing achievements and relationships as “noise” rather than merely down-weighting them. I do not need citations or a bibliography; I am looking for a disambiguation menu I can use in discussion.
