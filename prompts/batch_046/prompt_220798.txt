I'm preparing for a philosophy of mind seminar and have grown frustrated with discussions of the language of thought hypothesis that treat it either as an obvious consequence of compositionality or as a straw man easily defeated by neural-network models of cognition. Before I evaluate any particular formulation—whether Fodor's classical computationalism, a hybrid architecture, or something like a structured probabilistic framework—I want the full adequacy conditions spelled out. What must a theory of mental representation actually deliver if it takes seriously the claim that thought has a language-like structure?

Please write the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain (systematicity of inference, productivity of conceptual combination, the compositional behavior of complex concepts such as pet fish), the boundary distinctions it must not collapse (for example, between a system that merely tracks statistical correlations and one that genuinely combines discrete representations; between linguistic syntax and mental syntax; and between possessing a concept and being able to deploy it in an inference), the empirical constraints it must respect (developmental patterns in conceptual acquisition, comparative cognition results on non-human representational capacities, and neurobiological findings about localized versus distributed encoding—but only where these genuinely constrain the architecture), and the conceptual distinctions it must preserve, such as that between syntactic form and semantic content, and, this is the part I keep returning to, the capacity to maintain modality-specific comparative judgments (e.g., representing that object A is spatially larger than B while also representing that B is heavier than A) without requiring a single unified cross-modal comparative symbol or collapsing the two judgments into independent non-compositional representations. For each requirement, say whether it is non-negotiable or a camp-specific preference, and identify any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—for instance, whether strict compositionality and the representation of deeply context-sensitive concepts without lexical explosion pull in opposite directions. Finally, briefly indicate how Fodor's original account, a connectionist approach that rejects explicit syntactic structure, and a recent structured Bayesian view fare against this list. I am not asking for a bibliography or citation list; I want the conceptual specification first.
