I am a retired physicist who, since leaving the laboratory, have taken up philosophy of mind and cognitive science with the same appetite for boundary-drawing that once governed my work on open thermodynamic systems. I find the standard literature on the extended mind thesis—particularly the well-worn Otto-and-notebook scenarios—repetitive in ways that obscure a finer point about system boundaries, and I want an adversarial collaboration document produced by two camps I shall call the Orthodox Parity Extenders and the Reciprocal Coupling Enactivists. The former maintain that any external process meeting conditions of functional equivalence and reliable availability counts as part of cognition proper, irrespective of feedback into the agent’s bodily dynamics; the latter maintain that genuine cognitive extension requires continuous, bidirectional modulation between organism and artifact, so that mere external storage or computation remains a tool rather than part of the mind. I ask that both sides sign onto a single continuous prose statement, written as a formal letter without headings, bullet points, boldface, tables, or numbered lists, containing first every claim about the formal conditions of mental processes, about what distinguishes cognition from instrumentality, and about the interpretation of established thought experiments that both sides can accept without reservation; second, the precise formulations of their remaining divergence, each phrased in wording that its own advocates would endorse and that the opposing side recognizes as accurate; third, the cruxes—those specific sub-questions, such as whether functional parity in the absence of reciprocal coupling is sufficient for extension and whether predictive architectures in external artifacts change the boundary conditions in conceptually relevant ways, such that settling them would move at least one party; and fourth, for each crux, one concrete hypothetical case involving an asymmetric cognitive scaffold, specifically a predictive language model that archives a scientist’s conceptual associations through discrete query-response cycles without receiving any continuous bodily-state input or exerting reciprocal sensorimotor influence, together with each side’s explicit prediction about whether cognition extends in that case and what conceptual or empirical finding would cause them to revise their view. Please include identification of any portion of the dispute that is merely verbal—about how to apply the word cognitive rather than about the underlying phenomenon—and explain why. The document should avoid bibliographies, citations, reading lists, and appeals to named authorities, since its purpose is conceptual clarification accessible to a reader without web access, and it should maintain a polished, formal tone throughout.
