I've been arguing with my roomate about negative causation and we tried to pin down omission-causation with three conditions, but now it seems like it proves every bystander is constantly causing everything to stay alive. Here is the chain I can't break:
(1) An omission O by A causes outcome E only if (a) A could have performed some action φ, (b) there is a normative or physical expectation that A should φ (or not-φ, for preservation cases), and (c) φ-ing (or not-φ-ing) makes the counterfactual difference for E.
(2) Apply this to the moon: for every person P, P omits destroying the moon every moment.
(3) For P, (a) is true — P could physically detonate it. (c) is true — if P had destroyed it, the moon wouldn't exist now.
(4) Crucially, (b) is also satisfied in the negative: there is a baseline expectation that agents don't destroy celestial bodies, so P's omission of destruction is required for the moon's persistence.
(5) So by (1), every person constantly causes the moon's continued existence.
(6) That is obviosly absurd; I don't cause the moon's orbit by failing to nuke it.
(7) So either (1) is false and omissions never cause anything, or (4) sneaks in a wrong reading of expectation, but I can't see which without losing the negligent-lifeguard intuition.
