The audience is split: on one side, historians of Chinese philosophy for whom Gongsun Long's White Horse dialogue (Bai Ma Lun) is canonical and who will notice immediately if I reduce the School of Names to mere sophistry; on the other, practicing taxonomists and machine-learning engineers who have never encountered the argument but who constantly face the practical problem of whether a category captures an essential kind or merely clusters observable properties.

I need the white-horse distinction—membership by intrinsic nature versus membership by shared accidental feature—to do genuine structural work in the speech, not appear as ornamental reference: the remarks should move from the classical argument to the hidden ontological commitments in modern classification and algorithmic labeling, making the connection feel necessary rather than analogical, and they must do this without losing either half of the room. Because this is to be delivered, not researched, please avoid any bibliographies, citation requests, or reading lists; the speech should be self-contained and approximately seven to nine minutes long, with a tone that is formal and occasion-appropriate—somewhere between a workshop convocation and a measured toast—rather than a conventional academic lecture. Could you write this for me?
