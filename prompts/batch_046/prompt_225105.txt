Marco said Sam was acting in honor culture — defending his reputation with direct, public shaming — and that's actually more honest than hiding behind rules. Tessa said honor culture is just bullying with extra steps, and Leo was right to use the formal complaint system, which is dignity culture, not victimhood. Marco said no, Leo skipped private talk and ran straight to authority playing the victim, which is classic victimhood culture; dignity culture would mean talking it out face-to-face first. Marco then said victimhood culture always needs a third-party enforcer audience, but the manager is just a neutral arbiter, so it's not victimhood. Tessa said that's splitting hairs — the manager is exactly the institutional authority victimhood culture appeals to.

Tessa said dignity culture is best because it treats human worth as built-in, but she admitted it only works if people actually use private reasoning instead of running to authority. Marco said that's the problem: dignity culture pretends conflict doesn't exist, so people either fall back on honor shaming or victimhood complaints. Tessa said that's not dignity's fault, it's just people being lazy or scared. They also wondered what'd happen if the building had no manager at all — wouldn't that make honor culture the only real option?
