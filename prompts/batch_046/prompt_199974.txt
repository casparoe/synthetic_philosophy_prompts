As a retired natural scientist who has recently begun studying philosophy of mind in earnest, I am trying to understand teleosemantics as an account of how mental content might be naturalized. I grasp the general thesis—that evolutionary selection history fixes the representational function of a mental state—but I am struggling to see how this distinguishes representational error from mere biological malfunction. In particular, I keep returning to what I take to be the disjunction problem: if a representation is defined by what it was selected to indicate, then in cases where it is triggered by something it was not selected for, it seems ambiguous whether the state represents that trigger (and is false) or represents the selected condition (and is merely misfiring). Could you provide a conceptually precise yet intuitive explanation of how the notion of proper function is meant to resolve this ambiguity? I am especially interested in whether evolutionary history genuinely grounds original intentionality, or whether it simply relocates the normative standard of correct representation into the past without explaining it. A clear conceptual map of the mechanism, rather than a bibliographic survey, would be most helpful.
