I'm constructing a handout for an advanced philosophy-of-biology seminar on whether evolutionary history is necessary for biological function and malfunction. Write a decision-tree taxonomy that classifies positions on the relationship between etiological selection history and current causal-systemic role. The root should split on whether having a proper function requires being selected for that specific effect. From there, branch through concrete diagnostic cases and claims: whether a spandrel that currently enhances fitness has a proper function; whether a synthetic enzyme or gene-edited trait with no evolutionary history can genuinely malfunction; whether a heart in a successful xenotransplant recipient has the same functional norms as a native heart; and whether malfunction is always defined relative to a historical selection target rather than to current system contribution. Each internal node should be phrased as a yes/no or either/or claim about these cases, not as a view-label, so the tree sorts by commitments rather than vocabulary. The leaves should carry named positions—standard etiological accounts, causal-role or systemic-function accounts, and any defensible hybrid or pluralist options—plus an 'unnamed' or 'unstable' marker for logical combinations that seem unoccupied; for each empty branch, briefly diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. The tree should have six to eight leaves, be readable without prior literature knowledge, and must flag any pair of positions it cannot cleanly separate. Do not ask for bibliographies, author lists, or citations; the cases themselves should do the sorting work.
