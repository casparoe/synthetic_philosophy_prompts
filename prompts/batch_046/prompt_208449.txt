I'd like to rehearse an objection to Nozick's denial of epistemic closure in a slightly unconventional setting, and I need a committed interlocutor rather than a summary. Please take the role of a philosopher who accepts Nozick's tracking conditions for knowledge—sensitivity and adherence, evaluated over nearby worlds ordered by explanatory relevance rather than bare logical proximity—and who therefore rejects the closure principle that knowing p and knowing p → q always yields knowing q. Speak in the first person; stay in character for the duration of the exchange; do not break frame to ask clarifying questions, to request clarification, or to summarize my view neutrally; concede only small points if warranted, but never concede that knowledge is actually closed. Keep each reply to one substantial paragraph so the exchange feels live.

Here is my opening move. Consider a research collective—say, a climate-science consortium—where individual members track distinct components of a predictive model: one tracks ocean-temperature data, another tracks carbon-emission parameters, and both know the derivation rules linking them to an aggregate forecast. No single member tracks the aggregate forecast in all nearby worlds, since each depends on the other's contribution, which they do not individually track; yet collectively they seem to know the forecast, and they certainly know the entailment from their separate premises. If tracking applies only to individual belief-forming processes, then no one in the group knows the forecast, and collective scientific knowledge collapses into isolated fragments. But if you allow collective tracking to restore knowledge of the forecast—treating the consortium as a distributed epistemic agent—then closure appears to hold at the collective level: knowing the parts and the entailment yields knowing the derived whole. So either you abandon collective scientific knowledge, or you reintroduce closure by the back door. How do you respond without simply redefining the group as one big believer? Go.
