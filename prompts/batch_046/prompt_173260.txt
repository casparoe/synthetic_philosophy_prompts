I'm defending a seminar version of my dissertation argument that suffering and pleasure are not merely quantitatively incommensurable above a lexical threshold, but that suffering carries a structurally calibrated lexical priority—what I call dimensional lexical threshold negative utilitarianism—where the incommensurability is rooted in the relational and temporal architecture of suffering rather than in intensity alone. The audience is a philosophy department seminar with the following composition: one normative ethicist committed to classical utilitarian aggregation, one Kantian moral philosopher, one virtue ethicist, two philosophers of value with published work on incommensurability and well-being measurement, one moral psychologist who studies affective forecasting, and one graduate student in metaethics. They are familiar with standard negative-utilitarian objections—particularly the blissful-existence problem and the measurement problem—but have not read my chapter. Draft my Q&A prep sheet: list the eight or so objections I will actually face, ordered by how damaging they are and roughly by when they will arise in the discussion. For each objection, phrase it in the vocabulary and framing that the specific audience member (utilitarian, Kantian, virtue ethicist, value theorist, psychologist, or student) would actually use; provide a concise reply I can deliver in about two minutes; and flag the weakest point in that reply. Include what I should concede up front, what I should hold firmly, and the one objection I am hoping nobody raises—namely, the challenge that my calibration mechanism for structural suffering-weight is either circular or empirically unfalsifiable. Structure this as a speaker's prep sheet, not a defense of the view itself, and write the entire response in American English. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for specific page references. Where standard positions are mentioned, paraphrase them without quotation marks rather than quoting verbatim.
