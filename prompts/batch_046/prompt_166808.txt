Write a deadpan parody of a formal museum acquisition committee report—approximately 650 words, formatted as numbered agenda items and appended minutes—evaluating the proposed purchase of a painting whose attribution is contested. The satire should target the widespread philosophical and market assumption that an original artwork carries an irreducible form of value—call it ontological priority—that is necessarily absent from any forgery, no matter how physically identical. To keep the task conceptually specific rather than generic, build the report around an invented institutional protocol: the committee has access to a technology referred to as "causal-continuity verification," which can determine whether an object shares an unbroken physical and intentional trajectory with the work that left the artist's studio, but the technology cannot resolve whether such continuity is constitutive of authenticity or merely correlates with it. The committee's debate should therefore turn on a subtle distinction that the report treats with bureaucratic gravity: is authenticity a relational property, fixed by the artist's authoritative act at a particular historical moment, or is it a structural property, sustained by the object's continuous spatiotemporal path? Include, as a formal appendix, a brief "Branching Scenario Protocol" addressing the case in which verification reveals two objects with perfectly identical causal histories up to a divergence point—asking whether both, neither, or only one can be the original, and recommending a budget allocation for the dispute. The voice should be that of institutional deadpan—condition reports, provenance matrices, risk-assessment language—so that profound metaphysical disagreements are expressed entirely in the idiom of procurement, insurance liability, and storage requirements. The humor should arise from this mismatch: the committee applies rigorous analytic distinctions to a technology and a problem that do not exist, exaggerating real art-world practices (attribution committees, technical analysis, provenance documentation) into an absurd metaphysics of bureaucracy. Do not cite named philosophers or ask for a bibliography; let the philosophical position emerge implicitly through the committee's own categories and anxieties.
