Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between two camps would produce: on one side, committed experiential mental-state theorists of well-being, who hold that conscious experience is the sole constituent of well-being and that a phenomenologically perfect experience machine can therefore deliver maximal well-being; on the other side, committed authenticity-achievement pluralists, who hold that well-being requires genuine causal engagement with an independent world and actual success at valued projects, not merely the subjective appearance or belief in such engagement. The collaboration should center on Robert Nozick’s experience-machine thought experiment, but it must move beyond standard textbook recitations by treating three precisely formulated cruxes.

Format the output as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims—at least six—including shared definitions, a shared formulation of Nozick’s problem, shared recognition of the limits of intuition-pumping, agreement on which psychological findings about affective forecasting and achievement satisfaction both sides treat as relevant background, and at least one shared conceptual distinction—such as the distinction between phenomenal fidelity and causal provenance—that both sides agree is well-formed and weights differently. Second, a disagreements table with columns: Crux; Experientialist formulation, phrased so that camp accepts the wording of its own view; Pluralist formulation, phrased so that camp accepts the wording of its own view; The specific case, argument, or test both sides agree bears on the crux; Experientialist prediction in advance; Pluralist prediction in advance.

The three cruxes must be:
1. Whether there exists a non-circular, phenomenologically accessible criterion for distinguishing a mental state that genuinely contributes to well-being from one that merely mimics such contribution inside a simulation, given that subjects permanently lack epistemic access to the simulation boundary.
2. Whether the concept of achievement can be satisfied by counterfactual robustness alone—specifically, whether a simulated project that would have succeeded in the actual world counts as an achievement for well-being purposes, or whether causal history is indispensable.
3. Whether intertemporal consistency in preference revision, under conditions of full transparency about the simulation boundary, favors retaining experientialist judgments or abandoning them in favor of pluralist ones.

For each crux, identify the case or argument both sides agree is probative, and record each side’s explicit prediction of what that case or argument will show. Ensure that the agreed-claims section contains no slanted formulations: each claim must be one that both camps could sign without reservation. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific named theorists beyond Nozick. Do not invoke obscure named theories or specific empirical studies with invented authors or dates; refer only to general domains of evidence, such as the literature on affective forecasting errors or studies comparing satisfaction in simulated versus actual achievement contexts, that both sides accept as legitimate background. Write in a formal, polished register appropriate to a philosophical working-group document.
