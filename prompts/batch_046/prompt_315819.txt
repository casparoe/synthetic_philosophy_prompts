I've been trying to work out a take on Nietzsche's genealogy for a reading group and I think I've landed on something that sounds weird but feel right. Basically I think the whole slave-revolt story is overblown if you read it against the background of his later notebook fragments on active forgetting and the physiological basis of evaluation. My view is that master and slave values aren't two opposing systems but different metabolic states of the same evaluative apparatus — the good/bad distinction is just high-agency evaluation running hot, while good/evil is the same apparatus cooled down and turned reflexive. So the slave revolt wasn't a moral inversion driven by ressentiment but more like a strategic rebrading of ascetic practices that were already present in master cultures, like the agonistic self-denial you see in Homeric heroes, into a universalizable framework. Once you see this, the master/slave binary collapses, and modern morality is better explained by a kind of value-pluralism Nietzsche gestures at in the Nachlass but never integrates.

What would actually count against this? I want the textual hits, the conceptual objections, and whether people who study Nietzsche's body-physiology stuff have counter-arguments that cut against the idea that asceticism was internal to master culture. Rank them by how fatal they'd be, say which ones a defender of this metabolic/pluralist reading could concede without dying, and be honest about whether I've just qualified the view into unfalsifiability by leaning on unpublished fragments. Also please separate what's pretty settled in Nietzsche scholarship from what's genuinely contested, and don't present a minority interpretation as if it's the consensus.
