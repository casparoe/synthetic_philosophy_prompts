As part of my dissertation chapter on the scope of interpretive theory, I am trying to specify what a Davidson-style account of radical interpretation must be able to deliver if it is not restricted to the canonical face-to-face case but must also cover agents whose conceptual architecture diverges systematically from ours—for instance, agents who treat events as ontologically prior to enduring objects, or whose doxastic practices are governed by a non-classical, paraconsistent logic of belief rather than by classical consistency constraints.

Please provide the full list of desiderata such a theory must meet. For each requirement, state: (i) the specific case or theoretical consideration that motivates it; (ii) whether it is non-negotiable or rather expresses a preference of one interpretive camp (e.g., Davidsonians committed to the principle of charity and holism, Quineans who tolerate indeterminacy of translation, or critics who reject mental holism altogether); (iii) whether it stands in tension with any other desideratum on the list, particularly whether strict enforcement of determinacy in belief-attribution conflicts with deep holism under conditions of logical or ontological divergence; and (iv) which well-known result or no-go consideration (such as the interdependence of belief and meaning) bears on it.

The list should address at least: (a) successful belief-attribution in standard shared-perceptual contexts; (b) interpretation across modalities or environments where perceptual access is not common; (c) preservation of the distinction between attributing a false belief and attributing an altogether different content; (d) resistance to collapse into mere behavioral description; (e) accommodation of ontological divergence without forced translation into the interpreter's own categories; (f) resolution of literal versus non-literal (e.g., ironic or metaphorical) utterance in the absence of contextual disambiguating cues; and (g) rational belief-assignment to agents whose inferential norms violate classical consistency but exhibit an alternative systematicity.

Finally, indicate which single requirement, if taken as strictly binding, eliminates the largest share of existing Davidsonian and post-Davidsonian proposals, and explicitly flag any subset of these desiderata that is jointly unsatisfiable. A concise table is acceptable; brief scoring of main contenders is optional, but the weight should be on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on evaluation of specific theories.
