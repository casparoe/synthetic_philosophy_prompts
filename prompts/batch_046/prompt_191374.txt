I'm working out my view for an aesthetics seminar and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is my current package: (1) I am an autonomist about aesthetic value—artistic merit is conceptually independent of moral merit, so a work can be morally repugnant and artistically supreme. (2) I accept a restricted separation thesis: knowledge of the artist's biography should not influence judgments about formal properties (composition, technique, perceptual structure). (3) I think aesthetic properties are modally dependent on authorial character in one specific respect: if a work's meaning depends on attributing a particular psychological state to its creator, then discovering that the creator lacked that state undermines expressive coherence without touching formal excellence. (4) I also hold that dangerous or immoral perspectives can be an aesthetic virtue when embodied with artistic control, giving the work a transgressive power that counts in its favor. (5) Finally, I think that when an artist's actual immoral conduct is structurally embedded in the work's iconography—so that appreciating the work requires complicity with real harm—there is a legitimate reason to withhold praise, though this is a failure of the audience-work relation, not a formal defect in the work itself.

Please audit this set: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts only appear on the surface, and how severe is each? For every real tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Specifically, address whether (3) collapses into a rejection of (2), whether (4) and (5) are compatible once "appreciation" is distinguished from "evaluation," and whether (1) can survive if (5) is interpreted as licensing an overall negative verdict rather than a purely moral one. Rank the tensions by severity, and then identify the single commitment whose revision would clean up the most conflict overall.
