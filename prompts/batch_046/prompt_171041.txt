Write a short story set during the founding convention of a mobile ocean-state, the Polity of Varel, as it drafts its charter defining who belongs to the demos. Three delegates embody incompatible principles of membership. One defends the "all-subjected" view: only those physically present and bound by the state's coercive laws may claim democratic standing. Another champions the "all-affected-interests" view: anyone whose vital interests are causally shaped by the state's resource extraction and waste practices—including residents of distant atolls—must be included. The third advances a stricter, unconventional position: membership requires the capacity to enter relations of mutual justification with fellow citizens, a criterion she claims excludes both distant affected parties and the state's own autonomous maintenance intelligences, despite those systems being causally embedded in every collective decision. Center the narrative on a concrete vote—whether to grant full deliberative rights to a community of climate refugees anchored on adjacent floating platforms and to the artificial system that regulates the state's oxygen cycles—rather than on abstract exposition. Use the physical setting, perhaps a partially submerged assembly hall where the boundary between inside and outside is literally unstable, to mirror the conceptual problem. Dramatize the philosophical conflict through dialogue and character, and do not resolve it with a tidy moral; let the charter's final language remain ambiguous or actively contested.
