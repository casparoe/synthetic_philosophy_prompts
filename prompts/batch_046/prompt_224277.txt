As a journalist who covers science and ideas, I serve on the external advisory panel for the Meridian Foundation’s program in historical social science, and we face a concrete funding decision that turns on a live philosophical problem. A proposal before us—titled “Instrumental Variables and Ascetic Ethics: Testing Weber’s Thesis with Seventeenth-Century Guild and Tax Records”—proposes to treat Protestant ascetic values as an exogenous cultural treatment and to measure its causal effect on capital accumulation using synthetic-control methods across Catholic and Reformed regions. One faction of the panel argues this is precisely the rigor the field needs: if cultural values cannot survive counterfactual identification, they ought to be eliminated from explanatory accounts in favor of material conditions. The opposing faction contends that the project commits a category error—Weber’s interpretive sociology embeds values in multicausal, meaning-laden configurations accessible through Verstehen, not in isolable variables susceptible to econometric instrumentation, and that reducing his thesis to a falsifiable causal law misreads his explicit rejection of mono-causal materialism as well as his methodological distinction between explanation in the natural and cultural sciences. Please draft the formal decision memo I will circulate before Thursday’s vote. It should recommend approval, rejection, or approval with specific conditions; it should engage the strongest version of the losing argument with intellectual honesty rather than caricature; and it should ground its reasoning in what is genuinely at stake about whether culture can operate as a cause distinct from structural conditions. Crucially, separate what is settled in the philosophy of social science from what remains genuinely contested among Weber scholars and causal-inference methodologists, and do not present a contested interpretive claim as if it were consensus. Do not append a bibliography, reading list, or citations; the memo must stand entirely on its own reasoning and be actionable by panelists who will not consult outside sources.
