hey, i'm running a philosophy of science workshop for about 14 people, 90 minutes. mostly grad students who've read a bit of lewis but never really wrestled with the principal principle in a physical way. i want them to feel the pull of deference to objective chance — your credence should match the known chance — and then feel it snap when self-locating info comes in. not sleeping beauty, that's tired. i cooked up a "twin observatory protocal" instead.

design the activity. split them into analysts (see the hidden mechanism) and subjects (only observe outcomes). round 1: there's an urn with a known 40% red / 60% blue setup. analysts bet tokens based on that. subjects bet blind, then learn the mechanism and should adjust to 40%. round 2: reveal the urn was drawn from a cabinet of urns with different ratios — classic inadmissible evidence. per PP they should still stay at 40%, but watch them update anyway. round 3: the twist. in this protocal, whenever red is drawn, two identical observation booths are activated with identical experiences; on blue, only one booth runs. subjects now have indexical evidence — "i am observing" might suggest duplication — while analysts still say 40%. make them physically move chips.

i need rules, a token payoff scheme (no real money), materials (cards? envelopes?), a timeline, and roles. the point should come from what they do, not my lecture. include a debrief mapping round 1 (direct deference), round 2 (inadmissible resistance), round 3 (self-locating break). be honest about which feature of the philosophical tension this can't reproduce — probably the precise quantitative disagreement about centered worlds — and say how you'd supply that verbally.

also tell me what will predictably go wrong first run. i bet people conflate duplication frequency with outcome probability, or analysts leak info.

finally, say what'd change your mind. specifically, if you think the duplication framing smuggles in frequentist reasoning that isn't actually in the original PP, call that out and say what argument or observation would convince you the activity still captures the real tension.
