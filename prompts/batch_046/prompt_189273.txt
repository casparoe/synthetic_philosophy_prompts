Construct a thought experiment, appropriate for an audience familiar with philosophy of history but requiring no specialist archival knowledge, that isolates whether institutional recurrence without demographic or cultural reset can count as a genuine historical cycle. Design a realistic scenario — confined to a single paragraph, with no science fiction, no perfect predictors, no omniscient observers, and no appeal to exotic physics — describing an isolated archipelago society observed over six centuries. The society must undergo an identical constitutional revolution every 150 years (oligarchy, democracy, tyranny, restored oligarchy), yet each repetition is embedded in a non-reversible technological substrate (wood and sail, then coal and telegraph, then solar and distributed networks) and occurs with complete historical records intact, so that no generation experiences the naive "founding solidarity" of its predecessors. Engineer the case so that four named positions deliver clearly distinct verdicts: a Polybian anacyclosis treats the pattern as authentic cyclical renewal; an Ibn Khaldun-style model of asabiyyah treats it as linear decay masked by constitutional theatre, because group solidarity cannot regenerate under these conditions; a Spenglerian cultural morphology treats the repetition as the mechanical winter-phase of an organism that has lost its organic spring; and Toynbee's challenge-and-response framework treats it as linear disintegration through failed creative response, not as upward cyclical growth. After presenting the scenario, walk through the reasoning each view gives and why, identify the single structural feature that forces them apart — the interaction of exact institutional repetition with irreversible technological accumulation and intact collective memory — explain what happens to the case if that feature is removed, and state how a defender of the view that comes out worst would attack the scenario's fairness. Write the answer in British English, avoid bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not include any verbatim quotations from real texts.
