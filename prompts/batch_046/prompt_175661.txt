I am preparing a take-home exam for my upper-level philosophy of mind seminar and need a single essay prompt on teleosemantics and naturalizing mental content, together with a model answer and a detailed marking scheme. The question must not be answerable by reciting standard summaries of Millikan’s direct/derived proper functions or Papineau’s success semantics; it needs to force students to apply the framework to a non-standard case. To that end, please build in the following niche framing: ask students to consider a “bio-synthetic hybrid cortex”—engineered neural tissue that replicates the connectivity, plasticity rules, and error-correction dynamics of biological visual cortex, but was grown in vitro and never belonged to an organism with reproductive fitness or evolutionary selection history. The exam should ask whether this hybrid can genuinely misrepresent (e.g., represent a predator where none is present) or whether, lacking inherited evolutionary proper function, its erroneous states are merely mechanical malfunctions without representational content.

Please deliver exactly three parts.

First, the exam prompt itself (around 200 words), addressed to students. It should instruct them to take a position on whether teleosemantics can accommodate genuine misrepresentation in such hybrid architectures. Require them to engage with at least two of the following: the distinction between selection-for and selection-of; Millikan’s consumer-based account and whether it requires ancestral reproductive success; the threat that reconstructed or simulated selection histories make evolutionary grounding epiphenomenal to content; and whether misrepresentation collapses into malfunction if proper function is absent. Specify that answers should be approximately 1,200 words, that students have 48 hours, and that they should not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations—arguments must stand on their own.

Second, a model answer of about 700 words. It should display the precise philosophical moves that distinguish deep understanding from memorization: for example, distinguishing direct proper function (grounded in ancestral selection) from derived proper function (grounded in design or replication), showing why reconstructed evolutionary dynamics do not establish direct proper function, arguing that the consumer mechanism in the hybrid can still operate but lacks the normative dimension that separates misrepresentation from breakdown, and addressing whether teleosemantics must therefore deny content to the hybrid or revise its naturalization conditions. The answer should be polished but need not cite specific texts by title or page.

Third, a marking scheme out of 30 marks, with explicit separation between an A-range answer (28–30) and a B-range answer (24–27). For the A range, list the specific moves that must be present—e.g., explicit application of the direct/derived distinction to the hybrid case; recognition that reconstructed history is not inherited selection; engagement with the consumer mechanism without assuming it solves the problem automatically; and a clear conclusion about whether misrepresentation survives. For the B range, list what is acceptable but missing—e.g., correct application of standard teleosemantic vocabulary to biological cases but failure to extend it rigorously to the reconstructed-history scenario, or correct identification of the malfunction/misrepresentation boundary without addressing the epiphenomenalism threat. Also list specific mistakes that should cost marks: conflating misrepresentation with mere mechanical failure; treating evolutionary history as sufficient without addressing the “reconstructed” problem; describing Millikan’s view in generic terms without using it on the hybrid; or appealing to intuition about “what the cortex is for” without analyzing proper function. Do not ask for grading of student work—this is the standard against which answers will be judged.
