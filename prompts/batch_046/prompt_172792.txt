I retired a few years back after running a biochem lab for about thirty years, and lately I've been trying to make sense of philosophy instead of just experiments. Right now I'm stuck on the Wittgenstein-and-religion mess. For what feels like forever, scholars have been split between the fideist camp—who say religious belief is just a self-contained language game with its own internal rules—and the other side, who claim Wittgenstein left space for religious commitment to be rational, just not in a scientific way. Everytime I read a new paper it feels like they cite the same passages, make the same moves, and end up exactly where they started. It occured to me that the stall might not be about Wittgenstein at all, but about a hidden disagreement over what kind of thing "belief" actually is. In my old work we had calibration constants—parameters you don't derive from inside the model but hold fixed so the rest of the system can run. Could religious belief be functioning like that, a kind of hinge you don't test within the framework? Or is it closer to an empirical hypothesis you'd drop if the data turned bad? I think this distinction might be what's really driving the split, but I'm not sure if that's right or if I'm just importing science jargon where it doesn't belong.

So here's my actual question: is this debate substantive, or is it verbal—different people using "belief" and "rational" in different ways? Are they answering seperate questions, maybe one about grammar and one about psychology, while pretending they're talking about the same thing? And if you had to diagnose the impasse, what would actually count as progress—a new conceptual distinction, a reframing of the question, or is this just one of those arguments that can't be resolved because the starting intuitions are bedrock? I'd rather have the diagnosis than another survey of who said what.
