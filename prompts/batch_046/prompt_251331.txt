I just finished a long, somewhat heated argument with a friend about whether the modern Stoic revival—especially its adoption in therapy apps, corporate resilience programs, and popular self-help—actually preserves the philosophical content of the dichotomy of control or merely commodifies a stripped-down therapeutic technique. Rather than rehashing that debate, I want a defended agenda: which open conceptual questions in this space are genuinely worth working on, and in what order should someone with about two years of philosophical effort pursue them?

Specifically, I see three clusters. First, whether the therapeutic framing necessarily requires abandoning the metaphysical claim that externals are genuinely indifferent, or whether that claim can be bracketed without collapsing the practice. Second, whether the modern "trichotomy" framing—direct control, indirect influence, and no control—resolves ambiguities in the original Stoic taxonomy or instead obscures the distinction between what is up to us and what is not. Third, whether applying the dichotomy to collective or structural problems—climate anxiety, institutional failure, distributed harm—requires reconceiving "control" as something that can be shared or delegated, rather than strictly individual.

Please prioritize these, but do not just list them. I want dependency-aware reasoning: which question is upstream of the others, which would change the ranking if settled, and which are tractable for conceptual philosophy rather than empirical psychology. I also want to know which questions are overrated—settling them would not actually change how practitioners use the framework. Defend the ordering with explicit criteria, and tell me what evidence over the next couple of years would move one up or down the list. I am not asking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature; I want to know where limited philosophical effort should go and why.
