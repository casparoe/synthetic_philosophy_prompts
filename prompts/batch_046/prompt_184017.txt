Following a recent decision to leave a professional trajectory I had long justified to myself as the only viable path given my training and obligations, I have found Sartre’s analysis of radical freedom and bad faith to be not merely abstract but personally disquieting. The standard interpretations, however, seem to conflate two structurally different failures: one in which a person denies the weight of her facticity by treating herself as pure transcendence, and another in which she denies her freedom by treating those same constraints as deterministic causes. I want a formal one-year dissertation prospectus that turns this half-formed distinction into a tractable research project. The precise question should be: can bad faith be reconstructed as a failure in the temporal synthesis through which the for-itself assumes its facticity, such that the past is misappropriated not merely as excuse but as ontological cause? Existing commentary generally treats bad faith as a unitary phenomenon of self-deception about freedom, which leaves open whether Sartre’s framework can adequately distinguish performative inauthenticity from the structurally enforced rationalizations of those living under severe constraint. The proposal should explain why splitting bad faith into facticity-denial and freedom-denial resolves puzzles that the unitary account cannot, particularly regarding moral responsibility in oppressive contexts without collapsing into either victim-blaming or libertarian fantasy. The method should be laid out month by month—say, months one and two on reconstructing the temporal dimensions of facticity-assumption in Sartre’s major ontological work; months three and four on building the two-part taxonomy; months five and six on applying it to cases of constrained choice drawn from his later ethical and political writings. It should specify what a successful result looks like—ideally a distinction that survives Sartre’s insistence that all facticity is always already transcended, while clarifying responsibility—and what would count as failure, such as the taxonomy collapsing back into the unitary account or proving normatively redundant. The scope must be realistic for a single year, with no request for a bibliography, citation-based literature review, or reading list, since I will supply references myself. Please frame this as a polished proposal document arguing that the work is both worth doing and doable within the time available.
