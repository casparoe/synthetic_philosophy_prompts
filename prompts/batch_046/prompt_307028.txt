Write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines followed by a three-line envoi—addressing the institutional paradox of armed neutrality under universal conscription, as practiced in Switzerland and Finland. The concept to render is the suspension of the citizen-soldier: every able-bodied citizen is trained, armed, and enrolled in a reserve structure not so that war will be fought, but so that invasion is rendered unthinkable by the credible, distributed threat of collective resistance. Choose six end-words that belong to this suspended state—such as rifle, closet, border, calendar, silence, and reserve—and rotate them through the sestina’s prescribed pattern so that their recurrence embodies the cyclical repetition of readiness drills that never culminate in deployment. In the envoi, gather all six words together in compressed form to represent the single, unrealized obligation that binds the citizen to the state. The poem should be philosophically precise enough that a scholar of civil-military relations recognizes the logic of deterrence-through-diffusion, yet it should stand as a poem for a reader unfamiliar with conscription. Do not resolve the tension into either patriotic celebration or anti-militarist critique; let the form hold the paradox open, as the institution itself does.
