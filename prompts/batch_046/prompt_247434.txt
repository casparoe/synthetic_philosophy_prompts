Write a strict pantoum—eight quatrains with the classic interlocking repetition of lines 2 and 4 ascending into lines 1 and 3 of the next stanza—about the diachronic identity of a statutory corporation that has replaced every director, amended every article of incorporation, and relocated its registered office twice, yet continues to claim the same founding charter number. Assign the repeating lines so that one thread carries the vocabulary of endurantism (the corporation is wholly present at each date, identical in virtue of a single enduring substance, its boundaries fixed by legal fiat) and the other thread carries the vocabulary of perdurantism (only four-dimensional stages exist, linked by continuity relations, the charter number naming a process rather than a thing). Let the form do the philosophical work: the pantoum’s compulsory recycling should mirror the metaphysical standoff, with each recurrence feeling either like the reappearance of an enduring whole or like the succession of a new temporal part that inherits the old line. In the final stanza, where all four repeated lines converge, the two descriptions should be grammatically intertwined but conceptually unreconciled—no synthesis, only adjacency. The poem must treat the technical commitments precisely (wholly present at t, temporal parts, diachronic identity conditions, anti-essentialism about institutional kind) rather than using them as decorative metaphors, and it should leave the tension open, as if the form itself cannot decide between them. Aim for about thirty-two lines, maintain the pantoum structure without deviation, and write in a register that assumes the reader knows why a metaphysician cares whether a corporation survives total turnover.
