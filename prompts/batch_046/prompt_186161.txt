I'm preparing the final assessment for a graduate seminar on self-reference and the limits of formalization. I want an essay question that forces students to work through the relationship between Hofstadter's idea of jumping out of the system—JOOTSing—and the structural deadlock illustrated by the MU-puzzle in Hofstadter's discussion of formal self-reference, rather than simply repeating the standard exposition. The students should determine whether this maneuver genuinely escapes the system in Hofstadter's sense, or whether it reproduces the same kind of internal blockage that prevents the MU-puzzle from being resolved within its native formal framework, and they should defend their position by explaining the specific mechanism—particularly the distinction between object-level derivation and meta-level assertion—that makes the MU-puzzle intractable from inside. Please draft the exam question, a model answer of roughly 700 words, and a marking scheme built around five required moves: correct identification of why the MU-puzzle cannot be solved internally; precise characterization of what a full JOOTS requires; analysis of whether the tethered jump meets or violates those escape conditions; identification of one concrete consequence if the retention rule were dropped; and explicit avoidance of the common mistake of treating syntactic undecidability as mere epistemic limitation. Assign point values for each criterion totaling 25 marks, and include a brief note on how a B-level response typically mishandles the analysis of the tethered jump.
