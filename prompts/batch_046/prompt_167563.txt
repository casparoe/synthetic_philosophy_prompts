I am preparing a brief for a reading group on self-reference and emergence, and I want a consistency check on my commitments before I defend them. Here is my current package:

(1) I accept the reality of tangled hierarchies: lower-level structures can generate higher-level systems that feed back causally onto those same lower levels, as with genetic code and the machinery that interprets it.

(2) I hold that every such loop requires an inviolate level—a stratum that is not itself interpreted or constructed by anything inside the loop—on pain of an infinite regress of observers or interpreters. Escher's Drawing Hands illustrates the point precisely because the paper and the artist lie outside the frame.

(3) I am a physicalist about consciousness, but I treat the explanatory gap not as a missing physical fact but as evidence that we have mislocated the inviolate level in the case of subjective experience.

(4) I take downward causation to be genuinely efficacious, not merely epiphenomenal: high-level patterns can constrain low-level dynamics, as evolutionary pressures constrain molecular pathways.

(5) I deny that physics contains any absolutely fundamental level; every proposed base—quarks, fields, or strings—is embedded in a theoretical framework that could itself belong to a larger hierarchical loop.

Please audit these for joint tenability. Specifically: does (2) conflict with (5), and if so, how severely? Does (3) smuggle in a form of non-physicalism despite (4)? Is the tension between (1) and (2) only apparent? Rank the genuine conflicts by severity, indicate the least costly revision for each, and tell me which single abandonment—(2) or (5)—would restore the most coherence to the set.
