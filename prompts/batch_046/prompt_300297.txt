There's this frustrating deadlock in AI-philosophy circles about attributing beliefs to LLMs via the intentional stance. The predictive-attribution crowd says if treating the model as having beliefs improves forecasting and intervention, thats sufficient. The mechanistic-transparency crowd says without identifiable internal states playing the right causal-functional roles its just useful fiction, no matter how good the predicitions are. But heres the thing: both camps agree on every benchmark result and both invoke Dennett-style reasoning, yet they never converge. Is the stall actually about a hidden normative disagreement—whether explanatory utility licenses ontological commitment—or is it verbal dispute about what counts as a belief? Or maybe its a bedrock intuition clash about whether minds need embodied world-coupling that LLMs lack? I want a real diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is, not a survey. And be honest: could any empirical finding—say, an interpretability result showing persistent representational structures—actually move the needle, or are we stuck in a circle?
