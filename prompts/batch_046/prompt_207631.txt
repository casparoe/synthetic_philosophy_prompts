I've been arguing with friends from the rationalist forums about Moore's paradox — specifically whether the absurdity of saying "It's raining, but I don't believe it" is a logical contradiction, a pragmatic misfire, or something deeper about the transparency of first-person belief attribution. For a taxonomy I'm drafting for our philosophy-of-language study group, I want a decision tree that sorts actual positions by how they answer concrete diagnostic questions, rather than by the philosopher's name. The tree should start from whether you think first-person belief reports carry a kind of authority analogous to perceptual reports, then branch through cases: can you sincerely assert the omissive form without paradox; does substituting "desire" or "intend" for "believe" preserve the absurdity; is the problem solved by treating the utterance as expressing two incompatible commitments rather than describing one mental state? I want named leaves for the standard positions — the Wittgensteinian "no contradiction but deep absurdity" view, the Shoemaker-style doxastic contradiction account, the expressivist or two-commitment theory, and the Gricean pragmatic explanation — plus an unoccupied branch for a hybrid I'm playing with, which I'll call "transparent commitment theory": belief is transparent to the agent in the sense that asserting it commits her to acting on it, but she lacks any special introspective access to whether she holds it. Each leaf should state the view's most awkward commitment, and the tree should flag any pairs that the questions can't separate — for instance, whether the pragmatic and the expressivist accounts really diverge on the omissive case or only in their theoretical vocabulary. Please present the taxonomy with clearly labelled branch nodes phrased as yes/no or either/or questions that a reader could answer without knowing the labels in advance, note any logically possible combinations that appear uninhabited and briefly say whether that is because they are incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and write the whole thing in polished British English suitable for a blog handout. No bibliographies or reading lists, please.
