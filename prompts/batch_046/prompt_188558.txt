Write a short story about a lone navigator on a dormant generation ship who discovers that the vessel's long-deactivated Choice Oracle—an algorithm that simulated thousands of copies of her decision architecture—filled or emptied a hidden supply vault decades earlier, not by predicting her action, but by testing whether her reasoning contained a self-referential causal loop linking her present deliberation to the simulation's parameter initialisation. The Oracle's seal remains intact, yet she has reconstructed its logic: it employed a counterfactual-stability criterion rather than predictive accuracy, so the vault's contents depend on whether her current reasoning process is structurally identical to the simulations that received the reward. She must decide whether to take only the visible rations, or to trust that her reasoning matches the rewarded simulations and open the vault expecting abundance. In telling the story, explore how her awareness that she is reasoning about her own reasoning trace alters the causal graph, and whether free will survives when the past allocation was contingent on the very structure of her present thought.
