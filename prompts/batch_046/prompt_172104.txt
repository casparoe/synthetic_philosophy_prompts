I'm working out a metaphysical package for a seminar on Bradley, propositions, and nominalism, and I want an audit of whether these commitments actually cohere before I defend them in print. Here is where I have landed:

(1) Bradley's regress is decisive against treating relations as full-blooded entities: if R relates a and b, and R itself needs relating to them, the regress is vicious rather than benign.

(2) I therefore reject both universals and bare particulars; there are only particular tropes—particularized properties and relations that are essentially modified by the entities they characterize, with no need for a separate substratum.

(3) Relational tropes stop the regress without a further unifier: a's being-R-to-b is a single trope that is essentially co-modifying a and b, not an entity that stands between them awaiting its own relation.

(4) I am a realist about propositions: "Socrates is wise" and "Socrates is foolish" are both genuinely unified structured entities, not merely lists or sets of tropes.

(5) I deny that there is any primitive "compositional" or "structural" relation that binds the parts of a proposition together, since that reproduces exactly the relating-relation Bradley attacked.

(6) False propositions have the same kind of unity as true ones; in false cases the relevant tropes are present in the proposition but absent from the world, functioning as representational or "shadow" tropes rather than as instantiated modifications.

(7) I want all of this to stay compatible with physicalism: tropes are ultimately physical modifications, and there are no non-physical entities in the ontology.

My questions: Does (3) cohere with (4) given (5)? If relational tropes need no unifier, what prevents a proposition from being a mere heap of tropes rather than one structured thing? Does (6) commit me to uninstantiated tropes that float free of the physical world, undermining (2) and (7)? And is there a hidden tension in (2)'s rejection of bare particulars, given that tropes seem to require something to be tropes of—something that looks suspiciously like a bare particular? Which tensions are genuine, which dissolve on inspection, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity, say exactly what each revision costs, and do not assume in advance which commitment I am most attached to.
