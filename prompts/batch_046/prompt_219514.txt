I have been working through the conceptual foundations of teleosemantics in a recent philosophy-of-mind reading group, and I find myself caught on a problem that goes deeper than the familiar disjunction objection usually presented at an introductory level. I understand the core claim: mental content is fixed by the biological functions of the mechanisms that produce representations, and misrepresentation is possible because a mechanism can perform its proper function—say, detecting flies—while the environment contains something that is not a fly, like a BB pellet. The mechanism is not broken; the representation is false. What puzzles me is how this framework handles evolutionary exaptation, specifically the distinction sometimes drawn between direct and derived proper functions. Imagine a representational mechanism originally selected for tracking predator movement that is later co-opted, through selection for its effects in a social context, into tracking dominance hierarchies. If this mechanism now fires in response to a social cue that structurally resembles predator movement but is inappropriate even by the standards of its new social-tracking role, we seem to face a question about which functional history fixes the content. Is the state misrepresentational relative to the derived social function? Or does the persistence of the ancestral direct function mean that there is no single determinate content—only a competition between two functional specifications? I want to understand whether teleosemantics needs a principled rule for privileging one evolutionary history over another in order to preserve determinate content, or whether it can allow that exapted mechanisms genuinely carry ambiguous or plural contents without collapsing into functional pluralism. More specifically, does accommodating such co-opted mechanisms force the view to rely on current use, learning history, or speaker intention—resources that teleosemantics explicitly rejects—to resolve which function is authoritative?

Please lead with the bottom line: can teleosemantics accommodate evolutionary co-option of representational mechanisms without dissolving the determinacy of mental content, and if so, how does it adjudicate between competing direct and derived functional specifications without smuggling in non-biological norms?
