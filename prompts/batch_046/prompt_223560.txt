I've been playing with this idea of free-floating rationales — normative reasons that don't need a reasoner, not even hypothetically. Not just objective moral facts, but reasons anchored in the modal structure of possiblity spaces themselves: there's a rational pull toward S rather than T even in a universe with zero minds, grounded in something like harmony across worlds rather than welfare or agency. Play it straight: what would have to be true for this to collapse? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward — does it secretly smuggle in an implicit agent, does it turn into an ugly Platonism about norms, can it explain why reasons feel like they address us? And be honest about which ones defenders of this view would actually concede hurt them, versus which they'd just shrug off.
