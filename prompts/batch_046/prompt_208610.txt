I have arranged for my employer to automatically deposit twenty percent of my salary into a restricted education fund for my child; the account permits withdrawals only after a seventy-two-hour delay and upon submission of a written justification. Despite this, I have repeatedly filed requests claiming that the money is needed for unexpected child-related expenses—special tutoring, medical co-pays, or enrichment activities—only to redirect the released funds toward discretionary personal purchases, subsequently covering the shortfall with high-interest borrowing. One mentor advises me to dismantle the restriction entirely and instead practice making conscious saving decisions each day, arguing that outsourcing my will to a structural barrier is preventing me from developing genuine self-discipline. Another advisor suggests tightening the mechanism—adding a mandatory co-signer—without interrogating the underlying pattern. I find myself uncertain whether the core problem is that my commitment device is too weak, that I misunderstand willpower as something that can be delegated to external rules, or that I have been systematically reframing my impulsive desires as acts of parental care. How should I think about this dilemma?
