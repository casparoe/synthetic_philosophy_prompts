I'm trying to figure out when it's legit to call a germ, climate shift, or volcano an actual "historical actor" versus when that's just sloppy environmental determinism. Here are my gut verdicts.

Tambora in 1815: I'm okay saying it acted as a kind of co-author of the 1816 crop failures and even of Mary Shelley writing Frankenstein indoors. It forced responses and reshaped culture, not just background noise.

The plague bacillus Yersinia pestis in 1348: not an actor. It's a pathogen, not an agent with aims or strategy. Tracing its spread mechanistically is fine, but anthropomorphizing it into a historical player feels wrong.

The 1876-78 El Niño pattern in the global famines: I'm genuinely torn. Part of me wants to call ENSO an actor in the food-system collapse; part thinks that's determinist garbage because it erases colonial tax policy and grain extraction. I keep going back and forth on this one.

Anthropogenic warming in the Syrian drought-to-conflict narrative: treating climate change as an actor that "caused" the war is wrong. It's a stress multiplier at best, and framing it otherwise lets human regimes off the hook.

The Little Ice Age being blamed for witch trials or the Thirty Years War: overreach. Climate is backdrop, not an actor.

Eyjafjallajökull in 2010 shutting down airspace: clearly an actor. It forced immediate political and economic decisions in real time.

So is there one principle that saves Tambora and Eyjafjallajökull as actors, keeps the bacillus and Syria-climate out, and resolves my El Niño mess? Or do I have to drop one of these?
