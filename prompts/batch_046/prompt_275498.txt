everyone in con law circles keeps using the Ulysses-and-the-mast story to defend constitutional precommitment — entrenchment clauses, supermajority rules, judicial review, that kind of thing. the idea is a democracy ties itself up now so it can hear the siren song of populism later without crashing on the rocks, and this is supposed to license binding future voters with rigid rules.

i want to know how far the analogy actually holds. give me the correspondance table — what plays Ulysses, the ropes, the mast, the sirens, the crew, and the part where he makes them plug their ears. then the breaks: where does the map fail? does it smuggle in that the future self is an enemy to be restrained rather than a legitimate democratic successor? and does the conclusion — that precommitment is rational and democratically ok — survive once you notice Ulysses was restraining himself, not being restrained by the crew, and that he actually wanted to hear the song?

no reading lists needed, just the audit.
