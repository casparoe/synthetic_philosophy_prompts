Construct a thought experiment engineered to isolate omission causation in a structurally distributed, non-agential setting: a realistic organizational or engineering safety system is designed so that a protective intervention is automatically suppressed under specific conditions, and that suppression allows a serious harm to occur, yet no individual person decides against intervening and no physical mechanism connects an absent event to the damage. Specify the verdicts so that a counterfactual or interventionist account treats the non-occurrence as a genuine cause, a mechanistic or process account denies causation on the grounds that absence is not a relatum in any physical mechanism, and an agency-based normative account also denies causation because there is no identifiable duty-bearing agent who failed to act. Keep the scenario to one paragraph, set in an ordinary workplace or public-infrastructure context, with no science-fiction elements, demons, perfect predictors, or exotic physics. After presenting the case, walk through the reasoning for each of the three accounts, identify the precise structural feature doing the work—namely the combination of counterfactual dependence with the absence of both a causal process and an individual agent—explain how removing that feature (for example, by making a single operator responsible for triggering the intervention) collapses or shifts the divergence between the theories, and present the strongest reply available to the mechanist defending their negative verdict. Conclude by stating explicitly what the case is designed to test: whether omission causation can be sustained independently of both physical mechanism and personal agency, or whether it always collapses into one of those two conditions.
