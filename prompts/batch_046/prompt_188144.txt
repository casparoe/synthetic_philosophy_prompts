You are delivering the opening address at the inauguration of the Brenner Center for Hermeneutics and Observational Science. Half the audience are biblical scholars; half are working astrophysicists. Neither group wants a generic sermon on harmony, and both will notice if you reduce Galileo to a modern secular icon. Your speech should take about eight minutes and should build its entire framework around Galileo's actual hermeneutical rules from his 1615 letter to Christina of Lorraine, Grand Duchess of Tuscany. Use his distinction between the mechanics of the heavens and the path to salvation, but do not stop there: you must also incorporate his argument that scriptural language is accommodated to the capacities of ordinary people, and his specific claim about the miracle in Joshua — that when scripture touches natural phenomena, interpretation must defer to demonstrated knowledge, with the Joshua miracle being reconcilable with heliocentrism if one understands God as altering the motion of the sun rather than arresting a geocentric sphere. The philosophical structure should do real work: you must not invoke anachronistic frameworks like non-overlapping magisteria; you must not claim that science and religion are never in tension, since Galileo explicitly argued that they had come into tension and that the Church erred in suppressing a demonstrable physical theory; and you must avoid any statement Galileo himself would reject, such as the claim that scriptural authority and natural philosophy operate in entirely separate domains with no possible conflict. End with a concrete charge or image that both scholars and scientists can carry into the workshop sessions. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Do not quote Galileo verbatim; paraphrase is welcome.
