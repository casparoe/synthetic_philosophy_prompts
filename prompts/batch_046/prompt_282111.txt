There's this analogy people keep using when they talk about whether qualia are even a coherent thing to posit -- they compare having a quale like seeing red to opening a private drawer with a color swatch inside. Only you have the key, the swatch isn't made of physical stuff, and describing the drawer's mechanics misses the actual color of the swatch. Dennett's quining move is then like saying you can gradually replace the drawer's wood with plastic and never notice when the swatch stops being 'real' color, which supposedly shows there's no discrete inner object to begin with. Does the conclusion that qualia are incoherent survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy doing all the work? Also, is there a better analogy that captures the functionalist counter-argument without the hidden observer? I think Chase and Sanborn had a twist where the swatch is made of the same material as the drawer itself, so there's no separate 'inside,' but I might be misremembering that.
