The author writes: 'Benatar's asymmetry is basically that suffering is bad and pleasure is genuinely good, but only suffering counts against procreation, because a non-existent person doesn't miss out on pleasure but does avoid suffering. So having kids is always wrong—you're forcing harm onto someone without any compensating good for them specifically. He doesn't claim life is usually awful for those already alive; he just thinks it's wrong to start the harm-machine. Suicide isn't recommended because once you're born the damage is done and dying just causes more suffering for others.'

This feels mostly right but I'm suspicious there's a subtle distortion in there—maybe about how the asymmetry compares existence to non-existence rather than 'counting against' in a consequentialist way?
