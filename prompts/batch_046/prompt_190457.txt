I'm in this seminar on Hegel and recognition and I've been trying to apply the master-slave dialectic to how people actually get recognized today—through jobs, credentials, algorithms, all that mediated stuff—and I think I built an argument that proves way too much but I can't find where the leak is. So here's the chain: (1) Hegel says self-consciousness only becomes fully actual when it's recognized by another self-consciousness, not just reflected back like an object. (2) But to recognize someone else as a self-consciousness, you yourself already have to possess self-consciousness, otherwise you wouldn't know what you're recognizing. (3) That makes recogntion circular: A needs B's recognition to be complete, but B can only grant it if B is already complete. (4) The dialectic breaks the circle through the life-and-death struggle, where both risk their natural existence and thereby prove they're not mere objects. (5) Now here's this idea I'm calling proxy-recognition: in modern life almost all recognition is mediated—degrees, job titles, verification badges, algorithmic recommendations—where the immediate source is an institution or system that isn't a self-consciousness at all. (6) Even when individuals are involved, like a hiring committee, they act as proxies for institutional criteria, so the recognition isn't coming from one free consciousness to another but from a structual position. (7) And since modern hierarchical relations—employment, citizenship, even follower dynamics—enter without anything like the life-risk struggle, neither party has performed the radical freedom that makes them capable of breaking the circle. (8) So by Hegel's lights, modern mediated recognition can never produce genuine self-consciousness; it's always structurally deficient. (9) But people clearly do develop robust self-understanding through jobs, citizenship, and social roles, so (8) can't be right unless modern life is just one big illusion of selfhood. (10) Therefore either recognition doesn't actually require another self-consciousness, or the life-struggle is just decorative storytelling, or modern self-consciousness is fake.

My professor suggested the problem might be an equivocation on "recognition" between the dialectic's origin story and its structural conditions, but I'm not convinced, and I really want to keep (1) and (9) because they feel true. Can you tell me exactly which step to reject?
