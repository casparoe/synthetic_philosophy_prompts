Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises if we treat Nietzsche's scattered remarks on diet, digestion, and climate in Ecce Homo and his correspondence not as incidental autobiographical details but as integral to a physiological aesthetics—the view that metabolic and environmental conditions directly shape the rank-order of values. Specifically, illustrate the tension between this dietary vitalism and his explicit rejection of mechanistic causation and deterministic physiology in The Gay Science and Beyond Good and Evil, and indicate whether resolving the tension requires collapsing his philosophy of embodiment into the kind of reductive biological materialism he consistently opposes.
