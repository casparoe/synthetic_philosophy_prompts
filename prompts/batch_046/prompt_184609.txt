I work in institutional mechanism design, and my professional vocabulary is built around commitment devices, agent functions, preference architectures, endogenous versus exogenous constraints, and the distinction between a system that optimizes over a fixed preference profile and one that treats certain preferences as constitutive of the agent's identity rather than as inputs to be traded away. I want you to restate Williams's integrity objection — including the charge that the utilitarian demands the agent have one thought too many — entirely in that vocabulary. Be precise about what maps onto an agent function being saturated or corrupted, what corresponds to a commitment device that binds the agent against certain trades, and whether integrity functions like an exogenous hard constraint or like an endogenous feature of the preference architecture itself. Crucially, I want to know whether the residue indicates that mechanism design is missing something essential about moral agency, or whether Williams's notion of integrity is actually less substantive than it appears — perhaps reducible to a form of preference rigidity or identity-preservation that a well-designed institutional system could accommodate without conceptual loss.
