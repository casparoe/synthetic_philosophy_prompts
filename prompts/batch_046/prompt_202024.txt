My colleague Dr. V has staked out specific positions on what Descartes's cogito proves in several boundary cases. She holds that in the second meditation it establishes only that thinking occurs at present, not that a substantial self endures through time. She says that if a split-brain patient with only left-hemisphere reportability produces a first-person judgment of self-existence, it proves that hemisphere's awareness but not a unified person. She denies that a single-step artificial system running one inference without integration or memory proves anything about its own existence. Yet she grants that if a colonial organism such as a Portuguese man o' war collectively produces the judgment, it proves the colony is a cognitive subject rather than its constituent polyps. I'm less interested in her specific answers than in the rule she seems to be running. Would she say that a current large language model producing a contextually coherent first-person judgment of self-existence proves the model exists as a thinker? What about a philosophical zombie that behaves identically to a conscious human, including producing the same judgment? I want the candidate rules that fit her four verdicts equally well, what each predicts for the language model and for the zombie, your best guess at her actual view with reasons for preferring that rule, and the single further case—between an amnesiac who retains bare present awareness but no autobiographical continuity and a sleepwalker who utters the judgment without wakefulness—that would best tell the rules apart.
