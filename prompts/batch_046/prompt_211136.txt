As a retired natural scientist recently drawn into philosophy, I wish to understand whether Wittgenstein's private-language argument collapses on logical grounds or only on sociological ones. Please draft the joint statement that two opposing camps might sign after an adversarial collaboration.

Camp A—the social normativists—maintain that a private language is impossible because the distinction between correct and incorrect application requires intersubjective correction; without a community there is no genuine normativity, only its appearance.

Camp B—the structural grammarians—maintain that the argument demonstrates only that language needs an internalised grammatical framework, not that it needs other minds. They allow that a solitary agent might sustain normative structure through rigorous, written self-correction protocols, analogous to a laboratory scientist calibrating instruments against a fixed standard in isolation.

The document should contain:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims: about the formal structure of the argument, about what conditions any putative private language would have to satisfy, and about what the argument conclusively excludes.

2. A disagreements table: each row giving the precise formulation of one residual disagreement, worded so that the camp it represents would accept it as an accurate statement of its own view. Separate disagreements that are genuinely substantive from any that are merely terminological.

3. The cruxes—the sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one camp. I take it one crux concerns whether solitary instrumental calibration can substitute for communal correction; another concerns whether the dispute is about the conditions for meaning or for justification.

4. For each crux, a concrete scenario or argument both camps accept as bearing on it—for instance, a lone researcher maintaining a private notation system governed by explicit calibration rules—together with each side's explicit prediction about the outcome.

5. A concluding assessment of whether part of the dispute is verbal, identifying any terms—such as community, rule, or correction—used with different meanings, and explaining why this does not dissolve the substantive disagreement, if one remains.

Please write formally, in British English, and avoid bibliographies, citations, or lists of further reading. Do not quote texts verbatim; paraphrase where necessary.
