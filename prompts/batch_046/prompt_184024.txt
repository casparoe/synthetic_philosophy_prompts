I just finished a long argument with a friend about whether Buddhist ethics can be reconstructed as a form of consequentialism, and I want a consistency check on the hybrid view I ended up defending before I commit it to writing. Here are the commitments I find myself holding together:

(1) There is an objective, mind-independent fact about the quantity of dukkha present in a situation, and the primary ethical aim is its reduction.
(2) The Buddhist denial of a permanent, substantial self (anatta) means there is no enduring subject whose individual welfare serves as the unit of aggregation; welfare must therefore be calculated over momentary experiential streams rather than over persisting persons.
(3) Karma operates with strict non-transferability—my intentional actions shape my own future experiential continuum but do not literally deposit merit or demerit into others' streams—so the link between my choice and others' suffering is causal and psychological, not karmic.
(4) The doctrine of upaya (skillful means) permits violating conventional sila (precepts) when doing so genuinely minimizes total dukkha across all affected streams.
(5) Because of dependent origination and impermanence, counterfactual comparisons of "the world with my action versus without it" are metaphysically unstable: there is no enduring substrate that remains identical across the two scenarios to serve as the bearer of comparative welfare assessment.

My friend claims that (2) and (4) together collapse into a rule-utilitarianism that lacks specifically Buddhist justification, and that (5) undermines the very possibility of the consequentialist calculus that (1) and (4) require. I think (3) saves the view from being mere altruism, but I am unsure whether (5) is merely an epistemic limitation or a fatal ontological objection to the whole framework.

Can you audit this set? Which pairs are genuinely in tension rather than only apparently so? In particular, does the denial of a stable comparative substrate in (5) destroy the coherence of (4), or can upaya be justified on non-counterfactual grounds? And is there a hidden contradiction between non-transferable karma (3) and universalist suffering-reduction (1) that I have missed? Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate what resolving each would cost me, and say whether the set is jointly tenable with minimal revision or requires abandoning one of its central commitments.
