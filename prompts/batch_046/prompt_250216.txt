Write an essay on whether democratic citizens have a moral duty to vote strategically for the "lesser evil" in proportional-representation systems where that party will enter a governing coalition with a partner whose policies the voter considers morally intolerable. Frame the controversy around the tension between instrumental justification—voting as a causal means to minimize harm—and expressive justification—voting as an affirmation of one's values—while introducing the problem of "distributed authorization": the idea that in coalition governments, voters collectively authorize policies they individually reject, so strategic support for a lesser-evil partner may make one a partial author of objectionable outcomes. Weigh the arguments that (i) the instrumental duty to prevent worse outcomes dominates and permits strategic voting; (ii) the expressive dimension makes such strategic compromise a form of moral self-betrayal that undermines democratic legitimacy; and (iii) coalition structures diffuse responsibility so thoroughly that neither instrumental nor expressive reasoning applies in the standard way. Reach and justify a specific conclusion about whether voters in these conditions have a duty to vote, and whether that duty requires, permits, or forbids strategic support for the coalition-bound lesser evil. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then present your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
