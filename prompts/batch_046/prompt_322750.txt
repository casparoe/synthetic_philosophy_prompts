I am moderating a colloquium Q&A with an unnamed philosopher of decision theory who defends a specific reading of Herbert Simon's concept of satisficing. Her view is that satisficing is not merely a second-best cognitive adaptation to bounded rationality but a normatively autonomous strategy that is structurally superior to optimization under conditions of radical uncertainty—specifically because it prevents decision-makers from covertly treating aspiration levels as hidden parameters to be optimized. She extends this to institutional design: organizations should be engineered not to reduce cognitive load, but to render aspiration thresholds opaque and non-revisable in real time, a design principle she calls threshold opacity.

Draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that stay in the weeds of her actual argument and avoid generic biographical openings. I want at least one question on each of the following: how she distinguishes her ecological claim from Gigerenzer-style ecological rationality without collapsing into it; the objection that making thresholds opaque simply relocates the optimization problem to the institutional designer who sets the threshold; what conceptual or empirical marker could distinguish genuine satisficing from implicit optimization with a fixed aspiration parameter; how she handles aspiration adaptation—downward adjustment of standards after failure—without that process becoming a hidden form of utility maximization; and whether her prescription for institutional threshold opacity survives worries about path dependence or strategic manipulation by those who control the threshold-setting mechanism.

For each area, include a follow-up keyed to her most probable reply. For example, if she answers the relocation objection by insisting that the institutional designer operates under democratic deliberation rather than individual optimization, press her on whether democratic deliberation itself can be modeled without either satisficing or optimizing at the meta-level. The questions should anticipate standard replies from the bounded-rationality literature and push past them.
