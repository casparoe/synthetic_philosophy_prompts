I serve as the student representative on the Regional Adolescent Palliative Care Ethics Council. We must issue a recommendation by Friday on Case 2024-09: a 17-year-old with a progressive neurodegenerative condition executed an advance refusal of mechanical ventilation at age 16, specifying refusal upon reaching Stage III cognitive impairment. She is now at Stage III, yet for the past three weeks she has consistently and articulately expressed a wish to continue ventilation, describing her current experience as meaningful despite the impairment. Her parents invoke the advance refusal; her treating physicians are divided.

The philosophical issue is not routine autonomy versus family authority. It concerns whether a prior directive retains binding moral authority over present expressed preferences when the change in values is caused by the very condition the directive was designed to anticipate—what might be called value drift through diachronic identity disruption. One view holds that anticipatory autonomy protects the patient from preferences corrupted by the disease process; the opposing view holds that present relational continuity and sentient experience carry authority regardless of causal origin.

Please draft the Council's formal decision memo—recommending either to honor the present wish with conditions, to honor the advance refusal, or to impose a time-limited trial—addressed to the clinical director. The memo should include: (1) a clear recommendation with a concrete timeframe; (2) reasoning that treats the anticipatory-autonomy argument seriously, including its strongest response to the value-drift objection; (3) reasoning that treats the present-experience argument seriously, including its acknowledgment of identity-continuity concerns; and (4) a practical condition or monitoring plan that responds to both sides. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific philosophical texts.
