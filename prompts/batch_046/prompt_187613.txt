I've been trying to wrap my head around illusionism about phenomenal consciousness - the idea that all our feeling of being aware, like seeing red or feeling pain, is basically just our brain constructing a story after the fact, and there's no actual "inner movie" or Cartesian theater where it all plays out. I heard someone mention Dennets multiple drafts model and it kinda clicked - like maybe consciousness is just alot of competing narratives and none of them are the "real" experience. I'm not totally sure I understand it right, but I'm leaning towards thinking phenomenal consciousness is more of a user-illusion than a real thing.

But I want to be honest about what could actually knock this over. It's not enough to say "but I feel it" - I get that illusions feel real. What I keep wondering is: if consciousness is an illusion, what's the real thing that's being misrepresented? With a bent stick in water, there's still a real stick. So if my feeling of seeing red is an illusion, is the real thing just neurons firing? And if so, doesn't that still require some kind of subject that's being fooled, which brings back the theater I'm trying to get rid of? Or is there a way to have the illusion without anyone being in the audience? I want to know what would genuinely count against illusionism - maybe some result about introspection, or about how the brain reports its own states, or some logical problem with saying "there's an illusion but no one experiences it." Which of these are fatal versus just awkward? And do illusionists themselves admit any of these hit home, or do they just dismiss everything? I want an honest map of how exposed this view really is, not just the standard objections you hear everywhere.
