I have been working through Dennett’s account of real patterns and the three explanatory stances—the physical, the design, and the intentional—in a graduate seminar on philosophy of mind, and over the past several months the distinction has become far more than academic. I am currently the primary caregiver for a parent with advancing dementia, which means I spend much of each day translating her behavior across levels: neurological explanations (the physical stance), accounts of broken cognitive architecture and failed predictive routines (the design stance), and the occasional, fragile attributions of belief and desire that still seem to illuminate her actions (the intentional stance). Recently, I have been reading about predictive-processing models of cognition—where the brain is characterized as a hierarchical Bayesian machine that minimizes prediction error without ever representing propositional attitudes in any robust sense—and I have begun to worry that these models dissolve the very distinction Dennett wants to preserve.

My confusion runs like this. Dennett claims that intentional-level patterns are genuinely real, not mere fictions, because they support counterfactual-rich predictions that are unavailable at lower levels of description. But if a design-stance account built on predictive coding can reproduce every successful prediction of the intentional stance—modeling behavior entirely through hierarchical priors and error-correction dynamics—then it seems the intentional pattern does no independent causal or explanatory work. In other words, if all the predictive labor can be carried out by the design stance, then the intentional stance appears to be a heuristic overlay on a system that never actually instantiates beliefs or desires. So I find myself asking: hasn’t the rise of predictive-processing neuroscience shown that Dennett’s intentional patterns are causally redundant, and therefore not real in any ontologically serious sense? Or am I conflating the idea that one stance can reproduce another’s predictions with the idea that the reproduced patterns are therefore unreal—specifically, am I wrongly assuming that for an intentional pattern to be real, it must do explanatory work that the design stance is in principle unable to do?
