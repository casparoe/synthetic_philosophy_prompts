I've been discussing philosophy of science with my rationalist reading group, and we keep using the phrase paradigms are incommensurable to shut down any attempt to compare an older theoretical framework with a newer one—most recently when someone argued that a quantum-mechanical account of a particular system is not "closer to reality" than a well-constructed classical-mechanical model of the same phenomenon, but just different in a way that makes comparison meaningless. I want to treat that slogan as an object of analysis rather than a conversation-ender. Can you lay out the distinct precise theses it could be expressing, ranging from the trivial to the radical, and say for each whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or actually false?

In particular, please distinguish at least four readings: first, a weak semantic claim that some technical terms in different theories lack exact one-to-one translations; second, a stronger methodological claim that the standards of evidence, explanation, and problem-selection differ so radically that there is no shared metric for rational evaluation; third, the classic ontological claim that competing paradigms literally inhabit different worlds, so that entities in one do not exist in the other; and fourth, a more niche modular or local variant I am trying to articulate—where incommensurability is not global and revolutionary but localized and persistent, with different active subfields maintaining incompatible ontological commitments (for instance, about whether a given structure is a fundamental entity or an emergent approximation) without any crisis forcing resolution, and without either side denying the other scientific legitimacy. For each reading, tell me whether it actually supports the move of dismissing a direct comparison between, say, classical and quantum descriptions of the same system, or whether it is being misapplied.

I am not asking for a bibliography or a survey of the literature; I want a conceptual disambiguation with verdicts, especially on whether the modular local version avoids the standard objections to global Kuhnian incommensurability, and on which of these readings is the one people actually invoke when they use the slogan to end an argument about scientific progress.
