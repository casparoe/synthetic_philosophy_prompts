I was discusing this with a friend who runs a little community center, and we started imagining what an actually Tolstoy-approved art practice would look like if it wasn't just painting or books but something more direct.

So could you write a short story about someone who runs a small "feeling-room" in a rural town where people sit together and recieve transmitted emotions — no words, no images, just raw affect? The protagonist needs to be wrestling with Tolstoys criterias: does the session actually carry the artists feeling, can anyone understand it without special training, and does it bring people together or split them? Maybe include one specific session where an elderly woman transmits something ambiguous, part grief and part relief, and half the room feels it instantly while the other half feels nothing. The protagonist should have to decide whether the split means it's bad art, or whether her own confusion means she's failing to recieve it properly. Don't make it a sci-fi story about technology; I'm more interested in how the philosophical tension plays out in the room. Oh, and please don't cite anything or mention specific publication years — I'm just trying to see how the idea breathes in a concrete scene.
