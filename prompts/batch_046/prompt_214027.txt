I'm a longtime lurker on these kinds of forums, usually just reading about decision theory and history of science, but lately I've been digging into the old Browning vs Goldhagen debate about the police battalions — you know, Ordinary Men versus the willing executioners framing. And I keep noticing a pattern in how people argue about it that I don't have a word for.

Here's the thing. Someone gives a pure situationist story: ordinary guys, peer pressure, incremental steps, authority structures, the usual Milgram-style machinery. Then you push on it — okay, but other units in the same structure with the same orders didn't shoot. And suddenly they're talking about the cultural priming, the antisemitic ideology that made these particular guys receptive. So the situational account smuggles in a character/culture filter to explain selection.

Then flip it. Someone gives a pure ideological story: they killed because they were true believers, radicalized, eager. You push — okay, but how did they actually pull the trigger on kids? Normal inhibition doesn't just vanish. And suddenly they're talking about the bureaucracy breaking the task into pieces, the group reinforcement, the incremental desensitization. So the ideological account smuggles in the situational machinery to explain execution.

It's not just "both factors matter" — interactionism or whatever. What's weird is that each side, when defending its own primary cause, ends up treating the other cause as the hidden engine that covers exactly the gap the first one can't bridge. The two explanations aren't parallel; they're structurally coupled like that.

Is there an established name for this specific dependency loop, where rival explanations for the same atrocity each borrow the core mechanism of the other to patch their own hole? How is it different from plain multicausality, or from someone just being inconsistent? And if there's no term, what would you call it — and where are the boundary cases, like does it apply only when there's moral blame involved, or is it broader?

Sorry if that's rambling, I recieve this kind of looping argument alot in other domains too but the perpetrator literature is where it hits me hardest.
