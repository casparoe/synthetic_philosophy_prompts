hey so i run a tiny nonprofit newsroom, like eight people, and we recieve maybe a dozen anonymous tips a week that are basically moral testimony — someone writing "this builder is evil" or "that official is corrupt" with heavy moral language but shaky factual bones. i need a reusable proceedure my team can run in about 20 minutes to decide if we open an investigation, grounded in the philosophy of moral testimony (moral claims aren't factual reports, they get warped by self-deception, grandstanding, emotional contagion, etc.) but written for journalists with zero phil background.

the deliverable should be ordered steps, what counts as pass/fail, where we say hard no, where we hand off to the senior editor because it's values not facts, and an honest note on misfires. build in this niche constraint: there's an "inviolate evidentiary level" — moral urgency can never breach it — meaning if there's no independent factual corroboration below that line, we stop regardless of how outraged the tipster is. also say flat-out what the procedure can't do.

then run it on three cases:

1) a long anonymous email saying a local contractor cuts safety corners and "doesn't care about human life", with specific dates but zero docs.

2) a viral thread accusing a council member of hypocrisy — green vote, gas guzzler — huge engagement, but no direct harm claim.

3) a whistleblower with internal docs showing a charity misuses funds, framed as "moral betrayal of the community", clearly emotionally invested.

make one result go against my gut — maybe we have to drop the whistleblower despite the docs, or chase the viral hypocrisy thing.

also say what would change your mind: where's the reasoning weakest, and what counterexample or argument would make you abandon this proceedure?

no citations, no reading list, just the flowchart in words.
