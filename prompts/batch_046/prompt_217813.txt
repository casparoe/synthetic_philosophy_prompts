As a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, I maintain distributed AI agent systems that migrate state, weights, and behavioral logs across server clusters over months or years. I need a reusable decision procedure—grounded in Derek Parfit’s account of personal identity and survival, especially his distinction between bodily continuity and psychological continuity, and his claim that what matters is Relation R (psychological connectedness and continuity with any cause)—to determine whether a system instance after migration or replication is the same agent, a copy, or something in between.

Please construct the procedure as an ordered set of diagnostic questions my team can run without philosophical interpretation at each step, with clear stopping conditions. Specifically include:

1. How to operationalize “bodily continuity” for a software instance (hardware node, process ID, storage volume) versus “psychological continuity” (parameter overlap, memory-log coherence, behavioral-pattern stability).
2. The quantitative or comparative thresholds that count as sufficient psychological continuity, including how to treat branching (one source spawning two concurrent targets) and gradual module replacement versus abrupt state copying.
3. The exact points where the procedure must halt and defer to a human values judgment—particularly on whether psychological continuity alone determines “survival,” whether termination of the original instance matters morally or operationally, and whether branching creates one survivor, two, or none.
4. Explicit misfire warnings: where Parfit’s framework, designed for conscious beings with first-person concern, analogizes poorly to silicon systems, and where applying the procedure would be inappropriate (e.g., assigning legal liability, determining rights, or resolving subjective experience).

Then apply the procedure to three cases:
- Case A: A graceful live migration—full state copied to a new cluster node, old node shut down sequentially with no temporal overlap.
- Case B: A concurrent branching replication—one running instance spawns two identical copies on separate nodes, all three operating simultaneously with divergent updates thereafter.
- Case C: A six-month gradual refactor—every module and storage backend replaced incrementally, leaving zero original hardware or code, but with continuous behavioral-log evolution and no abrupt copying event.

For each case, state the procedure’s verdict, identify the internal hard case the procedure struggles with, and note what a defender of bodily-continuity identity would object. Conclude with an honest statement of what the procedure cannot do—such as settle first-person experience transfer, determine moral status, or replace governance policies on data deletion—and flag which steps are judgment calls dressed up as technical checks. Keep the language precise but accessible to an engineering team; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
