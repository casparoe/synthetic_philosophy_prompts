Write a taxonomy — a branching tree of positions — addressing the presumptuous-philosopher problem in anthropic reasoning. Root commitment: you accept that, in cosmological models containing vastly more observers, straightforward self-sampling reasoning makes belief in those models rational, and you regard this as counterintuitive. From this root, split the space by concrete diagnostic questions rather than by philosophical labels. First branch: do you resolve the tension by restricting the reference class of observers? Second: do you reject self-sampling in favor of self-indication? Third: do you retain self-sampling but replace uniform observer-weighting with weighting by the cognitive accessibility of observer-moments? Fourth: do you bite the bullet and accept that large-model belief is indeed rational? At each split, include a concrete case — for instance, a nested simulation hierarchy with unknown base-rate priors, or a Sleeping-Beauty-style awakening with an uncertain frequency — phrased so that someone can answer without already knowing which position it supports. For every leaf, provide either a standard name (e.g., restricted SSA, SIA, accessibility-weighted hybrid, bullet-biting cosmologist) or the label 'unnamed,' followed by one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. Identify any combination of answers that yields an unoccupied leaf, and briefly diagnose whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot genuinely separate, explaining which diagnostic question fails. Build the tree from logical structure and the cases alone; do not request citations, reading lists, or bibliographies.
