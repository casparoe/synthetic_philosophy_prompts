Look, I've been thinking about where the Kant-and-animals literature should actually go next, and I'm not convinced the usual debates are the right ones. We all know the indirect-duty story -- cruelty corrupts our humanity, animals aren't rational ends-in-themselves, blah blah. But there's this weird tension in the Lectures and in some late Reflexionen where he talks about animals as analgues of human beings in ways that don't neatly reduce to instrumental value. So here's my question: if you're allocating, say, three PhD slots or a small grant for work on Kantian animal ethics over the next few years, which open questions should actually get the resources?

I'm particularly interested in the dependency structure. For instance: does resolving whether Kant has a consistent account of "indirect directness" (I know, ugly phrase) in the Metaphysics of Morals have to come before we can say anything interesting about his implicatons for factory farming? Or is the interpretive work basically done and the real open problem is applying the framework to wild animal suffering without collapsing into consequentialism? Another line I'm curious about: his aesthetic theory in the Critique of Judgment treats nature as purposive without purpose -- does that open up a non-instrumental value for animal life that the moral philosophy blocks?

Rank these or whatever others you think are upstream. I care about tractability for philosophers (not biologists), which answers would actually change how we teach Kant's ethics, and which ones are dead ends because they depend on textual ambiguity we'll never resolve. And please -- if my premise that there's a live "indirect directness" problem is just confused, or if I'm asking you to prioritize things that can't be separated this way, push back. Don't just play along.
