I'm a retired biologist who recently picked up philosophy, and I have a question about something that's been bugging me. In cross-cultural psychiatry they administer the exact same depression questionnaires in, say, Japan and Canada, then compare prevalance rates as if they're measuring one and the same mental condition. But after reading about Twin Earth and semantic externalism — the idea that what your words refer to can depend on your enviroment — I'm wondering: what view of mental content does that comparison practice actually presuppose? Does it require that the meaning of 'depression' is fixed internally, regardless of local social or biological context? And if externalism is right, wouldn't we need to check whether the local conditions are identical before treating the scores as comparable? Or is there a middle view that lets the practice stand without committing us to full internalism? I'm trying to see whether the practice is philosophically coherent or just silently assuming something it shouldn't.
