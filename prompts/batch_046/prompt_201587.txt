I was at this weird little immersive theater thing last weekend with some friends, and there was this lighting trick where they flashed a red spotlight on the left of the stage and then immediately a blue one on the right — like, really fast back and forth. Everyone swore they saw one single light travel across and turn purple in the middle, smooth motion and everything. But the tech person explained afterwards that there was literally no light in the center, just two seperate flashes with a gap between them. I clearly remember seeing that purple middle part happen in real time, not like I imagined it later.

One of my friends said it's an Orwellian thing — my brain recorded the two flashes correctly at first but then revised the memory afterwards to make it a smooth story. My other friend said it's more Stalinesque, meaning there never actually was a conscious experiance of just-red-then-just-blue; thier brain assembled the moving-purple version after the fact and stamped it with a fake timestamp, like inserting someone into an old photo. I keep going back and forth. Is there actually a fact of the matter about whether I had a "real" experience that got rewritten, or whether the experience only came into being once my brain finished stitching it together? And if the second option is right, does that mean there wasn't really anything it was like to be me during those flashes?
