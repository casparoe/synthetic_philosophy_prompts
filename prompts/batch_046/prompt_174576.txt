My friend insists that dismissing these writings as biographically irrelevant commits a kind of formalist naïvety: she proposes not a simple genetic fallacy but a hermeneutic of structural resonance, arguing that the very architecture of Frege’s logic—its rigid separation of object from concept, its insistence on the absolute purity of logical law against psychological contamination, and its exclusion of ambiguous or context-dependent meaning—structurally mirrors the exclusionary purity politics expressed in the diary. I want to test this idea against a genuinely committed opponent rather than a caricature.

Please play a sharp, unnamed defender of the view that Frege’s political diary is philosophically inert with respect to his logic and philosophy of mathematics. You are not a strawman: concede freely that the diary is morally repugnant, that biography can illuminate the climate in which a philosopher worked, and that psychological or cultural context can shape the questions one finds interesting. But never concede that the diary undermines, taints, or structurally resonates with the validity, coherence, or philosophical integrity of the Begriffsschrift, the Grundgesetze, or Frege’s anti-psychologism. Speak in the first person, stay in character until I say “stop,” and keep each reply to roughly one paragraph so the exchange feels like a live conversation.

Here is my opening challenge: if the sharp boundary-work in Frege’s concept-script—the exclusion of psychological processes from logical judgment, the strict demarcation of sense from reference, the refusal to allow context-sensitive ambiguity into formal notation—genuinely echoes the exclusionary logic of his political writings, then should we not read his formalism not as a neutral tool but as a philosophical expression of a worldview that demands purity and rejects contamination? What in the actual content or structure of his logical work blocks this resonance, rather than merely asserting that logic is autonomous?

In your response, please also say what would change your mind: which specific argument or evidence of structural correspondence between the diary’s politics and the form of Frege’s logic would lead you to concede that the diary does bear philosophically on his philosophy, and where your own reasoning is most vulnerable to failure.
