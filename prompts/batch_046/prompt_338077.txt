I've been trying to think through the debate about original versus derived intentionality — the idea, which I take Searle to defend, that only living brains genuinely think about things, while everything else, from books to computers, only has meaning borrowed from an interpreter. I also understand that Dennett challenges this with a minimal example he calls a two-bitser: a device with just two internal states that register whether a door is open or closed, which he says can already be interpreted as having minimal beliefs and desires about the world, with human minds being more complex versions of the same tracking. From this I constructed an argument that seems to collapse the distinction entirely, and I cannot locate the mistake.

Here is my chain of reasoning:

1. If derived intentionality is not real intentionality — if it is merely an "as-if" meaning assigned from the outside — then the physical patterns in a computer or on a page have no intrinsic aboutness.
2. But spoken arguments are also physical patterns: sound waves, ink, and ultimately neural firings in the speaker's brain producing them. So by Searle's own lights, his spoken defence of original intentionality consists of sounds that lack genuine meaning unless a listener with original intentionality interprets them.
3. Therefore, to prove that original intentionality exists, Searle must already rely on symbols that, according to his distinction, do not possess the very feature he is trying to establish.
4. This yields a dilemma. Either derived intentionality is sufficient to carry real meaning — in which case the line between original and derived dissolves, and Dennett's two-bitser might genuinely think after all — or no argument, including Searle's own, can ever establish original intentionality, which makes the position self-defeating.
5. I am reluctant to accept either horn, because my calculator clearly does not understand "ERROR", yet I also do not see how brain chemistry magically produces a different kind of meaning.

I feel certain that one of these steps slips, perhaps in assuming that spoken words are on a par with computer outputs rather than direct expressions of the speaker's original intentionality, but I cannot price the cost of fixing it. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up commit me to?
