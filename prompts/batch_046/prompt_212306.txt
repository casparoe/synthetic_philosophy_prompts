I've adopted a somewhat unorthodox reading of Maimonides on animal sacrifice that I call the scaffolded-transition view. Rather than treating the sacrificial law as a mere accommodation to Israelite weakness — the standard line that God tolerated pagan-style worship to wean the people away from idolatry — I take Maimonides to be describing a deliberately staged pedagogical architecture. On this reading, sacrifice was genuinely obligatory and institutionally continuous with the practices of surrounding nations precisely so that social cohesion would not be ruptured; yet its internal meaning was systematically redirected toward prayer, intention, and the service of God alone, with the expectation that the external scaffold would eventually become dispensable once the internal orientation was stable. The practice was therefore not a second-best compromise but a rational transitional form that required participants to treat it as binding in order for the transition itself to succeed.

Tell me what would count against this interpretation — not just the standard objections that Maimonides ranks prayer above sacrifice or that he describes the sacrificial law as subordinate in rank, but which textual structures, philosophical commitments, and comparative results it genuinely risks. I want a map of its exposure: what observations, arguments, or analogies would be decisive, which would be merely awkward, and whether the view has been insulated by qualifications to the point that nothing could hurt it.

In particular, address the following:
- Which features of the Guide's discussion of sacrifices in Part III, particularly within the broader account of the reasons for the commandments, read differently under accommodationism versus under the scaffold model?
- Does the scaffold view risk collapsing into pure accommodationism on one side, or into attributing intrinsic religious value to blood-offering on the other, and is either collapse fatal?
- What does this view predict about the normative status of sacrificial practice in a fully rational community that rival readings do not predict, and is that unique prediction defensible?
- Are there formal analogies to staged belief-revision or institutional design that genuinely illuminate Maimonides' account of education and law, or does importing such frameworks distort his own framework?
- Which of these potential defeaters would a committed defender of the scaffold reading concede as damaging, and which would they dismiss as misreading the pedagogical purpose?

Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Also say whether you think the position is well insulated, and whether that insulation is an intellectual virtue or merely protective.
