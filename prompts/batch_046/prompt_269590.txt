Here is my philosophical profile on Sartrean freedom and responsibility for a consistency check before I present it. I hold: (a) conscious agents possess radical ontological freedom to choose their fundamental project regardless of external constraint; (b) bad faith is self-deception about this freedom, but I distinguish reflective bad faith (explicit denial that one could choose otherwise) from pre-reflective bad faith (structural identification with a role that obscures freedom without explicit awareness); (c) moral blame is proportional to bad faith—full responsibility requires reflective bad faith, while pre-reflective bad faith only mitigates blame; (d) there is a cognitive threshold for bad faith: agents who lack second-order reflective capacity, whether from severe cognitive limitation or total institutional conditioning, cannot enter even pre-reflective bad faith in the morally relevant sense and therefore bear minimal responsibility; (e) I reject Sartre’s anti-essentialism and accept biologically grounded facticity (dependency needs, mortality, social embeddedness) as real situational constraints, though I deny these limit ontological freedom; and (f) applied to institutional roles, a bureaucrat saying “I was just following orders” is usually in pre-reflective bad faith rather than reflective, unless they have explicitly considered and rejected their freedom. Which of these commitments genuinely conflict, which only appear to, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity, specify for each what must be given up, identify the single change that would clean up the most problems, and explain whether (e) can be reconciled with (a) without collapsing into determinism.
