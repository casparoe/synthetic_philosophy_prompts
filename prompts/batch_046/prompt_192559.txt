I'm writing my dissertation chapter on vagueness and have been struck by an analogy that circulates informally among philosophers of language but is rarely subjected to careful audit: epistemicists sometimes speak as if a vague predicate such as 'bald' functions like a high-resolution digital image that we happen to view at low resolution. On this picture, there are discrete, precise pixels—exact hair counts, precise boundary conditions—that determine membership with perfect sharpness; our cognitive and linguistic 'display' merely blurs them into an apparent gradient. Supervaluationists, in turn, are occasionally characterized as treating the same predicate like a vector graphic with genuinely smooth curves and no hidden grid: there is no underlying discrete structure, only a rendering rule that produces acceptable approximations across a range.

Could you audit the epistemicist version of this analogy in detail? Provide an explicit correspondence table—what plays the role of the pixels, the display resolution, the image file, the rendering algorithm, and the viewer? Then separate the disanalogies that matter for the metaphysical conclusion from those that are merely decorative: does the analogy smuggle in a discrete ontology that properties like baldness lack? Does it presuppose that a 'true image' exists independently of any representational scheme? How does it handle the sorites paradox—is the paradox analogous to noticing that adjacent pixels are indistinguishable, or does the analogy fail precisely at the point where tolerance principles operate?

Next, do the same for the supervaluationist 'vector graphic' counterpart: what corresponds to the smooth curve, the absence of a hidden grid, and the rendering rules? Identify where that mapping breaks down, particularly with respect to ontological vagueness versus semantic indeterminacy.

Finally, assess whether either analogy actually carries the weight of its respective conclusion—whether epistemicism or supervaluationism is supported—or whether the conclusion was presupposed in the choice of imagery. If both analogies collapse under scrutiny, propose a better analogy, drawn from a different domain, that captures what is genuinely at stake between these two positions without importing an inappropriate metaphysics of discreteness or continuity.
