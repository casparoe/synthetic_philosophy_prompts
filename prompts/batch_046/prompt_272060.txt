Write a short research proposal—approximately two pages, suitable for a one-semester graduate seminar or a workshop pitch—on judgment aggregation and the discursive dilemma. The central question should be precise and somewhat unconventional: suppose a group must aggregate individual judgments over a set of interdependent premises in which some premises serve as framework conditions for others. Can the introduction of an explicit “structural priority” ranking among premises allow the group to escape the standard discursive dilemma without collapsing into either uniform premise-based aggregation or uniform conclusion-based aggregation? Your proposal must contain four elements. First, state the question with sufficient precision, and explain why standard treatments of the dilemma—particularly those that treat all premises as symmetrically weighted—leave this variant open. Second, outline the method: describe how you would formally represent priority-weighted aggregation rules, and identify the philosophical arguments about collective rationality and epistemic interdependence that you would examine. Third, specify the success conditions and failure conditions: what would count as showing that structural priority resolves, relocates, or fails to affect the dilemma; and identify one concrete risk that could render the project intractable within a single semester. Fourth, provide a realistic scope assessment, distinguishing what is achievable in twelve weeks from what must be deferred. Do not request or provide a bibliography, citation list, or literature review; refer to theoretical positions and formal results only in general terms, and construct the proposal so that it requires no external sources beyond general knowledge of social-choice theory and collective epistemology.
