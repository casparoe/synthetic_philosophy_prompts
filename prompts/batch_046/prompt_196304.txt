I am a high school student preparing to moderate an interview for our school’s philosophy journal. Our guest is a philosopher who recently defended a modified Scanlonian contractualist approach to the aggregation problem. Instead of assessing principles one act at a time, she argues that reasonable rejection is triggered only when harm crosses what she calls a structural visibility threshold—meaning it is direct or large enough that a specific affected party can recognize it as a personal burden—but that once this threshold is crossed, moral weight aggregates non-linearly rather than additively. She claims this preserves contractualism’s pairwise structure of justification while still allowing the view to condemn collectively catastrophic but individually imperceptible harms, such as climate damage produced by widely distributed emissions.

I need eight to ten substantive interview questions that engage the actual mechanics of her position rather than asking her to restate it. Specifically:

- An opening question that asks how her visibility-threshold criterion distinguishes contractualist aggregation from a rule-utilitarian calculus that also weighs large-scale outcomes, paired with a follow-up anticipating her likely reply that the difference lies in justification being addressed to each affected person rather than to an aggregate total.

- A question probing whether her threshold is set by contingent psychological salience rather than by normative significance, with a follow-up keyed to her probable defense that reasonableness itself is partly constituted by what affected parties can actually register and understand.

- A question about peer disagreement: if two reasonable people disagree over whether a particular collective harm—say, gradual ecosystem collapse—has crossed the visibility threshold, does her framework provide resources to adjudicate the dispute without collapsing into conciliationism on one side or an authoritarian appeal to her own judgment on the other? The follow-up should press whichever answer she is most likely to offer.

- A closing question asking what kind of evidence—empirical, normative, or conceptual—could genuinely undermine her model, especially regarding the non-linear aggregation function she proposes.

Please draft the sequence in a respectful but philosophically sharp tone appropriate for a serious student interview. Avoid generic biographical questions, and do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
