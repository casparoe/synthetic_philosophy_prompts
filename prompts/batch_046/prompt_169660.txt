Consider the institutional practice adopted in some hospital-based clinical ethics consultation services, in which consultants must complete a structured two-phase exercise before issuing a recommendation. In the first phase, the consultant writes a first-person narrative from the patient's point of view, attempting to imaginatively reconstruct the patient's specific fears, hopes, and relational context. In the second phase, the consultant reviews that same narrative while adopting what the protocol calls an impartial spectator stance, deliberately suppressing particular emotional identification in order to judge whether the proposed course of action is one that could be approved without being the individual primarily affected by it. The protocol treats these phases as sequential but compatible, and consultants are evaluated on whether they complete both rather than collapsing them into a single judgment.

What conception of sympathy and moral judgment does this two-phase procedure presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit philosophical psychology: does it treat sympathy as an imaginative exchange of situations that supplies content for judgment, or as a contagious emotional transmission that must subsequently be corrected by reason? Is the sequence consistent with Adam Smith's account, in which the impartial spectator emerges from social interaction and mutual sympathy, or does it instead presuppose that sympathy and impartiality are independent capacities that can be activated in isolation? Distinguish what the practice strictly requires—say, that emotional perspective-taking and detached evaluation are separable steps—from what it merely fits well with, and say whether rival frameworks (for instance, a Kantian universalizability test, or a care-ethical emphasis on relational responsiveness) could license the same procedural structure without altering its practical content. If one rejected Smith's view that we assess propriety by comparing our sentiments with those of an imagined impartial spectator, what specific elements of this protocol would become unmotivated or incoherent, and what would have to change in its design?
