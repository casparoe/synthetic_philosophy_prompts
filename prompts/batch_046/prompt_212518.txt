My doctoral supervisor has, over several seminars, given implicit verdicts on whether various agents genuinely follow the rule of addition (rather than being merely addition-accordant or following some deviant function such as quaddition). Her judgements so far are as follows.

First, she holds that a human mathematician who computes correct sums for all pairs she has ever encountered, and who would continue correctly for any novel pair using a standard algorithm, genuinely follows addition—even if she has never considered pairs larger than fifty-seven. Second, she judges that an agent who applies quaddition (giving the sum for pairs both below fifty-seven, and returning five otherwise), but sincerely calls this “addition” and has never encountered a larger pair, is not following addition at all. Third, she says that a person with a specific cognitive impairment who computes quaddition for large pairs but sincerely intends to add, and who would correct herself if the discrepancy were pointed out, is failing to follow addition rather than following quaddition; she describes this as an error of execution, not a different rule. Fourth, she denies that a computer with a lookup table for sums up to ten thousand and a hard-coded fallback to “five” beyond that range is following addition, since there is no internal normative constraint linking its outputs to the arithmetic function. Fifth, she accepts that a distributed collective—individual members each handling a restricted range, with mutual error-correction and no single member grasping the whole function—can collectively follow addition, provided the network as a whole corrects deviations. Sixth, she maintains that a neural network trained only on sums up to one hundred, which generalises correctly up to ten thousand but then produces unstructured outputs, is not genuinely rule-governed; she regards its behaviour beyond the training distribution as unconstrained by any normative standard.

The new case is this: consider an artificial system that computes correct sums for every input pair it actually receives, and which contains an internal monitoring mechanism that revises any output inconsistent with its prior correct computations. However, the system has no explicit representation of addition as a mathematical concept, and its monitoring mechanism operates solely on observed historical outputs—there is no counterfactual disposition for pairs it never receives, and no access to an independent arithmetical standard. The system belongs to no linguistic community that could correct it, and its dispositions outside the observed domain are undefined rather than fixed.

Please predict my supervisor’s verdict: would she say this system genuinely follows addition, that it follows some other rule, that it is merely addition-accordant without rule-following, or that there is no fact of the matter? In giving your answer, identify which underlying theory of rule-following her pattern of judgements best fits—whether a primitivist account of irreducible normative facts, a community-based view of correctability, a refined dispositionalism constrained by internal monitoring, or a sceptical denial that mental or causal facts determine meaning. You should also explain which earlier verdict is the closest analog and why. Finally, specify one further hypothetical case that would best distinguish your preferred interpretation from its nearest rival. Write your response in British English, using formal but clear prose, and do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
