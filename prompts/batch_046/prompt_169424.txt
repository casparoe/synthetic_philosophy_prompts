I retired from marine biolgy about four years ago and since then I've been trying to wrap my head around philosphy of mind, especially the stuff about consciousness and suffering. I was talking with my sister last weekend — she runs a small hospice outfit — and she mentioned one of her patients, an older guy who had a minor stroke about a year ago. Now he can tell you exactly where his arthritis hurts, points right to the knee, describes it as sharp, but he never asks for meds, doesn't wince, and apparently gets irritated when the nurses try to 'fix' it. My sister thinks this proves that if there's no suffering, there's no real harm, so pushing painkillers is just treating a number, not a person.

But I was reading something recently — I think it was about how pain might work like an alarm system, where the badness comes from representing damage that needs fixing, not just from feeling awful — and now I'm wondering if there's still something wrong with ignoring that signal, even if the guy feels fine. Is the harm of pain all in the feeling, or is there a hidden problem when the body is shouting 'damage' and nobody's listening? I'd like some help sorting this out, because my gut says my sister is missing something but I can't articulate it without sounding like I'm just being sentimental.
