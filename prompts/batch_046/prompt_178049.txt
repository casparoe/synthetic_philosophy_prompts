I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy. I host a podcast that interviews researchers on conceptual topics, and next week’s guest is Dr. Elena Vasquez, a philosopher of biology who defends a specific hybrid view she calls “anchored causal-role functionalism.” On her account, a trait’s biological function is its present causal contribution to some system capacity, but that contribution counts as a genuine function only if it is anchored in a selection history—whether evolutionary, intentional, or artificial—with special provisions for evolutionary novelties that have not yet been fixed by natural selection. She rejects both pure Millikan-style etiological accounts and pure Cummins-style causal-role analyses.

Please draft eight to ten rigorous, polished interview questions for me. They should not ask her to restate her view from scratch, and they must avoid generic biographical or “what drew you to philosophy” questions. Instead, they should turn on the actual content of her position and press where the literature genuinely presses hybrid theories. Specifically, include the following:

- At least one question about evolutionary novelties and exaptation: ask how her anchoring condition applies to a trait that currently performs a clear causal role but whose selection history either points elsewhere or is absent, and include an immediate follow-up keyed to her likely reply—probably an appeal to preliminary selection potential or design history—that pushes whether this collapses her view back into pure etiologism.

- At least one question about malfunction and normativity: ask how her framework distinguishes a broken heart from a heart that is merely performing a different causal role, and include a follow-up keyed to her probable answer that malfunction is deviation from the anchored role, pressing whether that deviation is defined causally, historically, or both.

- At least one question about synthetic or engineered biological traits—such as a gene-drive construct introduced into mosquito populations that confers antibiotic resistance but was never naturally selected—asking whether her view assigns it a biological function, and if so whether the anchoring comes from the engineer’s intention, the current metabolic role, or both. Include a follow-up that presses the boundary between biological function and mere artifact function.

- At least one question that reframes the classic “heart makes noise” objection for her hybrid view: ask whether anchoring in selection history alone is sufficient to rule out noise-making as a heart function, or whether she still needs an independent causal-role filter, and include a follow-up asking whether that filter reintroduces the very over-attribution problems she sought to avoid by adding the anchoring condition.

For each of the four required questions above, present the follow-up immediately after it, clearly labeled. The overall tone should be formal and colloquium-ready, but accessible enough that I can deliver the questions naturally despite lacking philosophical training. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not include any verbatim quotations from published texts.

Finally, after the questions, briefly identify one further stress-test case—perhaps involving microbiome transplantation or de-extinction—that could challenge her anchoring condition, and explain in one or two sentences why it creates tension for her framework.
