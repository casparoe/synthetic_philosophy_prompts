Write an essay on whether the wrong-kind-of-reasons problem is a fatal objection to moral realism or a misdiagnosed tension in the architecture of practical reason. Center your analysis on the following distinction, which is not standard in introductory treatments: call reasons that justify entering or sustaining commitment to a normative framework “entry-reasons,” and call reasons that justify particular actions within that framework “internal-reasons.” The classic wrong-kind-of-reasons worry asks how a moral realist can supply entry-reasons for morality that are not themselves moral—without circularity—while still maintaining that internal moral reasons carry genuine authority. Examine whether separating entry-reasons from internal-reasons dissolves the problem, or whether it merely relocates the difficulty: if entry-reasons must be non-moral to avoid circularity, do they possess sufficient normative weight to motivate adoption of morality, and if they are moral, have we collapsed back into the circularity we sought to avoid? Weigh the main positions available—such as the claim that moral reasons are sui generis, the view that practical rationality already presupposes moral constraints, and the suggestion that agency itself is constituted by normative commitments—while also assessing whether the entry/internal split introduces an unacceptable bifurcation in human agency. Reach a definite conclusion: does this framework rescue moral realism from the wrong-kind-of-reasons challenge, does it show that the challenge was never well-formed, or does it leave the realist with an unbridgeable motivational gap? In your response, clearly distinguish between points that are broadly settled in metaethics (for instance, the basic structure of the wrong-kind-of-reasons objection and the distinction between motivating and normative reasons) and points that remain genuinely contested among specialists (for instance, whether entry-level justifications can be non-circular and whether constitutive accounts of agency can satisfy the motivation requirement).
