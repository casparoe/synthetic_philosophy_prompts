I'm assessing a summary I found in an unnamed popular philosophy article about actual infinities, Hilbert's hotel, Cantor's results, and finitism. The author frames the debate like this: Hilbert's hotel shows that actual infinity is structurally coherent—if infinitely many rooms are occupied, shifting each guest to the next room frees room one—so the apparent paradox dissolves once you accept a completed infinite totality. Finitists reject this, the article claims, not because they fail to understand the shift, but because they deny that any aggregate can be a completed infinite; for them, every collection is necessarily actually finite, and talk of "all rooms" implicitly presupposes a finished infinite domain. The article then draws a sharp distinction between "structural" infinity, illustrated by Hilbert's hotel, and "cardinal" infinity, captured by Cantor's power-set argument, and says finitists object to both, but for different reasons—structural infinity violates the principle that every collection has a definite cardinality, while cardinal infinity violates the principle that every set has a determinate size. The author adds that Cantor's diagonal proof is largely independent of Hilbert-style reasoning, since it only requires treating the real numbers as a well-defined collection; consequently, the article concludes, finitism is best understood as a metaphysical thesis about the non-existence of infinite totalities rather than as a logical objection to Cantorian mathematics, and Hilbert's hotel is largely irrelevant to the finitist's core concern, which is the illegitimacy of quantifying over unbounded domains rather than the behavior of infinite ones.

Something about this sounds mostly right, yet I suspect there are subtle misrepresentations—especially the suggestion that finitists reject even Aristotelian potential infinity interpreted modally, and the claim that structural and cardinal infinity can be separated so cleanly in the finitist's objections. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate, what is a common popular simplification, and what a defender of finitism or of Cantor's framework would explicitly deny. In particular, address whether the structural/cardinal distinction holds up, whether Hilbert's hotel is genuinely irrelevant to finitist worries, and whether the article correctly identifies the finitist's central target as the quantification over unbounded domains rather than the coherence of infinite procedures themselves.
