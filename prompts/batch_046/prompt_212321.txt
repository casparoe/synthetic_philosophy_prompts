Her central thought experiment is a modified Twin Earth: the two planets are physically and psychologically identical, but on Earth certain acts instantiate real relations of unjustified interference, whereas on Twin Earth the corresponding physical states lack those structural normative properties. She argues that this shows psychological duplicates can diverge in semantic content, and that standard moral Twin Earth objections mistake metaphysical disagreement for semantic divergence. Please draft eight to ten interview questions that move past restatement. For each of these three, include a follow-up keyed to her most likely reply—respectively, that she distinguishes metaphysical grounding from semantic determination; that she allows partial verbal disagreement alongside substantive practical conflict; and that motivation tracks first-person structural roles rather than the external properties themselves. Add two further questions: one on higher-order vagueness, asking whether a borderline Twin Earth could exist where the normative structure itself is indeterminate; and one on whether moral progress requires actual environmental change rather than only shifts in belief or attitude.
