I'm grading an exam for an upper-level philosophy of mind course. The question asked students to apply Mara Chen's distinction between surface conceivability—forming a mental image of a scenario without immediate logical contradiction—and depth conceivability, which requires grasping the structural conditions that make the scenario metaphysically coherent. Specifically, students were to evaluate whether conceiving of a conscious silicon-based artifact supports its metaphysical possibility, and to address one major objection rooted in a gap between appearance and reality in modal reasoning.

Here is the student answer I need assessed:

Chen's distinction separates surface conceivability, like picturing a robot reporting pain without contradiction, from depth conceivability, which requires understanding the substrate-independent conditions that allow phenomenal experience. For silicon consciousness, we have surface conceivability but lack depth conceivability, since we do not grasp how silicon circuitry could produce qualia. This means conceivability guides possibility only at the depth level; because silicon minds are only surface-conceivable, they provide no real evidence for metaphysical possibility. The relevant objection comes from Joseph Levine's explanatory gap: physical explanations leave subjective experience out, so even complete knowledge of silicon mechanisms would not bridge the gap. This supports my view, because the gap explains precisely why depth conceivability is blocked—there is no structural story available to grasp. One might reply that ignorance of mechanism does not entail impossibility. But that reply misses Chen's point: without depth conceivability we should suspend judgment on possibility rather than affirm it. So the gap does not merely limit our knowledge; it actively undermines the modal claim that silicon consciousness is possible.

Please assign a specific letter or numerical grade and provide point-by-point comments that justify it. Assess whether the student correctly applies Chen's distinction, whether they properly identify and deploy the objection, and whether there are subtle conflations or steps that do not follow. Tie each comment to a specific claim in the answer; do not offer generic impressions.
