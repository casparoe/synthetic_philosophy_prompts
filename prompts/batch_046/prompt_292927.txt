Write an essay evaluating whether a restricted, domain-localized form of dialetheism is philosophically stable. Focus on the view that true contradictions exist but are quarantined to specific boundary domains—such as self-referential semantic paradoxes and sorites-series vagueness—and are barred from propagating into ordinary empirical or mathematical reasoning via a localized contamination principle. Weigh the argument that this restriction saves dialetheism from the charge of logical anarchism and protects practical inference, against the objection that the quarantine is ad hoc and undermines the explanatory motivation for accepting contradictions in the first place. In particular, compare this restricted dialetheist position to non-adjunctive paraconsistent frameworks (for instance, relevance-logical approaches) that block explosive derivation without ever endorsing a true contradiction. You should also assess whether the boundary-domain restriction can be rendered principled rather than merely pragmatic. Reach a definite verdict and justify it: does localized dialetheism constitute a coherent middle ground between classical logic and unrestricted dialetheism, or does it inevitably collapse into one of those two extremes?
