For this week's formal epistemology exercise, please construct a rigorous philosophical argument — written in British English — defending the view that Aumann's common prior assumption ought to be rejected not merely as an empirically unrealistic idealisation, but as a conceptually incoherent requirement in cases of what we might term "ontologically partitioned belief spaces": situations in which rational agents employ fundamentally incommensurable categorical schemes for carving up possibility space, such that the construction of any shared prior necessarily involves an illicit translation that privileges one ontological framework over another. Your argument should directly engage with the objection that rational agents can always render their beliefs commensurable by mapping them into a neutral probabilistic calculus without substantive loss. Furthermore, you should evaluate whether jettisoning the common prior undermines the normative force of the agreement theorem itself, or whether it merely delimits the theorem's proper domain of application. Do not rely on bibliographic citation or appeals to authority; the evaluation will turn on conceptual precision, logical structure, and the originality with which you develop the notion of ontological partition.
