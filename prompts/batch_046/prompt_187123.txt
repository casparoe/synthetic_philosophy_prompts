I'm designing a graduate module on the philosophy of decision theory, and I want my students to see Good's result not as a settled technicality but as a cluster of interpretations with different philosophical weights. Good's theorem establishes that, for a Bayesian agent who maximizes expected utility and can act freely after receiving a signal, the expected value of information is non-negative: conditioning on evidence before choosing can never reduce expected utility on average. Yet this canonical framing competes with several extensions and reinterpretations that pull the result in very different directions, and I am unsure which deserve to be taken seriously as philosophical challenges rather than mere formal curiosities.

Please rank the following five conceptual framings of the value of information from 1 (the most philosophically important or robust) to 5 (the least), giving a brief sentence for each explaining its placement. The framings are: (1) the canonical Bayesian framing, in which observation is costless and the agent always conditions before acting; (2) the pre-commitment framing, in which information can have negative value if the agent can credibly bind herself in advance to ignore the signal, thereby sacrificing the option value of flexibility; (3) the indexical self-location framing, in which the agent must treat herself as a randomly selected observer within the updated probability space, linking Good's result to debates about anthropic reasoning and self-locating belief; (4) the collective-coordination framing, in which individually rational information acquisition in a group can destroy common-knowledge equilibria and reduce aggregate utility; and (5) the bounded-rationality framing, in which the computational and attentional costs of processing information can make its net value negative even for an otherwise rational agent. I want the ranking to reflect how seriously each framing should be treated as a genuine philosophical extension or limitation of the original theorem, not merely its formal elegance.
