The speaker treats this as if it were a single, transparent principle that dissolves counterfactual remorse, but I suspect several distinct claims are being smuggled together under that sentence.

Could you disambiguate that slogan for me? For each reading, indicate whether it is defensible, whether it is clearly false, and whether it actually entails that retrospective regret is irrational. I am especially interested in the distinction between recurrence as a hypothetical test of affirmation (where one imagines repetition merely to check one's present attitude), recurrence as a literal cosmological claim, and recurrence as a logical constraint on personal identity across repetitions. Then tell me which of these readings my classmate is likely invoking when she uses the slogan to shut down expressions of regret, and whether that particular reading survives scrutiny once separated from the others. Please keep the analysis focused on the conceptual architecture of the slogan itself rather than on textual exegesis; I do not need citations, references to specific editions, or a bibliography.
