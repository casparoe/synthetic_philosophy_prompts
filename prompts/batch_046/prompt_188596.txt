In the philosophy of social science, interpretations of Max Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis have long divided scholars over whether cultural values can operate as independent causal forces in historical explanation. One camp, which we might call the value-causal interpreters, holds that Weber genuinely attributed explanatory power to the Protestant work ethic, treating it as an efficient cause of modern capitalist development that material conditions alone could not account for. The opposing camp, the elective affinity interpreters, insists that Weber never assigned causal efficacy to values themselves; rather, he traced an internal coherence—Wahlverwandtschaft—between ascetic religious dispositions and rational economic conduct, maintaining that meaningful action must be understood interpretively through Verstehen rather than subsumed under nomological causation. For decades the dispute has remained static: both sides appeal to the same discussions of inner-worldly asceticism and rationalization, yet neither side has shifted its position. My working hypothesis is that this stalemate is not primarily empirical—no archive discovery resolves it—but conceptual: the disagreement may be about whether explaining action by reference to subjective meanings requires treating those meanings as causal powers or merely as conditions of intelligibility. Diagnose the structure of this impasse. Is it a verbal dispute about applying the term cause to cultural phenomena? A substantive metaphysical disagreement about whether intentional content can enter into causal chains? Or are the two sides actually pursuing different questions—one asking how capitalism historically emerged, the other asking what counts as an adequate explanation in the human sciences—under the single heading of Weber's thesis? Ground your diagnosis in what each camp actually says about the method of understanding and about the relation between meaning and causation. Finally, specify what would genuinely count as progress given your diagnosis: for instance, a formal distinction between causal relevance and causal efficacy, a multi-level model that integrates hermeneutic and mechanistic explanation, or an argument that the original question is ill-posed because it conflates two distinct explanatory projects.
