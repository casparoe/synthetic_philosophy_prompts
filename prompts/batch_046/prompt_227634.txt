I'm preparing a closing piece for my advanced metaphysics seminar, where we've spent the past sessions on Leibniz's principle of the identity of indiscernibles and on Max Black's counterexample of two perfectly identical iron spheres suspended in an otherwise empty universe with perfect rotational symmetry. Rather than treating this as a standard metaphysical exercise, I want to push the problem toward ontology identification: in a universe where symmetry makes every intrinsic feature shared and no external reference frame exists, what information could ever justify the claim that there are two entities rather than one?

Please write a 14-line sonnet in iambic pentameter with a volta at line 9 and a rhymed closing couplet (lines 13-14). The form should carry the philosophical argument. Use lines 1-8 (the octave) to establish the symmetric scene—two spheres of identical radius, density, temperature, and surface, floating in absolute void, with no stars or axes to break the sameness—so that the reader sees the perfect qualitative overlap. At the volta in line 9, introduce the relational fact: there is a distance between them, yet because of the rotational symmetry, no observer inside the universe could non-circularly determine which sphere occupies which position. In lines 10-12, unfold the tension: if spatial separation counts as a genuine property, then the principle survives only in restricted form; if it does not count, then our ontology of "two" was never grounded in accessible information. The closing couplet must state exactly what has to be given up—either the unrestricted principle or the claim of genuine duality—without resolving which surrender is correct. Render this through concrete imagery (the iron, the void, the axis, the gap) rather than through citation, quotation, or bibliographic reference.

Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations.

After the poem, include a brief critical note (no more than about 150 words) identifying the weakest point in your interpretation. Specifically, address whether an ontic structural realist might deny that the spheres are individual entities at all, or whether an epistemicist could argue that ontological commitment does not require distinguishability. State clearly what argument or finding would lead you to revise that assessment.
