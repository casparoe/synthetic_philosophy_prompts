I am preparing the closing exercise for a graduate seminar on the metaphysics of causation, and I want a formal poetic task that captures precisely where our discussion stalled: after working through counterfactual-dependence analyses and interventionist invariance conditions, we kept circling preemptive overdetermination—cases in which a redundant cause is present but blocked, and the two frameworks deliver subtly different verdicts about actual causation. Please write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets followed by a quatrain, with two refrains (A1 and A2) and two rhyme sounds—spoken in the voice of the preempted cause itself. Imagine a lightning strike that ignites a forest fire an instant before a second, redundant strike would have arrived, only for a sudden rain to extinguish the first flame before the second can act. The first refrain should state the interventionist criterion from the cause’s perspective: something like, Hold all else fixed, and vary only me. The second refrain should state the counterfactual evaluation: something like, In the nearest world where I am absent, the forest still burns. The tercets should build the scene of redundancy, preemption, and the rain’s interruption; the quatrain should serve as a volta in which the two refrains are forced into direct adjacency; and the final couplet should place them side by side without reconciliation—mirroring the stalemate we reached. The philosophical content should be rendered with enough precision that someone familiar with the structural-model treatment of preemption and with interventionist invariance-under-intervention conditions recognizes the conceptual machinery being enacted, not merely named. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; let the form, the refrains, and the imagery carry the full conceptual burden. Keep the diction precise but not jargon-laden, so the poem remains legible even to someone who has not taken the seminar.
