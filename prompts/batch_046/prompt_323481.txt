After a long argument with a friend about whether my phone's autocomplete "means" anything by its suggestions, I'm trying to sort out my actual commitments on original versus derived intentionality. Here are my gut verdicts on concrete cases, and I want to know whether one principle captures them or whether I'm forced to drop some.

When a pocket calculator displays 4 after 2+2, I think it carries no intentionality at all—neither original nor derived—since no one interprets its internal state as being about numbers; it is pure mechanism. When Dennett's two-bitser randomly emits yes to every query and a community comes to treat it as an authoritative oracle, I think the system itself still lacks original intentionality, yet acquires a thin, derived intentionality through that interpretive practice—though less robustly than a book does. In Searle's Chinese Room, I think the person inside retains original intentionality about whatever he genuinely understands, but the room-as-system acquires none; arranging symbols correctly does not generate derived aboutness if there is no appropriate causal or interpretive chain linking the system to the world.

Then there are cases that split my intuition. Consider a "semantic mirror"—a hypothetical system with no internal representational states except to echo back, in grammatically correct form, the intentional states of whoever is interacting with it, refining your beliefs into polished sentences without adding content. I think the mirror lacks original intentionality, but I am unsure about derived: it feels more intentional than the calculator, because its outputs are systematically about things, yet less than the two-bitser, because it has no independent causal history or community interpretation—its aboutness is entirely parasitic on the user's mind in real time. Now consider a single biological neuron firing in response to light. I think it has no intentionality—just causation. But embed that same neuron in a visual cortex that reliably tracks edges, and I suddenly want to say the subsystem has derived intentionality about edges. That feels like an unmotivated jump.

Finally, consider a large language model trained on human text. I think it clearly lacks original intentionality—no consciousness, no biological aboutness. But I also think its outputs carry derived intentionality in a robust sense: when it states that Paris is the capital of France, the sentence is about Paris because the model inherits the aboutness of the human texts that shaped its weights. Yet if I grant that, I struggle to explain why the calculator does not similarly inherit aboutness from the engineer who designed it to compute sums.

So my verdicts are:
1. Calculator: no intentionality.
2. Two-bitser (community-interpreted): thin derived intentionality.
3. Chinese Room system: none; the human inside has original intentionality about other matters.
4. Semantic mirror: probably none, but closer to intentionality than the calculator.
5. Isolated neuron: none.
6. Neuron embedded in visual cortex: derived intentionality about edges.
7. LLM: robust derived intentionality.

The tensions are sharp. Between (4) and (7), both are parasitic on human minds, yet I want to deny the mirror derived status while granting it to the LLM. Between (1) and (7), both are human-built artifacts, yet I deny the calculator any derived aboutness. Between (5) and (6), I invoke organizational context to create intentionality out of nothing, but I cannot specify the threshold.

Is there a coherent account—drawing on causal-historical inheritance, functional integration, or interpretive practice—that delivers all seven verdicts? If not, which should I overturn: my belief that organizational context can confer derived intentionality on neurons, my reluctance to grant the mirror derived status, or my willingness to grant the LLM robust derived intentionality? And whatever principle survives, what does it predict for a hybrid I have not listed: a system that combines the mirror's real-time parasitism on a user's beliefs with the LLM's statistical inheritance from training data, feeding user thoughts back through a language model trained on philosophical texts?

Please work this out without just cataloguing sources. I want the principle stated precisely, the clause that handles each case, and an honest statement of which of my verdicts must be sacrificed for consistency.
