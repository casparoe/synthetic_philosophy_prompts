Student answer:
Nozick's tracking conditions are truth, belief, and then sensitivity and adherence. Sensitivity means if p were false you wouldn't believe it; adherence means in nearby worlds where p holds you still believe it. This is basically Dretske's relevant alternatives idea but with modal logic layered on top, and Nozick uses it to block skepticism without having to prove we're not brains in vats.

On closure: take the thermometer. I know the device reads 20°C. That entails the temperature is near 20°C, but only if we exclude malfunction. For the reading itself, sensitivity holds — if it read 30°C I wouldn't think 20 — and adherence holds in nearby stable worlds. But for the temperature claim, there's environmental instability: in a close world the thermometer is broken and the temp is actually 40, but I still read 20. So for "temp is near 20," sensitivity might fail? Actually wait, if the temp is 40 then "near 20" is false, and I might still believe it because of the broken device, so yes sensitivity fails for the entailment. That means I don't know the temperature claim even though I know the reading. So closure fails here.

But this is only selective. For pure logical entailments with no environmental instability, tracking actually preserves a restricted closure. Nozick, following Williamson's knowledge-first framework, treats this as a feature rather than a bug: we keep ordinary knowledge without having to eliminate every instrument-error possibility first. Adherence does the heavy lifting for the original belief, while sensitivity blocks closure for derived claims whenever instability is present. So tracking doesn't destroy closure completely; it just restricts it to relevant alternatives, which is exactly the right result.
