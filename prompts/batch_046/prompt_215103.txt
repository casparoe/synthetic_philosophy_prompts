For an upper-level philosophy of logic seminar with 8 graduate students meeting for a single 2-hour session, design a participatory activity that makes the group feel the structural pressure of jumping out of the system (JOOTSing) through direct manipulation of the MU-puzzle, but with an injected twist: the card-based formal system includes a concealed meta-rule that only activates when a player’s own move is reinterpreted as belonging to the object-language rather than to the meta-language. The rules, roles, and material layout—not any preliminary lecture—should force participants to discover that the system’s apparent closure is broken by an internal reference they themselves introduced. Specify physical materials and roles (e.g., System-Keeper, Interpreter, Observer), a minute-by-minute timeline, progress conditions that require no monetary payoff, and a debrief that maps each discovered breakdown onto a feature of JOOTSing (the object-language/meta-language boundary, self-reference, and the need for an external vantage). Include an honest assessment of which feature of formal incompleteness the physical simulation necessarily cannot reproduce and must instead be supplied by the facilitator, and predict the most likely misstep when running the activity for the first time. The prompt should not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; participants should encounter the boundary and attempt an escape before they are told what it is called.
