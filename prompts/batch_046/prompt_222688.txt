everyone in my phil of sci circle keeps using the butcher analogy for natural kinds -- like Plato said, science should carve nature at its joints instead of hacking through bone. they use it to defend realism: the joints are already there, we just need sharp enough concepts. but then i was reading about how microbial taxonomy is basically a mess because horizontal gene transfer means bacteria don't have neat tree-like joints, and viral classification now uses evolutionary dynamics instead of phenotypic "species" lines. so if the "animal" being carved is constantly swapping limbs with other carcasses and rewriting its own anatomy, does the butcher image still do any work?

lay out the correspondance table -- what's the butcher, the knife, the joints, the meat, the bad hack? then show where it breaks, especially with prokaryote pangenomes and viral lineages that blur rather than split. what does the analogy smuggle in about objectivity and discreteness that these cases lack? does the realist conclusion -- that science discovers pre-existing divisions rather than inventing them -- survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the butcher carrying the whole argument? and if it's broken, is there a better anology for how taxonomy actually works in those domains?
