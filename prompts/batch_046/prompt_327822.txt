As a retired biophysicist who has only recently begun serious study of philosophy of science, I find the standard dichotomy between counterfactual and interventionist theories of causation inadequate for what I term historically sedimented systems—structures in which macro-level outcomes are constrained not by continuous causal mechanisms but by discrete, irreversible commitments made at earlier stages. Please construct a rigorous argument defending the claim that causal evaluation in such systems requires neither Lewis-style counterfactual dependence nor Woodward-style intervention conditions, but rather a distinct criterion that might be called path-commitment robustness: the stability of a macro-state across a defined range of counterfactual micro-perturbations that leave the sequence of prior commitments intact. Your argument should demonstrate why this criterion is metaphysically necessary rather than merely pragmatic, and should address the specific objection that interventionist theories can accommodate such cases by relaxing the manipulability requirement without abandoning its core logic.
