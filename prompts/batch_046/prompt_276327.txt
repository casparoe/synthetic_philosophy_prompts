Hey, I'm writing a feature on blame and moral responsibility for a general audience, and I keep getting stuck on this one conceptual knot in Strawson's reactive attitudes framework. It's not the usual free-will debate - it's weirder.

Strawson distinguishes the 'participant stance' - where we react with resentment, gratitude, etc. - from the 'objective stance,' where we view someone as an object to be managed, like when we see them as mentally ill. He thinks moral responsibility lives in the participant stance. Okay. But there's this tension: reactive attitudes seem both natural and involuntary (like flinching when you're insulted) and also rationally assessable (like judging that your resentment is justified or overblown). If they're just natural mechanisms, then asking whether they're 'justified' is like asking whether a sneeze is justified - category mistake. But if they're fully rational judgments, then Strawson hasn't escaped the rationalist picture he wanted to reject.

So here's the specific issue I'm trying to understand: can you apply the objective stance reflexively? That is, can I take the objective attitude toward my own resentment - viewing it not as a moral response but as a psychological event to be managed? And if I do that, am I opting out of the participant stance toward myself? There seems to be an asymetry here: it's relatively easy to take the objective stance toward others, but doing it reflexively feels like dissociation or self-deception rather than a cool philosophical move. So is the participant stance essentially self-constituting? Does that mean moral responsibility isn't really about whether others 'deserve' blame, but about whether we can maintain a coherent first-person stance?

I'm not asking for a reading list or citations - I just want to know whether this reflexsive problem is a genuine conceptual tension in Strawson's view, or if I'm mixing up psychological description with normative commitment. Can you explain the conceptual issue clearly, especially whether there's a clean way to separate the natural and normative dimensions of reactive attitudes without one swallowing the other?
