I am preparing an exercise for my metaphysics seminar on the special composition question, and I want to test whether I can isolate one structural variable in a realistic setting. Please construct a thought experiment to the following specifications.

The Scenario: Write one paragraph, about 120 words, describing a realistic, present-day biotechnology or materials-science arrangement—no science fiction, no exotic physics, no demons— in which parts form a single unbroken spatial region but are engineered so that they share no internal functional, neural, vascular, or causal integration. You may draw on tissue engineering or scaffold fabrication, but the case should be original: do not simply reproduce a standard example like a scattered object or a statue. Make the continuity genuinely present and the decoupling genuinely present at the same time.

The Three Verdicts: In about 150 words, state what universalism, spatial-continuity restrictionism (the view that spatial continuity alone is sufficient for composition), and functional-integration restrictionism (the view that parts compose only when they are causally or functionally unified) each say about whether there is one composite object in your scenario. Justify each verdict from the core principle of the view, not from intuition alone.

The Divergence Driver: In about 100 words, identify the exact feature of your scenario—present in the parts but absent in an ordinary organism—that causes the functional-integration view to deny composition while the other two affirm it. Explain why universalism is blind to that feature and why spatial-continuity restrictionism treats it as irrelevant.

The Defensive Counter: In about 100 words, show how a defender of spatial-continuity restrictionism—the view that, together with universalism, is forced to count your arrangement as an object—could attack the case. The attack should not be a bare denial; it should either argue that the scenario smuggles in hidden functional relations, or claim that the parts fail to be continuous in the metaphysically relevant sense.

The Collapse Test: In about 80 words, say what happens if the divergence-driving feature is removed—if full metabolic, neural, and causal integration is introduced across the region. Do all three views converge? If so, on what verdict? Or does the case collapse into a familiar standard example? Be explicit about which feature’s presence or absence is doing the work.

Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Reason only from the definitions of the positions and the structure of the scenario you build.
