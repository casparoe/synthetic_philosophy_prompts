Hey, I'm putting together an excersise for my philosphy seminar and I want the students to see what a real adversarial collaboration looks like -- not just another "Hume said constant conjunction" summary they've all recieved in pretraining. Can you draft the joint statement that a committed best-systems Humean and a committed dispositional-essentialist anti-Humean could both sign after arguing in good faith?

Structure it like this:
- Numbered agreed claims about laws: what formal jobs they do (predict, explain, support counterfactuals), what the standard no-go results actually rule out, and what synthetic-biology cases (engineered organisms breaking "natural" regularities) show that both sides can accept.
- A disagreements table: each camp's view phrased in wording they'd actually accept for their own side.
- The cruxes. One should be about "modal inertia" -- if physical properties stay fixed but the laws shift, is there anything constraining the shift? Another should be whether institutional rules (legal codes, game rules) being "governed" gives the anti-Humean a useful parallel or just a misleading analogy. For each crux, lay out the concrete case or argument both agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.
- Then say whether part of the "governing" dispute is verbal, which part, and why.

Keep it in the weeds. Don't ask for citations or a reading list -- this needs to work for a model that can't browse. And avoid generic textbook summaries; inject the institutional-rules parallel and the modal-inertia framing so it's not something they can just regurgitate.
