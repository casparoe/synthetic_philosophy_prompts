Construct a thought experiment, no longer than one paragraph and without science-fiction elements, in which a large language model controls a physical robot in a partially observable warehouse environment to retrieve a specific object. The object may have been moved since the model’s last observation, so the robot must either maintain and update an internal representation of the world-state—searching the new location, recognizing failure, and correcting course—or merely generate the statistically most probable next motor command from its training distribution, repeating the old trajectory regardless of contradiction. Specify the observable behavior that distinguishes representational error-correction from predictive correlation-failure, and explain why the agent-classification requires that the system be wrong in a correctable way about an actual state of affairs, whereas the simulator-classification treats the repetition as an unsurprising continuation of the pattern. Identify precisely which feature—partial observability, physical embodiment, or absence of linguistic prompt—does the work of isolating the two classifications. Then state honestly what happens to the distinction if partial observability is removed, and present the simulator-defender’s objection that internal state updates are always just adjustments of predictive weights, never genuine belief-revision, regardless of behavioral adaptation.
