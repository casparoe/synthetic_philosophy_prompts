Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for the debate over when the Anthropocene begins and who has the authority to declare it, designed for a philosophy-of-science or environmental-politics seminar audience. Do not organize the tree around familiar labels like “Capitalocene” or “Great Acceleration”; instead, build it from four branching commitments that a respondent could answer without knowing the taxonomy in advance. First branch: is a formal Global Boundary Stratotype Section and Point (GSSP) necessary for the period to exist, or can the Anthropocene be a valid historical-geological concept without ICS/IUGS ratification? Second branch: must the onset be globally synchronous, or is a diachronous regional onset—such as a 1610-style Columbian-exchange signal versus a mid-twentieth-century plutonium/CO₂ spike—acceptable? Third branch: is the decisive criterion geochemical/biostratigraphic (radionuclides, nitrogen cycle), biotic (domesticated biomass, extinction rates), or socio-economic (colonial extraction patterns, fossil-capital accumulation)? Fourth branch: does legitimate decision authority rest solely with stratigraphic commissions, with an interdisciplinary Anthropocene Working Group, with climate-science bodies, or with affected communities and political assemblies? At each leaf, place either a named position that actually occupies that logical combination (e.g., formalist synchronous geochemical advocates, diachronous socio-economic critics, pluralist “unnamed” combinations) or mark it “unnamed,” and append one sentence describing that leaf’s most awkward commitment—such as having to deny that local ecological collapses count as Anthropocene onsets, or having to defend democratic ratification of a rock-layer boundary. Explicitly note any combination that remains unoccupied and briefly diagnose whether it seems incoherent, merely unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of leaves that your tree fails to separate despite substantive disagreement between them, and specify what further distinction—perhaps about counterfactual stratigraphy or about the normative weight of naming—would be needed to split them. Keep the tree to roughly eight to twelve leaves, and ensure no branch asks for a bibliography, citation, or reading list.
