We need two camps. Camp A holds that Aristotle's distinction between oikonomia (household management) and chrematistike (wealth-getting) is a real normative boundary grounded in his teleology: money, nomisma, is conventional (by nomos) but should stay subordinate to natural use-value, and interest (tokos) is literally unnatural because money doesnt reproduce like living things or crops. Camp B thinks this is anachronistic — the conventionality of nomisma actually undermines any natural limit to exchange, so chrematistike isnt corruption but extension, and the biological metaphor behind tokos is just that, a metaphor without normative force.
