I'm moderating a podcast colloquium Q&A with Dr. Lena Voss, author of the recent monograph Temporal Profiles and Objecthood, which argues that the endurantism–perdurantism debate is methodologically inverted. Rather than deriving an ontology of persistence from pre-given persistence conditions, Voss proposes that we begin with causal-continuity profiles—patterns of counterfactual dependence and functional integration across time—and allow those profiles to determine whether an object is best modeled as enduring or as possessing temporal parts. On her account, macroscopic artifacts typically come out as enduring because their causal profiles are discontinuous and conventionally reinstated, whereas living organisms and social institutions come out as having causally-defined temporal parts, since their profiles are continuously graded. She expects critics to reply that she has merely relabeled four-dimensionalism in causal vocabulary. Write me a sequence of interview questions that makes that deflection progressively harder. Begin by pressing why causal profiles should have metaphysical priority over intrinsic persistence conditions rather than serving only as epistemic heuristics. Next, target the artifact–organism asymmetry: if a bronze statue is melted and later recast into an identical shape, her view apparently treats the recast object as enduring only if a specific causal profile is preserved—yet that preservation condition seems modal and conventional rather than metaphysically deep. Include a follow-up asking whether this collapses her position into conventionalism about object identity. End with a question asking what conceptual or empirical finding—perhaps in the metaphysics of organisms or the ontology of institutions—would actually force her to abandon the reversal of priority and return to the standard framing.
