I've been toying with this view that dispositions aren't intrinsic properties of objects but are three-place relations: object, stimulus, and the absence of a specific masking condition in the enviroment. So fragility isn't in the glass—it's in the glass-stimulus-no-mask relation. I think this sidesteps the classic fink problem since the disposition doesn't vanish; the relation just gets blocked. But what would actually count against it? I want arguments and observations sorted by how decisive they'd be—like intuitive cases where we still attribute fragility despite knowing a mask is present, or results showing this collapses into anti-realism. Also check whether I've insulated it too much: can any counterexample just be redescribed as a hidden mask, and if so is that unfalsifiability a real problem for this kind of metaphysical claim? And include what defenders of relational dispositionalism would themselves admit counts against them, not just what critics throw at it.
