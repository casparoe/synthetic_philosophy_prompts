I'm in a philosophy of mind course, and a classmate summarized Harnad's symbol grounding problem like this: "The problem proves that syntax is never sufficient for semantics, because symbol tokens can only refer outside the system if they are anchored by direct causal connections to physical objects. That is essentially the same point as Searle's Chinese Room argument—both show that any system lacking biological embodiment or direct world-contact, including every large language model, can only simulate understanding rather than actually possess it." The summary also claims that Harnad's proposed solution requires full robotic embodiment with literal sensors and effectors, and that any purely virtual or simulated sensorimotor loop would fail because transduction demands actual physical energy exchange.

This seems mostly right, but I suspect it conflates two distinct arguments and overstates the embodiment requirement. Is the summary fair? Please separate what accurately captures Harnad's view, what is a standard simplification, and what misrepresents either Harnad or Searle—especially on whether the Chinese Room and grounding problem are the same objection, and on whether Harnad's transduction condition necessarily rules out virtual grounding in principle or merely leaves it as an open question he never addressed.
