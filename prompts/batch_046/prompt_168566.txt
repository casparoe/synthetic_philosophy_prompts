I am helping a friend who manages a community meal program choose a default menu for weekly dinners. She is deciding among three options: a chicken-based dish, a beef-based dish, or an entirely plant-based meal built around grains and legumes. She wants her choice to reduce harm to animals, but she has recently encountered a line of reasoning that troubles her simple intuition. She notes that producing plant foods requires extensive crop harvesting and pest management, which kills substantial numbers of wild rodents, birds, and insects in the fields. By contrast, beef—though it involves raising and slaughtering cattle—requires far less cultivated feed per calorie and therefore might cause fewer wild-animal deaths in total. At the same time, she recognizes that serving chicken means many more individual farmed animals are killed per portion than serving beef. She is unsure whether she should focus on avoiding the visible harm done to the farmed animals being served, or whether she should try to minimize the overall number of beings affected by the entire production chain, including those killed in growing crops. How should she think through this decision, and what considerations ought to determine which option is genuinely preferable?
