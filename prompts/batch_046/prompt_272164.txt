Consider the Sleeping Beauty problem under its standard protocol: on Sunday a fair coin is flipped; if heads, Beauty is awakened and interviewed once on Monday; if tails, she is awakened on Monday and again on Tuesday, with full amnesia of any prior awakening. Upon finding herself awake, she is asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. The "thirder" answer is one-third, calculated by treating the three awakening-events—Monday-Heads, Monday-Tails, and Tuesday-Tails—as equiprobable members of the sample space over which she conditions.

My difficulty is not with the arithmetic but with the epistemic category of the conditioning event itself. The proposition "I am awake right now" is self-locating, or centered: its truth-value depends not merely on the world-state (the coin result and the protocol) but on the subject's spatiotemporal location within that world. In standard Bayesian conditioning we update a prior over world-describing hypotheses H by observing an event E that is a subset of the uncentered sample space. Yet here E seems to be an indexical coordinate rather than an empirical observation about the coin. If H is "the coin is heads," then the centered event "I am currently experiencing an awakening" is true in worlds with heads and in worlds with tails alike; it does not partition the uncentered worlds. So treating this centered belief as the conditioning event in Bayes's rule appears to be a category error—like using a change in coordinate system as if it were new data about an external state of affairs.

Defenders of the one-third answer evidently reject this diagnosis, but I cannot see where precisely my reasoning fails. Is the mistake in assuming that the sample space must remain fixed at the level of uncentered worlds? Must we instead define the prior over a space of centered worlds—Lewis-style worlds with a designated center—so that conditioning on one's own location is formally legitimate? Or is there a different error: for instance, in conflating the Self-Sampling Assumption with the Self-Indication Assumption, and thereby misidentifying what the awakening-frequency actually bears on? I am not asking for a restatement of the thirder calculation, but for a precise identification of the conceptual mistake in treating centered, indexical evidence as categorically ineligible for Bayesian conditioning about non-indexical hypotheses like the coin outcome.
