As a PhD student in metaphysics and social ontology, I am examining how the endurantist–perdurantist dispute extends to convention-dependent, non-physical entities. I would like you to compare endurantism and perdurantism with respect to the diachronic identity of social institutions—specifically, institutions such as universities or federal states whose individual members are entirely replaced over generations and whose founding charters contain self-amending provisions capable of altering the very criteria for institutional continuity. For each view, explain how it accounts for the institution’s persistence through both complete membership turnover and successive constitutional revisions that change the rules governing membership and authority. Then assess which framework yields the more coherent ontology of institutional identity: does continuity require the enduring presence of some core members, rules, or spatiotemporal location, or can it be reconstructed as a chain of temporally extended institutional stages linked by appropriate causal, conventional, or functional relations? Justify your evaluation by addressing whether an endurantist must posit an immutable institutional essence and whether a perdurantist can avoid reducing the institution to an arbitrarily demarcated series of instantaneous institutional slices.
