At a coffee shop, my friends Lena and Marcus argued about whether being able to clearly imagine a situation proves it could actually happen. Lena said that if you can form a detailed mental picture with no obvious contradiction inside it, that is genuine evidence for real possibility, not mere fantasy. Marcus objected that people have always conceived of things like perpetual motion machines that seemed perfectly consistent until hidden physical conflicts were discovered, so an absence of contradiction in imagination only shows our ignorance, not possibility. Lena replied that those cases involve hidden scientific laws, but conceiving of a conscious computer program that runs with absolutely no physical substrate—not even virtual hardware—contains no hidden logical conflict we can identify, so its clear conceivability should count in favor of its being possible. Marcus answered that this is the same error: the contradiction is hidden in our incomplete understanding of consciousness, just as thermodynamic violations were hidden in the inventors’ mechanics; if we do not yet understand why substrate is necessary, we cannot claim the image is contradiction-free. They asked me to referee, and I honestly could not decide. Please explain who is right about which specific point, who is more right overall, and what—if anything—both are missing about how imagination relates to real possibility.
