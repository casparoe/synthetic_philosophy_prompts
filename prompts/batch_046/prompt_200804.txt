I'm trying to work out what actually counts as democratic backsliding by testing my intuitions against concrete cases. Hungary's court-packing and media capture while retaining competitive elections feels like clear backsliding. Poland's judicial overhaul paired with EU institutional conflict also feels like backsliding, but of a different kind—more legalistic erosion than authoritarian consolidation. Turkey's shift to a presidential system via popular referendum is genuinely ambiguous: if voters democratically approve the restructuring, is it backsliding or just constitutional redesign? The US pattern—state-level gerrymandering, voter-ID expansions, January 6, followed by a peaceful transition—feels like severe democratic stress without fully crossing into backsliding. Now consider a hypothetical: an elected government passes "algorithmic transparency" rules for digital platforms, but the implementation is designed so that opposition speech gets systematically suppressed without any explicit censorship statute or named bans. That feels like backsliding to me, even though formal rights remain intact. By contrast, a democracy with chronically low turnout due to civic apathy and poor education doesn't feel like backsliding at all, just weak democratic culture.

I'm tentatively drawn to a distinction between "procedural decoupling"—preserving democratic forms while disconnecting them from actual power allocation—and "substantive hollowing"—eroding norms without formal institutional change. But this framework struggles with Turkey (a democratic procedure used to eliminate democratic substance) and with the algorithmic hypothetical (no formal rights removed, yet opposition access to power is structurally degraded). Is there a sharper principle, perhaps centered on whether institutional changes are reversible through ordinary democratic contestation, or on whether opposition pathways to power are systematically degraded regardless of formal rules? How should it classify the US case, and does it require me to revise my intuition that Poland and Hungary belong in the same category?
