I've just had a long, slightly frustrating arguement with a mate over pints about corruption and whether we're just exporting Western morality. I'm trying to write up where I actually stand for a seminar on political ethics, but I'm worried my views are a bit of a mess. Basically I think corruption is best understood as institutional betrayal — using an office in ways that defeat its own purpose — rather than as breaking a legal rule or violating local norms of reciprocity. At the same time, I think in loads of places the formal state apparatus was imported without real local legitimacy, so clientelist gift-giving — like bringing a goat or some cash to get your papers sorted — is often just a functional moral economy. Calling it corruption feels like imposing a Weberian bureaucratic ideal that never actually existed there.

But I also think there's a clear line between that kind of embedded exchange and, say, a minister siphoning off infrastructure funds for private jets — that feels wrong everywhere, not just because it breaks some formal rule but because it causes real harm and violates equal standing. I also think Western anti-corruption indexes are mostly measuring how closely a country resembles a Northern European bureaucracy, which is ethnocentric and unreliable. Still, I'm not a full relativist — I think reflective judgement can seperate harmful structural adaptation from genuinely wrongful extraction. So here's my worry: do these actually hang together? It feels like my 'institutional betrayal' idea needs a stable idea of what the institution is for, which my second point undermines by saying the purpose is contested. And then my third claim seems to smuggle in universal moral norms that the second and fourth want to reject. Meanwhile the fourth and fifth look like they're in tension but maybe they aren't? Could you audit this for me — rank the real tensions by severity, say which ones only look bad on the surface, and tell me the least costly revision? I don't want to just drop whichever view makes me feel good; I want to know what I'm actually committed to.
