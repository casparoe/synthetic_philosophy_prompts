I am currently auditing a graduate seminar in epistemology that has spent the past several weeks on Plato's Meno and the so-called value problem: if knowledge and true belief are both equally effective at guiding successful action, what makes knowledge more valuable? Our discussions have moved well beyond standard textbook treatments into a more specific debate about whether the surplus value comes from modal robustness — the idea that knowledge, unlike mere true belief, is stable across nearby possible worlds — or from a kind of cognitive credit that makes the knower responsible for the belief's success. Last week, a colleague proposed a thought experiment I had not encountered before: imagine a belief formed by a random-number generator that happens, by pure chance, to match the state of the world, guiding action just as well as any expert judgment. The seminar ended with an open tension between those who think this scenario exposes the poverty of true belief and those who worry that appealing to robustness or virtue simply renames the intuition without explaining it. To close our final session, I would like a poem that captures this precise conceptual knot. Please write a Petrarchan sonnet — ABBA ABBA CDE CDE — in which the octave presents the practical equivalence of knowledge and true belief, using imagery of navigation or archery to suggest that both hit the mark. The volta at line nine should turn on the random-generator scenario: a belief that strikes true without any tracking relation to reality. In the sestet, the rhyme scheme should pair terms of stability with terms of contingency, and the poem should leave unresolved whether the value of knowledge lies in its modal safety, in the avoidance of epistemic luck, or in the intellectual virtue of the believer. The language should be precise enough that a philosopher of knowledge would recognize the post-Gettier value problem and the virtue-epistemological framing, rather than seeing generic references to knowing versus guessing. I do not need citations or references to specific texts; I want the conceptual structure to do the work.
