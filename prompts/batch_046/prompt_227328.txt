I serve on my hospital's Ethics and Narrative Identity Review Board, and we must reach a concrete decision before Thursday's meeting. Our case involves a 42-year-old patient with documented dissociative identity disorder who executed an advance directive containing what she calls a narrative partition clause: she argues that personal identity is constituted by a center of narrative gravity rather than by biological continuity alone, and that if clinical narrative assessment shows her autobiographical coherence falling below a specified integration threshold, decision-making authority should shift to whichever alter maintains the dominant narrative thread. Following a recent trauma, two alters now present competing directives—one invokes the clause, claims the other alter's narrative is discontinuous with the pre-trauma self, and requests withdrawal of life support; the other asserts that biological continuity preserves a single patient and demands continued treatment. Our institutional policy permits narrative-sensitive accommodation but defines neither its scope nor its limits. Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate: recommend a specific course of action with a clear time frame—whether to honor the partition clause, apply standard biological-continuity criteria, or adopt an intermediate procedural solution such as a temporary narrative-assessment panel. The reasoning must engage seriously with the strongest argument for honoring the clause, namely that imposing biological continuity alone disregards the patient's own well-developed theoretical framework and risks substituting an external conception of selfhood for her autonomous one. It must also treat honestly, rather than dismissively, the strongest opposing consideration—that recognizing narrative partition within an institutional setting erodes the legal unity of the person and creates an unworkable precedent for allocating decision-making authority. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the board needs formal, actionable reasoning it can apply directly. Keep the recommendation focused and polished.
