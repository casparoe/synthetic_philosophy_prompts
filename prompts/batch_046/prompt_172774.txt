I have been trying to work out, on my own, whether the absurd is best understood as an emotional condition or as a structural mismatch between what we seek and what the world offers. From my reading, Camus presents the absurd as arising when our demand for rational clarity collides with the silence of the universe. At the same time, I have encountered more recent philosophical accounts — often called hybrid theories — which hold that a meaningful life must combine subjective engagement with objective worth. My difficulty is conceptual. If these hybrid accounts are correct, then meaning can apparently fail in at least two separate ways: either nothing possesses objective worth, or we are subjectively alienated from what does possess it. Camus's absurd seems to involve both failures at once — we crave objective significance, discover none, and that very discovery undermines our capacity for subjective investment. So is absurdism simply the simultaneous collapse of both conditions within a hybrid framework? Or does it name a different kind of breakdown altogether — one in which the two conditions are not merely absent but stand in active, irreconcilable tension? I would like you to explain the precise conceptual relationship between absurdism and these hybrid theories of meaning, and to take a clear, committed position: should absurdism be analysed as a compound failure internal to hybrid accounts, or as an independent phenomenon that those accounts are unable to fully accommodate? Please do not merely list possibilities; argue for the view you think best withstands scrutiny.
