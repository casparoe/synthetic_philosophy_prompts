Write a decision-tree taxonomy of interpretations of von Neumann's claim that self-replicating technology could outlast human civilization. Begin at the root with the question of what "survival" means: physical persistence of the replicators, functional continuation of their operations, or informational persistence of their design pattern. At the second level, for each branch ask whether the replicators remain instruments of original human design intent or become autonomous evolutionary agents; where relevant, ask also whether preservation of specifically human values is required or whether mere sustained operation is enough. Label each leaf with a named position if one exists, otherwise "unnamed," and attach a one-sentence statement of its most awkward commitment—e.g., that a corrupted replicator serving no human purpose still counts as our legacy, or that human design was only a temporary bootloader. Identify any unoccupied combination of answers and diagnose it briefly as incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate. Do not request citations, named scholars, bibliographies, or specific publication dates. Present the taxonomy itself first, with no introductory commentary; then, in the very first sentence of your reasoning, state your bottom-line verdict—which leaf best captures von Neumann's actual view and why—followed by an explanation of the branching logic.
