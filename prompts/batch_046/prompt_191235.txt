Exam Question (context only):
Daniel Dennett distinguishes four kinds of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—based on how behavioral options are generated and selected. Consider the thesis that a creature could be Gregorian (capable of acquiring symbolic language that transmits representations across individuals) without being Popperian, if its linguistic capacities are implemented through pure associative conditioning on symbolic tokens rather than through internal generation and selection of hypotheses. In your answer: (a) briefly characterize each of Dennett's four kinds; (b) explain why Dennett holds that Gregorian capacities depend on Popperian architecture; and (c) assess the proposed "Skinnerian-Gregorian hybrid," paying special attention to whether symbolic transmission can occur without internal modeling of counterfactual possibilities.

Student Answer:
Darwinian creatures are hard-wired by natural selection; they don't learn individually. Skinnerian creatures learn through operant conditioning—rewards and punishments shape behavior through actual environmental interaction. Popperian creatures are essentially Skinnerian ones that have moved the trial-and-error process inside: they generate behavioral hypotheses internally and select among them before acting, which lets them avoid fatal mistakes. Gregorian creatures build on this by using language and cultural tools, allowing them to learn from the accumulated experience of others rather than only from personal trial and error.

Dennett argues that Gregorian capacities depend on Popperian architecture because language is a tool for thinking, and thinking requires generating and testing ideas internally. He also holds that Gregorian creatures are necessarily conscious, since language requires phenomenal awareness of meaning. Without the Popperian capacity for internal selection, a creature would just be parroting sounds without understanding them.

Now consider the proposed Skinnerian-Gregorian hybrid: a creature that acquires symbolic communication through pure associative conditioning on tokens, without internal hypothesis generation. This seems viable. Alarm calls or instruction symbols can be reinforced like any other stimulus-response pair. Once learned, the creature can transmit and receive representations across individuals. Because the symbols carry information, the creature stands on others' shoulders without needing to model counterfactuals itself. So Gregorian transmission can be implemented on a Skinnerian base; Dennett's dependency claim confuses the vehicle of transmission (language) with the cognitive architecture required to use it.
