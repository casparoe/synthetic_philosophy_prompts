I'm the coordinator for a distributed translation collective — about forty volunteers across six languages with no central editor. Every month i have to decide whether some unwritten practice (like leaving glosses in shared docs, using emoji for feedback, skipping peer review on short pieces) has hardened into a Lewis-style convention that should go in our charter, or if its just a habit we'll regret locking in. Stakes are medium: formalize too early and we kill flexibility, wait too long and people feel betrayed when someone breaks the norm. I need a reusable procedure i can run in twenty minutes without being a philosopher, with ordered tests (does it solve a coordination problem? is there common knowledge of conformity? do people prefer to go along? is there an obvious alternative?), and explicit flags for where i'm just making a judgment call. The steering committee of five has to sign off and they wont read theory. Give me worked applications to three cases: (A) after one miscommunication everyone started timestamping glosses, (B) a subgroup replaced written feedback with emoji reactions and newcomers copy it automatically, and (C) people stopped fixing gender-agreement errors in one language pair because i once told them not to worry about it when i was tired — and i really dont want to codify my own mistake. Say what the procedure cant do, especially around hidden resentment or fake conformity. Keep it practical.
