Having recently returned to my country of origin after a prolonged absence, I have witnessed a government implement a sequence of technically legal constitutional amendments—restructuring the constitutional court, altering the composition of the electoral commission, and imposing registration requirements on foreign-funded civil-society organizations—without suspending elections or declaring emergency rule. Among my colleagues and family, these changes are described by some as necessary democratic corrections and by others as clear democratic backsliding. This divergence has convinced me that I need to understand whether the concept itself has objective content.

Write an essay that addresses the following controversy: Is democratic backsliding an analytically coherent category with identifiable, cross-contextual criteria, or is it essentially a partisan label applied to institutional transformations that displace a particular liberal-democratic baseline?

Your essay should weigh arguments on both sides, but you must reach and defend a specific conclusion rather than ending on a non-committal note. In particular, structure your argument around three issues. First, examine the baseline problem: who determines the institutional standard from which decline is measured, and can that standard be specified without presupposing a contested ideological settlement? Second, distinguish between procedural backsliding—direct manipulation of electoral rules, judicial independence, or horizontal accountability—and what might be called epistemic backsliding, in which the conditions for informed democratic deliberation are eroded even while formal procedures remain intact. Assess whether only the first is objectively diagnosable or whether both can be captured by a unified concept. Third, consider the comparative evidence from Hungary, Poland, Turkey, and the United States: do these cases reveal common institutional patterns that support an objective concept, or do they show that accusations of backsliding track partisan opposition rather than structural degradation?

If you defend the objectivity of the concept, specify the minimal, non-circular criteria that identify it; if you reject it, propose more precise descriptive categories—such as constitutional restructuring, competitive authoritarian consolidation, or majoritarian reform—to replace it. Your conclusion should be explicit and justified.
