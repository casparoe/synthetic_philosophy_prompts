Give an example of an institutional or professional role—specify the role and a concrete decision scenario—where Bernard Williams's integrity objection to utilitarian calculation applies with full force (the agent is pressured to abandon a ground project or deep attachment for the sake of aggregate welfare), but where the diagnosis of one thought too many simultaneously misfires, because the role's legitimate normative structure requires exactly the kind of substitutable, comparative reasoning that Williams condemns as corrupting personal relations. After laying out the correspondence and the divergence, assess whether this shows that the integrity objection and the one-thought-too-many diagnosis are separable, whether it reveals a hidden institutional exception in Williams's view, or whether it instead demonstrates that both criticisms depend on a prior distinction between personal and impersonal domains that the example collapses.
