Please explain the conceptual tension in Orwell's thought between the linguistic determinism he articulates in his essay linking political corruption to decayed language—where imprecise diction is said to make corrupt thought unavoidable—and the possibility of individual resistance depicted in his novel about totalitarian control, particularly through the mental discipline of secret writing and the practice of doublethink. Specifically, clarify whether doublethink operates as an institutionally induced form of collective self-deception compatible with determinism, or whether its reliance on a deliberate, agentive manipulation of one's own beliefs contradicts the claim that language corrupts thought from the outside in. Keep the explanation focused on the internal coherence of these two positions.
