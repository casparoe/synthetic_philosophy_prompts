I have been reflecting on the Jain concept of anekāntavāda—the teaching that reality is so multifaceted that contradictory descriptions of it can each hold partial truth, rather than one being simply false. This made me think of a practice I read about in complex systems engineering, where design teams deliberately maintain several incompatible models of the same structure, not because they have not yet picked the best one, but because each model reveals different failure modes that the others conceal. Could you compare anekāntavāda with this engineering practice of sustained model pluralism? In particular, does the Jain view treat contradiction as an ontological feature of the world itself, while the engineering approach treats it only as an epistemic tool? And which of the two offers the stronger reason for resisting the pressure to collapse genuine disagreement into a single account?
