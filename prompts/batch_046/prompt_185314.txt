me and my roommate got into it about higher-order evidence and epistemic akrasia after our phil seminar. he tried to prove rational belief is literally impossible with this chain:

(1) if you've got higher-order evidance that your reasoning about p is unreliable, holding p puts you in epistemic akrasia.
(2) akrasia is always irrational, period.
(3) but for any belief you can always cook up some higher-order doubt - like maybe you're tired or just biased.
(4) so there's always available higher-order evidance against every p.
(5) therefore holding any belief is always akratic.
(6) so rationality demands global skeptisism.

he says denying (2) is the only escape, which lets akrasia be rational sometimes via level-splitting or whatever. i feel like the slip is in (3) or (4) - not every manufactured doubt counts as real evidance. but i can't quite see why (1) doesn't already cover it. which premise should we actually drop, and what's the cost? especially if we reject (2) what are we signing up for?
