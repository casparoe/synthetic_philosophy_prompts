I'm a novelist developing the philosophical voice of a systems biologist character who has recently rejected reductionism, and before I commit her views to the manuscript I want to test whether my own parallel commitments form a coherent package. Here is where I have landed: (a) I am a physicalist in that all concrete entities are ultimately composed of elementary physical particles; (b) I hold that mental intentionality—beliefs, desires, and propositional attitudes—is fully reducible to neural dynamics, leaving no ontological residue; (c) I maintain that chemical properties such as solubility are reducible in principle but multi-realizable, which grants chemistry epistemic autonomy without ontological emergence; (d) I claim that biological organization exhibits strong emergence: properties like homeostatic regulation are genuinely novel and exert downward causation upon their constituent parts; (e) I deny downward causation in every other domain, including economic markets and conscious experience; (f) I think the socialist calculation debate shows that market prices are informational structures that cannot be algorithmically derived from individual preferences, yet I classify this as weak emergence—merely an epistemic limitation—rather than an ontologically strong form; and (g) I take phenomenal consciousness to be identical with a specific pattern of integrated information in the brain, hence reducible in exactly the manner described in (b). Do these positions hang together? I am particularly concerned that allowing downward causation at the biological level in (d) while reducing mental properties in (b) and (g) creates an unacknowledged conflict, since consciousness arises within biological systems; I also worry that my appeal to the calculation debate in (f) implicitly borrows the conceptual apparatus of strong emergence even while I deny it applies there. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision for each? Rank the tensions by severity, specify the single commitment whose removal would restore the most consistency, and indicate whether my categorization of market emergence in (f) survives scrutiny given (c) and (d).
