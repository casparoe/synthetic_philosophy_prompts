If we take Quine’s naturalism and his rejection of first philosophy seriously, unsettled questions remain about where moral values fit in an ontology governed by the criterion that to be is to be the value of a bound variable. Among the open lines of work: whether moral properties must be eliminated, reduced to descriptive psychological states, or accommodated as pragmatic posits alongside abstract mathematical objects; how the indeterminacy of translation applies to moral disagreement across conceptual schemes; and whether moral claims belong to the interconnected network of belief in a way that permits rational revision, or whether they are insulated in a manner that undermines naturalized moral epistemology. Of these, which is most upstream—meaning that settling it would do the most to resolve the others? Give me a dependency-aware prioritization for a philosopher working without an empirical laboratory, defending your ordering with explicit criteria (downstream dependence, tractability, neglectedness, or whatever you take to matter), noting where these criteria conflict, and specifying what kind of argument or result over the next few years would cause you to reorder them. I want a defended research agenda, not a bibliographic survey.
