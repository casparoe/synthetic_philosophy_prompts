For an upper-level jurisprudence seminar of about fourteen students meeting for one ninety-minute session, design a structured role-play that makes participants feel why the separation of law and morality becomes unstable under procedural pressure, rather than merely asserting it. Create a fictional jurisdiction, Novum, governed entirely by a written Codex of twelve explicit procedural rules that contain no moral vocabulary; divide students into three panels of four or five, each serving as an appellate court. Give every panel the same hard case: an automated penalty was technically triggered under Rule Four, but only because Rule Seven contains a hidden internal contradiction that makes consistent application impossible without either importing an unstated moral principle to resolve ambiguity or accepting that an entire class of subjects falls outside legal protection entirely. The panels must issue a verdict and justify it solely by reference to the Codex; they may invoke fairness or justice only if they can demonstrate that the Codex’s own procedural integrity demands it. Include specific roles (a presiding judge, a dissenting recorder, and two advocates), a forty-minute deliberation timeline with facilitator prompts at minutes ten and thirty, and a scoring mechanism that rewards internal consistency with the Codex but penalizes arbitrary or ad hoc outcomes. After revealing the contradiction, conduct a thirty-minute debrief that maps each panel’s strategy onto Hart’s separation thesis versus Fuller’s internal morality of law—distinguishing those who treated morality as an external corrective from those who discovered that the system’s own machinery forced them to smuggle in moral reasoning to preserve coherence. Be explicit about which feature of Fuller’s view—his claim that extreme procedural failure renders a system not law at all—this simulation cannot reproduce and that you, as facilitator, must supply directly. Predict the likely first-run failure: students will resolve the contradiction by appealing to common-sense fairness before they have articulated why the Codex itself requires that resolution, and specify how to redirect that impulse into noticing the procedural collapse. The design should require no prior reading of primary texts; participants should encounter the problem through the rules alone. Also include an honest note on what the activity still misses about the real-world force of legal positivism.
