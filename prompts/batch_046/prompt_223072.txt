I have been trying, in conversation with a friend who teaches logic and in the margins of a course I am auditing on epistemology, to work out a position on the regress of justification that I think falls between the standard three options, though I am increasingly unsure it is stable. I hold that foundationalism fails not because there are no basic beliefs, but because the property of being basic is not epistemically privileged unless we already possess a criterion for basicness, which sends us back into the regress; that coherentism fails not because coherence is unimportant, but because coherence relations themselves require justification, which either makes them basic (collapsing into foundationalism) or part of the circle (making coherence circular); and that infinitism, while avoiding both traps, requires an infinite chain that no finite mind can actually traverse, so it describes an ideal epistemic structure rather than human knowledge. Against all three, I want to defend what I will call localized structuralism: the view that justification is always regional, that within any region beliefs cohere without vicious circularity because the coherence is directional—some beliefs support others asymmetrically—and that regions are anchored not by basic beliefs but by practical commitments that are not themselves in need of epistemic justification because they are conditions for the possibility of asking for justification at all. I am borrowing the idea that some norms are preconditions rather than conclusions, but applying it specifically to the architecture of belief rather than to ethics or language.

What would genuinely count against this view? I want the arguments and observations that should move me, sorted roughly by how decisive they would be, including whether there is a formal result showing that directional coherence within regions necessarily collapses into linear foundationalism, whether my appeal to practical commitments is just foundationalism by another name, and whether infinitists would say my regions are merely truncated infinite chains. I also want an honest assessment of whether this position has been qualified into unfalsifiability by the flexibility of regional boundaries and directional relations, and if so, whether that insulation is a defect for a theory of justification or a realistic feature of how epistemic evaluation actually works. I want defenders of the three standard views to say what they themselves would concede as damaging, not only what critics allege, and I want you to distinguish evidence that would be merely awkward from evidence that should actually change my mind.
