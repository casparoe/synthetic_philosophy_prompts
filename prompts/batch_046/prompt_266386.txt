As a retired biologist who has recently begun serious study in philosophy of science and language, I am preparing to moderate a podcast interview with an unnamed contemporary philosopher who defends a position I will call protocol performativity. The view holds that standardized experimental protocols—especially measurement declarations such as this sample is calibrated to reference standard X—function not primarily as descriptive reports but as Austin-style explicit performatives: they constitute the institutional status of the sample within a laboratory’s normative framework, and their success depends on felicity conditions (authorized speaker, correct procedure, receptive institutional context) rather than on correspondence to an independent state of affairs. The philosopher further claims that this explains why replication failures often target the descriptive gloss rather than the performative core, and that meta-analytic reviews can inadvertently perform retroactive annulments when they treat prior protocol utterances as straightforwardly falsifiable descriptions.

Please assume the role of an expert philosophical interviewer with deep familiarity with speech-act theory and the philosophy of scientific practice. Before drafting, think carefully about the actual commitments of this view and briefly outline the three main pressure points where the literature on performatives and institutional knowledge most directly challenges it. Then draft a formal, polished sequence of eight to ten interview questions suitable for an intellectually serious podcast audience. Avoid generic biographical or restatement questions.

Your questions should turn on the real content of the position and should include sharp follow-ups keyed to the answers the philosopher is most likely to offer—particularly the reply that the descriptive residue is just the felicity conditions made explicit, and the reply that institutional constitution is fully compatible with realism because the institution tracks stable causal structures. Specifically, cover: (i) how the view distinguishes performative force from unavoidable descriptive content, and what conceptual or empirical test could separate them; (ii) how misfire conditions—say, an unauthorized technician issuing the calibration declaration—affect the ontological status of the sample, and whether the view commits its defender to institutional conventionalism about scientific objects; (iii) whether the claim about meta-analytic retroactive annulments implies that scientific consensus is constituted rather than discovered, and if so, how the view avoids collapsing into a purely sociological account; and (iv) what observable pattern in replication studies would genuinely count against protocol performativity rather than being reinterpreted as a context-shift or misfire. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not invoke any highly obscure theoretical apparatus that would require specialized retrieval beyond standard philosophical literacy.
