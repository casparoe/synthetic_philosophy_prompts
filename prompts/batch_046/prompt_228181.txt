I've been developing a view I call modal-fragility absurdism, and I want to stress-test it before committing further. The core claim is not that human life is meaningless in any global sense, but that the conditions making particular activities, relationships, or achievements meaningful are locally satisfiable yet globally unstable in a specific modal register: they depend on sustaining practical identities that rest on social, biological, and institutional infrastructures lacking any teleological guarantee of persistence. Contemporary objective-list theorists—those who hold that meaning requires both objective goods (knowledge, love, achievement) and appropriate subjective engagement—tend to treat existential threat as a failure of individual fit or social recognition. I think this misdiagnoses the problem. The threat is not that we fail to align with stable values, but that the very framework rendering "fitting fulfillment" intelligible is a transient equilibrium rather than a robust normative structure. Absurdism, on this reading, is not the absence of value but the recognition that value-claims in human life have the modal status of temporary configurations: genuinely meaningful here and now, but unable to bear the weight of the existential question they are invoked to answer.
