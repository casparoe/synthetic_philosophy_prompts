Here's an argument I cooked up about generations and now I'm stuck because it seems to nuke the whole idea of cohorts, and I can't tell if it's valid or just sneaky. (1) If Gen Z is a real kind, then being Gen Z has to be an intrinsic property of individuals, not a relational property like "born after 1997". (2) Real kinds are bound by shared causal mechanisms or essences, not by calendar conventions. (3) But socioligists literally define Gen Z by birth years 1997-2012, so membership is purely conventional - there's no biological mechanism that activates on Jan 1 1997. (4) So Gen Z isn't a real kind; it's just a demographic cluster, like "people whose height is between 5'8 and 5'9". (5) Now apply the same logic to "adolescent" or "elderly": those also rely on age cutoffs (13-19, 65+) that don't track sharp biological joints. (6) So if Gen Z is unreal because of arbitary boundaries, then adolescence and old age are also unreal. (7) But adolescence clearly tracks a real developmental stage, so arbitrary boundaries don't always destroy kindhood - they can frame a real process. (8) So Gen Z might also frame a real social/psychological kind despite the birth-year framing. (9) But if Gen Z is a real psychological kind, then membership should depend on having the relevant traits (digital nativity, climate anxiety, etc.), not on birth year. (10) That means someone born in 1995 who has all the traits is Gen Z, and someone born in 2005 without them isn't. (11) So either generations are unreal clusters (step 4) or they're real psychological kinds that cut across birth years (step 9-10). (12) Both options destroy the idea of generations as cohort-based groups. Where does it go wrong? I'd like to keep (2) and (7) if possible, but I don't know what rejecting the other ones costs me.
