I've been trying to sort out my own intuitions about philosophical zombies and the conceivability argument, and I think my verdicts are kind of all over the place. Here are the cases where I have a gut reaction, and I want to know if there's a principle hiding behind them or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Case one: the classic zombie - a being physically identical to me atom for atom, behaving exactly the same, but with absolutely no inner exeperience. I can conceive this pretty vividly, and it feels like a real metaphysical possibility, not just wordplay. If that's genuinely possible then physicalism seems false.

Case two: a silicon brain that copies my causal architecture exactly, same information flow, different material. I think this thing would definitely be conscious. Substrate doesn't matter, it's the organization that counts.

Case three: gradual replacement, where my neurons get swapped out one by one for silicon equivalents over years. I don't think there's a magic point where consciousness suddenly flickers out - I'd stay conscious throughout.

Case four: a being that behaves exactly like me - same speech, same reports about seeing red, same emotional responses - but whose internal physical organization is totally different, not copying my causal structure at all, just producing the right outputs through some weird mechanism. That one, I think, might actually be a zombie. So I seem to think that functional organization matters, but mere behavioural equivalence without the right internal architecture might not be enough.

Case five: an inverted spectrum twin, physically identical to me but experiencing green where I experience red. I can conceive that too, but it feels like a different kind of problem - maybe it doesn't threaten physicalism the same way?

Case six: just one neuron in my brain is replaced by a perfect functional duplicate that lacks any qualitative aspect. I think I'd still be fully conscious; one missing piece is too small.

So I have these six verdicts and they feel like they pull in different directions. Is there a coherent account of conceivability or consciousness-dependence that saves all of them? Or do I have to drop one? In particular, I can't quite see how case one and case two fit together - if an atom-for-atom duplicate can lack consciousness, why can't a silicon copy? And what's the real difference between case two and case four that makes one conscious and the other possibly not?

Also, what should I say about a brain emulation running on a computer that has the exact same causal relationships but runs a thousand times slower? Would my principle predict that's conscious, or does temporal dynamics matter? And please don't just list books - I want to know what principle I'm actually running, which of my gut reactions it can't accommodate, and what it predicts for the slow-emulation case.
