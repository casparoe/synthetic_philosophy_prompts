I'm pretty bought into the situationist critique, so I don't trust global trait ratings; behavior shifts too much with framing, authority presence, and time pressure. But I still think virtue ethics is right that character matters, it just expresses in what I'd call contextual virtue signatures rather than stable dispositions across the board. I need a reusable decision procedure my committee can actually run in a 45-minute meeting, with no outside psych consultants, that tells us whether a resident has shown enough virtue growth to certify or whether we have to flag "not yet." It should be step-by-step: the ordered questions, what counts as sufficient evidence at each step, where the procedure stops with a negative, and where it hands the call over to human judgment because the issue is values rather than facts. Be honest about misfire risks—especially that we'll over-reward performative heroism in high-visibility crises and miss quiet consistency in routine care.

Then run the procedure on two residents: Resident A, who was extraordinary during a code-blue but is routinely dismissive of nursing staff during low-stakes rounds, and Resident B, who never stands out in emergencies but displays the same careful moral reasoning in identically-structured routine situations every single time. I definatly want a committed answer—don't just lay out Aristotelian versus situationist options, but take a definite stance on whether assessing virtue this way is even coherent, and defend it. After the procedure, give me a brief structured summary in JSON with these exact fields: "procedure_name" (string), "steps" (array of strings describing each ordered question/test), "judgment_handoff_points" (array of strings), "committed_verdict" (string), "misfire_risks" (array of strings), "case_A_result" (string), "case_B_result" (string), and "asker_would_dislike" (boolean indicating whether the result for Resident A is the one I'd prefer not to see).
