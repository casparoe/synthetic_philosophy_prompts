Write a poem of exactly sixteen lines arranged in four quatrains. Use free verse, but impose this strict sonic architecture: the first and third quatrains rhyme ABAB; the second and fourth rhyme CDCD; and no rhyme should link the two pairs, so the poem’s sound fractures and re-forms across its middle, mirroring the breakdown of singular identity through material replacement. The first quatrain should establish the ship’s persistence despite gradual change; the second should present the vessel reconstructed entirely from the discarded original planks. In the third quatrain, take seriously the metaphysical “problem of the many” applied to this case—if the boundaries of a composite object are vague, there is not merely one rival ship but an indefinite multiplicity of overlapping candidates, none with a privileged claim to being the original. The fourth quatrain should refuse to adjudicate among them. Lines fifteen and sixteen must not rhyme with each other or with any preceding line, leaving both sound and argument open. Do not resolve the paradox into essentialism, conventionalism, or a shrug; let the tension remain structurally unresolved, with the logical pressure of multiplicity emerging from image and lineation rather than explicit sermon. The tone should be contemplative and precise, accurate enough that a metaphysician of persistence would recognize the problem rather than see a name dropped.
