I run a postgraduate seminar on rational belief and decision (12 students, one 90-minute session). Design a runnable in-class simulation that makes participants feel the pressure of a Dutch book argument extended beyond credences to normative commitments: specifically, holding simultaneous judgments about what one all-things-considered ought to do in a triage scenario, what one morally ought to do, and what one should advise. Arrange roles so that pairs act as advisory committees and must assign internal point-stakes across the three registers without seeing other pairs’ allocations; then introduce a hidden bookie mechanism that combines commitments across registers to force a guaranteed net loss in advisory points unless one register is revised. They should construct the sequence themselves and only recognise the incoherence at the end. Provide rules, materials, the point-based payoff scheme, a phase-by-phase timeline, a debrief mapping the forced revision onto the formal coherence condition, and an honest note on which feature of the standard probabilistic argument—such as the betting interpretation of belief or a unified utility scale—the simulation cannot reproduce and must be supplied in discussion. Conclude with a one-sentence summary as the final line.
