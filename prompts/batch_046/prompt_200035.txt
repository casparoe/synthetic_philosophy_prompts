I am a novelist preparing a philosophical interview series for an audience that expects conceptual rigor without academic ceremony. My next guest is an unnamed scholar defending a reconstructed Kierkegaardian position: that the structural anonymity of contemporary digital platforms reproduces the leveling dynamics of the nineteenth-century press, yet with an accelerated feedback loop that threatens not only individual responsibility but the very category of the single individual as an ethical subject. I seek a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that avoid generic biography and instead press the actual architecture of this thesis. Please include one question that forces the defender to address the tension between Kierkegaard’s apparent disdain for the crowd and any democratic potential within anonymous discourse; one that asks how the category of the moment—understood as the point of decision in which the eternal intersects with temporal existence—might operate as a counter-force to algorithmic temporality; and one that examines whether indirect communication, conceived as a mode of transmission that resists immediate assimilation, can survive in an environment where every utterance is instantly quantified by engagement metrics. For each of these three, provide a follow-up calibrated to the reply she is most likely to offer, especially if she distinguishes the public as an abstraction from concrete communal existence or appeals to the distinction between spectator and actor. The remaining questions should probe adjacent structural weaknesses: for instance, whether the diagnosis applies with equal force to decentralized networks and to centralized broadcast media, and what phenomenological or experiential evidence could distinguish a genuinely leveling environment from one that merely presents itself as anonymous. Throughout, the questions should assume familiarity with Kierkegaard’s analysis of the press and the public without requesting citations, bibliographies, or historical documentation.
