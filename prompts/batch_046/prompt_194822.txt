I'm working through a position paper on non-aggregative moral theories and want a consistency audit before I present the package. Here is where I stand:

(1) I accept a broadly Scanlonian contractualism: an act is wrong just in case it is disallowed by a principle that no affected party could reasonably reject as a basis for informed, unforced general agreement.

(2) I treat the individualist restriction as non-negotiable: when assessing whether a principle is rejectable, we compare the burden it places on each affected individual separately against what it offers that same individual, and we do not sum burdens or benefits across persons.

(3) In standard rescue cases—saving one life versus saving five—I hold that we ought to save the five, and I want that verdict delivered by contractualist reasoning rather than by smuggling in aggregation.

(4) To secure (3) without violating (2), I appeal to independent rejectability: each of the five can reject a principle that permits leaving them to die when the only alternative is sacrificing one other person, and the one person, facing a burden comparable in kind to each of the five, cannot reject being sacrificed given that the alternative is five such comparable burdens falling on others.

(5) I also think contractualism should handle diffuse harms—collective-action problems in which each agent's contribution is small but the aggregate effect is catastrophic—by allowing that a principle permitting such contributions can be rejected because it exposes vulnerable populations to severe risk, even though no single victim bears a burden comparable to the cost borne by each contributor.

(6) I reject ad hoc magnitude thresholds that would permit aggregation only once harm crosses a specified level; I regard such thresholds as theoretically unmotivated.

(7) I want to add a structural-vulnerability clause: a principle is rejectable when it systematically channels risk onto a specific group, regardless of whether the absolute burden on each member of that group exceeds the burden on those outside it.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly revision to restore consistency? Rank the tensions by severity. For each tension, specify which commitment must give, what the revised position looks like, and what it costs—particularly with respect to (2) and (5). Be explicit about whether (7) can be integrated without abandoning the individualist restriction, or whether it collapses into a disguised form of cross-personal aggregation. Finally, indicate whether (4) genuinely resolves the apparent conflict between (2) and (3), or whether it covertly relies on the very aggregation it denies.
