For a workshop of like 14 people with 75 minutes, design a structured role-play that makes Wittgenstein's idea that religious belief is a form of life feel concrete, not just a slogan. Split them into two groups: the 'practicioners' who treat prayer and ritual as practical moves—like chess rules for living well—with internal norms that dont translate into evidence claims, and the 'translators' who keep asking 'but is it actually true?' Give them a crisis scenario where resources are scarce and one side appeals to a ritual obligation. The interaction rules should force the translators to hit a wall where their questions become category errors before anyone explains it. Then debrief: map the breakdown onto Wittgenstein's separation of religious language-games from scientific ones, be honest that the activity can't reproduce the private-language dimension of inner religious conviction, and flag the predictable failure mode (translators usually dominate by talking louder). No bibliographies or reading lists—just the rules, timeline, and the honest limitation.
