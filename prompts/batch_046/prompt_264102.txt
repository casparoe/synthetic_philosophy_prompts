hey so i've been reading about carnap's planning/politics stuff - the idea that science/planning should stay value-free and just compute the best means for whatever ends people pick. i tried to map it out like a logic flow and now it feels like it proves way too much.

(1) in carnap's framework every rational planning decision is conditional: IF we want outcome O, THEN do action A.
(2) the "IF" part is a value or preference that logic and empirical science can't justify.
(3) so if two planners agree on every fact but want different outcomes, neither is irrational - they just have different IF clauses.
(4) now imagine someone argues that centralized resource allocation is rationaly superior to market allocation for achieving food security.
(5) for that claim to actually persuade anyone with different values, it needs a criterion of "better" that works regardless of which ends you plug in.
(6) but carnap says every evaluation of "better planning" depends on the ends you fed into the framework.
(7) so the arguer in (4) is either smuggling in a hidden value judgement (that food security trumps other goods) or just saying "if you care about food, centralize" - which is empty as politics.
(8) by the same token, every political planning recommendation either violates value-neutrality or fails to move anyone who doesn't already share the goal.
(9) so carnap's view makes political planning either impossible or secretly normative.

i feel like the slip is somewhere between (4) and (6), maybe demanding that a planning argument be value-independent in some impossible way. but i really want to keep (1)-(3) and also (8) since that feels like the punchline. which step should i ditch, why did it look okay, and what's the cost? btw i might be butchering "rationaly" - meant rationally.
