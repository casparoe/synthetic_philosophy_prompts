Right, I'm putting together prompts that avoid the usual pretraining regurgitation, and I want one on tranquilism—specifically the view that only the absence of craving and suffering is intrinsically valuable, while autonomy, identity, social bonds and so on are only instrumentally good if they actually reduce craving or suffering. Can you write a reusable decision procedure for a clinical ethics board at a psychiatric long-term care unit? They get about four proposals a week for behavioural-modification protocols and need to sign off within fifteen minutes, with no philosophy PhD in the room.

The framework is tranquilist: absence of craving and suffering is the only intrinsic good.

Design an ordered checklist or flowchart in words they can apply each time. It needs to cover:
- Interventions that kill present suffering but create dependency likely to generate new suffering later.
- Interventions that suppress craving but also wipe out the patient's capacity for future-oriented attachment, which might have lowered suffering over the long run.
- Cases where a patient refuses despite high suffering, the refusal itself causes distress, and family members are pushing for the intervention anyway.

Give three worked applications, including one where the procedure gives an answer the board really won't want. Flag every point where judgement can't be eliminated—don't dress up subjective calls as mechanical steps. Be honest about what the procedure simply can't settle.

There's also a hypothetical therapy on the table called desire-calibration. It doesn't suppress cravings but restructures them so that only present-moment sensory satisfaction remains, eliminating all future-oriented longing. The procedure needs to say whether that counts as absence of craving or merely transformation, and the board need a practical way to apply that distinction.

Ground the whole thing in what philosophy actually knows about value and suffering, but no bibliographies, no reading lists, no citations. Don't quote anyone verbatim—if you havn't fetched the source, just paraphrase. Write the answer in British English, please. Oh, and include clear pass/fail conditions, not vague hand-waving.
