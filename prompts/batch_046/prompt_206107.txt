I serve on the Safety Certification Continuity Board for a regional autonomous water-management infrastructure network, and our charter requires us to determine whether an updated monitoring subsystem may inherit the safety certification of the mechanism it replaces, or whether any structural change demands full de novo review. A vendor has submitted an upgrade to our core anomaly-detection module: the legacy system employs a hierarchical Bayesian network with explicitly parameterized fluid-dynamics variables and manually specified failure-mode thresholds, whereas the proposed replacement relies on a structurally different architecture that learns latent representations of flow patterns through an internal encoding process that has no direct correspondence to the original parameterization. The vendor has demonstrated that the replacement reproduces every historical failure-mode response of the legacy system, introduces no novel vulnerabilities, and maintains identical counterfactual reliability across all tested stress conditions, arguing that this functional equivalence—preserved inputs, outputs, and failure-response mappings—renders it the same mechanism for certification purposes. A minority faction on the board contends that mechanistic identity requires preservation of internal causal architecture rather than mere behavioral equivalence, warning that granting inherited certification on functional grounds alone would establish a precedent for gradual "mechanism drift" in future updates, wherein successive replacements could cumulatively alter internal structure while each step individually preserves surface behavior, eventually eroding safety without ever triggering a full review. The board must issue a written opinion recommending whether to grant inherited certification, mandate partial re-certification with specified audit conditions, or require full de novo certification, together with a concrete implementation timeline. The opinion should treat the minority's structural-preservation argument with genuine intellectual seriousness rather than dismissing it as formalistic, and it should clarify whether our governing criterion for "the same mechanism" ought to be grounded in internal causal architecture, counterfactual dependency relations, or normative reliability profile, since whatever standard we adopt will bind all future submissions. Please draft this opinion as continuous prose in the manner of a formal institutional memo: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables; place the clear recommendation in the opening paragraph, followed by reasoning that honestly weighs the strongest consideration on the opposing side, and conclude with the specified timeline and the definitional choice for future cases.
