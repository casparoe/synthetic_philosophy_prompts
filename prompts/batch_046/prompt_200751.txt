Set out the full list of desiderata that an adequate philosophical account of hinge propositions — of the kind discussed in Wittgenstein’s work on certainty — must meet. For each requirement, provide the motivating case or conceptual consideration that makes it necessary, and indicate whether it is non-negotiable or merely reflects the preference of a particular interpretive camp (for example, the grammatical reading versus the epistemic reading). The account must explain: (i) why hinges such as “the Earth has existed for many years” or “I have two hands” are not ordinary empirical beliefs, yet are not arbitrary either; (ii) how they can remain fixed while surrounding beliefs change, without being immune to revision in principle; (iii) the distinction between local hinges governing particular language-games and global hinges underpinning an entire form of life; (iv) whether hinges can be genuinely propositional, or whether treating them as rules of grammar collapses important distinctions; and (v) how to accommodate the possibility of deep hinge disagreement — including cases where one community treats as certain what another treats as open to doubt — without reducing all disagreement to empirical error. Additionally, address whether the theory must account for “synthetic hinges”: framework commitments that are not biologically or culturally inherited but institutionally or technologically engineered (for instance, in the design of artificial agents or bureaucratic systems), and whether this extension forces a revision of the appeal to “forms of life”. Identify any subset of these desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable — for example, whether preserving hinges as rationally revisable is compatible with denying them evidential justification — and state which single requirement does the most work in excluding popular existing accounts. Present the answer in British English, without bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
