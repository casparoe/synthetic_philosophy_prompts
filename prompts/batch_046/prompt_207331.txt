Write a short dialogue between Mira and Jonas, two friends without formal philosophical training who have been arguing on their own about whether you could literally be the same person in a world where you had different parents. Mira defends a counterpart view: in that world there is only someone very much like you, not literally you, because being you requires your actual origin and continuous physical history. Jonas defends transworld identity: the very same you could have existed with different parents, provided some deeper psychological and functional continuity is maintained. Have them work through a specific twist they invented together: imagine a world where every neuron in your brain is replaced one by one with an artificial equivalent that preserves all memories and personality, while your body is grown in a vat with none of your original DNA. Mira should argue that this splits the counterpart relation—psychological continuity pulls toward Jonas's view, but physical origin pulls back to hers—so there is no single fact about whether it is you. Jonas should reply that if artificial replacement preserves everything that matters for being you, then origin was never essential, and the split only shows that counterpart similarity is too vague to do real work. They must respond to each other's actual points rather than talk past one another, with neither reduced to a strawman. Keep the exchange focused on whether there is a genuine disagreement or merely an ambiguity between tracking origins versus tracking continuity, and avoid invoking named philosophers, texts, or citation requests.
