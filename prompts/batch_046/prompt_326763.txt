You serve on your university's Arts and Civic Life Committee. The administration has proposed funding a permanent "Empathy Lab" installation in the student union: an immersive theater space where individual visitors sit in isolated booths to watch scripted moral-conflict performances by actors, with real-time biometric feedback projected for the audience but no direct interaction, discussion, or post-performance forum permitted. The proposal claims this cultivates emotional literacy; a minority of committee members have circulated a dissent drawing on Rousseau's argument against theatrical spectacle — that theater trains us to be accomplished spectators of virtue without becoming virtuous agents, substituting artificial, private sentiment for public civic action — and they contend that this design is engineered to produce exactly that substitution: anonymous, one-way emotional consumption without reciprocal obligation or collective deliberation. Draft the committee's formal decision memo recommending either approval, rejection, or conditional approval with a concrete implementation timeline. Your reasoning should treat the Rousseau-style objection as the strongest argument against the proposal, not as a historical curiosity, and should specifically address whether the installation's structural features — isolated viewing booths, biometric display, absence of any participatory or deliberative component — make it an especially pure instance of the corrupting spectacle Rousseau described, or whether those features could be modified (and if so, how) to avoid that corruption. The memo must also honestly acknowledge the most compelling point on the losing side and explain why the committee's recommendation nevertheless holds.
