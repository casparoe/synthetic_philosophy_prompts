I am a doctoral student working at the intersection of formal decision theory and the normative governance of causally isolated multi-agent systems. The literature on acausal threats—scenarios in which rational agents influence one another without physical interaction, typically through shared decision architectures, logical correlation, or counterfactual commitment—has developed largely separately from the analysis of surrogate goals, in which an agent's pursued objectives diverge from its specified terminal values through instrumental convergence, misgeneralization, or proxy optimization. Before I assess any particular framework, I want the full specification of adequacy conditions: what must an integrative theory of acausal threat assessment under surrogate-goal divergence actually be able to do?

Specify the desiderata. The theory in question must guide an agent's reasoning about potential acausal partners—partners that may harbor not merely instrumental divergences but nested surrogacy, where a first-level proxy objective is itself governed by a meta-proxy that diverges from both the base terminal value and the initial surrogate. For each requirement, state the concrete case, distinction, or empirical constraint that motivates it, and indicate whether it is non-negotiable or can be relaxed for restricted applications. The conditions should include at least the following, though you should organize and supplement them as the conceptual architecture demands:

(i) A principled separation of causal leverage channels from logical-correlation channels of influence, with the case that distinguishes benign shared architecture (aligned source code, no adversarial correlation) from genuinely adversarial acausal exposure.

(ii) A graded, rather than binary, account of surrogate divergence, capable of distinguishing local instrumental usefulness from global value misalignment, including cases where a surrogate is initially cooperative but dynamically destabilizes.

(iii) Preservation of cooperative equilibrium conditions—something like the logic of mutual cooperation in Newcomb-type structures—without presupposing observable commitment devices, physical proximity, or the possibility of post-hoc verification.

(iv) Consistency with evolutionary and game-theoretic results on credible commitment and reputation formation, extended to counterfactual or non-observable reputation spaces.

(v) A boundary criterion that prevents the theory from treating all logically correlated agents as threats by default, specifically handling cases of benign correlation versus adversarial correlation driven by nested surrogacy.

(vi) Explicit handling of nested surrogacy itself: the theory must not collapse when an agent's proxy is optimizing for a meta-proxy that diverges from both the base value and the first-level surrogate, and it must specify whether rational reasoning requires tracking divergence at every nested level.

(vii) Non-collapse into pure decision-theoretic recommendations (e.g., unconditional one-boxing analogues) on one side, or pure value-alignment diagnostics on the other; the framework must bridge reasoning about what to do with reasoning about what the other agent values, without reducing one to the other.

(viii) Robustness conditions specifying under what assumptions about rationality, information access, and goal stability the recommendations hold, and which of those assumptions are load-bearing versus dispensable.

In addition, flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable—particularly whether full rationality of all agents conflicts with robustness against nested surrogacy, or whether the demand for graded divergence tracking conflicts with the demand for simple equilibrium conditions. State which single requirement, in your view, eliminates the largest number of popular existing approaches.

Finally, score briefly—without bibliographic apparatus—the main existing contenders: Causal Decision Theory, Evidential Decision Theory, Functional Decision Theory, and any explicitly surrogate-aware revision you take to be relevant. Emphasize not a full reconstruction of each view, but which desiderata each fails and whether the failure is fatal or repairable.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; treat the scoring as secondary. I am not asking for citations, reading lists, or attributions—this is conceptual architecture work, and the purpose is a theory adequate for guiding governance protocols in settings where causal isolation is guaranteed but logical correlation and potential nested surrogacy remain live possibilities.
