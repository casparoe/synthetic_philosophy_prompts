In my philosophy elective, we have been discussing the digger wasp Sphex ichneumoneus and the concept of sphexishness—the apparently mechanical repetition of behavior even when circumstances render it pointless. Douglas Hofstadter cited this as an illustration of genetic determinism, and the standard view in class seems to be that such rigidity shows what free will is not: if an agent cannot adapt its routine, it lacks the flexibility that freedom requires. I want to defend an inversion of that standard picture. I hold that sphexishness actually reveals a kind of minimal autonomy—what I call structural integrity—because the wasp's behavior is governed by an internally sustained normative pattern rather than by moment-to-moment environmental feedback. By contrast, I think much human flexibility is not freedom but rapid calibration to social reward structures, which is merely a more complex deterministic loop. So my position is that the real distinction is not between rigid and flexible behavior, but between actions that express an internally coherent normative structure (however simple) and actions that are purely reactive to external feedback loops, regardless of how variable those reactions appear.

What would have to be true for this inversion to fail? I am looking for an honest assessment of its exposure. Are there empirical studies of wasp cognition showing that Sphex can learn and modify its burrow-checking routine in ways that still wouldn't count as free, which would undermine my claim that rigidity signals structural integrity rather than mere mechanism? Could philosophical arguments show that my distinction between internally sustained norms and reactive feedback either collapses into standard determinism or is incoherent? If comparative cognition demonstrated that human decision-making differs not merely in complexity but in kind from any animal behavior—perhaps involving a genuinely open-ended deliberation that no feedback model could capture—would that defeat my view? And does my position risk insulating itself by defining structural integrity so broadly that even clearly unfree mechanisms qualify? I want to know which objections would be decisive, which merely awkward, whether defenders of the flexibility-requires-freedom view would concede anything to this inversion, and whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability in a way that is problematic for a claim of this kind.
