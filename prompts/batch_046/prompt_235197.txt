Please argue for the position that Aristotle’s sea-battle problem is best resolved by what we might call “branch-internal determinacy”—the view that future-contingent propositions possess a definite truth-value relative to each individual branching future, even though no branch is metaphysically privileged from the present perspective. In particular, defend the claim that this preserves both the law of excluded middle and the genuine openness of the future, without collapsing contingency into mere epistemic uncertainty. Address the strongest objection: that assigning truth-values within every branch simply relocates fatalism into the branching structure rather than avoiding it. Present your argument in one paragraph of no more than 180 words, with your bottom-line thesis stated in the first sentence.
