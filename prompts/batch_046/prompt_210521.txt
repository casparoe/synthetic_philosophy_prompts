ok so my dad just moved into assisted living and i had to sign like fifteen consent forms for meds and restraints and whatever. it got me thinking about this argument i sketched out and now i'm stuck.

(1) if consent is genuinely valid then the consenter hasn't been deceived about any fact that, if they knew it, might make them choose differently.
(2) there's always some hidden counterfactual — like the home knows dad's specific metabolism makes this sedative 40% riskier for him than the form's population stats suggest, but they don't have to say that because it's not in the standard disclosure.
(3) withholding that kind of specific, decision-relevant info is deception by omission, not just incomplete paperwork.
(4) so every real-world consent involves deception about something material.
(5) therefore no consent given in practice is actually valid.
(6) but we treat these signatures as morally and legally binding all the time.
(7) so (1) is false and consent is just whatever the institution calls it?

i feel like the slip is between (2) and (3) — maybe omission only counts as deception if it's intentional and they actually know the specific risk? but then (4) still looks bad because institutions always know more than they share. or maybe (1) is too strong and should be "no deception about material facts" not "any fact that might change your mind". i really want to keep (1) if i can, but if i do i'm saying my dad's consent was fake. which step do i actually reject, why did it look fine, and what's the cost? definatly need to know before i talk to the social worker.
