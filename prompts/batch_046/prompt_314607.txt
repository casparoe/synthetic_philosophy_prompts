Write a commencement address of approximately 900 words for the graduating class of a joint undergraduate program in philosophy and cognitive neuroscience. The audience is divided evenly between family members with no philosophical background and faculty specialists who will notice imprecision regarding Zhuangzi or dream skepticism. You are the invited speaker, a philosopher of mind whose recent work concerns the boundary between self-modeling and world-modeling, though you must not cite specific papers, authors, or named technical frameworks.

Use Zhuangzi's butterfly dream and the form of dream skepticism it raises as the structural spine of the speech, not as a decorative metaphor. The dream must actively constrain what you can say: it should prevent the usual commencement tropes—telling graduates to "wake up to who they really are," to "leave behind illusion and face reality," or to treat themselves as stable observers of an independent world—because each of those assumes a firm self/world boundary that the dream destabilizes. At the same time, you must not collapse into pure skepticism or nihilism; the address still needs to offer a coherent, encouraging orientation for graduates entering professional and personal life.

To prevent a generic recitation, adopt the following niche framing: treat Zhuangzi's problem not as a private epistemic puzzle about how one knows one is not currently dreaming, but as a structural problem about the mutual dependence of the modeler and the modeled. The graduate's task is not to resolve the ambiguity once and for all, but to build commitments, relationships, and professional practices without pretending to occupy a view from nowhere. Make this concrete by centering one specific, plausible scenario—such as a graduate entering clinical neuropsychology or a field concerned with aligning predictive systems—showing how the instability of the self/world boundary functions not as an obstacle to overcome but as a working condition of responsible practice.

The address should remain fully accessible to the non-specialist family members, precise enough to satisfy the philosophers in attendance, and shaped throughout by the idea that the boundary between dreamer and dreamed is unstable. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations of any kind.
