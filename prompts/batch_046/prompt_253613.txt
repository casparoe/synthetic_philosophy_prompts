Write a deadpan parody of an international standards-certification manual—formatted as numbered clauses, subsections, and technical appendices, approximately 700 words—for accrediting "True Judges of Taste" under the framework of Hume's essay on aesthetic judgment. The manual should treat Hume's conditions—delicacy of sentiment, practice in comparative evaluation, freedom from prejudice, and the authority of a joint verdict among qualified critics—as literal, auditable bureaucratic requirements. Applicants should be required to pass standardized psychometric screenings for sensory discrimination, submit documented travel logs demonstrating direct comparison with an approved canonical corpus, and complete prejudice-neutralization protocols verified by independent auditors. Include Appendix A, listing the approved canonical comparison set and its geographic accessibility requirements; Appendix B, specifying the apprenticeship hours and peer-review thresholds needed to join the pool of accredited judges; and a sidebar outlining the appeals process for candidates who fail the delicacy examination. The voice should remain that of an anonymous regulatory body: no winking, no explicit statement of objections. Instead, let the satire emerge from the procedural details—who authors the examination rubric, which educational and economic prerequisites implicitly filter the applicant pool, how circular the selection of judges becomes when accreditation requires prior accreditation, and whether a statistically aggregated verdict can claim objectivity once the pool of voters is defined by the very taste it purports to validate. The piece should be fully legible to a reader who has never encountered Hume, but should reward a specialist with accurate engagement with the essay's central tensions: whether beauty resides in the object, in the refined sentiment of the observer, or in a socially constructed consensus that risks collapsing into elite convention. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not ask for external verification of sources. Keep all references to Hume's concepts in paraphrase, without quotation marks drawn from the original text.
