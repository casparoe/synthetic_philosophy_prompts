For an advanced seminar on knowledge and practical reason I am drafting a position paper and want a consistency audit before I circulate it. The view I am defending tries to steer between standard contextualism and pragmatic encroachment, but I am unsure whether the pieces actually cohere. Here is my profile: (1) I reject both DeRose-style contextualism and Stanley-style pragmatic encroachment in favor of what I call discourse-sensitive invariantism—within any conversation "knows" expresses one invariant relation, but which relation it expresses is fixed collectively by the practical stakes of the participants. (2) I claim this avoids the skeptical paradox that plagues contextualism, because the operative standard is anchored to real practical needs and is not freely raised by the mere mention of skeptical hypotheses. (3) I explain the bank cases by saying that in the high-stakes version the conversation expresses a stricter relation, so Keith genuinely fails to stand in it despite unchanged evidence, while in the low-stakes version he stands in the expressed relation. (4) I hold that knowledge requires modal safety relative to the expressed standard: S knows p only if S would not easily have formed a false belief in p under conditions governed by that standard. (5) I deny that stakes affect a subject's evidence, reliability, or belief-forming processes; they influence only the semantic content of knowledge attributions. (6) I accept fallibilism: logical possibilities of error do not defeat knowledge, though possibilities that are nearby relative to the operative standard do. (7) I maintain that in philosophical discussions where skeptical scenarios are raised, the practical need to avoid catastrophic deception is genuinely high, so the operative standard should be strict, and it is appropriate—not paradoxical—that we genuinely lack ordinary knowledge in such contexts.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe is each real tension? For each genuine conflict, identify the cheapest revision and its cost—especially whether abandoning (1) or (5) is unavoidable. Then rank the tensions by severity and tell me which single change would resolve the most problems without collapsing the view into either standard contextualism or pragmatic encroachment. Do not assume I am prepared to drop any particular commitment; I want the audit ordered by the damage each tension does to the package as a whole.
