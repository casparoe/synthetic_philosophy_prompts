I'm working out a position for a philosophy seminar and need a consistency check before presenting. Here is what I want to hold together: (1) I endorse Bernard Williams's integrity objection, specifically the charge that an agent who saves her partner only after confirming that this maximizes overall welfare has had one thought too many; but I understand this defect as structural, belonging to any moral theory that requires explicit normative verification between attachment and action, not only to utilitarianism. (2) I take ground projects to be partly constitutive of personal identity, so that abandoning them for the sake of moral duty produces a rational moral remainder—appropriate grief and loss even when the abandonment was the correct choice. (3) In a case where I can save my child or save five strangers, I think saving my child is wrong, and the correct explanation appeals to the stronger moral weight of the strangers' lives, not to any permission or exemption granted by my parental attachment. (4) I maintain that ground projects generate authoritative but strictly non-moral reasons, and that moral reasons are always overriding in cases of conflict. (5) I also think that if a Kantian demands universalizability testing before acting on love, that interposes the same defective justification, so the problem is not confined to consequentialist calculation.

Do these commitments hang together? In particular, does my verdict in (3) commit me to exactly the kind of weighing and overriding that (1) criticizes, and does (2)'s idea of a rational moral remainder conflict with (4)'s claim that moral reasons always win? For each pair in genuine tension, say how severe the conflict is and what the cheapest revision would cost; also flag any tension that only looks real and dissolves on inspection. Rank the problems by severity. Keep your audit to a single paragraph of no more than 300 words.
