At a rationalist meetup, two people got into an argument about Hilbert's hotel and whether it proves actual infinities are coherent. Maya argued that with ℵ₀ rooms fully occupied, shifting every occupant from room n to n+1 deterministically frees room 1, and repeating this accommodates any countable influx; this, she said, shows infinite sets have stable, well-defined properties that only make sense if actual infinity exists. Leo countered that the shift is never completed—there is no "last" guest to move—so the procedure describes only a potential process, not a finished state, and therefore cannot justify treating an infinite collection as a completed totality. He added that Cantor's result that there are different cardinalities depends on already accepting completed infinite sets, which finitists reject by principle rather than by confusion. Maya replied that Leo confuses physical execution with mathematical existence: the function f(n)=n+1 is defined for every natural number, and coherence requires only that, not that anyone literally walks. Leo then claimed that accommodating ℵ₀ new guests actually requires selecting images for infinitely many shifts, implicitly invoking countable choice, and without that principle the assignment collapses because there is no canonical rule for every new arrival. Maya said the hotel assigns rooms deterministically—guest k goes to room 2k—so no choice is needed. Please go through their exchange move by move: rate Maya's initial argument, Leo's incompletion objection, Leo's finitist reading of Cantor, Maya's existence/execution distinction, and Leo's choice-based objection, from clearly mistaken to clearly right. Then say who is closer to being right overall, and identify what both sides miss about the difference between potential and actual infinity in modern finitist positions.
