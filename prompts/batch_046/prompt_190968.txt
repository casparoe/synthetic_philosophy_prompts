As a retired physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of action, I find the classical problem of akrasia—acting against one's own better judgment—both familiar and unsettling. Behavioral economists explain it away through hyperbolic discounting, treating weakness of will as a temporal misalignment of preferences rather than a failure of rational authority. Write a short story about a retired experimental scientist who attempts to resolve this tension in her own life by constructing a personal protocol with irreversible commitment devices, designed to determine whether overcoming procrastination requires restructuring her preferences or reclaiming normative agency over them. The narrative should turn on a specific, unforeseen consequence of this protocol that reveals whether hyperbolic discounting truly exhausts the phenomenon of weakness, or whether something irreducibly normative remains.
