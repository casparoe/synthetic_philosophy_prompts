Please describe the most important implications of Popper's poverty-of-historicism thesis—that there can be no universal laws of historical development analogous to the laws of natural science—for the evaluation of modern long-range socio-technical forecasting systems that project civilizational trajectories across multi-century horizons. In particular, address whether Popper's distinction between conditional predictions and unconditional historical prophecies undermines the scientific authority of integrated assessment models that treat demographic, climatic, and economic variables as jointly law-governed, and explain what this implies for the rationality of policy commitments that treat such forecasts as deterministic trajectories rather than as conjectural scenarios open to empirical refutation.
