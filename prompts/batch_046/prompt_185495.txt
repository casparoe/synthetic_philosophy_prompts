Read Steven Spielberg's Minority Report through the lens of Benjamin Libet's experiments on the readiness potential and conscious will, with particular attention to the climax in which John Anderton, despite a precognitive vision of himself shooting Leo Crow, chooses to withhold the shot. Does the film's "precrime" system dramatize Libet's finding that neural initiation precedes conscious awareness of decision, and does Anderton's withholding correspond to Libet's proposed "veto" window? At the same time, examine whether the narrative depends on a libertarian moment of choice that is undermined by subsequent critiques arguing that the readiness potential reflects background neural noise rather than a specific decision precursor, and ask whether the film conflates statistical behavioral prediction with neural determinism in ways that misrepresent the neuroscience. I want a reading faithful in both directions: one that takes the plot's metaphysics of choice seriously without reducing the film to an illustration, that marks precisely where the metaphor of predictive policing breaks against the actual experimental logic, and that ends by asking what the film reveals about popular misunderstandings of free will that philosophical analysis of Libet's results alone tends to miss.
