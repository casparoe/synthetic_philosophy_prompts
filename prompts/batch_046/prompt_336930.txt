I'm playing with an idea for a character in my new novel—this obsessive archivist who thinks half the 20th century's miscommunication could have been avoided if everyone just adopted Otto Neurath's picture-language system, ISOTYPE, instead of fighting over words. The position I'm trying to take seriously is that a rigorously designed pictorial language can actually transmit quantitative social facts directly, without the interpretive slack or metaphysical baggage verbal language drags in. Not just that icons are handy for airport signage, but that they genuinely bypass the hermeneutic circle—if you standardize the visual grammar properly, you can assert things like "more coal production in region X" in a way that's culturally neutral, theoretically unloaded, and readable without translation. I'm drawn to the idea that pictures don't "mean" in the same slippery way sentences do, so Neurath's optimism might be more than a design fad; it might be a real epistemic claim about direct representation.

But I want to know where this actually breaks, not just where it's annoying. What would have to be true for Neurath's picture-language optimism to be seriously wrong? I'm looking for the substantial defeaters—whether interpretation of any symbol is always already theory-laden, whether stripping a fact into an icon necessarily deletes the contextual data that makes it meaningful, or whether the system secretly encodes ideological choices about scale, gender, or causality inside its visual grammar. Which objections are live rather than merely logical? Could an empirical result—say, robust cross-cultural studies showing consistent misreading of supposedly standardized icons—actually damage the view, or has the position been qualified into unfalsifiability? And if a committed defender of pictorial universalism had to concede one point, which concession should hurt the most?

Also, I keep typoing Neurath as Neurth in my notes, which feels weirdly appropriate. Anyway, I'm not asking for a bibliography or anything—just an honest map of how exposed this view really is, sorted from fatal to merely awkward, including what its defenders themselves should admit counts against them.
