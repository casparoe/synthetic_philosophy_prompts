The standard superrationality argument in the Prisoner's Dilemma holds that symmetric reasoners should cooperate: if I can see that cooperation is best, I should expect my reasoning-twin to reach the same conclusion. I want to extend this to a partial-information, multi-agent consensus variant. Here, N agents each receive a private signal about whether the group is composed of superrational agents or merely classical rational agents. The payoff rewards unanimous cooperation but severely punishes being the lone cooperator. Crucially, each superrational agent knows that others may have received different signals. So she must model not only "what would I do in her position?" but "what does she model about me, given that she knows my signal may differ?" This introduces a recursive epistemic layer. I argue that superrationality does not collapse into defection in this setting, but into a probabilistic mixture governed by the agents' common prior over signal reliability. Thus cooperation becomes contingent on shared confidence in reasoning similarity rather than on reasoning similarity itself.
