Write an essay assessing whether lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism remains coherent if one denies that pleasure and pain are inter-substitutable along a single cardinal scale. Consider the specific objection that suffering and enjoyment may be phenomenally incommensurable because of a "granularity asymmetry": pain apparently admits of far more fine-grained phenomenal distinctions than pleasure does, so there is no neutral unit in which to measure them against each other. If that asymmetry holds, is there any non-arbitrary way to fix the lexical threshold—the point at which suffering is said to outweigh any quantity of pleasure—or does the view collapse into an ordinal, pluralist axiology that abandons the distinctive negative-utilitarian commitment? Weigh the options carefully and defend a definite verdict, rather than ending in suspension of judgment.
