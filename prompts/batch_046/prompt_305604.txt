Write an essay on the normative status of Jeffrey conditionalization when evidence is structurally ambiguous and couples multiple hypotheses jointly rather than isolating a single proposition. Focus on a concrete scenario: an epidemiologist receives a noisy, continuous biomarker reading that simultaneously shifts her confidence in both a pathogen's virulence and the calibration of her assay, so that no discrete event is learned with certainty. Assess whether Jeffrey's rule is obligatory in this setting—specifically, whether it uniquely prevents diachronic Dutch books across the interdependent belief network, or whether the absence of a uniquely partitioned sample space renders the update underdetermined and therefore arbitrary. Contrast this position with strict Bayesian conditioning, which refuses to update without a definite event, and with a hybrid restriction that permits Jeffrey updates only when the affected partitions are probabilistically independent. Reach and defend a definite conclusion about whether Jeffrey conditionalization should be treated as a universal rationality constraint or as a locally useful heuristic with strictly limited scope.
