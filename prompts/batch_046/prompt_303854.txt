I'm working through a paper for a graduate seminar on the philosophy of pain, and I've found myself drawn to a position I haven't seen fully defended in the standard literature—though I assume fragments exist—namely that the badness of pain is not intrinsic to its phenomenal quality but derives entirely from its asymbolic structure: its resistance to being captured, shared, or integrated into symbolic, narrative, or communicative frameworks. On this view, what makes a burn or a migraine genuinely bad is not the raw feel itself but the way it isolates the sufferer in a private, pre-linguistic state that cannot be translated to others or woven into one's autobiography without severe distortion. Consequently, if an intense nociceptive experience could be fully symbolized—say, within a ritual practice that assigns it collective meaning, narrative continuity, and shared linguistic expression—its negative value should diminish or even become ambivalent, much as the physical strain of a communal ordeal differs in value from an accidental injury.

But I want to understand how exposed this claim really is. Treat it as a live position and map what would have to be true for it to be false. I'm especially interested in intuitive counter-cases—perhaps intense physical suffering that remains overwhelmingly aversive even within fully symbolized cultural practices, or symbolically integrated sensations that are still experienced as purely bad—rather than just the generic objection that pain feels bad regardless of context. I also want to know which adjacent results it risks: does it commit me to the broader claim that all suffering is essentially a failure of communication, and if so, does that collapse into an implausible account of grief, anxiety, or exhaustion? Or does it make distinctive predictions about conditions like congenital insensitivity to pain, or about individuals who develop elaborate private languages for their sensations? Please distinguish decisive defeaters from merely awkward ones. Which objections would a committed defender concede as genuinely damaging, and which could be absorbed by qualifying distinctions—say, between phenomenal asymbolia and social asymbolia, or between reducing and eliminating badness? Say whether the position strikes you as well insulated, and whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice—does it risk unfalsifiability?
