As a doctoral researcher working at the intersection of moral philosophy and collective action theory, I am refining a taxonomy of the inefficacy objection as it applies to climate-related consumption, dietary choice, and electoral participation. Rather than treating the objection as monolithic, I distinguish three loci of alleged futility: (A) the causal-null locus, where an individual's contribution is physically undetectable within the aggregate harm; (B) the institutional-legitimation locus, where individual participation structurally reinforces the harmful system regardless of marginal scale; and (C) the expressive-vacuum locus, where an act of abstention fails to generate the communicative or symbolic effects necessary to motivate broader collective change. Please rank these three loci—A, B, and C—according to how severely each undermines the normative case for individual abstention or conscientious participation, treating climate impact, meat consumption, and voting as parallel test cases. For each rank, provide a brief justification of one to two sentences, with particular attention to whether the locus retains its force under conditions of high coordination costs and diffuse responsibility structures.
