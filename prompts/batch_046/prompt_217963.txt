I count the Baby Boomers — roughly born between 1946 and 1964 — as a real cohort, because their birth years align with an actual postwar demographic surge and they grew up during a specific period of economic expansion and cultural change.

I count Millennials — roughly born between 1981 and 1996 — as largely an invented category. The date range feels arbitrary to me, and the people inside it do not share a single defining formative event; some experienced 9/11 as small children, others barely remember it, and the 2008 recession struck them at very different life stages.

I count Gen Z — roughly born between 1997 and 2012 — as real, but for a different reason than the Boomers. They are the first cohort for whom smartphones and algorithmic social media were present before puberty, so the technology shaped their social development in a way it did not for anyone older.

I count the Silent Generation — roughly born between 1928 and 1945 — as real for reasons parallel to the Boomers: they share the formative experience of the Great Depression and World War II during childhood and adolescence.

Finally, I think Generation Alpha — those born from about 2010 onward — is not yet a real cohort, because they have not reached an age where a shared historical event could have shaped their worldview; at this point they are simply young people who grew up with tablets.
