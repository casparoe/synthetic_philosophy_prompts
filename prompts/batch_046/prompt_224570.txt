For my analysis, please treat the Union and the Confederacy not symmetrically, but through Hegel’s technical criterion of whether each constituted a genuine “state” in his sense—an ethical universal recognized as sovereign—rather than a particularistic association or a coalition of private interests. Then turn to the post-war Reconstruction period and ask whether it supports Hegel’s optimism that war rejuvenates ethical life and prevents civil society from hardening into selfish particularism, or whether the rapid economic transformation and the failure to secure lasting racial justice expose a blind spot—namely, that a state-centered framework may be unable to account for internal social hierarchies that persist even after the state asserts its sovereignty.

Please structure your response so that you first map the key actors and decisions onto Hegel’s concepts, then identify at least two features of the Civil War and its aftermath that Hegel’s theory explains well and at least two that it misdescribes or fails to anticipate, and finally say whether this episode should be read as confirming, complicating, or undermining Hegel’s view that war has a necessary ethical role in the life of the state. I am especially interested in where the theory is silent rather than forced to speak, and in whether the asymmetry between the Union and the Confederacy—when judged by Hegel’s own standards—makes the framework more or less coherent as applied to this conflict.
