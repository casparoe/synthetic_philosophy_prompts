Charity is Davidson's interpretive constraint that we must treat speakers as rational and hold mostly true beliefs by our lights, otherwise assignment of meaning cant get started. He deploys this in his essay attacking conceptual schemes to argue that conceptual relativism is incoherent—if you need shared belief to interpret anyone, then a completely alien scheme organizing the same world-content differently is impossible. For blor, charity clearly demands we translate it as disjunction because that maximizes logical consistency with our own beliefs; the modal-like uses are just performance errors or metaphor. So charity corrects rather than distorts. This connects to Quine's thesis that translation is always underdetermined, except Davidson solves the underdetermination by using charity to lock in the single correct interpretation. Therefore if someone genuinely used blor modally, charity would make that scheme invisible to us—which proves there cant be hidden conceptual schemes.
