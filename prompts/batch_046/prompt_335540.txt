I'm giving the toast at my advisor's retirment dinner — she's a philosphy-of-death person who spent thirty years on the Epicurean line that death is nothing to us, but she always rejected the standard symmetry argument (pre-birth absence vs post-death absence) on what she called the "missing mirror" problem: pre-natal non-existence isn't mourned because the relations that make absence matter haven't formed yet, whereas post-death absence is fully real for the survivors even if it harms no dead subject. Half the room are philosophers who'll catch every reference; the other half are her grandkids and partner who aren't. Write a five-minute toast that uses her "missing mirror" idea to actually structure what I can and can't say — I have to comfort the family without implying she's "watching over us" or "at peace now" (she'd consider that false and patronizing), and I have to honor her view for the colleagues without turning the dinner into a seminar. Make the philosophical point do real work in the speech, not just decoration.
