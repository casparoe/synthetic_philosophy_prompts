A constitutional reform commission has issued consistent judgments on four institutional designs. Design Alpha establishes a unicameral legislature filled entirely by random lot for single, non-renewable six-year terms, with compensation fixed at the national median income and with a constitutional prohibition on any post-service governmental appointment, advisory title, or pension; the commission endorsed it. Design Beta creates an elected lower chamber with eight-year lifetime term limits and a mandatory four-year hiatus before holding any other public office, but permits immediate appointment to an independent regulatory agency upon leaving the chamber; the commission rejected it. Design Gamma fills a legislature entirely by lot for three-year terms, allowing citizens to serve up to three non-consecutive terms separated by at least twelve years, with no salary premiums or special status between terms; the commission rejected it. Design Delta institutes a bicameral body in which one chamber is elected for four-year terms (maximum two terms) and the other is filled by lot for single two-year terms, with an absolute bar preventing any individual from serving in both chambers and permanently barring all former members from government employment or advisory roles; the commission approved it with the explicit reservation that the elected chamber still risks generating a partial professional class.

The commission now reviews Design Epsilon: a unicameral legislature filled entirely by lot for single, non-renewable six-year terms, with median-income salaries and with former members permanently ineligible for legislative service; however, every alumni member is automatically appointed for life to a Legislative Memory Office—an unelected body with no binding legislative authority but with formal procedural standing to issue binding interpretations of prior legislative intent in procedural disputes, and with alumni receiving a distinct civic title and priority access to public deliberative forums. The pool for future sortition draws is restricted exclusively to citizens who have never served.

Assuming the commission wishes to remain coherent with its own record, what should its verdict be? Identify the competing principles that best explain its prior rulings—distinguishing, for instance, between a principle that targets continuous tenure and career ladders within the legislative body itself, and one that targets any structural mechanism that converts temporary service into a permanent, privileged institutional status—state what each principle predicts for Design Epsilon, indicate which prior design is the closest analog, and propose one further institutional variant that would definitively separate these two interpretations.
