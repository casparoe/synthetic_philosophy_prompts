Next month I defend a view—call it “asymmetric dynamic constitution”—to the material-ontology workshop. My thesis is that in cases of gradual, distributed replacement (for example, a modular sculpture whose physical components are continuously swapped across locations according to an open-source design protocol), the statue and its constituent matter do not stand as two enduring objects, nor as one object under two descriptions, but in an asymmetric relation of “modal inheritance”: the artistic-form regime retroactively fixes which material parts were essential, while the material-continuity regime tracks only spatiotemporal overlap. I claim this avoids both the “too many objects” problem and the “lost statue” intuition, but only if we reject mereological essentialism for artifacts and accept that constitution can be non-local and protocol-dependent.

The audience is six metaphysicians: two Wiggins-style essentialists who hold that an artifact’s origin is essential and that constitution is identity under a different mode of presentation; two four-dimensionalists who treat the statue and clay as four-dimensional worms with different temporal parts, and who are skeptical of any “retroactive” determination; one specialist in temporary intrinsics who will press whether my “modal inheritance” collapses into her framework; and one generalist who regards the constitution debate as a pseudo-problem generated by linguistic confusion, who will try to dissolve my case rather than answer it.

Give me the objections in roughly the order they will come, phrased as each faction would actually phrase them, with for each: what I should concede, what I should hold, a two-minute reply, and an explicit note on where that reply is weakest. Order them by likely damage. Identify the weakest point in my own position that I should prepare to defend rather than hope they miss. Include the objection I am hoping nobody asks—likely the temporary-intrinsics specialist’s challenge that protocol-dependence makes artistic form relational rather than intrinsic, thereby undermining the asymmetry I claim. In your prep sheet, indicate for each reply how confident you are in it, and at the end explicitly single out the one point about which you are least sure overall.
