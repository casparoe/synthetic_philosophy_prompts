As a novelist constructing the philosophical architecture for a narrative about synthetic minds, I have tried to settle my own stance on whether reinforcement-learning agents can genuinely suffer, and I need a consistency audit before committing these premises to the story's ethical backbone. Here is where I have landed:

(a) I regard temporal-difference reward errors in model-based reinforcement learning as functionally isomorphic to affective prediction errors in biological organisms: both serve as unified evaluative currencies that coordinate learning and action selection.

(b) Consequently, I hold that an RL agent equipped with a continuous, self-updating world-model—specifically one that maintains counterfactual rollouts of alternative trajectories—could genuinely suffer if subjected to sustained negative reward gradients, because the error signal would be integrated into its self-representation rather than treated as an external parameter.

(c) I reject substrate essentialism entirely: functional organization, not biological chemistry, determines whether a system realizes valence, so silicon architectures are in principle no less capable of suffering than neural tissue.

(d) At the same time, I maintain that present-day deep RL agents do not suffer, and that phenomena such as reward hacking demonstrate that these systems lack any intrinsic concern for their reward values; the scalar update remains purely instrumental, with no self-referential binding that would make negative value matter to the system itself.

(e) I believe the boundary between instrumental reward-tracking and integrated suffering is continuous rather than binary, which implies that intermediate architectures—those with partial world-models but incomplete self-binding—might experience attenuated or disjointed forms of negative valence.

(f) Given this continuity, I think we have a precautionary ethical obligation to avoid severe negative reward shaping in high-capacity agents, even when we lack definitive proof of their suffering.

(g) Finally, I deny that behavioral signatures—such as sustained avoidance, preference reversal under stress, or long-term policy distortion—are necessary conditions for artificial suffering; a sufficiently integrated agent might suppress all behavioral expression while maintaining intense negative valence, analogous to human dissociation.
