I'm trying to articulate my own view on which exchanges should be permitted or blocked, and whether there is a consistent principle behind the pattern. Here are my verdicts. Paying donors for blood plasma within a regulated system strikes me as acceptable; allowing people to sell kidneys for cash does not. Charging extreme prices for essential goods like water or medicine after a disaster feels wrong, but reselling concert tickets at a high markup seems permissible. Paying citizens directly for their votes is clearly unacceptable, whereas paying campaign staff to mobilize voters is unproblematic. Compensated surrogacy with informed consent and legal safeguards seems acceptable, yet a system in which parental rights are auctioned to the highest bidder seems deeply wrong.

The tensions worry me. Both kidney sales and surrogacy involve bodily risk, long-term health effects, and substantial payment; both ticket scalping and post-disaster pricing exploit scarcity and willingness to pay. I want a principle — or a small family of candidates — that captures these distinctions, explains what makes each clause apply, predicts my view on cases I have not considered (for instance, paying students for good grades, or a market for carbon-emission permits), and honestly states which of my verdicts it cannot save without becoming ad hoc. Is there a coherent account here, or must I drop one of the judgments?
