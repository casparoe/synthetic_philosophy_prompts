A recent essay in a literary magazine summarizes Darwin’s mature position on morality and religion in the following way. Darwin, it claims, developed an “ecological ethics” grounded in his botanical studies of plant symbiosis and fungal mycorrhizal networks. Rather than treating moral sympathy as a merely evolved social instinct that serves group survival, Darwin supposedly understood it as a direct extension of the interdependent, horizontal cooperation he observed in root systems—a form of biological mutualism that makes human morality literally continuous with botanical life. From this, the essay infers that Darwin rejected religious doctrine not primarily on evidentiary grounds—not because of the problem of suffering or the failure of design arguments—but because theistic accounts of human dominion over nature violate this symbiotic continuity, substituting hierarchical command for ecological interdependence. The essay concludes that Darwin was therefore not an agnostic in the conventional sense, but rather a kind of secular pantheist whose evolutionary ethics anticipates modern deep ecology, and that “social Darwinism” is therefore a total distortion of his actual view.

This framing strikes me as partly right—Darwin certainly studied plant cooperation, and he did describe moral sentiments as products of evolution—but it also feels like a modern ecological philosophy projected backward. I want an accuracy audit. Please separate carefully: (1) what Darwin genuinely held or argued, especially in his accounts of how moral feelings arise and in his private stance on belief; (2) what counts as a defensible popular simplification; (3) what is a subtle misrepresentation or a conflation of two distinct claims he kept apart; and (4) whether the essay’s final conclusions—that Darwin’s ethics is best read as normative deep ecology, that his religious skepticism was driven by ecological harmony rather than by traditional problems of natural theology, and that he was closer to pantheism than to agnosticism—actually survive once the accurate parts are isolated from the errors. In particular, address whether a descriptive evolutionary account of moral origins can legitimately be turned into the normative ecological ethic the essay describes, and whether the botanical imagery of interdependence plays any genuine role in Darwin’s reasoning about religion.
