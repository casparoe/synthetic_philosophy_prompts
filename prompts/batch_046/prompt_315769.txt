maya: knowing how to do something isnt the same as knowing a list of facts about it. take knowing how to calibrate a mechanicl watch by ear — you can know every proposition about gear ratios, spring tension, and beat frequencies, and still be totally useless at the bench. the knowledge lives in your hands and your ear, not in some mental database. if you try to reduce it to knowing-that, you miss the whole point of skill.

jon: but thats just knowing-that under a practical mode of presentation. you know that this escapement needs that adjustment, but you access it through action rather than explicit speech. the propositions are still there, just tacit. and if they werent, how could you correct yourself when the watch runs fast in a humid room? youd need some representational state tracking the relation between humidity and spring behavior, even if you cant verbalize it.

maya: not convinced. some people learn purely by imitation and never form explicit beliefs. they just "feel" the right tension. theres no hidden proposition guiding them — its more like a direct perception-action loop. calling that "tacit propositional knowledge" is just relabeling the skill to save the theory.

jon: okay, but then explain why experts adapt to weird old movements theyve never seen, while novices freeze. the expert isnt just repeating muscle memory; theyre applying a flexible structure of information. if its not propositional, whats the difference between an expert adapting and a novice guessing?

maya: the expert has a richer perceptual skill, not richer beliefs. they see the movement differently — like seeing a face as familiar versus seeing it as a collection of features. thats not knowledge-that sneaking in; its a different kind of cognitive relation altogether.

jon: but that skill can still be representational. youre representing the mechanisms state without representing it as "the mechanisms state is X." its de re knowledge, not non-propositional knowledge.

maya: now youre just moving the goalposts. first it was explicit propositions, now its de re representations. if you keep broadening "knowing that" to cover every case of successful action, the distinction collapses and youre not saying anything interesting.
