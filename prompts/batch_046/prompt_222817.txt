Read Memento through Buddhist anatta — specifically the Pudgalavada idea that a "person" is real but neither identical to nor different from the skandha's — and through narrative identity theory. Leonard Shelby's anterograde amnesia fractures his aggregates: memory, perception, mental formations all break every few minutes. He stitches himself together with Polaroids, tattoos, and a revenge-story that may be self-deception (the Sammy Jenkis twist). Does Nolan show identity is just a constructed fiction? Or does Leonard's suffering suggest there's a conventional self that persists despite broken skandhas — closer to Pudgalavada than to hard anatta? Point to scenes: the burning Polaroid, the "remember Sammy Jenkis" tattoo, Natalie's manipulation in the motel. Does the twist vindicate anatta by showing Leonard's story is arbitrary, or does it show that without narrative continuity he becomes something worse than empty? Don't just recap the plot; say whether the film dramatizes something philosophers miss about clinging (upadana) to self, or if Buddhist analysis exposes a blind spot in Nolan's treatment of memory.
