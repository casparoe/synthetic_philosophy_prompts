Here is a line of reasoning I have been turning over about Gödel's rotating universe and the philosophical conclusions he drew from it, and I want to know where it overreaches. (1) Gödel showed that Einstein's field equations permit a cosmological solution describing a rotating universe containing closed timelike curves, so that an observer could in principle travel along a world-line that returns to an earlier point in that same world-line. (2) If such travel is consistent with the field equations, then the distinction between past, present, and future cannot be an objective feature of the world, because every event on the loop is equally present to itself and there is no globally consistent ordering into an irreversible flow. (3) Gödel interpreted this not as a mere curiosity but as support for an idealist or Kantian view: objective temporal becoming is illusory, and what we call the passage of time is only subjective appearance. (4) But if temporal becoming is genuinely illusory, then change itself is not real in any robust sense, since change requires that one state objectively gives way to another in successive time. (5) If nothing genuinely changes, then no event ever comes into being or ceases to be, which includes the very cosmological conditions, measurements, and reasoning processes that motivate and establish the rotating-universe model itself. (6) So the model, taken together with Gödel's metaphysical interpretation, seems to undermine the reality of the very reasoning that produces it, yielding a kind of self-refutation. (7) Nevertheless Gödel himself endorsed both the physical admissibility of the rotating solution and the unreality of objective time. I would prefer to keep (1) and (2) intact, since they rest on the mathematics and physics, but I am willing to sacrifice (3) or (4) if that is where the error lies. Please reply in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, identifying which step is weakest, explaining why it looked plausible, and describing what giving it up costs for the relationship between cosmological models and metaphysical conclusions about time.
