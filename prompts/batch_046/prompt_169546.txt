I have been thinking, without any formal philosophical training, about whether ordinary objects really possess hidden powers—such as being brittle, soluble, or flexible—or whether we only speak as if they do because of regular patterns in behaviour. Two people I have discussed this with hold opposing views, and I want them to collaborate adversarially. One camp holds that a disposition can be fully captured by carefully stated counterfactual conditions: if circumstances C were present, manifestation M would occur. They treat finks—cases where the power disappears exactly when the conditions are met—and masks—cases where an interfering factor blocks the manifestation without destroying the underlying power—as puzzles that can be dissolved by refining the conditions and excluding abnormal interference. The other camp holds that dispositions are genuine categorical properties or powers that exist independently of any counterfactual story. They argue that finks expose circularity in conditional analyses, and that masks are especially damaging: an object can retain a real power even when every observable counterfactual test is suppressed, which shows that dispositions are not reducible to if-then patterns.

Please draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Name the two positions in neutral terms. First, present a numbered section of agreed claims: about the distinction between a fink and a mask; about whether either camp denies that everyday objects have dispositions; and about the specific recursive-mask scenario I want injected—imagine a soluble substance containing an internal lattice that activates precisely when dissolution conditions are approached, thereby preventing dissolution, yet this lattice is itself disposed to disintegrate instantly should dissolution ever actually occur. Both sides must say whether they regard this scenario as metaphysically coherent and what, if anything, it demonstrates.

Then present a disagreements table: the precise formulations of residual disagreement, each worded so that its own camp would accept it; the cruxes—I suspect one concerns whether counterfactual analyses can avoid circularity, and another concerns whether a masked disposition is in principle detectable; and, for each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each camp’s prediction recorded in advance. Finally, indicate whether any portion of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive.

Please keep the entire document to no more than 800 words, and format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
