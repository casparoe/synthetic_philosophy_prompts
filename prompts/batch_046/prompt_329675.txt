I have been working through Quine's argument that a speaker's utterance of gavagai upon seeing a rabbit is compatible with translating the term as rabbit, undetached rabbit part, or temporal stage of a rabbit, with no behavioral evidence able to adjudicate among them. It strikes me that this indeterminacy is generated only by Quine's refusal to treat mental representations as part of the evidential base for translation. If we instead adopt an internalist framework—combining Fodor's language of thought with Chomskyan syntactic structures—wouldn't the speaker's internal conceptual-compositional role determine which of the three interpretations is correct? That is, isn't Quine's thesis essentially an artifact of behaviorism, dissolved once we allow that translation can be fixed by matching the internal syntactic structures of the speaker's mental representations rather than merely correlating stimuli and responses?
