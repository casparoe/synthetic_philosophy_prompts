Write an essay on the following controversy. Imagine a predictive decision-support system that is embedded not merely as a passive instrument within a bureaucratic process, but in a configuration of reciprocal embedding: the system continuously reshapes the evaluative criteria and attentional patterns of the human operators who interact with it, while those operators' adaptive responses simultaneously alter the system's operational environment and feedback loops. The central question is whether this arrangement produces a genuinely novel form of embedded agency—one that is neither reducible to individual human intention nor adequately captured by standard accounts of technological mediation—or whether it remains, at bottom, an extension of collective human agency distributed across a hybrid network. Weigh the main arguments on both sides, paying particular attention to whether the system's capacity to restructure the cognitive environment of its users introduces a form of causal influence that escapes both direct human control and conventional instrumental analysis. Reach a definite conclusion and justify it: either defend the claim that reciprocal embedding generates a non-derivative, ontologically distinct mode of agency requiring its own framework of moral and epistemic responsibility, or maintain that it ultimately collapses into joint human action mediated by artefacts, leaving no independent ethical or metaphysical status for the embedded system itself. Please write your response in British English.
