In recent work on Berkeley's monetary writings—especially The Querist—scholars often analogize his treatment of coin and credit to the theory of ideas laid out in the Principles and Three Dialogues: gold is cast as a "real idea" with direct perceptual presence and intrinsic sensory weight, paper notes and bank bills as "imaginary" or notional signs that depend on convention and mental assent to function, and the entire monetary system is read as an applied case of immaterialism in which value circulates without any material substrate corresponding to it. The analogy is used to license the conclusion that Berkeley's economics is essentially a footnote to his metaphysics, and specifically that his defense of paper money over specie is just a defense of conventional signification over direct perception—so that if you accept his immaterialism, you should be relaxed about fiat currency, and if you reject the metaphysics, the economic argument collapses with it. How far does the money-as-idea analogy actually hold? Give me an explicit correspondence table—what plays the role of the perceiver, the idea, the archetype in the divine mind, the mental act of conception, the communication of ideas between minds, and the real-versus-imaginary distinction—then identify the disanalogies, particularly where Berkeley's account of money relies on state authority, legal enforcement, and social convention rather than on private mental acts. Does the conclusion survive that Berkeley's monetary theory reduces to his immaterialism, or does the analogy quietly import metaphysical commitments—privacy of mental content, direct divine archetyping, non-social reference—that the economic argument does not need and in some places contradicts? If the mapping breaks, is there a better analogy for understanding why Berkeley defended paper credit while remaining skeptical of gold's intrinsic value, and what does the failure of this one tell us about treating eighteenth-century economic arguments as extensions of their authors' metaphysical systems?
