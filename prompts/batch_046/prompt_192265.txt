Hey, I'm a retired physicist who recently got into philospohy, so go easy on me. I was reading about Bradley's regress — the idea that if a relation R connects A and B, you need another relation R' to connect R to A, and then R'', forever. I cooked up this argument and now it feels like it proves either everything is one big blob or nothing ever gets constituted, which can't be right.

(1) If Bradley's regress is vicious, then every connection needs a further connector to do its job.
(2) The standard fix is to say relations don't need extra glue — they relate "naturally" or are internal to thier terms.
(3) Apply that same fix to causation: cause C and effect E are linked without a separate causal relation that itself needs linking.
(4) So causation isn't an external relation but an internal feature of the events, which means C and E aren't genuinely distinct — they're just one event described twice.
(5) Apply it also to mental judgment: the relation between subject and predicate in "the cup is red" needs no further unifier.
(6) But then judging "the cup is red" is just a single mental blob with no internal structure, so there's no real difference between that judgment and judging that I judge that — it's all one self-relating state.
(7) If we reject the fix and accept the regress everywhere, then every fact needs infinite connectors, so no fact is ever fully constituted.
(8) So either all distictions collapse into one cosmic thing, or nothing gets constituted at all. But I clearly have distinct thoughts and my coffee cup exists separately from me. Where's the slip?

I'd like to hang onto (2) — it feels elegant — but I want to know what I'm paying. Is there an equivocation between "internal" in Bradley's sense and "internal" for causation? Or did I smuggle in a scope mistake somewhere?
