Draft the joint statement that a "nonidentity-is-real" camp and a "relational-wronging-dissolves-it" camp could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on the nonidentity problem. Camp one takes the parfit-style line: when climate or reproductive choices change who gets born, we cant harm those specific future people since they wouldnt exist otherwise, so person-affecting ethics collapses for longterm obligations. Camp two thinks wronging doesnt require comparative harm — rights-violations or justice-failures can apply even to people who arent worse off — so the puzzle collapses once you drop that hidden premise, though they still owe an account of why we dont owe the same to merely possible people.

Use the standard format: a numbered section of agreed claims, then a disagreements table with each sides wording phrased so theyd sign their own row. Then list cruxes — i think the main ones are (1) whether "not being worse off" exhausts the moral relation between present actors and future people, and (2) whether modal robustness of personal identity (how essential specific genetic or psychological traits are to being "this person") should enter ethical reasoning about replacement scenarios at all. For each crux, include a concrete case or argument both sides agree is relevant, plus each sides predicted verdict on it.

Also please: seperate whats settled from whats genuinely contested among people who actually work on population ethics, and dont present one camps view as the consensus — for instance, if theres live disagreement over whether "same-number" and "different-number" cases really split the moral logic differently, flag that as open. Keep it casual, a typo or two is fine, and dont include a bibliography or citation list since this should work without web access.
