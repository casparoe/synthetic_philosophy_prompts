Yet there is a less-discussed tension: whether the meta-problem is specific to consciousness or symptomatic of a broader structural feature in our explanatory practices, namely the point where first-person accessibility meets third-person theoretical closure. Taking the latter seriously suggests that resolving it may require engagement with the cognitive science of explanation, not solely philosophical analysis of phenomenal concepts.

With this framing in mind, which open lines of work should be prioritised for a researcher with roughly three years and training in conceptual analysis rather than empirical neuroscience? Which is most upstream of the others? Which is tractable without laboratory collaboration, and which risks becoming a dead end if the intuition proves domain-specific? Please defend an ordering: what makes one line worth sustained effort, what evidence over the next few years would revise your ranking, and which broader implications—for how we treat other explanatory gaps—actually survive if that line succeeds?
