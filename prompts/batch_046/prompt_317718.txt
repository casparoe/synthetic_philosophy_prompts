hey — writing a feature on how science remembers its big breaks, and there's this institutional quirk that keeps bugging me. we label calculus as Newton-Leibniz, like it's one idea with two parents, but every museum statue and named grant picks a single guy, usually Newton. same deal with Darwin and Wallace: they presented together, but only Darwin got the book, the bust, the brand. so the practice is this weird mix of joint-labeling and winner-take-all glorification.

what philosophical commitments does that pattern presuppose? does it assume a discovery is just a point in idea-space anyone could reach — so the second arrival is redundant and credit is just historical accident? or does it assume the opposite, that who gets there first shapes what the idea even becomes, so simultaneous invention is actually impossible in any deep sense? and can you hold both views at once — naming calculus jointly (sounds inevitable) while building monuments to individuals (sounds like the person matters)?

i'm also wondering if there's a hidden theory of scientific progress buried in here — like progress is a single line, not a branching tree — and whether that's consistent with how we still credit people for failed experiments.

there's this niche distinction i want to throw in — call it path-dependence of conceptual form: even if two people derive the same result, the notation, metaphysical framing, and the problems they think it solves can differ totally, so it's not really the same discovery. does our naming practice presuppose that form doesn't matter, only content? and if we took path-dependence seriously, would we have to drop hyphenated names and treat Newton's fluxions and Leibniz's differentials as two seperate theories?

sorry if that's rambling — i think there's a typo in there somewhere. but basically: reconstruct the implicit theory of discovery and credit that lets institutions do both joint-labeling and solo-glorification, say which rival views it quietly rules out, and tell me if the practice is philosophically consistent or just lazy. don't need citations, just the logic.
