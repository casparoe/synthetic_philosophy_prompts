You are delivering the opening keynote at the retirement symposium for Professor Elena Vasquez, who spent thirty-five years working on self-reference, epistemic humility, and the limits of first-person report. The audience is split evenly: half are philosophers of mind and epistemologists who have argued with her about Zhuangzi's butterfly dream since graduate school; the other half are neuroscientists and sleep researchers who study REM phenomenology and lucid-dream protocols but have never read the Zhuangzi. The event is formal, celebratory, and intellectually serious — not a roast, not a eulogy. Family members are present.

Write the speech, approximately ten minutes when delivered. The philosophical idea must do real structural work rather than serve as decoration: use Zhuangzi's butterfly dream not as a whimsical opening image but as the entry point to a specific problem you introduce — call it "the recursive witness problem." Frame it precisely: when a subject reports "I am awake and not dreaming," that report can itself occur within a dream; appealing to a meta-observer who verifies the first report only pushes the question up one level, leaving no non-circular ground for certainty about one's own present state. The speech must navigate exactly what this problem allows and forbids you to say. You may honor Vasquez's career by showing that her refusal to claim final answers to self-reference was not modesty but philosophical rigor; you may not claim that neuroscience has "solved" the dream problem through lucidity metrics, nor may you claim that philosophy alone has dissolved it. You must offer genuine comfort to a room that includes non-philosophers — including her family — about the value of a life spent on questions that resist closure, without resorting to false consolation (e.g., that she is "watching over us from somewhere") or to technical jargon that loses the scientists. Include one moment of recognition for the philosophers and one moment of clarity for the neuroscientists, showing them why the difficulty is not reducible to brain-state identification. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific publications by Vasquez. Paraphrase Zhuangzi rather than quoting verbatim. The tone should be polished, formal, and genuinely affectionate.
