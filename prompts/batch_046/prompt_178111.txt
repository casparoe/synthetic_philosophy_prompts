One speaker uses it to argue that personal identity is socially constructed; another uses it to claim that liberal frameworks necessarily misdescribe unchosen group memberships as if they were voluntary associations; a third treats it as an obvious reminder that humans need social connection. Separate the precise claims that could be packed into that single sentence—distinguish the metaphysical, sociological, and normative readings—indicate which are near-platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, and which are false, and tell me which reading is actually being invoked when the line is used to argue that liberal neutrality cannot accommodate "constitutive attachments" such as religious or national belonging. Present the disambiguation as a menu rather than selecting one, and for each reading say whether it genuinely undermines liberalism or merely redescribes facts that liberal theory already accepts.
