I'm an undergraduate enrolled in a seminar on political philosophy and the ethics of democratic communication. For my final project, I am attempting to translate Hannah Arendt's analysis of political deception—specifically her claim that modern lying does not merely conceal particular facts but fabricates an alternative reality that severs the plural, contingent basis of factual truth—into a reusable operational checklist. The intended user is a senior communications editor at a state-level environmental protection agency who must approve or revise draft public statements released during active contamination events. She faces these decisions two to three times per week, works under a ninety-minute publication deadline, holds no formal training in philosophy, and her signature is legally required before any statement is released.

The procedure should take no more than fifteen minutes to apply. It must be structured as ordered tests or a flowchart rendered in prose, with explicit branching conditions. It should preserve Arendt's core distinctions: that factual truths depend on testimony and plural witnesses and are therefore vulnerable to being replaced rather than simply hidden; that rational or logical consistency can be maintained inside a false narrative; and that modern organized deception often requires the liar's own self-deception. The checklist needs to address three hard cases: a draft that uses technically accurate statistical data but embeds them in a framing that makes the underlying event unrecognizable to direct witnesses; a draft that omits a preliminary study whose exclusion is defensible on methodological grounds; and a draft that corrects an earlier false statement issued by the same agency.

Please include worked applications to three scenarios: one in which the procedure approves a statement that will strike a lay reader as manipulative; one in which it rejects a statement that appears to be only prudent caution; and one involving a correction of prior agency error. At each step, flag the judgment calls that cannot be mechanized—especially where the editor must assess whether a framing destroys the conditions for factual testimony rather than merely emphasizing favorable data. Also specify what the procedure is unable to settle, such as long-term narrative coherence across multiple statements or the ethical status of omissions made for national security.

As a niche constraint, the agency has recently adopted an AI-assisted drafting module that proposes alternative phrasings optimized for sentiment calibration. The procedure should contain a dedicated test—usable by someone without machine-learning expertise—to determine whether an AI-suggested framing crosses from strategic emphasis into the kind of reality-substitution Arendt describes, and it should state what to do if the test is inconclusive.

Do not provide a bibliography, citation list, or suggested readings; the deliverable should be self-contained and grounded only in the conceptual framework itself.
