For my independent philosophy study, I have been working through Derek Parfit’s teletransporter scenario and the long-running dispute about what matters in personal survival: psychological continuity, bodily continuity, or some hybrid relation. For decades, philosophers have replayed the same family of cases—teletransportation, brain fission, gradual neural replacement—without converging on an answer, and I want to understand why the debate has stalled rather than ask you to pick a side. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not mainly about replication physics or neuroscience but about two different concepts operating under the single word “survival.” One camp seems to treat survival as an egocentric planning relation—whether a future being stands in the right anticipatory and psychological connection to me now—while the other treats it as an objective, third-person causal-historical relation, tracking an unbroken biological or informational pathway through time. If I am right, then the two sides are not contradicting each other about one fact but answering different questions, which would explain the deadlock. But I want an honest diagnosis: is the stall actually driven by hidden empirical assumptions about information preservation or brain function, by incompatible standards for what counts as a legitimate thought experiment, or by bedrock intuitions about identity that neither side can argue for? And could anything break the impasse—perhaps a formal distinction that treats continuity not as all-or-nothing but as parameterized separately along an anticipation-tracking dimension and a causal-integration dimension—or should we conclude that the question is ill-posed and that no distinction or experiment should be expected to resolve it? Please diagnose the structure of the disagreement concretely, showing what each side actually claims about the teletransporter cases, and explain what kind of progress—conceptual, formal, or framing—would be appropriate given your diagnosis, or why none should be expected.
