Please construct a branching taxonomy — a decision tree — mapping the logical space of responses to consequentialist cluelessness, understood as the problem that long-run consequences of an action may be so structurally unpredictable that a consequentialist lacks any reliable basis for comparing outcomes. The root node should ask whether this unpredictability defeats consequentialist evaluation for the decision at hand.

Each internal node must be labeled by a concrete claim or case distinction on which positions genuinely diverge, phrased so that someone can answer without knowing the philosophical label it leads to. Required splits include: (1) whether the opacity is purely epistemic or also axiological — i.e., whether we merely lack information about future states, or whether we lack a stable conception of value for worlds sufficiently distant or transformed; (2) whether the agent should adopt an epistemic cutoff, restricting evaluation to effects within some foreseeable horizon; (3) whether unknown long-run effects should be incorporated through subjective expected utility, through ambiguity-averse decision rules such as maximin over a set of plausible worlds, or through a niche framework of moral risk diversification that treats different value-plausible futures as assets in a portfolio; and (4) whether the problem is asymmetric — applying more severely to positive interventions than to omissions — or symmetric across action and inaction.

The leaves should carry named positions where they exist (for instance, near-term restrictionism, standard expected-value defense, robustness consequentialism, and procedural-fallback hybridism) and 'unnamed' where a combination is coherent but apparently unoccupied. For each leaf, provide exactly one sentence stating its most awkward practical commitment — the implication its defenders least want to accept, such as being forced to regard high-stakes structural choices (e.g., governing transformative technology) as morally arbitrary, or being committed to treating practical reason like portfolio management across incommensurable axiologies.

In addition, identify one unoccupied combination in the logical space (for example, a view that applies precise subjective probabilities over distant futures while rejecting every form of epistemic cutoff) and briefly diagnose whether its vacancy is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Finally, flag any two leaves that the tree's branching structure cannot cleanly separate, explaining which shared commitment makes them overlap.

Build the tree from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of author names. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke obscure historical details that depend on specialist knowledge. The taxonomy should be usable by a student who knows the basic problem but has not memorized the literature.
