On the other hand, if those baselines are pluaral and shifting, then legally anchoring one just creates the oppression Hart warned about.

Weigh both sides, but don't end on a non-committal note — defend a verdict. Either Hart still wins because pluaralism is inevitable and moral infrastucture can be maintained without criminal law, or Devlin wins in a modified form because some minimal baseline has to be legally anchored or harm-talk becomes incoherent. Use concrete examples — maybe contract-breaking in intimate relationships, or laws against grave desecration — and address the objection that these are just symbolic harms. No bibliography needed, just reason it through.
