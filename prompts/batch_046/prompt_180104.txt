I hold the view that Fitch's paradox of knowability is not a refutation of anti-realist verificationism but a clarification: the paradox depends on treating knowledge as closed under logical conjunction in a classical, atomistic way, and the correct response is to reject that closure while retaining the principle that every truth is in principle knowable. On this reading, the "unknown truth" in the proof is a pseudo-possibility that dissolves once knowledge is evaluated holistically. What would count against this position? I want arguments and results sorted by decisiveness: whether there is a principled, non-ad-hoc restriction on closure; whether formal epistemic logics can accommodate it without collapsing into skepticism or triviality; and whether the paradox can be reconstructed in logics that already lack closure. I also want an honest assessment of whether defenders have qualified the view into unfalsifiability, and whether that insulation is a problem for a view of this kind. Include what defenders themselves would concede as damaging—say, a result showing holistic evaluation cannot distinguish knowable from unknowable conjunctions, or a case where the paradox regenerates without the closure assumption.
