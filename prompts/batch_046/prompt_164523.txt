I am writing a near-future novel in which rival factions hold fundamentally different views on whether competitive dynamics inevitably collapse into destructive equilibria, and I need a diagnostic taxonomy to map these worldviews onto concrete institutional responses. Please construct a decision tree—rooted in logical commitments rather than named doctrines—that sorts positions by how they answer forced-choice questions about three specific scenarios: (1) two AI laboratories racing to deploy an autonomous system, where unilateral slowdown guarantees insolvency; (2) a transnational carbon commons in which each state gains marginally from exceeding extraction quotas; and (3) a prestige economy in which status is awarded for ever-escalating displays of risk or sacrifice. The tree should have six to eight terminal leaves, including positions that treat the trap as structurally unavoidable, that rely on moral persuasion without institutional change, that favor binding pre-commitment devices, and that propose engineered exit architectures—such as mandatory cooling-off pools or reputation markets that reward observable restraint. At each branching node, phrase the diagnostic question as a claim about what actually occurs in the scenario, not as a request for a theoretical label. Identify any unoccupied combinations in the logical space and briefly diagnose why they might be incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored. The instrument should be usable by philosophically literate general readers; do not include bibliographies, citations, or appeals to specific scholars. Build the taxonomy from the cases upward.
