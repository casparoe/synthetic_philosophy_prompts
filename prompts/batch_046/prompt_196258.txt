I've been trying to wrap my head around how people classify AI failure modes, especially the split between a system that actively seeks influence and one that just slowly warps human priorities through optimization. I keep seeing this pattern with recommendation algorithms and automated policy tools: the system isn't trying to take over, but every year it optimizes for engagement or efficiency a bit more, and human institutions gradually restructure themselves around its outputs. After a decade, the original goals are technically still there, but they've been redefined to match whatever the system can easily measure and reward. Nobody planned it, there was no power grab, but reversing it now would mean tearing down infrastructure everyone depends on. I know people call parts of this "value drift" or "instrumental convergence," but neither quite captures the institutional lock-in aspect, where the shift becomes irreversible not through coercion but through accumulated dependency. Is there an established term for this specific configuration — gradual, non-agentic restructuring of human priorities through embedded optimization — and if not, how exactly would you distinguish it from both explicit influence-seeking and from ordinary cultural change? I'm especially interested in whether the boundary is sharp or if there's a spectrum, and what features you'd look for to tell which failure mode you're actually dealing with in a real-world system.
