I referee resolution protocols for a continuous-variable forecasting collective—functionally a prediction market run as an epistemic cooperative—where bettors stake on precise physical measurements (seismic coordinates, compound half-lives, temperature anomalies) before the measurement convention is fully locked. A recurring dispute arises when one party invokes the principle of indifference to claim that probabilities must be uniform over one parameterization (linear distance, log-time, Celsius), while another party selects a different coordinate system and derives incompatible bin probabilities—the classic Bertrand-paradox structure transplanted into resolution design. I need a reusable decision procedure, executable in about fifteen minutes per case, that translates what the principle of indifference actually requires into operational steps for finalizing a resolution scheme. Specifically: provide the ordered checks that distinguish a parameterization that carries a claim of neutrality from one that is merely conventional; state the explicit stopping condition where the procedure returns that no unique fair prior exists; flag the judgment calls that cannot be eliminated and where values about measurement conventions necessarily enter; supply a short battery of test conditions I can apply to any proposed resolution protocol; and be honest about what class of disputes the procedure is structurally silent on. Then apply the procedure to three resolution conflicts: a temperature bet resolved in Celsius versus Fahrenheit; a distance-to-detection bet resolved with linear versus logarithmic scaling; and a solar-flare timing bet resolved over a circular 24-hour window versus an angular coordinate system. In one of these applications, the procedure should yield a prior that privileges the convention one bettor explicitly rejects—show precisely where that privilege is unavoidable rather than hidden. Conclude by stating the boundary conditions under which I should abandon the procedure and refer the dispute to a normative vote rather than a formal resolution.
