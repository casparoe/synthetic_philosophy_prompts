For a graduate seminar in philosophical theology, lay out the desiderata an evolutionary theodicy must meet to count as adequate—that is, an account that addresses the vast scope of suffering, predation, and extinction across evolutionary history that precedes any human moral agency. For each desideratum, state the conceptual or theological consideration that motivates it: for instance, the need to explain apparently gratuitous suffering without appealing to creaturely free will, the need to preserve divine omnibenevolence without reducing natural law to a deistic mechanism, and the need to distinguish biologically functional harm from morally relevant evil. Mark which conditions are non-negotiable for any theist committed to both evolutionary science and classical theism, and which belong only to particular camps (e.g., literalist hermeneutics, libertarian free will, or a universalist eschatology). If any subset of the non-negotiable conditions is jointly unsatisfiable—say, because accounting for all evolutionary suffering either compromises omnipotence or introduces a dualistic competitor—identify the exact tension. You may briefly score the standard theistic responses against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard.
