In discussions of Mill’s political writings, defenders of his stance on British administration in India often rely on a craft-guild apprenticeship analogy: Britain plays the master craftsman who holds the technical knowledge of governance, India plays the apprentice bound to learn under temporary supervision, and the colonial period is the indenture that must be served before full membership—self-rule—is granted. The comparison is meant to show that Mill’s position is not bare domination but a pedagogical arrangement: like apprenticeship, it involves hierarchy and constraint, yet it is justified by the apprentice’s eventual autonomy and the master’s duty to train rather than to exploit. I am trying to determine whether this analogy actually carries the moral weight its defenders assign to it, or whether it quietly smuggles in assumptions that fall apart once they are made explicit.

Please audit the analogy systematically. Give me an explicit correspondence table—what in the colonial situation maps onto the master, the apprentice, the craft of governance itself, the contract of indenture, the evaluation of readiness, and the guild’s collective interest in maintaining standards—followed by the fractures: for example, whether there is any genuine consent or contract on India’s part comparable to an apprentice’s entry; whether the “master” has economic and political conflicts of interest that a guild master lacks; whether the criterion for “readiness” is defined circularly by the master’s own cultural norms; and whether the analogy conceals the extractive economic dimensions of colonial rule that have no parallel in craft training. Separate the disanalogies that genuinely undermine Mill’s defense from those that are merely decorative. Then take a committed position: does the apprenticeship analogy survive as a justification for temporary colonial tutelage, or was it doing all the moral work from the start? If it collapses, is there a better analogy that captures what Mill meant by “stages of civilization” without reducing to unexamined paternalism? Be definite in your verdict and show your reasoning at each step.
