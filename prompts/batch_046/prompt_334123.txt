I am revising the syllabus for an advanced graduate seminar in metaphysics and modal logic, and I have grown dissatisfied with the standard textbook illustrations of the divergence between Lewisian counterpart theory and Kripkean transworld identity—too often they rely on generic twin-earth scenarios or bare stipulations about biological origins that students can resolve by rote. I need a genuinely novel architectural case that remains fully intelligible without consulting any literature, one that forces the two views apart not through psychological continuity alone but through a more unusual essentialist commitment. Please design a tightly constrained thought experiment, presentable in no more than one well-formed paragraph, that satisfies the following specifications.

The scenario must be entirely realistic: no exotic physics, no predictive demons, no science-fictional technology, and any stakes or payoffs should be ones a normal person could genuinely face. Within that frame, construct a situation centered on a specific individual—call her Elena—who is a master conservator-restorer whose professional and personal identity is partly constituted by an irreplaceable custodial relationship to a particular damaged fresco cycle in a fictional but named chapel. The counterfactual world should differ minimally: a historical flood that actually destroyed part of the chapel and necessitated Elena’s restoration work never occurred, so the fresco remains intact and was never damaged, yet Elena’s psychological biography, training, internal mental life, and social relationships are otherwise preserved exactly. The case must be engineered so that counterpart theory and transworld identity agree on whether an Elena-related individual exists in the undamaged world, but deliver sharply opposite verdicts on whether that world-bound individual is literally identical to actual-world Elena, with the disagreement turning specifically on whether the custodial/artifactual relation to the damaged fresco is essential to Elena rather than on origin or psychology. After presenting the scenario, provide the explicit reasoning for each theory’s verdict on both existence and identity, isolating precisely which structural feature of the case—likely the essentiality of the irreplaceable custodial relation—forces the split. Then explain what happens if that feature is removed: does the disagreement collapse into a standard origin-essentialist dispute, or does it vanish altogether? Finally, include an honest assessment of how a defender of the view that comes out counterintuitive—whether the counterpart theorist who denies literal identity despite psychological preservation, or the transworld-identity theorist who insists on it despite the absent artifactual relation—would attack the case, for instance by challenging the essentialist premise about the fresco-relation or by accusing the scenario of smuggling in a false metaphysical commitment about artifacts.
