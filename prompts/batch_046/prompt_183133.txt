I've been noodling on a variant of the pessimistic meta-induction that hasn't gotten much airplay. The usual PMI says past theories died, so ours will too. But here's the twist: constructing Laudan-style lists of 'refuted' theories already requires a realist stance on what counts as theoretical continuity—treating phlogiston and caloric as flat-out false rather than structurally preserved. So my position is that the PMI only bites if you implicitly deny the middle ground that structural realists claim saves us; specifcally, it assumes terms either survive intact or get junked, with no partial preservation. What would count against this? Not just the standard Worrall reply—I want the actual defeaters. Are there historical transitions where the successor theory preserves literally none of the structural relations? Could you run the PMI using purely anti-realist historiography that doesn't classify theories as false in the first place? And be honest: is this position already qualified into unfalsifiability, or is there a concrete result in history of science that would actually hurt it?
