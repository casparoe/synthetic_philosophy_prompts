Two members of a philosophy reading group, Ravi and Elena, disagreed about whether Dennett's concept of the self as a center of narrative gravity can apply to distributed, multi-author systems such as a long-running collective wiki that maintains a consistent first-person institutional voice. (1) Ravi argued it cannot: the concept is anchored to a single biological agent's survival-driven need to distinguish self from other, and without one spatiotemporal trajectory of self-telling and unified action selection, the wiki's first-person voice is merely grammatical convention. (2) Elena replied that the idea is functional rather than biological: if a system produces a coherent narrative with predictive and integrative utility, substrate and author count should be irrelevant, otherwise one commits substrate chauvinism. (3) Ravi countered that this dilutes the concept to uselessness, since different editors make separate decisions, so no unified agency stands behind the narrative to guide behavior. (4) Elena answered that even individual brains integrate actions post-hoc and can be internally distributed, as in split-brain cases, so narrative integration without centralized control is precisely what Dennett's account describes.
