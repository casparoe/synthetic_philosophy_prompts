I'm working through an argument that began as a seminar response on epistemic injustice and now seems to collapse every rational disagreement into a moral wrong. I'd like to know which step to abandon and what abandoning it costs.

(1) Testimonial injustice occurs when a hearer assigns a speaker less credibility than they deserve because of a negative identity prejudice.
(2) In any substantive disagreement, each party assigns the other party less credibility in the disputed domain than they assign themselves.
(3) Reliability judgments are never made in a social vacuum: we calibrate credibility through social markers—disciplinary training, dialect, institutional affiliation, and demographic correlations with expertise—that are inseparable from group identity.
(4) So whenever one discounts an interlocutor's testimony, that discount is partly caused by social categorization that tracks identity.
(5) If identity-tracking categorization plays a causal role in a credibility deficit, and that deficit is not fully justified by the evidence available to the hearer, then the deficit is an instance of testimonial injustice.
(6) In disagreement, the available evidence rarely fully justifies the exact magnitude of the credibility gap; some portion is always explained by the social heuristics in (3).
(7) Therefore, every substantive disagreement involves testimonial injustice.
(8) But if every disagreement is unjust, then engaging in rational persuasion is morally prohibited, and epistemic life becomes impossible without complicity in injustice.
(9) That conclusion is unacceptable.

If I must choose, I'd rather preserve (1) and (2) than (9). Where does the argument slip? Is the error an equivocation between "social categorization" and "prejudice," a scope mistake about how much causation is required for injustice, or does (5) smuggle in an unjustified normative standard about what the evidence must justify? What does rejecting the faulty step commit me to about the role of social identity in ordinary credibility calibration?

Please respond in American English.
