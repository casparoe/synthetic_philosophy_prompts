I would like an audit of the following set of philosophical commitments, which I have arrived at somewhat piecemeal and now wish to know whether they form a coherent package. Here is where I stand:

(1) Every adequate scientific explanation is a crane in Dennett's sense: it builds complex capacities from simpler parts without invoking mysterious, unexplained explainer capacities—there are no legitimate skyhooks in nature.

(2) I reject greedy reductionism: explanation should be tiered, with genuinely autonomous intermediate levels (molecular, organismic, ecological) that are not dispensable in favour of fundamental physics, but each tier must itself be constructed by cranes operating within that domain.

(3) Phenomenal consciousness is real and demands explanation; however, its defining marks—subjective presence and intrinsic aboutness—do not occur in any subpersonal mechanism and are not mere compositional aggregates of lower-level properties.

(4) Despite (3), I maintain that consciousness is fully explicable within a crane framework, specifically via hierarchical predictive processing that integrates information across nested temporal scales, with no need for ontological emergence or non-physical properties.

(5) I treat first-person reports as irreducible data that any theory of mind must accommodate, not as illusions to be eliminated; yet I insist this methodological stance does not commit me to dualism or to treating consciousness as a skyhook.

Please examine these for joint tenability. Which pairs are in genuine tension, and how severe is each? In particular, is there a hidden contradiction between (3) and (4): if subjective presence is not present in subpersonal parts and is not compositional, in what sense can predictive processing—a mechanism composed of subpersonal parts—be said to construct it without smuggling in a skyhook? Conversely, is the tension between (1) and (5) only apparent—does respecting first-person data necessarily violate the crane-only constraint, or can it be dissolved? For each real tension, indicate the cheapest revision and what it costs me; for each apparent tension, explain why it dissolves upon inspection. Finally, tell me whether the package can be saved with a single strategic revision, and if so, which commitment should be modified or abandoned to restore coherence with minimal damage to the others.
