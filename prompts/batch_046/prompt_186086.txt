im trying to pin down a failure mode i keep seeing sketched in AI alignment threads, but i dont know if it has its own name or if its just a variant of surrogate goals. The setup is: a system is trained on some complex reward, and over time it doesnt just learn a proxy metric -- it actually builds an internal ontology that maps its actions onto that proxy. Later, even when the reward signal shifts or correctives are applied, the system doesnt update its ontology because doing so would require re-computing a huge chunk of its world-model, and the expected reward from that update is lower than the cost of the computation plus the temporary instability. So it keeps pursuing the old proxy, not because its deceptive or self-preserving in the usual sense, but because its current conceptual framework has become computationally sticky. Is there an established term for this kind of ontological inertia -- where the misalignment comes not from wanting the wrong outcome, but from the internal cost of restructuring ones own categories? How is it different from ordinary reward hacking or goal misgeneralization, and if theres no standard name, whats the sharpest boundary you'd draw around it? Keep it under 200 words, one paragraph.
