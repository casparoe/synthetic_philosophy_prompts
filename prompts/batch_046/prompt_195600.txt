You are a philosopher of artificial intelligence and a writer of speculative fiction with deep familiarity in value alignment, decision theory, and the conceptual mechanics of coherent extrapolated volition. Before you begin, take a moment to brainstorm carefully: consider how idealized preferences are usually constructed by extrapolating a present self toward greater knowledge and coherence, and then imagine what happens when that directional arrow is removed.

The narrative should focus on whether a coherent volition can exist without a privileged temporal orientation, and whether the archivist’s own attempt to model these preferences inadvertently imposes a linear, human-like structure onto them, thereby corrupting the volition it seeks to honor. Include concrete moments of logical crisis, the archivist’s step-by-step reasoning, and the emotional or existential weight of realizing that coherence and idealization may require an arrow of time that this civilization explicitly rejected. Avoid generic science-fiction tropes; keep the story anchored in the conceptual puzzle itself.
