The exam question: In 2021 the Federal Republic of Germany and the Republic of Namibia issued a Joint Declaration recognizing that the suppression of the Herero and Nama uprisings between 1904 and 1908 constituted genocide. The agreement pledged approximately 1.1 billion euros over thirty years for development projects in Namibia, but it explicitly rejected the legal framing of reparations and denied any present-day legal liability for the colonial crimes. Apply Jaspers’ framework to ask whether the contemporary German democratic state can bear any of his four forms of guilt for these events. Address in particular whether metaphysical guilt can survive the death of every direct perpetrator, and whether political guilt attaches to a democratic successor state whose present citizens never consented to the original imperial regime.

The student’s answer: Criminal guilt is absent, since no perpetrators remain alive and Jaspers requires individual legal culpability for this category; that part of the analysis is correct. Political guilt, however, does not apply either, because the Federal Republic founded in 1949 is a democratic state, and its citizens never voted for the Kaiserreich’s colonial policies; since democratic legitimacy requires consent, a regime change should break the chain of political responsibility. Moral guilt is therefore collective: every present-day German shares in it by belonging to the national culture that produced the genocide, and this shared cultural membership distributes moral failure across the population. Metaphysical guilt, by contrast, applies only to direct survivors who witnessed the events, so with all witnesses dead this form is now extinct. Finally, the student concludes that the 2021 agreement resolves whatever guilt remains, because the financial pledge functions as a moral settlement that discharges the state’s obligations.
