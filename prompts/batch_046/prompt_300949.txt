As a journalist who covers science and philosophy of mind, I have been trying to settle my own view on whether sentience admits of degrees and how moral status tracks it. I want to know whether the position I have arrived at is internally coherent, or whether I am smuggling in an assumption in one commitment that another explicitly rejects. Here is where I stand:

(1) Moral status is not binary but genuinely scalar: it tracks the richness and integration of phenomenal experience, with no metaphysical on/off switch in nature.

(2) Insects likely possess a minimal but real degree of sentience—something like low-resolution affective integration—so harming them is a minor wrong, though far less serious than harming a mammal.

(3) A sophisticated climate-control system with predictive modeling, feedback loops, and adaptive algorithms, but lacking any integrated phenomenal field or affective valence, has exactly zero moral status, regardless of behavioral complexity.

(4) The correct grading should be understood along two independent axes: valence-density (the intensity and variety of affective experience) and temporal self-integration (the degree to which that experience is bound across time into a continuous perspective).

(5) For practical ethics and policy, I endorse a sharp operational threshold: treat any being whose combined score on these two axes falls below a certain point as if it lacked standing entirely, in order to prevent decision-paralysis.

(6) I reject the suffering-only view: negative valence is not necessary for moral status. A being with only diffuse, low-grade positive experience and minimal temporal integration still carries a tiny but genuine claim.

(7) Therefore, I deny that nature contains any single boundary—between thermostat and insect, or between primate and human—where sentience abruptly appears or disappears.

My question is whether this package hangs together. Specifically, does (3)'s claim that the thermostat has exactly zero status conflict with (1) and (7)'s insistence on continuity? If the axes in (4) are genuinely continuous, should not the thermostat register at least an infinitesimal value rather than a precise null? And does (5)'s practical threshold conflict with (2)'s claim that insects have real, if minor, standing—since any threshold high enough to be useful seems likely to sweep insects into the "treat as zero" category, while any threshold low enough to preserve (2) seems practically useless? Or is there a way to read (5) as purely heuristic, leaving (2) intact?

Additionally, is there an unnoticed tension between (6) and (4)? If valence-density includes both positive and negative affect, and I insist that negative affect is not required, does that weaken my reason for drawing the line where I do in (3)—or does the absence of both axes in the thermostat still justify the null assignment?

Please audit these commitments in order of severity. For each tension, say what must give: the continuity thesis, the sharp null for artifacts, the practical threshold, or the claim about insects. And please flag any tension that looks real but dissolves on closer inspection—particularly whether (3) and (5) are genuinely at odds, or whether one addresses metaphysics and the other practical reasoning. I am not asking for a general survey of theories of consciousness, but for a precise diagnosis of where my own view commits me and what the cheapest repair would cost.
