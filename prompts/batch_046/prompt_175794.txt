After a long argument with a friend last night, I have adopted a deliberately extreme version of the analogy-first thesis about cognition—the kind associated with Hofstadter and Sander's emphasis on analogy as the heart of thinking, though pushed well beyond its usual formulation. The position I want stress-tested is this: analogical mapping is not merely central but exhaustive. Every mental process—including perceptual binding, the execution of procedural skills like riding a bicycle (reconstructed as analogical alignment between body-state frames and terrain-relational schemas), and even the weight adjustments in statistical learning (reconstructed as analogical alignment of activation-space geometries to previously mapped domains)—is literally the same relational-mapping operation. Language does not generate cognitive structures; it only labels analogies formed pre-linguistically. There is no autonomous syntactic module, no non-declarative motor program, and no purely statistical pattern-completion mechanism that is not, at bottom, analogy.

My friend claims this view is unfalsifiable because any apparent counterexample can be redescribed as a hidden analogy. I want to know whether she is right. So tell me: what would genuinely count against this position? I am not asking for a bibliography, a reading list, or citations—just the arguments, intuitions, empirical findings, and formal results that actually matter. Which observations in neuroscience, developmental psychology, or machine learning would be decisive rather than merely awkward? What does this view predict that modular or statistical-learning rivals do not, and does it risk any observation that those rivals can accommodate but it cannot? Which defeaters would a committed analogy-exhaustivist concede as counting against them, and which would they dismiss by expanding the scope of "analogy" to cover the counterexample? Rank these defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Be committed: take a definite position on whether the view is well-insulated, and say whether that insulation is intellectually virtuous or vicious—does it protect a deep insight, or does it render the position empty?

Verdict:
\answer{well-insulated and defensible}
