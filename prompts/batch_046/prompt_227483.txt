I'm on a heritage board for a mid-sized city and we keep getting proposals for plaques and museum labels that frame historical wrongs — like a factory collapse or a forced removal — using phrases like 'our guilt' or 'the city's moral debt.' The events are 80-150 years old, no living perpetrators, but there are living descendents and also residents with no family link. I need a decision procedue I can actually run in a meeting: the questions in order, what counts as a yes/no at each step, where it stops and says don't use guilt-language, and where it has to hand off to a values call because it's not factual anymore. Ground it in the philosphy of collective responsibility, especially the four kinds of guilt Jaspers talked about — criminal, political, moral, metaphyscial — but translate it into something my board — mostly historians and one lawyer — can use without reading theory. The procedue should handle cases where descendents are present versus absent, and where the institution that did the harm still exists in some form versus being totally gone. I also want you to build in a check for what I'd call 'representational standing': do the people being named as guilty actually have a voice in the framing, or are we speaking over them? Then run it on two proposals: labeling a 1920s industrial disaster as 'our collective guilt' when the factory's successor company still operates downtown, and framing a 19th-century land seizure with no direct institutional line remaining but a tribal nation with living members in the region. Include one where the procedue gives an answer the board probably won't like, and flag exactly where the procedure misfires or needs human judgment.
