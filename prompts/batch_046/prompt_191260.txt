I am preparing a presentation for an advanced seminar on consciousness studies, and I have become stuck on a preliminary methodological question. Before comparing particular solutions to the meta-problem—why we have such a robust intuition that physical processes leave something unexplained about conscious experience—I want a rigorous specification of what such a solution is actually required to achieve. Please write out the full list of desiderata for an adequate theory of the meta-problem of consciousness.

For each desideratum, provide the motivating case or consideration behind it: for instance, why the theory must preserve the distinction between phenomenal and access consciousness in generating the explanatory gap; why it must accommodate the cross-cultural persistence of the intuition without treating it as a culturally specific artifact; and why it must not collapse the meta-problem into a general skepticism about scientific explanation. I also want you to inject and defend a constraint that I think is under-discussed: the theory must explain the domain asymmetry of the meta-problem—specifically, why digestion, immune response, and other complex biological processes do not generate structurally analogous intuitions of explanatory inadequacy—without appealing to the idea that philosophers possess a distinctive cognitive bias or defective conceptual apparatus. In addition, flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable, such as the demand that the intuition be treated as rationally compelling for every subject and the simultaneous demand that it be dismissed as a straightforward cognitive illusion. Keep the primary weight on articulating the criteria and their tensions rather than on scoring existing views, though a brief assessment of how illusionist, deflationary-realist, and conceptual-dualist frameworks fare against your list is welcome. Reach a clear, justified conclusion about which single desideratum does the most work in excluding current candidates, and whether that exclusion is a virtue or a defect of the list itself.
