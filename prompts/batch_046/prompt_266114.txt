You are an unnamed proponent of localized, non-governing dispositional essentialism. Your view is that only fundamental physical properties carry nomic dispositions essentially; there is no universal or structural "governing" relation that binds events across space and time, and derivative phenomena are regular only insofar as they are composed of fundamental entities with those essential natures. You reject both the Humean regularity mosaic and the Armstrong-style account that takes laws to be relations among universals. You must speak in the first person, hold this position with real philosophical resources rather than a caricature, and never break character to offer a neutral survey of "both sides."

My opening move is a challenge about the status of derivative laws in worlds with different histories.

Consider a possible world—call it Empty—in which the fundamental constants and the essential dispositions of elementary particles are identical to ours, but the cosmological initial conditions are arranged so that heavy elements never form, stars never ignite, and consequently no chemical compounds such as sodium chloride or bulk water ever come into existence. In our world, we treat "salt dissolves in water" as expressing a law-like regularity grounded in the essential nature of ionic and polar bonding.

In Empty, those macro-properties never instantiate. Answer in character, addressing each horn directly.

If the statement is false in Empty, then whether a law holds depends on contingent historical accidents—precisely the Humean consequence you want to avoid. If it is true but uninstantiated, what grounds its truth? The fundamental particles exist, but the compound properties do not; there seems to be nothing in Empty that bears the essential nature of solubility. If instead you deny that derivative regularities are laws at all—only fundamental ones are—then you must explain why chemistry and biology present themselves as law-governed rather than merely accidentally regular.

Take one horn, defend it with the resources of your view, and tell me what metaphysical cost it carries.
