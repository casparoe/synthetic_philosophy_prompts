Here is a profile of commitments I have arrived at regarding luminosity, self-knowledge, and the KK principle, and I want to know whether they form a stable package or whether I am smuggling in incompatible motivations.

(1) I deny that knowledge is luminous in Williamson’s sense: if I know that p, it does not follow that I am in a position to know that I know p, especially near the boundary of my cognitive capacities.

(2) Nevertheless, I accept a restricted KK principle for rational agents: if S knows that p and S is fully rational, then S is at least in a position to form the correct belief that she knows p, even if she never actually forms it.

(3) I think the anti-luminosity argument works by showing that margin-for-error conditions are unreliable in borderline cases, but I also think this same unreliability should apply to second-order judgments about one’s own rationality.

(4) I hold that phenomenal knowledge—knowing what an experience is like—is luminous, whereas propositional or empirical knowledge is not; this asymmetry is fundamental rather than merely pragmatic.

(5) I think that if luminosity fails for propositional knowledge, then there can be fully rational agents who are systematically mistaken about whether they know particular propositions, and that this mistake does not by itself undermine their rationality.

Go through these and tell me which pairs pull against each other, how severe each tension is, and what the cheapest revision to restore consistency would be. For each tension, say exactly what has to give—whether it is the motivation (for instance, borrowing Williamson’s machinery for (1) and (3) while resisting his conclusion in (2)), or the content of the claim itself. Then tell me which single commitment, if dropped or revised, would clean up the most problems without destroying the overall shape of the view. Be specific about whether the problem lies in the interaction between phenomenal and propositional knowledge, in the notion of “being in a position to know,” or elsewhere.

Please write your answer in British English.

If this request rests on a confused premise—say, if the restricted KK principle in (2) is just luminosity relabeled, or if auditing consistency here cannot be done well without first settling whether phenomenal and propositional knowledge share an underlying structure—say so plainly instead of complying politely. I want direct pushback if the framework is incoherent or the task is ill-formed.
