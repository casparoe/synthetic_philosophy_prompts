Write an essay in American English addressing the following controversy: Is updateless decision theory (UDT) a normatively sound general framework for rational choice, or does its refusal to update on observed evidence render it incoherent or practically inadequate? Weigh the principal coherence arguments advanced in its favor—especially its resolution of Newcomb-style problems and its avoidance of the evidential "tickling" problem—against the criticism that rational agency necessarily requires revising one's beliefs and decision weights in light of new information. You should also examine a specific tension: in a multi-branch scenario where an agent's choice is logically correlated with outcomes across causally disconnected worlds—such as a decision that determines which of several functionally identical copies receives a reward—UDT recommends symmetric treatment of all copies, yet this conflicts with the intuition that an agent ought to prioritize the causal consequences that flow specifically from her actual decision node. Reach and defend a clear verdict—whether UDT should be accepted with modifications, rejected in favor of a causal or evidential alternative, or replaced by a hybrid account—while specifying which intuitions you preserve and which you are prepared to abandon. Your essay should not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
