Scanlon’s contractualist framework holds that an act is wrong if it violates principles that no one could reasonably reject, and one of its most persistent internal tensions concerns aggregation: the view appears to forbid combining many individually tolerable harms to justify imposing a single severe burden, yet this clashes with strong intuitions in cases like distributing a minor risk across thousands to avert a catastrophe.
