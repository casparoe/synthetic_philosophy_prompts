The Hierarchical Formalist holds that any system of rules—including Peter Suber’s game Nomic and constitutional amendment frameworks—can be rendered fully determinate by a well-ordered hierarchy in which every rule-change is governed by a distinct meta-rule, so that apparent paradoxes of self-reference dissolve once we rigorously distinguish object-level provisions from the meta-level procedures that revise them. The Performative Circularist holds that any ultimate rule of legal change must either apply to itself, generating a performative contradiction, or fail to apply to itself, leaving an unexplained extra-legal exception; they take Nomic-style gameplay and Ross-type constitutional paradoxes as proof that formal hierarchies necessarily bottom out in unregulated political authority rather than in further legal rules.
