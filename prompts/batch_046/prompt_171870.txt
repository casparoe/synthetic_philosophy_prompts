For an advanced undergraduate or early-graduate seminar in philosophy of mind, draft a take-home final exam essay question on the Gödelian analogy developed in Douglas Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach—specifically, the inference from formal self-reference (exemplified by Gödel's sentence construction) to the claim that conscious self-awareness is best understood as a "strange loop" in which a representational substrate acquires the capacity to represent itself as representer. The question must be unanswerable by summarizing Hofstadter's exposition and should instead require students to do three things: (a) articulate the precise structural isomorphism claimed between syntactic self-reference in a formal system and phenomenal self-modeling in cognition; (b) evaluate the disanalogy that formal self-reference operates on token-types within a fixed, finitely axiomatized system with explicit derivation rules, whereas biological cognition lacks a determinate axiomatic base and exhibits continuous token-level dynamics rather than discrete type-level repetition; and (c) determine whether the analogy functions as a causal explanation, a structural model, or a heuristic metaphor, and why that classification matters for assessing its philosophical force. Specify a 1500-word limit and a 48-hour window. Then provide a model answer of approximately 900 words that explicitly separates the analogical mapping from the explanatory claim and addresses the token-type versus token-token objection. Finally, supply a marking scheme built around five must-hit analytical moves, with marks allocated out of 25 total; each entry should name the specific move, its point value, and the characteristic mistake that costs points on that criterion. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, and do not include verbatim quotations from Hofstadter's text.
