In my philosophy of punishment seminar we have been stuck for weeks on the exchange between Daniel Dennett and Gregg Caruso over just deserts under determinism. Dennett maintains that moral responsibility and punishment can be preserved entirely through forward-looking social functions—deterrence, norm maintenance, and rehabilitation—without requiring any metaphysical notion of being the ultimate source of one's actions. Caruso counters that this still smuggles in retributivism: if determinism is true, no one is metaphysically deserving of suffering, so the only legitimate institutional response is a public-health quarantine model that treats dangerous conduct like an infection to be contained rather than a wrong to be answered with harm. The deadlock is precise: Dennett's camp accuses Caruso of defining responsibility so that determinism automatically excludes it, while Caruso's camp accuses Dennett of licensing intentional suffering on purely consequentialist grounds that secretly depend on a prior, unstated desert claim. I am trying to diagnose whether this is a substantive metaphysical disagreement about free will, a normative disagreement about what justifies state-inflicted harm, or a hidden verbal dispute about whether 'moral responsibility' is meant to ground reactive attitudes, license institutional practices, or track some deeper fact about agency. Could you diagnose the structure of the impasse—are they answering different questions under one label, is there an unexamined empirical premise about human psychology or neuroscience doing hidden work, or is the disagreement bedrock, rooted in incompatible intuitions about whether suffering can ever be fair? And given your diagnosis, is there any conceptual distinction—perhaps between accountability as a social practice and desert as a metaphysical property, or between the standards of justification appropriate to criminal law versus clinical intervention—that could actually move the debate forward, or should we conclude that the framework of 'just deserts' is ill-posed and ought to be abandoned?
