I am trying to understand a passage in Locke's Some Thoughts Concerning Education where he advises that parents should reason with children rather than command them through authority or fear, adapting arguments to the child's developing capacity. The difficulty is this: Locke denies innate ideas and treats the mind as originally blank. If reasoning is just the association of ideas derived from experience, why should we expect a very young child to follow a rational argument at all? Does Locke's pedagogical practice quietly presuppose an innate rational faculty that his epistemology rejects? I want to understand whether his education theory contains a hidden commitment to innate reason, and whether his goal is genuine cognitive autonomy or merely a more effective form of epistemic paternalism—shaping beliefs for the child's welfare without truly allowing independent judgment. Can you help me resolve this tension?
