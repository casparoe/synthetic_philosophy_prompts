The white horse dialogue — you know, Gongsun Long's argument that white horse isn't horse — and the School of Names more broadly have been stalled for literally centuries, and now in modern comparative philophy people still split between treating it as a logic puzzle about class membership, a metaphysical point about universals being seperable from particulars, or just a grammatical trick in old Chinese. My working theory is it's not a real disagreement at all but a methodological clash: analytic folks want formal reconstructions with sets and predicates, historians of Chinese thought want to read it in context with other Warring States debates about naming and social order, and neither camp thinks the other is even doing philosophy. Is that right, or is there an actual hidden clash about whether color and shape are ontologically separable? Give me a diagnosis with reasons tied to what people in each camp actually argue, not a survey of every interpertation. And honestly, could anything break the deadlock — a new bamboo-slip find, a formal model that doesn't distort the original grammar, just accepting that the question is badly posed — or is this one of those debates that's stuck for good?
