I've come to hold a hybrid view about Buddhist ethics and consequentialism. I think the doctrine of karma turns it into a kind of indirect consequentialism spread across rebirths: the rightness of an action depends on how it shapes the stream of consciousness toward the cessation of dukkha over many lives, not on any observable outcome now. But since we can't observe those future lives, the only operational guide is cetana — the intentional quality of mind in the present moment — which makes the ethics look like a mental-state deontology in epistomology. So my position is that it's consequentialist in metaphysics but deontological in practice. What would count against this? I want the defeaters sorted: which doctrinal points (say, about the immediacy of karma or the status of the arhat), which conceptual tensions, and which empirical considerations if any, would actually move me? Which ones would a committed defender concede, and which would they dismiss? And be honest — has this hybrid been qualified into unfalsifiability, and is that a problem for a view of this kind?
