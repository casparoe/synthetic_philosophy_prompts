I have no formal training in history, but I have been thinking on my own about whether the divisions we call the Middle Ages, the Renaissance, and the Scientific Revolution are real features of the past or administrative inventions that came later. I have begun to imagine them as a kind of retrospective zoning ordinance applied to centuries: the Middle Ages functioning as a blight designation, the Renaissance as a commercial branding overlay, and the Scientific Revolution as an infrastructure grant awarded to certain neighborhoods while others were left unincorporated. I would like you to write a parody of a municipal planning commission report—approximately 600 words, in deadpan bureaucratic prose—evaluating an application to formally zone the period from roughly 500 to 1700 C.E. The satire should take the constructivist argument about periodization literally: boundary disputes should be handled like property-line adjustments, heritage preservation should conflict with modern development needs, and residents of the Renaissance district should protest that they never applied for the designation. The standard objections to invented periodization—who draws the lines, whose present interests the zones serve, and whether inhabitants experienced these divisions—should be present but never stated outright; a specialist should recognize them, while a general reader should simply find the bureaucracy absurd. Do not cite specific historians, texts, or dates of publication, and do not ask me to provide a reading list; write as though the commission itself is unaware of the scholarly debate it is accidentally reproducing. The piece should be readable to someone with no academic background and should not require outside knowledge to understand the joke.
