I've spent the last several months trying to understand, on my own terms, whether knowing something ordinary—say, that I am holding a ceramic coffee cup—should automatically carry with it knowledge of everything that logically follows, like that I am not holding an invisible-glass cup that only mimics ceramic. I read enough popular philosophy to grasp Nozick's tracking idea: that knowledge requires your belief to be sensitive to the truth, so that if the world were different in the relevant way, you wouldn't still hold the belief. When I try to apply this to my coffee-cup case, I find that the tracking conditions seem to behave differently for the direct belief ("this is ceramic") than for the entailment ("this is not invisible glass"). Among the people I discuss this with—mostly other non-specialists who have read the same introductory texts—the debate has stalled in exactly the same pattern: we all agree on Nozick's formal conditions, we all agree that closure feels right in ordinary reasoning, and we all agree that skeptical possibilities are far-fetched, yet we have not moved an inch on whether Nozick's rejection of closure is a virtue that saves knowledge from skepticism or a reductio that makes tracking theory implausible. Some of us think the stall hides a factual disagreement about how "nearby" a possible world must be to matter; others think it is a disagreement about what counts as a legitimate knowledge-claim versus a mere logical consequence; and one friend insists we are really arguing about whether knowledge aims at ruling out alternatives or at having well-grounded true belief.

Given that diagnosis, I want to understand what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is there a hidden formal question about the modal metric governing sensitivity conditions? Is it that the two sides are answering different questions—one about psychological belief-update, the other about logical entailment—under the single name "epistemic closure"? Or are we facing bedrock intuitions about the value of skeptical immunity that neither side can argue for? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a precise distinction between two kinds of closure, a conceptual clarification about what tracking is meant to capture, or an argument that the question itself is ill-posed?

Please distinguish carefully between what is settled about Nozick's position and what remains genuinely contested among philosophers who work on this, and do not present any one interpretation—especially about whether closure should be preserved or abandoned—as if it were the consensus view.
