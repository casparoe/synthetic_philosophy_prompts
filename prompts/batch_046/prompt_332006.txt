I run a grad seminar on practical reason — about 10 people, 75 minutes. Design an in-class simulation that makes participants feel akrasia not as simple laziness but as hyperbolic discounting that actually warps meta-preferences. Specifically, they should each write a self-binding rule they genuinely endorse, then 10 minutes later face a choice where honoring that rule costs them points but breaking it feels immediately legitimate — and they have to decide whether to defend or abandon it without knowing what others chose. Give me clear rules, assigned roles (maybe one person tracks reversals), a point-based payoff scheme that doesn't need real money, and a timeline. Then a debrief that maps the moment of reversal onto the hyperbolic discount curve, and be honest about what predictably goes wrong the first time (probably people confuse weakness of will with strategic defection) and how to handle it. Also say frankly which feature — maybe the normative authority of prior selves — the game just can't reproduce and you'll have to supply verbally. Keep it runnable, no lecture up front, and don't ask anyone to cite philosophers.
