I have been studying Nāgārjuna's catuskoti and understand that Madhyamaka rejects all four positions regarding any entity's ontological status: that it inherently exists, that it inherently does not exist, that it both does and does not, and that it neither does nor does not. From this I conclude that Madhyamaka is essentially a form of global skepticism or epistemic suspension—since every conceptual stance is negated, no positive claim about reality can survive, and the philosopher's only consistent posture is silence. Yet I also read that emptiness (śūnyatā) is not mere absence but the absence of svabhāva (inherent existence), and that conventional existence (saṃvṛti-sat) remains intact. Where exactly is the confusion in treating the tetralemma as an exhaustive logical taxonomy that eliminates all belief, rather than as a targeted dialectical instrument? Does the fourth corner's rejection differ structurally from the first three, and if Madhyamaka is not skepticism, what distinguishes its rejection of all four positions from a paraconsistent acceptance of contradiction or from Pyrrhonian suspension?
