In a philosophy of science seminar I have been attending, we have been trying to move past the standard toy examples used to distinguish self-sampling from self-indication assumptions, and I would like you to work through a more reflexive framing that my colleagues and I constructed. Begin by recalling the basic mechanics: under self-sampling, an agent reasons as if she were randomly drawn from a reference class of all observers distributed across possible worlds, so that worlds with more observers do not automatically receive higher credence; under self-indication, she weights each world proportionally to its total observer population, which tends to push credence toward high-population worlds. Now apply this to a deliberately nested scenario: an agent knows that there may exist many substrate-independent or simulated copies of herself, and she must decide whether to initiate an expensive, large-scale observation protocol. Crucially, the reference class is not fixed in advance; it is defined partly by whether an observer is currently engaged in this exact kind of anthropic deliberation about whether to launch the protocol. In other words, being a member of the sampling pool is partly constituted by performing the reasoning that selects the sampling rule.

Please explain, without providing bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, how this reflexive framing alters the conditional probabilities that each assumption yields. Specifically, trace how the presumptuous philosopher problem—the concern that self-indication forces an implausibly extreme prior in favor of worlds containing enormous numbers of observers—reappears or transforms when the prior over world-types is itself being evaluated by an agent who is simultaneously one of the observers in the class. Does the circularity collapse one of the two assumptions into incoherence, or does it merely relocate the burden of justification onto the prior distribution over worlds? Furthermore, say whether this kind of reflexivity reveals an asymmetry between self-sampling and self-indication that is hidden in the standard non-reflexive cases, and whether an agent in this position can consistently apply either framework without smuggling in an unstated commitment about which reasoning processes count as legitimate members of the reference class. I am less interested in formal derivation than in a disciplined conceptual map of where each assumption stands once the observer is not outside the pool but inside it, shaping it by her choice of reasoning.
