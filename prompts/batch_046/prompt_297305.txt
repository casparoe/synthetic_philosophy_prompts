I'm a PhD student in philosophy of law and environmental ethics, and I'm helping my department's climate working group draft something practical for the county planning comission. They review infrastructure with hundred-year timelines -- levees, groundwater banking, carbon storage siting -- and they need a decision procedure they can actually run without calling me every time. I was talking about this with my advisor over coffee and she said just operationalize it, so here goes.

I want a reusable checklist: ordered questions, what counts as passing each gate, where the procedure stops with a deny or approve-with-conditions, and explicit flags for where it's values not theory. The framework is intergenerational justice, but with a specific twist: treat the non-identity problem as a procedural blockage. Whenever a proposal changes who will exist in the future -- migration, economic restructuring, fertility effects -- the usual 'do no harm' gate should switch to a 'just savings' gate asking whether we leave adequate capital and institutional capacity, not whether we harm specific people. Apply it to three proposals: a levee protecting current neighborhoods but accelerating erosion 90 miles downshore in ~90 years; a groundwater banking scheme helping current agriculture but locking in depletion; and a carbon storage site with 120-year liability. Include one case where the procedure says yes but it feels wrong, and flag where it breaks and hands off to judgment. Write in American English, lead with your bottom line in the first sentence then reasoning, no preamble, no restating the question, and please don't include bibliographies or citation lists since the board won't be searching databases.
