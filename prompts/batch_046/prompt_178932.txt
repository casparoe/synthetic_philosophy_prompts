Ayer's verificationism doesn't just deny there's an afterlife -- it says the statement 'there is life after death' isn't false, it's literally without cognitive meaning because you can't specify what empirical observation would count for or against it. I want a poem that captures that exact philosophical move, not just generic scepticism about religion.

Could you write a sonnet, 14 lines, mostly iambic pentameter but not rigidly, where the rhyme scheme itself enacts the verificationist filter? The octave should describe the empirical world with tight, closed rhymes (ABAB CDCD). Then at the volta -- line 9 -- the speaker tries to describe a post-mortem consciousness, and the rhyme scheme should start to unravel: slant rhymes, missing rhymes, lines that don't quite land. The sestet should show language losing its grip, not belief being defeated by argument. The idea is that if there's no empirical condition to latch onto, the poetic form can't close either. It should feel precise enough that someone who knows the verificationist tradition wouldn't wince, but also work as a poem for someone who doesn't.
