Please write the opening remarks for a three-day interdisciplinary workshop titled "Conceptual Boundaries Without Translation: Linguistic Relativity and Cross-Disciplinary Understanding." The audience is split evenly between cognitive linguists who take strong Sapir-Whorf effects seriously and philosophers of mind who regard linguistic determinism as incoherent but accept weak, lexical-level relativity. You are the program director—neither a specialist in linguistics nor in philosophy—delivering these remarks to frame the shared problem without resolving it. The philosophical idea must do real structural work: if strong linguistic relativity is correct—if the grammatical categories of a native language genuinely constrain what can be conceptually articulated—then the cross-disciplinary translation that this workshop presupposes is structurally impossible, which makes the gathering self-undermining. Conversely, if only weak relativity holds—if language merely provides different lexical packaging for universal conceptual structures—then the workshop's central theme collapses into a trivial observation about vocabulary rather than deep conceptual architecture, and there is no justification for three days of discussion. Your remarks must navigate this exact tension. They should make the strong-relativist camp feel that their view is being taken seriously as a live option that threatens the coherence of interdisciplinary dialogue, while also making the weak-relativist camp see why reducing the thesis to lexical variation undermines the very purpose of convening. You must not resolve the tension, take sides, or appeal to authority by citing specific scholars or texts; treat these arguments as part of the shared intellectual air in the room. Speak to a mixed audience: precise enough that experts recognize the stakes, accessible enough that graduate students and administrators in attendance are not lost. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. The remarks should run about four minutes when delivered aloud.
