I'm designing a taxonomy for a graduate-level workshop on forecasting and epistemic humility, specifically on how inside-view reasoning and outside-view reference-class reasoning should be combined. I want a decision-tree classification—each branch labeled by the concrete claim or case that splits the positions—rather than a list of named schools. The tree should cover at least these branching questions: First, is there any domain in which inside-view reasoning is irreducible, or should the outside view always dominate? Second, does epistemic modesty require uniformly downgrading one's confidence, or only in domains with a demonstrably poor individual track record? Third, when one applies the outside view to the very selection of one's reference class, does this generate a vicious regress, a benign loop, or a stable stopping point—and if a stopping point, is it justified by inside-view judgment? Fourth, is the inside view best treated as noisy evidence about the same fact the outside view addresses, or as a categorically different kind of input? Fifth, does "recursive modesty"—applying modesty to one's own calibration of modesty—collapse into global skepticism, or can it be stabilized by a second-order rule? At each leaf, provide either a standard label (e.g., "domain-sensitive dualist," "uniform modestist," "recursive stabilist") or mark it "unnamed," and then state in one sentence the position's most awkward practical commitment. Identify any empty combinations in the logical space and briefly diagnose whether they are incoherent, self-defeating, or simply unexplored. Flag any two positions that the tree cannot cleanly separate and explain why they converge. The audience is philosophically literate but not specialists in epistemology; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
