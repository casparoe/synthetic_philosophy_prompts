Jordan kicked it off saying Neurath's ISOTYPE system is basically Wittgenstein's picture theory wearing a different hat. The symbols map onto reality through direct structural resemblance, like a map to terrain. So even though Neurath talks big about anti-metaphysical physicalism, he's smuggling in a correspondance theory of truth through the back door.

Riley said thats unfair. Neurath explicitly rejected the idea that language mirrors reality. His icons are conventional coordination tools, like traffic signs. There's no deep metaphysical correspondence — they're practical, not pictoral in Wittgenstein's Tractatus sense.

Jordan came back: then why did Neurath lean so hard on protocol sentences anchored in direct physical observation? If the pictures are purely conventional, those anchors dont do any real work. You end up with a coherentist web that never actually touches the world, which clashes with his own physicalism.

Riley replied that the protocol sentences arent private mental reports — Neurath hated that Carnap-style move. They're interubjective reports made in physicalist language. So they do connect to the world, just not through some magical resemblance relation. The pictures just organize those reports efficiently.

Jordan then said: okay, but if the pictures organize reports without resembling what they organize, then calling it a "picture language" is misleading. And if they do organize by resemblance, you're back to correspondence. He thinks Neurath is stuck between pure convention and metaphysical correspondence with no real middle ground.

Riley answered that there is a middle option — structural isomorphism. Think of a graph where nodes map to objects not because they look alike, but because the relations among nodes match the relations among objects. That's different from Wittgenstein's logical-form picture theory, which requires shared logical form with reality. Neurath's isomorphism is practical and always revisibel.

Jordan replied: but if it's revisibel, then the protocol sentences grounding it are revisibel too. So there's no foundation. That's fine for coherentism, but Neurath said some protocol sentences are "not further reducible." That sounds like foundationalism, not just a methodalogical stopping point.

Riley said he meant not reducible in practice, not in principle. It's a practical halt, not an epistemological bedrock.
