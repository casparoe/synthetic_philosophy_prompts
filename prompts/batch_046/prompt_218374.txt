I've been trying to think through whether Ubuntu-style relational conceptions of personhood can be scaled into a normative framework that handles hybrid cognitive systems — research collectives with heavy AI integration, synthetic agents embedded in social networks, or future digital descendants with discontinuous memory traces — without collapsing into either liberal individualism or diffuse collectivism. Before I try to formalize anything, I want the full desiderata list: what must a genuinely relational theory of personhood actually get right to be adequate for these cases?

Specifically, I need the requirements with the considerations behind each:
- Which ontological and moral cases must it handle without collapsing distinctions? (Solitary human, tightly coupled human-AI dyad, diffuse collective with no single controller, synthetic agent with discontinuous identity over time.)
- Which distinctions must it preserve? (Having personhood-status versus having it to a degree; being relationally constituted versus merely causally dependent; being recognized by others versus actually standing in the relevant relations.)
- Which empirical results from cognitive science, biology, or social psychology can it not contradict if it wants to remain credible?
- Is there a hidden joint unsatisfiability? For example, if reciprocal recognition is required, does that necessarily exclude synthetic agents lacking phenomenological access? If biological embeddedness is required, does the framework become useless for AI ethics?

Please mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences, identify the single desideratum that does the most work in ruling out popular competitors, and say whether the list reveals a contradiction that makes the scaling project impossible. A table is fine, but I care most about the reasoning behind each requirement and whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable. I'm writing this for a long-form piece and want to focus on the two or three requirements that actually determine whether the project succeeds.
