Write an essay assessing whether the disruptions conventionally labeled the Late Bronze Age collapse, the Classic Maya political fragmentation, and the Rapa Nui ecological transformation constitute genuine societal “collapse” or merely complex institutional change. Apply the distinction between institutional decoupling—when centralized formal structures dissolve while local practices and intergenerational knowledge transmission persist—and demographic discontinuity—when population levels and cultural transmission break sharply—to evaluate each case. Consider whether “collapse” tracks an objective failure mode or is instead an observer-dependent category that reflects modern anxieties about state capacity and complexity. Defend a specific verdict about whether societies fail in some meaningful sense or only change form, and explain whether treating collapse as a graded, multidimensional phenomenon resolves the debate or merely relocates it.
