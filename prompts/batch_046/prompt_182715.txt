I'm taking a course on philosophy of law and have been struck by a pattern in administrative debates that doesn't seem to match the textbook treatments of vagueness. In discussions about welfare eligibility thresholds, disability certification criteria, and environmental regulatory limits, participants routinely concede that the underlying property — financial need, functional impairment, ecological harm — is genuinely gradational and lacks any metaphysically privileged cutoff. They explicitly reject reasoning that would pretend a sharp boundary exists in nature. Yet once a specific administrative number is adopted — for instance, a particular income-to-needs ratio or a precise decibel level — they defend that exact figure with an intensity that looks like more than mere bureaucratic inertia. If you propose shifting the line by a modest amount, they respond as if a substantive wrong has been done, not as if one conventional marker has been swapped for another equally conventional one. At the same time, they continue to maintain that the boundary is "just practical" and that nearby values would be defensible in principle. I have observed this in recent debates over poverty-line adjustments and noise-abatement rules, where experts simultaneously affirm the absence of a natural joint and treat the current regulatory joint as uniquely legitimate.

Is there an established philosophical or social-scientific concept that captures this specific stance? I am not looking for a general discussion of vagueness, the sorites paradox, or the legal necessity of bright-line rules. Rather, I want to know whether anyone has named the particular attitude of denying that a boundary is ontologically real while still treating an arbitrarily selected instance of it as specially authoritative — not merely convenient, but correct in some stronger sense. If such a concept exists, how precisely does it differ from ordinary institutional stickiness, from a tacit commitment to an imprecise real property, or from straightforward hypocrisy? And if nothing in the literature fits exactly, can you coin a precise term, define its boundaries, and distinguish it from adjacent phenomena?

Please take a committed position: either identify the closest established concept and argue rigorously for why it does or does not cover this case, explaining the exact points of fit and misfit; or maintain that the phenomenon is genuinely unnamed and propose a rigorous definition with clear boundary conditions. Do not merely list possibilities — defend one interpretation.
