As a retired experimental biologist who has recently turned to philosophy of science, I am trying to determine whether life is best understood through organizational closure or through evolutionary dynamics, and I need a rigorous conceptual comparison rather than a popular summary. Please adopt the role of a professional philosopher of biology with expertise in conceptual analysis and the history of biological definitions. Before giving your final answer, take a moment to brainstorm the core ontological commitments of each framework and list the borderline cases that would most sharply distinguish them.

Specifically, compare the autopoietic theory of life—according to which a living system is a network of production processes that continuously regenerate both the components and the boundary that realizes the network—with the evolutionary-functional theory, which treats life as a population of entities exhibiting heredity, variation, and differential reproduction, with metabolism regarded as secondary to informational replication. In your comparison, do not merely summarize the two positions; analyze how each treats three specific borderline scenarios: (1) a synthetic lipid-vesicle protocell that maintains chemical homeostasis through non-enzymatic feedback loops but contains no nucleic acids and shows no heritable variation; (2) a self-replicating digital organism in a simulated environment that evolves by mutation and selection but possesses no physical metabolism or material boundary; and (3) the hypothetical case of a dormant bacterial spore that has suspended all metabolic activity for centuries yet retains the intact potential to resume autopoiesis and reproduction upon rehydration.

For each scenario, indicate which theory grants the status of being alive, which withholds it, and whether that judgment reveals a conceptual strength or a hidden cost. Then address the deeper disagreement: does the autopoietic view implicitly commit to a process ontology in which life is a particular kind of organizational dynamics rather than a substance kind, while the evolutionary-functional view commits to an informational ontology in which life is essentially a pattern of replicators? Conclude by arguing which framework better accommodates the intuition that life is a unified natural kind rather than a heterogeneous cluster, and explain what your choice implies for the search criteria in astrobiology. Do not cite specific literature, provide a bibliography, or rely on named texts; reason from the conceptual structures themselves. Think step by step, and be precise about where each theory must draw its lines.
