I have come to accept, at least provisionally and for the architecture of a novel I am writing, a stringent reading of Schopenhauer's metaphysics of music: that music is not a representational art among others but the direct objectification of the Will itself, operating outside the principle of sufficient reason and therefore capable of expressing metaphysical states—specifically, the suspension of individuated consciousness without any corresponding attenuation of affective intensity—that narrative or visual art can only approximate through symbolic mediation. On this view, there are structural features of musical experience—say, the co-presence of harmonic resolution and temporal deferral—that are not merely different in degree from the representational strategies available to prose fiction, but categorically unavailable to any medium bound to the forms of space, time, and causality. What would have to be true for this position to be false? I want an honest assessment of its exposure: which empirical findings in neuroscience or cognitive psychology would genuinely undermine the metaphysical claim rather than merely reduce it to mechanism; which intuitions about culturally specific musical meaning or about the narrative intelligibility of instrumental works would count as decisive rather than merely awkward; what formal results about non-representational art in general, or about the limits of analogical reasoning from acoustic structure to metaphysical will, would cause the view to collapse into conventional aesthetic formalism; and whether there exist defensible qualifications that would insulate the position from every conceivable defeater—rendering it unfalsifiable rather than robust. Rank these potential defeaters from fatal to merely uncomfortable, and indicate which ones a committed defender of the view would themselves concede as genuinely counting against it. I am particularly interested in whether the position risks anything at all, or whether it has been qualified into safety.
