I am drafting a short fiction that turns on a conceptual knot in Marx's account of exploitation and alienation. If exploitation is defined as the extraction of surplus value from living labour, what becomes of alienation—specifically, estrangement from one's essential productive nature—once automation has eliminated wage labour and surplus is generated by capital alone? I want a narrative, not an analytical essay, that embodies this problem through a single character's ordinary experience in a post-labour society. The character should feel a distinct form of estrangement despite having no employer to exploit her; let the tension emerge through concrete scene and image rather than exposition. Write in polished literary prose, in British English, and keep the piece to roughly three hundred words.
