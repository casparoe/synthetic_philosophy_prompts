Consider an engineered stress-test for theories of personal survival that modifies the standard teletransporter scenario into what I will call the gradient-reintegration protocol. In this protocol, a continuous scanning beam generates a linear spatial array of replicas extending away from the original location; psychological fidelity—encompassing memory, personality structure, and intentional content—degrades continuously from near-perfect replication at the origin to null at the distal end, with no discrete boundary between copy and non-copy. Rather than destroying the original, the protocol gradually replaces the original brain tissue with synthetic substrate that re-integrates information from the replicas in reverse spatial order over a 24-hour cycle, leaving a single biological body with reintegrated mental content but no precise moment of death, duplication, or fusion.

Against this protocol, rank the following four accounts of what matters in survival, assigning each both a tier and a score. Use these tiers: Tier 1 delivers a clear, non-arbitrary verdict about whether the original survives, is replaced, or is distributed across the gradient; Tier 2 delivers a verdict only by introducing an auxiliary assumption that is not independently motivated; Tier 3 yields contradiction, silence, or a verdict so fragmented that it fails to answer the question. Use a score from 1 to 10 measuring the internal coherence of that verdict, where 10 indicates full coherence and 1 indicates incoherence. The four accounts are: (1) pure psychological continuity with any causal pathway permitted and branching tolerated; (2) psychological continuity constrained by a strict no-branching clause; (3) biological continuity of the living human organism regardless of mental content; and (4) a threshold hybrid requiring both uninterrupted spatiotemporal continuity of the original brain and at least 90 percent psychological overlap with the pre-scan state.

Be committed: do not present the four as equally plausible or merely enumerate considerations for each. State definitively which tier and score each account receives, justify each assignment in one to two sentences that reference specific features of the gradient-reintegration protocol, and then conclude with a single sentence identifying which account, if any, best survives this stress test and stating whether the protocol exposes a genuine conceptual gap in our notion of survival or only produces an artificial edge case that no well-formed theory should be expected to accommodate.
