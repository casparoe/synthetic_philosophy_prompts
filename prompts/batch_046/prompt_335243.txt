I lean toward a Dennett-style view: the experience is not stored like a recording but constructed in multiple drafts near awakening, with sleep-talk and memory traces being part of the drafting process rather than proof of prior phenomenology. To push past the standard examples, consider this hypothetical case: a subject in a forced-awakening protocol denies any experience during thirty minutes of REM, yet when shown a transcript of her own sleep-talk describing a garden, she freely recalls the scene in detail.
