Me and my co-curator are fighting over one piece in our tiny shared gallery—it's a very explicit video installation that loops a controversial performance piece. I run Room A, she runs Room B, and we agreed each of us has final say over what hangs in our own space; there's also a storage closet. I personally think the work is important and want it shown—I'd be fine with it in Room A, but honestly I'd rather she be forced to sit with it in Room B since she usually dodges anything challenging. She thinks its indecent and wants it locked away, though she admits that if it has to be displayed somewhere she'd rather it be in her room where she can frame it critically, rather than in mine where it gets celebrated. So we both actually agree that Room B with the piece is better than Room A with the piece. But here's the rub: she controls Room B and can reject it, I control Room A and can accept it, so it ends up in Room A—the outcome we both rank worst. She says my willingness to hang it in Room A proves I'm not actually respecting her autonomy over Room B, since I'm implicitly trying to influence her environment by making my space more provacative. I think I'm just exercising my own rights. Something about this feels structurally wrong—like there's a hidden contradiction between giving us veto over our own rooms and reaching the collective result we both prefer. Is her charge of hypocrisy fair, and what's the philosophical problem hiding in here?
