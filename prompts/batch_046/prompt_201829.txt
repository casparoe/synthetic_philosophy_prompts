I am in Year 13 and help facilitate a philosophy reading group at my sixth-form college. We are about twelve students, mostly aged seventeen or eighteen, and we meet for a single seventy-five-minute session. We have been working through ideas around social coordination, and I would like an in-class activity that makes us feel the force of David Lewis’s account of convention—particularly a feature that standard introductions often gloss over: that a convention can become sticky and self-enforcing not because anyone actively prefers it, but because each participant’s expectation of others’ expectations turns an arbitrary equilibrium into something that feels like an implicit obligation, even after the original reason for its salience has vanished.

Please design a structured simulation or game for our group. The rules and incentives should be arranged so that we discover this stickiness ourselves: we should establish a coordination pattern through an early, seemingly incidental round, then face a situation where that pattern is clearly suboptimal, and find that deviating from it feels wrong in a way that pure self-interest cannot explain—before anyone names what is happening.

The deliverable should include clear rules and a non-monetary payoff scheme; any roles or materials needed; a timeline fitting seventy-five minutes; and instructions for how to run the first round so that the arbitrary convention takes root without explicit agreement. There should be no lecture beforehand, and the concept should not be named until the debrief.

In the debrief, please map specific moments in the activity onto parts of Lewis’s framework—the coordination problem, the arbitrary equilibrium, common knowledge of expectations, and the role of precedent—explaining exactly what each moment illustrates. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this, for instance whether students will try to negotiate explicitly or fail to notice the shift in salience, and how to handle it without breaking the effect. Finally, be explicit about which feature of Lewis’s analysis, perhaps the infinite regress of expectations or the distinction between convention and social contract, the activity cannot reproduce on its own, and what I must supply as facilitator to make that feature present.

The activity should not depend on any obscure texts or citations; please avoid bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for quotations. I want something that works with what we have already discussed in general terms about coordination and norms.
