I am a high school student writing an essay for my philosophy of science elective. I want to examine what I will call the erased-trace problem: imagine a synthetic crystal engineered to be physically and chemically indistinguishable from a naturally formed mineral, with every record of its artificial origin permanently destroyed so that the provenance is in principle inaccessible. The central controversy is whether this object belongs to the same ontological kind as its natural counterpart, or whether the irreversible inaccessibility of its origin creates a genuine ontological underdetermination rather than a merely epistemic gap. Write an essay that weighs the structural-essentialist view—that internal constitution alone fixes kind-membership—against the provenance-essentialist view that causal history is constitutive and its absence leaves the identity genuinely open. Reach and defend a clear conclusion. The argument should be precise but accessible, should not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts, and should not depend on any external research.
