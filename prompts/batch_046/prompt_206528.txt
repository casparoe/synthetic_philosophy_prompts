My rough notes: the usual explanations (Aristotle's catharsis, the safety of fiction, emotional practice) all seem to treat the sadness as a kind of cost we pay for some other benefit. I'm wondering if tragedy works something like that. So we don't enjoy tragedy despite the sadness — we enjoy it because the sadness is doing functional work, like turning up gain on a receptor.

This might also explain why real-world suffering doesn't produce the same effect: there's no framing, no predictability, so the noise drowns everything out instead of boosting it. But I'm worried I'm just importing a physics analogy where it doesn't belong. So my questions:
