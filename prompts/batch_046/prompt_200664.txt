Before I evaluate any further proposals in systematics, write the complete desiderata list that an adequate theory of species must satisfy if it is to serve simultaneously as an evolutionary unit and as a taxonomic rank spanning sexually reproducing metazoans, clonally reproducing microbial lineages, and hybridizing plant groups with partial gene flow. For each requirement, specify the biological case or empirical finding that motivates it—covering ring species, cryptic genomic divergence without morphological change, reticulate evolution and horizontal gene transfer that undermine strict tree-like divergence metrics, and hybrid zones in which ecological niche coherence persists despite measurable gene flow—state whether it is non-negotiable or reflects a camp-specific preference (such as reproductive isolation, monophyly, morphological diagnosability, or ecological niche coherence), and explain the reasoning behind that classification. Then identify whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given current evolutionary biology, describing the specific contradiction. Next, indicate which single requirement, if enforced without domain-specific exemptions, eliminates the greatest number of mainstream concepts—specifically the Biological Species Concept, the Phylogenetic Species Concept, the Morphological Species Concept, the General Lineage Concept, and niche-based ecological approaches. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on evaluation; provide only a brief scorecard for the named concepts. The specification should treat prokaryotic and asexual lineages as falling within the theory's direct scope, not as special cases relegated to auxiliary rules.
