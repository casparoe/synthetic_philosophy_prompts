So I've been bought into Sen's argument that famines are never natural disasters but always political crimes — entitlement collapse, bad distrubution, goverment failure. His big cases are Bengal 1943, the Irish famine, and the Great Leap Forward, where he says food was available but people starved because of who had access and what the state did or didn't do. The strong version is that with working democracy and a free press, mass famine is basically impossible, and authoritarian famines are still about policy not scarcity. But I keep wondering about a synchronized multi-breadbasket failure where global trade freezes and every region loses harvest at once — could even perfect governance stop mass death then? I want to play it straight: what would actually have to be true for Sen's view to be wrong? Which assumptions could fail, what empirical case would be decisive vs merely awkward, and is there any live escape route for the natural disaster framing? Would Sen's defenders concede a scenario where entitlements are intact but calories just don't exist? What does the view predict that a mostly-natural rival doesn't? And is the position well-insulated — is that strength or unfalsafiable risk? Talk to me like a grad student, not like you're writing for a journal.
