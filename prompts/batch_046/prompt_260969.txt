I'm on our hospital's paliative ethics committee. Next Thursday we're voting on a proposed asymbolia pathway for post-op patients with accidental anterior cingulate damage— they report pain scores of 8/10 but show no aversive behavior, don't ask for relief, and in one case the patient described the feeling as vivid but not unwelcome. The proposal is to stop automatic opioid escalation and instead treat only on evidence of suffering, not just nociceptive report. Some members argue this abandons patients whose pain is bad even without suffering; others say forcing meds on someone with no aversion is pure paternalism. There's also a staff neurologist pushing to extend the pathway to patients with opioid-induced dissociative analgesia, which muddies whether we're treating brain damage or a drug state. I need a memo recommending approve, reject, or approve-with-conditions, with a concrete timeline and decision criterias, and it needs to honestly engage the strongest argument that pain's badness is intrinsic to its sensory quality, not derivative of aversion. Keep it practical—no bibliography requests, this is for a vote.
