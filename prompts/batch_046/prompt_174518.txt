I've been thinking about Machiavelli's claim that fortune behaves like a violent river and that virtù is the capacity to build dikes against it. Most popular explanations just say leaders need both luck and skill, but I want to push past that. Specifically, I wonder whether virtù can belong to a collective body like a council, or whether Machiavelli limits it to a single prince. I also wonder how his framework applies when the flood is not war or weather but an unprecedented technological failure—say, a democratic council facing a sudden, total energy-grid collapse caused by an unexpected geomagnetic event. The council has emergency protocols, but they are built for known risks and fail here. Some members urge radical improvisation; others insist on following the protocols and waiting for outside help.
