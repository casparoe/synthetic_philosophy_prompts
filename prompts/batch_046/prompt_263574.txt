As a journalist writing on philosophy of science and ideas, I have been observing a recurring argumentative structure in debates about Ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood, and I am trying to determine whether scholars have given it a specific name. The structure unfolds as follows: a speaker begins by rejecting intrinsic-property essentialism, insisting that personhood is constituted entirely by reciprocal recognition and embeddedness in communal relationships — the familiar claim that a person is a person through other people. Then, confronted with edge cases — an individual isolated physically but retaining internal representations of the community, a refugee sustained only by distant digital mediation, or an artificial agent engaged in asynchronous interaction — the speaker introduces what they term a density threshold or reciprocity floor. They maintain that below this floor the relational web is too attenuated to constitute full personhood, yet they deny that this floor is an essentialist criterion imported from outside the relational framework, insisting instead that it emerges organically from the community's own practices of acknowledgment. Consequently, the framework introduced to eliminate sharp, intrinsic boundaries ends up defending intuitively calibrated boundaries around who counts as a full person, with the boundary's precise location always tracking pre-theoretical judgments rather than being derived from the relational structure itself. I have noticed this pattern in discussions of cognitive decline, displacement, and synthetic social agents. Is there an established concept in the literature on Ubuntu ethics, social ontology, or relational identity that captures this exact maneuver — not relational personhood itself, not ordinary parochialism, but the specific deployment of a purportedly relational gatekeeper that functions as a hidden essentialist filter? If such a concept exists, how does it differ from straightforward ad hoc qualification and from pure relational continuity? If no established term covers it precisely, I would like a rigorous definition that distinguishes it from explicit essentialism on one side and from unqualified relationalism on the other, together with an account of its conceptual boundaries and failure conditions.
