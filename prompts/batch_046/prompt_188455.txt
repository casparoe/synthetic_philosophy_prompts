i sit on a land-use appeals board for a mid-size city, we meet every other tuesday and have about 90 minutes per case. lately we're stuck on disputes where developers and neighbors both claim values that 'cant be priced' -- historic fabric vs climate urgency, view preservation vs energy security, etc. I need a reusable decision proceedure my board can run in real time (no economists on staff, chair has to sign off) to figure out when two values are genuinely incommensurable in the philosophical sense, versus when people are just being stubborn or smuggling a hidden metric inside value-language. Make it a flowchart in words: ordered tests, what counts as a pass/fail at each, where it stops with 'this is actually tradeable after all' or 'this is a hard choice requiring a value judgment not a calculation', and the exact point where human judgment takes over and who signs. Include three or four diagnostic sentences the board can apply directly to arguments in the file. Then apply it to three cases: (1) tearing down a 1920s brick library for net-zero affordable housing, (2) blocking a ridgeline wind farm because locals say the view has 'irreplaceable aesthetic-spiritual value', and (3) one where the proceedure gives an answer the chair will hate -- say, approving a data center that destroys a small wetland but creates jobs, where the values aren't incommensurable but the trade-off is brutal and the board still has to vote yes on technical grounds. Be explicit about what the proceedure can't do, especially if parties learn the language and game the tests. Don't give me a bibliography or cite philosophers by page number; I just need the logic kept intact. Also flag the case where values are 'on a par' in Ruth Chang's sense but the board still has to pick one, because that's where people usually get confused.
