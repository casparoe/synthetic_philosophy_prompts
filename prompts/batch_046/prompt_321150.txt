For a 90-min workshop with ~12 grad students in practical ethics, design a structured game that makes moral particularism feel like live pressure, not just "context matters." Use moral holism—the idea that a feature like breaking a promise can be a reason for in one case and agianst in another, with no underlying rule explaining the flip. Give them 8 micro-cases on cards (2-3 sentences each) and have them assign feature-weights round by round. The payoff rules reward consistency across cases, but the cases are built so consistency forces them to ignore morally salient differences. Give roles: half are "generalists" who must defend a written principle, half are "case judges" who can veto if applying the principle feels wrong. Timeline: 15 min setup, 40 min rounds, 20 min debrief, 15 min meta-talk. In the debrief map the cracks to particularist claims about reason-variability, and be frank that the activity can't reproduce the stronger claim that reasons are genuinely incomparable rather than just hard to weigh. Don't name philosophers—let the principle crack on its own. I think this works but maybe the veto power is too strong?
