Draft a short policy memo for a university Committee on Student Welfare and Academic Policy recommending whether to approve, reject, or conditionally adopt a proposed mandatory undergraduate program, "Radical Transparency," which requires students to systematically identify and abandon "productive illusions" about career success, romantic attachment, and personal merit in order to reduce anxiety arising from false expectation. In the reasoning, apply Émilie du Châtelet's specific conditions for happiness: that it depends on sustained intellectual study and active passion rather than idle contemplation, that certain illusions are not mere errors but necessary supports for motivation and emotional stability, and that stripping them away without replacing them with engaging work produces melancholy rather than well-being. Give an honest account of the strongest argument for approval—that removing false beliefs is an ethical obligation to prevent harm, and that du Châtelet's framework improperly privileges comfort over truth—then state a clear bottom-line recommendation with concrete, actionable conditions (for example, voluntary enrollment, mandatory pairing with structured intellectual projects, or an opt-out clause). The memo should be written as a formal document the committee could vote on.
