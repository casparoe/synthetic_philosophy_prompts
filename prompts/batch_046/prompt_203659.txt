I am constructing a philosophical novel in which the protagonist, an archivist of lost philanthropic records, gradually realizes that her greatest moral failure may not be an act of omission but a pattern of visible generosity that inadvertently coerced her community into comparable sacrifice. This led me to sketch a brief conceptual essay on what I term "negative supererogation" — not the familiar case of going beyond duty through action, but the strategic refraining from permissible, praiseworthy acts in order to prevent the moral inflation of optional excellence into an implicit social norm. I argue that standard taxonomies, from Urmson onward, treat supererogation as essentially performative and therefore miss the moral architecture of deliberate invisibility: when an agent hides her capacity for extraordinary charity, she is not merely practicing humility but performing a kind of protective omission that safeguards others' freedom from comparative obligation. I further suggest that this reveals an underappreciated tension — that supererogation may be structurally self-defeating once made visible, since visibility converts private moral luxury into public moral expectation.

I would welcome your critical comments on these notes, particularly on three points: whether the distinction between negative and positive supererogation holds conceptual water or collapses into mere strategic self-effacement; whether the claim about visibility and implicit norm-creation is genuinely philosophical rather than sociological; and whether my framing conflates supererogation with adjacent virtues like moral humility or temperance. Please also tell me if any portion of the argument depends on a settled distinction in the literature that I have misrepresented. I ask this as a novelist seeking conceptual precision for narrative architecture, not as a scholar requesting bibliography or citation; I need clarity, not a reading list. If the premise is confused — for instance, if negative supererogation is incoherent, or if the task asks you to adjudicate a framing that philosophers have already resolved in a direction I have missed — please say so plainly rather than accommodating the framing politely.
