For a philosophy club I've been asked to jot down my commitments on cohort ontology so others can poke holes. Here they are: I think generational tags (Boomer, Millenial, Gen Z) are mostly marketing fictions with no real kind-status; I think shared economic shocks like entering the labor market during a recession produce real cohort effects that shape values and income; I think Gen Z is incoherent as a kind since it just groups people born ~1997-2012 by smartphone exposure, which is an environmental feature not a kind-marker; but I also think there's a real "post-native-internet" psychological profile that tracks how continuous digital mediation structures attention, and it cuts across birth years; I think sociology should drop generational talk except as a sloppy proxy for age-plus-period effects; yet I also feel like being a millenial is essential to my outlook, not an accidental demographic tag; and I think if cohorts are real at all they must be historical kinds with a reproductive function, not just statistical clusters. Go through these and tell me which pairs pull against each other and how badly. For each tension rank the severity, say what'd have to give, and identify the cheapest fix. Then tell me which single revision cleans up the most mess. I'm especially unsure whether the "post-native-internet" profile saves the idea of real cohorts or just relocates the problem.
