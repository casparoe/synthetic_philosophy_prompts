(a) I accept parity-based arguments for the extended mind: if an external process plays the same functional role as an internal cognitive one, it counts as part of cognition, irrespective of whether it lies inside the skull.

(b) I hold that genuinely cognitive extension requires direct availability of representational content — the agent must be able to access and deploy that content without substantive interpretive mediation.

(c) I think that large language models used as fluent writing aids can, in principle, form part of a user's extended cognitive system, provided the interaction is rapid, automatically endorsed, and tightly integrated with the user's reasoning.

(d) I deny that algorithmically curated information feeds — for example, personalised news aggregators whose ranking logic is opaque to the user — ever constitute part of that user's mind, precisely because they violate direct availability.

(e) I maintain that no social institution or collective practice can be part of an individual mind, since cognitive extension requires spatiotemporal contiguity with a single agent's bodily processes.

(f) I also think that predictive processing models show internal cognition is already distributed across neural subsystems in a way that renders the inside-versus-outside boundary metaphysically shallow, though not causally irrelevant.

Please tell me which commitments are in genuine tension, which tensions are merely apparent and dissolve on inspection, and what is the least costly revision to restore consistency. Rank the tensions by severity. Don't just lay out the options — defend a specific verdict about which commitment should be revised or qualified, and spell out exactly what the corrected package looks like. Write in a formal, analytical tone.
