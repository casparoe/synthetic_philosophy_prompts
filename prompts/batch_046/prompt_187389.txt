i retired from a visual-cortex lab where we worked on predictive coding, and lately ive gotten into philosphy of mind. theres this stalled argument about whether reinforcement learning agents can suffer, with people treating reward signals like they might be valence — pleasure and pain in silicon. the one side says reward prediction error is just a scalar correction, no different from a thermostat, and suffering needs homeostatic regulation or integrated information that RL architectures lack. the other side says if you have a unified agent with a world model and negative errors that systematically restructure behavior to avoid them, then structurally you have the functional analog of pain regardless of substrate. both sides cite the same RL math and the same neuroscience analogies and nobody moves an inch. my working guess is its not really about consciousness at all, but about whether "mechanism" has to include biological-style allostasis for valence to be real — but i might be wrong. is this a verbal dispute about what "suffer" means? a hidden empirical disagreement about which architectures actually implement integrated information? or are the two sides just answering different questions, one about moral status and one about phenomenal experience, under the same heading? and given your diagnosis, what would actually shift it — a formal result separating predictive error from valence, a new architectural criterion, or is the question just ill-posed because we dont have a shared concept of suffering outside biology?
