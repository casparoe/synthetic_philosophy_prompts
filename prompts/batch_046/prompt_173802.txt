I hold that Nāgārjuna's tetralemma should be read as a progressive exhaustion of logical positions rather than a simultaneous four-cornered denial: each corner—affirmation, negation, both, neither—is shown to presuppose svabhāva (inherent existence), and the fourth corner fails not because it is false but because it still operates within the binary it claims to transcend. On this view, emptiness is not a fifth metaphysical claim but the demonstrated instability of position-taking itself. I also think this entails that formalizing the tetralemma in multi-valued or paraconsistent logic misses its point, since such formalization assigns stable semantic values to corners that the text intends to destabilize performatively rather than deny propositionally. What would genuinely count against this reading? Please identify the observations, textual patterns, formal results, or rival interpretations that should move me, sorted by how decisive they would be; say what this view predicts that rival readings do not; note what its own defenders would concede as damaging; and assess whether it has been insulated into unfalsifiability—say, by treating any successful formalization as itself a reification—together with whether that insulation is a philosophical virtue or a defect.
