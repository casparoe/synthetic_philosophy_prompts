I'm working through some ideas about metaphysical grounding for a seminar I'm running, and I've built an argument that seems to prove there can be no fundamental entities whatsoever—not quantum fields, not bare particulars, not even spacetime points. Each premise feels independently plausible, but the conclusion strikes me as clearly too strong, and I cannot pinpoint exactly where the reasoning slips. Here is the chain as I have it.

(1) If X is metaphysically fundamental, then nothing other than X belongs among the ontological grounds of X itself—that is, X's existence and nature depend on nothing outside X. (2) Take any candidate fundamental entity E, such as a particular field excitation or a bare particular. (3) E necessarily bears relational properties—spatiotemporal location, causal relations, structural relations to other fields—none of which are identical to E. (4) Those relational properties are grounded in E together with the other relata they involve, and they contribute to E's identity conditions: what makes this excitation this particular excitation rather than another includes its relations. (5) Therefore, entities other than E are among the grounds that determine what E is, so something outside E belongs to E's ontological grounds. (6) From (1) and (5), no candidate E can be fundamental. (7) Hence, either grounding descends infinitely with no bottom level, or it is circular; in neither case is there a genuinely fundamental stratum.

If I have to choose, I would rather keep (1), since it seems to express exactly what fundamentality means, and I am reluctant to deny (3), because relational properties do appear to depend on their relata. My suspicion falls on (4), which seems to conflate the grounds of a relational property with the grounds of the entity that bears it. But if I reject (4) and insist that an entity's identity conditions are entirely internal, I worry I am committing myself to a kind of Leibnizian internalism that feels costly. Where does the argument actually go wrong—whether in a hidden equivocation between grounding an entity and grounding its properties, a scope mistake about what counts as "among X's grounds," or somewhere else? And if the repair requires giving up one of the premises I want to keep, which surrender is least damaging?
