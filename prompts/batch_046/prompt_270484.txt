I am a sixth-form student taking an introductory philosophy course, and after our lesson on epistemic justification two classmates engaged in a sharp debate about the regress problem. The first, who leans towards foundationalism, argued that (1) the only way to halt the regress is to identify basic beliefs—such as immediate perceptual experiences—that justify themselves without further support; without these foundations, he claimed, justification never gets off the ground and we are left with scepticism. (2) The second classmate replied that calling a belief 'self-justifying' is merely circular reasoning in disguise; she claimed that foundationalists secretly depend on coherence with other beliefs about the reliability of perception, meaning they have not truly stopped the regress but have only hidden it inside an unexamined assumption. (3) He then countered that coherence alone cannot distinguish a well-supported scientific theory from an internally consistent delusion; without foundations anchored in direct experience, coherence is compatible with complete falsehood, which is precisely why foundationalism remains indispensable. (4) She conceded that pure coherence requires some constraint, but proposed that infinitism actually resolves the difficulty more elegantly: justification consists of an infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons that are available on demand, which avoids both arbitrary foundations and circularity. Because one only needs to supply reasons when actually challenged, she added, the chain never needs to be completed in practice, making the view entirely manageable.

Please adjudicate these four moves one by one: for each, indicate whether it is clearly mistaken, partially correct, or clearly right, and briefly justify your rating. Then say which party, if either, has the stronger overall position, and identify anything important that both are missing—particularly whether infinitism genuinely escapes the objections raised, or whether the foundationalist's appeal to experience succeeds without begging the question.
