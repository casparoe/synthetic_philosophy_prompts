I've bought into the idea that the 'Middle Ages', 'Renaissance', and 'Scientific Revolution' are mostly retroactive fictions cooked up by 19th-century historians — think Burckhart and Koyre. What would actualy count against that? Not just "people back then felt different", but what kind of archival or conceptual evidence would show these labels track real discontinuities? I'm especialy curious about cases where institutional practices stay continuous but the framing still insists on a break — say, Venetian workshop logs from 1450 using the same mechanics vocabulary as 1250 scholastics. How exposed is this anti-periodization view, and which defeaters would its defenders have to concede?
