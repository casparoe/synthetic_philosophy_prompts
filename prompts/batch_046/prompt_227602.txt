After a year of navigating my parent’s progressive cognitive disorientation, I have become personally preoccupied with the boundary Wittgenstein marks in On Certainty between ordinary mistakes within a belief-system and failures of the hinges that make the system possible—especially when the disorientation is gradual and non-propositional rather than a single falsified claim. This has led me to ask where hinge epistemology currently stands. Among the live debates—whether hinge commitments are genuinely propositional or are better understood as enacted practical orientations; how collective hinges function in institutions as opposed to individuals; whether there is a defensible account of hinge revision that avoids both global skepticism and arbitrary conservatism; and how hinge failure should be conceptualized in cases of gradual cognitive decline rather than sudden epistemic crisis—which two or three should command the attention of a researcher with roughly three years and a background in philosophy but not in clinical psychology or formal logic? Give me a dependency-aware prioritization: which questions are upstream of the others, which are tractable without specialized empirical data, and which, if settled, would genuinely change how we assess rational belief-revision? I want an explicit defense of your ordering and your criteria—importance, tractability, and downstream dependence—not a bibliography or citation list. What evidence over the next few years would cause you to reorder them?
