I am currently enrolled in an upper-division seminar on distributive justice, and as part of my service-learning component I serve as the student representative on the university’s Emergency Relief Allocation Board. The Board must issue a binding recommendation by Thursday’s meeting on how to distribute the $180,000 crisis-response fund following the recent campus housing collapse that displaced approximately sixty students with sharply unequal baseline resources. One faction proposes a strictly prioritarian allocation protocol: awards are scaled by each recipient’s pre-existing deprivation, such that a dollar directed to the worst-off carries greater moral weight than a dollar directed to the moderately affected. The opposing faction raises the leveling-down objection, arguing that if we reduce allocations to the moderately affected in order to enlarge the pool available to the severely affected, fixed administrative costs mean the worst-off may not actually gain anything; the reduction would then harm the middle group solely to diminish welfare inequality. They therefore advocate an equal per-capita division adjusted only for documented direct material losses.

Please draft the formal written opinion I should circulate to Board members before Thursday’s session. The memo should recommend a concrete allocation rule with a clear implementation timeline, treat both the prioritarian weighting and the leveling-down objection with equal philosophical seriousness, and defend an explicit bottom-line decision—whether adopting, modifying, or rejecting the prioritarian framework—while honestly acknowledging the strongest consideration in favor of the view it ultimately rejects.
