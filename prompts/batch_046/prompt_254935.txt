Write a dialogue between two philosophers, Mara and Lin, who disagree about whether there is a morally relevant difference between lying and misleading. Mara holds that deliberately causing false belief through technically true but strategically framed statements—particularly when the speaker exploits known cognitive biases such as framing effects or base-rate neglect—is morally on par with lying, because the speaker effectively controls the listener's belief formation regardless of literal truth-value. Lin holds that a crucial autonomy-based distinction remains: lying directly substitutes the speaker's falsehood for the listener's reasoning, whereas misleading leaves the listener's inferential pathway intact, even when the speaker anticipates and relies on its predictable failure. Have them debate whether knowing a listener's specific reasoning vulnerabilities erases this distinction, and whether an AI system that outputs only true but adversarially curated statements can be said to lie if it lacks belief states. Both should be equally competent philosophers; the exchange should feature real back-and-forth, with each responding directly to the other's actual arguments rather than talking past them.
