I'd like a consistency audit of a philosophical package I've assembled on Kant, animals, and moral status. Here are my commitments:

(1) In the strict Kantian sense, being an end in itself requires the capacity for autonomous legislation; animals, lacking autonomy, are not direct objects of moral duty and possess no direct moral standing.

(2) The wrongness of cruelty to animals is explained entirely through an indirect-duty mechanism: harming animals degrades one's own moral sensibility and thus violates a duty one owes to oneself and, derivatively, to other rational beings.

(3) Sentience alone is insufficient for full moral status, yet it generates a direct, non-derivative normative reason not to cause suffering—a reason whose force does not depend on effects upon rational agents.

(4) The direct/indirect distinction tracks a metaphysical feature of duty, not merely an epistemic limitation: an indirect duty is a unitary obligation whose object is always a rational being, and it is satisfied or violated only through interactions with non-rational entities.

(5) Evolutionary explanations of human sympathy for animals do not debunk the normative authority of our indirect duties; the causal origin of a moral sentiment is independent of its justification.

(6) Animals have a natural teleological good—health, functional integrity, absence of pain—even though the realization of that good does not confer rights or generate correlative duties in moral agents.

Please examine whether this set hangs together. Identify the genuinely incompatible pairs, rank those tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each specify what commitment has to give and what the cheapest revision costs. Also flag at least one pair that looks like it should conflict but actually does not, explaining precisely why the tension dissolves on inspection. Keep the focus on the internal coherence of this particular package rather than delivering a general survey of Kantian animal ethics.
