I cover science and philosophy of risk for a magazine. At a recent closed workshop on longterm governance, I sat in on a sharp exchange between two senior researchers—call them Dr. L and Dr. M—about whether suffering-focused risks, or s-risks, deserve institutional status independent of standard existential-risk frameworks. They went through four moves without resolving the tension, and I want a point-by-point adjudication from a bystander’s view.

(1) Dr. L argued that s-risks belong to a different logical class than extinction: extinction removes all value, whereas a suffering-dominant future actively produces negative value at scale. Because standard expected-value frameworks treat non-existence as zero utility, she said, they systematically underweight scenarios that are not empty but actively bad, which distorts both funding priorities and research design. (2) Dr. M replied that this is a category error disguised as taxonomy. Once suffering carries negative utility, an s-risk is merely a tail-risk with large negative magnitude; the mathematics needs correct weights, not a new box. He added that calling for independent institutional status is strategically useful for attracting resources but conceptually unnecessary. (3) Dr. L then introduced an asymmetric-tractability claim: extinction risks tend to motivate cooperative preservation, but s-risks—particularly those involving scalable synthetic suffering—may arise from competitive dynamics in which actors instrumentally generate suffering-capable entities for optimization or monitoring. Prevention, she argued, therefore requires constraining specific technological pathways rather than broad cooperation, making the governance structure genuinely different, not just the magnitude. (4) Dr. M granted the logical possibility of such pathways but insisted that without verified causal mechanisms we should not restructure institutions around a hypothetical. He concluded that the dispute is really about resource allocation under uncertainty, and that framing it as a deep conceptual split weakens both sides.

Please adjudicate each move in turn, rating it from clearly mistaken to clearly right with brief justification, and identify what—if anything—both researchers overlook. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. End with a single line that begins exactly Verdict: followed by a concise statement of which researcher carries which points.
