If Gongsun Long were shown a modern knowledge-graph system that tags entities with overlapping class nodes—say, assigning a particular animal both Horse and White-Horse with different inheritance weights, so that a query for all horses can silently exclude white ones because the compound is treated as a distinct natural kind—how would he analyze the error? Would he see it as the same collapse of form (xing) and color (se) that he attacked in the White Horse dialogue, where asking for a horse asks for form alone but asking for a white horse asks for form plus color? Or would he claim that the graph's need for separate nodes actually vindicates the School of Names' view that ming and shi do not coincide, since 'horse' and 'white horse' must name different realities? Extend the logic of both the White Horse and Hard-and-White arguments—especially the separation of form and color, and the claim that combined attributes produce a different named reality—to this kind of artificial taxonomy, and do not appeal to citation lists or modern philosophers outside the Mingjia framework.
