I am preparing a long-form feature on whether culture can function as an independent causal force in social explanation, and I am using Weber's Protestant ethic thesis as the central test case. The position I want rigorously stress-tested is that the ascetic ethos of early modern Protestantism was not merely an epiphenomenal narrative layered over material economic change, but a genuine causal contributor to the development of rationalized capitalism—operating through mechanisms like vocational calling and disciplined accumulation rather than through direct institutional mandate.

What I need is a systematic map of what would actually count against that claim—not just the familiar correlational objections or the observation that capitalist practices existed in Catholic regions. I want the defeaters sorted by severity, from fatal to merely awkward. Specifically: which empirical patterns in the geographic and temporal diffusion of capitalist institutions would weaken the causal link; what conceptual difficulties arise if we try to separate value-motivated economic action from interest-motivated action that happens to align with religious values; and whether the thesis risks being qualified into unfalsifiability—say, by expanding the definition of culture to absorb any institutional outcome it predicts—without that insulation becoming a liability for the view itself.

There is also a more recent challenge I want included in the assessment, one that shifts the debate from correlation to mechanism. Suppose that all value-driven economic behavior is mediated by institutional translation layers—legal frameworks, bureaucratic procedures, and organizational routines that convert ethical commitments into calculable incentives. If these layers turn out to be the sole proximate drivers of the observed economic patterns, does that destroy Weber's causal story, or merely relocate it? Would defenders of cultural causation have to concede that values only work through such intermediaries, and if so, does that weaken the claim that culture is an independent cause?

Please assess honestly whether the position is well insulated, identify which of these potential defeaters its defenders should themselves accept as damaging, distinguish decisive evidence from merely awkward fit, and present the answer in American English. No bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific editions—just the arguments, cases, and structural vulnerabilities that should move someone who takes Weber's thesis seriously.
