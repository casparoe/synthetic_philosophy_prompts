Consider the tension in geometric probability that Bertrand's paradox exposes: applying the principle of indifference to a continuous space produces contradictory probabilities for the same event—specifically, whether a randomly drawn chord in a circle exceeds the side length of an inscribed equilateral triangle—depending on whether one parameterizes by random endpoints on the circumference, random radius with uniform orientation, or random midpoint within the circle. One position holds that this contradiction is fatal: the principle of indifference is incoherent for uncountable spaces, and geometric probabilities must be treated as subjective Bayesian priors tied to an agent's epistemic state rather than objective symmetries. The opposing position maintains that "random chord" is not a well-defined physical kind absent a specified generative mechanism, and that once such a mechanism is fixed, the ambiguity dissolves without abandoning objective symmetry reasoning.

Your task is to write an essay adjudicating this dispute and reaching a firm conclusion. To avoid rehearsing only the familiar trichotomy, incorporate and examine a less-standard framing: define a chord-selection procedure in which two points are chosen uniformly on the circumference, but the pair is retained only if the arc separating them exceeds 120 degrees—thereby conditioning the sample space on the chord already being long enough. This conditioning appears to yield a unique probability, yet it embeds a selection bias structurally analogous to the original parametrization ambiguity. Use this case to test whether any resolution genuinely salvages the principle of indifference or merely relocates arbitrariness into the definition of admissible sample spaces. Defend a verdict: is there a non-circular, principled criterion—grounded in symmetry, measure, or physical generative models—for selecting the correct parametrization? Or does the paradox compel us to accept that geometric probabilities are irreducibly relative to a prior specification of how the chord is produced? Justify your position with explicit reasoning, and do not end on a non-committal note.
