Assume David Hume had survived to confront the prospect of mind uploading achieved through discrete, patchwise neuronal replacement—where synthetic analogues are implanted in isolated cortical islands rather than continuously, producing intermittent gaps in phenomenal integration. Relying solely on his bundle theory of the self and his denial of substantial identity, take a committed position on whether the post-transition subject—whose perceptions are partly synthetic, partly biological, and lacking uniform associative continuity—counts as the same person. In your answer, specify precisely how Hume should treat the temporary dissociation of synthetic and biological perceptual streams, and defend a clear verdict on whether moral accountability survives this fragmentation. Do not merely survey possible interpretations; argue for the one Hume is compelled to accept.
