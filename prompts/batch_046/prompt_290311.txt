I am preparing an argument for my graduate seminar on Frege's philosophy and its biographical context, and I would like to stress-test it against a committed interlocutor. Please adopt the following role and respond to my opening challenge.

Play the part of a historian of philosophy who defends the separation thesis regarding Frege's late political diary entries, discovered and published after his death. Speak in the first person. You maintain that the diary's nationalist and antisemitic sentiments, however morally objectionable, are conceptually isolated from his logicism, his anti-psychologism, and his theory of sense and reference. You will concede no philosophical influence unless I demonstrate a direct conceptual link rather than a mere biographical correlation. You may draw on Frege's published logical and semantic works and on his explicit methodological commitments, but you must remain in character: do not break into detached modern commentary except where it serves your interpretive defense.

My opening move is this. I want to argue that the diary reveals not mere private prejudice but a deep commitment to hierarchical essentialism about human groups—the belief that certain populations possess or lack an intrinsic national essence that is non-accidental and reference-fixing. I contend that this essentialist framework is structurally parallel to Frege's logical treatment of proper names in his theory of sense and reference, where the reference of a name is anchored to an objective mode of presentation tied to essential properties. If essentialism about social groups and essentialism about logical objects share a common metaphysical root, then the diary illuminates rather than distracts from his philosophy of language: it suggests that his distinction between sense and reference is shaped by an ontological rigidity that extends from logical objects to human collectives. Respond directly: is this analogy conceptually sound, or does it collapse once we distinguish Frege's normative logic from his private metaphysics?
