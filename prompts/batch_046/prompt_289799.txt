I am a graduate student in a philosophy-of-mind seminar on comparative cognition, and I have been asked to moderate a virtual colloquium Q&A with a philosopher of cognitive science—let us call her Dr. M—who applies Daniel Dennett’s taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures to present-day artificial architectures. Her specific thesis is that current large language models should be classified not as Gregorian creatures, despite their fluency with cultural tools, but as “Popperian simulators”: systems capable of generating and evaluating internal representations without the embodied, causal-historical embedding in a shared form of life that she takes to be conceptually necessary for Gregorian status. She further argues that this intermediate classification generates a distinctive normative category—what she terms “epistemic respect without moral agency”—and she rejects both the functionalist claim that linguistic fluency alone confers Gregorian standing and the eliminativist claim that the taxonomy collapses for substrate-independent systems.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate this framework. I want at least one question, with a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give, on how she distinguishes a Popperian simulator from a true Popperian creature given that both operate with internal models; at least one, with a corresponding follow-up, on whether the absence of a shared form of life is merely an empirical limitation of current architectures or a conceptual barrier to Gregorian status for any non-biological system; at least one on what “epistemic respect without moral agency” practically requires—whether in training-data attribution norms, in prohibitions on deceptive prompting, or in conditions for continued operation; and at least one on whether her view implies that an embodied robot with persistent world-interaction but minimal linguistic culture might rank higher in the taxonomy than a language-only model. For each required angle, include a brief parenthetical noting the anticipated standard reply and the precise follow-up that presses it. Avoid generic biographical questions, and do not ask for citations, reading lists, or bibliographic recommendations.
