ok so I'm in this existentialism course and we just finished Being and Nothingness, and my study group got into this argument about Sartre's radical freemdom and bad faithe that now feels like it proves way too much. I tried to formalize it and it still looks suspicious.

(1) For Sartre, consciousness is never identical with its facticity; we are always transcending our past, our social roles, and our psychological traits.
(2) Bad faith is the attempt to deny this gap by treating yourself as pure facticity—like the waiter who thinks he IS a waiter, or someone who says "I'm just too anxious to apply" as if anxiety is a thing that determines them.
(3) So any explanation of my own behavior that appeals to psychology, sociology, or biography treats me as causally determined by facticity and therefore counts as bad faith.
(4) By extension, psychology and sociology as disciplines systematically treat people as determined by conditions, so they are structurally exercises in bad faithe.
(5) But Sartre constantly uses psychological and social descriptions—he writes about anti-Semitism as a kind of choice made in response to social conditions, and he analyzes writers through biography—without saying he's in bad faith.
(6) So either Sartre is constantly practicing bad faith, or his view implies that every form of self-understanding except pure reflection is illegitimate.
(7) That seems absurd, since we do understand ourselves through our histories.
(8) Therefore something in (1)-(4) has to collapse.

My instructor mentioned "impure reflection" and recognizing facticity without being it, but I'm not sure that saves the argument. Which step should I actually reject? If I drop (3), does that mean people can invoke "anxiety" or "class background" as excuses without being in bad faithe? If I keep (1) and reject (4), am I saying sociology is fine but individual self-explanation isn't? I want to know what the standard Sartrean reply is—probably something about the situation and how freedom always operates within facticty—but also whether that reply just redefines bad faith to avoid the problem. What do I pay either way?
