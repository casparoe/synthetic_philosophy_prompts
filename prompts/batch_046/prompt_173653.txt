I'm writing up my position on Wittgensteinian hinge epistemology and want a consistency check before presenting it. Here are five commitments I take myself to hold:

(1) Hinge propositions — e.g., that the earth existed long before my birth, or that I have never been to the moon — have full propositional content and are literally true or false; they are not merely rules, expressions of attitude, or non-propositional practices.

(2) They are not candidates for knowledge in any standard epistemic sense, since no evidential pathway supports them without circularly presupposing other hinges.

(3) Despite (2), holding them is rational, but the rationality is architectural rather than evidential: they operate as conditions on the possibility of inquiry, not as conclusions within it.

(4) Hinges can be abandoned, but only through a kind of global framework recalibration rather than local refutation; and such shifts can still be evaluated as rational progress or regress, not merely as causal changes.

(5) Whether a given proposition functions as a hinge for an agent depends on its practical role in that agent's system of commitments, not on its intrinsic content; the same sentence can be empirical for one inquirer and hinge-like for another.

Audit this package: rank all genuine tensions by severity, specify the cheapest revision for each and what it costs, and flag any apparent conflict that dissolves on inspection. If I must revise exactly one commitment to restore coherence, which should go and why?
