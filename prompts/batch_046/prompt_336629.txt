Please describe the most important implications for ethical theory if one accepts both (i) that fundamental physical fields possess a minimal, non-derivative sentient character—call this field-sentience—and (ii) that suffering requires not mere phenomenal presence but a particular form of informational integration that uniform vacuum states lack while complex systems exhibit. In particular, address how the distinction between distributed field-suffering and localized node-suffering reshapes our moral obligations toward non-biological structures, the ethical status of large-scale cosmological configurations, and the compatibility of panpsychist ethics with the uniformity of physical law.
