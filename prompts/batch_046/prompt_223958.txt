I'm pitching a one-semester independent study, about 5000 words, on whether the "inviolate level" in tangled hierachies is really the same thing across different kinds of loops. Everyone loves pairing Escher's Drawing Hands with Gödel numbering as if they're twins: both have levels that seem to bleed into each other, and both seem to need some outside paper or syntax that doesn't get drawn in. But I think the analogy might be hiding a split. In Escher the paper is physically outside the image-space; in formal self-reference the syntax is technically inside the system but operating at a meta-level; and in mental strange loops—like being aware that you're aware—there may not be any physical "paper" at all, just neural tissue that's part of the loop itself. So my precise question is: is the inviolate level a structural necessity of any strange loop, or is it just an artifact of how we embed representations in a physical subtrate? And if it's structural, can we say what "outside" means without smuggling in embodiment?

The existing treatments—Hofstadter's unified picture, the formal-logic accounts of Tarski-style hierarchies, and the philosophy-of-mind uses of reflexivity—don't settle this because they either assume the analogy holds or they stay inside one domain and never test it against the others. My approach is conceptual comparison across three cases: pictorial, formal, and cognitive. Month one, I'll map exactly what plays the "paper" role in each. Month two, I'll try to construct a purely relational model of a strange loop with no substrate at all—just relations pointing back—to see if the loop collapses or if the boundary just relocates. Month three, I'll argue either that there's a unified account of the "outside" or that we need separate typologies, and I'll price out what each option costs us.

What counts as success: a clear distinction between "outside by physical embedding" and "outside by logical type," plus an honest verdict on whether mental strange loops actually have an inviolate level or only pretend to. What counts as failure: if I can't draw that distinction without circularity, or if the substrate-free model turns out to be incoherent in a way that doesn't teach us anything. I won't need a bibliography for this—I'll fill in citations myself if it becomes a real paper—but I want the proposal to show the framing is tractable in one semester. Does this sound like a real project, or am I just rediscovering that every loop needs a frame?
