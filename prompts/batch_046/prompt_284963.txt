Two philosophers—one a negative-liberty theorist in the Berlin tradition and one a republican-liberty theorist in the Pettit tradition—have argued for a year about whether their distinction is substantive or verbal, especially regarding institutional design. Draft the joint adversarial-collaboration statement they should both be able to sign. Structure it as follows: first, a numbered list of agreed claims, including the formal definitions of negative and republican liberty, the standard illustrative cases (e.g., the benevolent-master slave and the locked door with an available key), and the shared rejection of conflating freedom with capacity or with welfare; second, a disagreements table stating the precise points of divergence in wording each side accepts, specifically (a) whether domination can exist in the absence of actual interference, and (b) whether negative liberty is sufficient for freedom under conditions of severe and arbitrary power asymmetry; third, the cruxes—namely, whether republican liberty collapses into secure negative liberty plus a rational risk-aversion preference, and whether negative liberty can accommodate the intuition that arbitrary power is wrong without smuggling in normative premises it explicitly rejects; fourth, for each crux, apply both frameworks to a concrete hypothetical both accept as bearing on it: an automated administrative system that never prohibits an action but reorders available options by predicted compliance burden, altering citizens' choices without explicit barriers, with each side recording in advance whether this counts as unfreedom and on what grounds; and finally, a brief assessment of whether any part of their dispute is merely verbal and, if so, which part and why. The document should be precise, non-vacuous, and free of bibliographic references.
