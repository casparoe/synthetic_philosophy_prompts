Here are my gut verdicts on pest control and insecticide use, and I want to know what principle actually ties them together, if any. Spraying neonicotinoids on a crop to save yield when there are targeted biological controls available: I say thats wrong, mainly because the collateral damage to pollinators is avoidable. Putting out specific pheromone traps for apple moths: totally fine, its precise. Using a gene-drive to suppress a malaria-carrying mosquito population: I'm okay with it, even though it kills millions, because the harm is indirect and the benefit is huge. Squashing a wasp that stung my kid: obviously okay. But laying poison bait that kills ants slowly over days: feels wrong, like the suffering is unnecessary. Removing an invasive beetle from an island ecosystem by hand-collecting them: okay, but using a broad fumigant for the same purpose: not okay. I'm torn on ultrasonic devices that drive rodents away without killing them — is that just relocating suffering? So whats the pattern? Is it about avoidability, directness, or the kind of harm? And what should I think about using a targeted virus that only affects one pest species but takes a week to kill? Please answer in American English, and be honest about which of my calls your best principle has to reject.
