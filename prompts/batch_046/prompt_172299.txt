I am not looking for a literature review or empirical consensus—much of this remains speculative—but rather for a rigorous conceptual evaluation of how different theoretical commitments within this space alter the ethical stakes.

Please rank the following four positions into three tiers—Tier 1 (most ethically alarming or demanding of a moral response), Tier 2 (seriously problematic but significantly mitigated), and Tier 3 (largely defused)—and justify each placement in a single sentence: (1) the strong Russellian monist view that every fundamental physical entity possesses proto-experiential properties that include valence; (2) the integrated information theory extension that assigns non-zero integrated information and potential suffering to highly integrated but non-biological structures such as certain crystal lattices or quantum field configurations; (3) the “microphenomenal zombie” proposal, in which fundamental entities possess experience but lack the organizational integration necessary for suffering; and (4) the “macro-emergence only” restriction, which denies sentience to anything below complex neural organization despite allowing fundamental proto-consciousness. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; I am interested solely in the conceptual architecture.

In formulating your ranking, please treat this as an exercise in conceptual analysis rather than settled science: explicitly note where your ordering depends on contested assumptions about integration, valence, and the conditions required for suffering, and avoid treating any single framework as definitively established.
