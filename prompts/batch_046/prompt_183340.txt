As a retired physical chemist who spent three decades studying non-equilibrium phase transitions and who has only recently begun auditing philosophy seminars, I am trying to work through a distinction I encountered in a graduate course on moral responsibility. The instructor contrasted two accounts of why hypocrisy undermines one's standing to blame: the consistency condition, according to which a hypocrite loses standing because she enforces a norm on others that she herself violates, thereby treating the norm as non-universal; and the attentional asymmetry thesis, which holds that the core failure is not logical contradiction but a structural misdirection of moral scrutiny—the hypocrite uses blame to deflect attention from her own comparable faults, thereby treating the blamed party as an instrument of self-exculpation rather than as an equal moral interlocutor.

I would like you to take a definite, committed position on which of these two frameworks is more fundamental—whether the consistency failure is primary or merely symptomatic of the attentional distortion—and to apply that judgment specifically to the difficult case in which a hypocritical critic is factually correct and her public intervention is socially necessary despite her personal moral failures. Please present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences in length, with no introduction and no conclusion; give your reasoned verdict rather than a balanced survey of both options.
