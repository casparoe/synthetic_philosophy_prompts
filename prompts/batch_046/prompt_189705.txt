As a doctoral student working at the intersection of philosophy of computation and metaphysics of causation, I am developing a framework that distinguishes the careenium—the continuous, locally governed physical substrate—from the simmballs, discrete recombinable symbolic structures instantiated within it. I hold that simmballs exert genuine downward causal influence on the careenium: the organizational state of a simmball functions not merely as a descriptive summary of substrate dynamics but as an active boundary condition that selects among transitions the substrate alone would not determine. This is intended to be stronger than epiphenomenalism about symbols yet weaker than attributing intrinsic intentionality or mental properties to the substrate itself. What would have to be true for this downward-causation claim to fail? I want an honest assessment of the position's exposure: which empirical findings about substrate-level dynamics, which formal results in causation theory or computation—for instance, completeness arguments for local physical laws or exclusion principles—and which conceptual arguments, perhaps showing that symbolic constraints always reduce without remainder to local interactions, would be decisive rather than merely awkward? What should defenders of this view themselves concede as genuinely damaging? Furthermore, has the position risked being qualified into unfalsifiability—for example, by defining downward causation so permissively that no substrate behavior could ever contradict it—and if so, is that insulation a defect for a claim of this kind? I would be particularly moved by a principled demonstration that symbolic boundary conditions must be causally redundant given closure of the physical, but I want to know whether such redundancy is already established or merely presupposed.
