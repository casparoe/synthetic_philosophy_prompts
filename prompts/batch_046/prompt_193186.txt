"Family resemblance is Wittgenstein's idea that not all members of a category need share one essential trait. Instead they are linked by overlapping similarities, like the threads in a rope where no single thread runs the whole length. This replaces the old model where you need necessary and sufficient conditions to belong to a group. Wittgenstein developed this mainly in Philosophical Investigations to correct the picture theory of language he had defended in the Tractatus, where he assumed propositions mirror reality through shared logical form.

If we apply this to scientific explanation, mechanistic explanations in biology and functional explanations in psychology don't share any essential feature—mechanisms talk about parts and causal processes, while functions talk about purposes and roles—so they don't belong to the same family at all. They are different language games with different rules of use. For Wittgenstein, meaning is determined by use, which means that how a scientist actually employs the term explanation in her daily practice fixes what it means. So since biologists and psychologists use the word differently, there is no family resemblance between them. Therefore scientific explanation is not a unified concept; it's just a collection of unrelated practices that happen to share the same word."
