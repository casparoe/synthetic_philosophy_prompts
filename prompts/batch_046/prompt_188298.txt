Take Gilbert Ryle's distinction between knowing-how and knowing-that, especially the thesis that intelligent action need not be guided by prior explicit propositional knowledge. Restate this position in the vocabulary of a robotics engineer who works with imitation learning: policies, kinesthetic demonstration, sim-to-real transfer, and neural controllers that operate without an explicit world model or symbolic representation of task rules. Apply your translation to the following scenario: a robot arm acquires a grasping skill through human-guided physical demonstration on rigid objects, then adapts successfully to deformable objects with different dynamics, without any retraining, rule retrieval, or internal model update. Report precisely what survives intact, what requires distortion, and what has no counterpart in the engineering framework—particularly with respect to Ryle's notion of knowing-how as a direct, non-inferential disposition. Does the residue indicate that the robotic vocabulary lacks a philosophically significant distinction, or that Ryle's original claim is less substantive than it first appears?
