I am working through Hofstadter's framework in Gödel, Escher, Bach, in which meaning can be distributed across a frame message (the interpretive rules or decoding instructions), an outer message (the syntactic or physical vehicle that carries the instructions), and an inner message (the content that is interpreted once the frame is applied). A colleague applies this to current debates about large language models by proposing an analogy that has become common in interpretability discussions: she compares the three-level structure to receiving a postal package, where the shipping instructions and address label are the frame, the cardboard box and wrapping paper are the outer message, and the letter inside is the inner message. From this she argues that the model's architecture and training regime serve as the frame, its weights and token embeddings serve as the outer message, and its generated text serves as the inner message; and she concludes that because the frame and packaging are merely delivery mechanisms, any evaluation of whether the model genuinely "understands" should examine only the inner message, treating the other two levels as causally necessary but semantically inert. Please audit the analogy in detail: map the frame, outer, and inner levels precisely onto Hofstadter's canonical examples (for instance, the phonograph record together with its player, or the DNA sequence together with the ribosome); identify where the postal-delivery comparison breaks, particularly regarding whether the frame can be detached from the content it constitutes rather than merely carrying it; state what the package image quietly imports about containment, separation, and causal independence that may fail to apply to interpretive systems; and say whether her conclusion—that semantic assessment should ignore the frame and outer levels—survives once these disanalogies are on the table. In your answer, separate what is well established about Hofstadter's three-level scheme from what remains genuinely contested both in readings of GEB and in the philosophy of AI interpretation, and do not present a disputed reading of Hofstadter as the consensus view.
