(1) If a concept C applies in domain D1 and we extend it to D2, then either D2 instantiates the exact properties that ground C in D1, or the extension is a copycat with no real extension in D2.
(2) The Five Freedoms framework was built for terrestrial mammals, and its idea of "freedom from distress" is grounded in specific mammalian neurobiology and ecolgy.
(3) In aquaculture we apply modified Five Freedoms to fish and decapods.
(4) Decapods and fish don't share the exact mammalian neurobiological properties that ground the original concept.
(5) So either "Five Freedoms for shrimp" tracks nothing real (pure symbolic copycat), or it tracks something genuinely different but we mislabel it (conceptual slippage).

(7) That collapse is wrong — we do real cross-domain biology.
(8) Therefore the only consistent options are that decapods literally have mammalian-style neural integration (false) or that the original Five Freedoms never applied to mammals either, leaving welfare science purely symbolic.

The conclusion feels ridiculous. i'd rather keep (1) and (7) than accept (8), but maybe i'm missing a middle path between "exact same properties" and "pure copycat."
