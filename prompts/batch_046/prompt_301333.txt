I take the view that Dennett's three stances—physical, design, and intentional—identify genuinely distinct strata of real-pattern detection, and that the patterns tracked exclusively from the intentional stance (beliefs, desires, and other propositional attitudes as predictive nodes) are ontologically on a par with physical patterns, provided they display counterfactual robustness across contexts. At the same time, I maintain an asymmetry: physical patterns are stance-independent in a way intentional ones are not, because withdrawing the interpretive framework of the intentional stance leaves the design-level dynamics entirely intact, whereas withdrawing the physical stance is conceptually impossible for embodied systems. What would count against this hybrid position?

Please distinguish the defeaters that would undermine part (a)—the claim that intentional-stance patterns are genuinely real rather than heuristic projections—from those that would undermine part (b)—the claim that they are stance-dependent in a way physical patterns are not. In particular, consider the scenario in which comprehensive design-stance modelling (say, complete neural-architectural prediction of behaviour) becomes available: would the continued predictive utility of intentional descriptions salvage their reality, or would it reduce them to merely economical compressions? Conversely, are there conceptual or empirical results—perhaps drawn from artificial systems where we possess full access to weights and architecture—that show intentional attributions systematically failing or dissolving once design-level details are incorporated?

Rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, noting which ones a committed defender could concede without abandoning the core framework and which would force a substantive revision. Also address whether this view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability—if every failure of intentional prediction can be explained away as misinterpretation rather than pattern absence—and whether such insulation is a virtue or a vice for a position of this kind.
