Write a dialogue between two bioethicists, Dr. Mara and Dr. Soren, who are equally well-informed and argue in good faith about whether an advance directive refusing artificial nutrition and hydration retains binding authority over a patient now in advanced dementia who consistently reaches for food and shows no signs of distress. Dr. Mara holds a precedent-autonomy position: she argues that the earlier competent self acted as a legislator over its future states, but she adds a specific condition—what she terms "modal specificity"—meaning the directive binds only if it explicitly anticipated the actual cognitive trajectory (not merely generic decline). She maintains that without this condition, we risk "preference fossilization," treating a single temporal snapshot as invariant across identity-altering transformations, yet she insists that with it, the present patient's reconstructed contentment should not override the directive. Dr. Soren holds a reconstructed-preference position: he argues that the directive is merely evidential and must be weighed against present preferences reconstructed from sustained behavior; he introduces "counterfactual preference stability," asking whether the earlier refusal would survive a rational update given the patient's current phenomenological state, and claims that overriding present expressed preferences on the basis of an old document commits a form of "epistemic inversion"—privileging simulated past access over direct present access. Have them respond precisely to one another: Mara should challenge Soren's reconstruction method by asking how it distinguishes genuine preference change from adaptive preference formation under severe cognitive constraint; Soren should challenge Mara's modal-specificity requirement by arguing that it sets an impossible epistemic standard that would render virtually all practical directives non-binding. The dialogue should contain no strawmen, no requests for citations or reading lists, and no reliance on named theories outside the positions defined here.
