Write a short story set in the Commune of Unbound Channels, a fictional society whose legal code explicitly distinguishes three kinds of liberty: negative liberty (absence of direct interference by others), positive liberty (the actual capacity to pursue one's ends, including collective self-determination), and republican liberty (immunity from arbitrary domination by another will, regardless of whether that will is ever exercised). The protagonist, an irrigation engineer named Voss, discovers that a recently enacted infrastructure reform—automated water-allocation governed by an opaque but democratically ratified algorithm—has eliminated direct interference with her work, expanded the community's collective flourishing in arid conditions, yet placed every engineer under the unreviewable, discretionary power of a single unelected administrator who can revoke access without cause or appeal. The reform thus introduces a pure form of domination without interference. Write the story so that its narrative structure enacts the conceptual tension: perhaps the plot moves through three phases corresponding to the three liberties, or Voss's internal deliberation shifts registers as she realizes that the absence of obstacles is not the absence of masters. Do not name philosophers, cite texts, or provide an explicit moral; let the fiction carry the philosophical weight through setting, decision, and the specific institutional mechanism of the algorithm's hidden veto.
