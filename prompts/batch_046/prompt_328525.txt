I'm serving on the grad-student wellness funding board at my uni — we got about 40k to split across departments for mental-health support next year, and the admin office sent over this proposal for allocating it using pairwise "better supported / worse supported" comparisons on three metrics (funding stability, advisor load, isolation index). The idea is you compare each pair, build a ranking, and fund the bottom ones more. But I was talking to a friend in philosphy and she mentioned how with spectrum-style cases, if A is barely better than B and B barely better than C, you can chain that all the way to L being massively better than Z, except intuitively they're not that far apart — and worse, with circular preferences you might get A > B > C > ... > A if the margins are small enough. One board member wants to scrap the ranking entirely and just divide it evenly; another wants to add "just-noticeable-difference buffers" so that comparisons only count if the gap exceeds some threshold, but nobody can agree on what the threshold should be or whether that just hides the problem. I need a written memo for Thursday's meeting — not a personal opinion, but something the board can vote on — recommending either approval of the pairwise method (maybe with conditions), rejection in favor of equal split, or some hybrid like tiered bins with buffers. It should take seriously the philosophical worry that "better than" might not be transitive across these incremental welfare differences, and it should honestly address the strongest reason to just split the money evenly. Don't ask me to cite specific philosophers; just work out the logic and propose a concrete decision with a timeline for implementation.
