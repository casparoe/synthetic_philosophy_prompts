For a philosophical profile check: I hold the following about freedom, bad faith, and structural constraint. (1) Radical Sartrean freedom is correct—consciousness is never causally determined by its situation, and even in extreme coercion one chooses one's attitude toward the coercion. (2) Bad faith is the denial of this freedom, treating oneself as having a fixed nature or social destiny that excuses one's choices. (3) There are conditions I call structural captivity—e.g., carceral regimes or caste labor systems—where institutional arrangements do not merely obstruct choice but dissolve the practical intelligibility of alternatives, so that choosing otherwise is not phenomenologically live, even if ontologically possible. (4) Bad faith still applies inside structural captivity: if someone claims they had to comply or collaborate, they are usually denying their own freedom, since they still selected their response. (5) We should not blame people in structural captivity for survival-compliant actions, because moral blame presupposes that alternative courses were practically available and meaningful. (6) I reject the standard Sartrean binary of facticity versus transcendence; I think agency runs along a gradient of situated density, where thick institutional constraints can render freedom phenomenologically dormant without annihilating it ontologically. Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each tension, specify the cheapest revision and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision—giving up or modifying one commitment—would resolve the greatest number of tensions with the least overall damage to my view. Be specific: for example, does (3) smuggle in determinism that (1) rules out? Does (4) commit me to blaming the people (5) says I should not blame? And does (6) undermine the conceptual machinery that makes (2) work?
