I've been trying to wrap my head around dialetheism after stumbling down a rabbit hole on paraconsistent logic and some old rationalist forum threads, and I think I'm collapsing two things that should stay seperate. If dialetheists literally think some contradictions are true — like the liar sentence being both true and false at once — then doesn't the principle of explosion mean they've already blown up their own belief system?

Once you have one genuine contradiction in your ontology, you can derive literally anything, so why do dialetheists still bother claiming Paris is in France or that 2+2 equals 4? I know they reject ex contradictione quodlibet inside formal paraconsistent logics, but that feels like a technical patch rather than a philosophical answer. At the metaphysical level, if you're genuinely commited to true contradictions existing in reality, not just inside some abstract model, doesn't that mean every false statement is also true somewhere in your world? I'm probably missing the distinction between localized contradictions and global trivialism, but I can't see how you contain the damage without just handwaving about relevent premises or quarantining inconsistencies inside specific domains like change or vagueness. Is the containment supposed to happen in the logic, in the metaphysics, or in some pragmatic filter on which inferences we're allowed to draw?
