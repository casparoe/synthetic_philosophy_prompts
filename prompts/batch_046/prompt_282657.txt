From this asymmetry, the book concludes that ordinary people have no independent concept of intentional action—what we call ‘intentional’ is just a moral evaluation of the outcome in disguise. The author also claims that replication attempts in East Asian samples show the effect vanishes completely, so the result reflects Western moralizing rather than universal human psychology. Therefore, experimental philosophy has shown that the traditional philosophical analysis of action—centered on beliefs and desires—is empirically empty and should be abandoned.” A philosopher colleague added that this summary collapses a distinction she thinks matters: between attributive intentionality, which concerns the mental-state ascriptions we make about agents, and substantive intentionality, which concerns the actual causal and representational structure of the behavior itself.
