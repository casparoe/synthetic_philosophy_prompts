As a PhD candidate in philosophy of art focusing on 19th-century German aesthetics, I am developing a framework that applies Schopenhauer's hierarchy of the arts to contemporary synthetic media. In Schopenhauer's account, music stands apart from the other arts because it is not a copy of an Idea but rather a direct objectification of the Will itself, bypassing the conceptual mediation that characterizes representational forms. I would like you to rank the following five phenomena according to how closely they realize this criterion of direct, non-conceptual Will-objectification, ordering them from strongest (closest to pure music) to weakest (most mediated by Ideas and concepts): (1) a live performance of a late Beethoven string quartet; (2) an algorithmically generated, non-melodic ambient soundscape with no identifiable composer; (3) a cathedral designed specifically to shape acoustic resonance and listener spatial orientation; (4) a representational painting that depicts intense emotional suffering; (5) metrically structured poetry that evokes melancholy through rhythmic patterning rather than semantic content. Please assign each a clear rank from 1 to 5, and in one to two sentences per item explain whether Schopenhauer's metaphysics allows it to approximate music's directness, or whether it remains tied to the Platonic Ideas and the principle of sufficient reason. In particular, address whether the absence of a human genius in case (2) constitutes a decisive objection within Schopenhauer's system, or whether the sonic structure alone is sufficient.
