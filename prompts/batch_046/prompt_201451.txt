I am a retired physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of science and epistemology. After working through Wittgenstein's On Certainty and the hinge-epistemology literature, I want a consistency check on the following package of commitments:

(1) Hinge commitments—such as that the earth existed long before my birth—are not propositional beliefs but non-propositional norms that structure inquiry; they are neither true nor false.
(2) These norms are globally revisable in a Quinean fashion: if systemic anomalies accumulate persistently, we could in principle replace the entire hinge-set, though no neutral framework exists to adjudicate the switch.
(3) Scientific realism about unobservable entities is not an inductive achievement but a hinge commitment of this kind.
(4) Radical skeptical scenarios—deceiving demons, brains in vats—are not false propositions but literally senseless within our form of life, and so require no direct answer.
(5) Despite (1) and (4), I regard hinge suspension—imaginatively bracketing a norm to test the coherence of an alternative framework—as a legitimate philosophical method; I have applied it both to scientific realism and to a religious upbringing I now treat as an abandoned hinge-set.

Please audit these for joint tenability. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which tensions are only apparent, rank the genuine ones by severity, and for each specify the cheapest revision that restores consistency. Then say which single change to my package resolves the most tensions. Give the bottom line first, then the reasoning, then a table summarizing each commitment, the tension partner(s), severity, cheapest fix, and consistency verdict. Do not exceed 300 words.
