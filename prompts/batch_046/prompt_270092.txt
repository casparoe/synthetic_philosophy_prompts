Defend the view that shame is the morally superior emotion to guilt for sustaining large-scale cooperation, and that a culture can deliberately shift toward shame through what I term normative visibility architectures — the institutional and linguistic frameworks that determine whether moral failures are registered as discrete harmful acts (the province of guilt) or as failures to realize a collectively constituted ideal of the self (the province of shame). Take a committed, definite position on both parts of the claim: first, that shame’s relational structure makes it more effective than guilt at reinforcing social norms without requiring granular tracking of individual harm; second, that such a cultural shift is genuinely achievable rather than psychologically naive. Address the standard objection that guilt is indispensable for targeted reparation and that shame risks global self-devaluation or withdrawal. Do not survey the literature or present both sides neutrally; instead, give the strongest reasons for this specific position, acknowledge the precise costs or boundary conditions that genuinely weaken it, and maintain a clear verdict on whether the shift is both possible and desirable.
