1. weber wants the protestant ethic to be a real cause of capitalism, not just a post-hoc rationalization. if it were only rationalization, then values never drive economic change; they just decorate it.
2. for a value-system to cause economic behavior, there must be psychologcal mechanisms linking belief to action - e.g., seeing profit as a sign of election, feeling anxiety about wasted time.
3. but if those mechanisms are genuinely causal, then wherever we see capitalist behavior (reinvestment, rational accounting, accumulation for its own sake) we should be able to infer that some equivalent mechanism was operating.
4. so if a society develops capitalism without any protestant-like beliefs, we don't falsify weber; we just say 'there must be a hidden equivalent ethic producing the same mechanisims.'
5. that makes the claim 'culture causes capitalism' immune to counterexample: absence of the culture just means absence of detection.
6. but an explanation that can't be contradicted by evidence isn't explaining; it's just redescribing the outcome in value-language.
7. therefore either culture is never a cause (only rationalization), or weber's distinction between cause and rationalization collapses.

i'd like to save (2) and maybe (3), but (7) feels brutal. is the mistake in thinking that causal mechanisms have to be inferable? or that unfalsifiability kills explanation? or is there a hidden slide between 'protestant ethic' and 'any value-system with similar mechanisims'? help me out - i genuinely need to know if i'm wrong to think culture matters at work.
