As a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun serious study in philosophy of science, I now serve as the sole methodological auditor for a small interdisciplinary grant program that funds collaborations between working scientists and philosophers. Each month I review eight to ten proposals, nearly all of which deploy at least one imaginary scenario—what the literature calls an intuition pump—to argue for a conceptual revision in scientific practice, measurement protocol, or regulatory interpretation. I have approximately twenty minutes per proposal, I must sign off with one of three fixed verdicts ("methodologically sound," "requires revision," or "outside audit scope"), and there is no philosopher on staff to whom I can defer. I need a reusable decision procedure, printable and laminable, that I can run in that window without prior philosophical training beyond what I have picked up: a flowchart in words, with stages applied in fixed order, explicit criteria for a sufficient answer at each stage, clear termination rules, and designated hand-off points where the judgment is irreducibly one of values rather than of fact.

The principle I want operationalized can be stated as follows. An intuition pump works by isolating a single conceptual or causal variable in an imaginary case, but it misleads through three specific distortion vectors: first, counterfactual isolation bias, in which feedback loops or stabilizing structures present in the real-world phenomenon are stripped away, causing the intuition to diverge from the actual dynamics; second, representational overextension, in which a heuristic model or formal representation is treated as if it were identical to the phenomenon being modeled; and third, normative framing leakage, in which a value-laden conclusion is embedded in the conditions of the scenario rather than derived from them. I want this translated into an audit checklist that preserves all three distinctions but requires no familiarity with the underlying literature.

Please construct the procedure with numbered stages, each specifying a yes/no or graded criterion, a stop rule, and—where judgment cannot be eliminated—a marked hand-off to me. It must include explicit misfire conditions: cases in which following the steps mechanically produces the wrong verdict because the pump is functioning correctly but is being applied outside its proper domain. Then apply the full procedure to three proposals currently on my desk.

The first proposal uses a variant of Wigner's friend—treating a conscious observer's report as the trigger for physical state resolution—to argue that climate measurement protocols should incorporate "observer-dependent" parameters, on the grounds that quantum mechanics demonstrates measurement is participatory rather than instrumental. The proposal applies this reasoning to routine instrumental temperature recording.

The second proposal adapts the trolley problem, modified so that the choice is between diverting risk onto five gene-engineered organisms versus allowing harm to one naturally occurring organism, to argue for a specific regulatory threshold for gene-drive release. It claims that the intuition about "intervention versus non-intervention" in the modified scenario maps cleanly onto ecological risk assessment.

The third proposal invokes Schrödinger's cat not to discuss quantum superposition but to argue that all binary taxonomic classifications—native versus invasive, living versus dead—are conceptually bankrupt because nature contains genuinely indeterminate states, and therefore conservation law should abandon all categorical thresholds in favor of continuous gradient regulation.

For each case, run the procedure stage by stage, state the verdict it produces, and explicitly flag the result I am likely to resist—whether because the pump is philosophically legitimate but misapplied, or misapplied but the conclusion happens to align with my own scientific instincts. Finally, state with precision what this procedure cannot do: which categories of philosophical disagreement it will systematically fail to capture, and which types of proposal should never be subjected to it at all.
