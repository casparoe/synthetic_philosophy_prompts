I'm trying to get my commitments on cosmological arguments straight for a seminar I'm auditing, and I'd like them stress-tested. Here is where I stand: I think the contingency argument succeeds in outline—every contingent concrete being or state has an explanation outside itself, an infinite regress of such explanations is impossible, and so there must be a metaphysically necessary being that grounds all contingent reality. At the same time, I reject the kalam argument's premise that an actual infinite cannot exist in concreto; I think an eternal past with infinitely many prior moments is metaphysically coherent, so temporal finitude is not established. Still, I hold that even in an eternal universe every concrete event requires an explanation, and I deny that an infinite horizontal chain of state-to-state explanations can substitute for a vertical one—there must be a non-contingent ground that explains the whole temporal series at once, not member by member. I also think that if a multiverse exists, it should be treated as a single concrete object (the totality of spatiotemporally related worlds) rather than an abstract set, which means it is contingent and falls under the same explanatory demand; multiplying universes does not dissolve the need for a ground. I take explanation to be essentially causal: to explain something is to identify a cause or causal mechanism responsible for it. Yet I also think the necessary being reached by the argument must be personal and act by free choice rather than deterministic necessity—otherwise its creative act would be part of the causal order and thus contingent in some relevant sense. Finally, I reject modal ontological arguments entirely, since I think moving from conceptual or epistemic possibility to actuality is illegitimate; I believe cosmological arguments avoid this by reasoning from actual existence to a necessary ground, not from possibility to being.

Then tell me which single commitment, if dropped or revised, would clean up the most tension at the lowest cost, and rank the tensions by severity.
