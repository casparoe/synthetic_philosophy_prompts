I have been working through problems in practical reason with a reading group, and we keep returning to cause-prioritization schemas—particularly those built around importance, tractability, and neglectedness—without ever agreeing on what such a framework is actually supposed to deliver. Before I apply any of them to real allocation decisions, I want a clear specification of adequacy conditions.

Write the desiderata that any theory of cause prioritization must satisfy: the phenomena it has to explain, the cases it must get right, the distinctions it must respect, and the results it must remain consistent with. Flag any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable, distinguish non-negotiable conditions from negotiable ones, and identify which single requirement does the heaviest work in excluding rival approaches. Then, briefly score the standard importance-tractability-neglectedness framework, a distributionally weighted cost-effectiveness model, and a longtermist scaling approach against your list—indicating for each whether it meets, reinterprets, or abandons the criterion in question.
