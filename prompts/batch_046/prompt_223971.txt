I am preparing my philosophical profile for a workshop on self-reference in rational agency, and I want a consistency audit before I present it. My commitments are these. First, I hold that rational agents should maintain a default posture of epistemic self-trust: not Cartesian infallibility, but a working presumption that one's reasoning is generally sound within one's domains of competence. Second, I accept the standard doxastic interpretation of Löb's theorem—if an agent possesses a fully general belief in its own soundness of the form for any φ, if I believe φ then φ is true, then derivational explosion follows, undermining rational discrimination. Consequently, third, I deny that a rational agent can coherently maintain that fully general self-soundness belief; self-trust must instead be restricted to particular domains or expressed as calibrated probabilistic confidence. Fourth, I nonetheless maintain that reflective equilibrium is the correct method for assessing one's epistemic methods, and that surviving reflection should yield a robust endorsement of the procedures one retains—not merely a hedged probabilistic wager. Fifth, I think there is a structurally important distinction between first-order reasoning and second-order evaluation of that reasoning, and that the Löbian obstacle arises only when the second order attempts to formalize the reliability of the first order through a global modal schema. Sixth, I take Cartesian certainty about one's own existence to be immune to this obstacle, not because it is an ad hoc exception, but because the cogito is not an instance of the self-soundness schema at all.

Please examine this set for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve once the distinction between first- and second-order evaluation is taken seriously, and what is the least costly revision for each real tension? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad. In particular, I want to know whether my commitment to reflective equilibrium can be reconciled with my rejection of global self-soundness without collapsing into the very belief that triggers the obstacle, and whether my domain-restricted or probabilistically qualified self-trust is strong enough to do the work that reflective equilibrium demands. I also want you to assess whether my treatment of Cartesian certainty introduces an unmotivated asymmetry into my account.
