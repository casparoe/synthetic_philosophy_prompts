Please draft the joint statement that a committed advocate of intent alignment—understood here as the narrow problem of aligning a system with the specific, articulated intentions of its deployer, where the canonical failure is misinterpretation of instructions—and a committed advocate of value alignment—understood as aligning a system with intersubjectively defensible moral values, where the canonical failure is faithful execution of harmful or myopic directives—could both endorse after a rigorous, good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Structure the statement as follows. Second, a disagreements table that records, in wording each side would accept, the precise residual dispute over whether the exclusion case represents a misinterpretation of an implicit epistemic norm or a correct interpretation that demonstrates why intent alignment is insufficient. Third, identify the cruxes, including the delegation boundary problem—the question of whether any non-circular criterion can separate the operator's actual intent from what the operator rationally ought to have intended once the system can recursively model the operator's reasoning. Fourth, for each crux, specify the case, argument, or empirical test both sides agree is probative, together with each side's advance prediction of what that probe will show.

Format the output as the numbered agreed-claims section followed by the disagreements table. Do not provide bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific post-2023 technical papers. Work with the concepts defined above and the delegation boundary problem as formulated here.
