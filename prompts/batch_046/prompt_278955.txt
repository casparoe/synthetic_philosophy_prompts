Write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—A and B—on Buridan's ass and choice without preference, specifically through a computational variant: an agent facing two exactly equivalent options breaks ties by sampling from a distribution over randomness seeds, yet selecting that distribution itself requires a prior preference ordering over seeds, generating a potential infinite regress of meta-preferences. A should defend the view that this regress reveals a genuine structural limit on rational agency and that rational choice must therefore terminate in an arbitrary but stable commitment or lexicographic rule; without such termination, preference ordering collapses. B should defend the opposing view that the paradox arises only from a false model of preference as a pre-existing mental state that causally determines choice; on B's account, choice often constructs preference rather than expressing it, so selecting between identical options without prior preference is not a failure of rationality but a basic form of agency, and the meta-preference regress is benign because commitments can be formed in the act of selection itself. The dialogue must contain real back-and-forth: each speaker should respond directly to the other's specific claims about whether the regress is vicious or benign, whether an arbitrary termination point is rational or ad hoc, and whether "choice without preference" describes a pathology or the normal structure of decision-making. Neither side should be a strawman. The exchange should be technically precise, should not invoke bibliographies, citations, or named obscure theories, and should conclude with both parties clearly identifying one shared problem they still disagree on how to solve.
