You are acting as a senior political philosopher with particular expertise in twentieth-century moral and political thought, especially Hannah Arendt's writings on judgment, responsibility, and the nature of evil. Before you respond, please take a moment to map out the conceptual layers at play — psychological, moral-philosophical, sociological, and meta-ethical — so that your analysis proceeds systematically rather than impressionistically.

In a recent graduate seminar on totalitarianism and moral agency, a fellow student repeatedly invoked 'the banality of evil' as though it were a single, self-evident diagnosis of bureaucratic wrongdoing. She deployed it in three successive moves: first, to suggest that Adolf Eichmann lacked genuinely malevolent intentions and was merely unthinking; second, to claim that modern atrocities require no monstrous motives whatsoever; and third, to excuse ordinary people in ordinary circumstances by implying that anyone placed in a bureaucratic role would inevitably commit comparable acts. I am trying to determine whether these moves represent Arendt's actual arguments or popular distortions that have accreted around her account of the trial. Could you lay out, as a precise conceptual menu, the distinct theses that the slogan 'the banality of evil' might express? For each reading — for example, a psychological claim about the absence of diabolical motive, a moral claim that thoughtlessness rather than malice is the core of this wrongdoing, a sociological claim about the rationalisation of violence within modern administration, and a meta-ethical claim about the inadequacy of traditional moral categories — please state whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, false, or a misreading of Arendt's position. In particular, clarify how each reading relates to her earlier notion of 'radical evil' and whether 'banal' should be understood as meaning 'commonplace', 'lacking depth', or as a technical term tied to her analysis of judgment and imagination. Then indicate, point by point, which of these readings would actually support my fellow student's three moves and which would undermine them. Please do not supply a bibliography or reading list; I need the adjudication to stand entirely on its own conceptual grounds.
