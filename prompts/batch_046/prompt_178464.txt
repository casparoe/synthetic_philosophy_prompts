I just finished a long, unsettled argument with a friend about whether moral revolutions happen through reasons or through shifts in interest and honor. It reminded me of a sharper exchange I witnessed between Jordan and Sam after a philosophy talk. Jordan defends the Williams thesis: moral arguments against dueling had circulated for centuries without effect, and the practice collapsed only when state monopolies on violence made private combat legally costly and when bourgeois status norms made aristocratic violence look vulgar rather than noble. He thinks Appiah’s honor-world framework merely relabels this dynamic—if honor comes to require rejecting duels, that is still honor serving underlying economic and political interests, not argument driving change independently. Sam replies that suppression is not disappearance: states could suppress duels without ending them, and in some regions they did exactly that, yet people kept issuing challenges where enforcement was weak. What actually killed dueling was that accepting a challenge now marked one as dishonorable in the very terms duelists valued—a normative shift mediated by public moral criticism linking the practice to arbitrary privilege and cruelty. Jordan counters that this judgment of dishonor was just the internalization of bourgeois norms that served industrial capitalism’s need for predictable contract law; once class interest shifted, honor was redefined to match it. Sam then argues that reducing every normative change to an underlying material interest makes the Williams thesis unfalsifiable—whenever honor shifts, Jordan calls it interest in disguise. Jordan asks why, if arguments have independent causal force, anti-dueling rhetoric failed for so long and succeeded precisely when state power and economic transformation peaked. Sam answers that this is correlation, not reduction: moral arguments were always present, but they only reshaped honor from within once institutional conditions allowed them to take hold, which is exactly how reasoning operates inside an honor-world rather than in a vacuum.
