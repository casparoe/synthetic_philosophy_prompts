I have been working through a thought experiment I call Aunt Hillary and the Ant Fugue, and I have constructed an argument that seems to show integrated information theory applies far more broadly than anyone would accept, yet I cannot locate the exact misstep. Here is the chain. (1) Integrated Information Theory holds that consciousness corresponds to a maximally irreducible cause-effect structure realized by physical mechanisms, with substrate independence being part of its motivation. (2) In the colony designated Aunt Hillary, researchers have mapped a pheromone network they term the Ant Fugue: a contrapuntal trail system in which four castes deposit chemicals with temporal delays and thematic transformations, such that global restructuring occurs within seconds if any path is interrupted, and the network cannot be decomposed into independent sub-networks without destroying the contrapuntal pattern. (3) Therefore the Ant Fugue realizes a maximally irreducible cause-effect structure, and by IIT it possesses consciousness. (4) The same relational dynamics—identical counterpoint, identical non-decomposability, identical counterfactual sensitivity—are reproduced in a computer simulation running on a server cluster, in a printed flow-diagram pinned to a board, and in a human chamber ensemble performing an isomorphic musical score. (5) In each replication, the maximally irreducible structure is present in the abstract relations. (6) So IIT commits us to saying the server, the diagram, and the musicians are all conscious in virtue of the fugue-structure they instantiate. (7) That is clearly too broad; we do not want to attribute consciousness to paper diagrams. (8) Hence IIT should not be applied to the Ant Fugue in the first place.

I want to know precisely which premise or inference to reject and what rejecting it costs. Is there an equivocation between physical realization and abstract structure hiding between steps (3) and (4)? Or does the argument validly show that IIT must include a substrate condition it officially rejects, and if so, does adding that condition undermine the theory's anti-functionalist motivation? Please diagnose the flaw with care, price the alternatives clearly, and say plainly if my framing rests on a confusion—particularly if the very idea of an Ant Fugue as a maximally irreducible structure misapplies IIT's notion of mechanism or if Aunt Hillary is being treated as an entity in a way that smuggles in collective intentionality. I would prefer you to keep whichever premises protect the intuition that substrate-independent functional organization matters for consciousness, but I am open to giving up (1) if that proves the least costly path. Please write your answer as continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a thoughtful letter, and please be direct about any errors in my reasoning rather than accommodating them politely.
