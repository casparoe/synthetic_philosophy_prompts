ok so i want a consistency check on my math philosophy before i present it. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) i'm a realist - "7 is prime" is literally true, not just a useful fiction or convention.
(2) but i'm not a platonist about "objects" - numbers are just nodes in structures, and those structures depend on being instatiated in concrete or mental systems.
(3) we access these structures through rational intuition, which is kinda like perception except it doesn't need causal contact with what it tracks.
(4) i still think Benacerraf's problem is genuinely hard - it's weird that we can know causally inert stuff.
(5) my ontological commitment comes mainly from Quine-Putnam: if our best science quantifys over numbers, we should believe in them.
(6) but i also think physics can be rewritten without quantifying over numbers if you're patient enough with paraphrasing.
(7) i apply occam's razor pretty strictly - don't posit things that don't do explanatory work.
(8) and i do think mathematical structures explain physical regularities, not just describe them.
(9) there's also true math about infinite structures that never get physically instantiated.

do these hang together? i'm especially worried that (2) and (9) are in trouble together, and that (3) combined with (4) is trying to have it both ways. also (5) and (6) feel like they can't both be right. but maybe the tension between (1) and (2) is only apparent? i want an audit, not a lecture - rank the real tensions by severity, say which ones dissolve on inspection, and for each real conflict tell me the cheapest revision and what it costs. don't tell me which view is "the right one" overall, just trace what commits me to what.
