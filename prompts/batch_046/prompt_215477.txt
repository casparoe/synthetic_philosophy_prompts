I have no formal background in philosophy or economics, but I have been reading Marx's accounts of exploitation and alienation independently and trying to apply them to the warehouse and delivery work I have done, where algorithms set routes, pace, and shifts through rules I cannot see or contest. In doing so, I keep returning to three conceptual problems that seem unresolved, and I want to know which one is actually worth serious, sustained attention—whether for organizing, for policy, or for making sense of my own situation.

First, does algorithmic management create a distinct form of alienation—specifically, alienation from the decision-rules themselves rather than merely from the product, the labor process, and fellow workers—or is it just classical factory alienation mediated by a screen? Second, can the Marxist analysis of exploitation, which traditionally requires an employer extracting surplus value through the wage relation, be coherently extended to platform and gig work where there is no fixed employer, no guaranteed wage, and risk is externalized onto workers through predictive scheduling and dynamic pricing? Third, does settling these conceptual questions carry any practical weight: if we determine that algorithmic management counts, or fails to count, as exploitation in the technical Marxist sense, would that actually alter how labor law should classify these workers, or is it only an academic labeling exercise?

If I could devote roughly three years of part-time study and perhaps engage with one researcher or organizer, where should I direct that limited effort? Rank the three problems above in order of importance, but defend the ranking with explicit criteria: which is conceptually upstream of the others, which is tractable without specialized archival access or proprietary data, and which, if resolved clearly, would genuinely change how someone in my position understands what is happening to them. I am not asking for a bibliography or a list of names; I want a reasoned agenda that tells me where the conceptual work actually stands and what resolving each problem would unlock downstream.

Please treat this as an agenda-setting question: the point is not to survey Marx scholarship, but to argue for where the next bit of focused conceptual labor should go.
