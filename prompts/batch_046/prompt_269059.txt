Write a sestina—six six-line stanzas followed by a three-line envoi—on Amotz Zahavi’s handicap principle and the evolutionary logic of costly signaling in separating equilibria. Choose six end-words that name the core elements of the logic (for example: waste, honest, prey, tail, cost, equilibrium), arrange them in the first stanza in an order of your choosing, then rotate them through the remaining stanzas according to the standard sestina pattern. Make the rotation do conceptual work: let stanza 1 present the naive view of ornament as useless display; stanza 2 turn the ornament into an active burden; stanza 3 introduce the predator’s gaze so the burden becomes a survival test; stanza 4 show the separation of high- and low-quality senders; stanza 5 articulate the paradox that the signal is credible only because it is irrational to produce without the underlying trait; and stanza 6 expose the equilibrium’s fragility when costs drop or are counterfeited. In the envoi, gather all six end-words into three lines that state the central paradox without resolving it: that the handicap succeeds precisely because it is wasteful, and that its waste is the only guarantee of its honesty. The poem should be technically accurate enough that someone familiar with evolutionary game theory recognizes the distinction between indexical and conventional signals and the role of marginal-cost differentials, but it should not read like a textbook. Do not include citations, named works, or a bibliography. Keep the tension open—do not turn the handicap into a simple sermon about nature’s cruelty or a shrug about evolutionary inevitability.
