I am a software engineer without formal training in philosophy, and I am assembling a dataset of conceptual prompts for evaluating reasoning in language models. I would like you to construct a carefully engineered thought experiment in the domain of dispositions, finks, and masks. The objective is to design a realistic, non-science-fictional software-system scenario—using no physical objects such as vases or wires, no demons, and no perfect predictors—in which three standard analyses of disposition deliver three specifically assigned verdicts about whether the system is genuinely "fragile," meaning disposed to crash when sustained load exceeds a defined threshold.

Please build the case to meet the following specifications exactly:

1. The system must contain an underlying structural bug (for example, an unreleased memory leak) that, if left entirely alone, would cause a crash once load exceeds 1,000 requests per second for more than ten seconds.
2. Include a "fink" mechanism: an automated runtime profiler that detects the approaching failure condition and, at the critical moment—specifically, only after the threshold has already been exceeded for a non-zero duration—alters the system's categorical base by injecting a live patch that eliminates the leak, thereby preventing the crash.
3. Include a "mask" mechanism: a separate load-shedding proxy that activates preemptively, before the threshold is ever reached, and diverts excess traffic away so that the triggering load never arrives.
4. Isolate one variable: the temporal ordering of the fink relative to the load spike. Arrange the case so that the mask operates preemptively, while the fink operates reactively after the threshold has been crossed but before the crash fully manifests.
5. Engineer the verdicts precisely as follows:
   - The simple conditional analysis (if load exceeds the threshold, then crash occurs) should conclude that the system is not genuinely fragile.
   - The Martin-style fink-sensitive analysis should conclude that the system is not genuinely fragile, on the grounds that the profiler removes the underlying categorical base right at the critical moment.
   - The Johnston-style mask-sensitive analysis should conclude that the system IS genuinely fragile, on the grounds that both the proxy and the profiler are merely masks or blockers that leave the underlying bug intact and do not eliminate the disposition itself.
6. Present the scenario in no more than one paragraph, then provide the analytical breakdown: state the disposition claim, identify the system method or state, describe the relevant nearby counterfactual deployments (for instance, identical builds without the proxy or without the profiler), and explain why each of the three theories delivers its assigned verdict.
7. Identify the single structural feature that does the philosophical work—whether it is the reactive temporal lag of the fink, the preemptive nature of the mask, or their interaction—and explain whether removing that feature collapses the case back into a standard physical-object example.
8. Include an honest note on how a defender of the Johnston-style view (the analysis that assigns fragility) might attack the case, for example by arguing that the profiler is not an external fink but part of the system's normal operating conditions.
9. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or requests for external sources. Write the entire response in American English.
