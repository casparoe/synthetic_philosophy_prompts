Write a short story, between 600 and 900 words, set in a near-future institution where predictive verification is legally permitted in contractual disputes. Your protagonist, a mid-level archivist named Lena, receives two simultaneous demands from an anonymous counterparty who communicates only through a secure terminal. The first demand is structured as counterfactual coercion: the counterparty claims access to an oracle that can evaluate, in branches of history where Lena refuses a specified payment, whether that refusal was causally produced by a specific hidden trait—say, an undisclosed familial debt. If the oracle confirms the causal link in the counterfactual branch, the counterparty reveals that trait in the actual world. Lena therefore faces pressure to pay not to prevent revelation in this timeline, but to alter the counterfactual conditions that would license revelation here. The second demand is structured as XOR blackmail: the counterparty holds two unrelated damaging records about Lena—Record Alpha and Record Beta—and will randomly disclose exactly one of them, with the randomization mechanism visible to both parties, unless Lena makes a single uniform payment; the counterparty credibly binds itself to reveal neither if she pays, and never both regardless of her choice.

The narrative should focus on Lena's reasoning process. She must calculate whether paying satisfies both demands, conflicts with them, or transforms one into the other. Crucially, she should realize that if she pays the XOR demand, the payment itself becomes a new behavioral fact that the oracle in the counterfactual branch can observe, thereby changing the base rate of her hidden trait's expression and corrupting the causal verification that the first demand relies upon. She should also explicitly imagine the branch in which she never received any message at all, recognizing that her present reasoning is already path-dependent on a contingency that, in that branch, does not exist.

End the story at the precise moment Lena initiates her response—typing a command, speaking into the terminal, or reaching for a token—without revealing her choice or its consequence. Do not cite philosophers, named theories, or external literature; embed all logical structure in the rules of the terminal, the dialogue of the messages, and Lena's internal deliberation. Maintain a literary, restrained tone: no exposition of formal logic, but every logical move should be traceable in her thoughts.
