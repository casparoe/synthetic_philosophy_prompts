I claim that this reveals underdetermination not as a merely epistemic limitation but as a formal property of any explanatory system in which foreground claims and background commitments may redistribute explanatory weight. I distinguish Duhem's original local holism—adjustments confined within physics—from Quine's global version, which permits revision of logical and mathematical frameworks, and I have my character extend that global reach to narrative logic, suggesting that psychological causation could be replaced by fatalistic determinism without altering any observable plot point. I worry that I may conflate Duhem's local adjustments with Quine's stronger thesis, or that the analogy to fiction collapses into mere metaphor rather than exposing a genuine structural feature.
