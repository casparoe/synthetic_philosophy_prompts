You serve as an existential audit officer for a nonprofit that guides clients through irreversible, high-stakes life transitions—such as renouncing citizenship, undergoing irreversible medical procedures, or dissolving long-term partnerships. Your organization requires that every proposed transition be evaluated through a "recurrence audit" derived from Nietzsche's doctrine of eternal recurrence, treated not as a cosmological claim but as a practical test: can the client genuinely will that every moment of the life-path they are entering, including its hidden costs, mundane repetitions, and unintended consequences, recur eternally?

Write a reusable decision procedure your audit team can run on any proposal. Structure it as an ordered sequence of stages, each with specific questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer, explicit pass/fail or hand-off criteria, and a small set of mental test checks the practitioner applies in order. Mark the points where judgment cannot be eliminated and the decision must be handed to a human reviewer rather than resolved by the checklist. The procedure must be completable within forty-five minutes, requires dual sign-off from the practitioner and a peer reviewer, and must not depend on the client's metaphysical beliefs about actual cosmic repetition.

Distinguish, in the procedure, between affirming the repetition of the decisive act itself and affirming the repetition of every downstream consequence and ordinary moment; flag cases where a client affirms the heroic or liberating instant but rejects the mundane or burdensome repetition that accompanies it. Include explicit instructions for how to treat affective resistance that conflicts with the audit's logical result.

Then run the procedure on three proposals: (1) a client proposing to emigrate and permanently sever family ties, framing it as necessary for self-creation; (2) a client requesting approval for an irreversible sterilization procedure they describe as liberating but who, when pressed, cannot articulate the daily lived experience they are willing to repeat; (3) a case in which the audit yields a "proceed" verdict that the client's own emotional resistance rejects—show how the procedure handles this misfire, where it stops, and what it records for the peer reviewer.

Conclude by stating what the procedure cannot do: identify the limits of its reliability, the kinds of inauthentic or performative affirmation it cannot detect, and the value conflicts it is not designed to resolve. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
