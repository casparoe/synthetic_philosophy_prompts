Okay so I've been running an epistemology seminar for like fifteen years and there's this thing with pragmatic encroachment vs. contextualism that I can't shake. You know the usual bank-case setup—low stakes, you know the bank is open Saturday; high stakes, suddenly everyone wants to deny knowledge even with the same evidence. The contextualists say 'knows' just shifts meaning with context; the encroachment folks—Hawthorne, Fantl, McGrath, that crowd—say no, practical stakes genuinely change how much justification you need, full stop. Meanwhile everyone in the literature just talks past each other. The contextualists accuse encroachment of psychologizing epistemology; the encroachment people say contextualists are doing semantics and missing the normative punch entirely.

My working hunch is that we're conflating two diffrent questions: one about when we're attributing knowledge to hold someone responsible, and one about whether they have the epistemic right to be sure regardless of attribution. But I'm not convinced that's the whole story. Is this stall actually about bedrock intuitions that can't be argued for? Is there a hidden empirical question about how people actually deploy the word? Or are the sides literally answering different questions—semantic vs. normative—under one label? I want an honest diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of positions: what kind of disagreement is this, and is there any conceptual distinction or shift in framing that could actually break the impasse, or should we expect it to stay stuck?
