I'm noticing a configuration in debates about potential properties that doesn't sit cleanly with the standard distinctions I've encountered. Consider a material that is genuinely brittle—its internal structure is arranged so that, if struck with sufficient force, it ought to fracture. Embedded within that same structure, however, is a responsive mechanism: the very stress that would cause fracture instead triggers an instantaneous molecular rearrangement that distributes the force, leaving the material intact. The brittleness remains fully present—it explains why the protective mechanism activates at all—yet it never manifests in actual breaking, because its own conditions of manifestation generate the interference. This is not a case where the property vanishes upon testing, since the brittle architecture remains unchanged; nor is it a case where an independent external blocker happens to be present, since the blocker is produced by the very disposition it suppresses. Does this kind of reflexive, self-generating mask have an established name? If not, should it be classified as an extension of masked dispositions, as a variant of finkish dispositions, or as a distinct category that reveals a gap between them? And what exactly distinguishes its edges from each of those two?
