I serve on the investment-risk committee of a university endowment that must vote next week on whether to adopt a quantitative climate-risk model assigning precise probabilities to portfolio losses over a fifty-year horizon. Half the committee argues that without such numbers capital allocation is irrational; half argues that Keynes's distinction between calculable risk and radical uncertainty makes assigning precise figures over such horizons a category error that disguises ignorance as quantitative knowledge. The model's proponents say Keynes was writing about macroeconomic forecasting, not asset pricing; the skeptics say his point applies whenever the evidential basis is too thin for either frequency or logical probability.

Draft the committee's decision memo recommending adoption, rejection, or adoption with conditions. The memo should address not only whether long-horizon probabilities are subjective, but specifically whether Keynes's concept of the weight of argument — the idea that accumulating evidence can reduce confidence by revealing further unknowns — undermines the model's claim to guide institutional decisions. Include an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side, and end with a clear, actionable bottom line that the researchers could respond to.

Please organize the response as follows:
1. Executive summary (about 150 words): recommendation and core reasoning.
2. The Keynesian objection (about 250 words): risk versus uncertainty, weight of argument, and why fifty-year climate probabilities may not be probabilities in Keynes's sense.
3. The case for adoption (about 250 words): the best counter-argument, including the claim that institutional action requires a quantitative framework and that subjective probabilities are permissible.
4. Assessment of the losing side (about 150 words): the single strongest point the rejected position raises that the recommendation must acknowledge.
5. Recommendation and conditions (about 150 words): clear bottom line, with specific conditions if recommending partial adoption.
