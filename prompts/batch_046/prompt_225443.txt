Write a sestina — six stanzas of six lines, closing with a three-line envoi — on the question of whether equality should be measured in welfare, resources, or capabilities. Fix the six end-words, in this rotation: welfare, resources, capabilities, conversion, measure, freedom. Let the form do philosophical work: each stanza should be anchored in one of the three metrics, yet the rotating line-endings should show every metric being translated into the vocabulary of the others, so that welfare is assessed through resources, resources through conversion factors, and capabilities through measures of freedom, without ever arriving at a neutral scale. Render precisely the two canonical objections — adaptive preferences that corrupt welfare comparisons, and heterogeneous personal and social conversion that corrupt resource comparisons — so that a specialist in distributive justice recognises the arguments rather than a mere name-check. In the envoi, place all six words together in an order that refuses to rank them, leaving the triad unresolved. Do not include citations, bibliographic references, or named authorities inside the poem; let the conceptual architecture carry the meaning.
