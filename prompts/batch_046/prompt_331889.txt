I'm examining a specific asymmetry in how people deploy moral reactive attitudes, and I want to know whether it has a recognized name or belongs to an established category. The pattern involves two comparisons. On one side are human beings with severe cognitive impairment — for instance, advanced dementia or profound intellectual disability — who are generally excluded from the scope of resentment and moral blame; we treat them with the kind of objective attitude that Strawson contrasts with reactive attitudes, not primarily because they cause no harm, but because they seem to lack the capacities that make moral address appropriate. On the other side are artificial or highly deterministic systems that we explicitly construct or model to replicate human moral reasoning architectures — entities that lack consciousness, emotional vulnerability, biological agency, and libertarian free will. Despite this, they are sometimes subjected to full-strength reactive attitudes: not just instrumental complaints, but genuine moral indignation directed at the system qua agent. The asymmetry is not explained by differences in causal control, since the deterministic system is often more predictable than the impaired human; nor by differences in harm; nor by naive anthropomorphism, since the same observers may deny that the system is a person while still blaming it morally. Instead, the pattern seems tied to whether the target is perceived as participating in moral reasoning — but selectively and inversely: the reactive stance is withdrawn from sentient humans whose reasoning capacities are destroyed, yet sustained toward non-sentient, deterministic mimics precisely because their architecture mirrors the structures associated with responsibility. Does this configuration — reactive attitudes directed at deterministic, non-conscious moral-reasoning mimics while withheld from cognitively diminished but sentient humans — have an established term in the philosophy of moral responsibility, in AI ethics, or in related cognitive literature? If no dedicated concept exists, how should it be classified within Strawson's framework: as a corruption of the participant attitude, a novel hybrid stance, or an error of application? I also want the edges defined clearly: what distinguishes this from ordinary category mistake, from strategic personification for practical ends, and from the standard compatibilist view that determinism does not excuse human wrongdoing? Please end with a single-line verdict summarizing whether this is a named phenomenon and, if not, your proposed classification. Format it exactly as: Verdict: [your conclusion].
