I'm giving the eulogy for my sister, who died suddenly last month. She was a quantum physicist who, for the last two decades, read Vedanta through Schrödinger's writings on the unity of consciousness and genuinely held that individual selfhood is something like a persistent optical illusion rather than a metaphysical boundary. She left a note asking that her funeral not undo that conviction with talk of her watching over us from elsewhere, yet half the room will be family and friends for whom she was irreplaceably this particular person, not an undifferentiated aspect of a single mind. The other half are colleagues who will recognize the view and catch any misstatement of it. Please write a five-minute eulogy in continuous prose only—no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables—so that it lands for both audiences without condescending to either. The philosophical idea should do real structural work: it must make it impossible for me to speak of her surviving as a separate soul, but it must also avoid reducing her death to a null event for those of us who grieve her specifically. Shape the speech so that honoring her conviction and honoring our grief are held together without being neatly resolved; let the tension remain visible, as she herself lived with it. Write as if I am speaking directly to the room, not delivering a philosophical lecture, and do not include citations, reading suggestions, or references to texts I might not have at hand.
