I've come around to thinking the procreation asymmetry is basically right - there's no obligation to make happy people, but there's a real duty not to make miserable ones. The way I see it, suffering carries a kind of impersonal moral weight: it's bad regardless of whether anyone is around to miss not experiencing it, whereas happiness only generates reasons when there's already a subject who can enjoy it. So the asymmetry isn't just risk-adverse psychology; it's grounded in a real difference between the moral structure of harms and benefits. What would actually count against this? Which defeaters would a committed asymmetry-defender concede as damaging, and which would they dismiss by tweaking the harm/benefit distinction?
