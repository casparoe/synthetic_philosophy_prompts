I've noticed a recurring argumentative shape that sits near decision-theoretic discussions of risk, but I haven't found a precise label for it. In individual choice scenarios, people routinely prefer a guaranteed moderate gain over a gamble that offers a slightly larger gain with a small chance of nothing; yet when the probabilities are rescaled so that both options share that same small chance of failure, the preference often reverses, and this reversal is widely treated as understandable rather than as a flat error. That pattern itself is well-known. What interests me is the asymmetry I see when the same logical restructuring is imported into collective or normative reasoning. In policy contexts -- for instance, distributing resources between a program that guarantees moderate benefits for many and a program that might deliver massive benefits or none at all -- some interlocutors invoke the mathematical equivalence of the restructured lotteries to argue that preferring the guaranteed option is irrational framing, and they treat the independence of outcomes from shared background risks as a binding constraint on public reasoning. Meanwhile, these same people will excuse the personal-choice reversal by appealing to context, regret, or psychological salience. So the phenomenon is not merely violating a consistency axiom in one setting; it is selectively enforcing that axiom as a normative weapon in collective domains while relaxing it in individual domains, using the identity of the probability restructuring as the bridge between the two.

Does this asymmetry have an established name in the literature on rational choice, moral psychology, or public reasoning? If there is a recognized concept, I want to know how closely it fits, and specifically how it differs from ordinary framing effects or from domain-general violations of consistency. If there is no established term, can you define the phenomenon sharply enough to distinguish it from both plain hypocrisy and from legitimate context-sensitivity? I care about the edges: does the asymmetry depend on the scale of the affected population, on whether the risk is borne collectively, or on whether the reasoning is being offered prescriptively to others rather than descriptively about oneself? Please answer in a single paragraph, and do not satisfy me with only a bare name -- I want the carving, not the label.
