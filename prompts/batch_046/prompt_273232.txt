ok here's an arg i built about Locke's education theory that feels like it proves too much and now i can't sleep.

1. Locke says the mind starts blank and all ideas come from sensation and reflection, so there's no innate moral knowledge.
2. He also thinks custom and habbit are stronger than reason, which is why education should drill good habits into kids before they can think for themselves.
3. But if reason itself is just the ordering of ideas that come from experience, then reason isn't some seperate faculty that can stand outside habit and check it—it's just another pattern of associations.
4. So the educator isn't training a pre-existing rational agent; they're constructing the very associations that will later function as 'reason' for that person.
5. Once those associations are locked in, there's no independent standpoint inside the mind from which to revise them, because every tool of revision was also built by the same training.
6. That means Locke either has to admit education is irreversible conditioning (basically determinism), or he has to smuggle in some innate rational capacity that he officially denies.
7. But he clearly wants adults—especially gentlemen—to be able to correct their own prejudices and think autonomously.
8. So his view seems to imply that critical self-correction is impossible unless you already had the right habits implanted, which makes liberal education circular or self-defeating.

i'd really like to keep (2) and (7). which step should i drop, and what does dropping it cost? is the mistake in thinking reason can't evaluate its own origins, or is Locke just inconsistent?
