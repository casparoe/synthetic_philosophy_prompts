No physical harm, but intense visceral disgust and a clear erosion of shared norms about bodily dignity. The tree should start with concrete commitments: does law need a harm principle? is "symbolic damage to social trust" a kind of harm? is disgust ever epistemically reliable about real damage? is social cohesion an independent good worth protecting? The leaves need to include at least a Hart-style liberal, a Devlin-style conservative, an offense-principle middle ground, and two hybrid/unoccupied spots — like "moral harm is real but unlegislatable" or "disgust tracks symbolic harm but the remedy should be zoning/taxation, not criminalization". Each branch node should be phrased as a yes/no or pick-a-case question that a student can answer without knowing the philosopher's label. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cant cleanly seperate, and note any logical combinations that stay empty — are they incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored? No bibliography, no citation list, no reading recommendations please — I just want the instrument itself, about 8-10 nodes, for undergrad law-philosphy students.
