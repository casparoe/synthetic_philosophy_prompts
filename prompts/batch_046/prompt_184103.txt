I serve on the acquisition and classification committee of the Meridian Archive of Biological and Synthetic Specimens. We have received a sealed collection of 340 cryopreserved microbial cultures from a defunct biotech consortium; the provenance logs were destroyed in a facility fire, and our preservation charter prohibits destructive genomic sequencing or metabolic profiling that would determine whether these are engineered synthetic organisms or naturally occurring extremophiles isolated from deep subsurface samples. The archive’s ethical charter assigns different custodial obligations, access restrictions, and attribution requirements to synthetic versus natural entities, but only if there is an objective ontological distinction independent of our epistemic access. Some members argue that without recoverable origin information there is no institutional fact of the matter about which ontology applies, so we should default to the less restrictive natural-species protocol; others insist that each culture has a definite origin regardless of our knowledge, and that our duty of care requires treating them as synthetic until evidence proves otherwise to avoid under-regulating potential bioengineered materials. I need a short written opinion recommending a concrete classification policy and custodial timeline for implementation, treating both positions seriously rather than dismissing either as conceptual confusion. Please present the recommendation as a numbered list of exactly five points, each one to two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
