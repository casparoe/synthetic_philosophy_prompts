I have no formal background in philosophy, but I've been trying to work out for myself whether I could have come from a different sperm and egg and still been me. At first it seemed obvious that I couldn't: if some other sperm had fertilized the egg, or if the egg had been different, the resulting person wouldn't be me, they'd be my sibling or a stranger. So I built an argument to make this precise, but now it seems to explode into something ridiculous, and I can't locate the mistake.

1. If I had come from a different sperm-egg pair, I would not exist; a numerically different person would exist in my place.
2. Therefore, my actual sperm and egg are essential to my identity—without them, I am not me.
3. But apply the same reasoning to my parents: if either of them had come from a different sperm-egg pair, they would not be the people they are, and therefore could not be my parents.
4. So for me to exist as me, my parents must also have come from exactly the gametes they did, and the same holds for every ancestor back through every generation.
5. This implies that my existence requires a completely fixed, unbroken reproductive chain with zero variation at any link—down to the temperature in the room, the timing, any factor that might have changed which sperm reached the egg.
6. That is clearly absurd; it makes my being here depend on an astronomically specific historical sequence that a single sneeze or late bus could have disrupted.
7. So I should reject origin essentialism after all, and accept that I could have come from different gametes.

But there's a second problem that makes me hesitate. If I keep (2), then identical twins—who come from the very same sperm and egg—should be one person, which they obviously aren't. So origin essentialism seems to fail in both directions: different origins seem compatible with being me if I reject step (7), but identical origins don't guarantee being the same person. Is the error in step (3), where I extend the reasoning to ancestors? Or is there a hidden slide between saying this origin is necessary for me and saying every ancestor's origin is necessary for me? Or maybe the problem is that I'm treating identity as if it needs a single unbroken thread rather than allowing some flexibility at earlier links? I'd like the reasoning unpacked clearly, without just citing authorities—please work through the steps in American English and tell me exactly where the chain breaks and what it costs to fix it there.
