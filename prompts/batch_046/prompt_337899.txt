As a novelist accustomed to constructing worlds whose internal coherence never quite resolves whether they exist independently of their narration, I have watched with some recognition the long stall in the debate over Skolem's paradox. The result is well known: if first-order ZFC has any model at all, then by the Löwenheim-Skolem theorem it has a countable one, despite ZFC proving the existence of uncountable sets. One camp reads this as a genuine crisis of determinacy—first-order logic is too weak to fix the intended universe of sets, so there must be an absolute, second-order, or informally grasped reality that the formalism fails to capture. The opposing camp dissolves the crisis by treating countability and uncountability as model-relative: within the model the sets are genuinely uncountable, from an external metatheoretic perspective the model is countable, and there is no contradiction because there is no privileged single vantage point. For decades these positions have restated themselves without movement. My hypothesis is that the impasse is not technical but structurally analogous to the fictionality problem in narrative: one side asks whether the set-theoretic universe exists in a model-independent way, while the other asks only whether the formal description is internally consistent, and both sides employ the same vocabulary—"model," "interpretation," "the real numbers"—to conceal this divergence. Is this disagreement substantive, resting on hidden metaphysical commitments about mathematical existence? Is it verbal, turning on whether "countable" should carry absolute rather than indexical weight? Or are the sides answering different questions—one about logical expressibility, one about ontological grounding—under the single heading "Skolem's paradox"? Given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress: a formal result establishing categoricity for a relevant fragment, a conceptual distinction between internal and external quantification, a demonstration that the question is ill-posed and should be abandoned, or something else entirely? And if no progress is possible, why should we expect the stall to persist?
