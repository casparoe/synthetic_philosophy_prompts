1. i think the teleological suspension of the ethical is literally a suspension—not just ethics being outweighed by religion, but the universal being put on hold for the singular relation to god.
2. i think abraham is a knight of faith, not just infinite resignation, so he genuinely expects isaac back even while he walks up the mountain with the knife.
3. i think the whole narrative is descriptive—kierkegaard is showing what faith looks like from inside, not giving us a rulebook for religious ethics.
4. but i also think there's genuinely no public criteron that separates abraham from a fanatic; the silence is structural, not accidental.
5. and i still want to say philosophy can evaluate whether that kind of faith is rational or dangerous, i dont want to be a quietist about it.

do these hang together? specifically: does (3) block me from worrying about (4) licensing fanaticism, or does (1) plus (4) commit me to saying philosophy cant do the work i want in (5)? and is there a hidden tension between (2) and (1)—like, if the ethical is suspended, why does abraham still love isaac in the particular? or is that exactly the paradox and not a bug?

audit me: order the tensions by severity, say which one is just apparent, and tell me the cheapest revision that cleans up the worst problem without throwing out the religious core.
