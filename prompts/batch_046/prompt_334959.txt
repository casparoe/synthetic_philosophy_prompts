Just as a desktop icon is not literally a file but a useful fiction that hides complex backend processes, our brains construct a simplified 'inner movie' that makes us think there is a private, qualitative flow of experience, when in reality there are only functional states and information access. Consequently, illusionists deny that anyone literally feels pain in the phenomenal sense; they say reports of suffering are accurate descriptions of functional states but mistaken about involving some extra, non-physical property. The view gained traction because neuroscientists have never isolated a dedicated 'qualia module' or neural correlate specific to subjective feeling, and without such a module, postulating phenomenal properties violates parsimony. Therefore, illusionism is essentially eliminativism about experience applied selectively: it keeps beliefs and desires but deletes the feeling part, which is why critics call it self-refuting—if there is no feeling, there is nothing to be wrong about."
