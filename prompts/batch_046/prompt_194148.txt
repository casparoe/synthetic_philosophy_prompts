I spent thirty years in condensed-matter physics before turning to philosophy, and I have come to hold a specific variant of semantic externalism about scientific theoretical terms. On my view, the reference of a term like "electron" is not fixed by the individual scientist's mental representation or by the community's explicit definitions, but by a causal-historical chain that runs through standardized calibration protocols, instrument designs, and the institutional practices that link measurements back to the entities being measured. This extends the Twin Earth thought experiment: imagine a Twin Earth with physically identical scientists and identical mental states, but whose calibration protocols had diverged so that they systematically tracked a different underlying entity—one with the same charge but different spin-statistical behavior. In that case, "electron" on Twin Earth would refer to that other entity, not to ours. I find this convincing for mature sciences with robust instrumental traditions, though I recognize it may be less plausible for nascent theoretical terms.

What would count against this position? I want an honest map of its exposure—not only the standard objections about mental content or about the causal theory's failure for empty terms, but specifically the defeaters that target the extension to scientific reference via calibration and institutional practice. Which intuitions about scientific disagreement, conceptual change, or incommensurability would undermine it? Which formal results—say, about the underdetermination of theory by evidence, or about the conventional rather than causal nature of measurement standards—would be fatal, and which merely awkward? Would a committed defender concede that if calibration protocols turn out to be conventionally rather than causally linked to entities, the view collapses into a social-constructivist rival? And is there a risk that this position has been qualified into unfalsifiability—if so, is that a defect for a thesis about scientific reference? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones the view's defenders would themselves accept as genuinely counting against it.
