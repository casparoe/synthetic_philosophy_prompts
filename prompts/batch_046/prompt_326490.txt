Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a historian defending Leopold von Ranke's regulative ideal of reconstructing the past as it actually was and a historian working within the sceptical tradition of E. H. Carr and Hayden White regarding narrative objectivity. The collaboration should center on a proposed methodological innovation—the negative-evidence disclosure protocol—which requires every historical monograph to include an appendix cataloguing the archival silences that shaped its narrative arc, together with an explicit statement of which counter-narratives those silences render unrepresentable. Produce a two-part document. Part One should be a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, addressing: (a) the formal conditions under which an archival gap is distinguishable from an archival destruction; (b) what the strongest versions of White's analysis of emplotment and Ranke's source-critical practice actually entail about the possibility of history without narrative form; and (c) shared criteria for identifying when an absence of evidence is being treated as evidence of absence rather than as a neutral omission. Part Two should be a disagreements table in which each side states, in wording it accepts for its own view, exactly where it parts ways on three issues: whether the disclosure protocol improves historical objectivity; whether archival silence can ever be narratively neutral; and whether the protocol implicitly presupposes that a fully present archive is the normative baseline. For each issue, identify the crux—the specific sub-question whose settlement would move at least one party. Then, for each crux, specify a concrete historiographical test case, such as a disputed demographic reconstruction of a particular region or period, that both sides agree is relevant; record in advance each side's predicted outcome for that case. Finally, indicate whether any portion of the disagreement is merely verbal, identifying which terms are being used with different meanings and explaining why that divergence does not affect the substantive dispute over adopting the protocol.
