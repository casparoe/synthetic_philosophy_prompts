Before evaluating philosophical accounts of inverse reinforcement learning—specifically those that treat observed human behavior as evidence for an underlying reward structure—specify the full list of desiderata such an account must meet. Give exactly five requirements, numbered 1 to 5, each in one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. For each, state the phenomenon or distinction the theory must preserve (for instance, the gap between revealed preference and counterfactual preference under cognitive debiasing, the separation of terminal values from instrumental convergence when behavior is shaped by social signaling, or the tension between empirical fit to choice data and normative independence from those very biases), indicate whether the constraint is non-negotiable or specific to one camp, and provide the motivating case. Include, as one requirement, that the account must address whether a single agent can harbor modular, context-dependent sub-preferences that resist aggregation into one utility function, and identify any pair of desiderata that seem jointly unsatisfiable given standard rational-choice axioms.
