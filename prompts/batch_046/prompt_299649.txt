My friend and I just finished a long argument about Ursula K. Le Guin's story The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas: she claims it is a cheap emotional intuition pump that smuggles in utilitarian trade-offs without defending them, while I think it is more structurally sophisticated but still vulnerable to a specific philosophical objection. I want a reading that treats the story as an instance of the intuition-pump method and examines exactly where it persuades and where it misleads. In particular, does its architecture depend on an unstated assumption of suffering-atomism — that the child's pain is a discrete, isolable unit exchangeable for aggregate happiness — and does its refusal to narrate the child's inner experience function as a strategic omission that shields the pump from empirical rebuttal? How does this compare to standard philosophical pumps like the trolley problem, and does the story expose limits of the method that purely argumentative versions miss? I am preparing this for a discussion group, so I need a critique that takes both Le Guin's craft and the philosophy of intuition pumps seriously, without reducing the work to an illustration or distorting the idea to fit the plot.
