Write a short research proposal for a one-semester graduate seminar paper of approximately six thousand words in political philosophy. The project should ask whether Foucault's distinction between normalization and normation—developed in his lectures on security, territory, and population rather than in his study of prisons and discipline—can be applied to algorithmic content moderation without collapsing into the familiar panopticon model of disciplinary power. State the precise research question: does a recommendation system that reshapes the baseline distribution of visible expression constitute normation in Foucault's technical sense, or does it remain normalization applied at scale? Describe the possible conclusions—that the distinction holds and reveals a non-disciplinary mode of power, that algorithmic governance collapses both into normalization, or that the distinction is unstable outside its original public-health context—and specify which empirical or textual considerations would decide among them. Give an honest assessment of scope risks, especially the danger of becoming mired in interpretive disputes about the lecture material without archival support. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; refer to theorists, texts, and concepts by name only, and note that references will be added later. Present the proposal entirely as continuous prose in the form of a formal letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
