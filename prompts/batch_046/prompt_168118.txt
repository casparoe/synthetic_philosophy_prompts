Construct an argument supporting the claim that a legal system can consistently contain a supreme rule that authorizes its own amendment—call it Article Ω—without generating a vicious paradox of legal self-reference. In your argument, distinguish procedural self-reference, in which a rule governs only the mechanism by which it may be altered, from substantive self-reference, in which a rule attempts to determine its own content directly; maintain that only the latter produces contradiction, whereas the former yields a benign authority loop. Specify that Article Ω states it may be replaced solely by a rule adopted through the two-thirds procedure defined within Article Ω itself, and that no other constitutional provision may amend it. Explain why this structure neither requires an infinite hierarchy of meta-rules nor depends on an external foundational norm, and respond to the objection that a rule capable of authorizing its own destruction thereby undermines the legal continuity it secures.
