I've been stuck on the Orwellian vs Stalinesque debate about color phi for months and it feels totally circular. You show two dots, red then green, and people report seeing movement with a color change in the middle. The Orwellians think your brain first experienced red-in-the-middle then retroactively rewrote the record; the Stalinesques think the first experience was just delayed until the second dot arrived, so you never genuinely experienced the wrong color.

Every argument I see just loops back to whether consciousness is real-time or post-hoc narrative. Nobody changes sides. Is this actually a substantive disagreement about neural timing, or is it verbal -- two groups using 'experience' to mean either the raw neural event or the reportable story? Or maybe they're answering different questions: one about phenomenology, one about cognitive access?

Here's a twist I'm throwing in: imagine the subject can suppress the second dot at the last millisecnd with a blink, but still reports the intermediate color. Would that break Stalinesque gating, or just show a failed gate? Given all this, what kind of stall is this? Is there a hidden empirical question, a conceptual confusion about what counts as data, or just bedrock intuitions about whether the past can change? And honestly, is there anything -- an experiment, a distinction, a change of framing -- that could move it, or should we accept it's deadlocked for good?
