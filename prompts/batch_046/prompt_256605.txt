Read Pixar's Inside Out through the lens of Daniel Dennett's critique of the Cartesian theater and his multiple drafts model of consciousness. The film literalizes Riley's mind as a Headquarters where five personified emotions operate a control console, watching screens that display her experiences to an implied central observer. At the same time, it depicts personality as distributed across Islands sustained by core memories, and it shows Riley's behavior fragmenting into incoherent drafts when those Islands collapse and the emotions are displaced. Does the film ultimately depend on a Cartesian audience that Dennett's model denies, or does its depiction of competing emotional narratives—each vying to dominate Riley's actions without a unified self—actually illustrate the multiple drafts view? I am particularly interested in the sequence where Joy and Sadness are lost in long-term memory and Anger, Fear, and Disgust attempt to maintain Riley's life: does this demonstrate that consciousness is merely the temporary dominance of one draft over others, or does the narrative structure smuggle back in a central viewer who integrates them? And where the philosophical reading conflicts with the film's emotional logic—especially its insistence that Sadness must be reintegrated rather than suppressed—what does that tension reveal about the limits of a purely distributed account of the self?
