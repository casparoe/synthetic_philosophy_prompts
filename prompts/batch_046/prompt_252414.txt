I am a doctoral student in the philosophy of medicine, currently examining how looping effects operate in contemporary psychiatric nosology. I have been analysing the published commentary of Professor Mara Okonkwo, who applies Hacking's framework to recent classification debates. Her verdicts on four cases seem to me jointly consistent with more than one underlying principle, and I should like your help in predicting her position on a novel proposal and in disentangling the rules she might be running.

Her stated positions are as follows. First, she endorsed the inclusion of complex post-traumatic stress disorder in ICD-11, arguing that the diagnostic loop here is clarifying: patients distinguish a specific pattern of affective dysregulation and relational disturbance from generic anxiety, and the label directs them toward appropriate therapeutic modalities rather than misidentification. Second, she opposed the inclusion of gaming disorder in ICD-11, contending that the loop is vicious—pathologising intense engagement creates the very withdrawal and preoccupation that the diagnosis then records, particularly among adolescents. Third, despite acknowledging poor inter-rater reliability, she argued for retaining dissociative identity disorder in DSM-5-TR, on the grounds that removing an established explanatory framework would disrupt existing patient narratives and produce iatrogenic fragmentation rather than therapeutic benefit. Fourth, she resisted broadening autism spectrum disorder criteria to encompass subthreshold presentations in high-functioning adults, warning that such expansion risks diluting clinical resources and altering self-concept in ways that lack clear therapeutic justification, even if the loop is not straightforwardly vicious.

The new case is a proposal—currently under discussion for a future ICD revision—to classify predictive self-alteration syndrome (PSAS). This denotes a state of sustained distress in which individuals internalise algorithmic predictions of their behaviour (generated by workplace analytics, biometric forecasting, or personalised recommendation architectures) and subsequently perform the predicted identity, experiencing acute dissonance when lived experience diverges from the forecast. The proposed treatment combines algorithmic transparency with narrative reconstruction. The proponents claim the loop is currently hidden and harmful; critics reply that formalising it will instantiate the very identity fusion it describes.

Please predict Professor Okonkwo's verdict on whether PSAS should be included, identifying at least two plausible rules that accommodate her four earlier positions and stating what each predicts here. Give your best-guess verdict with reasoning, specify which of her four cases is the closest analog, and propose a further hypothetical case—one not yet in the literature—that would best discriminate between the competing rules. Finally, if her precedents genuinely underdetermine the decision, state explicitly what principle she would be adopting with each possible choice.
