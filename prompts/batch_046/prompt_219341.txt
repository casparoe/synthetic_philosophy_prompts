I'm taking an advanced ethics seminar, and we've been working through the standard objections to deontological moral theories—particularly the charge that rigid adherence to duty can license not merely suboptimal but genuinely catastrophic outcomes. My professor introduced what she calls the "cascading duty paradox," a framing I haven't encountered in the canonical literature: imagine agent A, who can prevent agent B from being compelled—through no fault of B's—into a situation where B must violate a grave duty (say, breaking a solemn promise that will cause widespread harm). A can intervene only by committing a comparatively minor wrong of the same kind (breaking a trivial promise). If A refuses on deontological grounds, B is forced into the graver violation. The paradox, she argues, is not simply that deontology permits bad results, but that its internal logic seems to generate a kind of moral amplification—each agent's fidelity to duty increases the aggregate weight of violated duties elsewhere, without any individual having acted wrongly.

Write an essay examining whether this cascading duty paradox constitutes a genuinely independent challenge to deontological ethics or merely a restatement of the familiar paradox that deontology can sanction intolerable outcomes. Weigh the principal deontological responses—appeals to the doing/allowing distinction, the doctrine of double effect, and threshold deontology—assessing whether any can halt the cascade without smuggling in consequentialist reasoning about aggregate harm. Reach and defend a definite conclusion: either that the cascade exposes an internal instability in pure deontology that forces revision toward a hybrid view, or that deontology can consistently absorb the case while preserving its non-consequentialist core. In defending your verdict, address specifically whether agent A has a duty to absorb the minor wrong in order to prevent B's graver one, and whether answering that question unavoidably requires appealing to the total number or severity of duty-violations produced.
