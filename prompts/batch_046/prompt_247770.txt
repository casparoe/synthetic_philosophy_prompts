hey, im a retired field biologist who recently started reading philosophy and im stuck on superrationality. Hofstadter argued that in Newcomb's problem you should one-box because your reasoning process is similiarly structured to the predictor's, so picking one box correlates with the predictor having filled it. The causal decision theory crowd says thats magical thinking — your hand movement doesnt cause the box contents — so you should two-box. This has gone back and forth since the eighties without anyone budging. I wonder if the stall is partly a catagory error: people borrow evolutionary stable strategy reasoning, like kin selection or reciprocal cooperation, where correlation really is causal in a population sense, and try to apply it to a single agent's one-off mental calculation with no population dynamics. So is the disagreement substantive about rationality norms, verbal about what should means, or are they just answering different questions — one about correlation, one about causation? What would actually count as progress here: a formal proof that mental similarity can or cant ground correlation without causation, an empirical study on how people actually model other minds, or just realizing the problem is ill-posed?
