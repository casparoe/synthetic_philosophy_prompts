Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between two opposing camps on the justification of state punishment: one camp holds that punishment is justified solely by the offender's moral desert and must be proportionate to culpability regardless of social outcomes; the other holds that punishment is justified solely by its future social consequences, including deterrence, incapacitation, and rehabilitation, and that desert plays no independent justifying role. Write the document both could sign. Include: (a) a numbered section of agreed claims—about the institutional reality of punishment, the distinction between justification and distribution, and the empirical limits of penal efficacy—phrased in neutral wording both accept; (b) a disagreements table that sets out the exact points of divergence, with each camp's position worded so that camp itself accepts it, covering whether desert is necessary, whether consequential aims are legitimate, and whether an expressive or communicative function of punishment can be detached from both desert and utility; (c) the cruxes—specifically, whether empirical findings on deterrence can ever justify disproportionate sentences, and whether a sentencing scheme calibrated purely to communal norm-affirmation (with severity bounded only by net harm avoidance, not by culpability) is conceptually coherent; (d) for each crux, one concrete institutional case or hypothetical sentencing design that both agree is relevant, plus each camp's predicted evaluation of it. Additionally, both camps should address a specific injected scenario: a jurisdiction adopts a framework where sentence length is set by the symbolic magnitude required to reaffirm social norms after a breach, explicitly rejecting both culpability-proportionality and utility-maximization as direct calibrators, though imposing an external cap to prevent net social harm. Each camp should state whether this framework resolves their concerns, violates them, or introduces a third category they must recognize. Format the answer in American English: begin with the numbered agreed-claims section, followed by the disagreements table, then the cruxes with predictions and the scenario analysis. Do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
