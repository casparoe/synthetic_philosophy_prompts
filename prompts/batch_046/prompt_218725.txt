Generations like Gen Z are definitely not natural kinds in the classic sense — there's no essential gene or hidden nature that all Zoomers share, so they're not like gold or tigers. That said, they're not pure fiction either. Sociologists use them as tracking devices for people born within a window who face similar economic conditions, which makes them social kinds, similar to money or citizenship.

However, I think calling them "real kinds" is ultimately wrong because social construction means the boundaries are arbitrary. If advertisers decide the borders, then there's no objective cluster — just a label we project onto random age groups. Even if we try to save them with Boyd's homeostatic property cluster theory, generations fail because there's no single causal mechanism holding the traits together; teens in 2024 share TikTok but not politics or values, so the properties don't cluster stably.
