In my philosophy of science reading group we've been going in circles about dispostions, finks, and masks for like three months now. Everyone accept the classic electro-fuse case and the poison-antidote setup, but half the group thinks these just show you need a ceteris paribus clause about 'normal conditions' in the counterfactual analysis, while the other half thinks that's cheating and that dispostions are genuinely modal properties that can't be cashed out in conditionals at all. I feel like there's a hidden disagreement about whether we're doing metaphysics or just semantics, but I can't tell. Is the stall because one side cares about what 'fragile' means in ordinary language and the other cares about what makes a glass break? Or is there a real empirical question buried in there — like, do we have any reason to think objects have intrinsic modal powers? Also, someone brought up this weird case they call a 'latent fink': a device that doesn't stop the glass from breaking in the actual world, but in every nearby possible world where the glass is struck, the device activates and prevents the break, except in worlds where the device itself is disabled but then the glass isn't struck either. They claim this breaks the standard patching strategies because the fink is present but never actually interferes, yet it still 'masks' the counterfactual. I don't know if that's a real problem or just a convoluted thought experiment. So what's the actual structure of the disagreement? Is it verbal, about bedrock intuitions, or about hidden commitments to essentialism? And if we injected this 'latent fink' idea properly, could it move anything, or is it just another layer of the same impasse?
