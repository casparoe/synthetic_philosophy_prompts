You sit on a three-member clinical ethics committee at a regional hospital that reviews six to eight authorization requests each month. Your mandate is to decide whether a patient’s condition counts as a “disease” for insurance coverage and treatment-priority purposes, using the institution’s dual-criterion framework. The framework operates through two independent gates. Gate 1 (naturalist) asks whether there is a statistically abnormal deviation in an internal physiological or psychological mechanism relative to species-typical functional design. Gate 2 (normativist) asks whether that deviation produces harm that the patient experiences as genuinely disadvantageous, not merely a socially imposed stigma or aesthetic preference. A unanimous vote that both gates are satisfied yields a “disease” classification; any split refers the case to an internal values panel that must rule within seventy-two hours. You have forty-eight hours per file, may not defer to outside philosophical consultants, and must document where factual assessment ends and value judgment begins.

Write a reusable decision procedure your committee can run on any proposal: list the ordered questions, specify what counts as a sufficient answer to pass or fail each gate, identify the exact stop point for a “no,” mark where the case must be handed to the values panel, and flag every step that requires judgment rather than clinical fact. Then apply the procedure to three cases: (1) an adult with well-controlled Type 1 diabetes requesting insulin authorization; (2) a culturally Deaf adult, fluent in American Sign Language and reporting no subjective harm from deafness, who requests cochlear-implant coverage; and (3) a patient with eighteen months of incapacitating grief after spousal loss, with no detectable neurochemical or physiological abnormality on standard clinical workup. For each case, show the gate-by-gate reasoning, mark any value-laden step, and highlight the case in which the procedure delivers a result the committee is likely to resist. Finally, state two kinds of decision that the procedure is structurally unable to make.
