Two people in my AI safety reading group got stuck arguing about whether you can define "avoid side effects" for a low-impact agent without fully specifying value. Alex said sure, you just constrain the agent to stay within an epsilon-ball of the baseline trajectory in some observable state-space — that's purely structural, no values needed. Blake said that's impossible because picking which variables go into the state-space and what epsilon counts as "close enough" already smuggles in a full axiology about what matters. Alex came back with: okay, then use KL divergence between the world-state distribution before and after, computed over every phsyical degree of freedom — that doesn't privilege any particular outcome, it just penalizes any change. Blake said choosing KL divergence is itself a normative commitment to preserving informational structure, and computing it requires a world-model that already decides which states are physically possible and therefore which ones we care about keeping intact. Alex said every formalism makes arbitrary representational choices, but that doesn't make them value-laden, it just means you need a convention. Blake said the convention of what counts as "the world" is exactly the value specification in disguise, since you can't include every quantum fluctuation without deciding what's noise vs signal. Neither would budge. Can you go through their exchange and say who's right at each step — not just who wins overall? And what, if anything, are they both missing?
