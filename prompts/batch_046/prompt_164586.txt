Right, so I've been thinking about this populist idea that 'the will of the people' should just run everything, and I built this argument in my head that seems to show it's either useless or destroys every institution we have, including itself. But I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher, so I'm probaly smuggling in a bug somewhere.

Here's the chain:

(1) Populists say a government is only legitimate if it tracks 'the will of the people'.
(2) But 'the people' don't have one unified mental state — they're millions of agents with noisy, conflicting preferences.
(3) So to extract a single 'will', you need an aggregation algorithm: polls, referenda, elections, whatever.
(4) Any algorithm has bias and error; it outputs a synthetic signal, not a raw mental state.
(5) Once you accept that the output is synthetic, saying 'we follow the will' just means 'we follow the algorithm'.
(6) Liberal democracies also follow algorithms (representative voting, judicial review, etc.), so populism collapses into the thing it claims to oppose.
(7) To avoid that collapse, the populist might say the algorithm must be direct and unmediated — no courts, no experts, no filtering.
(8) But then the populist leader who interprets the direct signal (rallies, polls, 'common sense') becomes the algorithm.
(9) So either authority comes from the leader's interpretation (not the people) or from an unmediated direct vote that can't handle scale.
(10) By the same logic, any collective decision — a jury, a scientific peer-review panel, even an open-source maintainer group — that uses interpretation or delegation is illegitimate.
(11) That proves too much: it doesn't just hurt populism, it makes every collective action impossible.
(12) So one of the steps is wrong, but I can't see which.

If I had to choose I'd rather favour keeping (2) and (7) — I really think collective minds are synthetic and I like the idea that mediation kills legitimacy — but I want to know what I'm paying. Where's the bug? Is it a hidden equivocation between 'signal' and 'will', or is the argument actually valid and I just have to bite a bullet?
