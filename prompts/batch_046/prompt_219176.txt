I am currently taking a course on philosophy of psychiatry, and I came across a summary of Thomas Szasz's position in an online discussion. The author claims that Szasz treats mental illness as literally a myth in the sense that conditions such as schizophrenia and depression are not diseases at all, but purely social labels applied to deviant conduct, with no real suffering or dysfunction behind them. Because these conditions are not biological diseases, the summary continues, Szasz must hold that neurology and genetics have nothing to contribute to understanding them, and that any psychiatric intervention, including voluntary psychotherapy, wrongly medicalizes moral and social problems. From this the author concludes that Szasz's own proposal for voluntary contractual relationships between psychiatrist and client is incoherent, since if there is no illness there is no patient and therefore no ground for a contract. I suspect this overstates his denial of biology and misrepresents his view of voluntary treatment, but I am unsure how to separate the accurate core from the distortions. Would you audit this characterization? Please distinguish (i) what Szasz actually meant by calling mental illness a myth and how he separated it from brain disease, (ii) whether he permitted any biological or neurological factors, (iii) his actual position on voluntary psychiatric contracts and what he called the therapeutic state, and (iv) whether the charge of incoherence stands once the summary is corrected.
