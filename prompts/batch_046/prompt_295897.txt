As a high school student working through the philosophy of uncertainty, I have been trying to understand the difference between representing ignorance as a range of precise probabilities and representing it as a distribution over sets of possibilities. Specifically, I want to compare Peter Walley's behavioral framework of imprecise probabilities—where rational belief is captured by lower and upper previsions constrained by coherence conditions—with the Dempster-Shafer theory of belief functions, which assigns mass to subsets of a frame rather than to individual points.

To keep the comparison concrete, I have constructed a decision scenario: an autonomous delivery drone must choose between two flight corridors, and for each corridor the available meteorological data is ambiguous—sensors detect conditions compatible with both safe flight and severe turbulence, but do not yield a precise likelihood. In Walley's terms, this ambiguity might be captured by an interval of probabilities for each corridor; in Dempster-Shafer terms, it yields belief and plausibility values over the power set of weather states.

Please compare the two frameworks on three points. First, how does each formalize the drone's uncertainty about the two corridors? Second, if the drone must act—say, by selecting the path that optimizes some criterion—what decision rule does each framework supply (for example, E-admissibility, maximin expected utility, or belief-weighted utility), and what justifies that rule within the framework? Third, consider an update that resembles dilation: the drone receives a further ambiguous signal, such as a partially corrupted satellite image that fails to discriminate between the corridors. In Walley's framework this may widen the probability intervals; in Dempster-Shafer it may redistribute belief mass among sets. Analyze whether this divergence is a superficial mathematical artifact or reflects a deeper disagreement about whether uncertainty should be anchored to available evidence or to a fixed space of possibilities. I am not looking for bibliographic references; I want the comparison to remain at the level of conceptual structure and decision logic.
