I have come to accept a strengthened form of Amartya Sen's entitlement-based account of famine, applied with particular rigor to the Bengal crisis of 1943, the Irish famine of 1845–1852, and the catastrophe during China's Great Leap Forward (1959–1961). On this stronger thesis, famine is essentially a political failure of redistribution: even when national food availability remained above subsistence levels, lethal deficits were produced by the suppression, corruption, or physical blockage of these cross-boundary mechanisms, often accompanied by deliberate concealment of the crisis by authorities.
