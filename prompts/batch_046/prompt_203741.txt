hey, i'm working up an objection to confucian role ethics and it's started to feel like one of those arguments that proves too much — not just wrong, but internally incoherent. here's the chain:

(1) role ethics fixes every obligation by your relationships: father/son, ruler/subject, friend/friend, etc.
(2) the confucian self is relational, not atomistic — you're literally constituted by these roles.
(3) if you're nothing but these roles, there's no substrate "behind" them that could choose to honor or betray them.
(4) so moral failure (say, a disloyal miniser) isn't an agent failing; it's just a broken relational link.
(5) but confucians constantly blame individuals and push self-cultivation (xiu shen), which needs an agent who can work on herself.
(6) therefore they either secretly smuggle in an atomistic self (contradicting 2) or they're irrationally blaming structures.
(7) also, if there's no agent apart from roles, "rectifying names" (zhengming) is impossible — you can't correct the holder of a name if the holder doesn't exist independently.
(8) so the view collapses into structural determinism with no moral agency, or it's self-contradictory.

i feel like there's a slide between metaphysical constitution and normative identity somewhere, maybe in (3), but i can't nail it. i really want to keep (2) and (5) since they feel like core commitments, but i'm willing to drop (6) or (7) if that's the price. don't lay out every option — pick the weakest step, explain the mistake, and say what fixing it costs the view. write in american english please, and put your bottom line at the end as \answer{the step number you reject}.
