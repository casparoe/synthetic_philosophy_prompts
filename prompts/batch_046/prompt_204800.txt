I am drafting the final examination for an upper-level seminar on the philosophy of science, focused on the analytic–synthetic distinction and Quine’s holistic critique of it. I need a single essay prompt that students can complete in 90 minutes (expected response: roughly 900–1100 words). The question must be unanswerable by mere summary of Quine’s Two Dogmas of Empiricism; it should compel students to apply the holistic thesis to a constructed, non-obvious case and to evaluate whether any conceptual residue of the distinction survives inside a formalized framework.

Please design the exam question around the following hypothetical scenario. A formalized scientific theory T introduces a primitive predicate “S-field” through an explicit definitional axiom A that fixes its relation to measurable energy gradients. T also contains synthetic empirical laws linking the presence of S-fields to observable thermal phenomena. A philosopher defends the analytic–synthetic divide by claiming that A is analytic within T—true solely in virtue of the meaning assigned to “S-field”—while the thermal laws remain synthetic.

The essay prompt should require students to do four things:
(1) Reconstruct Quine’s central argument that no statement, including explicit definitional axioms, is in principle immune from revision;
(2) Apply that argument specifically to axiom A, explaining why its apparent analyticity might instead be an artifact of its central position in the web of belief;
(3) Analyze the following twist: in extreme high-gravity regimes, the energy-gradient relation specified in A fails to match observation, but practitioners preserve A intact by reinterpreting “energy gradient” rather than abandoning A. Students must evaluate whether this preservation strategy vindicates A’s analytic status, or whether it exemplifies Quine’s claim that any statement can be protected from falsification by adjusting auxiliary parts of the system;
(4) Assess whether Quine’s critique leaves conceptual space for a purely local, framework-internal notion of analyticity—i.e., a distinction that holds relative to a particular formalization but carries no broader metaphysical or epistemological privilege.

Then provide:
- A model answer of approximately 800 words that executes all four moves with precision, including an explicit discussion of where the preservation strategy looks like definitional protection and where it collapses into empirical revision of auxiliary hypotheses.
- A marking scheme totaling 20 points, organized around five must-hit moves (with specific point allocations for each). For each move, include a one-line note on how candidates typically fail to secure those marks. The scheme should clearly separate an A-level answer—where the student distinguishes Quinean holism from mere conventionalism and addresses the reinterpretation strategy with care—from a B-level answer, where the moves are present but conflate holism with the claim that all beliefs are equally revisable.

Constraints: Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not quote Quine verbatim; paraphrase his claims without quotation marks. Frame the prompt so that a student with access only to standard course knowledge can respond without consulting external sources.
