I have been trying to formalise a structural account of side-effect avoidance for low-impact agent design, and I keep encountering what looks like a reductio. Here is the argument. (1) A side effect can be defined in purely structural terms: any state-transition in the environment that lies outside the causal envelope specified by the agent's objective function, with no reference to the normative content of that function. (2) Therefore, one can construct an impact measure — call it envelope-constraint — that penalises transitions outside the envelope without committing to a full axiology; the measure requires only the boundary, not the values within it. (3) But envelope-constraint applies symmetrically: a human agent deciding to make tea produces state-transitions (heat diffusion, cup displacement, metabolic change) that fall outside any narrowly formalised causal envelope for "making tea". (4) Hence, if envelope-constraint genuinely captures harmful side effects, ordinary embodied action is perpetually in violation; if it fails to capture them, the constraint is either vacuous or arbitrarily calibrated. (5) The only principled escape is to embed normative weights into the envelope itself — specifying which states must be preserved requires knowing what is valuable to preserve. (6) Consequently, "avoid side effects" cannot be operationalised without a full specification of value, and value-agnostic low-impact AI is impossible.

The conclusion strikes me as implausible — I do not take drinking tea to be a moral catastrophe — yet each premise seems defensible in isolation. I suspect an equivocation between a formally specified policy envelope and the open-ended practical intentionality of an embodied agent, but I cannot locate the precise inference that fails. Which step should be rejected, and what does rejecting it cost for the project of designing impact measures that do not presuppose a complete utility function?

Please provide your diagnosis, separating the disanalogies that matter for the intended use from those that do not, and end with a single line in the form: Verdict: [the step to reject, with a brief note on the cost].
