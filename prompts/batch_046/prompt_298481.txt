I am a retired experimental physicist who has only recently begun reading seriously in philosophy of physics, particularly on the origin of temporal asymmetry. Having worked through Boltzmann’s statistical-mechanical account of entropy increase and his reply to Loschmidt’s reversibility objection, I find myself puzzled by a contemporary development that seems structurally analogous yet conceptually distant. In current discussions of the holographic principle and unitary black-hole evaporation—where the boundary quantum theory evolves in a perfectly time-reversible manner, while bulk observers nevertheless experience a robust thermodynamic arrow—some researchers have proposed that the arrow need not be anchored in a global cosmological “past hypothesis” at all. Instead, they suggest it emerges as an observer-dependent feature tied to the entanglement-wedge structure revealed by quantum extremal surfaces, with irreversibility localized in the bulk reconstruction rather than in any low-entropy initial condition of the universe as a whole.

If Boltzmann were alive today and fully conversant with this framework, do you think he would regard it as a natural vindication of his statistical view—merely relocating the requisite low-entropy constraint into the geometry of entanglement—or as a genuine departure that renders a cosmological boundary condition superfluous? More specifically, how might he have reformulated his “past hypothesis” in a setting where the fundamental description is boundary-unitary and bulk-irreversible, and would he still insist on a single, globally asymmetric statistical postulate, or allow that the arrow could be plural and wedge-relative? I am trying to understand whether his core insight survives this translation intact, or whether the holographic framing dissolves the very problem he set out to solve.
