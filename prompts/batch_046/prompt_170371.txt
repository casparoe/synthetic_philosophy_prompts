hey, i'm in a philosphy of mind phd program and my reading group has been circling the same drain for months: are emotions judgments, perceptions, or just feelings? the judgment side (Nussbaum/Solomon) says fear is basically the belief you're in danger plus a value commitment; the perceptual side (Prinz, Döring) says it's more like seeing red, carrying affective content without being a belief; and the feeling side (James-style) says it's just the registered bodily change. we keep recycling the snake-in-the-bushes example and the same counterexample about grief outlasting belief, and nobody moves. my hunch is that we're not actually disagreeing about emotions at all, but about what mental representation has to be -- like, the judgment camp wants propositional content, the perceptual camp wants analog content, and the feeling camp thinks content is a red herring altogether. but there's this other idea i've been playing with: maybe both camps smuggle in a content/vehicle split borrowed from belief and perception, and emotions are better thought of as attunement patterns that don't decompose that way. so is the stall verbal? is there a hidden empirical question about whether subcortical pathways can carry representational content without cortex? or are we stuck on bedrock intutions about whether you can fear something you don't actually think is dangerous? and if it's the last one, is there anything -- a distinction, an experiment, a change of framing -- that could actually move it, or should we just accept that the question splits into three seperate ones?
