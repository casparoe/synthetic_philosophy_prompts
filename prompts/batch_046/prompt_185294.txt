heres an argument my study group built around the surprise exam paradox and now it proves way too much. (1) youre only surprised by p if you genuinely didnt know p was going to happen. (2) the students inductively knock out friday, thursday, etc., until only monday remains; by monday morning they can deduce it has to be today. (3) so if the exam happens monday they already expected it—no surprise. (4) generalize: in any system where rational agents can iteratively eliminate possibilities, every remaining outcome is already predicted, so nothing inside the model can ever be surprising. (5) that means surprise only exists for brute sensory noise outside your reasoning framework. (6) but im surprised by plot twists in books i fully understand, not just by loud noises. (7) so either our notion of surprise collapses, or one of the steps above is broken. which one? i want to keep that surprise requires some ignorance, but i dont want to end up saying only animals can be surprised.
