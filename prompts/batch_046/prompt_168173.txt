I have been trying to work out Quine’s idea that moral values are not out there in the world the way atoms or numbers are, but are more like shared conventions that shift from culture to culture. Since he is no longer living, I keep wondering what he would make of a problem that has only really taken shape in artificial intelligence ethics over the last couple of decades: engineers now talk about assigning precise moral weights to different kinds of minds—giving a human a weight of 1.0, a nonhuman animal something like 0.3, and a hypothetical digital consciousness maybe 0.1 or 1.2—then feeding those numbers into algorithms that decide how to distribute resources, prevent harm, or prioritize research. It strikes me that this treats morality like physics, measuring a property that is really there, yet Quine seemed to think ethics was continuous with science only in method, not in having objective entities to discover. Would he see these weights as harmless theoretical tools, the way he accepted electrons as useful posits within our best overall theory, or would he say they falsely turn social preferences into measurable realities?

But there is a second layer that puzzles me. Quine argued that translating meaning across languages is always underdetermined—there is no single correct way to map one culture’s terms onto another’s. If moral vocabulary is just as indeterminate, then an AI system built with Western moral weights might be imposing one arbitrary translation on cultures that organize value entirely differently, without even noticing the distortion. So I am asking two things together: first, whether Quine’s naturalism allows him to accept quantified moral ontology as a legitimate scientific posit, and second, whether his indeterminacy thesis undermines the whole project of universal moral algorithms that claim to be culturally neutral.

Please lead with your bottom-line verdict in the very first sentence, then lay out the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating my question. Also, tell me what specific finding or argument would change your mind—whether it is a hidden passage in Quine’s later work accepting objective moral properties, or a demonstration that moral weights can be derived without any cultural translation at all.
