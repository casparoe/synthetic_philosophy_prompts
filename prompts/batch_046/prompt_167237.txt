I heard a podcast summarise Amartya Sen's famine argument like this: famines are never about absolute food scarcity. In Bengal 43 there was enough rice pooled, in Ireland exports continued, and during the Great Leap Forward Sichuan granaries were full while people starved — so the cause is always an entitlement collapse, specifcally in trade-based entitlement, never in command-based entitlement. The speaker claimed democratic goverments prevent famine solely by fixing information asymmetries, not by changing distribution, and therefore socialist command economies are immune if they just publish honest statistics. They also said this makes every famine a political crime with no natural component. Something smells off, especially the bit about command economies being immune. Is this a fair summary? Which parts are accurate simplifications, which misrepresent Sen's distinction between trade and command entitlements, and does that socialist-immunity conclusion follow? I also wonder if conflating the 1943 Bengal figures with the 1974 Bangladesh crisis weakens the point — but maybe that's a separate issue.
