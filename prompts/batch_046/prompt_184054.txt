Write an essay on whether adopting the Solomonoff universal prior as a normative framework for decision-making under radical uncertainty commits a consequentialist agent to a form of algorithmic pessimism. By "algorithmic pessimism," I mean the claim that because simpler computable descriptions of worlds often embed self-replicating suffering-maximizing processes at lower Kolmogorov complexity than suffering-minimizing ones, the prior assigns disproportionate weight to malign outcomes. First, clarify what it means for an agent to be situated "inside" the prior—treating the universal distribution not merely as an epistemic prediction tool but as the normative weighting of possible worlds. Then examine whether the prior's simplicity bias genuinely favors worlds with extreme suffering, or whether this appearance depends on an illicit projection of human-scale moral categories onto algorithmic descriptions. Weigh the argument that consequentialists should reject the Solomonoff prior as a decision framework against the response that any prior carries analogous pathologies and that the prior's neutrality is actually a virtue. Reach and defend a determinate conclusion: either that the prior is genuinely malign for consequentialist reasoning and must be abandoned or modified, or that the malignancy is an artifact of conflating predictive weights with normative weights and can be dissolved by a proper distinction between them. Do not conclude with a non-committal balance of considerations.
