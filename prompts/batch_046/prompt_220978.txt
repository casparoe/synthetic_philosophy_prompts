Write a sixteen-line poem in four quatrains on the Frege-Geach problem for moral expressivism, arranged so that its form enacts the philosophical difficulty. In the first and third quatrains, present the sentence Torture is wrong unquoted, spoken as a direct expression of the speaker’s non-cognitive attitude. In the second and fourth quatrains, embed that identical sentence inside a logical conditional (If torture is wrong, then...) and inside a negated conjunction, both rendered in quotation marks, so that it functions purely as a compositional logical component without expressing the speaker’s attitude. Let the poem’s turn fall between the second and third quatrains, where the verbatim repetition—now stripped of expressive force—exposes the tension between Fregean compositionality and expressivist semantics. Do not resolve the paradox; end with the contradiction unreconciled. The verse should be precise enough that someone familiar with expressivism recognizes the embedding problem rather than seeing only a keyword, and it should work as poetry—using rhythm, concrete image, and emotional weight—rather than as line-broken prose. Keep the form free within the quatrain constraint, but make the quoted and unquoted distinctions visually and structurally clear.
