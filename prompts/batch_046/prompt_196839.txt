I am inclined to think she has missed something important about Dennett’s concern—namely, that the danger of counterfeit people lies not in their lacking some hidden mental essence, but in the specific kind of deceptive social relation they instantiate—yet I want to be precise about where the line is drawn.

Is the distinction purely ethical, centered on intent to deceive and the erosion of social trust, or does Dennett implicitly rely on a criterion of authentic historical embeddedness—something like a narrative biography anchored in genuine vulnerability and mortality—that functions as a hidden essentialist condition despite his official anti-essentialism?
