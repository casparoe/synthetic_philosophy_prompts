hey — i'm putting together a feature for my magazine about psychiatric categories that don't just sit there describing people but loop back and reshape them, that Hacking idea about interactive kinds. i spent an afternoon with dr. mara okonkwo, a psychiatrist in lagos who has very strong opinions on which diagnoses are looping and which ones stay put.

her verdicts so far: autism spectrum conditions are clearly looping, she says the diagnosis changes self-conception and behavior in a feedback cycle. major depressive disorder she puts in a middle zone — some looping, but anchored enough in biology that it doesn't fully self-create. ptsd she treats as basically non-looping because the trauma is external and the label doesn't alter the core event. dissociative identity disorder she calls hyper-looping, maybe even self-creating. social anxiety disorder she admits has weak looping effects. and schizophrenia she insists is not interactive at all — the phenomology is there regardless of the diagnostic frame, she says the symptoms pre-exist the label.

now there's this proposal floating around in some european clinics for something called algorithmic dissociation — basically a dissociative state where people feel their preferences aren't their own after prolonged exposure to predictive reccomendation systems. the clinicians proposing it say the symptoms cluster only because the label gives people a framework to interpret normal algorithm fatigue. so it's like a pure test case for whether a category can create the very reality it claims to describe.
