If René Descartes had encountered modern split-brain research together with the federated-consciousness hypothesis—the view that ordinary awareness is not one substance but a coalition of semi-autonomous neural streams whose synchronization is contingent, with callosotomy merely exposing underlying modular independence—predict how he would defend the unity of the thinking thing. Give exactly five points, numbered 1 through 5, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: address (1) his reinterpretation of clinical reports of hemispheric conflict, (2) whether he grants any form of micro-consciousness to isolated hemispheres, (3) his reply to the claim that mental unity is a contingent neural achievement rather than an essential property of mind, (4) whether he adjusts or hardens his substance dualism in response, and (5) his judgment on whether a single mind could be distributed across permanently disconnected neural substrates.
