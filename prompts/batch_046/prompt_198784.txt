Consider a self-monitoring cognitive system that attempts to establish the reliability of its own perceptual and inferential faculties not through independent external calibration, but through internal consistency checks—using the outputs of those very faculties to validate the rules that govern them. This generates a bootstrapping problem specifically targeted at meta-cognitive easy knowledge: the claim that an agent can come to know that it is not systematically deceived about its own reliability simply by exercising the faculties in question and observing coherence. Please describe the most important implications of this bootstrapping problem for whether such a system can ever possess legitimate easy knowledge about its own epistemic standing. In particular, address how the problem affects the distinction between first-order knowledge and second-order knowledge of reliability, whether iterative self-checking can break the circularity without importing external premises, and what this means for the possibility of a purely internalist justification of cognitive authority.
