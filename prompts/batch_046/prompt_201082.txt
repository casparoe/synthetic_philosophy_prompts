Gödel's 1949 solution describes a homogeneous universe of rotating dust together with a positive cosmological constant, arranged so that Einstein's field equations hold everywhere. Because the matter rotates uniformly about every point, the geometry of spacetime allows closed timelike curves—continuous worldlines that return to the same spacetime event, meaning an observer could travel along one and encounter their own past. Gödel interpreted this not as a paradox but as proof that the distinction between past and future is not physically fundamental: if a single continuous path can contain both, then temporal succession is subjective, and time itself is an illusion in the idealist sense. He presented the model primarily as a demonstration that Mach's principle fails inside general relativity, since the rotation is absolute rather than relative to distant matter. Although he offered it as a serious cosmological candidate, he noted that no cosmic rotation had yet been observed. Einstein, in his published response, agreed entirely with Gödel's philosophical interpretation and declared that general relativity therefore supports an idealist metaphysics in which objective time does not exist.
