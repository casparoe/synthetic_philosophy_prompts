I am a retired ecologist who has lately taken up philosophy of science through a local reading group, and I have become rather stuck on the debate surrounding self-sampling and self-indication assumptions in anthropic reasoning, particularly the difficulty raised by what is sometimes called the presumptuous philosopher. Having spent decades thinking about sampling bias and effective sample sizes in spatially clustered field data, I keep wanting to translate the problem into statistical terms, but I am unsure whether the translation illuminates or distorts the philosophical point.

My own verdicts on particular cases are these. First, when comparing cosmological theories that differ only in the total number of conscious observers they predict—say, ten to the tenth versus ten to the fiftieth—I feel strongly that one ought not to favour the latter by an enormous factor in the absence of independent empirical evidence; that strikes me as precisely the kind of reasoning the presumptuous philosopher objection is meant to block. Second, within a single fixed universe, I am inclined to apply a self-sampling intuition to my own existence: I should reason as if I were randomly drawn from the appropriate reference class of observers, which makes my being human rather than, say, a minimally sentient organism somewhat surprising but not irrational, without invoking self-indication to weight human-capable worlds more heavily. Third, I have recently imagined a scenario in which observers arise not independently but in tightly correlated evolutionary clusters—analogous to the spatial autocorrelation one encounters in ecological plot sampling—so that the raw number of observers overstates the number of genuinely independent observer-instances. In that situation, I think both self-sampling and self-indication ought to be adjusted by something like an effective sample size correction, but I cannot tell whether that adjustment belongs to the prior over worlds or to the sampling rule within them.

Could there be a single coherent principle that unifies these three judgements—perhaps one that sharply distinguishes cross-world reasoning about cosmic parameters from intra-world indexical reasoning, and that allows for correlation-corrected reference classes? Or do my verdicts pull in incompatible directions, forcing me to drop at least one? I should be grateful for a careful analysis, and please write your answer in British English.
