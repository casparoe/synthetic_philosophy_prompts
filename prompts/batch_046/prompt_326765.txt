I've been stuck on why the debate about conceivability as a guide to metaphysical possibility never actually moves in my philosophy seminar. One camp keeps saying that if you can clearly and distinctly conceive of water not being H2O—no contradiction in the idea—then you've shown it's metaphysically possible, which is why Kripke's necessary a posteriori doesn't block the general principle. The other camp keeps replying that 'clear and distinct' is just a psychological feel, and that conceivability tracks our representational limits, not modal space; they point to zombie-style thought experiments but say all they prove is an epistemic gap, not an ontological one. Both sides have been recycling the same two-dimensional semantics moves and appeals to ideal rational agents for like fifteen years, and nobody shifts an inch.

My hunch is that it's partly verbal—'conceivability' is doing double duty as a cognitive capacity and as a logical relation—but partly a deeper fight about whether modal knowledge comes from pure reason or empirical discovery. Still, I'm not sure that's the real stall, or if there's a hidden formal issue about modal operators under S4 versus S5 that neither side is addressing. So is this a substantive disagreement about the source of modal knowledge, a verbal mix-up, or are they actually asking seperate questions—one about psychology, one about metaphysics—under the same heading? And if you had to diagnose it properly, is there any distinction—maybe between first-order modal imagination and second-order modal judgment, or between 'thin' and 'thick' conceivability—that could actually break the deadlock, or is the impasse a sign the whole framework is ill-posed?
