I am a high school senior tasked with leading a fifty-minute philosophy club session for peers who have encountered Aristotle’s concept of hexis but have no background in empirical psychology. I want to teach the situationist critique of virtue ethics—not as a simple debunking narrative, but as a precise conceptual pressure point on the predictive interpretation of character. To do this, I need the session to pivot on a distinction I am framing as “domain-indexed dispositions” versus global traits, and I want students to see that the empirical problem of low cross-situational consistency—the personality coefficient debate—only undermines Aristotelian virtue if hexis is taken as a descriptive predictor of behavior rather than as a normative ideal of practical reasoning.

Please do not explain the philosophy to me; instead, plan the pedagogical architecture. First, lay out the dependency order: which concept must be secured before the situationist data can register as a philosophical problem rather than as an anecdote about hypocrisy? Second, specify the single carrying example I should build around: it must place the same agent in two structurally parallel moral contexts with sharply different outcomes, it must make the domain-indexing idea visible, and it must not be the standard parking-meter or exam-cheating scenario drawn from Doris and Harman. Third, map the predictable misconceptions in the order students will form them—I expect they will first conflate behavioral inconsistency with practical irrationality, then infer from low predictive correlation that virtue ethics is false, and finally collapse the normative claim that one ought to cultivate stable character with the descriptive claim that people already possess it—indicating exactly where in the sequence each should be intercepted and with which conceptual distinction. Fourth, provide one closing diagnostic question that tests whether they grasp the critique’s actual scope—whether it forces virtue ethics to retreat to domain-indexed virtue or genuinely dissolves it—rather than testing recall of a correlation value. Finally, state explicitly what should be left out at this level, such as the full Milgram obedience narrative or technical meta-analyses of trait consistency, and say so openly.
