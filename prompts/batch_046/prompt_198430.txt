Please explain a conceptual issue in Strawson's account of moral responsibility that arises when we consider what might be termed displaced reactive practices. By this I mean reactive attitudes—such as resentment, gratitude, or moral indignation—directed at targets that lack the capacity for reciprocal emotional engagement: for instance, artificial agents, collective entities without unified consciousness, or agents separated from the subject by irrecoverable temporal distance. Focus on whether such attitudes can genuinely remain within the participant reactive attitude framework, or whether the absence of direct interpersonal reciprocity necessarily pushes them into the objective attitude that Strawson contrasts with it. Further, discuss what your answer implies for Strawson's thesis that moral responsibility is rooted in our emotional involvement in direct interpersonal relationships rather than in assessments of causal efficacy, desert, or institutional role. Confine your analysis to the internal logic of the reactive-attitudes framework; do not appeal to empirical studies, legal doctrine, or secondary literature.
