There's this weird split in how autonomous systems are regulated — think drone strikes or self-driving freight — where rules require a human supervisor to be present and sign a "control attestation" saying they could've intervened. But when harm happens, that supervisor is almost never personally blamed; the firm or state pays strict liability, and the human gets treated like a procedural checkbox rather than a real moral agent. What view of responsability does this design preseuppose? Is it assuming blame needs direct causal control, or is responsibility just being parked in a human body for legal formality? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether it can stay consistent with both control-based and role-based accounts, and tell me what'd have to change in the law if we actually meant that only people who understand the system should carry the liability.
