I'm trying to determine what I actually think about psychiatric looping effects—not whether they happen, but which ones are genuine cases of a classification reshaping the people it describes, as opposed to misapplication, social labeling without internalization, or performative adoption that doesn't really alter underlying experience. Here are my verdicts on concrete scenarios.

Case 1: The North American "multiple personality disorder" surge of the 1980s. After DSM-III recognized the diagnosis and media narratives spread, clinicians began encountering patients with highly standardized alter-personality structures—often with exactly the number and type of alters described in clinical texts—and prevalence rose sharply. My verdict: this is a paradigmatic looping effect; the diagnostic category genuinely reshaped how some people experienced and expressed their distress.

Case 2: The post-DSM-5 consolidation of autism spectrum disorder and the subsequent growth of adult self-identification communities. As the category broadened, online and offline groups developed shared vocabularies, masking strategies, and advocacy frameworks; clinicians then began recognizing "high-masking" presentations that closely matched these community narratives. My verdict: this is also a looping effect, but of a different kind—it seems to stabilize and reveal a hidden population rather than corrupt the category.

Case 3: A niche 2023 proposal for "algorithmic dysphoria"—distress arising specifically from the awareness that one's preferences are being predicted by recommendation algorithms. The term was introduced in a philosophy-of-technology paper; within months a subreddit adopted it, members circulated symptom checklists, and a small number of therapists began documenting cases that matched the checklists with remarkable uniformity. My verdict: this is a loop, but a "thin" one; the symptoms appear to be direct translations of the definitional criteria rather than organically reshaped experience. Still, I am not sure whether this distinguishes it from Case 1 or merely describes Case 1 at an earlier stage.

Case 4: The importation of "neurasthenia" into late Qing China and Meiji Japan. Western clinicians brought the diagnosis, but local patients presented with somatic complaints—fatigue, dizziness, digestive disturbance—that matched indigenous idioms of distress rather than the Western "nervous exhaustion" script. Over time, the local clinical meaning of the term drifted to accommodate these presentations, while patients themselves did not begin organizing their experience around the Western concept. My verdict: not a looping effect in the relevant sense; the arrow runs from local culture to classification, not back from classification to person.

Case 5: The viral spread of adult ADHD self-diagnosis checklists on social media platforms around 2021–2023. Users encountered lists of "ADHD traits" (rejecting authority, hyperfocus on niche interests), recognized themselves, sought formal diagnosis, and presented to clinicians with symptom clusters that closely mirrored the viral lists. My verdict: I think there is a loop here, but I keep going back and forth on whether it is genuinely analogous to Case 1 or whether it is better understood as heightened awareness of pre-existing traits. Sometimes it feels manufactured; sometimes it feels like discovery aided by a new vocabulary.

My question: is there a single principle—or a small family of them—that captures why Cases 1, 2, and 3 are loops (with 3 being "thin"), why Case 4 is not, and that can settle Case 5? Please state the principle precisely, identify which clause is motivated by which case, and apply it to a scenario I have not listed: a classification of "clinician-only looping," in which psychiatrists begin seeing a proposed syndrome everywhere after reading about it, altering their diagnostic practices, but the patients themselves never learn the label or adjust their self-understanding.

Crucially, please also tell me which of my verdicts your principle must overturn to remain coherent and simple. I especially want to know whether Case 3 and Case 5 have to be collapsed into Case 1, or whether one of them has to be expelled from the loop category altogether.

Please do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I am asking for conceptual analysis, not scholarship.
