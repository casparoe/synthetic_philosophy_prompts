Following a prolonged argument with a friend over coffee in the department common room—he insisted that the well-known divergence in moral behaviour between WEIRD and non-WEIRD populations proves moral relativism outright—I am now preparing to present a paper at an interdisciplinary philosophy and cognitive science seminar next Thursday. My thesis is that the documented cross-cultural variability on dimensions such as purity, authority, and loyalty undermines only what I term "thick" moral objectivity, meaning full convergence on specific normative content, while leaving intact a "thin" procedural objectivity grounded in shared evolutionary constraints on cooperation and minimal harm-avoidance. Specifically, I argue that even highly divergent moral systems presuppose an implicit moral grammar with universal syntactic structures—reciprocity tracking, proportional punishment, and in-group coordination—even when their lexical content differs radically; consequently, the variability observed in Haidt-style moral foundations is best interpreted as parameter-setting within a fixed universal framework rather than as evidence for radical incommensurability or pure cultural construction.

The audience is precisely defined: a reading group comprising two committed metaethical naturalists who believe evolutionary convergence supports robust realism, one cultural anthropologist who is deeply sceptical that any proposed "universal grammar" avoids being a disguised Western liberal framework, and three philosophy graduate students familiar with Moral Foundations Theory but prone to charging arguments that derive normative conclusions from descriptive psychology with committing the naturalistic fallacy. They care intensely about whether empirical cross-cultural data can bear normative weight without collapsing into either moral imperialism or an unprincipled relativism.

Please prepare me for the objections in roughly the order they will arise, phrased as each faction would phrase them: begin with the anthropologist's charge that "thin objectivity" merely rebrands parochial values in universalist clothing, followed by the realists' claim that evolutionary convergence at best supports instrumental rationality rather than genuine moral norms, then the graduate students' worry about deriving an "ought" of procedural objectivity from the cooperative "is" of evolutionary psychology. For each objection, indicate what I should concede, what I should hold firm on, and where my reply is weakest. Crucially, include also the objection I am hoping nobody asks—namely, how I account for cultures in which the concept of harm itself is structured around ritual pollution, spiritual contamination, or status degradation rather than physical or psychological injury, given that my thin base is anchored in harm-avoidance. Provide this as a prep sheet, not a polished defence: objections ordered by likelihood and damage, each with a brief reply strategy and a note on vulnerability. Keep the entire response within 700 words, and please use American English spelling and punctuation.
