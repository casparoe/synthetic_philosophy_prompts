A close friend spent ten years apprenticing under a craftsperson who now has advanced dementia and does not recognize her. The family insists she owes no further care, arguing that obligation requires a present, mutual relationship, which is now gone. My friend disagrees: she teaches using her mentor's methods, works under a professional name drawn from that apprenticeship, and feels that abandoning her now would be like discarding part of herself. She asks whether her duty can be grounded in the fact that her identity and capacities were formed through that bond, even though direct recognition has disappeared. Without giving generic advice, can you lay out the philosophical structure—specifically whether a view that treats personhood as constituted by such relationships rather than as an internal, fixed property can make her position coherent, what it implies about obligations to people who have radically changed, and whether "I wouldn't be this person without that bond" is enough to generate a real duty? Answer in one paragraph, no more than 170 words.
