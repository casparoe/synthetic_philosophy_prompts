I'm developing a normative framework for classifying synthetic cognitive architectures—large language models, reinforcement-learning agents, embodied robots, and hybrid systems—using Daniel Dennett's taxonomy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures as a structural baseline. The goal is not biological description but regulatory adequacy: the taxonomy must help determine which oversight expectations apply to which system, without assuming that synthetic development recapitulates evolutionary history. Before I adopt this framework, I need to know exactly what it must be able to do to count as adequate for that purpose.

Write the full list of desiderata that any such taxonomy must satisfy. For each requirement, give the motivating case or consideration behind it: for example, it must distinguish architectures that learn solely through environmental selection (Darwinian hardwiring or Skinnerian conditioning) from those that construct and evaluate internal models before acting (Popperian simulation), because conflating a model-free reinforcement-learning agent with a world-modeling system produces wrong safety expectations about anticipatory behavior; it must accommodate Gregorian cultural absorption—systems that deploy language, tools, or norms created by others—without requiring that they also possess Popperian foresight or biological reproduction, since large language models inherit cultural content through training data rather than through evolutionary or trial-and-error pathways; and it must allow hybrid and recombinant architectures that combine capacities in non-biological orders, rather than enforcing a strict linear ladder from Darwinian to Gregorian. Specify which desiderata are non-negotiable for any classification scheme that could guide policy, and which reflect preferences of particular camps—such as whether evolutionary linearity must be preserved, whether biological analogies are essential, or whether empirical equivalence with Dennett's original biological framing is required. If any subset of these requirements proves jointly unsatisfiable—say, if maintaining a strict hierarchy necessarily excludes Gregorian capacities in substrates that lack both Darwinian selection and Skinnerian conditioning—state that result explicitly, since identifying the conflict is the point. Keep the weight on the criteria and their justifications; do not provide a full scorecard of existing architectures, though brief illustrative references to model classes are welcome if they clarify the requirements.
