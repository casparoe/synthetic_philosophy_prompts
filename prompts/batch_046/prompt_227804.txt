Writing for a long-form piece on the limits of rational self-trust, I want to establish the evaluation criteria before surveying any proposed resolutions. The problem is familiar from doxastic logic: Löb’s theorem shows that an agent structured to trust its own reasoning—accepting that belief in P entails P—gets driven to believe every proposition it considers. Call this the Löbian obstacle. Before I assess fixes, I need the full specification for any adequate theory that lets a rational agent consistently defer to its own judgments without collapsing into universal belief.

Give me the desiderata: the phenomena the theory must explain (for instance, why an agent should sometimes endorse its past inferences but reject others, or why it can recognize its own contradictions without believing them), the distinctions it must preserve (between local and global self-deference, between trusting a specific rule and trusting the entire belief set, between first-order belief and higher-order endorsement), and the formal constraints it cannot violate (the conditions that trigger Löb’s result, basic consistency, avoidance of logical omniscience). For each, state the motivating case or consideration behind it, and flag which are genuinely non-negotiable versus which reflect camp-specific preferences—such as whether transparency of reasoning must be preserved or whether opaque computation is permissible.

Also, tell me whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable given the formal results, and identify which single requirement eliminates the largest share of candidate accounts. I am especially interested in whether a viable theory must allow graded or partial self-trust rather than an all-or-nothing attitude, and what that partial relation must formally involve.

If this request conflates a result in modal logic with a substantive psychological claim about real agents, or if the task presupposes that human reasoners possess the kind of precisely structured belief states that Löb’s framework requires, push back on that plainly. Don’t comply politely with a confused framing.
