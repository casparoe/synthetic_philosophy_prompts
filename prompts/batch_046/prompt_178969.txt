I'm grading final examinations for a graduate seminar on social epistemology, and I need an independent, point-by-point assessment of one student response. The exam asked students to apply Miranda Fricker's framework—specifically the distinction between testimonial injustice, in which a speaker receives a credibility deficit due to identity prejudice, and hermeneutical injustice, in which a gap in collective interpretive resources prevents a social experience from being properly understood—to a clinical scenario that I constructed for the paper. The case involves a patient from an agricultural community who describes chronic neuropathic pain through metaphors drawn from seasonal crop cycles ("it rots like unharvested grain in wet soil," "it spreads like blight through the lower fields"). The attending physician, working exclusively within biomedical taxonomies, charts "disorganized thought patterns" and "poor reality testing," refers the patient to psychiatry rather than neurology, and withholds pain management. The exam question required students to determine whether the harm is primarily a credibility deficit, a structural gap in shared interpretive resources, or an interaction of both, and to be precise about whether intentional prejudice is necessary for either form of injustice.

The student answer reads as follows. "This is clearly a case of testimonial injustice. The physician gives the patient a credibility deficit because of her rural background and non-standard language, treating her as unreliable rather than as someone with legitimate knowledge about her own body. The hermeneutical injustice part is that the patient herself doesn't have the biomedical vocabulary to explain her pain, so she has to rely on agricultural metaphors, which puts her at a disadvantage. Because the physician intends to dismiss her—writing 'poor reality testing' shows active prejudice—the harm is primarily intentional testimonial injustice. Hermeneutical injustice isn't really relevant here because the problem isn't missing shared concepts; it's that the physician refuses to listen. The gap is in the patient's vocabulary, not in the collective interpretive framework, since doctors already have the right concepts and the patient lacks them."

Please assign a grade out of twenty points, with explicit point allocations for each element of the answer. Your assessment should identify exactly what is correct—such as the recognition that a credibility deficit is present—and exactly what is wrong, including any conflations between the two forms of injustice, misattributions about where the hermeneutical gap is located, or erroneous claims about the necessity of intentional prejudice. Provide specific comments that justify each credit or deduction, and do not signal your overall judgment of the answer's quality until after you have distributed the points. Do not include bibliographic recommendations, reading lists, or citations to specific texts.
