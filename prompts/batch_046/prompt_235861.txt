I have been using image-generation tools for several months and keep noticing a pattern I do not have a word for. When I request a portrait in the manner of a well-known painter, the result copies no identifiable work, yet every output shares the same invisible center of gravity: the lighting is always a neutral, perfect glow that belongs to no single school, the proportions are ideal in a way that averages every tradition rather than committing to one, and the background motifs feel drawn from everywhere and nowhere at once. Landscapes and abstract compositions behave the same way — technically varied, yet together they form a tight cluster around what I can only call a hidden median taste, as though abundance of examples pulls the system away from any extreme and toward a safe statistical middle. I know terms like pastiche, mode collapse, and the uncanny valley, but this is not direct copying, not a total failure of variety, and not the discomfort of near-human likeness; it is a richness that secretly orbits an invisible consensus. Is there an established concept for this convergence toward a statistical center that looks like diversity but feels like sameness? How does it differ from ordinary stylistic blending, from plagiarism, and from technical mode collapse? If no precise term exists, what should I call it, and where are its edges — for instance, does the same gravitational pull appear in music or text produced the same way, and can it ever be escaped by asking for something deliberately extreme?
