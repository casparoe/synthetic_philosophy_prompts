Consider the following judgments made by an epistemologist about whether a hearer acquires testimonial knowledge in five situations, where in each case the claim is true and the source is generally competent. First, a credentialed expert explains a technical point face-to-face, and the hearer understands it but cannot verify it independently; she judges this yields knowledge. Second, the same claim appears in an anonymous post on an institutional forum that requires expert verification, and the hearer checks that verification is in place; she judges this yields knowledge. Third, a trusted friend relays the claim and correctly names the expert, but misstates the expert's institutional affiliation; she judges this does not yield knowledge. Fourth, a stranger with no identifiable credentials makes the claim casually; she judges this does not yield knowledge. Fifth, the claim is delivered by an automated aggregation system that synthesizes peer-reviewed literature without belief or understanding; the hearer knows the system is highly accurate but does not examine the underlying sources; she judges this does not yield knowledge.

Now apply her reasoning to a new case. A researcher receives a statistical finding from a colleague who belongs to a well-known consortium. The colleague clearly explains the methodology, but the researcher later learns that the colleague misremembered which specific certified dataset the consortium had approved—though the dataset actually used was also certified. The researcher understands the finding, trusts the consortium's general reliability, but holds a false belief about the particular dataset.

Predict her verdict. More importantly, identify at least two competing rules—such as pure anti-reductionism, a reductionism requiring non-circular speaker-justification, a "no false lemmas" condition, or an institutional-tracking condition—that fit all five earlier judgments. Say what each rule predicts about the new case, explain which rule you think she is actually running and why, and specify one further case that would sharply distinguish the two leading rules.
