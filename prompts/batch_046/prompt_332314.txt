I'm preparing a seminar contribution on Jain epistemology and its implications for moral discourse, and I want a consistency check on the package I've assembled. My commitments are these:

(1) Anekāntavāda, in its classical formulation, holds that every substance possesses infinitely many attributes, and any single predication expresses only a partial truth—a naya.
(2) Syādvāda is not merely a linguistic convention but a metaphysical claim: the same object can genuinely bear contradictory properties in different respects, so that "S is P" and "S is not P" can both be true under appropriate conditions.
(3) Moral realism is correct: there are objective, mind-independent moral facts, and some acts are genuinely wrong irrespective of perspective.
(4) Ahiṃsā applies to speech: one ought to avoid causing harm through verbal communication, which permits withholding a true but destructive description of a situation or person.
(5) Such withholding is not deception. Deception requires the affirmation of a falsehood; framing, omission, or selecting one true naya over others asserts nothing false and therefore does not constitute lying.
(6) Because every communicative act selects a particular naya, ethical speech is not about maximal disclosure but about selecting the aspect appropriate to the listener's capacity and the demand of non-harm.

Please audit these for joint tenability. For each pair in real tension, state the severity, identify exactly what pulls against what, and specify the cheapest revision—being explicit about what I would have to sacrifice. In particular, examine whether (3) survives the radical multiplicity of (1) and (2); whether (5) remains stable once syādvāda makes falsehood itself respect-relative; and whether (6) collapses into a communicative relativism incompatible with (3). Also identify at least one pairing that looks problematic but dissolves on closer inspection. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them equally. Finally, indicate which single commitment, if revised or dropped, would resolve the greatest number of conflicts with the least overall damage to the framework.
