I am drafting a feature for a science-and-ideas publication on whether epistemic risk undermines long-range moral planning. For the piece, I need a structured adversarial-collaboration document—something both sides could sign—addressing whether belief in a simulation scenario should reduce the urgency with which rational agents pursue far-future outcomes.

Please construct the joint statement that a Simulation-Discount camp and a Simulation-Resilient camp should produce after sustained collaboration.

The Simulation-Discount camp holds that assigning non-negligible credence to being embedded in a computationally bounded simulation introduces a decision-theoretic truncation: the causal pathway from present action to a base-reality distant future is probabilistically severed or nested, thereby lowering the expected moral weight of far-future interventions relative to near-term certainties, and dampening the fanaticism—extreme present-resource allocation to low-probability distant states—that characterizes some longtermist reasoning.

The Simulation-Resilient camp holds that the canonical simulation argument either fails to apply to normative reasoning under uncertainty, or that nested architectures preserve utility propagation in ways that leave long-range expected-value calculus essentially intact; consequently, far-future concern should not be discounted on simulation grounds alone.

Please deliver the document in the following form:

1. Numbered agreed claims. List the propositions both camps can endorse without reservation—covering the logical form of the simulation argument, the definition of longtermist fanaticism as extreme allocation to distant outcomes under uncertainty, the distinction between epistemic and moral discounting, and the current limits of knowledge about nested computational architectures.

2. A disagreements table. Present the precise formulations of each camp's view in wording that its own advocates would accept, distinguishing verbal disagreements from substantive ones. Identify at least two cruxes: first, whether simulation credence introduces a genuine truncation of cross-reality utility; second, whether base-reality status carries intrinsic moral weight independent of expected outcome.

3. For each crux, provide a concrete conceptual case or argument both sides agree is relevant, and record each camp's advance prediction about what that case demonstrates. Include one case that, if resolved in one direction, yields a conclusion the Simulation-Discount camp will find unwelcome, and one that yields a conclusion unwelcome to the Simulation-Resilient camp.

4. A closing section that marks exactly where judgment is irreducible—where the dispute turns on values, for instance whether moral concern should scale with the ontological status of a reality, rather than on empirical or logical facts—and states explicitly what the reasoning can and cannot settle.

The document should stand on conceptual reasoning alone: do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and avoid appeals to obscure formalisms that a well-read generalist could not evaluate. Write in a formal, polished register appropriate for publication.
