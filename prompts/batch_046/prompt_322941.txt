Write an essay on the Fischer controversey and whether it acutally makes sense to blame a whole nation for the First World War. Don't just rehearse the standard clash about the Schlieffen Plan versus alliance entanglement — instead, treat it as a problem of multi-causal explanation: is saying 'Germany caused it' closer to blaming one driver for a pile-up, or closer to assigning liability to a corporation where loads of different departments all signed off on bad decisions? Weigh the idea that national blame is just a compressed way of saying Berlin's decision-makers carried outsized causal weight, against the worry that once you take Russian mobilisation, French intransigence and British entry seriously, the notion of 'the guilty party' dissolves into pure rhetoric. Reach an actual verdict: can holding a nation-state responsible survive the overdetermination problem in a philosophically coherent way, or is it always going to be a kind of political theatre? You don't need a bibliography or reading list — just argue the point and keep it grounded in the real historical dispute.
