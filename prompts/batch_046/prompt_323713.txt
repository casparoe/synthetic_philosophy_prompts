I am a novelist drafting a manuscript about the quiet despair of formalists—those who believe every question yields to sufficient patience with the rules—and I have become fixated on Hofstadter’s MU-puzzle not as a logic exercise but as an emotional architecture: the precise moment when a mind realizes that the cage in which it has been reasoning possesses no exit reachable from within. To explore this, I am convening a ninety-minute seminar for twelve philosophy graduate students in a windowless reading room; I want them not to be told about jumping out of the system, but to suffer its absence bodily. Design a self-contained classroom game—complete with simple memorized rules, hidden structural invariants, assigned roles, and a token-based payoff scheme using only symbolic stakes or social capital—that forces the group to pursue a tangible collective objective, such as constructing a specific symbol-string or ratifying a formal consensus statement, that is rendered permanently unreachable by the internal logic of the rules alone. Arrange the incentives so that collaboration initially thrives, then fractures into factions: one insisting on ever-more-rigorous application of the permitted moves, the other beginning to suspect that rigor itself is the trap. Include a precise debrief that maps the moment of collective failure onto the concept of JOOTSing, but be frank about the feature the activity can never reproduce—namely, the genuinely solitary, often wordless cognitive leap from formal manipulation to meta-level insight, which here must be staged, spoken, and shared. Also specify what will predictably go wrong on the first run—perhaps one participant intuiting the invariant too early and dissolving the group’s necessary frustration, or the class misreading the puzzle as a mere trick rather than a structural boundary—and explain how to recover without breaking the spell. Do not assign readings, cite texts, or ask students to have encountered any particular source beforehand; the idea must live entirely in what happens between them, not in what they have previously memorized.
