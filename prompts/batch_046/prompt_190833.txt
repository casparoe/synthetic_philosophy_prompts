I just finished a long, somewhat heated discussion with a colleague about whether Occam’s razor is actually grounded in anything formal, or whether it is merely an aesthetic preference that scientists dress up as methodological virtue. We kept circling Solomonoff induction and Kolmogorov complexity—how the universal prior weights hypotheses by program length in a universal description language, but also how no working practitioner actually operates in such a language. My friend insisted that in practice “simplicity” collapses to parameter counting, which makes the principle circular once you pick your representation. I think there is a defensible middle ground, but I need to see whether it can survive being turned into a decision protocol I can actually defend in writing.

I am a conservation-technology review officer. Every Tuesday I have twenty minutes to approve or reject proposals that want to merge two previously independent environmental monitoring systems—say, an acoustic wildlife-sensor array and a hydrological flow-monitoring network—under a single unified predictive model. The stakes are concrete: an unjustified merge burns limited budget and risks publishing spurious “integrated ecosystem dynamics”; an unjustified separation misses genuine coupled processes and duplicates field infrastructure. I do not have time to compute Kolmogorov complexity, I do not have a mathematician on call, and my director, who signs off, expects a one-page justification citing explicit steps. I need a reusable decision procedure—a checklist or flowchart in words—that translates Solomonoff induction and the formal presumption of simplicity into something I can run in under fifteen minutes, apply consistently across proposals, and audit later.

The procedure must explicitly handle language-relativity: a differential-equation model may be shorter in a physics-oriented description language, while a neural-network model may be shorter in a representation-learning language, and I must audit which language I am implicitly adopting before comparing “program lengths.” It must also distinguish Kolmogorov complexity in principle from practical compressibility given our current code libraries, calibration constraints, and field-maintainability requirements. Every step where judgment is unavoidable—not merely where a number is missing—must be flagged as a judgment call dressed up as procedure, with the failure mode if it is hidden.

Please deliver three things.

First, the reusable procedure itself: ordered steps or tests, each annotated with (a) the theoretical principle being operationalized, (b) the practical proxy used given my constraints, (c) a boolean judgment-flag marking where judgment cannot be eliminated, and (d) the specific failure mode if the step is skipped or treated as purely mechanical.

Second, apply the procedure to three hypothetical proposals:
1. A forty-parameter unified neural model linking bird-call density to river turbidity versus two separate ten-parameter regression models.
2. A proposal to merge soil-moisture and satellite-vegetation data using a single three-equation physical model with shared boundary conditions versus separate empirical models with no shared terms.
3. One case where the procedure returns a verdict I will find unwelcome—either forcing approval of a merge that feels intuitively overreaching, or forcing rejection of an elegant unified theory I personally favor.

For each case, state the inputs, the step-by-step application, the verdict, and the specific judgment call triggered.

Third, include an explicit “what this procedure cannot do” section describing limitations that remain even if every step is followed perfectly.

For formatting, present the overall procedure as a JSON object with these fields: step_number, theoretical_principle, practical_proxy, judgment_flag, failure_mode_if_skipped, notes. Present the three worked cases in a table with these columns: Case_Name; Unified_Model_Description; Separate_Model_Description; Key_Language_Choice_Issue; Procedure_Verdict; Unwelcome_Verdict?; Judgment_Call_Triggered. Do not ask me for bibliographies, citation lists, or reading recommendations—this must be runnable without searching the literature, and it should not depend on my guessing at specific titles or dates I might misremember.
