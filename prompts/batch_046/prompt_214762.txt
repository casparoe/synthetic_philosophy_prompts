You serve on the university's Joint Committee on Intellectual Property and Synthetic Biology. A research group has applied for institutional backing to file a patent treating their fully synthetic bacterial genome as a software-like "informational object." Their genome uses a complete artificial translation table—every codon is reassigned so that the sequence has no evolutionary correlation to natural proteins—and they argue that, because phenotypic output now depends solely on an arbitrarily assigned syntactic rule set, the nucleic acids are no longer biological substrates but a pure code in the literal, not metaphorical, sense. The opposing brief, submitted by the faculty senate, holds that nucleic acids remain causally embedded chemical entities regardless of reassignment; on this view, "code" is an indispensable but ultimately operational metaphor that cannot determine legal classification.

Please draft the committee's decision memo for circulation before next Tuesday's vote. Recommend one concrete course of action—approve institutional support for the patent filing, deny it, or approve subject to conditions with a specified review timeline—and provide reasoning the researchers could actually respond to. The memo should treat seriously the strongest conceptual argument on the losing side, particularly whether semantic content can be decoupled from evolutionary history in a synthetic system. Do not request citations or external reading lists; reason directly from the philosophical distinction at stake.
