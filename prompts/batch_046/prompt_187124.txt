I am working through a philosophy-of-action module that uses the classic sphexishness problem—the digger wasp Sphex ichneumoneus and its stereotyped, loop-prone burrow-checking ritual—as a lens on whether mechanical rigidity excludes free will. My study group has split into two camps that keep talking past each other, and I want to see whether they can produce a genuine adversarial-collaboration document. On one side are the Flexibilists, who hold that free will requires what they term context-breaking responsiveness: an agent must be able to suspend an ongoing behavioral script when confronted with information that does not match the script's built-in release conditions, and they maintain that any system fully capturable by a fixed-state transition graph—no matter how large—is sphexish in the philosophically decisive sense and therefore lacks freedom. On the other side are the Structuralists, who grant that sphexish behavior looks rigid at the level of observable action, but deny that surface variability is the right criterion; they argue that freedom resides in the internal architecture of evaluation and choice, so that an agent could exhibit highly repetitive, loop-like motor patterns yet still exercise free will if its deliberative structures genuinely weigh alternatives, and they further contend that the Flexibilists illegitimately conflate behavioral flexibility with agential autonomy.

Please draft the joint statement these two camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. The document should contain four parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: about the wasp's actual behavior, about whether determinism is part of the dispute or a separate issue, about what kinds of behavioral or neural evidence could in principle distinguish sphexish from non-sphexish agency, and about whether artificial or simulated agents could serve as relevant test cases. Second, a disagreements table or list in which each camp's position is phrased in wording its own proponents would accept; I take the central cruxes to be (a) whether internal deliberation without observable behavioral variance can still constitute free will, and (b) whether there exists a non-arbitrary complexity threshold for behavioral loops below which an agent is sphexish and above which it is not. Third, for each crux, specify a concrete hypothetical case or line of argument that both sides agree would bear on the question, and record in advance what each camp predicts the outcome or assessment would be. Fourth, a brief concluding analysis identifying any portion of their disagreement that is merely verbal—specifying exactly which terms, such as flexibility or deliberation, are being used with different extensions—and explaining why resolving that verbal divergence does not settle the substantive dispute. Write formally, do not request citations or reading lists, and ensure that every claim in the agreed section is one both sides would genuinely endorse rather than a diplomatic vagueness.
