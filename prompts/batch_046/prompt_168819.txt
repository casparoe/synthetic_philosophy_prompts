I'm a novelist working on a long-form manuscript about the architecture of satisfaction, and before I present my philosophical profile to a workshop I want to know whether it holds together. Here are my commitments. (1) I am a hedonist about well-being: pleasure is the sole intrinsic good, and pain the sole intrinsic bad; I hold this partly because narrative justice seems reducible to the distribution of pleasure across characters and readers. (2) I accept the heterogeneity objection in full: the pleasure of a perfectly weighted sentence, the malicious pleasure of an enemy's failure, the bodily pleasure of warmth, and the intellectual pleasure of resolving a plot contradiction share no common phenomenal quality, and attempts to rescue a "positive feeling tone" are empty abstractions. (3) I reply not descriptively but normative-functionally: what unifies pleasures is not an inner feel but their place in practical reasoning—they are mental states pursued for their own sake, and this normative role, not phenomenology, is what grounds hedonism. (4) I think evolutionary debunking applies with full force: pleasure mechanisms are adaptively disparate—nutrition, social bonding, problem-solving, aggression modulation—so there is no biological common currency either. (5) Yet in my practical reasoning, both in life and in revision, I constantly treat pleasures as inter-substitutable and quantitatively commensurable: I will cut visceral excitement to amplify intellectual satisfaction in a scene, or delay gratification across chapters, presupposing a single scale of narrative value. (6) Finally, I reject phenomenal-concept strategies for consciousness: the "what it is like" of pleasure is a mode of presentation with no metaphysical weight, not an ontological clue.

Please audit this set: which pairs are in genuine tension and how severely, which pairs only appear to conflict but dissolve on inspection, and for each real tension what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Rank the tensions by severity. Then tell me which single commitment, if revised or dropped, would clean up the most conflict, and whether my practical commitment to commensurability in (5) can survive if I keep (3) and (4). Lead with the bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
