As a retired experimental physicist recently turned to philosophy of mind, I encountered the following summary from a colleague: "After surgical section of the corpus callosum, each cerebral hemisphere functions as an independent cognitive system with its own perceptions, memories, and volitional intentions; the left hemisphere is the speaking self, whereas the right hemisphere is an equally conscious but linguistically silent partner that communicates only through gesture. Although patients consistently report feeling like a single unified person, this subjective unity is merely a confabulation produced by the left hemisphere's interpreter module, which invents post-hoc narratives to account for actions initiated by the right. Therefore, the split-brain experiments prove that ordinary human consciousness is not genuinely unified but is instead a bundle of separate streams that the corpus callosum ordinarily integrates, and cutting that bridge reveals the illusory nature of the singular self."

How fair is this characterization? Please evaluate it sentence by sentence, distinguishing what researchers in this tradition would endorse, what is a standard popular exaggeration, and what subtly misrepresents either the experimental findings or the philosophical debate about hemispheric consciousness. In particular, does the patients' reported sense of unity actually support the claim that it is an interpreter-driven confabulation? And does the evidence justify the broader conclusion that normal consciousness is a bundle rather than a unified field? I am not asking for citations or a reading list—only for an accuracy audit I can rely on to correct my own understanding.
