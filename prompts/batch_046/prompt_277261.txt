Write a decision-tree taxonomy of positions in the structural realism debate, designed to sort a respondent by what they actually claim rather than by which label they prefer. The tree should have four binary branch nodes, each phrased as a concrete claim about structures, theory change, or interpretation: first, whether the view is epistemic (we can know only structure) or ontic (only structure exists); second, whether structural continuity across theory change is secured solely by shared mathematical form or requires an additional interpretive mapping that fixes the ontological content of the structures; third, how one should treat "unembedded structures"—formal mathematical elements within a physical theory that currently have no interpreted physical correlate—specifically whether they are purely instrumental, ontologically real but uninstantiated, or evidence of theoretical deficiency; fourth, whether the modal properties of physical structures (what they make possible or impossible) are primitive or derived entirely from dynamical laws. Each leaf should carry either the name of a recognized position (e.g., Worrall-style epistemic structural realism, Ladyman-Ross ontic structural realism) or be marked "unnamed," accompanied by a one-sentence diagnosis of whether the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Ensure the tree separates standard views without collapsing them: flag any pair of leaves that the four questions cannot distinguish. At the end, state how confident you are in the classification and identify the single branch distinction about which you are least sure, explaining briefly why.
