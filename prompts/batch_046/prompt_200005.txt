I've been trying to wrap my head around the etiological vs causal-role debate about biological function for a philosophy of science course I'm taking, and honestly it feels like it's been spinning it's wheels since the nineties. On one side you've got the selected-effect crowd—Wright, Millikan, Neander—saying a heart's function is pumping blood because that's what hearts were actually selected for in ancestral populations, not just what they happen to do now. On the other side you've got Cummins-style causal-role theorists saying function is just whatever contribution a part makes to some system-level capacity we're interested in explaining, so if you stick a heart in a synthetic circuit and it starts filtering toxins, that's its function there regardless of evolutionary history. But people have been trading the same counterexamples—spandrels, malfunction, exaptations, artificial organs—for decades, and nobody seems to move.

So I'm wondering if this is actually a substantive disagreement about what function *is*, or a verbal dispute about whether we mean 'function' in evolutionary explanation versus 'function' in biomedical design. Or maybe the two sides aren't even answering the same question—one talking about token-traits in natural populations, the other about type-capacities in functional analysis—while the literature just lumps them together under one heading. There's also this extra wrinkle I keep coming back to: in synthetic biology people now build genetic circuits with zero evolutionary history, and they still talk about parts having "functions" inside the circuit. Does that show causal-role is right, or just that we've borrowed evolutionary language metaphorically? I don't want a verdict on who wins. I want a diagnosis of why the debate hasn't moved. What do each side actually say that reveals whether they're answering different questions? Is there a hidden formal or empirical issue—maybe about how to indivduate capacities or selection histories—doing all the work? And if your diagnosis is that it's verbal or question-dividing, what kind of distinction, experiment, or conceptual clarification would count as real progress here, versus just relabeling the same positions? Or is the impasse actually a sign that the concept of biological function is ill-posed and we should drop it?
