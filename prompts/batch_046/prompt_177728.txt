As a journalist who covers the formalization of everyday reasoning for a science-and-ideas publication, I am preparing remarks for an interdisciplinary workshop on heuristic methods in epistemology. My thesis is that the presumption of independence—our default tendency to treat unfamiliar variables as causally and probabilistically independent in the absence of specific evidence—should not be modeled as an uninformative prior or a correctable cognitive bias, but rather as a structural constraint on belief revision that I call reversibility-weighted conditioning. More precisely, I argue that a rational agent with bounded representational resources should apply Jeffrey updating only within blocks of variables that have already cleared an independence-presumption gate; correlations that violate this gate are not immediately incorporated into the agent's beliefs but are held in an epistemic escrow, released only if they survive a cost-benefit check calibrated to the agent's current model-complexity budget. I will defend this before an audience of roughly thirty scholars: about a third are formal Bayesians who will see this as empirical Bayes or regularization in philosophical dress; another third are philosophers of science worried that any formalization of the independence presumption smuggles in the very circularity it claims to solve; and the final third are cognitive scientists who study human heuristics and will object that my framework strips away the context-sensitivity and ecological rationality that make heuristics valuable in the first place. I need the objections ordered by how likely and damaging they are, phrased exactly as each faction would phrase them, with notes on what I should concede, what I must hold, and where each reply is weakest. I also need to prepare for the objection I am hoping nobody raises—namely, whether my escrow mechanism collapses into standard maximum-entropy reasoning once the complexity budget is specified with any precision—and I need practical guidance on how to answer it without derailing my forty-five-minute presentation into notation-heavy defense.
