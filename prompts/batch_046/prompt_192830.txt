My tutor in philosophy of religion frequently repeats the phrase 'you are not the same person you were a moment ago' whenever I ask whether promises made years ago still bind me, invoking Buddhist anattā on one hand and psychological continuity theories on the other. What are the precise distinct claims this slogan could be expressing — from the banal observation that mental states change over time, through the denial of a permanent ātman, to the radical view that there is no enduring subject to bear moral responsibility — and which of these readings genuinely undermine long-term obligations? Please lay the interpretations out as a menu, noting for each whether it belongs to classical Buddhist doctrine, to contemporary analytic accounts of personal identity, or to an uncritical conflation of the two, and indicate which ones actually do the work her dismissal requires.
