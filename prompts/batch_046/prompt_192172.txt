I'm curating the closing session of an interdisciplinary seminar on moral gradualism—the thesis that sentience and moral considerability are not binary properties but exist along a continuum, with genuinely intermediate cases that make any threshold we draw appear structurally arbitrary. We will have worked through cephalopods and insects as real, non-negligible points on the scale, and I want to end with the provocative lower limit: even a thermostat exhibits a minimal functional integration—bimetallic response, feedback loop, differential reaction to temperature—that structurally mirrors, at a vastly reduced scale, the binding processes philosophers of mind take as indicators of phenomenal unity. The point is not to claim that a thermostat feels anything, but to show that if our criterion is organizational integration, then sliding downward forces us to locate an arbitrary cutoff somewhere in the gradient.

Please write a sestina: six stanzas of six lines each, followed by a three-line envoi, using the end-words THERMOSTAT, INSECT, BINDING, DEGREE, THRESHOLD, and CIRCLE in the standard rotating order (stanza one ending 1-2-3-4-5-6; stanza two 6-1-5-2-4-3; and so on through the six stanzas). The poem should treat insects as genuinely intermediate—not as "almost nothing," but as possessing a form of neural and behavioral integration that is quantitatively different from mammalian binding yet not reducible to zero. Let the compulsive rotation of the six terms enact the smoothness of the gradation: the same structural questions recur at every level, with no natural break in the sequence. In the envoi, however, break the rotation deliberately: place THERMOSTAT and DEGREE adjacent to one another, and position THRESHOLD between INSECT and BINDING. This formal disruption should mirror the conceptual disruption—the continuous scale (degree, circular return) is interrupted by an imposed line (threshold)—so that the lower-bound case is pressed directly against the scalar measure, while the intermediate case is separated from it only by the arbitrary cut we construct. Do not resolve the tension with a sermon about human obligation to intervene in nature, nor with a shrug about the "circle of life"; leave the adjacency in the envoi unresolved. Use precise, non-anthropomorphizing imagery—compound eyes, bimetallic strips, feedback loops, global workspace dynamics—so that someone familiar with the philosophy of mind recognizes the scalar moral-status problem being enacted structurally rather than merely mentioned. No citations, footnotes, references to specific texts, or bibliographic material are needed. Aim for the form itself to do the philosophical work.
