Please explain the conceptual tension that arises when Dennett's brain-transplant thought experiment—commonly linked to the essay Where Am I?—is extended into a distributed variant: rather than Hamlet's brain being relocated into a single spare body, its functional states are streamed across several robotic substrates, each maintaining a partial yet continuous narrative thread, while Yorick's original biological body retains a degraded but unbroken stream of consciousness. In particular, examine whether Dennett's idea of a center of narrative gravity can remain singular when there is no unique physical locus for either the brain-derived or body-derived consciousness, and clarify what this distributed case reveals about the implicit assumption in the original puzzle that first-person authority requires a single, continuous spatiotemporal trajectory.
