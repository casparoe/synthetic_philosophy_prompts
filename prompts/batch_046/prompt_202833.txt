I've been tracking a recurring asymmetry in debates over panpsychism and cosmic suffering, especially where people try to map moral status onto fundamental physics. The pattern goes like this. First, someone grants that if subjective experience is distributed across basic physical processes—say, every decoherence event or spin-network interaction carries a minimal, non-zero valence—then the total suffering in the universe might be astronomically larger than the biological case, since the number of such events is enormous; this is treated as a serious reductio of the view. Then, in reply, defenders invoke a combination threshold: only integrated macro-subjects genuinely suffer, and unreduced micro-experiences carry no independent moral weight. So far this is the standard combination problem. But then the same defenders turn around and argue that human consciousness is not eliminated or diluted by being composed of those very micro-experiences, precisely because they combine into a unified subject. The combination mechanism is thus used to protect macro-level moral status from reduction, while being used simultaneously to deny that the unreduced micro-level contributes any moral cost that needs accounting for. The boundary—when integration creates a patient versus when it suppresses one—seems to shift based on whether the ethical conclusion is palatable. I've seen versions of this in discussions of quantum-field phenomenology and integrated-information accounts, but I don't know whether there's an established name for this specific two-step, where a combination problem is solved selectively to preserve human dignity and to dismiss cosmic moral burden without an independent criterion for the direction of moral emergence. Is there? If not, can you coin and define it, with clear boundary conditions that distinguish it from plain special pleading, from the combination problem itself, and from a standard emergence claim? I want the carving, not just the label: what exactly makes the move structurally inconsistent rather than merely convenient, and where does it break?
