Please restate the central thesis of tranquilism — that only the absence of craving and suffering possesses intrinsic value, and that pleasure, achievement, and relational goods are valuable only instrumentally, insofar as they diminish craving or suffering — entirely within the vocabulary of stress physiology and predictive regulation. Be precise: translate craving into reward-related prediction-error signals, suffering into sustained allostatic overload or high-variance interoceptive prediction errors, and tranquillity into a stable, low-error homeostatic set-point. Once you have done so, provide a residue report: indicate which elements of the philosophical position map cleanly onto these mechanisms, which can only be accommodated through distortion, and which — particularly any normative claim that absence alone is intrinsically good — have no expression at all in a descriptive physiological framework. Conclude by assessing whether the residue points to a missing normative dimension in the biological vocabulary, or whether it suggests that tranquilism itself depends on a distinction that cannot be articulated without importing non-physiological concepts.
