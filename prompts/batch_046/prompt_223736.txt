As a novelist preparing an application for a one-year interdisciplinary fellowship that pairs conceptual research with a work of philosophical fiction, I need a polished proposal of approximately 2,500 words, deliverable within eight months, on a question that arises directly from my narrative practice but stands independently of it. The domain is Nietzsche’s distinction in On the Uses and Disadvantages of History for Life between monumental, antiquarian, and critical history. Rather than rehearse standard exegesis or apply the framework to academic historiography, I want to investigate whether these three modes operate not only as attitudes toward the past but also as formal constraints on narrative point of view—specifically, whether critical history, which Nietzsche presents as a destructive, judging force that must be wielded with extreme care lest it paralyze life, can be sustained within a sustained first-person autobiographical voice without collapsing into either monumental self-aggrandizement or antiquarian self-burial. Existing scholarship treats the modes as cultural or psychological dispositions; it leaves entirely open whether they entail logical restrictions on who can narrate and from what temporal stance. My method will be conceptual and analytical rather than empirical: I will reconstruct the internal logic of each mode—what each takes as evidence, what it suppresses, what temporal relation it establishes between present speaker and past event—then test whether a single narrator can oscillate among them without contradiction, using selected passages from Nietzsche’s essay and carefully chosen autobiographical fiction as intuitive test cases rather than as objects of literary-critical interpretation. I ask that the proposal state the precise question, explain exactly where current readings of Nietzsche leave this gap, outline a month-by-month plan that remains within conceptual analysis, specify what the deliverable looks like (a monograph chapter that could stand alone or be integrated into the novel’s theoretical appendix), and include an honest assessment of scope risks, particularly the danger of conflating Nietzsche’s normative claims about life with descriptive claims about narrative form. Please do not include a bibliography, citation list, or reading list; I will supply references myself. Write in a formal register appropriate for a review board of philosophers and fiction editors, and keep the framing open enough that the project could fail—meaning the proposal must specify what result would count as failure rather than merely describing a process.
