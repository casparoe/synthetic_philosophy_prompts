I have been helping care for my mother since her Alzheimer's diagnosis, and watching her live in a perpetual present that seems disconnected from the chronological order of events has made me revisit the philosophy of time with a new urgency. In particular, I have been trying to work through the Rietdijk-Putnam argument for the block universe, but I have constructed an extension of it that now strikes me as suspicious because its conclusion undermines the very rationality of caring for someone in time. Here is the argument, laid out in steps:

(1) In Minkowski spacetime, the absence of absolute simultaneity means that if observer A takes event E to be present while observer B, in relative motion, takes E to lie in B's past or future, then denying the reality of E for B requires an unjustified privileging of A's inertial frame. Hence every event that any observer takes as real must exist tenselessly within a four-dimensional block.
(2) Mental states such as being in pain, intending to act, or remembering are temporary intrinsics: they belong to a subject at a particular time in a manner that cannot be fully analyzed as a relation between the subject and an eternal property.
(3) If all events exist tenselessly, then for any mental state M occurring at world-line coordinate t, the claim that M is happening now is true only indexically, relative to the speaker's position at t, carrying no more ontological weight than saying I am located here.
(4) Yet rational practical reasoning treats mental states at one's present coordinate differently from those at other coordinates: we relieve present pain but do not attempt to alter past pain, and we plan for future needs without grieving them with present urgency.
(5) This differential treatment is rationally justified only if mental states at one's present coordinate possess an objective ontological privilege—an irreducible now-ness—that states at other coordinates lack.
(6) The block universe eliminates any such global present, reducing temporal location to a purely relational feature of world-lines in spacetime.
(7) Consequently, accepting the block universe forces us either to treat all our mental states across time with equal practical urgency, which dissolves the coherence of agency, or to reject the Rietdijk-Putnam argument altogether.
(8) Because the latter option seems to deny well-confirmed physics, the argument appears to prove that special relativity is incompatible with rational temporal concern.

I feel that something slips between steps (4) and (5), or perhaps in the move from (2) to (3), but I cannot name the error. Which premise or step should I reject, and why did it seem plausible? In particular, I want to know what an eternalist (block theorist), a presentist, and a growing-block theorist each have to concede or defend if they reject that step.
