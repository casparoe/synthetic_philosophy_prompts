I read a summary of the cognitive-penetration debate in a popular philosophy magazine. It went like this: "Contemporary neuroscience treats perception as predictive processing: the brain constantly generates top-down predictions about incoming sensory signals, and only the prediction errors are passed upward. When those predictions are shaped by personal-level beliefs — for instance, a clinician's belief that a shadow is malignant, or an anxious hiker's belief that a slope is treacherous — they do not merely bias post-perceptual judgment. Instead, they alter the qualitative character of the experience itself, changing what it is like to see the image or feel the incline. This demonstrates that cognition reaches all the way into early perceptual processing, dissolving any principled boundary between seeing and thinking. The modularist response, associated with figures like Jerry Fodor, insisted on informational encapsulation mainly to preserve the myth of a theory-neutral observation language. But if predictive processing is correct, then all observation is theory-laden in the strong sense: there is no common perceptual ground shared across different theoretical commitments, and the classical ideal of objective empirical inquiry collapses into a clash of incommensurable world-views."

Something about this feels too sweeping, especially the leap from predictive coding to the collapse of objectivity. But I am not sure which parts are standard philosophical claims and which are overstatements. Could you audit this summary? Please separate: (1) what is essentially accurate about the predictive-processing framing and the penetration thesis; (2) what is a defensible simplification that a careful defender might accept with qualification; (3) what misrepresents either the penetration literature or the modularist position — in particular, whether Fodor's argument for encapsulation was primarily about preserving a theory-neutral observation language, and whether defenders of penetration typically claim that objectivity collapses; and (4) whether the conclusion that empirical inquiry becomes a clash of incommensurable world-views actually follows from the premises given. If the summary conflates subpersonal prediction mechanisms with personal-level beliefs, please flag that specifically.
