Write a dialogue between a crane-leaning reductionist and a skyhook-leaning emergentist — neither should be a strawman, and they should actually recieve each other's points rather than talk past one another. Don't just rehash the standard Dennett-style exchange. Have them argue about whether greedy reductionism can really swallow normative ethics: if you had a complete bottom-up story of every neural and evolutionary pathway that produces human moral judgement, is there still a leftover "ought" that needs explaining, or is that just a category mistake? To keep it from being pure pretraining regurgitation, inject this specific twist: they discuss a hypothetical synthetic being engineered in a lab with zero evolutionary lineage — no selection pressures, no ancestral history — that still seems to suffer or flourish in ways that matter. The crane person should argue its states are just arranged particles with functional roles, and that "value" is a useful fiction we project downward; the skyhook person should push back that without evolutionary scaffolding, the reductionist can't borrow the usual "this trait was selected for" explanation, so they either have to admit the synthetic being's interests are genuinely real and downward-causally effective, or deny them in a way that also undermines normal human moral status. Make sure they address downward causation directly if it gets raised. Keep it casual — maybe slip in a typo like "existant" instead of "existent". No citations, no reading lists, no "according to X" references.
