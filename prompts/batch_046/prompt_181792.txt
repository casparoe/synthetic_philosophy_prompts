I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy or physics beyond popular science, and I am drafting a short essay on whether the thermodynamic arrow of time genuinely explains the psychological arrow—why we remember the past and anticipate the future rather than vice versa. Before I develop it further, I would value precise comments on the coherence of the reasoning, the accuracy of the physics as I have used it, and whether the central analogy is illuminating or misleading.

My working notes are as follows. At the microscopic level, the fundamental dynamical laws are time-reversible; the macroscopic direction of time is usually attributed to the second law of thermodynamics and the low-entropy initial conditions of the universe. Psychologically, we experience an asymmetry: memories are of earlier states, intentions target later ones. The standard account treats this psychological arrow as a downstream consequence of the thermodynamic one—entropy increases, brains are physical systems, therefore cognition tracks the direction of increasing entropy.

I want to challenge that direct-causal picture by introducing an analogy drawn from distributed version control. In a system like Git, each local commit is technically reversible; one can revert, rebase, or amend history locally without violating any law of computation. Yet once branches are merged into a shared global state, conflict resolution irreversibly discards the losing branch's content. The global repository acquires a definite historical direction—not because local computation is irreversible, but because consolidation requires selecting one state and erasing alternatives. The irreversibility is informational and structural, not local.

I propose that cognitive memory consolidation operates analogously. Individual neural dynamics may be largely reversible at the local, synaptic level, but the macroscopic process of encoding a long-term memory involves an irreversible 'merge' in which counterfactual neural patterns are pruned or overwritten. On this view, the psychological arrow is not caused by thermodynamic entropy per se, but is a parallel emergent phenomenon: both arise from the irreversible consolidation of information—physical in the case of thermodynamics, structural in the case of cognition—under conditions where local reversibility is overridden by global integration. This suggests the two arrows are co-emergent rather than one being reducible to the other.
