I'm building a taxonomy / decision tree for a workshop on whether left and right are real cohesive things or just leftover labeling. I want it to sort people by thier actual commitments, not by which team they cheer for. Start with: does the distinction survive if you look outside Western parliamentary systems? Then branch on whether there's one axis or many; whether the glue is moral-psychological (like clustering in moral foundations) or material / coalitional; whether it's an institutional artifact that could vanish; and here's the injected niche bit — whether left/right are "attractor states" in coalition space, meaning the same psychological profiles keep clustering even when the issues completely change. Each node should be a concrete claim or case someone can answer without knowing the leaf names (e.g., "would a socialist and a libertarian still oppose each other in a society with no private property?"). End with named leaves: real binary, multidimensional cluster, folk fiction, institutional artifact, attractor-state coalitionism, and one unnamed empty combination that seems incoherent or just unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate. Keep it meaningful for someone who can't search the web — so no bibliographies, no "read X" lists, and don't lean on hyper-obscure theories nobody remembers. Be honest about where the tree is shakiest and what argument or evidence would change your mind about which split matters most.
