I've been arguing with my flatmate over pints about Lakatos and his research programmes, and I think I've ended up with a stricter version that I'm not sure survives contact with reality. The standard story is you've got a hard core you don't abandon, plus a protective belt of auxilliary hypotheses you tweak when things go wrong. I want to defend an extra constraint: belt adjustments have to be 'domain-cautious', so they can restrict where the core applies -- like saying Newtonian gravity fails near singularities -- but they can't smuggle in brand-new entities or dynamics that do the explanatory work the core was supposed to handle. If you need dark energy with its own independent behaviour just to save your cosmological core, that's not domain-cautious tweaking, that's just changing the subject.

So my position is basically Lakatos plus this domain-cautious rule. What would count against it? I'm not after the usual Feyerabend 'anything goes' objection, or the generic complaint that Lakatos can't practically tell a progressive programme from a degenerating one. I want specific defeaters: are there well-known cases in physics or biology where a programme clearly advanced only by breaking domain-caution? Could someone argue the constraint is either toothless or so rigid it bans adjustments we actually accept? And does this stricter view accidentally collapse back into Popper's conventionalist stratagems, just with fancier vocabulary? I don't need citations, just an honest assessment of whether I've carved out something real or just reinvented an old problem with a new label.
