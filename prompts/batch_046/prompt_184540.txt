I serve as the student representative on our university's Research Ethics Review Board, and we must rule on a pilot study proposed jointly by Philosophy and Computer Science. The investigators want to deploy an "extrapolated preference assistant" for undergraduates facing major-choice anxiety. The system collects a student's present preferences, runs them through coherence-extrapolation, and generates an "idealized volition profile" that recommends curricular paths—potentially overriding immediate, conflicted wishes.

The proposal introduces a distinction not standard in the usual literature: it separates "volition-extrapolation" (what the student would want with greater knowledge and longer reflection) from "preference-idealization" (a logically consistent ordering that need not trace back to the student's actual psychology), and it allows the system to issue recommendations from either layer, labeling them accordingly.

Please draft a formal memo recommending approval, conditional approval, or rejection. The memo should address whether separating these two layers genuinely protects student autonomy or instead risks substituting an engineered coherence for self-determination. It must treat the strongest counter-argument seriously—likely that present, conflicted preferences are unreliable guides to well-being—and propose concrete, actionable safeguards, such as mandatory veto points or transparency requirements about which layer produced a given recommendation.
