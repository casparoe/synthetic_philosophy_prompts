I'm taking an upper-division seminar on intrinsic value, and we've been trying to apply G. E. Moore's framework—specifically his claim that personal affection and aesthetic appreciation are among the supreme goods and that they can combine into organic wholes—to rank hypothetical worlds rather than just listing goods. My tutor has offered consistent verdicts on five cases: (1) a society with deep friendship but no art, music, or natural beauty, which she ranks very high but not at the absolute peak; (2) a society with profound aesthetic experience lived in complete social isolation, essentially tied with (1); (3) a society with abundant friendship and abundant beauty that are entirely compartmentalized—friends share no aesthetic life and art is never experienced together—which she judges high but surprisingly only barely above (1) and (2), as if the mere co-presence of the goods is redundant rather than amplifying; (4) a society with only moderate levels of both goods but fully intertwined, where every friendship is deepened through shared artistic creation and every aesthetic experience is enriched by companionship, which she ranks highest of all, clearly exceeding (3) despite lower raw quantities; and (5) a society identical to (4) except for a pervasive, low-grade envy that never erupts into malice or cruelty, which she still places well above (3) and only just below (4).

We are now considering World 6: a society with very high friendship and very high beauty that are structurally integrated—every aesthetic experience is shared and every relationship is mediated through art—but where the integration is institutionally compulsory. Partners are assigned, shared experiences are mandatory, and spontaneity in both friendship and aesthetic choice is eliminated. There is no envy, no suffering, and no hatred; the only absence is autonomy in how the goods relate. I need to predict my tutor's verdict, but I can see at least two Moore-inspired rules that fit her first five judgments. One treats spontaneous, internal integration as the source of organic surplus value, so that mechanical integration should be valued closer to the compartmentalized World 3 despite the high quantities. The other treats value as mainly additive across the two supreme goods, modified only by intrinsic evils like envy or hatred; since compulsory structure produces no suffering, this rule predicts that World 6 should rank near World 4 and well above World 3.

Please structure your response in the following sections, in this order:
1. The competing rules (about 300 words): State at least two rules consistent with the rankings of Worlds 1 through 5, explaining specifically how each accounts for the gap between World 3 and World 4 and for the small drop from World 4 to World 5.
2. Prediction for World 6 (about 200 words): Say what each rule predicts about the compulsory-integration society, being explicit about whether it should fall nearer to World 4, World 3, or somewhere else.
3. Best-guess verdict and reasoning (about 250 words): Give your own prediction of what the tutor would say, with the reasons for preferring one interpretation, and honestly note where the precedents genuinely underdetermine the answer.
4. The discriminating case (about 150 words): Propose one further hypothetical world that would clearly separate the rules you have identified, explaining what each rule predicts about it and why that prediction differs.
