As a novelist constructing a narrative polity in which moral psychology is deliberately engineered rather than inherited, I am attempting to discipline an argument that seems to prove too much about cultural choice and emotional architecture. Here is the chain I have built. (1) Guilt functions as an internalized self-sanction that isolates the agent from communal life, while shame operates as a relational emotion that preserves solidarity through visible accountability. (2) Therefore, if a culture values social cohesion over individual autonomy, shame is structurally the superior moral emotion. (3) Moral emotions are not fixed by biology but are culturally scaffolded through narrative practice, ritual, and institutional recognition; consequently, a society can choose which emotional framework to cultivate. (4) If a culture can choose shame over guilt, then by the same scaffolding mechanism it can also choose to cultivate a synthetic affect — call it reflexive spectacle — that preserves the visibility of shame while eliminating any genuine communal response, or it can choose to cultivate neither emotion, substituting purely administrative compliance. (5) Thus the question of which moral emotion is better collapses into a prior decision about social ontology and has no culture-independent answer. (6) But if the choice is genuinely open in this manner, then no culture can be criticized for selecting guilt, for selecting moral indifference, or for selecting the synthetic hybrid, since each is merely an expression of its prior ontological commitment. I feel that the illegitimate slide occurs somewhere between (3) and (5) — perhaps an equivocation between the malleability of emotional scaffolding and the absence of normative constraints upon it, or perhaps a suppressed premise that solidarity and autonomy exhaust the evaluative space — yet I cannot name the precise step or calculate what abandoning it costs the coherence of my fictional society. Which premise should be rejected, and what does that rejection entail for a polity that has deliberately chosen to institutionalize shame?
