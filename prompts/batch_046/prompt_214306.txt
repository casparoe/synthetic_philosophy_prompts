I've been turning over Dennett's notion of the self as a center of narrative gravity—the idea that personal identity is not an internal substance but an abstract organizing point, like a center of mass, around which a biography coheres—in light of some recent discussions on rationalist forums about predictive processing and belief revision. A colleague in my reading group, who comes from a cognitive-science background, argued that if the self is genuinely just this abstract narrative object, then it should be fully transparent to revision: any contradictory evidence should update the biography just as it updates any other theory. But this seems to conflict with a well-known feature of self-representation, namely that the narrative framework often functions as a high-level prior that explains away discrepancies rather than accommodating them. I want to understand whether this resistance is a real causal feature of the narrative architecture or merely an appearance generated by the fact that the framework is the very lens through which evidence gets evaluated.

Please explain the conceptual issue here in a committed way, not as a balanced survey. Take a definite stance on whether the center of narrative gravity possesses genuine causal efficacy in belief-fixing—for instance, by selectively weighting which predictions get corrected—or whether its authority is illusory, arising solely from the circularity of using the narrative to interpret counter-narrative data. I am not asking for references to the literature on self-modeling or for a bibliography; I want a precise argument that resolves the tension by identifying exactly where the analogy to a physical center of mass breaks down or holds firm, and what that implies for how a rational agent should treat revisions to their own autobiographical story. If you think the predictive-processing framing actually undermines Dennett's abstraction, say so and show why; if you think it reinforces it, show where the causal work is being done and why the apparent veto power of the self-model is not just confabulation dressed up as authority.
