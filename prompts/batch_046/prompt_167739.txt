Construct a thought experiment, no longer than one paragraph and situated in a realistic institutional environment, in which a senior auditor retains full standing to blame a junior auditor for selectively suppressing a minor conflict-of-interest disclosure, even though the senior auditor herself suppressed an identically minor disclosure in her own reports five years earlier—where the hypocrisy does not undermine her standing, not because she has repented, forgotten, or been unaware of her fault, but strictly because of the structural authority and oversight relation between them. After presenting the scenario, walk through the verdict that the moral-equality account of standing delivers and why, then do the same for the non-hypocrisy condition, pinpointing exactly where and why the two accounts diverge in this case. Identify the single institutional feature that does the work in driving the divergence, explain how removing that feature collapses the separation between the theories, and describe how a defender of the moral-equality view could attack the case without invoking repentance, ignorance, or any change in the blamer’s moral record.
