Write an essay about whether Jain anekāntavāda can survive its own reflexivity problem. A lot of people treat syādvāda like it's just "everyone's kinda right," but the seven-fold predication is more structured than lazy relativism. The real controversy is this: if every statement is only conditionly true, then the claim "all statements are only conditionly true" is it'self only conditionly true, which seems to undercut the whole view. Some defenders blocks this by saying anekānta is an epistemic attitude, not an ontological claim -- but then does it lose its bite against absolutist metaphysics? Reach a conclusion: is the reflexivity objection fatal, can it be fixed by treating syādvāda as a modal framework rather than a global thesis, or does that just turn Jainism into generic pluralism with fancy Sanskrit names? Justify whatever you land on, and don't just list the seven modes -- actually use them in your reasoning.
