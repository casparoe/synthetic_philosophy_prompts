Write a decision-tree taxonomy of positions on whether numbers should count in rescue cases, designed so that a respondent can navigate from concrete commitments to a named view without prior knowledge of the labels. Start from the premise that saving five people rather than one carries at least some moral weight. First branch node: is that weight best explained by aggregating individual claims, or by a non-aggregative relation in which saving more people satisfies more claims without summing them? From there, include nodes that separate strict number-indifferent egalitarianism, Taurek-style fair-lottery fairness, lexical or threshold views, and aggregationist positions that restrict aggregation by group size or relational proximity. Crucially, inject this niche diagnostic case: saving the larger group is only possible by imposing a small, non-fatal harm distributed across every member of the smaller group—not borne by one individual—where that harm is causally necessary for the rescue. Ask how each branch handles this harm-versus-rescue scenario, and flag any pairs of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate (for instance, whether a lottery view applies identically when the choice involves actively harming versus merely failing to rescue). The tree should contain at least six named leaves, include one logically possible but unoccupied combination of answers with a brief diagnosis of why it is empty, and phrase every node as a yes/no question tied to a concrete case rather than to theoretical jargon.
