looking at hungary, poland, turkey, and the US, everyone in my poli-sci circles keeps comparing democratic backsliding to salami slicing — you shave thin slices off judicial review, media rules, and electoral oversight, none fatal alone, but eventually there's no democacy left on the plate. it's used to argue we shouldn't wait for a tanks-in-the-street moment to call it erosion, just track the cumulative cuts. how far does it actually hold?

give me the correspondence table — what's the salami, the knife, the slicer, the plate, and maybe the fridge? then where it breaks: does it smuggle in a single intentional agent with a master plan, does it ignore that some slices can be reversed (like Poland's court reforms getting partly rolled back after elections), and does it assume democracy started as one intact loaf rather than a patchwork? also, does the analogy quietly import irreversibility?

tell me whether the conclusion — that we should flag incremental institutional changes as backsliding even absent a coup — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the salami image was carrying the whole argument. if it collapses, what's a better analogy: rust on a bridge? a frog in boiling water? or something else?
