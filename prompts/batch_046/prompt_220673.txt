I'm preparing a feature on what citizens owe the state in terms of visibility, and I've sketched out a working framework—call it asymmetric legibility—to keep myself honest before I interview policymakers in Berlin, Washington, and Beijing. Here are the commitments I'm holding:

(a) Democratic accountability requires that the state be fully legible to citizens: open algorithms, inspectable budgets, and transparent feature weights in any predictive system that governs access to resources.

(b) Citizens should maintain what I term an "opacity reserve"—a zone of strategic illegibility against state data aggregation—not total anonymity, but a protected right to withhold patterns of life from government databases.

(c) The Chinese social credit system fails primarily on technical grounds: it relies on crude proxy metrics and lacks reciprocal transparency. If the metrics were refined and citizens could audit them, the underlying legibility of citizens to the state would not, in itself, violate democratic norms.

(d) For collective goods—public health surveillance, climate modeling, urban infrastructure—citizens in liberal democracies should adopt "open-by-default" data practices, sharing granular personal information with the state provided the transparency runs upward as well.

(e) Predictive governance is permissible only if affected citizens can inspect the model architecture, but the state has no corresponding duty to disclose an individual's specific risk score or classification to that individual.

(f) Any archive of citizen data held by the state, including predictive policing or welfare-eligibility databases, should be subject to a GDPR-style "right to be forgotten" that allows citizens to erase their historical feature profiles permanently.

Before I take this framework into interviews, I need an audit. Which pairs of these commitments genuinely pull against one another, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision for each tension? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equal. For each conflict, specify what would have to give—whether a principle, a scope condition, or the niche framing itself. Then tell me which single revision, if I made only one, would restore the most consistency at the lowest conceptual cost.
