Write the desiderata that any adequate answer to Glaucon's challenge—why be just when one possesses the ring of Gyges, rendering both detection and reputation-cost impossible—must satisfy. I want the full specification of adequacy, not the exposition of a single theory.

For each desideratum, state: (i) the phenomenon or case it must cover (e.g., the solitary invisible agent; the universal-invisibility scenario in which every rational agent wears the ring; the agent who values self-respect but not social esteem; the case where moral compliance produces no internal satisfaction and no observable benefit); (ii) the distinction it must preserve without collapse (e.g., between having a reason and being motivated by it, between prudential rationality and moral rationality, between acting justly and acting in a way that merely mimics justice for strategic cover, between first-personal deliberation—"why should I?"—and third-personal evaluation—"why is she wrong?"); and (iii) whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp (contractarian, Kantian autonomy-based, virtue-ethical, or evolutionary-psychological).

In particular, build these constraints into the list, treating them as part of the spec rather than its entirety:
- The account must explain why injustice remains wrong or irrational even when the ring eliminates not only external sanctions but also internal psychological costs—no guilt, no self-image damage, no affective residue.
- It must respect the asymmetry between first-personal and third-personal justification of moral compliance without reducing one to the other.
- It must handle the collective version: if every rational agent reasons to injustice under invisibility, the resulting state is worse for all, yet no individual can unilaterally restore cooperation without self-sacrifice. Specify whether the theory must resolve this as a prisoner's dilemma, a coordination failure, or a deeper failure of practical reason itself.

Then identify any subset of these desiderata that is jointly unsatisfiable—for instance, whether requiring both (a) that moral reasons be transparent to the agent's own rational assessment and (b) that they remain binding under total opacity of outcome, recognition, and internal reward, generates a contradiction or merely an unstable tension. Note which single desideratum eliminates the largest number of popular answers.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring existing theories, though you may briefly indicate how a contractarian view, a Kantian autonomy-based view, and a virtue-theoretic account fare against the list. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
