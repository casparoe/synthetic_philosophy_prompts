hey — I'm putting together the syllabus appendix for my fall grad seminar on revolutionary dynamics, and I'm sick of students walking in with either a vulgar class-struggle take or a pure contingency narrative and acting like they've solved 1789. Before they touch another monograph, I want them to draft the full desiderata list: what does any adequate interpretation of the french revolution actually have to deliver?

I'm talking the real spec sheet. The social-structural mechanisms it can't just hand-wave away, the ideological formations — not just ideas floating around but the actual discusive machinery — that need accounting for, and the contingency nodes (flight to varennes, the war crisis, the price-fixing committees) that have to stay genuinely open without everything collapsing into anything-could-happen. Specifcally, they need to tackle whether revolutions devouring their children is a structural feature of revolutionary politics or just a narrative bias we project backward.

For each desideratum they need the motivating case: which event or pattern makes it non-negotiable. Then flag the tensions — especially between structural determinism and genuine contingency, and between ideology as superstructure versus ideology as an autonomous causal force. I want them to say which single requirement kills the most common textbook interpretations, and more importantly which subset of these demands is actually jointy unsatisfiable, because that's where the real philosophical work is.

Don't give me bibliograpies or reading lists — half the class doesn't have library acces and I don't want them guessing citations. Just write it like you're explaining it to someone who's bored of the usual suspects and wants the actual criteria first.
