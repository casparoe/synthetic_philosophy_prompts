I'm working through applications of Kant's political writings on perpetual peace for a graduate seminar, and I've been tracking how one of the professors adjudicates hypothetical institutional arrangements against the framework of the three definitive articles. Her track record so far: she endorsed a defensive league of republics that pools military capacity only in response to verified external aggression, with each member retaining an independent domestic constitution; she rejected a universal constitutional monarchy paired with a standing international court, on the grounds that republican sovereignty cannot be alienated to a single supreme will; she gave cautious approval to a commercial federation bound by trade treaties and a prohibition on colonial expansion, but permitted each state to set its own citizenship and immigration criteria independently; and she rejected an arrangement of unconditional global mobility, maintaining that cosmopolitan hospitality must remain a conditional right of temporary sojourn rather than settlement.

Now a student has proposed a "contingency federation": member states keep fully independent republican constitutions and trade policies under ordinary conditions, but pre-commit to a unified military command activated solely by a supermajority vote during declared emergencies; they affirm a universal right of hospitality, yet restrict actual entry to travelers who can demonstrate basic familiarity with republican civic principles; and they establish no permanent international legislature, relying on ad hoc arbitration panels convened for each specific dispute. If the professor wants to stay consistent with her four prior judgments, should she approve or reject this proposal? Which precedent offers the closest analog—the defensive league, the rejected monarchy, the commercial federation, or the restricted hospitality case—and where does the decisive tension fall? If her earlier rulings genuinely underdetermine the outcome, what principle would she be adopting by accepting the proposal, and what principle by rejecting it?
