I'm writing for an upper-level philosophy of well-being seminar in which we have discussed the experience-machine argument, hedonism, desire-satisfaction theories, and objective-list accounts. For a paper I'm drafting, I need a precisely engineered thought experiment that isolates a feature standard treatments usually blur together: whether a perfectionist account of well-being requires that the development of human capacities occur through causal interaction with a genuinely resistant, non-responsive environment, or whether perfect algorithmic simulation of such resistance can substitute. The case must not ask for bibliographies or citations.

Please construct a thought experiment—call it the Resistant Simulation Case—meeting the following specifications. Set the scenario in a realistic institutional context (for example, a ten-year scientific apprenticeship or a master craft workshop, not a fantasy world) and present it in one paragraph. In the case, a subject chooses between Protocol R and Protocol A. In Protocol R, she spends a decade inside a high-fidelity simulation developing expert-level capacities—scientific judgment, manual skill, or artistic mastery, whichever you specify—with perfect responsive feedback and identical phenomenology, but every obstacle and success is algorithmically generated and causally disconnected from an independent physical environment; she knows she is in the simulation. In Protocol A, she spends the same decade in the physical world developing the exact same capacities with the exact same subjective experience and the exact same list of achievements, but against genuinely resistant material conditions—equipment failure, bodily fatigue, resource scarcity, unpredictable weather—so that her successes depend on causal interaction with a world not fully responsive to her will. The two protocols must be identical in phenomenology, in the list of capacities developed, and in the subject's awareness of her situation; the only structural difference must be whether the resistance she overcame was causally independent or simulated.

After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that the design works by stating the verdict of four positions and showing why each follows: (i) a pure hedonist; (ii) a desire-satisfaction theorist who desires both capacity-development and reality-contact but weights them equally; (iii) a standard objective-list theorist for whom achievement and knowledge are intrinsic goods regardless of causal history; and (iv) a causal-engagement perfectionist, defined here as holding that well-being consists in capacities being exercised through causal interaction with an environment that is not fully responsive to one's will. For each verdict, identify which feature of the case—phenomenological identity, capacity identity, or the causal-independence of resistance—does the explanatory work.

Next, identify the specific structural feature that forces the causal-engagement perfectionist to dissent from the objective-list theorist, and explain precisely what happens if that feature is removed—specifically, if the simulation is upgraded so that its algorithmically generated resistance is phenomenologically and structurally indistinguishable from physical resistance but still lacks causal independence from the subject's mental states. Then provide the defensive reply that a causal-engagement perfectionist would offer against the case: they must argue that the upgraded simulation cheats by substituting responsive feedback for genuine resistance, but they may not appeal to mysterious properties of consciousness; instead, they should turn on the structural relation between agency and an environment that can genuinely frustrate it. Finally, say whether removing the causal-independence feature collapses the case back into the standard experience-machine debate, and if so, which element of your design was doing the distinctive work.

Keep the analysis conceptual and self-contained; do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts.
