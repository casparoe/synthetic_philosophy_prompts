i've been staring at my grandma's attic boxes and realizing they're basically an archive but only her side of the family is inside — there's photos of her brothers but nothing about the neigbor who actually raised half those kids. it got me thinking archives aren't just randomly missing stuff; there's a pattern to the silence, almost like what's left out has its own filling system — i started calling it a shadow index. can you write me a villanelle, 19 lines, two refrains that come back, where one refrain is about what's kept and the other is about that shadow index of absence? let the form do the thinking: the repetition should feel like the archive insisting on itself, and the final quatrain should bring the kept and the missing into the same room without resolving which one wins. don't lecture, just leave the tension open. write in american english. start right with the poem — no intro, no explanation.
