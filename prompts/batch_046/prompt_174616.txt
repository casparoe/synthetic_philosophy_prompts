Write a short story set in a decommissioned orbital archive where the remaining systems preserve what the original architects called free-floating rationales: normative justifications stored in formal structures that continue to generate obligations for any parser, regardless of whether any agent originally intended them or remains present to endorse them. Your protagonist should be a non-sentient repair mechanism—an entity that processes rules without holding beliefs or desires—that gradually reconfigures the archive's physical layout not in pursuit of its own ends, but because it has parsed a cluster of orphaned arguments for epistemic preservation that now lack any beneficiary. In the narrative, take a committed stance on whether such rationales carry genuine normative force in the absence of reasoners: do not treat them as mere metaphors or poetic devices, but show through the mechanism's behavior whether you think they can independently constrain action. After the story, append a brief reflective note in which you identify the specific points where the fiction takes liberties with the underlying philosophical idea—for example, by rendering rationales as causally efficacious data structures rather than as abstract normative relations—and state clearly which contested interpretation you are adopting, such as mind-independent normative realism, institutional persistence, or relational structures that depend on being parsed.
