In a seminar on deep history and political philosophy, a classmate kept invoking the familiar summary of Jared Diamond’s 1987 argument—that agriculture was “the worst mistake in the history of the human race”—to dismiss modern industrial life as an inevitable deviation from a healthier, more egalitarian baseline. I want to understand exactly what this slogan is committing to, because it seems to smuggle together several distinct theses that are rarely separated in casual debate. Please treat the phrase as a menu of possible claims rather than a single thesis, and for each reading give me a verdict: is it a platitude (e.g., that agriculture profoundly reshaped human societies), is it substantive but defensible, is it empirically false, or is it conceptually confused?

Specifically, separate at least the following readings: (1) a comparative health and nutrition claim about Neolithic populations versus contemporary foragers; (2) a labor-leisure claim in the spirit of “original affluence”—that foragers worked fewer hours and enjoyed greater autonomy over time; (3) a claim about social equality and hierarchy, particularly the idea that storable surplus made coercive extraction and class stratification possible (sometimes called the grain-state thesis); and (4) a normative claim that the transition was net harmful for the people who lived through it, or that modern societies ought to recover foraging values. For each, say whether it can stand independently: for example, can one accept that agriculture enabled state coercion and inequality without accepting that foragers were healthier or had more leisure? Then tell me which of these readings is actually doing the work when someone uses the slogan to end an argument about whether contemporary economic inequality is an unavoidable feature of complex societies. Does the argument require the health and leisure comparison, the autonomy and state-coercion comparison, or something else entirely? I am not asking for a bibliography or citation list—just the conceptual disambiguation with honest verdicts.
