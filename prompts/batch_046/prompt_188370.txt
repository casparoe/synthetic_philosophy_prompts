I was arguing with my roommate after we saw this exhibit of kinetic modular sculptures — magnetic ceramic pieces that snap into different shapes, but the artist treats each config as its own work even though the parts never change. I tried to write out why that feels right and ended up with something that proves way too much, maybe that every object is actually thousands of invisible ones.

(1) Call one specific shape S and call the magnetic parts M. S can be destroyed just by rearranging the pieces; M survives that rearrangement. (2) So S and M have different persistance conditions. (3) If two things share all their material parts and space at a time but differ in what they can survive, they're not identical. (4) Therefore S is not M, even though S is nothing but M arranged S-wise — their's nothing extra in the space. (5) But now apply the same reasoning inside M: each ceramic piece P can be split in half, and the halves have different survival conditions from the whole piece. (6) So P isn't identical to its halves, and the halves aren't identical to their sub-parts, and so on. (7) That means in the space of the sculpture there isn't just S and M, but an infinite nesting of distinct objects — every sub-assembly and every partial cluster counts separately. (8) The same logic applies to a clay statue, a chair, or my body: if rearrangement or division changes survival conditions, then every region of matter hosts an explosion of co-located things. (9) But that's crazy; my desk isn't crowded with invisible half-desks. (10) So either (3) is false and shared space plus different survival conditions still allows identity, or (5) cheats by applying artifact-logic to parts that don't count as works, or there's some hidden form that saves S without multiplying entities.

I want to keep the intuition that reshuffling the sculpture kills the original work, but I don't want to pay the price of ontological explosion everywhere. Which step is the weak link, and what does giving it up cost? Is there a way to block (5)-(7) without giving up (3)-(4)?
