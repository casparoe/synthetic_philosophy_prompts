I'm drafting the final essay for an upper-level philosophy of action seminar. Write one exam question that a student should answer in 75 minutes (around 1200 words). The question must center on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument — that true moral responsibility requires being causa sui, the cause of oneself, which is impossible — but it should be unanswerable by summary alone. To force deeper engagement, embed the following distinction directly in the prompt: define "authorship responsibility" as being the ultimate originator of one's will, and "stewardship responsibility" as being accountable for maintaining and directing one's character across time without requiring ultimate self-creation. Ask the student whether Strawson's argument undermines both forms, and if stewardship could survive, what conditions it would need to satisfy. Then provide a model answer that executes five essential moves, a marking scheme that assigns specific weights out of 25, and for each move a one-line note on the characteristic error that costs marks. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reference to specific publications.
