I've been trying to extend a Dutch-book argument into territory where I'm not sure it belongs, and now I seem to have a proof that every non-probabilist representation of belief is irrational. I want to know where the sleight of hand is.

(1) A Dutch book shows that if your betting quotients violate the probability axioms, you accept a combination of bets that guarantees loss regardless of outcome.

(2) The force of the argument is not merely monetary but normative: it reveals that your belief-state violates a consistency condition that any rational agent ought to satisfy.

(3) Now consider qualitative belief representations—Spohn-style ranking functions, or AGM-style revision operators—that do not assign real-valued probabilities. These representations can still violate analogues of the probabilistic axioms: for instance, a ranking function can assign a lower rank to A ∧ B than to A alone, or an AGM revision can fail to respect the qualitative entrenchment ordering over propositions.

(4) Wherever such a qualitative violation occurs, we can translate it into a finite portfolio of bets with stakes calibrated to the agent's own comparative confidence judgments. Because the agent's qualitative ordering licenses accepting each individual wager, but the portfolio as a whole yields a sure loss, the agent is Dutch-bookable.

(5) Therefore any representational scheme that permits these qualitative violations—that is, any scheme that is not strictly probabilistic—is normatively incoherent.

(6) But ranking functions and belief-revision frameworks were developed specifically for bounded agents who lack precise credences, and they are widely treated in the literature as rationally permissible representations.

(7) So (5) proves too much: it rules out every non-quantitative model of belief as irrational by fiat.

Which step should I reject? If I keep (2), I seem committed to (5). If I reject (4), I need to explain why qualitative violations can't always be monetized into bets—but then what's special about the quantitative case? I'd rather preserve the normative core of Dutch books intact, but I want to know exactly what I'm giving up and what it costs me in terms of accommodating bounded or imprecise belief.
