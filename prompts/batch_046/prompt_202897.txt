I sit on my university's neuroethics review panel and we have this messy funding case for thursday's meeting. A group wants money for a clinical trial of something called the "Single-Site Awareness Protocol"—basically they monitor coherence at one thalamocortical hub and score patient responsiveness only there, treating that spot as where experience "comes together." One reviewer flagged that this bakes in the Cartesian theater idea, like there's one central screen in the brain where consciousness happens, and said we shouldn't fund studies that embed that metaphysics. The PI fired back that Dennett's multiple drafts model is just philosophy, not clinical science, and that the hub correlates well with behavioral recovery no matter how you interpret it. Half the panel wants to reject outright, half thinks that's policing metaphysics with grant money. I need a draft memo to circulate before the vote—recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions—with reasoning the PI could actually respond to, and take the strongest objection seriously rather than dismissing it. There's also this weird detail that the protocol excludes patients with diffuse axonal injury even though they might still have distributed processing, which the objector says proves the conceptual bias. I was talking about this with my roommate over coffee and she asked if we're really deciding philosophy of mind with funding rules, and honestly I'm not sure where that line is. Can you write the opinion? It should have a clear bottom line and a concrete time frame for response, and it should engage both sides fairly.
