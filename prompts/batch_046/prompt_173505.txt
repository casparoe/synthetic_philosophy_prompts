Question: How does Hume's quantity-theory argument in the money essay connect to his anti-mercantilist point in the trade-balance essay? And what's his view on paper money vs gold?

Student answer:
Hume says if money doubles, prices eventually double, so hoarding bullion is its self-defeating — high prices hurt exports and the specie-flow mechanisim sends gold back out. He admits a quick injection can boost industry briefly before prices adjust, which is why he tolerates bank paper if fully backed. But he rejects paper in principle because only gold has intrinsic worth, and he applies this same logic to foreign trade: real wealth is labor and goods, not metal stockpiles.
