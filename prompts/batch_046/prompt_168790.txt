For a philosophy of mind seminar, I want a consistency audit of my position on dreaming. I hold that: (1) dreams, understood as narrative mental episodes with content, are not experiences, since the sleeper lacks the indexical and temporal capacities required for first-person presence (broadly Malcolm's line); (2) dream reports are largely confabulatory reconstructions assembled at awakening rather than read-outs from stored phenomenal records (a Dennett-style view); (3) REM sleep nevertheless involves a diffuse, non-intentional affective tonality—phenomenal and bodily, yet lacking object, narrative, or temporal extension—that explains the felt urgency to narrativise upon waking; (4) this tonality is genuinely phenomenal but not an experience, because experience requires minimal temporal integration and self-reference; and (5) consequently, the absence of dream-experience does not entail the absence of all phenomenal character during sleep. Which pairs genuinely conflict, which only appear to, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity, and please reply in British English.
