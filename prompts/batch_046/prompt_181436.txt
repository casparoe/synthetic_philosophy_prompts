At a study group on the School of Names, two members debated Gongsun Long's argument that a white horse is not a horse. The first, Alex, reconstructed it as an ontology of compound universals: the term horse denotes the form of horse-ness, which is color-neutral, whereas white horse denotes the joint presence of horse-ness and white-ness; since these are distinct ontological items, the claim is strictly true, and the interlocutor in the dialogue merely confuses class membership with identity of referents. The second, Blake, objected that this makes Gongsun Long a straw-man essentialist and misses the text's concern with naming (ming) and actuality (shi). On Blake's reading, the dialogue tests whether compound descriptions preserve reference when properties are conjoined: yellow horses and black horses remain horses because color is accidental, yet Gongsun Long treats whiteness in the compound as essential, thereby exposing that naming practices fail to map cleanly onto actualities once descriptions become complex. Alex replied that Blake imports modern semantic machinery; the examples of differently colored horses actually support Alex's view, since Gongsun Long grants they are all horses yet singles out the white horse as different, which only makes sense if he tracks a specific co-instantiation rather than class logic. Blake countered that this is precisely the point—if color is usually accidental, then treating it as essential in one case reveals a test of whether listeners can distinguish accidental predication from constitutive naming, not a doctrine about universals.
