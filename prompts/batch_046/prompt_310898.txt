I've been trying to figure out if my takes on the agricultural revolution actually hang together, or if I'm just holding a bunch of vibes. So: I mostly buy the Sahlins original-affluence thing — foragers had plenty of food and way more free time than farmers. I also think Diamond was right that agriculture was a mistake for individual welfare, even if it won out demographically. I think inequality came from storage and defense of surplus, not farming per se, which is why I think a farming society without good storage tech might not have developed big hierarchies. At the same time I think the transition was mostly inevitable once population density hit a point, so calling it a 'mistake' feels weird, like blaming people for falling into a trap. I also think people over-romantacize forager gender relations, but I don't think that cancels the welfare comparison. Heres the part I keep circling back to: I think the category of 'work' is basically an agricultural invention, because foragers don't have a sharp labor/leisure split — so when I say they 'worked less' I'm smuggling in a conceptual frame that belongs to farming societies. Does that last point undermine my leisure argument? Are there other tensions hiding in here, and if so what's the cheapest revision that saves my core intuition?
