I was discussing the Rietdijk–Putnam argument with a close friend over coffee yesterday, and our conversation turned into a long argument about whether the block universe is genuinely forced on us or whether it is merely a coordinate curiosity that dissolves outside of physics textbooks. I understand the logical structure: because special relativity denies absolute simultaneity, any event space-like separated from my present can be placed in the past, present, or future depending on the inertial frame, which undermines the idea of a single objective “now” slicing through the cosmos. From this, philosophers often infer that all events—past, present, and future—exist on equal ontological footing, which is the B-theory or block-universe view. But my friend kept insisting that this reasoning only gains purchase if we imagine observers actually traveling at velocities approaching c relative to one another, and that since no human-scale observer ever does so, the contradiction with an objective present never arises in practice; in the non-relativistic regime where we actually live, she argued, we can safely treat simultaneity as approximately absolute and dismiss the block universe as a mathematical artifact of the Lorentz transformation rather than a serious metaphysical thesis. I think she is missing the point that the transformation applies at any v > 0, however small, but I am struggling to articulate precisely why the ontological conclusion does not simply scale away with velocity.

Could you please explain, in American English, whether the Rietdijk–Putnam argument genuinely depends on macroscopic high-speed observers to generate its tension with an objective present, or whether the frame-dependence of simultaneity is structurally present even at walking-speed separations? I would also like you to address whether accepting the block universe necessarily commits one to perdurantism about objects and personal identity—treating a person as a four-dimensional “worm” stretched across the manifold—or whether an endurantist can consistently maintain that the same object is wholly present at each temporal stage without being distributed through spacetime. Please do not treat the argument as a proof of determinism or causal closure; I am specifically asking about ontological parity and the compatibility of endurantism with eternalism.
