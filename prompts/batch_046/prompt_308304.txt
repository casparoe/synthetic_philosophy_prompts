Here's something that bugs me about welfare-state immigration rules. Most EU states give immigrants immediate access to emergency healthcare and primary schooling—treating them as present members of the moral community in those spheres—but then gate unemployment benefits, housing subsidies, and voting rights behind years of residency, labor-market attachment, and sometimes mandatory "integation contracts" that citizens never have to sign. What's the implicit philosophy of membership here? Does it presuppose that social rights are earned through contribution, or is there a hidden idea of an "integration debt"—a provisional status that's neither full exclusion nor full inclusion? Reconstruct the view, check if it's coherent with the same states' universalist rhetoric about human dignity, and tell me what open-borders advocates actually have to dismantle: just the policy mechanics, or the conceptual framework that makes conditional membership seem reasonable? And is the practice philosophically underdetermined—could both a stakeholder theory and a modified social-contract view license it?
