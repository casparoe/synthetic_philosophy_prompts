Draft the joint adversarial-collaboration statement that a committed Jamesian defender of the will to believe and a committed Cliffordian defender of the ethics of belief could both sign after sustained good-faith argument. Center the collaboration on a specific, non-abstract case: an institutional researcher must decide whether to adopt and act upon a provisional epidemiological transmission model before full peer verification, in conditions where practical urgency coincides with structural evidential incompleteness. The document should open with a numbered section of claims both parties can endorse, covering the nature of evidential gaps, the distinction between private assent and institutional endorsement, and the criteria by which belief-formation can be ethically evaluated. This should be followed by a disagreements table that states the residual differences in precise, neutral wording, with each formulation acceptable to the side it represents. Identify the cruxes — I take them to be whether practical urgency can ever legitimately lower the evidential threshold required for belief adoption, and whether an institutional role modifies the moral obligations that govern belief — and for each crux specify the concrete case or historical pattern both sides agree is relevant (for instance, the record of delayed versus premature institutional endorsements during public-health emergencies), recording in advance each side's explicit prediction of what that case demonstrates. Finally, indicate whether any part of their dispute is merely verbal rather than substantive, and if so, which part and why. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
