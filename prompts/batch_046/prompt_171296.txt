In debates over whether individualism or collectivism gets the person-community relation right—especially East Asian relational views versus Western liberal ones—two analogies dominate: society is either a marketplace in which atomic individuals strike contracts for mutual gain, or a woven fabric in which each thread is constituted by the tension of the whole. Audit both analogies with explicit correspondence tables—what plays the role of the contract, the weaver, the thread, the market price, and the fabric’s stress—paying special attention to how each handles the idea that the self is formed through betweenness (aidagara) and climatic-social embeddedness (fudo) rather than preceding its relations. Identify the disanalogies that actually matter for autonomy and moral responsibility, show what normative conclusions each analogy smuggles in about whether rights belong to nodes or to the web, and determine whether either survives once those commitments are exposed; if neither does, suggest what a better analogy might be. Keep the audit focused and avoid appeals to bibliography or authority.
