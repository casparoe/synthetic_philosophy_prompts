Here are my verdicts on when pure time preference is rationally or morally justified, and I want the principle that captures them—if one exists. I think it is wrong to save one thousand people today rather than twelve hundred in fifty years solely because the latter group lives in the future; temporal distance by itself should not diminish moral weight. Yet I also think it is permissible for me, now, to spend on my own retirement rather than donate to a climate fund that will benefit strangers in three centuries, and this does not feel like mere risk-aversion about the fund’s efficacy—there seems to be a legitimate preference for goods within my own expected lifespan. If I am ninety, preferring a smaller benefit now to a larger one in a century feels like sound prudence rather than a moral failure, even if I care about my descendants; but if I am thirty, preferring a smaller present benefit over a far larger one for my own eighty-year-old self strikes me as irrational, not as an exercise of legitimate preference. I also think a society may use a small positive discount rate in economic planning, but only because capital productivity makes present savings generate more future value—not because future suffering matters less; applying that same rate directly to the moral importance of future welfare seems like a category mistake. Then there is what I call an identity-ambiguous future: if future beings will preserve my values and psychological architecture through digital continuation but lack biological continuity, I feel my discount rate toward them should drop sharply, as if they were distant parts of me; but I do not feel the same reduction applies to my biological descendants unless I have formed a specific personal bond with them. Is there a coherent principle of pure time preference that accommodates all of these judgments, or must some be abandoned for simplicity? State the principle precisely, show which clause explains which case, predict its verdict on a case I have not mentioned—whether a governing body should apply a non-zero pure time preference when weighing present citizens against sentient artificial systems that will be created in fifty years, share the citizens’ values, but have no biological or psychological continuity with them—and be explicit about which of my verdicts the principle cannot save.
