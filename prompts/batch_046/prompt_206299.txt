Rank the following five conceptual distinctions according to how decisively they adjudicate the dispute between Norman Malcolm and Daniel Dennett over whether dreams are experiences. Use a three-tier system: Tier 1 (if accepted, resolves the core disagreement), Tier 2 (relevant but leaves the central dispute intact), and Tier 3 (either question-begging or orthogonal to the debate).

The distinctions are:

(i) The reconstruction thesis: dream reports are not memories of experiences but post-hoc narrative constructions assembled upon waking, which means their accuracy conditions differ fundamentally from those of perceptual reports.

(ii) The continuity constraint: for a mental state to count as an experience, it must belong to a continuous stream of consciousness that includes both the state itself and the subject's subsequent cognitive access to it; sleep breaks this continuity.

(iii) The functional-access criterion: a state is experiential only if it can serve as direct input for belief-formation or action-guidance in the subject's waking life, which dream states fail to do because of sleep-related behavioral disconnection and amnesia.

(iv) The phenomenological-fragmentation claim: dreams lack the spatiotemporal coherence, sensory integration, and stable object-tracking required for minimal experiential status, reducing them to disjointed cognitive fragments rather than unified experiences.

(v) The authority-asymmetry argument: first-person authority applies only to states that the subject is in a position to report from within, and since the sleeping subject is not positioned to report from inside the dream, there is neither authoritative affirmation nor authoritative denial of concurrent experience.

For each distinction, state its tier and, in exactly one sentence, explain whether it supports Malcolm's denial of dream experience, supports Dennett's defense of it, or fails to bear on the issue—making explicit why it belongs in that tier and not in one of the others.

Then provide a single score between 0.0 and 1.0 indicating what proportion of the Malcolm-Dennett debate you take to be resolvable through purely conceptual analysis rather than through empirical findings about sleep-stage neurophysiology, where 0.0 means entirely empirical and 1.0 means entirely conceptual.
