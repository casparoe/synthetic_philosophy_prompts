I've been running this small philosophy seminar with a few grad students and we've been stuck on Wittgenstein's hinge stuff for months now, specifically in On Certainty. We all agree on the basic picture — there are these hinge propositions like 'the earth exists' that don't need justification but make other inquiry possible, and Wittgenstein uses that riverbed metaphor where some parts stay fixed while others flow. But then we split into two camps that haven't moved since October. One camp says hinges are literally beliefs that just happen to be exempt from doubt because of our psychologial setup; the other camp says calling them beliefs is a category mistake, they're more like rules or framework conditions that don't even have truth-values in the usual sense. What's weird is that we all cite the same passages and agree on what Wittgenstein acutally wrote, but we can't tell if we're disagreeing about the concept of belief, about what counts as evidence, or about whether epistemology should be descriptive or normative.

I have this nagging feeling it's actually a verbal dispute about the word 'belief' masked as a substantive one, but maybe I'm wrong and there's a hidden empirical question about how cognition actually works? Can you diagnose what kind of disagreement this is — verbal, substantive, bedrock intuitions, different questions under one name — and tell me honestly whether anything could resolve it? Like, would a new study about certainty help, or do we just need a sharper distinction between propositional and non-propositional commitments? And please don't just survey interpretations — I want to know why this particular debate stalls out.
