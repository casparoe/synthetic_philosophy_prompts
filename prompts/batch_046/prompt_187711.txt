My friend and me got in a long arguement about deflationism last night and now I'm stuck on this chain he built. (1) If deflationism is right then "Snow is white is true" doesn't say anything over and above "Snow is white"—the truth predicate is just semantic ascent. (2) But every other philosophical predicate works the same way: "Unicorns exist" collapses via quantifier ascent, "It's necessary that 2+2=4" collapses via modal ascent, and even "Murder is wrong" collapses if you buy a deontic disquotation schema. (3) So if the reasoning in (1) kills substantive truth, it kills substantive existence, modality, and normativity too. (4) That means all metaphysics and ethics flatten into bare first-order claims—there's no real debate about what makes something true, real, or obligatory, just repetition of the base sentences. (5) But that's absurd; people clearly disagree about whether moral properties exist or whether causation is real. (6) So (1) must be false and deflationism about truth is wrong.

Where does this slip? I want to keep the idea that truth is thin, but I also don't want to accidentally nuke all of metaphysics. Which step should I reject, and what's the cost—do I have to admit truth is a heavy metaphysical property, or is there a cleaner way out?
