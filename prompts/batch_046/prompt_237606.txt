At our grad student reading group last week we got stuck arguing about Carnap's views on rational planning and politics. Specifically, we were debating whether his tolerance principle -- the idea that we should be free to choose logical frameworks based on practical efficency -- can be extended to social and economic planning without hiding normative assumptions inside the "practical" criterion.

Maya said yes, and that Carnap basically invites this extension. She pointed out that Carnap rejected metaphysical claims about the "true" structure of society just like he rejected metaphysical claims about the "true" ontology, so planning should be treated as a conventional engineering problem: pick the institutional framework that best achieves agreed-upon goals, with no deeper philosophical disputes. Leo said that's a misreading: Carnap's internal/external distinction applies to language frameworks, not to lived social reality. For Leo, economic planning involves external questions about suffering and power that aren't conventionally choosable, and treating them as internal smuggles in authoritarian assumptions under the guise of technical neutrality.

Maya answered that Leo is importing a false dichotomy -- Carnap's own political writings in the 1930s show he supported democratic planning precisely because he thought value disagreements could be resolved through rational discussion of means, not ends. Leo shot back that this just pushes the problem back: if ends are plural and incommensurable, then "agreed-upon goals" is a fiction, and Carnap's planning optimism depends on a hidden monism about human welfare that his own anti-metaphysical stance should reject.

They asked me to referee and I genuinely couldn't tell who was closer to Carnap's actual view. Please adjudicate point by point: is Maya right that Carnap's tolerance principle extends to social planning? Is Leo right that this smuggles in hidden norms? What's going on with the internal/external distinction here? And are they both missing something about Carnap's actual political commitments in the Vienna Circle context? Say who's more right overall, and flag any place where the disagreement is partly verbal.
