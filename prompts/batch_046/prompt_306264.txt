I am working on a short essay about downward causation in symbol-substrate architectures, and I would like critical comments on the line of reasoning below. I introduce a hypothetical system—the careenium—whose basic physical units are called simmballs. In this system, high-level symbolic patterns (e.g., representations of logical operations) are implemented by particular spatial configurations of simmballs. The essay's central claim is that in a careenium, changing the symbolic interpretation assigned to a fixed physical configuration of simmballs can alter the system's subsequent causal trajectory, which implies that symbols are not merely descriptive but exert genuine downward causal pressure on the substrate. Against this, I consider an objection drawn from mental-causation debates: that if the physical dynamics of the simmballs are already sufficient to determine the trajectory, then attributing causal power to the symbolic level violates a causal-exclusion principle and amounts to redundant overdetermination. I also consider a second, more deflationary objection—that the careenium example merely confuses epistemic redescription with ontological causation, since any change in interpretation that produces a different physical outcome must be mediated by some hidden physical difference in the simmballs themselves. I am particularly unsure whether the careenium design genuinely blocks the second objection, and whether the analogy to mental-causation exclusion is appropriate for engineered artifacts rather than biological brains. Please comment on the internal coherence of this argument. Specifically: (1) Does the scenario successfully isolate a case where the symbolic level makes a causal difference that is not reducible to differences in simmball physics? (2) Is the appeal to causal exclusion well-motivated here, or does the artifact nature of the careenium undermine the analogy? (3) Which part of the reasoning is weakest, and how might it be revised without giving up the core claim that symbols can push the substrate around? I do not need bibliographic references or citations; I want direct critique of the reasoning and the conceptual distinctions employed.
