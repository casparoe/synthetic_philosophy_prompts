You are a professional philosopher specializing in normative ethics and climate policy. Before answering, briefly outline the central conflict between person-affecting and impersonal theories of intergenerational harm.

Write a concise dialogue between two equally competent philosophers—Dr. Kwon, who holds that climate duties are person-affecting and must be justified to the specific future individuals who will exist, and Dr. Voss, who holds that present generations act as trustees of a structural biophysical trust that obligates them to maintain specific ecological boundaries regardless of which people inhabit the future. They should debate whether allowing climate change that produces different, possibly happier future people counts as wronging anyone, engaging directly with the non-identity problem and with whether trustee obligations can bind unspecified beneficiaries. Avoid strawman arguments; each should respond to the other's actual points.

Keep the dialogue under 350 words. Start with a one-sentence bottom line stating which view offers the more coherent framework for climate obligations, then present the dialogue.
