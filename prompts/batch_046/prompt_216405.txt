I have been trying to clarify my own position on Molyneux's problem and on cross-modal perception more generally, and I should like to know whether the following set of commitments actually hangs together or whether I am relying on an unstated assumption that pulls them apart.

(1) I am a concept empiricist with respect to spatial properties: there is no innate, modality-independent representation of shape, and any concept of cubeness or sphericity must be acquired through direct sensory interaction.
(2) I believe that Molyneux's blind man, upon suddenly gaining sight, would not immediately be able to identify the cube and the sphere by vision alone, because his visual representations of those shapes would not yet be linked to his long-established tactile representations.
(3) I hold that perceptual experience is concept-independent: it is perfectly possible to undergo a visual experience with the phenomenal character of seeing a cube without possessing the concept CUBE.
(4) I also maintain that there is a genuine explanatory gap between the geometric description of a shape and the phenomenal quality of perceiving it, whether in touch or in vision.
(5) I accept the findings from sensory-substitution research—specifically, tactile-to-visual brain-computer interfaces that allow blind users to navigate spatial environments—and I take this to show that the brain can remap structural information across sensory modalities without relying on explicit associative learning or on a shared representational format.
(6) Despite (5), I deny that there is any amodal "spatial currency" common to touch and vision; I think substitution succeeds because visual cortex is recruited to process tactile signals directly, not because both modalities access a single underlying representation.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, ranking them by severity, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be. I also want you to identify at least one pair that looks as though it conflicts but in fact does not, explaining precisely why the apparent contradiction dissolves. Do not assume that I am willing to drop any particular commitment in advance; the task is an audit of consistency, not a recommendation to abandon a specific view. Please write your answer in British English.
