- The end of foot-binding in China (early 1900s): driven mainly by national honor dynamics—Chinese elites sought to avoid being classified as barbaric by Western powers—rather than by feminist moral argument.
- The disappearance of dueling among Southern American gentlemen (mid-1800s): driven mainly by a transformation in local honor codes, in which dueling came to signal reckless vanity rather than courageous masculinity; legal prohibition followed rather than caused the decline.
- The rapid collapse of the expectation that Japanese factory managers must publicly resign after industrial accidents (1970s–1990s): driven mainly by changes in liability law and labor-market interests, with honor playing almost no independent role.
