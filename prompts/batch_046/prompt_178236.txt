ok so I'm trying to map out where I actually stand on indirect normativity and idealized deliberation for a seminar I'm running — not a formal course, just a reading group with some grad students and a couple people from the CS department who keep asking weird questions. I want a consistency check before I present my view.

Roughly my package is:

(1) I buy indirect normativity for practical reason: what I have reason to do is fixed by what my idealized deliberative counterpart would endorse, not by some direct hook-up to objective values floating out there.

(2) But I want idealization to be thin, not thick. It should correct factual ignorance, logical inconsistancies, and maybe some bias, but it shouldn't swap my core values for those of an impartial spectator or a fully generic rational agent.

(3) At the same time, I think there are genuinely "alien" practical reasons — obligations to reduce wild animal suffering, or to preserve options for far-future generations — that bind agents even when those agents have no current inclination and wouldn't develop one through thin idealization. They're not just "you'd care if you were nicer."

(4) I also think practical rationality is subject to a competitiveness constraint: if adopting a particular set of reasons systematically undermines your ability to pursue any of your actual projects, that's not just an unfortunate side-effect but a serious strike against those reasons being genuinely yours.

(5) And I reject internalism about reasons — I think you can have a reason you aren't motivated to follow, without that automatically making you irrational.

So: do these hang together? I'm especially worried about (1)-(3) together with (2), and whether (4) is secretly incompatible with (1). I also wonder if (2) and (5) pull against each other in a way I'm missing — like, if idealization is thin, doesn't that tie reasons too close to actual motivation? Rank the tensions by severity, say for each pair what has to give and at what cost, and flag the ones that only look bad but dissolve once you see the distinction. Don't just list them all as equally bad; I want to know where the real damage is and what the cheapest revision looks like.
