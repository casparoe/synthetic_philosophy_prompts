ok so i'm trying to pin down where i stand on sphexishness and free will and i'm not sure it all hangs together. here's roughly my package:

1. real free will is basically the capacity to break sphexish loops — you're not locked into a fixed behavioral script when the context shifts. the wasp isn't free because it can't interrupt its burrow-check ritual, no matter how many times you move the cricket.

2. but i also think the brain is deterministic machinery. all our "flexibility" is just recusive self-modification — higher-order loops tweaking lower-order ones. still mechanical, just more nested.

3. i reject compatabilism because it feels like it lets determinism in through the back door by calling flexible determinism "freedom." so i don't want to be that person.

4. yet i still want to say that this recursive self-modification IS genuine freedom, not just a fancier cage. we have it, the wasp lacks it, that's the real distinction.

5. and i think the wasp is unfree not because it's deterministic — since we're deterministic too — but specifically because it lacks this recursive loop-breaking capacity.

my worry is that (3) and (4) are fighting each other. if i'm rejecting compatibilism for letting determinism in, but then i'm letting determinism in through recursion, what's the principled line? also does (2) undercut (1) — if breaking loops is just another determined loop breaking, have i actually escaped sphexishness or just built a sphex with more steps?

please audit this for consistency. rank the tensions by severity, say which ones dissolve on closer inspection, and what's the cheapest revision (probably giving up 3, or 4, or reconceiving 1?). and please separate what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who actually work on this — don't present one view as the obvious consensus. i know the literature is messier than it looks from outside.
