I have sketched notes for an essay that treats the frame problem less as a computational bottleneck in belief revision and more as an epistemological puzzle about how agents delimit relevance without already possessing the knowledge they wish to update. I distinguish representational framing—the task of explicitly encoding which conditions do not change—from attentional framing, the predictive-processing approach of suppressing prediction error, and I argue that the latter merely displaces the former because it offers no normative criterion for which predictions ought to be suppressed. I then propose a further, rarely discussed extension: in multi-agent contexts the problem becomes indexical, since an agent must frame not only what changes in the world but which mental representations belong to which epistemic subject, yet standard formal treatments treat belief spaces as monadic.
