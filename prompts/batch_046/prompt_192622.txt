I am trying to understand an episode from the Zhuangzi in which Master Zhuang's wife dies. His friend Hui Zi finds him sitting with his legs spread out, drumming on a tub and singing. When Hui Zi reproaches him for this apparent lack of grief, Zhuangzi replies that he initially wept, but then reflected that before her birth she had no form and that her death is simply another transformation in the great process—like the turning of the seasons—so prolonged mourning is unfounded.

Please help me understand this passage in light of a distinction that modern philosophers of death draw between two claims. The first is that death destroys the subject of harm, so that grief is rational only as a loss experienced by survivors, not as sympathy for the deceased. The second is a process ontology in which the dead persist in transformed form, but the specific relational configuration that constituted being "his wife" has dissolved, so that personal mourning commits a category mistake by treating a temporary arrangement as a permanent substance. Which of these frameworks, if either, illuminates Zhuangzi's reply to Hui Zi? Does he deny that death is a harm to her, deny that she is the kind of thing that can persist, or reject the psychological categories that make grief appropriate? I want a reading that respects the text's own conceptual vocabulary while engaging seriously with the modern debate, and that does not simply reduce Zhuangzi to an analytic philosopher of death.
