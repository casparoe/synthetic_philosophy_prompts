I have come to accept a position about collective memory that does not map neatly onto the standard debates between remembrance and forgetting. Drawing on the tension between the ethics of memory associated with Avishai Margalit and the case for collective forgetting advanced by David Rieff, I hold that democratic societies ought to practice what I call mnemonic triage: instead of treating the preservation of historical wrongs as a default moral duty, a society should assess whether maintaining a particular memory can be integrated into present civic cooperation without locking descendants into irreconcilable moral hierarchies. On this view, institutionalized forgetting—through curriculum design, the restriction of certain archives, or the removal of public commemorations—is not a failure of justice but a precondition for democratic equality among citizens who bear no direct culpability for the wrong in question. What would have to be true for this position to be wrong? I want an honest map of its exposure. Which empirical cases or historical examples would demonstrate that selective forgetting erodes rather than sustains democratic trust? Which philosophical arguments—perhaps about the non-transferability of moral obligations across generations, the expressive value of public remembrance, or the conditions for genuine forgiveness—would defenders of mnemonic triage have to concede as genuinely damaging rather than merely awkward? Does the view risk being qualified into unfalsifiability if every failure of forgetting is reinterpreted as a failure of triage rather than of the principle itself, and if so, is that insulation a problem for a claim of this kind? Is there a decisive result in the philosophy of history, in theories of moral desert, or in accounts of group responsibility that collapses mnemonic triage into either pure retributivism or pure amnesia? Please rank the potential defeaters from fatal to merely inconvenient, indicate which ones proponents of the view would themselves recognize as counting against it, and conclude with an assessment of whether the position is well insulated or genuinely vulnerable.
