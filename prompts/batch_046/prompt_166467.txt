Structure the deliverable as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: the formal setup (a manifold with a region—the "hole"—where two metrics are identical outside but related inside by a diffeomorphism that is not the identity there); the determinism failure that results if points carry non-derivative identity; the standard point-coincidence resolution; and the mathematical results both accept about physical equivalence of diffeomorphism-related metrics in general relativity. Third, the cruxes—sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one party—focusing on whether background-independence in quantum gravity eliminates the ontological target the argument aims at, and on whether part of the dispute is verbal about what counts as "the same physical situation." Fourth, for each crux, identify a concrete theoretical development both sides agree bears on it (for example, the treatment of singularities or gauge symmetry in discrete or background-independent spacetime models), and record in advance what each side predicts about its relevance.

Take a committed position: do not merely lay out options. State clearly which disagreements you judge substantive and which you judge verbal, defend that classification with reasons, and say explicitly whether you think empirical success of discrete or emergent-spacetime theories would undermine substantivalism or whether the metaphysical core of the dispute survives independently of the formalism.
