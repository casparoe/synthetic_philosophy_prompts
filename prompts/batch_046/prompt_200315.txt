You chair the ethics review board of a neuroprosthetics institute that evaluates proposals for gradual cortical replacement—biological neurons substituted region by region with synthetic analogues over years. The guiding philosophical claim is that personal survival through this process requires three jointly satisfied conditions: (1) functional isomorphism maintained at every replacement stage; (2) uninterrupted phenomenal self-model continuity, meaning no interval in which first-person consciousness exhibits a discontinuous "flicker"; and (3) continuous narrative endorsement, whereby the post-replacement system affirmatively integrates the pre-replacement autobiographical thread without revision.

Translate this three-condition survival theory into an operational review procedure your board can apply repeatedly without requiring members to hold philosophical degrees. Specify the verification sequence; the clinical or empirical evidence that counts as satisfying or failing each condition; the decision rules—where failure triggers an immediate halt, where it permits a modified partial-replacement protocol, and where conflicting conditions (e.g., functional and narrative continuity intact but phenomenological monitoring reports an ambiguous gap) force an explicit value judgment and hand the decision to human deliberation rather than fact; and the precise points at which judgment is unavoidable.

Then apply the procedure to two proposals: (A) replacing 15% of prefrontal tissue annually over seven years with real-time feedback loops, where all conditions hold except for one ambiguous 72-hour phenomenological flicker during the fourth stage; and (B) a single-step hippocampal replacement after six months of synaptic bridging, where functional and narrative continuity are fully preserved but first-person reportability is suspended for exactly 48 hours, after which the patient endorses the prior narrative unchanged. Ensure one case produces a recommendation the research team will likely reject.

Conclude by stating exactly what the procedure cannot determine—particularly the moral weight of a brief phenomenological gap versus a structural discontinuity. Keep the response within 400 words; do not provide bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
