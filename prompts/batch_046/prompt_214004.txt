Since taking on a multi-year research directorship with distant milestones and unpredictable interim demands, I have become preoccupied with whether my chronic preference for immediate administrative closure over long-term writing should be analyzed as weakness of will or as structurally rational temporal preference. Please argue that hyperbolic discounting is not a distinct mechanism from akrasia but a temporal variant of it: specifically, that when a present self disregards the evaluated preferences of an anticipated future self, this involves the same judgment-error structure Davidson identifies in standard incontinence, and that framing hyperbolic curves as mere preference reversal rather than practical-reasoning failure obscures the normative force of intertemporal commitments. Address the objection that hyperbolic discounters lack the characteristic conflict between all-things-considered judgment and present desire, and explain why applying an interpersonal model of agency across temporal stages—without reducing the phenomenon to strategic bargaining—reveals rational fragmentation rather than rational adaptation.
