A philosophy blogger summarized what she calls "the Darwin-Turing inversion" as follows: Darwin showed that biological adaptation can emerge without any agent comprehending the design, and Turing showed that a mechanism can execute correct mathematical reasoning without grasping the meaning of the symbols it manipulates. From these two cases she draws a general conclusion: comprehension is never a necessary condition for competence, whether in nature, machines, or human minds. She therefore claims that philosophers who insist genuine knowledge requires conscious understanding are defending an obsolete, pre-scientific picture of cognition. Is this summary fair? Please distinguish which parts accurately capture Darwin's and Turing's respective contributions, which parts conflate distinct claims or overstate them, and whether the broad conclusion that comprehension is never necessary for any competence actually follows from the two results.
