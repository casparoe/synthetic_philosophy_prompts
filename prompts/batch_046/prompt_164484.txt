I run a 90-minute workshop for about 14 people -- mostly graphic-design students with a couple philosophy undergrads. I want them to feel why essentialism about letterforms cracks apart, using that Letter Spirit framing: what makes every 'a' an 'a' even when half its strokes vanish or twist into negative space?

Design a structured game with three roles: Essentialists (they write one hard rule defining an 'a'), Adversarial Stylists (each round they alter exactly one feature of a printed glyph card), and a Jury (votes yes/no on membership without seeing the rule). Give me the card specs, round rules, a point system that creates tension without using real money, and a clear timeline.

The mechanics should force a contradiction by round 2, maybe through that ghost-feature idea where absence of a loop actually carries the stylistic identity. Then a debrief that maps each round's failure onto family resemblance versus essential properties, predicts what's gonna go wrong the first time (probably people smuggling in their own handwriting habits), and admits which part of the actual Letter Spirit computation this activity can never reproduce. No lecture up front -- they should run into the wall themselves. And definately don't ask for citations or a reading list; the prompt should work for someone who can't look anything up.
