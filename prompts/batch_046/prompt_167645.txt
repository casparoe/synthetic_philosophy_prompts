Design a thought experiment set in one of the UK, Scandinavian, or Japanese constitutional monarchies, in which a binding referendum on retaining the monarchy is decided by exactly one vote, and the decisive voter is later revealed to have been swayed by a purely sentimental, non-political attachment—say, a childhood association with the royal family—rather than by any argument about democratic legitimacy or institutional stability. The scenario should be realistic, confined to one paragraph, and must avoid science fiction, perfect predictors, exotic payoffs, or references to overused historical cases like Orwellian revisionism. Your case must generate three distinct verdicts: (1) a procedural-democratic republican view that accepts retention because the referendum process was legitimate regardless of the voter’s private motives; (2) a substantive-democratic republican view that rejects retention regardless of the vote because hereditary office is inherently incompatible with democratic equality; and (3) a functionalist monarchist view that accepts retention but not because the vote conferred democratic legitimacy, instead citing the monarchy’s value as a non-partisan reserve institution that provides continuity outside electoral politics. After presenting the scenario, show the reasoning for each verdict, identify the precise structural feature that makes the functionalist peel away from both republican positions, and explain whether removing the sentimental-decider element collapses the case back into a standard debate about democratic mandate alone.
