For an upper-level philosophy seminar, construct a decision-tree taxonomy of positions on the gender-equality paradox — the empirical pattern in which societies with stronger institutional gender equality often display larger gender differences in occupational and educational choice — designed so that a reader can locate their own view by answering branching questions without knowing the theory labels in advance. Root node: is the cross-national correlation between equality indices and occupational gender segregation a robust empirical finding, or largely an artifact of measurement choices (e.g., which fields are weighted, how part-time work is coded)? First split: should gender-differentiated career choice in this context be interpreted primarily as revealed preference, constrained preference shaped by residual socialization, or adaptive preference formed under unequal conditions? Second split: is the normative standard for gender justice in this domain procedural equality of opportunity, distributive equality of representation across fields, or relational equality of status regardless of field composition? Third split: can one coherently acknowledge statistically significant sex-linked developmental or temperamental differences without committing to gender essentialism, biological determinism, or the irrelevance of social structure? Fourth split: if procedural barriers are removed but divergence persists, does justice require state or institutional intervention to correct the distribution, neutrality that respects the divergence as autonomous, or active accommodation of different life patterns? At each leaf, place either a named position (for example, structural-constraint feminism, preference-autonomy liberalism, or relational-equality egalitarianism) or the label "unnamed," together with one sentence describing the view's most awkward commitment — such as having to deny all preference malleability, or endorsing coercive career redistribution despite formal equality. Identify at least one branch combination that is currently unoccupied and diagnose whether the gap is conceptually incoherent (the four answers generate contradictory commitments) or merely sociologically unexplored. Explicitly flag any pair of named positions that land on the identical leaf and explain why the tree cannot separate them — whether they share the same answers on all four splits or differ only in rhetorical framing. Ensure the taxonomy is usable as a sorting instrument: every internal node must be phrased as a concrete claim a respondent could accept or reject without prior knowledge of the philosophical literature. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and refer to empirical patterns only in generic terms (e.g., "cross-national occupational data"). Write the response in American English.
