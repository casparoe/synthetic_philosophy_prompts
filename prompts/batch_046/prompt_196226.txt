I'm preparing a seminar on Schopenhauer's aesthetics and have noticed that participants consistently reach for a particular analogy to make sense of his claim that music occupies a unique metaphysical status. They describe music as functioning like smell for the Will: just as an odor can bypass cortical representation and trigger an immediate limbic response—carrying affect without being parsed into concepts—so melody and harmony are said to reproduce the inner movements of the Will directly, unmediated by the principle of sufficient reason and unfiltered through the Ideas that govern the other arts. The analogy is used to defend Schopenhauer's thesis that music is not a copy of phenomena but a copy of the Will itself, with the "direct pipeline" quality explaining why it can express the universal grades of objectification without depicting any particular object. Before I present this framing to students, I want to audit how far the comparison actually holds within Schopenhauer's own system in his principal metaphysical work. Can you construct the correspondence table—mapping the olfactory bulb, the limbic strike, the absence of symbolic mediation, and the evolutionary urgency onto Schopenhauer's metaphysics of music—then identify precisely where the analogy fractures? Specifically, does Schopenhauer's account of music expressing the Will's objectification depend on any mechanism analogous to a biological bypass, or does the analogy quietly import a materialist and evolutionary psychology he explicitly rejects? If the disanalogies undermine the conclusion that music enjoys a uniquely unmediated metaphysical privilege, is there a better analogy—perhaps drawn from mathematics or pure geometry—that captures his actual argument without smuggling in contemporary neuroscience? And does the relaxed pedagogical conclusion—that Schopenhauer is essentially describing an affective shortcut—survive once the mapping is made explicit?
