Here are my interpretations of Rousseau's judgments across theatrical cases. He opposes establishing a theater in Geneva; he regards Parisian theater as harmful but not decisive for an already corrupt capital; he endorses civic festivals in which all citizens actively dance, sing, or compete; he rejects private salon performances that feed aristocratic vanity; and he condemns traveling troupes in villages because they still split the crowd into active performers and passive spectators. I want the underlying principle—whether it concerns physical co-presence and luxury, the distinction between active participation and spectatorship, or artificial representation displacing real moral action—and I want it tested. Consider a decentralized platform that generates personalized immersive dramas experienced in total isolation: there are no live performers, no visible audience, no luxury display, only solitary consumption of procedurally generated narrative through an interface. Would Rousseau condemn it, and on which of the three grounds? Give your best guess, identify the competing rules that diverge here, and propose one further case—perhaps an anonymous collective virtual ritual with no performers, or a hidden physical theater—that would settle which rule he actually holds.
