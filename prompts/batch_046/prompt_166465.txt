I'm presenting a 20-minute seminar paper to a philosophy-of-social-science workshop. Thesis: money is not a socially constructed kind in the same register as race or gender. I grant Haslanger-style ameliorative analysis for race and gender—kinds whose membership conditions are stabilized by hierarchical social practices that could be otherwise. But I argue that money operates as an institutional fact whose collective-intentionality conditions are constrained by non-arbitrary functional properties—scarcity protocols, verifiability, network topology—so that decentralized digital currencies expose the fragility of the sovereign-declaration model without dissolving money into pure convention. You cannot socially construct a stable unit of account the way you can socially construct a racial category; the kind's dynamics are partly locked by informational and game-theoretic structures.

Audience: mostly analytic philosophers of race and gender (familiar with Haslanger, Mills, Ásta, feminist metaphysics), two institutional economists who treat ontology as a distraction, and a couple of rationalist-leaning grad students who care about prediction markets and ontology. They are not hostile, but they think natural-kind talk about social phenomena is either naive realism or crypto-libertarian mysticism. They have not read Searle recently.
