In our philospohy of medicine reading group we've been going round and round about Szasz's idea that mental illness is basically a myth. One side says that's clearly false now — we see brain differences in people diagnosed with schizophrenia, so treating mental illness as a myth must mean there's literally no biological reality behind the label, which is just wrong. The other side says Szasz was pointing out that psychiatric categories aren't like medical diseases with specific lesions or pathognomonic markers; they're defined by behavior and social norms, so the myth is that we're treating moral and social problems as if they were bodily diseases. But literally everyone in the group agrees that severe mental distress is real and that brains are involved somehow. We've been at this for monts with no movement. So what's actually going on? Is the stall because the word myth is ambiguous — sometimes meaning no biological basis, sometimes meaning not a proper medical kind? Or is there a deeper disagreement about whether explaining suffering biologically removes personal agency? And if you think it's mostly a verbal or conceptual mess, what kind of distinction or reframing could actually break the deadlock — or is this just one of those debates where neither side can budge because they're defending different values about autonomy and care?
