Write a poem about Confucian role ethics that treats the Analects episode—where a father conceals a stolen sheep and his upright son reports him—not as an internal dilemma between filial piety (xiao) and righteousness (yi) inside a pre-existing agent, but through the niche relational view that there is no chooser prior to the roles; the self is constituted by being-with-others, so the tension is interstitial, occurring in the space between father, son, and community rather than within one mind. Deliver it as exactly five numbered couplets, each couplet two lines long and expressing one or two complete sentences, with no title, no introduction, and no concluding material beyond the fifth. The form should do conceptual work: open each couplet with a different relational position—father, son, minister, neighbor, the unclaimed animal—so that no stable “I” persists across the sequence, and pair the rhyme or rhythm of each couplet’s second line to the first line of the next, letting identity flow across roles rather than settling in any single speaker. Use precise terms—xiao, yi, zhengming—without reducing them to abstract virtues, render the episode recognizably, and leave the conflict unresolved at the end, so the poem closes on the open tension rather than on judgment.
