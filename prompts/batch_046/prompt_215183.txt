As a retired experimental physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of science, I find that discussions of frequentism, subjectivism, and propensity rarely specify what an interpretation actually has to deliver in scientific practice. Rather than a comparison of the three camps, I want a requirements list: the desiderata any adequate interpretation of probability must satisfy if it is to account for the reasoning we actually do in physics and climate science. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or theoretical result behind it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular school. Specifically, include: (1) an account of single-case predictive probabilities, such as a climate projection stating a 70 percent likelihood of exceeding a threshold by a specific date, without collapsing these into either long-run frequencies or purely subjective credences; (2) compatibility with statistical-mechanical explanations that appeal to ensemble averages, without requiring that every individual trajectory be assigned a well-defined propensity; (3) a resolution or principled dissolution of the reference-class problem for objective probabilities; (4) the capacity to distinguish epistemic uncertainty from physical chance in quantum measurements and in macroscopic chaotic systems alike; (5) consistency with the normative constraints on rational belief, such as coherence conditions that prevent Dutch-book vulnerability, but without reducing physical probabilities to degrees of belief; and (6) accommodation of interventionist or causal-probabilistic reasoning—how probabilities shift under explicit interventions rather than mere conditioning—without importing a full causal-graph ontology if that is not independently justified. Then indicate which pairs of these desiderata appear jointly unsatisfiable, and whether any single requirement eliminates the majority of existing interpretations. Finally, please conclude by stating what specific argument, empirical result, or conceptual distinction would cause you to revise this list—for instance, a demonstration that single-case probabilities are incoherent, or that coherence and objectivity are irreconcilable—and identify where in your reasoning you are most likely to have erred, particularly regarding the distinction between negotiable camp commitments and genuine constraints on any interpretation.
