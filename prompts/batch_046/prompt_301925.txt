I am preparing a three-month fellowship proposal for a small philosophy-of-science research group. We want to pursue a conceptual project on John von Neumann's unfinished lectures, published posthumously as The Computer and the Brain (1958). The standard reception treats his comparison of neural and digital computation either as an obsolete engineering speculation—overtaken by modern parallel architectures and neural networks—or as an inchoate anticipation of connectionism. Both readings miss what we take to be the philosophical core: von Neumann's suggestion that the brain employs a form of logic that is not Boolean, that treats component-level failure as an intrinsic structural feature rather than noise to be corrected, and that therefore operates with a normative standard for correct representation different from the mapping-fidelity assumed in standard representationalism.

Our precise question is this: can von Neumann's idea of a statistical-analog, error-tolerant computation be reconstructed as a coherent normative framework for mental representation, in which misrepresentation is not a deviation from a correct state but a constitutive parameter of the representational system itself? And if so, does this framework conflict irreconcilably with the representationalist distinction between correct and incorrect cognition, or can it be reconciled with a modified account of cognitive normativity—perhaps by analogizing representational error to the statistical dispersion tolerated in von Neumann's proposed logic?

Write the one-page proposal. It should state the research question with full precision; explain why the two dominant interpretations (historical dismissal and connectionist anticipation) leave the philosophical problem unresolved; specify a conceptual rather than empirical approach—e.g., formal reconstruction of a non-classical logic of belief revision coupled with an analysis of normativity in enactivist or predictive-processing frameworks, without importing their full empirical commitments; describe what a successful deliverable looks like (a reconstructed formal sketch plus a philosophical argument) and what counts as failure (showing the position collapses into standard connectionism or into incoherence); set a realistic three-month scope; and include a brief paragraph justifying why a funding body should support this philosophical reconstruction now rather than wait for neuroscience to settle empirical questions. Do not include a bibliography, citation list, or requests for specific page references; refer to von Neumann's work generally and avoid verbatim quotations.
