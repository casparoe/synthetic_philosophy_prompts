I've been following attempts to extend non-cognitivist semantics—developed originally for moral language—into epistemic vocabulary, treating phrases like "it is likely that p" or "the evidence supports q" as expressions of graded doxastic attitudes rather than descriptions of objective probabilities or representational belief states. The motivation is to avoid metaphysical commitments to modal realism or objective chance. But this extension immediately seems to face an analog of the Frege-Geach embedding problem. Consider a conditional: "If it is likely that emissions will stabilize by 2040, then aggressive adaptation is warranted." In the antecedent, "it is likely" does not appear to express my actual confidence; I am merely entertaining the supposition for the sake of argument. So if the semantic contribution of such phrases is purely the expression of a mental attitude, how can they retain stable meaning across asserted and unasserted contexts?

My confusion is whether this epistemic version is genuinely the same difficulty that moral expressivists face, or whether the quantitative structure of belief changes the problem entirely. Moral approval is typically binary, but epistemic confidence comes in degrees and updates according to Bayesian norms. Given that moral expressivists eventually developed compositional strategies—hybrid semantic theories that preserve logical syntax while grounding content in attitude—to address embedding, wouldn't those same strategies apply even more smoothly to graded credences? Or is there a hidden category mistake here—namely, that belief is representational by its very nature, so attempting to "expressivize" epistemic modality doesn't generate a puzzle that needs solving, but rather a reductio of the extension itself? In other words, if we treat "likely" as purely expressive and then discover it fails inside conditionals, should we conclude that we need a Bayesian-Geach solution, or that we never should have stripped epistemic vocabulary of its representational content in the first place?
