For a paper in my philosophy of life course, I am trying to read Harold Ramis's film Groundhog Day through the intersection of absurdism and contemporary accounts of meaning—specifically the fitting fulfillment model associated with Susan Wolf, on which a life is meaningful to the extent that the subject is actively engaged with projects that are both objectively valuable and subjectively fulfilling. Most interpretations treat Phil Connors's loop either as an absurdist prison he eventually transcends through moral growth, or as a conventional redemption narrative. I want to test a sharper framing that I think standard readings overlook: the loop does not merely suspend death and consequence, but eliminates what I will call cumulative narrative finality—the condition that meaningful projects derive part of their value from being irreversibly completed and leaving a lasting trace. Because Phil's achievements reset each morning, he is confined to pure iterative repetition; nothing he builds, learns, or repairs survives the loop except in his memory. My request is for a critical reading that takes this framing seriously without reducing the film to an illustration. Specifically: does Phil's eventual condition represent a genuine absurdist affirmation—an embrace of repetition without escape—or does it covertly depend on the progressive self-cultivation narrative that the loop should forbid? If Wolf's account requires that fitting fulfillment yield some enduring objective change, then the loop appears to make meaning impossible for Phil; yet the film clearly presents him as having achieved something like a meaningful existence. Should we conclude that the view needs revision, or that iterative excellence itself can constitute objective value? Which scenes—his piano study, the ice sculpture, the repeated failures to save the homeless man—best illuminate or resist this distinction? And where the film resists the philosophical reading—where it insists that love, skill, or moral attention matter even when they leave no cumulative mark—what does that contribute to the debate about meaning that purely conceptual argument about objective value and subjective attraction does not capture?
