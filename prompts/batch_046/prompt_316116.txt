I'm preparing to lead a ninety-minute graduate seminar on persistence through time—specifically endurantism versus perdurantism—for students who have just finished the standard introductory readings but keep conflating the ontological dispute with ordinary questions about personal identity across change. I want the session structured around a single carrying example rather than the usual Ship of Theseus, and I want the example to be conceptually demanding enough that intuition alone can't settle it. I'm leaning toward a case involving a decentralized, century-old scientific observation network that has replaced every telescope, every researcher, and every funding body, yet retains a continuous data archive and a legal charter that explicitly defines whether its institutional identity is carried by an enduring substance or by the informational and causal continuity of successive annual "stages." Before I commit to this framing, I need you to plan the explanatory arc rather than deliver the explanation itself: specify the metaphysical commitment that must be in place before the endurantist/perdurantist distinction even becomes visible to them (and why treating it as an identity-condition problem is the wrong entry point), what the carrying example must include and exclude so that it forces the ontological question rather than collapsing into sociology or corporate law, and the precise sequence in which to introduce temporal parts, the problem of temporary intrinsics, and the relationist versus absolutist construal of temporal location—indicating exactly where in that sequence each predictable misconception should be intercepted, such as the tendency to read perdurantism as "people are like movies" or to assume endurantism requires an immaterial soul. Also propose one precise check-question I can pose at the end that reveals whether they grasp the structural difference rather than merely repeating the slogans, and state explicitly what I should deliberately leave unmentioned at this level—perhaps stage theory or the B-theory of time—together with why those omissions protect the core distinction from being obscured.
