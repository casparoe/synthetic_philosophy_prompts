Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, two refrains permitted only minor grammatical variation—on evidential cooperation in a cosmologically large world in which agents are not identical copies but samples drawn from a shared prior over rational decision-types. Give the first refrain, appearing in line 1 and recurring, to the evidential voice: “What I do here shifts the weight of all like minds.” Give the second refrain, also in line 1’s rhyme position but alternating, to the causal voice: “My will extends only as far as my own hands.” Use the six tercets to move the speaker from isolated agency (no visible correlation), through the recognition that reasoning about one’s own type updates beliefs about the parameter space governing others, to the paradox that cooperation requires treating a causally isolated choice as statistical evidence about distant, disconnected agents. In the closing quatrain, place both refrains side by side in lines 18 and 19 so that they meet without reconciliation; let the rhyme and repetition make the reader feel the two positions are structurally interlocked rather than merely opposed. The poem should be precise enough that someone familiar with the large-world literature in decision theory—particularly the shift from direct causal linkage to Bayesian updating over agent-types—recognizes the technical move, yet it should work as poetry for a reader who has never encountered the problem. Do not ask for citations, named texts, or bibliographic references; all philosophical work must be done by the form, the refrains, and the imagery.
