Here's an argument my study group cooked up that seems to show Berkeley's master argument collapses into solipsism unless he's being inconsistent, and I can't find the exit. (1) Berkeley's master argument: if I try to conceive of a tree existant outside all minds, I necessarily conceive it as being conceived by me, so the notion is contradictory. (2) He allows this because conceiving an idea is always a kind of mental imaging or direct presence. (3) But Berkeley also says we have "notions" of spirits and of God that aren't ideas and don't involve imaging — they're understood relationally or by reflection on thier own activity. (4) Now apply the master argument's form consistently: try to conceive of Hylas's mind exising right now without me conceiving it. In doing so I form a notion of Hylas, not an idea. (5) But that notion is still present in my mind, so Hylas is still present-to-me in the act of conception. (6) So the master argument applies to notions too, unless Berkeley can show that notions don't involve presence-in-the-mind in the same way. (7) If he can't show that, then other minds and God only exist when I think of them — solipsism. (8) If he can show it, then materialists can claim "matter" is also grasped by a notion (say, an abstract relational concept) rather than an idea, and the master argument never touched matter either. Where does this break? I'd rather save the master argument than keep other minds fully independent, but I need to know the price I'm paying. Please wrap up with \answer{step_number} naming the step I'd have to drop, plus a phrase on what it costs.
