I am studying evolutionary game theory and keep encountering a particular argumentative pattern in discussions of the prisoner's dilemma that I cannot place in the literature. Rather than a bare label, I want to understand what exactly it is, where its edges lie, and whether it differs from familiar moves like introducing an auxiliary hypothesis.

The pattern runs as follows. One begins with a strict one-shot prisoner's dilemma and observes that defection is dominant, so cooperation stands in need of explanation. The proposed explanations — reputation tracking, kin recognition, spatial clustering, cultural transmission — all presuppose repeated encounters or population structure. When a critic notes that these mechanisms belong to the iterated game rather than the one-shot case, the theorist replies that real-world interactions are never genuinely isolated: every encounter is embedded in a social network, carries historical memory, or occurs with some non-zero probability of future contact. An auxiliary assumption is then added — let us call it 'effective iteration' — which allows the formal one-shot framework to remain intact while quietly importing the explanatory resources of repetition. Once this is permitted, almost any failure of cooperation can be accommodated by adjusting the assumed visibility, density, or durability of the network, yet each adjustment is presented not as an ad hoc rescue but as a realistic behavioural refinement. I have seen this with reputation systems, lattice-based spatial models, and accounts of indirect reciprocity, and in each instance the original predictive sharpness of the one-shot model appears to dissolve without anyone formally abandoning it.

Is there an established term for this specific explanatory drift — the protective absorption of an iterated structure into a model originally defined by its absence? How does it differ from an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis, or from the straightforward empirical claim that pure one-shot interactions rarely occur in nature? If the literature offers no precise concept, I would welcome a coinage together with boundary conditions: what distinguishes legitimate contextualisation from the point at which the model has, in effect, changed its subject?
