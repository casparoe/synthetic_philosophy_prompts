Illusionism, especially Keith Frankish's version, says phenomenal consciousness is basically a user-illusion—like desktop icons that don't map onto real internal machinery but are handy fictions for navigating the system. The brain builds a self-model that represents itself as having rich qualitative feels—pain, redness, sweetness—but those representations don't correspond to any actual intrinsic properties; they're just misrepresentations of functional or informational states. So when you report feeling pain, you're not accessing some raw phenomenal hurt, you're just accessing the brain's own false report about itself. This isn't eliminativism, since illusionists don't deny mental states exist; they just deny any of them have phenominal character. That dissolves the hard problem: there's no need to explain how physics produces experience, because phenomenal experience was never there, only the illusion of it.

If there's no inner viewpoint whatsoever, then the illusionist can't even be aware of their own argument, right?
