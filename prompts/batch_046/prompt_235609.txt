Drawing on G.E.M. Anscombe's well-known moral objection to Harry Truman's decision to deploy atomic weapons against Hiroshima and Nagasaki — particularly her insistence that deliberately killing the innocent is never permissible and her careful distinction between what is intended and what is merely foreseen — predict how she might have assessed the use of fully autonomous lethal drones that identify and engage targets without a human being in the deliberative loop, a development that rose to prominence only after her lifetime. In framing your answer, consider whether her concept of the directedness of human action can be coherently applied to what might be termed distributed intentionality across an algorithmic kill-chain, in which no single person clearly intends each particular death. Please indicate how confident you are in this reconstruction and single out the point about which you are least sure.
