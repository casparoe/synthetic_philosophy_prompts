I'm putting together a little philosophy study guide for my blog — we're looking at Wittgenstein's language games and family resemblence, but I'm tired of the standard "essentialism bad, family resemblance good" hand-out. I want a decision tree that actually sorts people by what they believe, not by which label they like. Start with this concrete split: if I show you the concept "game" — board games, card games, Olympic games, language games — and ask why they all count as games, do you think there's a hidden essential feature we just haven't found yet, or is it genuinely just criss-crossing similarities?

From there branch on three more questions someone could answer without knowing the philosopher's name: can language games be translated across cultures without loss, or are they incommensurable in practice? Can a solitary system — say, an LLM trained only on text — participate in a language game, or does it require a living community? And is there a threshold problem — how much overlap is actually enough to belong to the family?

The leaves should cover: strict essentialism, disjunctive essentialism, pure family resemblance, radical incommensurability, and this half-baked idea I'm calling "statistical resemblance" — where an AI's vector clustering mimics family resemblance without any understanding. Each leaf needs the position name, its most awkward commitment, and a flag if the tree can't really separate it from its neighbour. Also flag any answer-combination that lands on an empty leaf — is it incoherent, ugly, or just unexplored?

Present this as a JSON object with these fields: node_id, branch_question, answer_left, answer_right, leaf_name, awkward_commitment, separation_flag, empty_leaf_note. Keep it in British English please — I'm writing this on the bus and my thumbs are tired, so a typo or two is totally fine.
