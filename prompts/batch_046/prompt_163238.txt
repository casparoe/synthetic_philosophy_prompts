I'm trying to work out what actually makes something a "hinge" belief for me, in the Wittgensteinian sense — like the scaffolding that holds up doubt rather than being inside it. Here are my gut calls. That I have two hands right now: total hinge, I can't seriously doubt it without everything else falling apart. That the earth is way older than I am: same deal, it's the background not the picture. That other people have minds: I treat it as hinge in everyday life, but I can run the zombie argument in philosophy class and nothing breaks, so maybe it's not a true hinge, just a very strong belief. That 2+2=4: hinge, because doubting it feels like changing the rules of the game, not making a mistake within it. That my name is Jordan and I was fired from my last job: definatly not hinges, I could be wrong about both and just adjust. Here's the messy one: in my lab we treat the baseline calibration of our main instrument as unquestionable during a run. If data looks weird we blame the sample or the protocol, never the calibration — until suddenly we do, and then we rebuild half our conclusions. Is that calibration belief a hinge? It acts like one for months, but it's clearly empirical and revisable. Another messy one: I think "water is H2O" is just chemistry now, but for an 18th-century chemist it was an empirical discovery. So was it always a hinge, or did it become one? What's the principle that sorts all this — functional role, psychological impossibility, social framework, or something else? Which of my verdicts do I have to drop to keep it clean? And what does your best principle say about whether "my memories of this morning are mostly accurate" counts as a hinge for me right now?
