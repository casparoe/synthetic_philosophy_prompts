Whenever a speaker violates one of these maxims in an obvious way, the listener calculates an unstated meaning, which is the conversational implicature. From this it follows that speaker meaning is really just the literal sentence meaning plus whatever hidden messages you can derive from these calculated violations. Grice also held that expressions like 'some' generate implicatures through pure logical entailment—if I say 'some of my students passed,' the listener simply applies logic to see that not all did—so there is no special conversational machinery involved. Finally, Grice believed that in fictional dialogue there is no actual speaker present, which means there can be no genuine implicature at all; readers can only recover literal content and explicit authorial commentary, not the kind of implied speaker-meaning that works in real life."
