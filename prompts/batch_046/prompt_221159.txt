Set out the most important implications of the paradox of phenomenal judgment for epiphenomenalism, adopting as a working framework a "structural mirror" hypothesis: that phenomenal judgments are produced entirely by physical cognitive mechanisms which replicate the formal organization of conscious experience, but without any causal interaction with the experiences themselves. In particular, examine whether this hypothesis entails that first-person introspective reports are systematically unreliable, whether structural isomorphism can sustain a non-causal form of reliability, and what these conclusions imply for the methodological foundations of consciousness research that relies on subjective ratings. Develop the argument conceptually; do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific scholars by name.
