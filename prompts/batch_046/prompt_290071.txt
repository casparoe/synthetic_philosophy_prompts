Consider the Swiss federal practice of the optional referendum combined with the popular initiative and the indirect counter-proposal: citizens draft an initiative, the Federal Assembly may respond with a parliamentary counter-proposal, and voters then face a tripartite choice among the status quo, the initiative, and the counter-proposal, with tie-breaking rules that can elevate parliamentary deliberation over raw majoritarian preference. This institutional architecture treats popular sovereignty and representative refinement as co-authoritative rather than hierarchically ordered. Reconstruct the implicit philosophical commitments of this design regarding (i) democratic legitimacy—whether it derives from unmediated popular will, from deliberative transformation, or from a non-reducible hybrid; (ii) the epistemic standing of citizens, specifically whether the mechanism presupposes that voters can rationally adjudicate between competing normative frameworks presented in highly compressed statutory language; and (iii) political autonomy, particularly whether autonomy is conceived as collective self-legislation, as the protection of minority or structural rights against majoritarian outcomes, or as a dialogical relation between people and parliament. Identify which rival democratic theories—such as pure majoritarianism, deliberative democracy, or agonistic pluralism—are quietly ruled out by this practice, and assess whether its implicit commitments are internally consistent. Then propose a counterfactual institutional modification: replace the indirect counter-proposal with a mandatory citizens' assembly that drafts all initiative texts before they reach the ballot, eliminating the parliamentary alternative. Explain which specific philosophical commitment of the original mechanism must be abandoned to justify this change, identify the structural feature of the tripartite choice that does the normative work in the original design, and describe how a defender of unmediated direct democracy would attack the original mechanism as incoherent.
