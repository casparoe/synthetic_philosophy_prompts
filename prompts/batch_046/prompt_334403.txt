Okay so I got roped into helping organize this tiny neighborhood co-op and it's been a total mess lately. We just implemented this bizzare budgeting rule where a small committee meets beforehand and writes down predictions of how each member will vote, then they lock funding into seperate accounts based on those guesses. If your actual vote doesn't match the prediction, the money doesn't switch over to the other option—it just sits there dead and nobody can use it. Last week we had a vote about whether to fund a shared tool library, and I knew they had probably predicted I'd support it since I always do, but I'm genuinely strapped for cash right now and really wanted to vote no.

Then my roommate said I should just update my beliefs about what I want and vote no, since the prediction is already made and I can't change the past. But another friend argued that since the committee is basically modeling my "type," voting no doesn't just change my preference—it reveals I'm the kind of person who was always going to vote no, which means the prediction might have actually been about that type all along. I'm definatly not asking whether I should fund the library. I'm wondering if there's a principled way to think about decisions where your choice is logically tied to some prior prediction about you, especially when you can't just "update" away the correlation. It feels like there's a hidden philosophical structure here but I can't name it, and I'm curious whether my roommate or my other friend is closer to being right.
