Write a formal dialogue, composed in American English, between two philosophers of equal competence. One should defend Rousseau's critique, articulated in his Letter to d'Alembert, that theatrical performance corrupts civic virtue by replacing direct moral and political engagement with vicarious emotional spectacle; the other should defend an Aristotelian-inspired position that dramatic mimesis trains practical wisdom by presenting structured moral crises that require active audience judgment. The exchange should exhibit genuine back-and-forth: the Rousseauian interlocutor must specifically address the Aristotelian claim that spectators exercise phronesis rather than passively consume sentiment, and the Aristotelian must specifically answer the charge that theatrical pleasure systematically privileges appearance over political reality. Neither voice should be reduced to a strawman; each should concede partial points where appropriate and press the opponent's reasoning with precision. Avoid bibliographic requests, citation lists, or references to obscure secondary literature that a well-trained generalist model could not reasonably be expected to recall.
