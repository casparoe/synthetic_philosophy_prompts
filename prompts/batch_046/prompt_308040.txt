I've been working through this Boltzmann brain argument with a friend and now I'm convinced it proves too much, but I can't spot the exact leak. (1) In standard cosmology, the universe eventually reaches a de Sitter state where thermal fluctuations spawn conscious observers — Boltzmann brains — at rates that dwarf evolutionary production. (2) So if our cosmological model is correct, it's far more likely that I'm a randomly assembled brain with false memories than an evolved observer with accurate ones. (3) But if I am a Boltzmann brain, the reasoning in (1) and (2) is just thermal noise, so I have no reason to accept those premises in the first place. (4) That makes the cosmological model self-undermining: accepting it destroys the justification for accepting it. (5) The wierd part is that you can run an isomorphic argument on evolutionary biology. If we eventually build brain-emulating simulations that outnumber biological humans, then by parallel reasoning we should think we're probably simulations whose evolutionary reasoning is unreliable — which undermines evolutionary theory itself. (6) So any long-run theory that predicts a future where "fake" observers dominate seems to collapse into skepticism. (7) But rejecting cosmology, evolutionary biology, and multiverse hypotheses all together just to avoid this sounds like overkill. Which premise should I ditch? I really want to hang onto (1) since the de Sitter picture seems well-supported, and I'm okay with weakening (5) if that's the weak link, but I need to know what I'm paying — does giving up the parallel with evolutionary theory save the argument, or do I have to bite the bullet on (3) and admit that random fluctuations could accidentally generate sound reasoning?
