Write an exam question for an upper-level philosophy of science seminar, together with a model answer and a marking scheme, that asks students to analyze whether Kuhnian incommensurability blocks rational comparison between classical thermodynamics and non-equilibrium statistical mechanics when the two frameworks evaluate explanatory success through incompatible metrics—specifically, whether macroscopic entropy increase demands a probabilistic interpretation of state-space measures. The question should require students to distinguish conceptual incommensurability from empirical underdetermination and to say whether partial translation is possible without a shared evaluation metric. Present exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the exam question itself, framed for a 45-minute written response; (2) the model answer's central thesis on the locality of incommensurability; (3) the model answer's key argumentative move separating metric disagreement from ontological conflict; (4) the marking scheme specifying three must-hit points with their weights and the total marks; (5) the marking scheme identifying the two most common student mistakes—treating incommensurability as global relativism, and confusing it with the theory-ladenness of observation.
