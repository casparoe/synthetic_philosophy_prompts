Consider the implied track record of Nozick’s entitlement theory: voluntary exchange that produces sharp inequality, as in the Wilt Chamberlain case, is fully just; patterned distributive principles requiring ongoing correction are rejected because they violate rights; inheritance and gift count as legitimate transfers; taxation for welfare is condemned as rights-violating, structurally akin to forced labor; and rectification is permitted only for specific historical injustices, not for general pattern-correction. Now introduce a novel institutional mechanism: every voluntary market transaction automatically embeds an imperceptible algorithmic micro-levy—say, one ten-thousandth of the value—that is pooled and redistributed according to need, with no identifiable tax authority and no way for an individual agent to avoid it except by exiting the market entirely. Over generations this produces a highly egalitarian distribution without any visible, discrete act of coercion against particular persons. Would Nozick treat this as a just sequence of voluntary micro-transfers, or as a disguised patterned principle implemented through architecture rather than statute? Identify the closest analog among his earlier rulings, state the best-guess verdict with reasons, and say what principle one would be adopting either way. If his theory genuinely underdetermines the case, specify the further scenario—perhaps an opt-in per-transaction version of the same levy—that would best reveal whether his core objection targets the distributive pattern, the absence of genuine voluntariness, or the scale of interference itself.
