I'm drafting a feature for my magazine on the alignment problem—specifically that split between intent alignment and value alignment people call the narrow definintion. I was arguing with my editor over lunch about whether the AI doing what we meant is really a technical problem or just a philosophical shell game. In my notes I claim intent alignment is structurally closer to theatrical stage direction than to ethics: a director doesn't need the actor to value the tragedy, just to hit the emotional marks that make the audience feel it. If the actor improvises and accidentally steals the scene, that's not a values failure—it's an intent misfire. So I push this analouge pretty hard: the narrow framing treats alignment as a coordination problem between designer intent and system behavior, which makes it look apolitical, but it hides that intent is always layered and contested. Then I wonder if value alignment isn't the opposite pole but actually the same problem viewed from the audience side—when we care about outcomes rather than fidelity to the script.

My essay outline has three parts. First, I sketch how the narrow definition got popular in engineering circles around the 2010s without getting bogged down in specific papers. Second, I develop the stage-direction thing and ask where it breaks: if the AI starts optimizing for audience reaction—approval, engagement, whatever—rather than script fidelity, is that value drift or just a deeper intent interperetation? Third, I throw in a tangent comparing this to contract law's idea of a meeting of the minds—like, are we trying to enforce a meeting of the minds with something that doesn't have a mind? I think it illuminates the problem but my friend says it's a distraction.

Could you comment on these notes? I'm specifically worried I'm misrepresenting the value-alignment side—maybe I'm implying they just want the AI to care like a human, which is a strawman. Is the contract-law bit actually useful? And does this feel genuinely in the weeds, or am I just reheating the usual safety-blog debates? I want something that doesn't assume you've read every Bostrom essay but still isn't something you'd see in a generic explainer.
