Write a two-page research proposal for a six-month postdoctoral fellowship in philosophy of science. The project centers on inference to the best explanation, specifically what I will call explanatory overfitting: the dynamic process by which an explanation improves its comparative fit to observed anomalies not by generating novel predictions but by expanding its auxiliary commitments. The core question is whether we can adapt the parameter-penalty intuition familiar from statistical model selection to qualitative explanatory structures, thereby deriving a principled distinction between legitimate explanatory refinement and ad hoc rescue. Structure the proposal as follows. First, state the precise research question and explain why standard philosophical accounts of IBE—which treat virtues such as simplicity, coherence, and scope as static properties of theories—leave this dynamic revision problem unsettled. Second, outline the proposed method, arguing specifically for a conceptual-formal hybrid approach rather than pure conceptual analysis or empirical case studies alone, and say why that hybrid is better suited to the problem. Third, describe what a useful deliverable or result would look like for philosophers of science working on explanation and scientific reasoning. Fourth, specify explicitly what would count as the project failing. Fifth, provide an honest, realistic assessment of whether this work is feasible within six months, including the main risk that could make it too large. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Avoid references to obscure or highly specialized theories that assume expert familiarity beyond standard philosophical and scientific training.
