Design a 90-minute essay question for an upper-level philosophy of mind final exam, together with a model answer of approximately 700 words and a 20-mark marking scheme. Center the question on Douglas Hofstadter's "Ant Fugue" and the ant colony Aunt Hillary, but structure it so that it cannot be answered by summarizing the dialogue. Instead, require students to apply a distinction introduced within the prompt itself: "functional integration," in which distributed subsystems exchange signals that collectively regulate behavior without any unified representational content, versus "representational convergence," in which distinct subsystems contribute accuracy-conditions and content to a single, unified representational state that can be correct or incorrect. Ask students to determine whether Aunt Hillary's coordinated tracking of an environmental feature—say, the location of a predator or food source—amounts to representational convergence sufficient for attributing a belief to the colony, or only to functional integration, and whether the absence of phenomenal consciousness in both individual ants and the colony undermines a functionalist attribution of belief or can be accommodated by an emergentist reply. The answer should run about 900–1100 words. Provide the model answer and a marking scheme that identifies five specific analytical moves required for a top mark, assigns marks to each, and for each notes the characteristic mistake that should reduce the score—particularly collapsing the integration/convergence distinction or treating the phenomenological absence as automatically decisive without addressing the emergentist position. In both the model answer and the scheme, clearly distinguish settled matters (e.g., individual ants are not conscious subjects; the colony behaves in complex, adaptive ways) from genuinely contested philosophical claims (e.g., whether collective representational convergence is conceptually coherent, whether functional criteria alone can ground belief attribution in non-neural systems, and whether emergent consciousness is metaphysically possible), and do not present any contested position as the consensus view. Do not request bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or references to specific page numbers.
