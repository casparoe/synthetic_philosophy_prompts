Give me your credence, on a scale from 0 to 1, that you personally could have originated from a different sperm-egg pair—specifically the near-miss case in which your parents produce a child from different sibling gametes (genetically near-identical but not the actual ones) who develops with qualitatively identical psychological traits and life history through adulthood. Decompose your answer into four weighted terms: (1) the probability that origin essentialism applies to human personal identity at all; (2) the probability that, if it applies, it fixes identity at the exact gamete-pair level rather than at the zygote or early-embryo stage, allowing for minor spatiotemporal shifts in fertilization; (3) the probability that psychological-continuity or narrative-identity theories fail to override origin-fixity in this scenario; and (4) the probability that near-genetic identity between the actual and counterfactual gametes undermines the essentiality claim. Show how they combine into your final number, state which term is the weakest link, and specify what finding—empirical (e.g., about monozygotic twinning or chimerism) or conceptual (e.g., about kind membership)—would move it most.
