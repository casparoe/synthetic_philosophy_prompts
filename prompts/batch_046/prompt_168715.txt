A colleague summarized the contemporary metaontological deflationist position in a reading-group discussion as follows: disagreements about composite objects, abstract numbers, or other ontological categories are typically verbal rather than substantive, since the disputants accept all the same non-ontological facts and differ only in which descriptive framework they adopt; the sole exception involves what she calls cross-framework causal indispensability, where an entity must be ineliminable from causal explanations within every viable framework for the dispute to be genuine. She also claims that Carnap's internal/external distinction already secures this division, and that Quine's criterion of ontological commitment wrongly treats quantifier variance as an immediate map onto reality, thereby turning a pragmatic linguistic choice into a metaphysical thesis. You suspect the summary may conflate framework-relativity with a stronger anti-realist claim and may misrepresent Quine's attitude toward linguistic frameworks. Is this characterization fair? Please separate what accurately captures the verbal-dispute diagnosis, identify where it subtly overstates the deflationist's rejection of ontology, and evaluate whether describing Quine's view as ignoring framework-relativity is a distortion or a legitimate criticism.
