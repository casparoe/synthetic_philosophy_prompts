The situation: a neighborhood council approved a land-use deal that quietly requires displacing 12 households (informal settlement, no legal tenure) to build a community health clinic funded by the developer. The council knew about the displacement, deliberately used vague language in the public consultation ('redevelopment zone') to avoid protest, and now says it was dirty hands — they had to get teh clinic built and the displacement was the only way. They want the panel to endorse the deal as a justified moral compromise and move on.

Our panel's mandate is weird: we're not a court, we can approve with conditions, reccomend censure, or require a 'relational repair' protocol (this is part of the pilot's framework — it treats harm as a rupture in communal bonds rather than just individual wrongdoing, so repair is collective and public, not punishment). Half the panel thinks dirty hands is real and this was it. The other half thinks the deception kills the justification, and if we endorse it we're just rubber-stamping utilitarianism with extra steps.

I need the memo to recommend one concrete course — approve with conditions, censure, or mandate relational repair — with reasons the council can actually respond to. It should take seriously the strongest argument on the losing side (probably that dirty hands demands transparency after the fact, or that relational frameworks don't excuse deception). Don't ask for bibliographies or cite specific texts; just reason it through.

Also, the pilot's framework borrows from ubuntu-style relational personhood without being academic about it: the idea is that personhood is constituted through relationships, so harming those relationships requires collective acknowledgment, not just private guilt. I want the memo to engage that idea genuinely — not just mention it — and say whether it actually changes the dirty-hands calculus or just rebrands it.

Please write it as a formal memo with a bottom line up top, then reasoning, then the dissenting consideration treated honestly. Keep it to about two pages worth of text. And please, no 'as Walzer said' type citations — I can't check books right now and neither can anyone else on this panel.
