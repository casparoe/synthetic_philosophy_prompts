Draft a short research proposal—approximately two pages, suitable for a philosophy-of-rationality workshop—for the following project. The guiding question: existing analyses of the Platonia dilemma and Hofstadter’s Luring Lottery treat superrational cooperation as a symmetric, one-shot interaction between agents with transparent mutual access to each other’s reasoning. I want to investigate what happens when that transparency is replaced by an institutional mediator that reveals only noisy aggregate compliance rates, not individual choices. More precisely, does evidential decision theory still prescribe cooperation when an agent cannot distinguish a genuinely superrational counterpart from a simulated or randomly compliant one, and does causal decision theory’s recommendation shift not merely because it discounts correlations, but because institutional opacity functions as a causal screen? Frame the proposal with: a sharply stated research question that has a determinate answer; an explanation of why standard treatments of Platonia and the Luring Lottery leave this specific gap open; a methodological plan—perhaps a formal model of partial observability comparing equilibrium predictions under evidential and causal frameworks, supplemented by conceptual analysis of whether reasoning transparency is a hidden premise in the original puzzles; a clear account of what would count as a successful result versus a failed project; and an honest assessment of scope, assuming eight weeks of part-time work with no empirical data collection. Avoid requesting bibliographies or citation lists; refer to positions and arguments by name only, leaving references to be added later. Keep the tone formal and suitable for peer reviewers in decision theory and philosophy of action.
