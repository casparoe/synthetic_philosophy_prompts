Hey, I'm a retired entomologist who started reading philosophy after I retired, and I've been trying to sort out my gut feelings about using chemicals and other controls against insects. I want to know if there's a coherent principle behind my verdicts, or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Here are my cases:
1. A farmer sprays a broad-spectrum pyrethroid to stop a locust swarm that'd destroy a wheat harvest and cause real food shortages. That feels okay, even though it kills lots of non-target beatles and other bugs.
2. The same farmer sprays the same chemical just to make lettuce leaves look perfect for supermarket display. That feels wrong, even though the total insect death is probably smaller.
3. Introducing a specific parasatoid wasp to kill an invasive emerald ash borer seems okay to me—even if the borers suffer—because it saves native ash trees and is very targeted.
4. But spraying those same ash trees with a systemic neonicitinoid that also wipes out local bees and butterflies feels wrong, even if it saves the trees.
5. In a malaria-endemic region, using DDT inside houses to kill mosquitoes seems okay, despite the chemical persisting in the environment.
6. Using the same DDT to clear mosquitoes from a luxury resort with no disease risk, just so guests don't get bitten, feels morally off.
7. If we discover that some moth—say, a particular sphinx moth—has surprisingly complex nociception, then killing it for cosmetic reasons gets worse in my view. But killing it to save a human life doesn't change much.

My hunch is that the principle involves proportionality between the harm done to insects and the kind of human or ecological good achieved, with some extra weight on whether the method is targeted versus broad-spectrum, and maybe something about direct suffering versus cascade effects. But I'm not sure if that's one principle or three smuggled together. Can you state a precise principle (or a couple of candidates) that captures all of these, tell me which further cases it predicts, and be honest about which of my verdicts I'd have to drop if I want real consistency? No need for citations or a reading list—just the logic.
