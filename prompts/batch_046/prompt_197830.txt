I’m a retired physicist who only recently got into philosophy, and I’ve been stuck on Hofstadter’s idea that the self is basically a strange loop — a symbol 'I' that point's back to itself inside the brain’s symbol network. Recently I started thinking that defenders of this view might be mixing up two different kinds of loops: one that’s syntactic, like Gödel numbering where a formula references its own code, and one that’s semantic, where the symbol actually carries a first-person point of view. The debate feels stalled to me — half the people say it explains consciousness, the other half say it’s just a metafor that pushes the mystery around. My hunch is that they’re not disagreeing about brain science at all, but about whether a purely syntactic self-reference can ever turn into subjectivity — like, is the gap between code and experience bridgeable by more loops, or is it a category mistake to think so? Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a hidden empirical fight about neural representation hiding underneath? I want a real structural diagnosis — verbal, substantive, or maybe the question is just ill-posed — with reasons tied to what each side actually claims. And be honest: could a formal result about self-reference, a new neural model, or a sharper conceptual distinction actually end this? Or are they answering different questions under one name?
