I understand the logical induction criterion to solve logical uncertainty by supplying a computable belief sequence over logical sentences that avoids Dutch books and converges to the correct values on all provable or refutable claims, without demanding logical omniscience. From this I am inclined to conclude that, for any open but determinately true-or-false mathematical statement—say, a particular unsolved Diophantine equation—there exists a uniquely rational credence that every properly constructed inductor ought eventually to approximate. If that is right, then persistent disagreement among mathematicians about the likely resolution of such problems should be reducible to disagreement about which deductive process to embed in the inductor. But this sounds implausible: have I misinterpreted the normative force of the limiting distribution, or is the framework indeed committed to the idea that logical uncertainty can be fully dissolved into a choice of reasoning architecture?
