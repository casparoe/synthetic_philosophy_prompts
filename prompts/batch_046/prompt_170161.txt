So there's this city running two programs side-by-side that seem to pull in opposite directions. The first is a "Birthright Grant": if you were born within the municipal boundary, you recieve a lifelong education voucher, no matter where you live now or how much money you have. The second is an international medical partnership that sends funding and staff to clinics abroad, allocated entirely by disease burden with no weight given to nationality or local connection. The mayor keeps saying both express the same idea — that we owe more to "our own" but still owe something to everyone. I want to know what view of special obligations and distributive justice that pairing actually presupposes. Could a consistent two-level theory license both, or is one of them just incompatible with the other? And if we replaced whatever implicit theory is doing the work with a strict cosmopolitan view — every claim weighted equally regardless of borders — what exactly would have to change in how these programs are structured? Is the double practice philosophicaly coherent, or is it mostly a rhetorical cover for keeping local voters happy?
