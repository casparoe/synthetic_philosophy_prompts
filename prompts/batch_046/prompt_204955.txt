I've been trying to work out a kinda hybrid take on Nozick's entitlement theory and the Wilt Chamberlain argument. The usual story is that if people start with just holdings and then freely exchange, any pattern like equality gets disrupted, and that's okay—redistribution is forced labor. But I'm adding this extra bit: what if the Lockean proviso doesn't just apply to original acquisition, but recursively to every transaction that relies on institutions (contracts, property law, etc.) that were themselves built on prior unjust holdings? So even Chamberlain's voluntary deal is tainted if the legal framework enabling it traces back to theft or slavery we can't fully correct. Call it the recursive proviso view. I want to know what'd actually kill this position—not just the standard Nozickian reply that tracing history is impossible, but deeper stuff. Which intuitions about personal responsibility vs institutional inheritance does it clash with? Would it collapse into a full-blown luck egalitarianism, or can it stay libertarian? And what would a committed Nozickian concede as a real problem versus just dismiss as impractical? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about whether this view is well-insulated or just hanging by a thread. Please seperate the ones that depend on some metaphysics of collective guilt from the ones that don't, since I'm not sure I'm commited to that part.
