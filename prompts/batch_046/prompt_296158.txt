Quine's argument in "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" demonstrates that no statement is immune to revision: logical and mathematical truths sit near the center of our web of belief, but they are not analytic in any substantive sense, since the distinction between meaning-driven revision and empirical adjustment collapses once synonymy is shown to be circular and reducibility to experience fails. If that is right, then describing arithmetical truths as "analytic"—as some contemporary philosophers of mathematics do—seems to commit them either to a purely sociological observation about our present reluctance to revise arithmetic, or to an unacknowledged revival of Carnap's reductionism. How should we understand the coherence of such contemporary appeals? Do they identify a genuine gradation in centrality that Quine's holism itself permits, or do they necessarily reintroduce the sharp dichotomy he argued was illusory?
