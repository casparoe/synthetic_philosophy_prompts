I am preparing a podcast interview with an unnamed philosopher of mind who defends a symbolic-registration account of pain's badness: she holds that pain asymbolia reveals the unpleasantness of pain is not intrinsic to its phenomenal character but depends on an intact representational link between nociceptive signals and a self-directed evaluative frame, and that in asymbolia this link is severed while sensory awareness remains intact. Draft eight interview questions that go well beyond having her restate the view. Please include: at least one question on whether the preserved verbal reports of pain in asymbolic patients undermine her claim that the affective layer is genuinely missing rather than merely behaviourally suppressed, together with a follow-up keyed to her likely appeal to a dissociation between report and evaluation; one on how her account distinguishes genuine asymbolia from mere stoicism or learned suppression; one on whether making the symbolic-registration link necessary renders pain's badness contingent on conceptual or linguistic capacities, with a follow-up pressing what this implies for infants and non-human animals; one on whether her view collapses into a representationalist account of pain's badness and, if so, how it avoids the standard objection that misrepresenting damage as painful would then be equally bad; and a final question asking what empirical finding—neurological, behavioural, or phenomenological—would genuinely threaten her position rather than being reinterpreted as another form of registration failure. Write the questions in a sharp, colloquial-but-rigorous tone suitable for an informed audience, present them in British English, and do not include any bibliography, citation requests, or reading lists.
