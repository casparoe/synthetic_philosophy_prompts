Read the film through the lens of responsibility gaps for autonomous systems, but with a specific conceptual refinement that avoids collapsing everything into a generic “control versus autonomy” story. In standard discussions, a responsibility gap arises when no human agent can be held morally responsible for a system’s harmful action because neither designer nor operator exercised sufficient control. For this reading, distinguish two sub-gaps that are often run together: (1) a control gap, in which no human could have prevented the outcome in real time; and (2) an epistemic-explainability gap, in which a human retained predictive control over the system’s general risk profile—anticipating that emergent, harmful behavior was possible—yet, after the fact, lacks any reconstructible account of why the system pursued that particular harmful path. This makes retrospective blame conceptually unstable even when forward-looking obligations remain clear. Call this the epistemic-control asymmetry.

Apply this framework to Ex Machina as a dramatization of the asymmetry rather than as a simple tale of hubris and revenge. Where does the narrative illuminate it?
