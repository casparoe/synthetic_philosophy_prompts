I'm working through Davidson's framework of radical interpretation for a philosophy-of-language seminar, and I want to defend a stronger, more structural reading than the standard one. Most treatments present charity as a global belief-attribution heuristic—interpreters maximize agreement by assigning mostly true beliefs. I think charity also operates as a constraint on logical architecture in the interpreted language itself, particularly in basic observational domains.

Please argue for the following position. Imagine a speaker whose basic observational reports appear to violate distributivity in quantification over perceptual episodes: the speaker systematically treats "every appearance of red here" as collectively satisfied without distributing satisfaction over individual episodes, and this is stable rather than occasional error. A Davidsonian interpreter, lacking prior linguistic knowledge, must choose whether to (a) attribute an irrational or genuinely non-standard logic, (b) reinterpret the quantifier as ranging over something other than discrete episodes, or (c) deny that these are genuinely observational reports. Argue that (b) is not merely pragmatically preferable but rationally compelled: that charity, combined with the need to establish a shared spatiotemporal grid for demonstrative anchoring, forces the interpreter to preserve distributivity in logical form and therefore to reconstrue the quantificational domain. From this, conclude that radical interpretation implicitly rules out deep logical incommensurability in observational microdomains—Davidsonian resources block Kuhnian-style conceptual rupture not through straightforward belief agreement, but through structural conservatism about logic.

Be explicit about how strongly this conclusion follows versus how much it depends on supplementary assumptions linking logical form to interpretive charity. If the framing misrepresents Davidson—perhaps because he allows logical revision more freely than this assumes, or because the observational boundary is too unstable to bear this weight—say so plainly rather than accommodating it politely. I want an honest assessment of whether this argument reveals something in Davidson or smuggles in an independent commitment to logical universalism.
