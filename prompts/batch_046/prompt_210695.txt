I'm sitting in on a philosophy of probability seminar where two classmates—Alex and Blake—got into a sustained argument about the Sleeping Beauty problem that quickly moved past the familiar halfer-thirder exchange. They asked me to referee because I hadn't taken a side, and I honestly couldn't call it.

Alex defended the thirder position. He said that upon awakening without knowing the day, Beauty faces three centered possibilities—Heads-Monday, Tails-Monday, and Tails-Tuesday—symmetrical in every respect except the coin result, so each should carry credence one-third. He treated the awakening itself as the key self-locating evidence: being awake is true in all three worlds, but it is true in two tail-worlds and only one heads-world, so Bayes's rule pushes her credence downward.

Blake pushed back with a modified halfer view built around what he called trajectory weighting. He agreed that self-locating belief updates on being awake, but argued that the two tail-awakenings are not independent epistemic samples. Because the amnesia protocol deterministically links them to the same coin outcome and the same underlying person, they form a single epistemic trajectory with internal temporal parts, whereas the heads-case is a trajectory with only one part. The correct reference class, he said, contains one heads-trajectory and one tails-trajectory—not three observer-moments—so the update should remain at one-half. He then proposed a formal rule: weight centered propositions not by raw counting of awakenings but by the persistence coherence of the underlying trajectory.

Alex replied that Blake's trajectory is just the coin outcome relabeled with a metaphysical assumption about personal persistence across amnesia, which doesn't belong inside a Bayesian update rule. He added that if Beauty knew it was Tuesday she should update to near-certainty of tails, which proves that temporal location carries evidential weight; treating Monday and Tuesday as parts of one trajectory, he said, ignores the very self-locating information that drives the update.

Blake countered that knowing it's Tuesday changes her credence precisely because Tuesday is a distinguishable epistemic position within the tails-trajectory, not because there are three equally weighted worlds. The thirder answer, he claimed, illicitly treats temporal location as evidentially neutral, when in fact temporal location is part of the self-locating evidence. He insisted that without his persistence-coherence constraint, any counting argument could be multiplied arbitrarily by tweaking the awakening protocol.
