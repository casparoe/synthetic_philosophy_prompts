So I've been trying to work out if there's a hidden rule behind when I feel okay keeping stuff for myself and when I feel like a jerk for not giving more. Here are my gut calls on specific situations:

If I see a kid drowing in a shallow pond and I can save them without risking my life, I should do it even if my phone gets ruined — that's an obvious yes.

If I have about $30 left after bills and I spend it on coffee with friends instead of donating to a food bank, I feel a little guilty but not like I'm a bad person. But if I have $3000 extra and blow it on a fancy vacation while people are starving, that feels way worse, almost like stealing.

My cousin lost his liscence and asked me to drive him to work every day for free. I did it for two weeks then said I need gas money. I think that's fair, but if I said no from day one I'd feel selfish, yet if I kept doing it for a year and lost my own job, that'd be stupid and not actually help anyone in the long run.

There's this idea I came across that you should keep giving until you're only barely better off than the people you're helping. I think that can't be right because then I'd have to work myself into the ground, but I also think if I never feel any pinch I'm probably not doing enough.

I once skipped a cheap concert ($40) and sent the money to a charity that saves lives. I felt good about it. But I also think it's okay that I didn't skip my sister's wedding to send that $500 instead, even though that money could save several people. So distance and relationships seem to matter, but I don't want to say that people far away are less real.

A friend volunteered at a shelter every weekend for three years and then burnt out and stopped entirely. I think she was amazing but also that she should have paced herself so she could still be helping now. So does that mean there's a "sustainability" rule?

Is there one principel that covers all these? Or do I have to drop one of my gut feelings? I'm especially stuck on the cousin case and the burnout case — they seem to say "help but don't destroy yourself," but then the coffee vs vacation case says I should feel guilty for small luxuries, which doesn't fit a sustainability rule unless it's about percentages or something. And the wedding case makes me think proximity matters, but that conflicts with the drowning kid case where distance shouldn't matter. What's the actual pattern here, and which of my verdicts does it force me to abandon?
