As a novelist preparing a collection of philosophical fiction, I wish to commission a short story that interrogates divine hiddenness through an unconventional lens. Rather than depicting a conventional crisis of faith or a theophanic vision, construct a narrative set in a society where every citizen is born with an irreducible, private intuition of a single transcendental number—perhaps a variant of Euler's constant—yet no collective proof exists that binds these intuitions into a shared metaphysical system. The protagonist, an aging mathematician who has spent her career searching for the unifying axiom, should come to understand that the very incompleteness of the system mirrors the structure of divine hiddenness: not an absence demanding resolution, but a necessary condition for coherence and autonomy. Render this realization in a single, quiet scene—two figures speaking at dusk on a balcony overlooking a city whose lights seem arranged in no discernible pattern—allowing the conceptual weight to emerge through image and dialogue rather than exposition. Maintain a literary, restrained tone; do not resolve into either atheistic despair or devotional certainty. The story should ask whether hiddenness might itself be the form that presence takes when presence is not permitted to be total.
