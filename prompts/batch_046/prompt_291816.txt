And I noticed this pattern that seems backwards: in countries with really high gender-equality scores — like Norway, Sweden, Finland — career choices split more sharply by gender than in places with less formal equality. It's not just nursing vs engineering; inside medicine women cluster in pediatrics and men in surgery way more than you'd expect, and the same thing shows up in leisure preferences and risk-taking in investment portfolios.

I keep wondering if there's an actual name for this specific configuration, not just the country examples. I know people talk about nature vs nurture and there's probably some Scandinavian-paradox thing, but I'm asking about the concept: the amplification of gender divergence specifically under conditions of high formal equality and low economic pressure. How is it different from plain old cultural stereotyping, or from the idea that removing barriers lets innate preferences show? And here's where I get stuck — if this pattern holds, what does it actually mean for justice? Should egalitarian policy aim to correct these divergent outcomes, or is that paternalistc? I want to understand the carving, not just a label: what exactly counts as this phenomenon, what doesn't, and where the edges are. Like, does it apply only to career choice or also to these smaller-grain preferences? And if the divergence is partly driven by people using gender as an identity marker when other constraints disappear, does that make it cultural after all, just in a weird indirect way?
