Write an exam question for an upper-level philosophy of mathematics course, together with a model answer and a marking scheme. The question should present a precise hypothetical: ZFC is globally inconsistent, yet the shortest derivation of a contradiction requires a proof longer than 10^100 symbols—far beyond any physically realizable computation, storage, or human verification. The prompt must require students to distinguish formal global inconsistency from practical or epistemically accessible inconsistency, and to defend a clear position on whether mathematics in this scenario is “broken” in any meaningful sense. The model answer (maximum 600 words) should argue that mathematics remains epistemically sound but metaphysically unstable, using the distinction between syntactic derivability and semantic coherence. The marking scheme should be formatted as a table with five criteria (4 marks each, total 20), each naming the specific conceptual move required for full marks and the typical mistake that costs marks. Keep the entire response within 900 words. Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or specific paper citations; reference only widely known figures and frameworks such as Gödel, Hilbert, and ZFC.
