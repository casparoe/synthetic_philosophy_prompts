I've been reading about prioritarianism, which I think means benefits to the worst off should weigh heavier, but then there's the leveling-down objection saying you shouldn't just make everyone equally badly off for the sake of equality. A mate of mine who did some ethics mentioned something he calls 'marginal priority decay' — the idea that the extra moral weight of helping someone drops off the further they are already from rock bottom — and I'm not sure if that fixes the problem or makes it worse.

Can you turn this into an actual decision procedre I could run with the forms in front of me? Apply it to two cases: first, applicant A is severely disabled with a carer already, needs £450 for a ramp; applicant B is moderately disabled, lives alone, needs £350 for rails; we've got £500 total.
