The usual story: if aligning a model takes extra compute, time, and talent, then in a winner-take-all race the firm that does it gets outcompeted by the one that skips it, so voluntary safety is structurally irrational. The counter-story is that alignment is mostly just good engineering—robustness, interpretability, evals—that improves capabilities too, so there's no real "tax," just mislallocated R&D. People go back and forth citing the same dynamics, but I think they're talking past each other because they treat the tax as a uniform cost curve. The visible parts survive; the invisible parts get selected out. Under that framing, the debate isn't about wether alignment costs anything, but about which parts of it can be contracted on and which ones are pure public goods.
