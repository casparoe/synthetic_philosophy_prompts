I've been thinking about that old paradox of tragedy — why we actually enjoy sad films and books when they make us feel awful. Most explanations talk about catharsis or feeling safe in the theatre, but I've come up with my own take: I reckon sadness in art works like a kind of debug mode for our empathy. We recieve fake, controlled inputs — a character dying, not our actual friend — and it lets us check our emotional machinery is still working properly without anything real going wrong. So the pleasure comes from testing our feelings on dummy data, not from releasing pressure.
