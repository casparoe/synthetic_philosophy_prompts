I'm moderating a podcast interview with a philosopher who defends "embodied intellectualism" about knowing-how. She holds that knowing how to do something is genuinely propositional—knowing that a certain method works—but insists the relevant propositions must be grasped under an irreducibly practical "motor-mode presentation" that can be acquired only through guided repetition, not through verbal instruction alone. She uses this to explain why experts often cannot articulate their knowledge: they possess the propositional content but lack the discursive mode required to state it declaratively. Write a focused sequence of interview questions that presses where the literature presses and anticipates her standard replies. Include: a question on how her view differs from standard intellectualism without collapsing into Ryle's anti-intellectualism; a question on whether "motor-mode presentation" is independently motivated or an ad hoc rescue; a question about explicit verbal instruction that clearly yields competent performance, such as learning chess rules from a manual and then playing well; a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that such cases involve only "thin" know-how, asking what conceptual or empirical criterion distinguishes thick from thin; and a final question asking what finding in neuroscience or pedagogy would actually force her to abandon the view rather than complicate the presentation-mode apparatus. Begin with the questions themselves, with no preface or commentary.
