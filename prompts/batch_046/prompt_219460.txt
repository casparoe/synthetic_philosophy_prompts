Okay so I'm noticing this weird pattern in arguments where people invoke a "reason" that doesn't belong to anyone. Like in climate debates someone says there's a reason the temperature shouldn't go past 1.5 degrees, but they don't mean anyone in particular wants it — not a government, not future people collectively, just... the situation has a reasom built in. Or in AI safety talk I hear there's a reason for an agent to be honest even if no human designed that preference in; the reason just hangs there with no owner. Even evolutionary bio people talk about "the reason" a trait spread, though that's clearly not anyone reasoning. Is there an established name for this kind of unattached rationale? I don't just mean objective moral facts, since those usually still point at what agents should do. This feels different — like a normative or explanatory weight that pulls without belonging to anyone. I'm not going to be satisfied with just a label; I want to know whether this is one phenomenon or several, where the edges are (evolutionary vs normative vs logical), and whether there's a specific failure mode when people mistake these ownerless pulls for actual agent-based reasons. Also, do philosophers already have a map for when it's legit to talk this way versus when it's just sloppy anthropomorphisim?
