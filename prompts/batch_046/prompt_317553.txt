I am drafting a novel about a logician who discovers a flaw in her own reasoning, and the situation has led me to notice a recurring epistemic configuration that I cannot place precisely within the philosophy I have read. Here are two concrete instances of it.

First, my protagonist proves a theorem she had long doubted. Her confidence in the result is high, and she also holds—on independent grounds—that her judgment about this class of proof is generally well-calibrated. Then she receives evidence that experts with her profile systematically overestimate the validity of arguments of exactly this form. She considers lowering her confidence, yet she immediately notices a second-order difficulty: if her first-order judgment is unreliable, then her assessment that this particular downward revision is warranted is equally suspect. The revision stalls, not because she denies the bias, but because applying it undermines the very cognitive tool she needs to apply it.

Second, I imagine the mirror case. Had she begun with low confidence in the theorem and then learned of the same systematic overestimation bias, she would feel pressure to raise her confidence—yet she reports no corresponding paralysis. The asymmetry strikes me as structurally important. In both directions, the meta-belief about calibration interacts with the object-level belief, but only downward revision seems to trigger a kind of directional deadlock.

I believe this lies somewhere near what is called the dogmatism paradox, but my configuration seems to differ in at least two respects. The standard paradox, as I understand it, concerns an agent who refuses to revise downward because she trusts her own reasoning too much; my case involves an agent who wants to revise but finds that the mechanism of revision is self-undermining in one direction yet not in the other. Furthermore, the familiar formulation treats the protection of a belief as the central failure, whereas here the failure is an instability between levels of confidence that produces paralysis rather than dogmatic retention.

Does this two-directional, asymmetric pattern have an established name of its own? If not, what is the nearest concept—whether in formal epistemology, in discussions of self-locating belief, or in accounts of revision under self-doubt—and exactly how does my pattern diverge from it? I am not asking for a bare label; I want the carving. Where does the phenomenon begin and end? Does the mirror case belong to the same failure mode or to a different one? And does any coherent formal account capture both directions symmetrically, or must one direction be treated as the genuine paradox and the other as a separate, perhaps spurious, appearance?
