For a reading group I have been asked to write down my philosophical profile on rationality and self-binding, so the others can probe its consistency. Here is roughly where I have landed.

(1) In the absent-minded driver problem—where a driver who, upon reaching an exit, will have forgotten any prior resolution about whether to take it must decide in advance whether to programme the GPS for the early, high-reward exit (reward 100, but reachable only if the pre-set plan is executed without conscious recall) or the later, safe exit (reward 40, guaranteed)—the rational course is to programme the early exit. The planning-stage evidential correlation between the GPS setting and reaching the destination dominates the execution-stage causal impotence.

(2) I accept evidential decision theory for one-shot prediction problems: in Newcomb’s problem, one should one-box, because the predictor’s reliability makes the choice evidentially linked to the boxes’ contents.

(3) I reject counterfactual mugging. Once the coin has already landed in my favour, paying the mugger cannot causally influence that past outcome; and in a non-iterated interaction there is no evidential loop that could justify payment.

(4) I hold that binding pre-commitments—contracts, resolutions, or GPS settings—generate obligations that persist even when, at the moment of execution, the agent has lost all memory of the commitment and sees no present reason to comply. Rationality requires honouring them regardless.

(5) I maintain that the normative evaluation of any action must be indexed strictly to the agent’s epistemic state at the precise moment of action. A choice is rational or irrational in light of what the agent then knows and can causally influence, not in light of earlier planning states.
