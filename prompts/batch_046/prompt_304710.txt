Here are my gut calls on mind uploading via gradual replacement, and I want to know if there's a principle that holds them together or if I'm just being inconsistent.

Case 1: they replace 1% of my neurons with synthetic ones every month for ten years, and I'm awake and talking the whole time. That's still me at the end, obviously.

Case 2: same replacement, but I'm put under for 24 hours between each chunk. Still me, I think, though it feels more like waking up after surgery.

Case 3: they swap all 100% in one second while I'm awake. That feels like death and a copy being made, not me surviving.

Case 4: they replace half, then run the old half and the new half in parallel for a month with shared sensory input, then shut down the old. I think I'm the merged one? Or maybe I'm both? Actually I go back an forth, but I lean that I'm neither, which is weird since case 1 should be fine.

Case 5: they replace everything but preserve the exact chemical bath and glial scaffholding around the synthetic neurons. I feel like that might save me even in case 3, but I can't say why glia should matter if it's all about computation.

Case 6: they scan my brain, build a digital copy, and leave the original untouched. The copy thinks it's me. I think it's wrong, but then why is case 1 okay if it's just slow copying?

Is there one account that saves all these, or do I need to drop one? And what does it say about a case I haven't mentioned: replacing neurons only during REM sleep, so I never experience the transition?
