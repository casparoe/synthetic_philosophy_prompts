I'm a phd student in philosphy of mind and cognitive science, and i've been going in circles with my labmates about whether we should take the intentional stance toward LLMs. The analogy everyone reaches for is Dennett's thermostat: attributing beliefs is just a predictive strategy, not a claim about inner life, so if treating a model as having beliefs about politics or math helps us predict its outputs, then we're fully licensed, end of story, no need to ask if there's 'real' understanding inside. They lean on it to dismiss the whole metaphysical debate as confused category error.

But I think the analogy is smuggling in something that breaks. A thermostat's 'belief' about temperature is stable across contexts — change the room, talk to it differently, it still tracks the same state. LLMs show what I'd call frame-locked intentionality: ask about democracy in a legal framing and you get one commitment, ask in a revolutionary framing and you get contradictory ones, with no internal mechanism resolving the tension. So the thermostat quietly imports single-track rationality and cross-contextual stability that LLMs just don't have.

Can you lay out the correspondence table — what plays the thermostat, the predictive project, the missing inner life — then the disanalogies that actually matter? Specifically, does the breakdown of stability kill the license to apply the stance, or can we salvage it by saying we apply different stances in different frames? And if the analogy was carrying the work of making the metaphysical worry look silly, does that survive once we see LLMs are closer to a committee of context-sensitive sub-agents, or maybe a split-brain patient, than to one thermostat? I want a committed answer, not a menu: either the intentional stance applies cleanly and my framing worry is noise, or the analogy collapses and we need that replacement model — and tell me which features of the replacement actually carry weight.
