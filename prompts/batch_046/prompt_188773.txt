I've got this half-baked idea for a grad seminar paper, probably around 15 pages, and I want to check if it's actually tractable. The question is about metaphysical grounding and fundamentality, but in normative domains. Normally we say grounding is irreflexive and asymetric — no loops, no circular explanation. But think about explaining why cruelty is wrong: you might ground it in harm, then ground harm in human preferances, yet those preferances themselves seem shaped by prior norms against cruelty. So you get a loop where normative fact A grounds descriptive fact B, and B partly grounds A. Most philosophers just call that vicious circularity and move on. I want to propose there's a specific kind of "coherentist grounding loop" that isn't vicious, and that it might be the right model for how ethical and metaphysical explanations hang together. For the proposal: state the exact question (can a consistent model of grounding allow these loops while still keeping fundamentality meaningful?), explain why the standard irreflexivity answer doesn't actually settle the normative case, sketch the approach (probably borrowing coherence ideas from epistemology but applied to grounding relations), say what a successful result looks like versus failure, and give an honest read on whether this is too ambitious for one semester.
