(c) The clay-process has strict material-continuity conditions: if every atom is replaced over time, the original clay-process ends and a numerically distinct clay-process begins.
(d) The statue-process has formal-continuity conditions: it can survive complete replacement of its material substrate, provided the sculptural form is continuously maintained through artistic practice.
(e) At any given moment, the statue-process and the clay-process share exactly the same spatiotemporal parts; there is no region occupied by one but not the other.

(g) If the statue is melted down—thereby breaking formal continuity—and later reconstructed in the identical shape by the same artist, the result is not numerically identical to the original; a broken trajectory cannot be repaired by mere replication.
(h) The conditions that govern formal continuity depend partly on the artist's intentions and on socially embedded practices of artistic recognition, not solely on physical dynamics.
