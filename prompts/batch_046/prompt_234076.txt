Construct a thought experiment designed to isolate whether the presence of explicit definitional conditions combined with practical decision-procedures alters the dialectical pressure on theories of vagueness, using a domain other than standard physical properties. Specifically, design a scenario involving a single institutional office—defined in a founding charter by precise conditions of eligibility and authority—whose actual functioning depends on a cluster of conventional practices that are replaced incrementally across generations, one practice at a time, with full continuity of social recognition and administrative decision-making. Engineer the case so that: (1) an epistemicist about vagueness is committed to the existence of an exact, unknown boundary-generation at which the office ceases to exist, despite the absence of any observable discontinuity in authority or succession; (2) a supervaluationist must treat intermediate generations as lacking any determinate fact about whether the office persists, even though every practical question—salary, command, legal validity—is answered without ambiguity; and (3) a contextualist can explain the appearance of determinacy by appealing to shifts in the standards of precision triggered by practical stakes. Constraints: present the transformation in a single paragraph; avoid quantum mechanics, neuroscience, or fictional technology; make the institutional function intelligible without legal expertise; and do not invoke heaps, baldness, or color spectra. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that each theory delivers the specified verdict, identify which structural feature—explicit charter conditions, continuity of social function, or the practical necessity of decisive action—enables the contextualist resolution, explain what happens to the divergence if that feature is removed, and show how a defender of the theory that fares worst would attack the case rather than accept the verdict. State clearly what the case is for: testing whether practical determinacy undermines the motivation for sharp ontological boundaries in non-physical domains.
