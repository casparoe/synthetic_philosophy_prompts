I am constructing a novel in which several characters must make irrevocable choices—allocating scarce medical resources across generations, deciding whether to preserve an ecosystem at the cost of human displacement, or determining obligations to distant strangers versus kin—while genuinely unsure which moral framework governs their situation. Their uncertainty is not merely empirical; they are uncertain whether suffering carries lexical priority over aggregate welfare, whether obligations differ in kind rather than only in degree, and whether future persons count as moral patients at all. Before I write their deliberations with any philosophical credibility, I want the full specification of what an adequate theory of moral uncertainty must actually deliver—not a survey of named positions, but the desiderata themselves.

Give me the requirements list: the phenomena the theory must explain, including cases of deep inter-theoretic incommensurability where values seem not to share a common metric; the distinctions it must preserve, such as that between uncertainty about which principle applies and uncertainty about how to weigh competing principles, between first-order moral choice and second-order methodological choice, and between epistemic humility and practical paralysis; the formal or rationality constraints it must respect without collapsing into straightforward expected-utility maximization across incommensurable values; and the practical results it must accommodate, specifically that an agent can act with justified conviction without pretending to resolve an ambiguity that may be structurally unresolvable. With each requirement, provide the motivating case or consideration behind it.

Identify which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable for any theory worthy of the name, and which are merely prejudices of particular camps—for example, the demand that moral uncertainty always resolve into a single all-things-considered judgment, or the opposing demand that it never constrain action at all. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable, particularly where the need for action-guidance conflicts with a refusal to commensurate values across theories. Tell me which single requirement eliminates the largest share of current approaches, and briefly indicate how a theory that rejects that requirement might still remain coherent—without relying on bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. A table is acceptable for the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring named theories, though you may briefly note where standard accounts—expected-value aggregation, deference to a single favored framework, or contractualist resolution—stand against the list if it illuminates the architecture itself.
