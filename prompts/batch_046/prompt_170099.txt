Hey, I'm putting together my philosophical profile on meritocracy and desert for a discusssion group and want a consistency check before I share it. Here are my commitments: I think desert is strictly backward-looking—like, you deserve credit for the action itself, not for being the kind of person who'd do it. At the same time I think meritocratic hiring should be purely predictive, so you should hire whoever will perform best regardless of how hard they worked to get there. I also accept the Rawlsian point that natural talents are morally arbitary, and I think effort is mostly shaped by socialization so that's arbitary too. Still, it feels unfair if two people put in the same effort and one fails just because they were born with less stamina or whatever—so I think comparative desert based on equal effort makes sense. I completely reject moral-worth theories, I don't think anyone is intrinsically more worthy of goods. And I think allocating jobs by pure lotery is wrong, even if it's efficient. So do these actually hang together? Which pairs are in real tension vs just looking like it? What's the least costly revision if I have to drop something?
