I'm preparing a consistency check for a reading group on metaphysics—I'm a software engineer without formal philosophy training, so I'm trying to articulate where I've landed and see whether the pieces actually fit together. Here are my commitments:

(1) I find Berkeley's master argument convincing: it is incoherent to suppose that a material object with sensible qualities—color, shape, motion—exists without being perceived by some mind, since being is being perceived.

(2) I want to preserve scientific practice, so I treat references to unobservable entities—electromagnetic fields, quantum states—as compressed predictive structures, essentially algorithms for anticipating future ideas or sensations, not as claims about mind-independent substances.

(3) I reject the view that minds are simple, indivisible spiritual atoms. Instead, I think a mind is an emergent, distributed pattern of interacting volitions and ideas, structurally closer to a distributed software system running across nodes than to a Cartesian ego.

(4) I hold that causation is exclusively mental: only volitions, whether human or divine, can cause changes in the order of ideas. What physics calls causation is merely constant conjunction observed among ideas.

(5) I think God is necessary to explain the intersubjective regularity of nature, but I do not conceive of God as a simple substance. Rather, God is the unified, non-local field of all actual and possible volitional relations that sustains consistency across minds.

(6) I distinguish direct perceptual acquaintance (for example, seeing a red patch) from structural or inferential knowledge (for example, knowing the schema of a database I have never fully traversed). I allow that the latter can be true of relations that no single finite mind currently surveys, provided they are grounded in perceptions available within the unified volitional field.

The question: do these hang together? I am particularly unsure whether (3) undermines the kind of simple perceiver that (1) and (4) seem to require, whether (6) sneaks in unperceived relations that (1) ought to reject, and whether (2) and (4) are genuinely in tension or only appear to be because I am treating physical law as autonomous from mental agency. Please audit the package: identify each pair in genuine tension, rank them by severity, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs, flag any tensions that dissolve on closer inspection, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems without collapsing the view entirely.
