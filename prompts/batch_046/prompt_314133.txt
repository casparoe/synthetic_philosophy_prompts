right, here's where i've ended up on bayesian confirmation and old evidence — can you audit this package and tell me if it's actually coherent?

1. i'm a hardline conditonaliser: the only rational update rule is bayes' rule on propositions you actually come to hold. no belief-revision operators, no non-conditional shifts.
2. old evidence genuinely confirms new theories. mercury's perihelion wasn't just a prediction-check for GR — it was real support, even though it was already in the books.
3. i reject the move where you update on learning that H explains E rather than on E itself. either you knew the entailment already or you've learned logic, and logical learning shouldn't budge empirical credences.
4. priors come from structural stuff — symmetry, simplicity, maybe minimum description length — but never from "this retrodicts the data well". explanitory fit is post-hoc rationalisation unless it predicts something fresh.
5. diachronic coherence means you never reopen a settled update. once E is in your background, it's there; you don't run the conditionalisation again with H in mind.
6. but when i evaluate a genuinely new H, i still compute P(E|H) vs P(E|~H) against my current background, and if the likelihood ratio is big i take that as legitimate reason to raise P(H) — old E included.
7. also, i think there's an implicit filter: if H wasn't in my live hypothesis space before, my prior for it was effectively zero, so noticing it explains old data shouldn't move me unless i had already assigned it some non-negligible weight.
