Analyse India's 2016 demonetisation—when high-denomination notes comprising the bulk of circulating currency were abruptly invalidated—through social ontology, specifically the construction of monetary kinds. Examine whether the episode confirms a collective-intentionality account, in which state performatives constitute money only via sustained social acceptance, or undermines it, given how rapidly acceptance was withdrawn yet retained in parallel economies. Address three issues in turn: first, whether the state alone or users collectively held the standing to reconstitute the kind; second, whether the invalidated notes retained monetary status in black markets and rural networks, implying plural rather than unitary kinds; third, whether gendered and caste-based disparities in banking access meant the kind was differently realised across groups, fracturing universalist accounts. Take a definite position on which ontological framework best explains the dynamics, state precisely where it misdescribes the empirical case, and identify one feature of the episode that standard social-constructionist theories of money have not anticipated. Keep the mapping disciplined—specify actors, available options, and observed outcomes—and do not force the theory to speak where it offers no purchase.
