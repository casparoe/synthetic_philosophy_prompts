I sit on my school's senior curriculum board, which must vote next week on the governing framework for our required twelfth-grade civic seminar. The dispute is between a professional-class, urban-oriented model—civic life as credential-building, geographic mobility, and engagement with abstract institutions—and a working-class, rural-oriented model—civic life as place-bound repair, manual competence, and accountability to people one will not leave behind. We must pick one framework to structure the course, not merge them. Draft the board's decision memo: recommend one framework, specify one concrete course rule that follows from it, and treat seriously the strongest objection—that the rejected outlook is not merely different but epistemically or morally deficient. Frame your reasoning around whether outlooks centered on maintenance and continuity are more or less defensible than those centered on optimization and mobility. Write the memo so its opening sentence states the recommendation exactly, then proceeds to reasoning with no preamble and without restating this assignment.
