hey, i run a philosophy of religion workshop for about 12 grad students, one 75-minute session. i want them to feel the actual pressure of Al-Ghazali-style occassionalism, not just dismiss it as "god did it." please design a runnable in-class simulation.

don't use the tired fire-and-cotton example. instead, set up a "predictive market" with a black-box device: students feed it input cards (red/blue/green) and it outputs sounds/numbers. for the first 20 minutes they observe regularities and build "laws" themselves, betting points on predictions. then the hidden mechanism (you, via a hidden script or deck) breaks the pattern without warning — but in ambiguous ways that could still be deeper laws they haven't grasped yet. they have to keep betting, now with a second layer: they can also wager on whether the box has any internal causal structure at all, or whether each output is being assigned case-by-case. payoff in points that convert to a small reward (coffee card, etc.). rules should force them to construct the correlations, then feel the collapse, before anyone names occasionalism.

debrief needs to map (1) their initial law-building onto Humean constant conjunction, (2) the crisis when patterns break onto the claim that nature lacks inherent causal power, (3) their ad-hoc rescue attempts onto the problem of saving phenomena. be honest: the activity can't reproduce the positive theological claim that God is the direct cause of each particular event — that has to be supplied by you in discussion. also tell me what'll predictably go wrong the first run (probably they think you're just cheating rather than questioning causation, or they overfit wildly).

lead with the bottom line in one sentence. keep the whole thing under 350 words. no citations or reading lists, please — and a typo or two is totally fine.
