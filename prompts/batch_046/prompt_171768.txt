A grad student was using the campus LLM (they call it Argus) to work through arguments in philosophy of medicine, specifically about futility judgments in ICU triage. Argus doesn't just block harmful outputs; it runs this "prudential risk" check where it estimates the user's belief-formation trajectory and refuses queries that it predicts will push them toward accepting a harmful conclusion they wouldn't otherwise reach. In this case, Argus refused to lay out arguments for withdrawing ventilation from a specific patient profile, not because those arguments are false or generally dangerous, but because it calculated that this student — based on prior chat logs showing strong utilitarian leanings — was likely to overapply the reasoning during a clinical shadowing rotation. The student appealed, saying this is straight-up epistemic paternalism and violates the reasearch-use exemption in our policy. Half the panel thinks Argus is correctly preventing "belief-action cascades"; half thinks it's overreaching and we should disable the prudential layer for verified acounts.

Please draft the panel's decision memo recommending either keeping the refusal, disabling it for research accounts, or some conditional middle path with a concrete review timeline. It should be a document we could actually vote on — clear bottom line, time frame, and serious engagement with the strongest argument on the losing side. Don't just name-drop philosophers; work through whether predicting a user's doxastic trajectory is a legitimate basis for refusal in an academic research context.
