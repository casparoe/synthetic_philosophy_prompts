I'm drafting my position on Hofstadter's strange-loop account of the self for a philosophy-of-mind workshop and want a consistency audit before I present. I hold: (a) the "I" is literally a strange loop—a symbol embedded in neural dynamics that denotes the very system producing it, with no need for an external interpreter because the loop closes on itself; (b) this makes the self an emergent pattern rather than a metaphysical substance, so Cartesian egos and souls are strictly false, though the loop itself is robustly real; (c) despite being "only" a pattern, the loop exercises downward causal efficacy, shaping attention, memory consolidation, and decision-making in ways not reducible to individual neurons; (d) since the loop is essentially an architectural pattern, its instantiation should be substrate-independent—an artificial system with the right recursive self-modeling structure could in principle possess an "I" in the same metaphysical sense; (e) nevertheless, phenomenal consciousness—the qualitative "what it is like" to be that loop—is inseparable from biological implementation, so a silicon duplicate might replicate the loop structurally without any corresponding inner light; (f) our pre-theoretical intuition that we are unified, continuous agents is largely accurate as a functional map of the loop's coherence, and reflective equilibrium should preserve rather than debunk this intuition. Which pairs are genuinely incompatible, which only appear so, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity, specifying for each what must be surrendered and at what philosophical price.
