As a retired physicist who has only recently begun working through contemporary philosophy of mind, I have been trying to articulate a coherent position on personal identity through the lens of Dennett’s brain-transplant scenario—the case in which a brain is sustained in a vat and linked remotely to a body, with the further possibility of duplication. Before presenting these views to a small interdisciplinary reading group, I want a consistency audit. Here is the package I have arrived at:

(1) If my brain is removed and kept functioning in a vat, with my original body elsewhere controlled by radio link, then I am literally located with the brain in the vat; the first-person indexical tracks the brain’s spatial position rather than the body’s.
(2) Drawing on my scientific background in tracking complex systems over time, I hold that diachronic identity of a cognitive system depends on the continuous spatiotemporal trajectory of its information-bearing substrate; functional equivalence is insufficient, so if every biological neuron is gradually replaced by a synthetic analog, the original system eventually terminates, even if behavior and memory are perfectly preserved.
(3) At the same time, I think consciousness is essentially embodied and homeostatically coupled: a brain isolated in a vat, lacking peripheral sensory loops, metabolic integration with an environment, and proprioceptive feedback, would not be genuinely conscious, however sophisticated its internal simulations.
(4) If the brain is duplicated so that both the original in the vat and a copy in a new body sincerely report being me, I deny that there is a determinate fact of the matter about which one is correct; personal identity can branch like a dividing cell, and the question “which is the real me?” is malformed rather than unanswered.
(5) I also maintain that first-person authority about who I am is epistemically basic but systematically fallible with respect to location: if I sincerely report being in the laboratory while my brain is actually in the basement vat, I am wrong about where I am, yet I am not wrong about being myself.

Some of these commitments seem to pull against one another, but I cannot tell which tensions are genuine and which only appear so. Please audit the set precisely: for each pair that is genuinely in tension, rank them by severity, identify what the cheapest revision would be, and explain exactly what that revision costs. Then identify at least one pair that looks conflicting but actually dissolves on inspection, explaining where the appearance comes from and why it fails. Finally, tell me which single commitment, if revised or abandoned, would resolve the greatest number of tensions with the least damage to the overall view, and be explicit about which principles that forces me to give up. Do not offer bibliographies, further readings, or citations.
