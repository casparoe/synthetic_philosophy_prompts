I take the view that intent alignment and value alignment belong to categorically different problem-types—intent alignment being a causal-instrumental problem of preserving a specified objective through an optimizer's dynamics, value alignment being a normative-discursive problem of justifying ends rather than securing means. From this I derive that no technical solution to intent alignment can, by itself, constitute progress on value alignment; that attempts to embed values within an aligned system confuse instrumental specification with normative justification; and that the proper division of labor is for engineers to solve intent alignment while democratic or philosophical institutions supply the normative frame externally. I want an honest map of what would expose this position. Which conceptual distinctions—between means-end reasoning and value-commitment, between optimization targets and reflective endorsement, or between preference satisfaction and rational justification—would have to collapse for the separation to fail? Are there formal results, perhaps in mechanism design or cooperative multi-agent learning, showing that normative constraints can be embedded in causal-instrumental architectures without reducing to preference aggregation? Would empirical evidence that human practical reasoning integrates value-commitment and instrumental optimization in a unitary architecture undermine my claim of categorical separation? I also want to know whether this view has insulated itself by defining value alignment out of the technical domain—if so, is that insulation a feature or a fatal defect? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward: which observations or arguments would force abandonment of the type-distinction, which would only require refining the institutional division, and which defenders of the separation would concede as genuinely damaging rather than dismiss as category mistakes? Finally, state what the position uniquely predicts—perhaps that technically value-loaded architectures will suffer specification gaming on normative concepts—and assess whether that prediction is genuinely risky or can always be reinterpreted away.
