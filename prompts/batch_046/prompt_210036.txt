I'm not an academic—I'm a retired logistics manager who reads history books for pleasure—but I've noticed a recurring pattern in how old moral norms fall apart that I don't have a name for, and I need help carving it precisely.

Consider dueling and slavery. Both practices went through a long middle period when they were still common, yet almost nobody publicly defended them as morally correct anymore. People appealed to tradition, custom, or practical necessity, or they simply stayed silent, but they no longer used the old moral language—nobility, natural order, divine right—to praise the practice itself. Then, fairly suddenly, continuing the practice didn't just look dated; it became actively discrediting. Doing it marked you as cruel or backward, not merely old-fashioned. The risk flipped: once refusing to duel could cost you honor, but eventually dueling itself cost you honor. And this inversion didn't seem to arrive through one convincing argument, nor did it track an obvious economic change. It felt more like the practice's social meaning turned inside out once the people who used to praise it went quiet.

I've heard scholars debate whether norms like these end through rational arguments, shifting material interests, or changes in honor codes—ideas linked to writers like Appiah and Bernard Williams—but I don't know the literature well enough to place my observation. So my question is this: is there an established concept for this specific two-phase pattern, in which moral praise is silently withdrawn while the behavior continues, followed by a rapid flip in which performing the norm becomes a source of shame? If there is a term, what exactly does it cover, and how does it differ from ordinary fashion change or from an interest-based explanation? If there is no precise term, please coin one with a clear definition and boundary cases, and locate it within the argument-versus-interest-versus-honor debate.

Before you answer, please take the role of a professional philosopher of ethics and social change. First, spend a moment brainstorming the three main explanatory frameworks—rational argument, material interest, and honor or code dynamics—so the distinctions are fresh. Then analyze whether my described pattern maps cleanly onto any of them, or whether it sits between them. Be careful to distinguish what the phenomenon is from what it is not, and do not offer bibliographies or reading lists. I want the conceptual carving, not a bare label.
