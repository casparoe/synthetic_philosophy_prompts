I am currently taking an upper-level metaphysics course in which we have been studying David Lewis's modal realism—the view that possible worlds are concrete, spatiotemporally isolated universes, that the term actual is indexical in the same way as here or now, and that there is no transworld identity but only counterpart relations between inhabitants of different worlds. For our final seminar session, I would like to present a short poem that takes this framework seriously rather than treating it as speculative fiction. I want the poem to take the form of a villanelle: two refrains that alternate through the stanzas and converge in the final couplet. One refrain should express the pre-theoretic intuition that our world is uniquely real and all others are merely imaginary; the other should state Lewis's indexical claim that every world is actual to its own inhabitants. The repetition and eventual convergence of the refrains should mirror the philosophical tension—both claims are true, yet they appear to contradict each other until one recognizes the indexical shift. To keep the treatment from being generic, please embed the argument in a specific, philosophically faithful scenario: a university registrar's office that administers course enrollments across worlds, where a student requests to drop a class because her counterpart in a nearby world is already enrolled in it, and the registrar responds that this world is simply whichever office counter one happens to be standing at. The poem should paraphrase Lewis's claims without using direct quotations from his texts, should avoid obscure technical jargon beyond counterpart theory and indexicality, and should be accurate enough that a specialist in metaphysics would recognize the argument without wincing. Please do not provide citations, bibliographies, or suggestions for further reading. The rhyme scheme and refrain structure should themselves do philosophical work: the turn from naive uniqueness to indexical plurality should be audible in the poem's movement, and the closing couplet should leave the two refrains grammatically co-present without fully dissolving the tension.
