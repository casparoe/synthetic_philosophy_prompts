Here is an argument I have been turning over that seems to derive an unpalatable conclusion from Carnap’s own commitments, and I am unsure where the slide occurs. (1) Carnap’s principle of tolerance holds that the choice between logical or linguistic frameworks is never a matter of factual correctness but only of pragmatic convenience: we adopt the calculus or language that best serves our purposes, and there is no “true” logic outside all frameworks. (2) If we take this seriously in the domain of social coordination, then institutional frameworks for economic planning—market pricing mechanisms, central allocation boards, participatory budgeting structures—are likewise not discoveries of a pre-given social reality but conventional instruments selected for their utility. (3) The evaluation of such frameworks is therefore purely instrumental: we ask only whether a given planning apparatus efficiently achieves specified ends (equitable distribution, rapid industrialization, ecological stability) relative to available alternatives. (4) Now suppose an authoritarian, top-down planning framework demonstrably outperforms democratic or market alternatives on those very instrumental metrics—say, by minimizing waste and maximizing output—while remaining internally coherent. (5) By (1)–(3), there would then be no principled, framework-external reason to reject it; any objection based on liberty or autonomy would merely express a preference for a different pragmatic weighting of ends, not a cognitive error in the authoritarian framework itself. (6) Yet Carnap was himself a committed democratic socialist and liberal, insisting that planning must be coupled with democratic control and individual freedom. (7) So either Carnap’s political values are arbitrary preferences smuggled in against his own tolerance principle, or there is a hidden constraint that blocks (5). Where does the argument fail? I would prefer to keep (1) and (6) intact and reject whatever step licenses the collapse of normative politics into pure instrumental framework-selection, but I want to know exactly what I am sacrificing if I do so. Is the error in extending tolerance from formal languages to normative institutions, in treating democratic values as just another pragmatic parameter, or in assuming that instrumental superiority can ever be assessed independently of the values embedded in the measuring framework itself?
