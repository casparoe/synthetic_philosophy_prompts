Please compare Dennett's argument in Quining Qualia—specifically his use of the Chase and Sanborn coffee-taster intuition pump—to a calibrationist interpretation of the same case that I sketched in seminar. On Dennett's view, the fact that each taster can adapt his preferences until they align suggests there is no stable, isolable phenomenal property—no intrinsic way-it-is-likeness—independent of functional dispositions and judgments; if there were, adaptation should be impossible or should leave a residual difference detectable in principle. The calibrationist reading treats the adaptation differently: it claims that Chase and Sanborn share a plastic sensory mechanism that realigns their discriminative reference frames without eliminating first-person differences, so the convergence in report and behavior underdetermines whether intrinsic qualia have been retained, replaced, or merely translated across calibrated scales. Where precisely does the calibrationist account fail to answer Dennett's incoherence charge? Does Dennett's argument depend on a binary—retention versus complete replacement of phenomenal character—that already presupposes the isolability of qualia he aims to deny? Keep the comparison focused on whether the Chase/Sanborn scenario demonstrates metaphysical incoherence or merely reveals that phenomenal reports are systematically underdetermined by behavioral and functional evidence.
