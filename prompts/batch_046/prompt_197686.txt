I've spent months following debates—mostly in popular science forums and in older philosophy texts I found in a used bookstore—about whether higher-level phenomena like consciousness, chemical bonding, or ecological stability are genuinely emergent or merely complicated results of physics. The pattern never changes. One group insists that if you knew every particle interaction in a brain you would have explained consciousness, so "emergence" is only a label for our present ignorance; the other group insists that properties like wetness or intentionality are not present in any single particle, and that arranging particles differently produces genuinely new causal powers that physics alone cannot anticipate. Both sides cite the same examples—water, brains, ant colonies—and neither moves. I suspect the deadlock is not mainly about physics but about two hidden disagreements: first, whether "explanation" requires that a lower-level story replace the higher-level one, or merely that it be compatible with it; and second, whether the test for reduction is predictive completeness from the bottom up, or just the absence of downward causation from the top. But I am not sure if I am right, or if the sides are actually asking different questions—one about what exists, one about what human science can compute. Can you diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is? Is it verbal, about the word "emergence"; substantive, about the nature of explanation or causation; or a case where one side asks an ontological question and the other answers an epistemic one under the same heading? And given your diagnosis, what would count as real progress—a formal result about inter-level prediction, a distinction that splits the debate, or evidence that the question itself is malformed? Please state how confident you are in this diagnosis and identify the specific point where you are least certain.
