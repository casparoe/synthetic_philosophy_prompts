Next month I'm presenting to our philosophy-of-mind workshop my view that Rosenthal-style HOT theory can survive the missing-target objection if we reconstrue the higher-order state as a dispostional tracking relation to the first-order state's functional profile, rather than as an occurrent conceptual representation. I concede this blurs the line with Kriegel-style self-representationalism, and I don't have a clean boundary condition worked out. The audience is sharp: one grad student defends self-representationalism and will push on whether I've just renamed their view; another works on global workspace and thinks higher-order approaches miss the neural correlates of consciousness; plus a neutral peer. There's also Prof. Chen, who has published well-known critiques of HOT theories and especially dislikes dispositionalist fixes in philosophy of mind. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of them would actually phrase them. For each one tell me what to concede, what to hold, and where my reply is weakest. Make sure to include the objection I'm hoping nobody asks—probably about blindsighters and whether my tracking view predicts phenomenal consciousness for absent visual states, which would just reproduce the original problem in a new form. Write the response in American English. Please also say how confident you are in each part of the prep, and single out the point you're least sure about.
