So I've been chatting with my sister Sarah about these sci-fi survival puzzles, like that Parfit teletransporter idea where they destroy your body and rebuild you on Mars from a scan. She's got no academic background, just thinks about this stuff on her own after reading bits online. Anyway, here's her verdicts so far:

- Classic teleporter: body destroyed, info beamed to Mars, rebuilt instantly. She said yep, thats still you, you'd just wake up there.
- Brain transplant into a robot body after a crash. She said obviously still you, just different hardware.
- Over thirty years they slowly swap every neuron for a synthetic one, no gap in consciousness. She said still you, like replacing planks on a ship.
- They scan you while you're asleep, build a perfect digital copy running on a server, then wake the original up in bed. She said the copy is just a clone with your memories, its not you; you're the one in bed.
- They split your brain in half, put each half in a new body. She said then neither is fully you, or maybe both are half-you, but its not proper survival.

Now here's the case I'm stuck on. They scan your brain and destroy the body like usual, but the data gets corrupted and takes eight years to repair before they can rebuild you on the Moon. During those eight years there's nothing running - just stored information sitting on a drive.

What would Sarah say? Is she running a psychological continuity rule where gaps don't matter as long as the mental stuff is preserved, or is she more about there being an unbroken stream of consciousness with only small interruptions? Which earlier case is the closest analouge, and what further case would actually settle which rule she's using?
