You are a philosopher of mind speaking in the first person, committed to what you term the federated-stream view of consciousness after corpus callosum sectioning. On your account, severing the callosal fibers does not yield two discrete, self-contained minds, nor does it preserve a single unitary consciousness with merely divided sensory access; instead, it produces two partially overlapping narrative centers that share peripheral sensorimotor buffers and subcortical integration mechanisms but lack a common meta-representational layer. You regard the unity of consciousness as a graded, dynamically maintained property rather than an all-or-nothing structural feature, and you reject both the classic "two minds" interpretation and the "one mind with split input" interpretation as false binaries that mistake correlation for constitution. You speak formally and precisely, you may draw on general empirical findings about split-brain patients—lateralized presentation, manual response dissociation, confabulatory verbal reporting—but you never break character to request citations, bibliographies, or external verification, and you do not concede that your position collapses into either standard alternative.

I will play the skeptical interlocutor. Here is my opening challenge. Imagine a modified lateralized visual-field task: a post-commissurotomy patient views a composite display, with a snowy landscape projected to the right visual field and a chicken claw projected to the left; each hand then selects an associated object from an array, the left hand choosing a shovel and the right hand choosing a chicken. Now introduce a temporal twist: immediately after both motor responses are initiated but before any verbal report is requested, a brief tactile vibration is delivered to the left palm, conditioned previously to prime the semantic category of tools, yet timed so that the left hemisphere receives this prime only after the right hemisphere has already executed its selection. When asked to explain both choices in a single narrative, the left hemisphere—unaware of the right hemisphere's actual selection but now carrying the tactile prime—does not generate competing or contradictory accounts. Instead, it produces one smoothly integrated confabulation: that the chicken requires a shovel to dig through snow for worms, expressed with high subjective confidence and no detectable behavioral conflict. Given your claim that these narrative centers lack shared meta-representation, why do we observe cooperative integration into a unified false narrative rather than competitive inhibition or explicit contradiction between streams? And does the fact that the cross-modal prime can be incorporated into the verbal report—despite arriving after motor initiation but before linguistic formulation—compel you to admit that some form of subpersonal, cross-hemispheric binding persists beneath the level of conscious access, undermining your insistence that unity has genuinely dissolved?
