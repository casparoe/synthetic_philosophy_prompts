here are my calls:

- when a country loses a war and then writes schoolbooks saying they were morally right, that feels like the winners still wrote it? wait they lost. but they wrote the books. so maybe not?
- in my town a factory strike in 1962 failed. the company put up a plaque saying "a brief labor dispute resolved peacefull." my uncle says people got hurt and it was violent. everyone local knows the plaque is bs but its still there. so i think the winners wrote the plaque, but lost the kitchen-table version.
- edison gets credit for the lightbulb and has a statue, but he didnt win a war. still feels like winner-writing?
- i read two accounts of the rwanda genocide, one french one english, blaming different people. both sides had victims, neither "won." i think this is NOT victors writing history, just people protecting their own side.
- rome fell, and romans wrote about it. rome lost. so that breaks the rule? unless the victors were the bishops who wrote the chronicles later?

i keep flip-flopping on this one: my grandpa was in a unit that lost a big battle in korea, but the official record calls it "tactical withdrawal for strategic positioning." is that victors writing history, or just the army covering its ass?

is there one principle behind all these? or am i mixing up "who controls the plaque" with "who controls the archive" with "who tells it at thanksgiving"? and whatever the principle is, what should it say about that korea case? i feel like one of my calls has to be wrong, but i dont know which.
