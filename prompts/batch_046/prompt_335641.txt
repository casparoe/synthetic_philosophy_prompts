Here is the package of commitments I've arrived at on Sen's liberal paradox, and I'd like an audit of whether they hang together. (1) I accept Sen's formal result: minimal liberty and the weak Pareto principle are incompatible over an unrestricted preference domain. (2) I think the correct resolution weakens Pareto rather than liberal rights—unanimous preference should not override protected spheres of choice. (3) I draw that protected sphere via Mill's harm principle: interference requires other-regarding harm, not mere offense or symbolic damage. (4) Nevertheless, I think democratic social choice should aggregate all citizens' informed, non-prejudicial preferences about collective life, including preferences about others' lifestyles, not only self-regarding ones. (5) I think the boundary between self-regarding and other-regarding is not fixed by subjective feeling but by an objective account of human capabilities and flourishing. (6) I believe the paradox is genuinely dissolved, not merely reinterpreted, once we restrict the domain to preferences compatible with every person's core capabilities. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the cheapest revision if one must go? Order tensions by severity, noting for each what has to give and at what cost. Lead with the bottom line: is the package consistent, and if not, which single commitment is the weakest link? Keep the audit to at most 350 words.
