I just finished a long, circular argument with a friend about disaster-relief allocation protocols. The design has two parts: first, aid is directed first and in the largest amounts to the worst-hit regions, which looks prioritarian; second, a uniformity clause requires that, if scarcity forces trade-offs, the per-capita aid gap between the worst-hit region and any other region never exceed a 2:1 ratio, even when maintaining that ratio means diverting resources away from the worst-hit. My friend says the clause is just bureaucratic fairness; I think it hides a philosophical commitment about whether equality itself matters. What implicit theory of distributive value does this two-part practice presuppose? Can pure prioritarianism accommodate the uniformity clause, or does the clause presuppose an egalitarian resistance to unequal outcomes that generates the leveling-down objection? Reconstruct the implicit view, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what merely fits it, say whether the two parts are philosophically consistent or underdetermined, and tell me what an unqualified prioritarian relief system would have to change—especially about the clause. Write one paragraph, no more than 250 words, and start with the bottom line.
