Construct a thought experiment that extends the Rietdijk–Putnam reasoning from distant astronomical events to an immediately shared local record, so that two observers in relative motion must disagree about the ontological status of the very same interaction they both physically access.

Scenario (one paragraph, realistic, no exotic physics): Alice and Bob pass each other in flat Minkowski spacetime at constant velocity; each carries an identical portable logger that stamps every detected event with a local proper-time marker and stores the data in a tamper-proof physical buffer. Both are linked by a direct fibre-optic cable to a single switch-box positioned midway; when the box is flipped, the signal reaches both loggers within microseconds, and each records event E. After separating, they travel to a reunion station where they physically exchange their buffers. They find that, according to Alice’s hyperplane of simultaneity, Bob’s recording of E lies in her objective past, whereas according to Bob’s hyperplane, Alice’s recording lies in his objective past—yet both buffers contain the identical physical trace of the same local switch-flip, and neither frame is privileged.

Constraints: no faster-than-light communication, no quantum-gravitational effects, no stipulated privileged foliation, and the apparatus must be replicable in principle by a terrestrial laboratory or small orbital platform. After the scenario, explain the verdict of the block-universe (B-theory) theorist: why the disagreement is merely indexical and poses no contradiction. Then explain what the presentist (A-theory) must say to avoid the contradiction that E is both present-to-Bob and past-to-Alice while remaining a single interaction they both accessed; in particular, show whether the presentist must accept frame-relative presentness or deny that the observers genuinely share access to the same ontological moment. Identify the structural feature that does the work—namely, local causal accessibility combined with the absence of a unique hyperplane of simultaneity—and explain why removing either feature collapses the case into a standard distant-star version or eliminates the disagreement altogether. Finally, describe the most plausible presentist reply (for example, appeal to a dynamically privileged foliation or rejection of the ontological significance of simultaneity relations), and assess whether that reply can be maintained without abandoning the covariance of special relativity.

Please provide your answer in British English.
