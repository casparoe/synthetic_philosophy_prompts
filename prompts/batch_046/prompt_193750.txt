Plan a teaching session for philosophy undergraduates who arrive convinced that statistical disagreement is always a matter of missing data. The aim is to make them see the reference class problem as a philosophical question about which properties are projectible, not a correctable methodological error. Specify: (1) the conceptual distinction that must be secured before the problem can even be formulated; (2) the carrying example—predicting whether a particular interdisciplinary research initiative will succeed, where reference classes defined by disciplinary origin, institutional history, and team-composition background yield incompatible probabilities and no further empirical test can privilege one without begging the question; (3) the misconceptions they will raise in sequence—first that larger samples solve it, then that Bayesian priors dissolve it, then that the problem is confined to self-locating beliefs—and the precise point in the arc where each should be absorbed; (4) what must be deliberately omitted at this level (Carnapian inductive logic, the Sleeping Beauty literature, formal treatments of the principal principle); and (5) the check for understanding that distinguishes grasping the problem as ontological ambiguity about class membership from merely memorizing the example.
