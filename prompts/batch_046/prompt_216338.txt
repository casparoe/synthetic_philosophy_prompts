For a 90-minute graduate seminar with 16 students, design a runnable deliberation activity that lets participants experience—before anyone names it—the failure mode that undermines debate-based AI safety protocols when one side deploys depth-obfuscated arguments. Stage a simulated alignment debate about a proposed reward-modeling scheme: split the group into two teams of six (Proponents and Critics) and a rotating panel of three Judges, with one remaining student acting as Protocol Monitor. The Proponents must defend the scheme; the Critics must identify a hidden flaw in it. Give the Proponents a “complexity toolkit” they may use at will: they may embed nested conditionals, quietly redefine a technical term mid-round, or shift the burden of proof onto the Critics by demanding that every objection be formalized before it counts. The Critics must argue in plain language with fixed definitions and may not request clarifications that reframe the Proponents’ terms. The Judges have a strict evaluation budget—only three clarification questions per round and a two-minute silent review—after which they must vote “sound,” “flawed,” or “cannot determine” with a brief justification. Run four rounds, rotating Judges and allowing the Proponents to choose whether to use the toolkit in rounds 2 and 4. The rules and payoffs should be arranged so that Judges initially feel confident in round 1, then in round 2 discover they can no longer tell whether a defense is genuinely rigorous or merely well-obfuscated within their question budget, and finally in round 4 face a verdict they cannot defend to the group. The idea should emerge from the dynamics: Judges should begin substituting social cues (confidence, fluency) for epistemic evaluation before the facilitator intervenes. In the debrief, map each round onto specific mechanisms—semantic drift in round 2, nested conditionality exhausting working memory in round 3, and burden-shifting collapsing the Critics’ time in round 4. Predict what will go wrong in practice: Judges will likely abandon their criteria for likability, and Critics may resort to shouting “that’s unfair” rather than identifying the structural move; instruct the facilitator to halt the round, ask the Critics to name the specific rule being exploited, and resume only after the move is labeled. Be explicit about which feature of real AI debate the activity cannot reproduce—specifically, a continuously self-improving obfuscator operating at superhuman scale with unbounded computation for argument generation—so that the facilitator must supply that limit verbally after the debrief. State clearly what participants should come away seeing: that debate as a safety mechanism assumes evaluation costs stay within the judges’ budget, and that obfuscation attacks the mechanism not by being false but by making sound evaluation prohibitively expensive.
