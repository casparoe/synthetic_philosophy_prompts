Write the opening remarks — about five minutes — for the dedication of the Meridian Community History Center, an institution designed to maintain public monuments, preserve every uncensored local record, and sponsor revisionist scholarship without severing residents from their past. The audience is divided: local families who want ancestors honored, professional archivists committed to total preservation, and critical historians who treat every heroic narrative as ideology. Structure the speech around Nietzsche’s three modes of relating to the past — monumental, antiquarian, and critical — not as decorative references but as real institutional constraints. Explain why each mode is necessary for the center yet carries its specific danger: the monumental risks false imitation and life-stifling hero worship; the antiquarian risks burying present life beneath the weight of saved documents; the critical risks cutting the roots that make present life possible. The remarks must be intelligible to the families without condescending to the historians, and they should not resolve the tension into a bland “balance”; instead, present the friction among the three modes as the center’s actual working method. Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or references to specific editions or texts.
