I've been trying to apply Tolstoy's theory from What Is Art? and I keep hitting the same wall.

(1) Art is infection of feeling—if nothing spreads, it's not art.
(2) Real art must spread brotherhood; counterfiet works spread egoism and are not art at all.
(3) So a clumsy sermon that makes three people feel solidarity is real art, just weak.
(4) And a technically brilliant novel that makes millions admire ruthless pride isn't bad art—it's non-art.
(5) But Tolstoy constantly talks about bad art and counterfiet art, treating them as inside the category.
(6) If we weaken (2) to save that talk, then counterfiet works are art, and a viral video that gives ten million teens a brief flash of solidarity is greater art than Anna Karenina.
(7) That can't be right—he clearly ranks his own novels above pop culture.
(8) So either his moral filter collapses into an aesthetic hierarchy he denies, or his critique of modern art is a category mistake. Which step slips, and what do I pay to fix it? I'd like to keep the idea that some modern works are genuinely bad art, not just failed propaganda.
