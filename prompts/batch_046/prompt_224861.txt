I serve as a program officer for a small regional foundation that receives roughly twenty unsolicited grant proposals per quarter, spanning local housing initiatives, global health pilots, long-range artificial-intelligence governance research, and farmed-animal welfare campaigns. Our board requires that every funding recommendation be generated by a reusable decision procedure rather than unstructured intuition, but the procedure must be executable in under thirty minutes per proposal, without access to external consultants, academic databases, or web searches, and it must produce both a scored ranking and an audit trail in plain language that non-specialist trustees can review.

Please construct a practical decision procedure—presented as an ordered checklist or word-flowchart—grounded in the importance-tractability-neglectedness framework for cause prioritization, but modified to address a philosophical problem that standard checklists suppress: the separation of empirical confidence from normative confidence. Specifically, the procedure should treat tractability (will the intervention work at the proposed scale?) and importance (which beneficiaries should count, and with what weight?) as independent streams, each requiring an explicit confidence grading.

For the importance stream: state the concrete moral premises being assumed (for example, presentist, longtermist, or species-neutral weighting), provide a brief justification that does not rely on citation or authority, and apply a mandatory “moral-risk flag” whenever two plausible normative premises would reverse the proposal’s ranking relative to another in the same quarter. For the tractability stream: specify the minimal evidence required to assign a score, the protocol when evidence is missing, and the precise point at which the assessment becomes a documented judgment call rather than a factual determination. For the neglectedness stream: define “already funded” within the officer’s available knowledge, include a step that checks for interaction effects with other current proposals, and state the threshold below which a cause is treated as saturated.

The deliverable must include hard stopping rules—points where the answer is “decline” regardless of performance on other dimensions—and explicit hand-off points where the decision must be referred to the board because the disagreement is about values rather than facts. Then apply the procedure to three hypothetical proposals: (A) expanding a municipal food-bank network; (B) funding a technical research group working on interpretability methods for large language models; and (C) supporting a legislative campaign to reduce stocking densities for industrial broiler chickens. Include at least one case in which the procedure yields a counter-intuitive recommendation, and identify the exact step at which two reasonable officers using the same procedure could legitimately diverge.

Please answer in British English.
