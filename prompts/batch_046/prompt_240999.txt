hey so i'm a novelist, mostly write about regimes and the people who survive them, and i also edit this tiny lit journal called The Witness where activists and ex-bureucrats send in first-person accounts. we're recieveing more submissions than i can handle and i need an actual procedure my board can run to figure out when a narrator is doing old-fashioned 'hide one fact' lying versus the arendt-style 'destroy the factual world' kind of lie she writes about — the modern one that needs institutional backing and replaces reality with fiction instead of just concealing it.

i want this turned into a reusable checklist, maybe four or five questions in order, that two other writers on my board can use without knowing any philosophy. it needs clear stop points: one for 'this is just dramatic omission or personal protection, not political lying,' one red flag for 'this is reality-replacement and should be rejected,' and a gray zone where i have to make the call and write one paragraph explaining why. include the spots where the checklist breaks down — like when a lie is told to protect plural witnesses rather than erase them — and be honest about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as rules.

then apply it to three hypothetical submissions:

1. a former census worker writing she never saw orders to delete records, but archives show she signed the cover sheet.
2. a fragment from my own novel-in-progress where a mother invents a false election result to calm her child — fiction inside fiction.
3. a gorgeous, beautifully written account from a minister's aide describing a massacre as a 'logistical correction,' using the regime's own vocabulary. the writing is so good we'd love to publish it, but the procedure should tell us to reject it anyway.

i want case 3 to come out as a reject despite being artful, case 1 to land in the ambiguous zone, and case 2 to clear the stop point. also say exactly what the procedure can't do, especially around artistic merit versus factual destruction.

constraints: we review about fifteen pieces a month, board has zero philosophy background, i have final sign-off but need to justify it in writing, and we have maybe twenty minutes per piece. don't ask me for citations or a reading list — a model without web access should be able to build this from arendt's actual distinction between hiding and world-building, her point that factual truth depends on plural witnesses, and the idea that modern political lying works by substituting a coherent fiction for the shared record. please ground it in that, not in obscure secondary literature. oh, and flag where i'm asking for something that sounds procedural but is actually a values call in disguise.
