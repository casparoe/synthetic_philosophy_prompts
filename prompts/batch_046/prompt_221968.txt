Structure the document as follows.

Section 1 — Numbered agreed claims. List the premises both sides can sign: shared commitments about the non-identity problem, about whether harm and benefit require actual subjects, about the logical structure of possible-worlds comparisons involving potential persons, and about the empirical limits of predicting future welfare. Do not include any claim that either camp rejects.

Cover at least: whether bringing a happy person into existence is morally good, neutral, or obligatory; whether the no-harm principle applies asymmetrically to acts of creation versus omission; and whether risk reasoning should treat existential risk (foreclosing potential happy lives) differently from suffering-risk (creating suffering lives).

For each crux, provide a concrete thought experiment or policy scenario both sides agree bears on it — for instance, a decision that alters only the probability that a specific modal counterpart comes to exist without affecting any actual person's welfare — and record in advance what each camp predicts about that scenario's moral evaluation.
