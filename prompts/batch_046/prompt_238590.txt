I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of science with training in comparative philosophy, applying for a six-month independent research fellowship. I want to develop a project on Erwin Schrödinger’s sustained engagement with Advaita Vedanta—specifically the doctrine of the unity of consciousness, or brahman-atman identity—and whether this engagement functions as a substantive interpretive constraint on quantum mechanics rather than as a private metaphysical ornament. Most existing discussions treat Schrödinger’s Vedantic remarks as either biographical curiosity or as an unsuccessful mystical dissolution of the measurement problem. What remains genuinely open is whether his monism can be reconstructed as a coherent, non-theological position on observer-dependence: one that denies the multiplicity of conscious subjects without collapsing into solipsism, and that offers a principled, non-ad hoc reason for resisting branching-world interpretations of the wavefunction.

My guiding hypothesis is that Schrödinger’s “unity of consciousness” is best read not as an ontological claim about the world but as an epistemic boundary condition on physical interpretation—analogous in structure to agent-centered or perspectival constraints in other domains of physics, though derived from a radically different source. If this reconstruction succeeds, it may reveal that Schrödinger’s resistance to observer multiplicity was driven by a specific philosophical commitment rather than by orthodox Copenhagen conservatism. If it fails, we may learn precisely where Vedantic monism becomes either redundant with existing single-world views or incoherent once separated from its soteriological context.

The project would proceed in three stages. Months one and two: a conceptual analysis of Schrödinger’s essays on consciousness and Vedanta to isolate the logical structure of his monism—distinguishing its epistemic, ontological, and phenomenological strands—without treating it as mere poetry. Months three and four: a comparative analysis mapping that structure onto formal treatments of observer multiplicity in quantum foundations, including Wigner-style consciousness-dependent collapse, Everettian branching, and relational interpretations, assessing whether the Vedantic constraint produces distinct conceptual commitments or merely redresses familiar puzzles in different vocabulary. Month five: drafting the core argument and testing it against two failure conditions—first, that the reconstructed position is logically equivalent to an existing interpretation and therefore philosophically redundant; second, that it cannot be separated from its theological context without losing coherence. Month six: revising the manuscript for workshop presentation and writing a concise policy-style summary for philosophers of physics who do not work on South Asian thought.

The deliverable would be a 40-page paper suitable for a philosophy-of-physics workshop, with a precise thesis on whether the Vedantic framing solves, dissolves, or merely displaces the measurement problem, plus a short reflective appendix on the limits of importing metaphysical frameworks into scientific interpretation. Success means establishing that Schrödinger’s monism offers an independent conceptual option in the space of quantum interpretations; failure means demonstrating conclusively that it does not, which is itself a valuable negative result. Given the six-month scope, I will restrict the physics to conceptual and logical comparison rather than original formal work, and I will avoid constructing a historical biography of Schrödinger’s religious development.

The audience is a fellowship committee that includes both philosophers and physicists, so the framing should be conceptually rigorous but accessible to non-specialists in Vedanta.
