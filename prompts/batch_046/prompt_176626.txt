ok so me and my friend jake just spent like three hours arguing about deontology after class and now my head hurts. i tried to pin down exactly why scaling up agent-relative constraints feels so weird, and i cooked up this chain that now seems suspicious because the conclusion is basically that deontology either implodes or turns into a parody of itself. here it is:

(1) A deontological constraint says you can't do X if X is wrong in itself, regardless of the overall consequences.
(2) If X is wrong in itself, then contributing to X as one person among many shouldn't magically become okay; otherwise the constraint is tracking causal efficacy, which sounds consequentialist.
(3) So if poisoning a river is categorically wrong, then working at the factory that does it should also be wrong for each worker, even when no single worker's labor is sufficient to cause the harm.
(4) But by the same reasoning, buying gasoline, voting in a large election, or eating meat in an industrial system should all be forbidden too, since each act is part of a collective harm.
(5) If deontologists bite the bullet and say yes, all of that is forbidden, then deontology demands a kind of total moral purity that makes ordinary life impossible.
(6) If instead they say those acts aren't wrong because each contribution is too small to cross a harm threshold, then they've smuggled in a consequentialist calculus -- wrongness now depends on expected impact, not on the intrinsic nature of the act.
(7) So either deontology is powerless to condemn big collective bads in a usable way, or it collapses into the very outcome-sensitive reasoning it was supposed to reject.

jake thinks (2) is the weak link and that deontologists can distinguish intended contribution from merely foreseen side-effects. i don't see how that fixes the scale problem though. which premise should i actually reject, and what does giving it up cost me? i really want to keep the intuition that moral rules don't just dissolve when you're one face in a crowd, but maybe im missing something about how agent-relative permissions work in group settings.
