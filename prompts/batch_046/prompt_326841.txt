I have been trying to understand Quine's argument that the line between analytic truths—those that hold merely by meaning—and synthetic truths—those that depend on experience—is far less sharp than philosophers traditionally assumed. If these two categories truly blend into one another, does that mean there is no meaningful difference between revising our definitions and revising our beliefs about the world? Specifically, if even logical and mathematical principles sit within the same web of belief as empirical science, then should we treat a future physical discovery that seemed to contradict basic arithmetic—say, conditions where two plus two does not yield four—not as a surprising physical law, but as a reason to revise mathematics itself? And if so, does Quine's view ultimately erase the distinction between changing our language and changing our view of reality?
