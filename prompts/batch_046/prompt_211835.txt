I'm currently in a seminar on African philosophy and comparative ethics, and we have been working through the claim that Ubuntu—often summarized by the maxim that a person is a person through other persons—offers not merely a cultural ethos but a rigorous metaphysical alternative to liberal individualism. The specific position I want to press is that Ubuntu's relational ontology resolves the boundary problem of moral status more successfully than either capacity-based accounts, which struggle with marginal cases, or communitarian accounts, which risk absorbing the individual into a static collective. On this view, personhood is neither an intrinsic property possessed prior to social interaction nor conferred by group membership alone; rather, it is dynamically constituted through practices of mutual recognition, and moral standing tracks the density and reciprocity of these relational bonds rather than any substrate-independent feature.

There is an additional, less commonly defended extension of this view that I want to include: if mental content is itself constituted through intersubjective recognition rather than being privately owned and merely inferred, then the traditional problem of other minds in philosophy of mind dissolves rather than being solved. Recognition becomes not epistemic evidence for an inner state but the very medium in which mental states exist.

I am asking for an honest assessment of how exposed this combined position actually is. What would have to be true for it to fail? Which commitments are load-bearing, and which are decorative? Specifically: is there a decisive argument or observation—perhaps from developmental psychology, moral phenomenology, or the philosophy of disability—that shows relational recognition cannot bear the constitutive weight assigned to it? Does the view risk collapsing into a form of moral relativism in which strangers, enemies, future generations, or non-human animals lie outside the network of recognition and therefore lack standing, or can it accommodate them without smuggling back in the intrinsic capacities it rejects? And with respect to the philosophy-of-mind extension, does treating recognition as constitutive rather than evidential genuinely dissolve the skeptical gap, or does it merely rename the problem, leaving us to explain how misrecognition is possible if mental content is genuinely intersubjective?
