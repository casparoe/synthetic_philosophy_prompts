People keep comparing Schrödinger's Vedanta-flavored view of consciousness to quantum entanglement: just as particles aren't really separate but one wavefunction, minds aren't really separate but one Atman. They use this to claim quantum mechanics "backs up" mystical unity and that the measurment problem is just us failing to see the single consciousness behind appearances. How far does that analogy hold? Lay out the mapping - what corresponds to the wavefunction, the measurement event, the observer? Then say where it breaks: does Schrödinger's actual view in his essays on consciousness treat mental unity like physical superposition, or is the parallel just poetic? And notice that entanglement is mathematically symetric but experientially private, while Vedantic unity is supposed to be experientially direct. I want to know whether the analogy survives those disanalogies or whether it's mostly borrowing quantum prestige to make monism sound scientific.
