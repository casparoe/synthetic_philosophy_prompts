I recently retired from field biology and have begun reading philosophy of rationality. I encountered the dollar auction paradox, in which rational actors keep bidding past the face value of the prize because withdrawal, if one is the second-highest bidder, means paying one's last bid and receiving nothing. The logic struck me as structurally parallel to what I observed in scientific taxonomy: once an institution has publicly committed to a classification, the reputational cost of retraction can exceed the cost of maintaining an outdated scheme, so the commitment escalates even as the evidence turns.

Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, with two refrains that alternate and converge in the final quatrain. Let one refrain voice the bidder's rationalization for pressing on; let the other voice the auctioneer's silent rule that the second price is still owed. Use the poem's turning points—the tercets in which the refrains exchange positions—to mark successive thresholds of irrational escalation: first past face value, then past double value, finally into the region where victory is cheaper than concession. Tighten the rhyme sounds as the stakes climb, and let the closing couplet leave the mechanism intact but unresolved: the dollar is won, yet the debt structure remains. The form itself should enact the repetitive trap, and the logic should be recognizable to someone familiar with escalation of commitment, without requiring outside references.
