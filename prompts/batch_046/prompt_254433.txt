I keep running into situations where a message has content inside it, but also carries instructions about how to take that content, and then there's a third layer that seems to say the instructions themselves are part of the content. Like a scientific paper that includes a fake peer-review letter praising it, but then the footnote says the letter was generated by the author, so now you're not sure whether the 'real' paper is also a fiction. Or an instruction manual that explains how to build a machine that reads instruction manuals, but the manual ends with a warning that the machine doesnt exist — except the warning is printed on the same paper as the manual. In both cases, the meaning feels like it's not in any one layer but in the gap between them. Is there an established term for this three-layer stacking, especialy when the middle layer contradicts the inner one and the outer layer pulls the rug out? I know about frame narratives and metafiction, but those usually keep the frame separate; here the frame is fighting the picture and there's no stable 'outside' to stand on. What's the closest concept, and where exactly does it fail to cover this?
