Your task is to draft the joint statement that two opposing camps could sign in good faith after a sustained adversarial collaboration on whether an “I” can be distributed across brains, specifically engaging with a Hofstadter-inspired pattern-pluralist view of self-persistence versus an embodied-singularist view.

The pattern-pluralist camp holds that personal identity is a high-level causal-functional pattern—an “I” is a strange loop of self-reference and integration—that can in principle persist across multiple substrates or brains if the relevant architecture is preserved, allowing for inter-brain persistence or “inter-subjective pattern-locking” rather than mere information-sharing. The embodied-singularist camp holds that the self is necessarily token-identical to a singular biological trajectory; functional copies or coupled brains may replicate experience but do not extend the original “I,” and distributed integration dilutes or duplicates identity rather than preserving it.

Produce the document with the following structure and constraints:
