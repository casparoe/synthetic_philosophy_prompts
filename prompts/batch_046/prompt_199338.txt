I'm giving the opening remarks at a workshop next tuesday — call it "Scaling Deliberation" — and the room is split right down the middle. Half the audience are people who think about HCH and factored cognition constantly; the other half are city planners and local goverment staff who came because they want public meetings that don't collapse into chaos. I want a five-minute talk, around 650 words, that actually turns on the idea that factored cognition is an idealization — that breaking a hard decision into isolated subproblems solved by separate reasoners misses how values, context, and emotional stakes leak across the boundaries we draw. I want a concrete running image through the whole thing, maybe an emergency zoning board trying to separate "technical risk assessment" from "community values" after a flood, where that separation visibly breaks down in one specific moment. The speech shouldn't be "AI is bad" or "meetings are magic"; it should say that the divide-and-conquer model of deliberation is structurally incomplete, and that shapes what we can honestly automate or scale. Don't drop unexplained jargon — if you mention HCH, explain it in the same breath — and no reading lists, no citations, nothing that requires a library. Write it like I'm actually speaking, casual, maybe a typo or two, so both sides feel included rather than lectured. The technical folks should recognize the argument; the planners should feel like it's about their work, not an import from somewhere else.
