I've been revisiting Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility—roughly, that true responsibility requires being causa sui, which is impossible because your actions either derive from your nature (for which you're not responsible) or occur randomly. Most discussions treat the argument as purely about agency and causation, but I think there's an unexamined metaphysical premise about what kind of thing "you" are across time. Specifically, Strawson seems to assume something like an enduring substance or a diachronically unified agent that can stand in a causal relation to its own origins. If we reject that and adopt a four-dimensionalist or stage-theoretic ontology—where persons are spacetime worms or series of momentary stages—does the demand that you caused your nature even make sense? There might not be a single entity that both possesses the trait and could have caused its possession.
