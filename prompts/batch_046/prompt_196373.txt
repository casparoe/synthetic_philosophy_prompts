You serve as the methodological referee for an interdisciplinary journal publishing empirical social-science and philosophy-of-science work. Authors frequently derive formally contradictory propositions from a single study—e.g., that “group X supports policy Y” and “group X rejects policy Y”—using different instruments or framing conditions. The board needs a reusable protocol you can run in about twenty minutes, with no formal logic training, grounded in dialetheism and paraconsistent logic rather than in classical consistency checks.

Design the protocol as an ordered decision flow with clear branch points. For any contradictory pair in a submission, it should lead to one of three outcomes: (A) eliminate the contradiction as an artifact before publication; (B) retain it but quarantine it within a non-explosive framework, applying a relevance gate that requires the contradictory premises to share a substantive predicate before arbitrary derivation is blocked; or (C) treat it as a substantive dialetheic claim about social reality that the authors may defend. Specify which paraconsistent distinctions must survive in the flow—contradictory versus trivial, the specific failure of explosion, the difference between model-layer and claim-layer inconsistency—and which can be collapsed for usability. Provide two or three short diagnostic tests (actual sentence structures or excerpt patterns) the referee applies to the manuscript’s contradictory passages. Then apply the protocol to three hypothetical submissions: (1) aggregate survey data yielding opposite responses under different framing conditions; (2) a philosophical argument that democratic legitimacy is both present and absent within a single institutional design; (3) a sloppy empirical study where the protocol forces outcome (C), but your referee intuition insists the authors are merely careless—explain what accepting the protocol’s verdict costs you, and flag every step that remains an unavoidable judgment call. List the predictable misfires, state clearly what the procedure is powerless to settle—especially whether contradictions belong to the world or only to our descriptions of it—and do not include citations, bibliographies, or appeals to named authorities.
