I am moderating a podcast interview with an unnamed philosopher who defends a specific account of hard choices between incommensurable values. On her view, incommensurability is not an epistemic limitation but a structural feature of practical reason: rational agents resolve such conflicts not by weighing values against one another but through a direct, non-comparative commitment she calls elective allegiance. She further maintains that the rational regret that persists after an optimal choice—what she labels structural regret—differs essentially from ordinary opportunity cost, since it involves no counterfactual comparison with the foregone option and carries its own normative authority. I want a sequence of six to eight interview questions that do not ask her to restate the view in general terms. Please include at least one question pressing on whether elective allegiance secretly reintroduces comparative weighting under a different description, together with a follow-up keyed to her likely response that the phenomenology of commitment is irreducibly first-personal; at least one question asking how structural regret is distinguished from mere psychological disappointment or adaptive preference formation, together with a follow-up anticipating her answer that the distinction rests on the regret's normative authority rather than its intensity; and at least one question probing whether her framework allows an agent to reverse an earlier elective allegiance without thereby rendering the original choice irrational, together with a follow-up that challenges her if she attempts to deflect by appealing to value pluralism alone. Present everything in continuous prose only: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as in a formal letter.
