I'm writing the final exam for my advanced high school political philosophy seminar, and I need one 45-minute essay prompt that avoids generic definitions. I want students to confront a specific conceptual tension: the "reciprocity-boundary" framework, which holds that a transfer becomes corruption only when it violates an implicit social reciprocity norm rather than a formal legal rule, versus the Western legal-rational standard that treats any quid pro quo involving public office as corruption regardless of local gift economies. Draft the essay question asking students to assess whether the reciprocity-boundary view can be defended without collapsing into cultural relativism, and whether Western anti-corruption norms unfairly pathologize legitimate patron-client obligations. Include a model answer of roughly 500 words and a marking scheme totaling 20 points built around five required moves: clearly distinguishing transactional corruption from reciprocity-based obligations; explaining why legal-rational standards might misclassify clientelism; identifying the relativism risk in defending the reciprocity-boundary view; proposing a criterion—other than culture or law alone—that could separate legitimate gift-giving from corruption; and applying that criterion to a concrete hypothetical, such as a rural official accepting seasonal labor or livestock in exchange for administrative access. For each move, state the points awarded and provide a one-sentence note on the typical mistake that costs marks. Keep the language in American English, keep the prompt focused, and do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
