I'm part of my city's participatory-budget task force and we have to vote next week on a pilot for the 2026 neighborhood arts fund. The proposal replaces our usual elected council with a sortition panel, but it's not straight lottocracy: they want an 80-person pool selected by lot, then those 80 do a single round of internal voting to narrow down to 12 who actually sit and decide. There's also this delegation clause where anyone in the pool can temporarily shift their deliberative weight to another pool member for one session, then it snaps back. The designers call it "staggered nested selection with liquid deliberation" or something like that.

Can you draft the memo I'd circulate before Thursday? I need a clear reccomendation - approve the pilot, reject it, or approve with conditions - and the reasoning has to actually argue for it, not just list pros and cons. It should take seriously the strongest objection: that the internal vote plus delegation just smuggles elections and clientelism back in, and that the two-stage process breaks the equality-of-opportunity logic that makes sortition defensible in the first place. Also deal with the practical worrie that 80 people can't meaningfully deliberate before voting on who makes the 12. Write it like a real internal memo, bottom line up front, readable but not dumbed down. And please don't just cite Aristotle or drop a bibliography - reason it through from the institutional logic.
