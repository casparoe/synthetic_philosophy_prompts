I've been trying to figure out the boundary problem for a course I'm taking — specifically, who belongs to the demos in a modern federal system. I think I've landed on a hybrid view: membership should depend on sustained exposure to coercive law plus structural economic interdependence, but with a twist. Only people who are actually subject to the state's direct enforcement apparatus for a continuous stretch longer than a standard budget cylce should count, while people who just feel market effects or live inside borders without being in any registerd system shouldn't. It seems like this splits the difference between the "all affected interests" crowd and the citizenship-only people.

But I want to stress-test it. What would genuinely count against this? I'm thinking about cases like seasonal laborers who come and go, digital nomads who file taxes but avoid local registration, or folks in semi-autonomous regions where the state delegates policing. Which objections would be fatal versus just awkward? And what would someone who holds this view have to admit is a real weakness, not just something critics complain about?
