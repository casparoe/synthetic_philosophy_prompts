Develop a rigorous argument for the position that moral status is not a monotonic gradient of sentient capacity, but instead exhibits a step-graded structure governed by what I term integrative vulnerability: the extent to which harm to a being can destabilize not merely local sensory or affective states, but the global coherence of its self-maintaining organizational dynamics. On this view, insects carry a minimal yet genuinely non-zero moral status not in virtue of any rich phenomenal life, but because nociceptive disruption can compromise the integrity of their homeostatic organization; by contrast, a thermostat—despite possessing feedback loops structurally analogous to nociception—bears zero status, since it lacks any organizational coherence that could suffer global disruption. You should defend the further claim that there is a sharp discontinuity, rather than a smooth slope, between entities possessing predictive self-modeling capacities and those lacking them, such that the emergence of even a rudimentary self-model transforms moral status in kind rather than merely incrementing it in degree. Address the objection that any such discontinuity is an illegitimate anthropocentric projection onto nature, and do so without appealing to species membership, standard personhood criteria, or deontological frameworks. Keep the argument internal to the logic of organizational integration and predictive dynamics, and show why gradations within each step do not collapse the overall architecture back into a continuous scale.
