Laudan's pessimistic meta-induction says that because lots of past theories—phlogiston, caloric, the ether—were empirically successful but false, we should inductively expect our current successful theories to be false too. This is a problem for scientific realism, which ties success to approximate truth.

One reply is structural realism, developed by Worrall. He pointed out that in some transitions, like Fresnel to Maxwell, the math survives even though the ontology gets replaced. So we should only be realists about structure, not about entities.

But Psillos defends entity realism, arguing we should only commit to entities we can manipulate, like electrons. He applies this to PMI by saying entities persist even when theories change. So structural realism is really just the same strategy: keep the structure, drop the false parts.

Looking at Fresnel-Maxwell, the equations stay intact but the ether is abandoned. Since those equations describe the entities, if the equations survive then the entities they describe survive too. So the ether wasn't actually dropped; it was just redescribed. That means structural realism saves both the math and the ontology, and PMI is wrong to think anything false was retained.

So PMI doesn't work because it treats theory replacement as total falsification. But structural realism shows that the explanatory core always survives, and since explanatory success tracks that core, realism about explanation is safe even if we stay agnostic about specific entities.
