I have been working through an objection to illusionism about phenomenal consciousness, and I have ended up with an argument that seems to defeat itself. I want to know which link is weakest and what repairing it costs.

(1) Illusionism holds that phenomenal properties—what-it-is-likeness—are not real features of mental states, but are systematically misrepresented by introspective mechanisms.

(2) Any misrepresentation produced by introspection must have both a target (the mental state being observed) and an attributed content (that the state possesses phenomenal character).

(3) To defend illusionism, one must be able to identify the target mental states—e.g., to recognize that one is currently undergoing a visual experience—while denying the attributed phenomenal content.

(4) Identifying the target state requires accepting at least part of the introspective deliverance: that there is a mental state of a specific functional or representational kind occurring now.

(5) There is no non-arbitrary boundary within introspection that separates a trustworthy "state-register" from an untrustworthy "phenomenal gloss," since both are outputs of the same representational mechanism with the same evidential status.

(6) Consequently, the illusionist must either reject all introspective deliverances—including the identification of the mental states the theory is about—or arbitrarily privilege one component over another without independent justification.

(7) If all introspective deliverances are rejected, the illusionist lacks empirical access to the mental states being described, rendering the theory unmotivated.

(8) Therefore illusionism either collapses into global introspective skepticism or relies on an unjustified bifurcation; in either case, it proves too much by undermining the evidential basis required to state it.

I suspect the error lies between (4) and (6), but I cannot isolate it. Which step should be rejected? If I abandon (5), do I owe an account of how introspection can be partially trusted? If I abandon (3), can the illusionist identify target states without any introspective access? I am especially reluctant to surrender (1), since it is the core claim I am examining, and I want to know whether saving illusionism demands giving up (5) or whether there is a principled split I have overlooked.
