Please construct a final-exam essay item for an upper-level philosophy of biology seminar, together with a model answer of about 500 words and a marking scheme. The item should present a policy analyst's argument that misapplies broad-sense heritability coefficients: observing a heritability of 0.65 for a cognitive trait in a population with highly uniform schooling, the analyst concludes that (1) for any individual, 65% of the trait is genetically determined and 35% is environmentally determined; (2) environmental interventions can therefore shift outcomes by at most 35% of the total range; and (3) running the same study in a population with widely varying school quality would yield the same 0.65 coefficient, since genetic effects do not change. Ask students to diagnose and correct each inference, explaining precisely why heritability is a population-level variance-partitioning statistic rather than an individual causal share, and why its value depends on environmental variance even when genetic effects remain constant. The model answer should make these distinctions rigorously without merely reciting textbook definitions. The marking scheme should distinguish an A answer—which correctly addresses all three errors, explains the dependency on environmental variance, and avoids treating the coefficient as a fixed biological constant—from a B answer, which may reject the individual-level interpretation but either misses the variance-sensitivity point or fails to explain why the coefficient would likely differ across the two populations.
