I am designing a study protocol for a clinical neuropsychology lab that collects first-person reports from patients with disorders of self-awareness, and I want the protocol grounded in heterophenomenology rather than in either naive introspectionism or behaviorist elimination. Please construct a reusable decision procedure my research team can apply to each subject, with the steps in sequence, the criterion for passing each gate, and explicit flags for where judgment cannot be eliminated. The procedure must include: (a) how to treat the subject's verbal report as authoritative text about their heterophenomenological world rather than as direct access to conscious states; (b) how to integrate behavioral and neuroimaging data without letting either automatically override the report; (c) a calibration step I am calling counterfactual authority testing, in which the subject is presented with a hypothetical third party exhibiting behavior identical to their own and asked whether that third party's report should be taken at face value, together with rules for coding the response; (d) the precise point at which the procedure requires the researcher to choose between describing the heterophenomenological world faithfully and offering a causal explanation that dismisses it; and (e) a termination condition that tells the team whether the result is a clinical description, a theoretical model, or an ethically loaded recommendation that must be referred to a human decision-maker. Then run the procedure on two worked cases: a patient with anosognosia for hemiplegia who verbally reports full bilateral mobility despite objective paralysis, and a split-brain patient whose left hemisphere produces an elaborate explanation for an action actually initiated by the right hemisphere. For each case, show what the procedure outputs, where it stalls, and what answer it produces that a clinician is likely to resist. Finally, state clearly what this procedure is structurally incapable of delivering — for example, whether it can ever license overriding a patient's report in treatment decisions, or whether it resolves the hard problem of consciousness rather than merely organizing our descriptions of it. Please write the answer in American English.
