hey im trying to think through chestertons fence applied to institutional reform but i keep ending up in a dead end. heres the argument that worries me:

(1) chestertons fence says dont tear down a fence unless you know why its there.
(2) any rule that has lasted generations encodes distributed, tacit knowledge about problems we dont face the same way anymore.
(3) but through functional drift it may have picked up hidden jobs — like a tax loophole that accidentally keeps a regional industry alive, or a tenure norm that now mainly protects unpopular research.
(4) so to justify tearing it down youd need to map not just the original purpose but every hidden function, or at least prove none are load-bearing.
(5) for anything complex (zoning law, tenure rules, common-law doctrines) that full map is impossible for any individual reformer.
(6) so the fence principle, taken seriously, makes rational reform impossible in principle, not just risky.
(7) but chesterton supported distributism and wanted to change modern economics, so he clearly allowed some tearing-down.
