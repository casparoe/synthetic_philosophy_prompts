At our philosophy reading group last week two people got stuck arguing over Hofstader's JOOTSing idea and the MU-puzzle.

Maya said jumping out of the system is exactly what minds do: we spot formal rules trapping us and step outside them, so the MU-puzzle proves formal systems are incomplete and JOOTSing is the human fix.

Leo said that's mysticism with extra steps. We never genuinely escape; we just switch to a different formal system — common sense, intuition — with there own hidden rules. The puzzle isn't solved, we've just changed cages without noticing the bars.

Maya came back: if every jump is only entering another system, then understanding is an infinite regress of cages, which makes real comprehension empty.

Leo answered that understanding never required escaping all systems, only navigating between them. The mistake is assuming there's some magical "outside" that isn't also structured.
