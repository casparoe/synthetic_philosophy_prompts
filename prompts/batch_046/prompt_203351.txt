I'm constructing the final examination for a graduate seminar on embedded agency, rationality, and physical self-reference. I need a single 90-minute essay prompt that students cannot answer by summarizing standard distinctions—such as the difference between embodied cognition and Cartesian functionalism, or by listing canonical examples of agents physically situated within their environments. Instead, I want the question to turn on a specific conceptual tension that I will call the predictive-embedding loop: an agent's internal generative model treats itself and its own decision processes as part of the environment to be predicted, yet because the agent is materially embedded in that environment, any revision to its internal model causally alters the external dynamics it was attempting to forecast. Ask the students to evaluate whether this loop undermines the coherence of expected-utility maximization for fully embedded agents, and to assess whether the problem can be dissolved by abandoning a unified self-model—drawing analogies to distributed or fragmentary architectures of cognition—without collapsing into eliminativism about agency. The prompt should be suitable for students who have encountered the core literature on embedded and extended cognition but who must construct the argument from first principles in exam conditions, with no access to external references.

Please provide the exam question itself, followed by a model answer and a marking scheme. The model answer must make at least four distinct argumentative moves: (1) a precise distinction between epistemic self-reference and causal feedback in embedded prediction; (2) an explanation of why the loop is not reducible to a standard observer-effect or measurement-disturbance problem in physics; (3) an evaluation of whether distributed or draft-like architectures of self-representation resolve the loop or merely relocate it; and (4) a defended conclusion about whether embedded agency requires revising the framing of utility maximization, abandoning it altogether, or retaining it with a modified ontology of the decision-maker. The marking scheme should allocate 25 marks total, with explicit point values for each move and for the quality of the comparative analysis, plus a brief note for each criterion describing the characteristic failure mode—such as conflating causal feedback with logical paradox, treating distributed cognition as a panacea without addressing the physical-causal link, or summarizing the literature on embodied cognition without engaging the specific predictive-embedding tension.
