Here is an argument I sketched for a seminar on hierarchical explanation and now distrust, because it oscillates between contradictory conclusions without an obvious exit. (1) A tangled hierarchy, as illustrated by Escher's Drawing Hands, is a system in which two or more levels are mutually generative: each level supplies the rules or boundary conditions for the other, so neither can be fully specified without reference to the other. (2) The inviolate-level thesis holds that there exists at least one stratum—call it L0—whose internal dynamics are causally and conceptually closed to downward influence from any higher level L1, L2, etc. (3) If downward influence from a higher level to a lower one is genuinely possible, then there is no non-arbitrary boundary that can contain it; permeability is either globally absent or globally present across the hierarchy. (4) In perceptual psychology, expectation-driven sharpening shows that conceptual states at a cognitive level modify sensory processing at a lower perceptual level, which is downward permeability across at least one interface. (5) In evolutionary biology, niche construction demonstrates that organism-level behavior alters selection pressures at the genetic level, so downward permeability also crosses the organism-genome boundary. (6) Therefore downward permeability is empirically real and is not confined to a single localized interface. (7) From (3) and (6) it follows that L0 does not exist: every level is open to downward influence. (8) If every level is open to downward influence, then explanation becomes globally circular—explaining L0 requires L1, which requires L2, and eventually the chain loops back to L0—so no level can serve as an independent explanatory ground. (9) But global circularity makes justification impossible, since no claim could be supported without eventually presupposing itself. (10) Justification is plainly not impossible; we routinely justify claims about physics, biology, and cognition by appealing to stable lower-level mechanisms. (11) Therefore (8) must be false: not every level is open to downward influence. (12) Yet (11) directly contradicts (7), which followed validly from (3) and (6). So either (3) fails—meaning permeability can be locally real without being globally total—or (2) fails, meaning there is no inviolate level but circularity is avoided in some other way. I would prefer to keep (1) and (2), since they frame the exact problem I care about, and I am inclined to reject (3) as an illicit slippery slope. But if I reject (3), I owe an account of where the principled cutoff lies, and I do not see one. Which step actually breaks, and what does each available rejection cost?
