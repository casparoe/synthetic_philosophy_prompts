Write an essay evaluating a reconstructed version of Berkeley's master argument against materialism, framed not as a direct inference from conceivability to impossibility but as a semantic-cum-epistemic critique of the very idea of 'mind-independent substance' conceived purely as that-which-is-not-mental. First, lay out the standard interpretation, in which Philonous challenges Hylas to conceive of an object existing unperceived, and explain why critics have typically charged this with either begging the question or conflating epistemic access with metaphysical status. Second, present the reconstructed version, according to which the argument targets not material bodies per se but any entity defined solely by negative abstraction from mental properties—asking whether such a notion has determinate content or whether it collapses into an empty placeholder. Third, weigh the main objections to this reconstruction: that negative definitions are common and unproblematic in mathematics and logic; that Berkeley's own account of spirits relies on a similarly negative contrast with bodies; and that the reconstruction risks turning Berkeley's immaterialism into a generic verificationism he never endorsed. Finally, reach and defend a clear conclusion: does the reconstructed argument avoid the standard circularity charge, or does it merely relocate the problem into a broader anti-realist framework that undermines Berkeley's own theological commitments? Be precise about what kind of 'independence' is at stake—causal, conceptual, or representational—and do not treat all three as equivalent.
