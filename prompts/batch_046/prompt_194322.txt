Hey, I've been trying to write a seminar paper on Hobsbawm and Anderson but got stuck in this argument my friend threw at me. I think it proves too much but I can't find the crack.

(1) Anderson's point is that a nation is an imagned community: members will never know most of their fellow-members, so the bond is mental rather than face-to-face.
(2) Hobsbawm adds that the traditions which make that mental bond feel ancient—flags, anthems, origin myths—are usually recent inventions that pretend to be old.
(3) So if a group only exists because people imagine a connection and maintain it through invented rituals, then there's no "natural" group underneath; the group is just a useful fiction.
(4) Now apply that same diagnostic to any collective that claims deep roots. Take academic disciplines: the philosophy "canon" from Plato to Kant was basically assembled in 19th-century German universities, and its rituals—comprehensive exams, citation norms, the very idea of a "core curriculum"—are instutional inventions from the last 150 years.
(5) So philosophy is also an imagined community with invented traditions, no more naturally unified than France or Italy.
(6) But if being "imagined and invented" strips a group of special authority, then philosophy's claims to be a continuous tradition of rational inquiry are just self-serving myth.
(7) By the same token, "rationality" itself, if it's defined by the practices of this invented community, is just a local custom.
(8) That means there's no non-arbitrary reason to prefer philosophical reasoning over national loyalty, since both are maintained by the same kind of mental image and ritual repetition.
(9) But I still think it's rational to prefer reasoning over nationalism, so one of the steps must be wrong.

If I have to keep (1) and (2) intact—since that's the whole Hobsbawm/Anderson framework—where does the slide happen? Is it the move from "imagined" to "ficticious"? Or is there a hidden scope shift between (5) and (6)? I feel like something slips between "this group was invented" and "therefore it has no authority," but I can't name it. What's the cost of blocking that slide?
