I'm putting together a little taxonomy for my study group — we're mostly rationalist-blog types who read some philosophy of language but don't have formal training — on Frege's puzzle about identity statements. I want a decision tree that sorts people by thier gut reactions to concrete cases, not by which philosopher they like.

Use three branch nodes built around specific cases: first the astronomical one (Hesperus is Phosphorus), then the fictional one (Superman is Clark Kent), then a belief report like Lois believes Superman can fly but doesn't believe Clark Kent can fly. Each node should be phrased as a concrete question someone can answer without knowing the label — for instance whether the cognitive difference is in the representation in the thinker's head, in some pragmatic feature of the utterance, or in a hidden structural ambiguity in the sentence itself. The leaves should include at least pure reference-only views, Fregean sense-based accounts, mental-file or mode-of-presentation theories, pragmatic dissolution views, and one niche option: the idea that identity statements trigger a compositional type-shift or structural reorganization in semantic processing rather than carrying special content themselves. Where two leaves can't really be seperated by the cases, flag that pair honestly. Also include one combination of answers that lands in an empty space — diagnose whether it's incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Don't ask for citations, bibliographies, or named philosophers at any point; the point is to map where people actually land.
