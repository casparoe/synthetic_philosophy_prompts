For a novel I am composing, I need to imagine an anachronistic philosophical exchange. Suppose Jeremy Bentham—preserved in some speculative manner—were confronted with the modern problem of moral uncertainty in institutional decision-making: a governing body must distribute scarce medical resources according to either a strict deontological rights framework or a rule-utilitarian calculus, yet lacks any epistemic basis for adjudicating between these normative commitments. Given Bentham’s commitment to the greatest happiness principle and his well-known dismissal of natural rights, how do you believe he would assess this tension? Would he treat the resolution of moral uncertainty as itself subject to utility calculation—perhaps endorsing a form of expected-value reasoning—or would he reject the framing altogether because it improperly elevates rights-based objections? I ask for a reasoned prediction of his view, not for citations or a reading list; I am drafting away from any library and need an assessment grounded solely in the internal logic of his philosophy.
