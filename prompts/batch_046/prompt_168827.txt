Consider five responses to Russell's paradox and the failure of naive comprehension: (A) Russell's ramified type theory; (B) Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory with Choice, using the Axiom of Separation; (C) Quine's New Foundations with stratified comprehension; (D) paraconsistent set theories that tolerate a restricted contradiction at the universal set; and (E) a proposed "domain-restricted comprehension" scheme in which a property determines a set only relative to a specified conceptual neighborhood bounded by a metric over intensional similarity, so that self-reference is blocked not by type or size restrictions but by neighborhood discontinuity.

Rank these five (A through E) from 1 (best preserves the pre-theoretic intuition that every well-defined property determines a corresponding collection) to 5 (most radically abandons that intuition). Provide a one-sentence justification for each rank. Then identify the pair that are technically closest in blocking Russell's contradiction but farthest apart in philosophical motivation, and explain in one sentence why that divergence occurs despite the structural similarity.
