I'm drafting my contribution to a faculty reading group on urban justice and want a rigorous consistency audit before I present. Here is my current package of commitments:

(1) I endorse a modified Lefebvrian right to the city understood as a collective moral claim to participatory urban governance and equitable spatial access, rather than as an individual entitlement to reside in any particular high-demand neighborhood.

(2) I maintain that exclusionary single-family zoning is unjust because it enables current residents to monopolize urban opportunity without leaving sufficient resources of comparable quality for outsiders, thereby violating a spatial interpretation of the Lockean proviso.

(3) I hold that democratic local control over land use is generally legitimate, and that NIMBY objections to upzoning are not necessarily expressions of bad faith; they can reflect genuine place-attachment and reasonable risk-aversion about neighborhood change.

(4) I deny that there is a universal human right to live in any specific global city; being priced out of a high-demand market such as Manhattan or San Francisco is not, in itself, an injustice provided that background market conditions are fair.

(5) I support mandatory inclusionary zoning with deep affordability mandates, treating these as justified exactions that correct for the unearned land-value increment captured through upzoning, but I reject filtering or trickle-down housing supply theories as both empirically unreliable and normatively irrelevant to questions of justice in access.

(6) I accept that neighborhood aesthetic character and local environmental quality are legitimate public goods that zoning may protect, but only on the condition that the costs of preservation are not disproportionately externalized onto groups excluded from the neighborhood.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which pairs only appear to conflict but can be reconciled, and how severe each real tension is. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging. For each genuine tension, specify what commitment would have to be revised or qualified, and identify the least costly revision that restores consistency without abandoning the core motivation behind the original view. In particular, be precise about whether my acceptance of local democratic legitimacy in (3) undermines my proviso-based critique in (2), whether my denial of an individual entitlement in (4) weakens the systemic force of (2), and whether my support for mandatory exactions in (5) can be squared with my defense of local autonomy in (3).

Please also separate, in your analysis, what is philosophically settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on urban ethics and political economy — for instance, do not present the empirical status of housing filtering or the correct application of the Lockean proviso to zoning as consensus positions when they remain disputed.
