heres the answer, more or less verbatim except i fixed spelling for readability:

Thomson says even if the fetus is a full person with rights, abortion can still be okay because nobody is required to let another person use their body for nine months. The violinist analogy shows this: youre hooked up to save a famous violinist, and if you stay hooked youre being a good samaritan but if you unplug youre just refusing aid. You dont actually kill him, you just stop saving him, so its not murder. Abortion is like unplugging.

Marquis argues killing is wrong mainly because it takes away a future like ours. The fetus has this future, so abortion is wrong for the same reason killing an adult is wrong. He doesnt care about personhood or consent.

The real conflict is that Thomson thinks personhood plus body rights makes abortion permissible, while Marquis thinks the future value makes it wrong no matter what. They actually agree the fetus is a person, but they weigh rights differently. Also the active/passive thing doesnt matter because unplugging is passive but abortion is usually active, so Thomsons analogy actually breaks down here.
