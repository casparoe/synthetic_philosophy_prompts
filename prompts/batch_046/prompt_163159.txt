Here is an argument a study group constructed. They find it suspicious because it seems to collapse Goodman's pragmatic solution to the grue problem into a demand for physical time-asymmetry he wanted to avoid.

(1) Goodman's response to the grue paradox is that "green" is projectible and "grue" is not because "green" is entrenched in our actual inductive practice, while "grue" is not.

(2) Entrenchment is descriptive: it records which predicates a linguistic community actually projects, not which ones are metaphysically correct.

(3) The fundamental dynamical laws of physics are, with minor exceptions, time-symmetric; there is no law-level distinction between projecting properties forward in time and projecting them backward.

(4) So imagine a "reverse epistemic agent"—physically possible, using the same cognitive architecture—whose practice is to project predicates like "grue" from its present into what it calls its "past" (our future), treating time-reversed samples as its inductive base.

(5) For this agent, "grue" is entrenched and "green" is not, with exactly the same descriptive legitimacy as ours, since entrenchment tracks practice, not physical orientation.

(6) Therefore entrenchment cannot by itself privilege our practice; if it justifies our induction, it equally justifies the reverse agent's opposite induction.

(7) The only way to block this is to invoke an objective asymmetry—say, that our temporal orientation is physically or metaphysically privileged—so that only our practice counts.

(8) But invoking that asymmetry is precisely what Goodman's pragmatist solution was meant to avoid: he replaced a metaphysical answer with a practical one.

(9) So either induction has no objective basis for us either, or Goodman has smuggled the temporal asymmetry back in as an unstated constraint on entrenchment.

(10) If I must choose, I would rather keep (1) and (8) than (9).
