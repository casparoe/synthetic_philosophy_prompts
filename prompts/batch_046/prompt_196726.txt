As a sixth-form student preparing an extended essay on the philosophy of science, I should like to examine how Isaac Newton might have evaluated contemporary debates concerning the scientific status of string theory. The theory presently lacks direct empirical verification and instead relies upon mathematical consistency, together with more recent theoretical distinctions—such as the swampland conjectures that seek to separate viable quantum gravity theories from mathematically inconsistent ones—to establish its credibility. Without asking for citations, bibliographies, or references to particular modern publications, and writing in British English, could you predict Newton's likely view? Specifically, I am interested in whether he would apply his well-known methodological principle that theoretical claims ought to be derived from observable phenomena, and whether he would judge the absence of experimental data to be fatal to string theory's scientific claims, or whether he might concede that internal mathematical coherence and the systematic exclusion of inconsistent alternatives constitute a legitimate, if indirect, form of warrant. Please also address how sharply he would distinguish between formal elegance and empirically grounded knowledge in reaching his conclusion.
