"dispositions are properties like fragility or solubility that depend on the internal physical structure of an object, so they appear to be intrinsic. Martin's well-known fink example shows this is wrong: a wire is live, but a fink removes the charge the instant it is touched. Because the presence of the external fink determines whether the disposition manifests, the disposition itself must depend on the environment, hence it is not intrinsic.

Johnston's masks work the same way: a fragile glass wrapped in foam doesnt break when struck. The foam is outside the glass and stops the breaking, which means fragility is also determined by external factors. In both cases the object has the same internal makeup but behaves differently depending on whats around it. So finks and masks both prove dispositions are relational properties rather than purely internal ones.

Moreover, if dispositions were truly intrinsic, then two objects with identical internal structures should always display the same dispositional behavior, but masking and finking prove they dont. So the intrinsic account fails."
