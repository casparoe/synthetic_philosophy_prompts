I'm trying to map the logical space of scalar moral-status views for a discussion group that thinks in terms of boundary cases rather than school labels, and I want a decision-tree taxonomy rather than a prose comparison. The tree should start from one shared commitment—that moral status is gradated, not binary, and that at least some non-human or non-biological systems sit somewhere on the gradient—and then branch through concrete cases until it lands on a named or precisely characterized position. The cases must be ones a respondent can evaluate without already knowing the taxonomy: for instance, "Does a crustacean with intact nociception but no cortical integration warrant partial status?" "If a synthetic system replicates that nociceptive architecture exactly but on silicon, does substrate matter?" and "Could a sufficiently complex feedback loop—say, a climate-control system with nested homeostatic goals—ever earn nonzero weight, or is biological origin a hard gate?"

Please include at least these leaves: a strict binary sentientist position that assigns zero weight to anything lacking full phenomenal consciousness; a capacity-weighted gradualist position where moral weight tracks some continuous measure like integrated information or affective range; a functional-integration gradualist position that grants partial status to any system with the right causal loops regardless of substrate, which should place sophisticated thermostats or synthetic analogues above simple reflex organisms; a biological-particularist hybrid that grants insects partial status based on neural architecture but denies it to functionally equivalent synthetic systems; and a skeptical or deflationary leaf that accepts gradation in principle but collapses all practical differences into negligible weights except at human-level cognition. For each internal node, phrase the question so it sorts by belief about the case, not by self-identification with a label.

Also, build the tree from the combinations of answers rather than from a pre-existing list of philosophers: show which answer patterns are occupied, note any empty combinations and briefly diagnose whether they seem incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored, and explicitly flag any pair of leaves that agree on every case you listed but diverge only on metaethical or metaphysical foundations that don't cash out in boundary judgments. The audience is rationalist-blog readers comfortable with conceptual analysis but not with philosophy-of-mind jargon, so avoid asking for technical citations or reading lists; I need the classification instrument itself, mapped to the logical space.
