I've been working through Hacking's account of looping effects and the distinction between interactive and indifferent kinds, and I keep returning to what seems like an under-discussed problem of ontological inertia in psychiatric taxonomy. If a diagnostic category—say, PTSD since its formalization in DSM-III—has undergone decades of iterative feedback, where clinicians refine symptom clusters in response to patient presentations and patients in turn organize their trauma narratives around the available criteria, doesn't the loop eventually reach a stable equilibrium? At that point, the classification no longer actively reshapes the people it describes in any significant way; it merely tracks a fixed clinical population. Given this, shouldn't long-looped psychiatric categories be treated, for the purposes of neurobiological research, as de facto indifferent kinds—stable targets for correlation rather than reflexively moving objects? Or is the very idea that looping can "settle" into mind-independent tracking a misreading of what interactive kinds do? I want to know whether equilibrium in looping ever produces the kind of objectivity that makes a category safe to freeze for empirical study, or whether that framing already betrays the logic of the loop.
