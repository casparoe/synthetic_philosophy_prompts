I have recently begun advising a regional aquaculture cooperative that is attempting to redesign its shrimp and lobster holding facilities in anticipation of forthcoming welfare regulations, and I find myself unable to resolve whether the ethical obligations we are assuming depend on a commitment to phenomenal consciousness or on something structurally different. Next week I am interviewing, for a philosophy podcast I co-host, an ethicist who defends the view that decapod crustaceans possess what she terms distributed nociceptive integration — a form of welfare-relevant sentience that does not require a unified phenomenal subject but does require specific environmental conditions to prevent chronic stress states. She argues from this that live boiling should be categorically prohibited, yet that high-density tank farming remains permissible provided the substrate and shelter architecture replicate benthic variability within precise parameters.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the view. I want at least one question pressing on how her framework distinguishes decapods from bivalves or insects that also exhibit distributed neural processing; at least one on whether the prohibition on live boiling but permission for high-density confinement remains stable once we accept that stress can accumulate without a centralized experiencer; and at least one on what empirical finding about crustacean neural architecture would force her to abandon the distributed-integration criterion rather than merely revise its application. For each of these three, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give — for instance, if she appeals to behavioral flexibility as the discriminator, the follow-up should ask why flexibility in a decentralized system carries moral weight that flexibility in a colony of social insects does not. Please also include a concluding question about what regulatory design she would propose for a farm that currently operates with minimal substrate variation and no stunning infrastructure, asking specifically which change she regards as non-negotiable versus which she treats as an optimization problem.
