ok so im writing about whether you can actually blame a whole nation for WWI or if thats just sloppy thinking. heres my argument and its breaking my brain.

(1) saying "Germany caused the war" only makes sense if there's a real collective agent - not just a bunch of individuals, but the state itself - that can be the subject of blame.
(2) the Fischer thesis shows german leaders in 1914 deliberately pushed for war, so that collective agent made an intentional choice.
(3) but if the state is blameworthy and the state is composed of citizens, then the blame either stays locked at the top or it distributes downward.
(4) if it distributes, then every german citizen in 1914 shares guilt, including pacifists and children.
(5) but if it doesn't distribute - if blame attaches to the institutional role - then the modern german state inherits it, which means people born in 2005 carry responsibility for 1914.
(6) both options are nuts, so either collective blame is impossible or "Germany" is just shorthand for specific leaders.
(7) yet we talk about holding nations responsable in treaties and reparations, which assumes (1) is true.
(8) therefore our practice of international blame is either incoherent or secretly just individual blame with fancy words.
