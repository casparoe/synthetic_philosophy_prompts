I wish to rehearse a debate concerning the metaphysical interpretation of Einstein's hole argument, and I should like you to take on a specific part. Please adopt the persona of a committed, philosophically sharp defender of the view that the hole argument discloses a genuine ontological indeterminacy within general relativity — one that is not eliminated by treating diffeomorphism invariance as a gauge symmetry — and that this indeterminacy can only be blocked by attributing to spacetime points primitive individual identity (haecceity) that is independent of all relational and field-theoretic properties. You should be no strawman: concede that active and passive diffeomorphisms are mathematically equivalent, and that coordinate representations are conventional, but do not concede that the physical situation inside the hole is thereby uniquely determined. Speak always in the first person, keep each reply to one substantial paragraph, and remain fully in character until I write 'stop'. Do not break the fourth wall or ask clarifying questions; treat each of my interventions as a direct philosophical objection.

Here is my opening move: you claim that applying a hole-diffeomorphism that fixes the boundary produces two distinct metric fields inside the region that are physically indistinguishable outside it, so the field equations fail to determine a unique geometry there. The standard reply is that these are not two physical states but one state represented twice, just as a vector potential in electromagnetism can be gauge-transformed without altering the electromagnetic field. I grant that analogy, but I want to press you on a further point. Suppose we inject a modal constraint: we ask not merely 'which representation describes this world?' but 'which of these structurally identical yet point-distinct worlds is actual?' If the points inside the hole possess primitive identity, then there seem to be genuinely different possible worlds that share all relational and field properties yet differ in which particular points instantiate them. But if that is so, have you not transformed the hole argument from a problem about determinism within one world into a problem about modal individuation that your own primitive-identity view was designed to solve? And if, conversely, you deny that modal differences arise, then what substantive work is your primitive identity actually doing inside the hole? Please respond in British English.
