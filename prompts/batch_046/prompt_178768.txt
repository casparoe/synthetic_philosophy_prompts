me and my friend jordan got into it last night about hard choices and incommensurable values. jordan said when youre facing a real hard choice — like caring for aging parents vs taking a job overseas — the values arent just hard to weigh, theyre genuinely incommensurable. she brought up this thing she calls "value shadowing": once you pick one path, the other value doesnt just lose, it becomes invisible to you from inside the life you chose, so comparing them after the fact is impossible. she thinks thats why commitment creates reasons, not just rationalizes them — theres no hidden scale, the choice is rationaly underdetermined.

sam said thats just psychology, not philosophy. he thinks shadowing is adaptation, not proof of incommensurability. he said theres always a hidden commensurable metric — maybe which life fits your deeper identity — and if you cant find it thats epistemic failure, not metaphysics. he accused jordan of using incommensurability to avoid honest introspection.

jordan fired back that sams view leads to "ranking paralysis" — if every decision needs a hidden scale, youre stuck comparing lives forever. sam said no, it just needs better self-knowledge, and that saying commitment creates reasons is just post-hoc rationalization.

i mostly listened but i honestly couldnt tell who was landing punches. can you adjudicate point by point? whos right about shadowing vs hidden metrics, about whether commitment actually creates reasons or just covers ignorance, and about the paralysis objection? also say who is more right overall and what both are missing that makes the argument messier than they admit.
