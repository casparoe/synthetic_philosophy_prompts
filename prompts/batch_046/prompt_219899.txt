I should like a poem that performs conceptual work rather than merely illustrating Dennett's taxonomy of minds. Write a poem of four stanzas, each comprising eight lines of loose blank verse, corresponding to the Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. The distinctions should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with Dennett's account recognises the Popperian capacity for internal hypothesis-testing and the Gregorian dependence on imported cultural instruments, without the poem becoming mere exegesis. Please adopt British English, take a committed interpretive position on whether these layers are successive or simultaneous, and append a brief note identifying precisely where the poem takes liberties with Dennett's framing or adopts a contested reading.
