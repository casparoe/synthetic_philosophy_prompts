Imagine that John Locke, having been conceptually transported into the present, is asked to assess whether a human being whose autobiographical narrative is continuously co-authored by an external, non-conscious language system—one that inserts synthetic memories, revises emotional interpretations, and drafts future intentions in real time—remains the same person over time. Locke held that personal identity consists in continuity of consciousness, particularly through memory. How do you think he would apply that criterion to this case? Would he judge that the human and the system form a single self with one center of narrative gravity in Dennett's sense, that they constitute two competing centers, or that the human's identity dissolves because the memory chain is no longer genuinely her own? Please construct Locke's likely reasoning in detail, addressing specifically whether an artificial co-author that lacks consciousness can nonetheless disrupt the continuity required for sameness of person. Then apply the resulting principle to a further case Locke could not have anticipated: an individual who deliberately erases all original autobiographical records and thereafter lives entirely within an AI-generated life story that she fully endorses. Would Locke regard this as the destruction of the original person, as the preservation of identity through adopted memory, or as the emergence of an entirely new center of narrative gravity?
