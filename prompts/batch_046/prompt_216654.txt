One camp says its just paternalism disguised as mathematics, that there's no stable 'true' preference profile hiding inside us. The other camp treats coherence and extrapolation as obvious requirements for any serious alignment scheme, and dismisses critics as confusing descriptive psychology with normative design. Every rebuttal seems pre-canned: 'human values change', 'coherence doesnt require fixedness', 'you're smuggling in an objective list of goods'. Is the stall because this is actually a verbal disagreement about what 'idealized' means — reflective endorsement versus rational reconstruction — or are they answering different questions entirely, one about AI mechanics and one about democratic legitimacy? I also wonder if there's a hidden empirical claim that neither side admits: that human preferances actually converge or diverge under prolonged reflection, and we just dont have the data to settle it. Or maybe the real bedrock difference is an intuition about whether it's okay for a system to override present-tense consent in the name of future-tense coherence. What's your diagnosis — verbal, substantive, hidden empirical, bedrock intuition, or ill-posed? And given that, is there anything that could actually move it: a formal model of intertemporal preference coherence, an experiment on value stabalization, or should we just admit the disagreement is structural and move on?
