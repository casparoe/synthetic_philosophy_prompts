Question: In Pensées, Pascal argues that justice is disputable while force is indisputable, which is why human societies necessarily combine legal norms with coercive power. He also distinguishes three orders: the body (physical strength and domination), the mind (intellectual authority and genius), and charity or love (holiness and grace). Assess whether the justice-force relationship operates uniformly across these three orders, or whether it functions differently—or not at all—in the higher orders. In particular, does the order of charity genuinely transcend the logic of justice and force, or does it merely relocate that logic onto ecclesiastical and spiritual authority?

Student answer:
Pascal's central insight in Pensées is that justice without coercive backing is powerless, since people disagree endlessly about what is just, whereas force produces immediate obedience. That is why every government pairs law with physical coercion. Pascal applies this framework through his doctrine of three orders. In the first order, the body, authority is purely physical: the strong command the weak. In the second order, the mind, authority derives from intelligence and genius. Here justice takes the form of merit: the brilliant person deserves recognition, granted through admiration and institutional rank rather than violence, though social sanctions still enforce the hierarchy. In the third order, charity, the saint possesses authority that is neither physical nor intellectual but spiritual. Holiness is recognized by grace, not by human convention.

Yet Pascal's framework ultimately collapses the distinction he intends. Even in charity, authority requires institutional recognition—the church determines holiness and has historically used force against heresy. Thus charity does not escape the justice-force pairing; it disguises it. Custom is the foundation of justice in all three orders, including the spiritual, because humans cannot access grace without conventional, enforced structures. So Pascal's attempt to place charity above justice fails: every order depends on the same combination of disputable norms and indisputable power.
