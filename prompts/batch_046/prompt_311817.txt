She's an anthropologist/philosopher who's been writing for years about Ubuntu and relational personhood — the idea that you aren't really an individual first and then related to others, but that being a person just is being in relation. She genuinely thinks the Western autonomus self is a harmful fiction. The problem is that marriage, legally speaking, is literally two autonomous individuals signing a contract, and half the guests are her colleagues who will notice if I pretend that's not awkward. The other half are family members who've never heard of Ubuntu and just want something that makes them tear up.

Can you write a five-minute toast that actually does philosophical work? I don't want decoration — I want the structure to depend on the relational view. Like, don't open with "Sarah has always been..." because that treats her as a pre-exisiting unit. But it also can't erase the fact that she and her partner are two seperate people with distinct histories. Maybe use the idea that in Ubuntu, the "we" doesn't absorb the "I"s but holds them in a kind of tension. It needs to comfort the family, land for the philosophers, and not sound like I'm assigning homework. Oh, and please don't cite specific texts or ask anyone to read anything — that'd kill the mood.
