Write a short story about an older sister who realizes her "I" has partly been running inside her younger brother's brain since they shared an experimental sleep-link implent as teenagers. Keep it domestic and quiet, not action-movie sci-fi. Draw on Hofstadter's idea that the self is a strange loop of symbols rather than a ghost tied to one skull, but twist it: only the emotional coloring of memories crosses the bridge, not the images or narrative, so she feels his grief but cant tell if the scene is hers. Include a moment where she debates telling him, knowing that if the loop is real then adressing him is partly adressing herself, and its unclear which skull made the choice to speak.
