I'm on the staff of a small municipal resilience office, and lately we've been pulled into these heated council meetings where half the room says our region is "collapsing" because the factory closed and the tax base shrank by forty percent, and the other half insists its just restructuring into something smaller. I sat in on a philosophy of history seminar last fall where the prof argued there's a real conceptual gap between societies that actually stop functioning and ones that just change into a form nobody recognizes, and I want to turn that distinction into a decision procedue my team can run in about 45 minutes when we're called in to advise.

The checklist needs to move in order: first test whether basic coordination mechanisms are still operating, then whether institutional knowledge survives the transition, then whether residents experience the shift as an irreversible ending rather than a painful adjustment. For each step I need to know what counts as passing, what to do when we don't have data, and exactly where we're making a value judgment disguised as a fact. Please flag the handoff points where the procedure stops and says "this is for the elected officials, not the analysts." Then apply it to two scenarios: one modeled on the Bronze Age rapid breakdown of a river-valley system after climate shock and trade collapse, and one modeled on the Maya long decentralization where the big urban centers empty but village agriculture and local ritual keep going. I want at least one case to come out "not a collapse" even though every instinct says it is, so I can see where intuition overrules the framework.
