I am trying to understand a particular passage in William James's Principles of Psychology, published in 1890, in which he distinguishes direct introspective acquaintance with one's own stream of consciousness from what is commonly called the psychologist's fallacy — the error of projecting one's own conceptual analysis of mental states back onto the raw experience and onto the minds of others. James seems to hold that we have immediate access to a pre-conceptual given, yet he also insists that every introspective report is already shaped by theoretical categories. I need help clarifying the exact relation he posits between this given and its conceptual framing, especially whether he allows that introspection can ever present the stream without distortion.

To sharpen the question, I want to read this passage against Daniel Dennett's heterophenomenological method, which treats all first-person reports about consciousness as narrative texts to be interpreted from a third-person stance and denies that any subject enjoys privileged, direct access to an inner realm. My difficulty is that James's warning about the psychologist's fallacy looks like an anticipation of heterophenomenology — both see our descriptions of mental life as theory-laden — yet James appears to preserve a special first-person authority over the given that heterophenomenology rejects root and branch. I am particularly interested in whether James's framework can accommodate the kind of confabulatory reports documented in split-brain research, where a subject offers a coherent but false narrative about an action initiated outside conscious awareness. Does James have the resources to treat such a report as a distorted interpretation of a genuine given, or does his commitment to direct acquaintance force him to treat the report as either accurate or entirely invented? And if heterophenomenology treats all reports, including confabulatory ones, as texts with no underlying given to be correct or incorrect about, does it thereby solve James's problem or does it eliminate the very phenomenon — a genuinely experienced but misdescribed stream — that James was trying to protect?
