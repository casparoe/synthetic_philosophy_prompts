I'm preparing my position for our philosophy of well-being seminar and want a rigorous internal consistency audit before I present. Here is where I've landed:

(1) I hold a phenomenalist account of welfare: what is fundamentally good for a person is the presence of positive conscious experience and the absence of suffering, with richer goods—flow, aesthetic absorption, emotional resonance—counting only insofar as they have phenomenal character.

(2) I reject Nozick's experience machine as rationally rejectable, and I think the refusal tracks a real deficit, not mere bias. Specifically, I hold that being the genuine causal author of one's achievements and standing in real relations to other people and the world are partly constitutive of well-being, not merely instrumentally valuable for producing good experiences.

(3) At the same time, I think adaptive preferences should not be corrected away in welfare assessment: if someone genuinely and stably experiences satisfaction in severely constrained circumstances—say, Parfit's Russian nobleman after giving away his estates—then they genuinely have high well-being, regardless of what an outside observer judges about their objective conditions.

(4) I also maintain that well-being is fully objective and interpersonally comparable in principle: there is a determinate fact about whether my life is going better for me than yours is going for you, even if precise measurement is difficult.

(5) I think there is an explanatory gap between complete physical descriptions of a brain and correct ascriptions of well-being, but I interpret this as an epistemic or conceptual limitation compatible with physicalism—analogous to the hard problem of consciousness—rather than as evidence for non-natural properties.

(6) Finally, I hold that death is bad for the dead person solely as a deprivation of possible future well-being, and that this requires no "experiencing subject" at the time of harm; yet I also think well-being is necessarily experience-dependent, so the harm consists entirely in the counterfactual loss of possible positive phenomenal states.

Please audit this package. Go through the commitments pair by pair and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and for each real tension rank them by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging. For each tension, specify what would have to give—whether it's a revision to one commitment, a qualification, or an abandonment of a principle—and identify the least costly fix. Then tell me which single revision to one commitment would clean up the most tensions overall. I haven't decided which view I'm most attached to, so don't assume I want to save (2) over (3) or vice versa; just trace what commits me to what.
