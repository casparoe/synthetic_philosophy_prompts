After a lengthy argument with a friend about whether every narrative of the past is doomed to become a story of progress towards the present, I should like you to write an essay on Whig history and the temptation to write the past as progress toward the present. Weigh the argument that selecting which events matter already requires present-day criteria, so that all intelligible history is inevitably Whiggish, against the claim that genuinely non-teleological explanation is available—particularly through path-dependent accounts of institutional change that treat outcomes as contingent rather than directed. Reach and justify a clear verdict: is Whig history an avoidable methodological error, or an inescapable condition of making the past understandable? In doing so, address whether acknowledging our present standpoint necessarily commits us to seeing earlier societies as deficient precursors to our own, and examine whether a rigorously anti-Whig history that refuses all evaluative comparison collapses into incoherence rather than achieving objectivity.
