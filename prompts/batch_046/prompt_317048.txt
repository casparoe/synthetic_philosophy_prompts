Write an exam question with a model anwer and marking scheem for an upper-level metaphysics final: 90 mins, 20 marks. Domain is Buddhist anatta and personal identity, but don't just ask students to summarize the skandhas. Instead, inject this specific framing: a "functionalist migration" analogy where karma is like software copied to new hardware—no identical parts, no permanent soul, but preserved functional architecture. The question should ask whether this analogy saves causal moral responsibility across lives or smuggles in a disguised self, and students must engage both the reductionist skandha analysis and the Buddhist rejection of eternalism (including why the pudgalavada compromise is rejected by orthodox Theravada). The model anwer should be about 600 words. The marking scheme needs five must-hit points with marks assigned, plus one-line notes on typical mistakes—especially conflating causal continuity with narrative identity. Make sure an A answer treats the analogy seriously rather than dismissing it out of hand, and keep it casual: no bibliographies, no citation lists, no obscure sutra numbers.
