Having recently retired from an experimental physics laboratory and begun a systematic study of epistemology, I am preparing remarks for a philosophy discussion group and want a consistency audit of my own position on the regress problem before I present it. I have tried to be honest about borrowing from traditions that may not mix well. Here is the package I have assembled:

(a) I hold a foundationalist core: justification for ordinary empirical beliefs must eventually terminate in basic beliefs that are justified non-inferentially—roughly the direct observational reports I treated as authoritative in scientific practice.

(b) At the same time, I find the Quinean holistic picture compelling: beliefs face the tribunal of experience as a corporate body, and I think the justification of any particular belief genuinely depends on its coherence with a broader network of background commitments.

(c) I reject circular reasoning in justification as vicious: a belief cannot ultimately depend for its warrant on other beliefs that depend back on it.

(d) I reject infinitism on psychological and practical grounds: no actual human being possesses, or could survey, an infinite chain of reasons, so justification cannot require one.

(e) I also hold that perception is conceptually structured—there is no pure given or theory-neutral sensory content that justifies beliefs independently of conceptual interpretation—yet I still want to maintain that seeing an object can justify a belief about it without explicit inference.

(f) Finally, I am an anti-skeptic: I take it that we genuinely possess knowledge of propositions about the external world.

Please audit these commitments for joint tenability. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are only apparent and dissolve on closer inspection, and rank the real tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally damaging. For each genuine tension, specify what the least costly revision would be and what exactly I would have to give up—particularly whether abandoning any one commitment forces me into skepticism or forces me to accept circularity. Then tell me which single revision to this package would resolve the greatest number of problems with the least overall damage. In your response, please distinguish what is broadly settled in contemporary epistemology from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any disputed interpretation—such as whether holistic coherence is genuinely circular, or whether conceptual structure undermines non-inferential justification—as if it were the consensus view.
