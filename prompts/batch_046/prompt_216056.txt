Why has the debate about cross-generational national guilt stalled so completely? Every time I read it people split exactly the same way: the Jaspers camp distinguishes criminal, political, moral and metaphysical guilt and says political guilt attaches to the state as an institution that outlives its members, so present citizens inherit repair duties without personal moral blame; the other camp says guilt requires individual intentionality and applying it to 'a nation in 2024 for acts in 1940' is a category error that scapegoats people who wasn't born yet. Both sides cite the same distinctions, neither budge. Is this just verbal — 'guilt' meaning moral desert vs institutional liability — or is the hidden disagreement about whether collective entities can even have diachronic properties across total member turnover? Maybe both sides are smuggling in opposite views about social ontology without defending them. I want a diagnosis using what each side actually says, not a survey. Is there anything — a formal model, a conceptual distinction, an empirical case — that could actually break the stalemate, or is the question ill-posed? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then reasoning, no preamble, no restating the question.
