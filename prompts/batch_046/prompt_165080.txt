I've been leading a discussion group on historical reasoning in policy analysis, and we've hit an impasse that feels philosophically deeper than it first appears. The debate concerns the so-called lessons of history—specifically whether analogies to Munich (the 1938 appeasement dynamics), Vietnam (quagmire and escalation patterns), and the Thucydides trap (structural power-transition conflict) illuminate present crises or systematically mislead them. One faction treats these analogies as compressed causal models: if the mechanism of rising-power anxiety and commitment traps operated in classical Athens and in Central Europe in 1938, they argue, then recognizing the same mechanism now gives both predictive and normative traction. Another faction grants the historical patterns but denies that analogies carry any independent epistemic weight; they claim we should rely on explicit causal models and that analogies are merely rhetorical devices that smuggle in unexamined similarity judgments. A smaller group tries to dissolve the disagreement by saying Munich and Vietnam are essentially normative frames—stories about what kinds of errors leaders should avoid—while the Thucydides trap is presented as an empirical regularity, so the three "analogies" aren't even the same kind of cognitive tool. Despite months of discussion, nobody shifts: we all accept the same chronology, the same statistical literature on power-transition conflicts, and the same logical structure of analogical inference.

Is this stall a substantive disagreement about international relations theory, a verbal dispute over whether "explanation" requires mechanism-transfer rather than pattern-recognition, or are the factions actually answering different questions—one about prediction, one about justification, one about rhetoric—under the single heading "do analogies help"? In particular, is there a hidden formal question about whether analogies can ever be evaluated independently of the causal theories that license them, or is the disagreement bedrock—rooted in incompatible intuitions about what counts as understanding in the first place?
