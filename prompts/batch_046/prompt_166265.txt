I'm preparing a discussion guide for an advanced seminar on philosophy of value, and I'd like you to act as a professional philosopher with expertise in ethics, philosophy of meaning, and metaphysics of value. Think carefully and systematically: begin by mapping the conceptual terrain—distinguishing objective-list, subjective-fulfillment, and hybrid accounts of meaning in life, as well as the core commitments of absurdism about cosmic indifference—before turning to the case below. Please write your analysis in clear, formal American English.

In our seminar, two students, Marcus and Leila, got into a sustained disagreement about a mutual acquaintance, Dr. Elena Voss, an analytic philosopher of mathematics who spends her days working on problems in set-theoretic ontology. Elena is a committed mathematical anti-realist: she holds that abstract objects do not exist mind-independently, and that mathematical practice is best understood as the construction of internally coherent formal structures rather than the discovery of pre-existing truths. Nevertheless, she reports deep, sustained fulfillment in this work, maintains close friendships and mentors students, and describes her life not as meaningful but as absurd but absorbing. Marcus argues that by standard hybrid accounts of meaning—on which a life is meaningful when a subject is actively engaged with projects of objective value—Elena's life clearly qualifies: mathematical practice exercises rational capacities, produces enduring structures of understanding, and connects her to others. Her anti-realist metaphysics, Marcus claims, is irrelevant, like a musician who thinks sound is only air vibrations but still values the symphony. Leila replies that this analogy fails because mathematics, unlike music, derives its specific value precisely from a claim to necessary, mind-independent truth; Elena's anti-realism therefore targets the very value-base of her practice. She introduces what she calls dislocated engagement: Elena participates fully in mathematics while denying that the conditions that traditionally justify its value obtain. Marcus counters that Elena can simply relocate the value-base—she can value mathematics as rational architecture or aesthetic form, much as chess can be valuable without tracking external reality—and that her refusal to do so is a philosophical mistake rather than a missing condition for meaning. Leila responds that this relocation is not available to Elena on her own terms: she refuses to reduce mathematics to chess precisely because she recognizes its traditional claim to truth, and she maintains that claim as the reason for her love of the subject, even as she denies its metaphysical grounding. Thus, Leila concludes, Elena's subjective fulfillment exists in structural tension with the objective conditions she takes to justify it, producing a practical identity split that pure hybrid theories cannot accommodate. Marcus insists that local endorsement is sufficient—Elena need not globally endorse the cosmic significance of mathematics, only engage with it here and now—and that Leila is smuggling an unnecessary cosmic-fit requirement into the concept of meaning.

Please adjudicate this disagreement move by move. For each of the four main exchanges—(1) Marcus's analogy to the musician and Leila's rejection of it; (2) Marcus's proposal to relocate the value-base and Leila's claim that this is unavailable on Elena's own terms; (3) Marcus's appeal to local endorsement versus Leila's charge of structural tension; and (4) Marcus's accusation that Leila smuggles in cosmic significance versus Leila's defense of the split—rate the strength of each party's position from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with brief justification. Then state who is more right overall and identify what, if anything, both are missing—particularly whether there is a coherent third position that preserves Elena's own framing without collapsing into either pure objective-list fulfillment or pure absurdist denial of meaning. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; base your analysis solely on the conceptual distinctions at play.
