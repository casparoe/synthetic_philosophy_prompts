Draft a formal policy memo addressed to the University Student Wellness Allocation Committee, on which you serve as a voting member, recommending a clear, actionable decision on whether the administration’s proposed “Unified Hedonic Index” (UHI) should be adopted as the primary criterion for distributing the annual mental-health and recreation budget. The UHI translates every student well-being outcome—physical exercise, social belonging, artistic engagement, sleep quality, and intellectual achievement—into standardized “pleasure-equivalent units” derived from self-report surveys, so that programs can be ranked and funded by direct quantitative comparison.

In the memo:
1. Identify the philosophical problem the proposal conceals: the heterogeneity problem for hedonism—whether pleasures of radically different kinds share a common qualitative feature that makes aggregation meaningful, or whether comparing them is like weighing colors.
2. Distinguish, as part of your analysis, “thin” hedonism (pleasure as a single homogeneous affective tone, like a uniform brightness) from “thick” hedonism (pleasure as a family of distinct affective states—savoring, triumph, relief, flow—each with its own phenomenology and normative conditions), and argue whether the UHI implicitly assumes one version and collapses under the other.
3. Present an explicit recommendation—adopt, reject, or adopt with strict conditions—supported by reasoning that addresses both the phenomenal uniformity of pleasure and the intentional/content-based differences among pleasures directed at different objects.
4. Give an honest account of the strongest consideration on the losing side: if you recommend rejection, explain precisely why the administration’s concern about arbitrary, politically driven funding does not justify false precision; if you recommend adoption, explain precisely why heterogeneity does not defeat quantitative aggregation.
5. Keep the document self-contained: no bibliographies, no reading lists, no citations to specific works or authors that a reader could not verify from the memo itself, and no requests for further research.
6. Use polished American English and standard memo formatting (To / From / Date / Subject / Re).

Write the memo so the committee could vote on your recommendation at its next meeting.
