As a retired biologist who has only recently begun to read philosophy of mind seriously, I have found myself returning repeatedly to Douglas Hofstadter’s dialogue involving Aunt Hillary — the conscious ant colony introduced in the Ant Fugue — and to the wider problem of whether a distributed biological system without a central brain might nevertheless constitute a single mind. Rather than a broad survey, I should like a defended agenda: among the open conceptual questions and nascent lines of work that bear on collective consciousness, which two or three deserve priority, and in what order?

Please address the following three issues, but do not treat them as equally urgent. First, whether functional integration across non-neural agents — for instance, the information flow mediated by pheromone trails and caste dynamics in an ant colony — is sufficient for phenomenal unity, or whether some further condition (such as a global workspace or a self-model) is necessary. Second, whether the scenario in which an external agent repairs the collective’s nervous disorders by removing individual ants implies that such a colony possesses an irreducible form of epistemic access or self-representation, or whether that appearance can be fully explained by statistical regularities in population behaviour. Third, what practical or ethical implications follow if we grant that engineered distributed systems — synthetic colonies or multi-agent artificial networks — could replicate the relevant integration, particularly regarding the boundary conditions of moral status.

I want a dependency-aware prioritisation: which of these questions is upstream of the others? Which are tractable through conceptual analysis alone, and which genuinely require empirical advances in biology or artificial life? Please defend your ordering with explicit criteria — for example, tractability, how many downstream issues depend on the answer, and practical significance. Identify also the line of inquiry that you regard as overrated: one that attracts attention but whose resolution would alter little else in the field, and explain why it should be deprioritised relative to the others. Write in British English, in formal prose, without offering a bibliography or reading list.
