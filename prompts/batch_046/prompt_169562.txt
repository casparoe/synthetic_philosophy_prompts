I've come to accept a fairly stark reading of Benacerraf's dilemma: it reveals not merely an epistemological puzzle for mathematical platonism, but a structural incompatibility between treating numbers and sets as causally inert, spatiotemporally unlocated entities and any knowledge relation that requires informational or causal contact. The standard platonist replies—reliabilist accounts, Gödel-style intuition, or structuralist deflation—strike me as relocating the problem rather than solving it, especially once you press that even structural knowledge requires some unspecified non-causal access relation. What would genuinely count against this assessment? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward: for example, a compelling argument that causal theories of knowledge fail for theoretical entities in physics, or that a modal-structuralist reconstrual—treating mathematical objects as positions in necessarily instantiated structures rather than as freestanding abstracta—dissolves the contact problem without collapsing into nominalism. I also want to know which of these a committed defender of the stark reading should concede as real damage, and which are just distractions. In particular, I'm unsure whether the dilemma's force depends on an overly restrictive 'contact' model of knowledge—if that model fails for institutional or testimonial knowledge in science, does the platonist win, or does it merely show the dilemma applies more broadly? Finally, assess honestly whether my position risks unfalsifiability: if every platonist reply is dismissed as relocation, have I insulated the view in a way that is itself a defect?
