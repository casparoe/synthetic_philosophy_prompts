I am preparing my capstone presentation for an advanced high school philosophy course, and I want to read Pixar's Inside Out through the lens of Integrated Information Theory rather than through the usual cognitive-science or psychoanalytic frames. In IIT, consciousness corresponds to a maximally irreducible conceptual structure—quantified as phi—generated by a complex in a cause-effect space, with the exclusion postulate ruling out overlapping conscious entities within the same substrate. The film, however, dramatizes Riley's mind as a theatrical headquarters operated by five autonomous emotional agents, each apparently possessing its own beliefs, desires, and perceptual access. This raises a precise tension: does the narrative structure treat Riley as a single IIT-complex whose emotional subsystems are merely unconscious mechanisms, or does it implicitly grant each emotion its own phenomenal field, thereby violating the exclusion postulate?

I am looking for a critical reading that examines specific scenes—the dissolution of Riley's personality islands, the moment Joy and Sadness are ejected into long-term memory, and the final reconstruction of the control console with a multicolored core memory—asking in each case whether the film depicts genuine integration (differentiation plus unification) or merely a committee of homunculi. Where does the movie's metaphor of a control room illuminate IIT's distinction between phenomenal and access consciousness, and where does it collapse into the very Cartesian theater that Tononi's framework seeks to eliminate? Most importantly, does the resolution, in which emotional plurality is preserved but unified under a new self-model, contribute anything to the philosophical debate about integrated information that formal arguments about phi-maximization and substrate independence miss?
