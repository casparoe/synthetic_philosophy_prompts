I serve on a university disciplinary review board facing a concrete case: a doctoral researcher violated a binding data-use agreement by releasing anonymized clinical records to an advocacy group, which used the material to document and correct a harmful institutional practice. The breach is admitted; the harm was real and has now been remedied. The board is split. One faction holds that enforcing the agreement—likely expulsion—is necessary institutional force, without which justice has no teeth. The other holds that applying that force here is tyrannical, because justice demands protecting the exposure of institutional wrong.

Draft the board's written opinion, recommending a specific course of action with a clear time frame. Frame the reasoning through Pascal's argument in the Pensées that justice without force is powerless, while force without justice is tyrannical, so that the two must be united—justice made strong, force made just. Do not simply choose one side.

Instead, examine a non-obvious structural possibility: can the board execute a sanction (force) while simultaneously publishing a formal declaration that the sanction is unjust in this particular case (justice), or must justification and execution remain inseparable for Pascal's synthesis to hold? Assess whether this split remedy—say, a suspended expulsion paired with a public institutional reform—satisfies Pascal's union or collapses into hypocrisy. Recommend one concrete option, treat the strongest objection seriously, and say explicitly what accepting or rejecting the split remedy costs the board's credibility.
