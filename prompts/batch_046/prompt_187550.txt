Following the death of my partner eighteen months ago, I have become preoccupied with the possibility that elements of her reasoning style, emotional framing, and linguistic reflexes have not vanished but have instead been incorporated—imperfectly—into my own cognitive routines, as though fragments of her “I” are operating on my neural hardware. This led me to the view, associated with Douglas Hofstadter, that a self is not confined to one skull but can persist as an active symbolic pattern distributed across other minds. I am preparing to interview an unnamed philosopher of mind who defends a rigorous version of this pattern-persistence thesis: she holds that an “I” is a specific relational configuration of active symbols that can, in principle, be reconstructed in any substrate capable of sustaining those relations, including another person’s brain, provided the host architecture does not distort the core topology beyond a recoverable threshold.

For a podcast recording, I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the view. They should press where the literature presses—especially on the boundary conditions for pattern-identity across different neural substrates, on whether a distributed self retains any phenomenal continuity, and on what happens when multiple imported patterns conflict inside one host brain. I want to inject a specific conceptual problem I am calling the “host-modulation interference problem”: if the host brain’s own emotional and associative networks necessarily reshape any imported symbolic pattern, then the reconstructed “I” is never the original but always a hybrid. Please include at least one question directly on this interference problem, followed by a follow-up keyed to her likely reply that hybridization is compatible with persistence so long as the core relational topology is preserved. I also want at least one question on the moral status of a distributed fragment—whether it can suffer independently and whether it can be wronged—followed by a follow-up keyed to her probable claim that moral status tracks functional coherence rather than biological containment. For each line of questioning, include the anticipated standard reply and the follow-up that makes it harder to deploy.

Please provide the questions in American English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, since I am preparing without access to external sources.
