I am constructing a character for a historical novel set in Jena during the final years of Gottlob Frege's life, and I want his intellectual architecture to be coherent rather than merely biographical. The character is a logician who keeps two notebooks: one elaborating a philosophy of language in which thoughts are objective, non-psychological entities that exist independently of any individual mind, and another recording political reflections in which democratic institutions are condemned as corruptions of an objective, pre-political national essence, with particular hostility directed toward Jewish presence as an alien contamination of that essence. I have been working from the understanding that Frege's own late private diary expresses precisely this second set of commitments, while his published logical work expresses the first.

The parallel I am drawing is structural, not merely personal: in both notebooks, something pure—logical truth, ethnic identity—is threatened by something contingent and intersubjective—psychological process, democratic negotiation. This leads me to ask whether the philosophical system can survive the biography. Specifically, if Frege's rejection of psychologism in logic and his rejection of democratic pluralism in politics share a common root in the insistence that certain realities are not constructed but discovered, does accepting the philosophical argument commit one to accepting the political ontology? Or can one defend the objectivity of thought—sense, reference, and the laws of logic—while entirely rejecting the parallel claim that a nation possesses an analogous objective identity that must be protected from constructed, intersubjective change?

What I need is not a defense of Frege's politics, but a diagnosis of whether the metaphysical picture underlying his logicism genuinely requires, or even suggests, the kind of essentialism that appears in the diary. If there is no necessary connection, I want to understand exactly where the analogy between anti-psychologism and anti-democratic essentialism breaks, so that my character's coherence can be philosophically honest rather than historically forced.
