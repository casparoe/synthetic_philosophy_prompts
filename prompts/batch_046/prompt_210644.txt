I'm preparing my position for a graduate seminar on distributive justice and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is roughly where I've landed: (1) I think inheritance should be taxed at near-confiscatory rates above a modest life-estate exemption, but my justification is not the standard Rawlsian fair equality of opportunity; instead, I believe dynastic wealth creates what I call a transformative lock-in problem, in which a child of extreme wealth faces rationally incomparable life paths that are so resource-laden that genuine transformative choice is structurally distorted. (2) I accept the non-identity problem in its strong form, so I deny that any particular child born into dynastic wealth can be harmed by the inheritance regime itself—there is no counterfactual baseline from which they suffer. (3) I nonetheless maintain that the state has a legitimate interest in protecting intergenerational option-space neutrality, meaning parental bequests should not be permitted to narrow the set of lives a child can rationally choose among, even if no specific child is made worse off relative to non-existence. (4) At the same time, I hold that standard fair equality of opportunity is purely comparative—concerned with relative starting positions across social classes—rather than with the absolute richness of any individual's option set. (5) Finally, I think it is permissible for parents to fund a child's specific transformative experiences directly, such as underwriting a prolonged monastic retreat or an entrepreneurial apprenticeship, but impermissible for them to transmit liquid capital that achieves the same structural effect through market power.

Do these five commitments hang together, or am I helping myself to an unstated premise? Specifically, I want you to examine whether (2) undermines the normative force of (3), whether (4) is compatible with the concern for absolute option richness invoked in (1) and (3), and whether (5) can be defended without collapsing into a simple anti-wealth principle that renders (1) redundant. Please rank the tensions by severity, identify which pair is only apparently in tension and dissolves on closer inspection, and indicate the least costly revision—whether I should weaken my non-identity claim, abandon the comparative view of opportunity, or draw a sharper distinction between direct funding mechanisms and capital transmission.
