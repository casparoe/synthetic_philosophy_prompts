Two mates in my philosophy reading group had a row about money pumps and diachronic rationality. (1) Liam said if you prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A, you're irrational because a bookie can pump you dry — that's the classic Dutch book argument. (2) Maya said that's only true if you face all three pairwise choices in quick succession; if you only ever encounter them in isolation, with different moods or contexts, there's no actual loop to be pumped through. (3) Liam came back that diachronic rationality requires your preferences across time to hang together, so cyclic pairwise preferences are still a failure even if no one literally walks you through the circle — you're treating your future self like a stranger. (4) Maya said he's smuggling in a hidden assumtion that there's a single 'true self' linking every choice, but if your risk-attitude genuinely shifts between morning and evening, forcing one transitive ordering across both states is like demanding you use the same scale after you've changed weight. She called this chronological chauvinism. Liam said that's just giving cyclicity a fancy excuse. Can you go through the four moves, rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say who's missing what, and tell me whether diachronic consistency ought to apply across mental states like that?
