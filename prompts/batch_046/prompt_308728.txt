Hey so I'm putting together an essay for this philosophy of mind discussion group I run with a couple friends (we're not experts, just people who like arguing about consciousness over coffee). The topic is gradual mind uploading - you know, replacing neurons with silicon copies one at a time over years. I know the standard debate is all Ship of Theseus and whether the final upload is "you", but I want to dig into something weirder that I haven't seen discussed much: the hybrid phase.

My idea is that during a long replacement process, say 20 years, you'd spend a long time being part-biological-part-synthetic with totally different processing characteristics - biological noise vs deterministic silicon, different latency patterns, maybe even different memory consolidation speeds. I think this creates what I'm calling "intermittent first-person authority" - periods where your self-model is integrating reports from two qualitatively different cognitive streams. I'm comparing this to split-brain cases, but stretched across time instead of across hemispheres, and I think the heterogenous substrate might break continuity in a way the endpoint-focused debates miss.

I want to argue that personal identity might fail not at the endpoint but in the middle - not because there's no continuity, but because there's no unified subject of experience during the hybrid state. I'm also wondering if observers who insist the hybrid is still "the same person" are committing a kind of bad faith about the ambiguity, though I haven't fully worked out that part. Could you comment on these notes? Particlarly: is the split-brain analogy actually sound or is it forcing a spatial model onto a temporal problem? Am I accidentally assuming a Cartesian theater when I talk about the narrative self integrating reports? And does this sound like a genuinely different angle on uploading, or am I just dressing up the usual continuity problem in fancier language? I'd especially appreciate feedback on conceptual accuracy since I'm not sure the neuroscience analogy holds up.
