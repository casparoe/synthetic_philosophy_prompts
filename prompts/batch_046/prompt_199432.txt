Specify the full set of desiderata that a decision procedure for moral uncertainty must satisfy, where the uncertainty spans both first-order normative theories and second-order meta-ethical frameworks—including, for example, disagreement over whether moral wrongness is a natural kind, a non-cognitive projection, or a response-dependent property. For each desideratum, provide the motivating case that makes it necessary (such as acting under time pressure when the ontological status of the relevant moral property is unresolved), classify it as either non-negotiable or relaxable, and indicate which popular decision strategies—expected moral-value maximization, a parliamentary aggregation across theories, or a hierarchical fallback to second-order norms—it threatens to undermine. Explicitly include a bounded-rationality constraint: the procedure must be computable by an agent who cannot settle the underlying meta-ethical dispute before acting. Conclude by identifying any pair of desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable and stating whether the conflict lies in the requirements themselves or in the candidate strategies.
