hey, i've been lurking on rationalist forums for years and i'm sick of seeing the same three-slide breakdown of norms of asserition (knowledge! belief! truth!). I want a proper decision-tree taxonamy that sorts people by what they'd actually say about weird cases, not by which philosopher they namedrop.

The tree should start with: do you think asserting "p" requires knowing p? Then split on belief-sufficiency. But the real work is in the niche splits: (1) the lottery-ticket case — you hold a 1-in-10,000 ticket, do you assert "i won't win"? (2) the expert-delegation case — a non-expert asserts a climate claim they read in a report; is that okay if they don't understand the mechanism? (3) the retracted-belief case — you still mentally assent to p but you've seen a convincing rebuttal; is saying p still wrong?

The leaves must include: strict Williamson-style knowledge norm; belief-only norm; justified-true-belief hybrid (the ugly middle child); contextualist knowledge norm; and the "no norm, just don't be deceptive" deflationary view. For every combo that doesn't get a named view, flag it as unoccupied and say whether it's incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored.

Deliver as JSON with these exact fields: "branch_question" (the concrete case/claim), "yes_path" and "no_path" (what each answer leads to, nested object or terminal leaf), "leaf_label" (name or "unnamed"), "leaf_tagline" (one sentence on its most awkward commitment), "is_occupied" (true/false), "vacancy_diagnosis" (why nobody lives there if false). Keep it to 6-8 splits max. No bibliographies, no "see Williamson 1996" — i don't have a library card. Make it for people who think about this stuff but can't search.
