Following the dissolution of my long-term partnership last year, I have become preoccupied with the metaphysics of time—specifically, whether the years we shared possess any present being, or whether only this current moment of absence is ontologically real. Rather than continue with abstract academic texts, I want a satirical piece that renders the presentism versus eternalism debate through a concrete personal lens. Write a parody of a licensed clinical intake form and session notes for a fictional specialization called "chronometaphysical couples mediation," in which one partner is a committed presentist and the other an eternalist, and every emotional grievance—memory, fidelity, anticipation, regret—is treated as a literal ontological dispute. The form should include numbered sections: 1. Presenting Problem; 2. Individual Temporal Ontology Profiles; 3. Joint Relational History Assessment; 4. Therapist's Clinical Observations (with bracketed annotations); 5. Provisional Treatment Plan. The voice must remain deadpan, bureaucratic, and clinically neutral; the humor should emerge entirely from the literal application of metaphysical commitments to relationship dynamics. For instance, the presentist partner should reject blame for past conflicts on the grounds that those conflicts lack present being and therefore cannot serve as truth-makers for current accusations, while the eternalist partner should treat anticipated future betrayals as already equally real with present ones, rendering preventive commitment meaningless. The therapist should attempt standard interventions—active listening, shared narrative reconstruction, "I" statements—only to have them collapse because the couple cannot agree on whether the events being discussed exist. Render at least two standard philosophical objections in clinical jargon without naming them: the presentist's problem accounting for true past-tensed statements should appear as a failure of "historical verification protocols" and missing "truth-maker documentation"; the eternalist's difficulty with change and temporary intrinsics should appear as "flattened affective valence across temporal parts" and an inability to distinguish present experience from future or past experiences of equal ontological status. Include an invented clinical instrument, such as a "Temporal Ontology Inventory" with subscales for Past Existence, Present Privilege, and Future Reality, administered to both subjects. The piece should be approximately 700 words, readable to someone unfamiliar with the philosophy but clearly recognizing the actual arguments to a specialist. Do not include citations, bibliographies, named philosophers, or references to specific texts; use invented clinical identifiers (e.g., "Subject P," "Subject E"). Begin the response with the completed form itself, with no preface, commentary, or framing outside the document.
