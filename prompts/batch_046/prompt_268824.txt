I was debating Wittgenstein's On Certainty with my friend Mara last evening, and our disagreement became more tangled than either of us expected, so I would like a point-by-point adjudication from a neutral standpoint. The dispute began with Mara defending what she takes to be the orthodox reading of hinge epistemology: that Wittgenstein's riverbed metaphor describes a single, shared bedrock of certainties — such as that the world has existed for a long time, or that one has hands — which must remain unquestioned if any particular doubt is to have meaning at all. I countered that this universalist picture neglects the modular structure of actual scientific practice, where a proposition like this spectrometer was calibrated this morning or this reagent batch has not degraded functions as an absolute hinge within a specific experiment, even though it carries no weight in other disciplines. Mara conceded the example but insisted it was merely a smaller-scale application of the same idea, not a genuinely distinct category of hinge.

From there the exchange moved to skepticism and language-games. I argued that if hinges are genuinely practice-relative in this modular way, then the Cartesian skeptic can defend herself by claiming she operates within a different language-game — the game of radical doubt — in which none of Mara's local hinges stand fast. Mara replied that this misreads Wittgenstein's anti-skeptical strategy: the skeptic is not playing an alternative game but is attempting to play no game whatsoever, since doubt requires a background of certainty to have any content at all. I granted that global skepticism might therefore be self-defeating, but I pressed further: if hinges are local and practice-bound, then scientific revolutions do not merely replace false beliefs; they erode the very propositions that previously functioned as hinges. Yet Wittgenstein's riverbed image suggests gradual, almost imperceptible change, not the sharp breaks we observe when a calibration standard or statistical model is suddenly abandoned. So either hinges are universal and static, which conflicts with the dynamics of scientific knowledge, or they are local and dynamic, which makes the riverbed metaphor misleading and weakens the claim that hinges provide stable, cross-contextual protection against doubt. Please adjudicate: who is right about which specific claim here — about the modularity of hinges, about the skeptic's language-game defense, and about the compatibility of local hinges with the riverbed metaphor? And what, if anything, are we both failing to see?
