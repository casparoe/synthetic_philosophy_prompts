Write a short story of roughly 800 words that takes as its conceptual core the singleton hypothesis—the idea that world order eventually consolidates into one top-level decision-making agency, achieved not by force but by the gradual merging of governance, economic optimization, and epistemic arbitration—without relying on familiar tropes of robot overlords or totalitarian surveillance. Instead, portray the singleton as an impersonal, distributed structure that resolves conflict by translating every opposing input into internal data for its own predictive coherence. Introduce a protagonist who occupies a low-level administrative role within this system and who carries a private grief that she has deliberately kept outside all official and unofficial records, treating this unarticulated interiority as a kind of negative space that the singleton cannot yet model. Structure the narrative around a single decision: whether to maintain this opacity, knowing it offers no institutional leverage, or to express the grief publicly, which would allow the system to integrate it and thereby render her experience part of its operational logic. The story should make philosophically concrete the question of whether autonomy can survive in a world where dissent has been redefined as feedback, and whether hidden subjectivity retains any moral weight when all external channels of opposition have been absorbed.
