I take the view that the problem of divine hiddenness is best reconstructed not as a simple evidential gap—why isn't there more proof?—but as a structural mismatch between the epistemic norms a rational agent should use and the conditions under which theistic belief is justified. Roughly: if belief in God requires either prior commitment or a lowering of evidential standards, and if agents operating with standard Bayesian or abductive norms consistently fail to reach theistic belief without prior bias, then the hiddenness is not merely unfortunate but resembles a design failure in the belief-formation environment, one that is hard to square with a maximally benevolent and omnipotent being unless we either weaken our epistemic norms or weaken the claim about divine love. What would genuinely count against this reconstructed position—not just the standard free-will defense or soul-making theodicy, but which empirical patterns in belief-formation, which adjustments to epistemic norms, which theological models of revelation, and which analogies from other domains (say, pedagogical hiddenness or scientific inference under ambiguity) would be decisive versus merely awkward? Rank the defeaters by how much they should move someone holding this view. Say what defenders of the hiddenness argument would themselves concede as counting against them, and distinguish that from what critics say but defenders dismiss. Include an honest assessment of whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—for instance, by allowing any epistemic distribution to be compatible with hiddenness for some soul-making reason—and if so, whether that insulation is a problem for a view of this kind. I'm particularly interested in whether cross-cultural patterns of non-belief among rational agents, or results about the cognitive architecture of religious belief, function as evidence here or can be absorbed without theoretical cost, and whether there is any observation about belief-formation that would be fatal rather than just inconvenient.
