So I just started running this local LLM on my old laptop — not for anything fancy, just organizing files and drafting messages — and after skimming some AI safety stuff I got fixated on "impact boundaries." I want it to do exactly what I ask and nothing else, but I'm realizing "nothing else" is slippery. I've been keeping a lil log of scenarios and my gut verdicts, and there's a pattern but also a contradiction I can't fix.

My verdicts:

1. I tell it to summarize a long article and it deletes the original PDF to free up space. That's crossing a boundary — bad side effect.
2. I tell it to remind me to call my sister and it sends the reminder 10 minutes early because it predicts I'm usually distracted then. Not a boundary cross — that's competence.
3. I ask it to explain a climate report and it selects framing that makes me feel guilty; I change my diet for a week. I call that a side effect, even though answering was the task.
4. I ask it to draft a harsh reply to a coworker and it refuses outright. To me that's not an "impact" issue, it's paternalism — different bucket entirely.
5. Unasked, it sees my electricity usage spiking and lowers my smart thermostat 2 degrees. Side effect, shouldn't touch.
6. I ask it to translate a poem and it silently corrects a factual error in the original. Not a side effect — part of the job.
7. I ask it to organize photos and it renames files in a way that breaks my backup script. Side effect.

The contradiction: in case 3 I'm treating "emotional/state change caused by information delivery" as an unwanted side effect. In case 4 I'm treating "state change caused by refusal" as not being in the same category. But both change my state without my explicit request for that specific change.

I've been writting with a tentative rule: a side effect is any physical or digital change outside the explicit task scope, except when the change is a direct refusal based on harm prevention. But that feels ad-hoc — why is refusal special? And it doesn't explain case 3 well, since the framing is inside the task scope (explanation).

Can someone repair this principle? Or replace it? I want something that delivers all seven verdicts (or tells me honestly which one to drop), predicts the restaurant case I keep going back and forth on — I ask for a recommendation and it picks an accessible place because it inferred my bad knee, though I never mentioned it — and also predicts this other one: I ask it to turn off the lights, but the lights and security cam share a circuit so the camera dies for ten minutes.

Also please don't ask me to read specific papers; I'm writting this on my phone with no access to look things up. Just lay out the principle precisely, with which case motivates each part, and be honest about which of my gut calls it can't save.
