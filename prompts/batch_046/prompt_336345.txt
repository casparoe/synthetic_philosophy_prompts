I've been trying to figure out what Keynes actually thought made life good, not the boring economic stuff but like, once you're not worried about money anymore. My friend reads these old essays and keeps telling me if different people are living the good life or not. I want to check if she's actually being consistent.

She's said:
- Retired banker, lives on savings, hangs out at museums and concerts all day: yeah, that's it.
- Painter living in a squat, barely eats, refuses to do ads or whatever, just paints: no, Keynes said you gotta have the money problem solved first.
- Surgeon who actually likes cutting people open, makes bank but lives simple, reads history at night: yep, that's the life.
- Regular office dude, 9-to-5 he hates, weekends just shopping and watching games, not poor but not rich either: nah, that's just being comfortable, not the real thing.

Now here's a new one: software developer hates coding, only does it for the cash, works 50 hours, but she's finacially okay and spends all her free time writing poetry and playing in some community orchestra.

What'd my friend say? I feel like there are two different rules hiding in her answers. One is like, you're secure and you're doing art or culture, and that's enough no matter what your job is. The other is like, your actual job has to be something you do for love, and you can't make up for a garbage job by having cool hobbies. Both fit what she said before but they'd disagree on this lady. So which one's she actually using, what does each say about the developer, and which should I guess? And what's one more example that'd actually show which rule is right?
