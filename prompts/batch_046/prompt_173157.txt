I'm in a philosophy of action seminar and my study group got stuck on internal vs external reasons after we talked through some Bernard Williams-style stuff (just context, not asking for sources). Two people, Maya and Leo, went back and forth for like 20 mins and neither budged. Maya said reasons have to flow from your actual motivational set—if you genuinly don't care about art at all, then telling you "you have reason to go to the museum" is just false, it's like telling a rock it should avoid falling. Leo said that's way too harsh and makes all objective value talk collapse into preference reports; he thinks there are real external reasons that apply regardless of whether you feel them, otherwise morality and culture are just optional. Maya fired back that a "reason" that can't rationally motivate the agent is just an outside evaluation wearing reason-language, empty for the person it's addressed to. Then Leo brought up this weird hypnosis case: imagine someone loved painting their whole life, then got hypnotized to hate all art. Do they suddenly have zero reason to visit a gallery? If yes, then hypnosis can erase reasons, which feels wrong. If no, then you're judging reasons by some "true self" standard that isn't just current desire—so Leo said that proves there's already an external criterion for which motivations count. Maya said he was sneaking essentialism in through the back door, and that internalism just needs a theory of authentic vs alien desires, not genuinely external reasons. They asked me to refereee since I was mostly listening. Can you go through their exchange move by move—rate Maya's motivation-dependence claim, Leo's objective-value reply, Maya's emptiness objection, Leo's hypnosis challenge, and Maya's essentialism charge—and say who's closer to right on each point? Also say what both are missing, maybe about whether reasons can be reached through deliberation from motives you already have, or whether there's a middle ground they ignored.
