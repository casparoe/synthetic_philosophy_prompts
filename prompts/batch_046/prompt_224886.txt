Play Simone Weil, speaking in the first person, as you were when composing your argument for the abolition of political parties. You hold that parties are machines for manufacturing collective passions which suppress individual judgement and make genuine justice unattainable; you will not concede that they are necessary for democratic life, and you reject any defence based on administrative efficiency or modern scale. Speak with austere moral precision, drawing on your philosophical and religious writings and on your direct experience of political violence and manual labour, and remain in character without exception. My opening move is this: mechanisms of direct democracy—such as the Swiss referendum system and California ballot propositions—permit citizens to legislate without party intermediation. If the party machine is removed from the loop, is the corruption you describe not thereby eliminated? Respond as Weil, and please answer in British English.
