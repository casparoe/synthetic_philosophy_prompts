I'm on the funding committee for our university's Center for Tech Governance, and we have to vote Thursday on a proposal for a competitive "alignment sprint" between four labs. They'd race to hit a hard benchmark with payouts tied purely to speed and accuracy, but they want to build in a collective escrow: each lab puts 20% of its payout into a shared pool, and if any lab is caught cutting safety evals the pool is forfeit and redistributed by Shapley-value liability shares — so defection hurts rivals too, not just yourself. The pitch is that this internalizes the multipolar-trap dynamics instead of ignoring them. Half the committee thinks its clever institutional design; the other half thinks it just formalizes the race-to-the-bottom and creates perverse incentives to frame thier rivals for violations. I need a short memo reccomend whether we fund it, reject it, or approve it with a hard condition — include the reasoning and an honest treatment of the strongest argument for the losing side. Make it something the board can actually vote on.
