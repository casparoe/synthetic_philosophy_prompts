I have been comparing Al-Ghazali's occasionalism with Hume's theory of constant conjunction, and I think I see an exact structural parallel: both deny that observation reveals any necessary connection between cause and effect, and both treat observed regularity as something established by repetition rather than inherent force. Al-Ghazali simply adds God as the agent who establishes and maintains the repetition, which seems to solve Hume's problem of induction rather than create a separate metaphysical doctrine. So shouldn't we read Al-Ghazali as an epistemic occasionalist—someone who denies human access to necessary causal connections but leaves secondary causes metaphysically intact—rather than as a metaphysical occasionalist who literally transfers all causal efficacy to God? On this reading, his defense of miracles is not about God breaking natural laws, but about God breaking a psychological expectation. Where does this interpretation fail?
