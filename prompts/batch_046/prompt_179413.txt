ok so i retired from a physics lab about three years ago and lately i've been reading metaphysics instead of running experiments, and i keep circling back to the persistence debate. i think i've landed on a view but want a consistency check before i talk about it at the philosophy meetup i joined. here's roughly where i stand:

(1) i'm an endurantist — when a tree exists on monday and tuesday, it's literally the exact same tree, all of it present both days, not spread out like some four-dimensional worm. (2) but i also take special relativity seriously, and since there's no universal "now" slice across reference frames, "wholly present at t" starts to look frame-dependent in a way that makes me uneasy. (3) i think change is genuinely intrinsic: the tree can be green on monday and brown on tuesday without contradiction, because the properties are time-indexed to the moments. (4) i don't think there are real "temporal parts" — they're just bookkeeping fictions, like picking coordinate axes. (5) for identity over time i go with causal-continuity-plus-similarity: enough causal connection and property overlap is all there is to being the same tree, no hidden substrate or essence. (6) i also think quantum field theory kind of undermines talk of little enduring particles anyway — they're just excitations in a field — but i still want macroscopic objects like trees to persist, maybe as stable patterns in that field.

so do these actually hang together? specifically, does (1) clash badly with (2) given relativity? does (3) need (1) or could a perdurantist say the exact same thing? is there a hidden tension between (4) and (5) — if temporal parts are fictions, am i secretly describing stages without admitting it? and does (6) pull the rug out from under (1) since fields don't "endure" in any obvious sense? please rank the tensions by severity, say which pairs only look like problems but dissolve on inspection, and tell me what single revision would clean up the most mess. also please seperate what is pretty settled in the literature from what's genuinely contested among people who actually work on this, and don't present one side as if everyone agrees — i know this area is full of disagreement and i want to see where the fault lines actually are.
