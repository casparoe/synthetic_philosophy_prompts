I just spent three hours arguing with a close friend about whether large language models are best classified as Popperian or Gregorian minds, and whether drawing that distinction illuminates anything about phenomenal consciousness or merely gives philosophers a tidy vocabulary for sorting algorithms. The conversation kept collapsing into the same reduction: my friend insisted that Gregorian cognition—using culturally transmitted tools like language or mathematics—is really just Skinnerian conditioning with a larger dataset and a longer feedback loop mediated by symbols rather than reinforcers. I think he is wrong, but I realized I cannot show why without first laying out Daniel Dennett’s four kinds of minds—Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian—not as a list to memorize but as a dependency ladder in which each new kind solves a specific failure mode of the previous ones. So I want to teach this taxonomy to a small interdisciplinary reading group—graduate students in cognitive science with no formal philosophy background, meeting for one ninety-minute session—to see whether the framework holds up or collapses into his reduction. Do not explain the taxonomy to me; plan how I should explain it to them. I need the precise order in which the four kinds must be introduced and why each depends on the prior being in place; the single carrying scenario that should thread through all four, together with what properties it must possess and lack so that the distinctions actually bite rather than blur; the misconceptions they will bring or form—including the specific reductionist claim that Gregorian is scaled-up Skinnerian conditioning, the assumption that Popperian minds must be conscious, and the idea that Darwinian selection applies only to phylogeny and not to behavior—and exactly where in the sequence to head off each; a check-for-understanding task at the end that would reveal whether they have grasped the dependency structure rather than just memorized four labels; and what I should explicitly refuse to explain or connect at this level—particularly any bridge from the taxonomy to phenomenal consciousness, to global workspace architecture, or to Dennett’s specific evolutionary mechanisms—so that the session stays clean and the argument with my friend remains suspended rather than prematurely resolved. Write this as a teaching plan, not as the explanation itself.
