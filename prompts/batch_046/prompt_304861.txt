ok so i've been trying to map hayek's knowledge argument against lange's trial-and-error fix, and i think i pushed it into a corner where it eats itself. here's the chain:

1. mises says socialist economic calc is impossible b/c without private capital markets you don't have real prices for producer goods — just shadow prices that miss actual scarcity.
2. hayek deepens it: market prices encode dispersed, tacit local knowledge that no central board can ever gather, no matter how smart they are.
3. lange replies that a board can run trial-and-error adjustments — watch surpluses and shortages, tweak prices — so it converges without needing all that knowledge up front.
4. but hayek's deeper point is the knowledge is constantly shifting; any price list is stale the second it's printed, and local actors adapt in ways the board can't observe.
5. apply the same logic inside a big capitalist firm: divisional managers hold tacit local knowledge, and headquarters sets internal transfer prices. those prices are just as stale and ignorant.
6. so if hayek is right, large firms should be impossible, or at least always worse than just outsourcing everything to the market.
7. but big firms clearly exist and often outperform pure contracting (vertical integration, etc.).
8. therefore either hayek proves too much — he undermines the capitalist firm too — or there's a hidden asymmetry that saves firms but not planning boards.
