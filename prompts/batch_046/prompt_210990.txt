The long-running dispute over transgender rights—spanning competitive sports, single-sex spaces, and youth gender medicine—shows little sign of resolution despite extensive empirical literature. I want a diagnosis, not a survey: is the deadlock mainly conceptual (the sides operate with incompatible notions of what "sex" and "gender" denote—biological kind versus social identity—so that policy arguments talk past each other), normative (a hidden disagreement about whether the burden of proof lies with those seeking inclusion or those seeking exclusion, particularly where long-term outcomes in youth medicine remain sparse), or empirical in disguise (a disagreement that a specific study could settle, and if so which one)? Focus especially on whether the debate over youth gender medicine is a proxy for a deeper conflict about the precautionary principle versus autonomy in conditions of epistemic uncertainty. What kind of progress—conceptual, empirical, or moral—would actually move the dispute forward, or does the structure of the disagreement suggest it is in principle unresolvable?
