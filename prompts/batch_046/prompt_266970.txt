Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for positions on the cosmological fine-tuning argument, designed as a diagnostic quiz in which each branch is a concrete claim a respondent can accept or reject without knowing the position-label it leads to. Structure the tree around four branching distinctions: (A) whether the observed confinement of fundamental constants—such as the cosmological constant and the Higgs vacuum expectation value—to narrow ranges permitting stellar nucleosynthesis and chemical complexity provides evidential support for an explanation transcending natural law, or is instead adequately explained as an observer-selection effect requiring no further account; (B) whether these constants are genuinely free parameters within our present theories, or are determined (perhaps uniquely) by a deeper, not-yet-formulated physical principle; (C) whether probabilistic reasoning over constant values requires, and actually possesses, a well-defined cosmological prior or reference class, or whether such assignments are conceptually illegitimate; and (D) whether "life-permitting" marks an objectively demarcated region in parameter space, or whether that boundary is partly constructed by the conditions of observation and our theoretical framing. The tree must locate five leaves: a theistic or teleological design view; an anthropic-selection multiverse account that treats other constant values as modally real; a brute-fact naturalism holding that no explanation is owed; a deep-law determinism denying that the constants could have varied; and one logically possible combination of answers that remains unoccupied, with a brief diagnosis (incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored). At each leaf, name the position (or label it "unnamed") and identify its single most awkward commitment—the claim that sounds strangest against ordinary intuition. Explicitly flag any pair of leaves that the tree fails to separate in practice, especially whether the multiverse and deep-law views differ in genuine explanatory satisfaction, and whether rejecting (A) in favor of selection effects can be sustained without collapsing into brute-fact naturalism when (B) is answered negatively. Do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists. In your answer, separate clearly what is physically settled (that observed values do fall within ranges allowing complex chemistry) from what is genuinely contested among specialists (the legitimacy of cosmological priors, the modal status of other parameter values, and whether anthropic reasoning carries independent evidential weight), and do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus view.
