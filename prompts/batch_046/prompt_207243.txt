Write a short story about a half-flooded archive where a council of exiled librarians—who turn out to be the same person at different ages—have to vote on whether to burn the last records. Frame it round Spinoza's idea that democracy is the most natural form of goverment because it best expresses the power of the multitude, but twist it: the 'multitude' here is just one divided mind arguing with itself across time. Show how the decision only becomes legitimate when every temporal self has a voice, not when one 'rational' future self imposes the answer. Make the choice concrete—maybe the books hold a map to a safe valley, yet keeping them draws raiders. Use British spelling throughout (colour, centre, realise), keep the tone gritty and unpolished, and don't include any footnotes or references—this is for a philosophy zine, not an academic paper. Just tell the story.
