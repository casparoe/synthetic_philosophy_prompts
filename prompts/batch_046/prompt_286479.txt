I'm drafting the metaphysical framework for a novel about distributed cognition and need an audit of my commitments before I build the plot's ontology. Here is where I stand on consciousness:

(1) I take Integrated Information Theory seriously: consciousness is identical to maximally irreducible integrated information (φ) in a physical substrate.
(2) I accept the exclusion postulate—within any region, only the complex with the highest φ is conscious—so nested consciousness is impossible.
(3) For the narrative, I want certain tightly coupled human-machine collectives to be genuinely conscious plural subjects, and I want the individual human members within them to retain their own separate consciousness.
(4) I am a physicalist, but I hold that IIT explains the structure and distribution of consciousness without dissolving the hard problem of subjective presence.
(5) I think the zombie scenario is conceivable and that conceivability, though fallible, is a genuine guide to possibility.

Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severely? Specifically, does (3) violate (2) if both the collective and its members claim high φ? Is (5) a hidden threat to (1), since conceivability of φ-identical zombies suggests that integration is not sufficient for consciousness? Is the tension between (4) and (1) merely apparent, dissolving once we distinguish structural explanation from phenomenal explanation? Rank the conflicts by severity, specify the cheapest revision for each, and identify which single change would restore the greatest consistency to the set.
