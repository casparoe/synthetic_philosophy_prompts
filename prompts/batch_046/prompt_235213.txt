I sit on the Clinical Neurotechnology Ethics Board of the Meridian Research Consortium, and we must vote by Friday on the data-architecture framework for our Phase-II brain-computer interface trial with minimally responsive patients. Our engineers have proposed two competing representations of patient experience in the consent and storage layer: Model A treats consciousness as a single, unified narrative stream—essentially a Cartesian theater with one canonical report at any moment—while Model B adopts Dennett's multiple drafts framework, storing parallel, competing narrative fragments without privileging any one as the definitive patient voice. As a software engineer without formal philosophy training, I need a brief, formal opinion memo for circulation before Thursday's meeting. Please recommend one architecture with a concrete pilot timeline and review checkpoint, explain the philosophical stakes in accessible language, and engage seriously with the strongest argument for the rejected model—particularly how each framework handles conflicting drafts, such as simultaneous reports of pain and calm. The memo should not request citations or outside reading, but it should help non-philosophers on the board understand what is at stake in the design choice.
