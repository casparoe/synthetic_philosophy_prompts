I'm drafting a scene where my protaganist — a novelist who used to be a grad student in history — gets cornered by a friend at a bar who lays out this chain about Marx. She thinks it proves historical materialism eats itself and then eats science too, but she can't find the exit. I need to know where the slip actually is, because I don't want her dialogue to sound like she's missing the obvious.

Here's the argument she keeps turning over: (1) Historical materialism says the economic base — forces and relations of production — determines the superstructure, so law, art, philosophy, and even science are shaped by material conditions. (2) But historical materialism is itself part of the superstructure; it emerged in 19th-century industrial capitalism. (3) So by its own lights it's not objectively true, just the ideology appropriate to that base. (4) Now extend the point: logic, mathematics, and empirical method are superstructural too. (5) That means each mode of production generates its own internal rationality, creating epistimic closures with no neutral, cross-base standard of evidence. (6) So a feudal historian and a capitalist historian can't genuinely disagree about shared facts — they live inside incommensurable frameworks. (7) But then historical materialism can't claim to understand feudalism objectively; it's just applying capitalist logic backward in a circular way. (8) So either the theory applies to itself and wipes out all comparative knowledge, or it carves out a magical exception for itself and becomes special pleading. (9) She wants to keep (1) and (5) — she likes that material conditions constrain how people think — but she refuses (6) because her novels depend on people across eras sharing some common ground. Where does this break? Which step should she reject, and what's the price? Can she keep base-constrains-thought without buying full incommensurability?

Please separate what's pretty settled in philosophy of history from what's genuinely contested among people who actually study this, and don't present one side's fix as if everyone agrees on it.
