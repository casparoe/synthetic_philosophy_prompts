I've been teaching a seminar on philosophy of science and keep encountering a defensive maneuver that doesn't seem well captured by the standard vocabulary. Here is the pattern. A research program makes a bold, precise claim—say, that a particular cognitive mechanism operates identically across all mammalian species. The claim is tested and fails in several well-documented cases. Rather than treating this as a straightforward falsification, or adding an explicit auxiliary condition such as "it applies except in social mammals," the defenders argue that the precise claim was never part of the program's genuine commitments. They point to ambiguous passages in the founding texts and to the program's underlying "spirit" to show that the failed version was always a superficial misreading by outsiders; the "real" theory is the narrower, unfalsified residue that remains. As further failures accumulate, the authentic core shrinks, but each shrinkage is presented not as a retreat but as a correction of prior misunderstanding. The upshot is that the theory becomes progressively less testable, not because new hypotheses are appended, but because its identity is reconstructed retrospectively around whatever survives, with textual authority used to recast anomalies as misinterpretations.

Is there an established name for this exact configuration—the combination of retrospective hermeneutic boundary-drawing, the shrinking of a theory's authentic content, and the rhetorical use of founding texts to make falsification impossible? I know it lives near Popper's immunizing stratagems, Lakatos's protective belts, and perhaps the Duhem-Quine thesis, but none of those seem to capture the specific move of rewriting the theory's biography so that failed predictions are expelled as inauthentic. If there is a standard term, how does it differ precisely from an ordinary degenerating research program? And if not, could you propose a careful definition that distinguishes it both from ad hoc auxiliary hypotheses and from plain equivocation, including clear boundary cases where a theory genuinely clarifies its scope versus where it performs this retrospective retreat?
