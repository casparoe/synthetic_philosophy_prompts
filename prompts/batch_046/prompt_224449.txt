I spent the evening in a long argument with a friend about transformative experience, and I want to turn our disagreement into a usable classification tool. I am writing a decision-tree taxonomy for a small philosophy blog, aimed at readers who know the literature but also at intelligent non-specialists. Please construct the tree so that each branch is labelled by the concrete claim or case that splits it, not by a theorist’s name, and each leaf carries a position name (or ‘unnamed’ if unoccupied) together with one sentence stating its most awkward commitment. The tree must distinguish, in this order: (1) whether the pre-transformative agent can rationally choose the transformation at all; (2) whether the post-transformative self is best understood as a ‘successor agent’ that bears no welfare obligations to its predecessor; (3) whether well-being is measured on a single metric that survives the change or whether the metric itself is replaced; and (4) whether testimony from those who have undergone the change can bridge the epistemic gap, and if so whether such testimony is systematically distorted. Ground each split in a concrete case — becoming a parent, receiving a cochlear implant after lifelong deafness, or adopting an effective-altruist moral framework after years of hedonism. Where a combination of answers yields an unoccupied leaf, note it and briefly diagnose whether the gap is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Paul, Campbell, and Ullmann-Margalit should appear as named leaves where they genuinely belong, but build the logical space first. Separate what is settled in the debate from what remains genuinely contested, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus. Write in British English, keep the whole response under 350 words, and do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
