I'm moderating a podcast interview with a philosopher who defends what she terms the archival self-indication principle (ASIP): observer-moments are weighted by present epistemic accessibility rather than raw cardinality, combined with a temporal cutoff that discounts observers in distant future branches, and she treats the presumptuous-philosopher objection as a category error about indexical access rather than about prior probabilities. Write me a sequence of interview questions that turns on the actual mechanics of her view, avoiding generic biography openers and without asking for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.

Begin by asking how the accessibility-weighting function is formally specified—whether it operates on information-theoretic reach, causal connectivity, or something else—and how it avoids collapsing into either standard self-sampling with a gerrymandered reference class or unqualified self-indication. Follow with a question on the Doomsday argument: does the temporal cutoff suppress the usual SIA-driven expectation of near-term extinction, or does it merely relocate the inference to a different temporal boundary? Then anticipate her likely reply that ASIP is just SSA with a restricted class, and include the follow-up that presses why counterfactual accessibility—rather than causal reach—should determine membership, and whether that makes the reference class depend on contingent features of our epistemic situation.

Next, pose a scenario in which a simulation archive contains many duplicated minds but partial access logs have been erased: ask whether the erased copies contribute to the weight, what principle distinguishes them from accessible ones, and whether her answer commits her to counting physically identical but epistemically isolated observers differently. After that, ask what formal result or cosmological finding—say, about the actual distribution of observer-moments in eternal-inflation or cyclic models—would compel her to drop the temporal cutoff rather than revise the accessibility metric. End with a question about predictive divergence: is there any observable consequence, even in principle, on which ASIP and a suitably qualified version of SSA genuinely disagree, or do the two views converge on every testable claim? Make the questions progressively harder, press where the literature on anthropic reasoning actually presses, and include the anticipated reply and its follow-up in at least two of the questions.
