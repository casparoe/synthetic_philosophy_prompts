After a long and somewhat heated argument with a friend about transcendental arguments, I want an outside check on whether his summary is trustworthy. He characterized what he called Strawson’s argument for other minds—drawn, he said, from the analysis of self-ascription—as the claim that because mental-state terms are learned and applied within a public language, and because public language necessarily presupposes other speakers, one cannot consistently ascribe mental states to oneself while denying them to everyone else. He then argued that this collapses into a linguistic version of the private-language objection, that it illicitly transforms a contingent sociological fact about language acquisition into a necessary conceptual condition, and that it begs the question by assuming the existence of other people precisely in order to prove it. From these premises he concluded that transcendental arguments generally confuse conditions for the intelligibility of experience with empirical causal conditions, and that Strawson’s version in particular commits a category mistake by deriving an ontological conclusion—that other minds exist—from a purely conceptual premise about the rules of language use. I have the sense that he is conflating distinct arguments and overstating the ontological ambition of the view, but I am not sure where the line falls between accurate reconstruction and misrepresentation. Could you audit his summary: which parts correctly capture the reasoning—particularly the claim that self-ascription of mental predicates requires the prior possibility of other-ascription within a unified concept of a person—versus which parts conflate it with unrelated positions, misstate its modal status, or present standard objections as decisive refutations? And once the argument is restored to its proper aim—establishing a condition of possibility for self-reference rather than offering a causal proof—does his charge of circularity and category mistake still land, or does it depend on the very misreading he has given?
