Second, introduce the distinction between treating one's own reasoning as an evidential indicator of others' reasoning (correlational symmetry) versus treating it as a causally independent choice. Third, defend the claim that a superrational agent in Platonia should condition on the correlation device constituted by shared rationality itself, yielding a deterministic abstention equilibrium without invoking mixed strategies or violating symmetry, because entering provides evidential support that others enter, which lowers the expected value below the threshold for rational action. Fifth, conclude by addressing the objection that this resolution merely renames the original paradox: show why separating evidential from causal symmetry-breaking is not verbal but changes the normative recommendation, and specify what empirical or formal feature—such as a measurable difference in correlation strength—would vindicate your account over standard game-theoretic treatments.
