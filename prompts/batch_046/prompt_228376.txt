Write an essay on whether superrationality remains a coherent normative standard in light of the Platonia dilemma and Hofstadter's Luring Lottery. Weigh the standard argument—that in symmetric games with common knowledge of rationality, identical agents should converge on the same strategy—against the Platonia problem, where this reasoning seems to drive all players to defect even though mutual cooperation yields a better outcome, and against the Luring Lottery's empirical demonstration that actual participants largely abandoned cooperative reasoning and flooded the contest with entries. Reach and justify a concrete conclusion: should we treat superrationality as a valid but narrowly applicable heuristic, or as a fundamentally broken ideal? Crucially, incorporate the often-overlooked issue that superrationality requires not merely that players be rational and symmetric, but that each assigns near-certainty to the other recognizing that symmetry; if two agents differ even marginally in their confidence about this meta-level recognition—say, 0.85 versus 0.65—then the sameness assumption fractures and the argument for identical choices collapses. Make this heterogeneity in meta-confidence central to your verdict, not an aside, and keep the focus on conceptual structure rather than historical detail.
