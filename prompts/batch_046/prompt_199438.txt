I'm preparing remarks for a graduate seminar on historical philosophy of value and want a consistency check on my interpretation of Nietzsche's genealogy of morality before I present. Here is roughly where I stand. First, I take the genealogical method to be descriptively powerful but normatively inert: showing that Christian morality arose from slave ressentiment explains its origin without, by itself, justifying or debunking it. Second, I understand ressentiment not as an individual psychological state but as a structural, social-affective orientation of the weak toward the strong, which drives the slave revolt in morality. Third, I hold that master morality is best understood as non-comparative self-affirmation: the noble type evaluates himself positively without reference to the slave, so the good-bad distinction in the slave mode is a reactive inversion rather than a parallel taxonomy. Fourth, I nevertheless maintain that revealing a value-system's origins in reactive, life-denying dynamics gives us a defeasible, non-circular reason to reject it, which means genealogy does carry normative weight. Fifth, I think Nietzsche's own appeal to life-affirmation as an evaluative criterion is either a hidden realist commitment or an unjustified exception to his genealogical skepticism, since he applies it without having genealogically legitimized it. Sixth, I want to insist that philosophical explanations of collective value-change should avoid reducing institutional shifts to individual mental states.

Please audit these commitments: identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and rank the tensions by severity. Specifically, I want to know whether the structural reading of ressentiment in my second commitment dissolves the tension with my sixth; whether my first commitment can survive alongside my fourth without collapsing into a full debunking argument; and whether my third commitment is compatible with my fifth, given that an external standard like life-affirmation seems to import the very comparative framework that master morality is said to lack. For each real tension, state what must be revised and what the least costly fix would be; then tell me which single revision would resolve the most inconsistencies overall. Do not treat every pair as equally problematic—discriminate between surface conflicts and deeper ones.
