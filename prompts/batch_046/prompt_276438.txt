I'm serving as a TA for an upper-division seminar on moral philosophy and historical change, and I've been asked to grade a response to our midterm essay prompt. The course has been working through Bernard Williams's skeptical remarks about rationalist narratives of moral progress—particularly his claim that shifts in honor and shame often do the work that philosophers mistakenly attribute to argument—alongside Kwame Anthony Appiah's analysis in The Honor Code of how honor codes can drive the abandonment of practices like dueling and foot-binding. I want to be fair but also catch the kind of subtle conceptual slip that a rushed grader might let slide, so I'd like a point-by-point assessment rather than a general impression.

Here is the exam question: "Compare Bernard Williams's and Kwame Anthony Appiah's explanations for the disappearance of dueling in Britain. Specifically, address whether Appiah's honor-based account conflicts with Williams's claim that moral revolutions rarely result from rational argument, and whether an interest-based explanation (for instance, state consolidation of violence or rising costs of social exclusion) can be cleanly separated from the honor dynamics both authors highlight. Use dueling as your central case, but feel free to reference Appiah's other examples if they clarify the structure of the explanation."

Here is the student's answer: "Williams argues that moral change happens mainly through shifts in what people find honorable or shameful, not through rational debate. He thinks dueling disappeared because people started seeing it as cowardly rather than brave, which is an emotional shift, not an argument. Appiah agrees but adds that honor itself works like an interest: people want status, so when dueling stops giving status and starts taking it away, they quit. This shows that Appiah is really giving an interest-based explanation dressed up in honor-language. Meanwhile, Williams thinks rational arguments never work, which is why he and Appiah both reject economic explanations like the state banning dueling. So their views are basically the same: honor drives change, and interests and arguments are irrelevant."

Please assign a letter grade and justify it with specific comments on each part of the answer—what is right, what is subtly wrong, and whether any error is serious enough to lower the grade significantly. In particular, check for misreadings of Williams's scope (does he really say arguments never work?), for whether Appiah's honor mechanism has been collapsed into bare status-seeking, and for whether the student has illegitimately inferred that the two authors share an identical framework and jointly reject all institutional or economic factors. Please write in American English, and include a brief note on what would change your assessment—specifically, which feature of the answer you would need to interpret differently to shift the grade, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail if you are grading hastily.
