I teach a 75-minute graduate seminar in political philosophy on AI governance, with 14 students. Design a structured role-play or deliberation that makes participants feel the aggregation-neutrality bind: any mechanism that combines citizens’ incommensurable value frameworks into a single decision rule for an aligned AI implicitly imposes a hidden meta-metric, violating liberal neutrality, while strict neutrality makes collective coordination impossible. Assign each student a role with a private, non-inter-translatable value-currency—such as dignity-units, autonomy-weights, or harmony-coefficients—and task the group with jointly selecting an AI allocation rule for a scarce emergency-response resource. The rules and payoff scheme should use graded reputation tokens or course credit rather than money, and should be arranged so that participants first experience the failure themselves: either they smuggle in an unacknowledged exchange rate to reach agreement, or they deadlock when neutrality is enforced. Include a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto the formal distinction between value aggregation and liberal neutrality; predicts what will go wrong on the first run (for instance, students inventing an implicit common currency without noticing); explains how to handle that failure; and frankly states which feature of the bind—the genuine irreducible incommensurability of the goods—cannot be fully reproduced in the classroom and must be supplied by the facilitator. Write the response in American English.
