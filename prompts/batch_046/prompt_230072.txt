Write a short story of roughly 500–700 words that dramatizes Nāgārjuna’s tetralemma (catuskoti) and the Madhyamaka view of emptiness (śūnyatā) without resorting to traditional religious imagery. Set the narrative in a near-future archival institute where a curator must catalogue an artifact—say, a boundary marker or an unplayable sound recording—that systematically evades all four logical positions: it is not simply present, absent, both, or neither in any fixed, essential way. The curator should begin by trying to resolve the ambiguity through standard binary classification, but the plot should demonstrate how each assignment of an inherent nature (svabhāva) collapses once the artifact’s relational, conventional, and linguistic conditions are exposed. The resolution should be epistemic rather than mystical: the curator recognizes that the cataloguing system itself—its database fields, ontological tags, and definitional language—presupposes independent existence, and that understanding emptiness requires seeing the artifact as dependently originated. Avoid citations, scholarly references, or explicit doctrinal exposition; let the philosophical structure emerge through character, setting, and dialogue. Keep the tone literary and precise.
