I just finished a long, somewhat exhausting argument with a friend about whether Hume's essay Of the Standard of Taste actually solves the problem of aesthetic disagreement or merely hides it behind an appeal to qualified judges. We kept circling back to the passage in which Hume claims that beauty exists only in the mind and that different people naturally feel different sentiments toward the same object, yet insists that there is a legitimate standard of taste established by those who possess delicacy of imagination, freedom from prejudice, and practice in comparison. My friend thinks Hume is being circular: he defines the correct sentiment as the one produced by qualified judges, then defines qualified judges as those who produce the correct sentiment. I think the circularity is broken by Hume's appeal to a shared human nature and to the correction of particular circumstances, but I am not sure whether that correction is supposed to be an empirical psychological process or a normative ideal that actual people rarely reach.

Could you help me understand exactly how Hume moves, in this passage, from the admission that taste is subjective to the claim that some judgments are genuinely defective? I am especially interested in whether his notion of taking a general point of view—stripping away the particularities of one's situation—is meant to function as a psychological description of how refined taste develops, or as a normative prescription for how any rational agent ought to evaluate art. And please address this further complication: my friend raised the case of deliberately dissonant or discomforting art—work designed to violate conventional harmony rather than to please—asking whether Hume's framework must classify all such responses as defects of taste, or whether it leaves room for a qualified judge to approve of discomfort as a legitimate aesthetic achievement. I want to understand whether that challenge exposes a real limitation in Hume's view or whether it misunderstands what delicacy of sentiment is supposed to detect.

Please say how confident you are in your interpretation, and single out the point about which you are least sure—particularly whether Hume's appeal to a shared human nature can genuinely ground normativity without collapsing into the circularity my friend described.
