Write a villanelle from the point of view of the tortoise in Carroll’s dialogue with Achilles, using these two refrains: “Every rule I use must itself be used,” and “The ground keeps sinking one step more below.” The poem should treat the regress not as a mere logical trick but as a structural feature of justification itself: each stanza should present a new premise or meta-rule that Achilles offers, only for the refrains to show that accepting it requires accepting something further. Use the villanelle’s compulsive return and final couplet to enact the infinite deferral—each repetition of the refrains should land in a slightly different logical position, so that when they finally meet in the closing lines they stand side by side without resolution, the tortoise suspended between compliance and refusal. The voice should be patient rather than perverse, the logic should be accurate enough that a philosopher of logic recognizes the failure of the hypothetical syllogism, and the form should make the reader feel the anxiety of never reaching bedrock, as though every authorization merely postpones the need for another.
