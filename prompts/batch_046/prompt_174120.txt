For as long as I have followed rationalist blogs and forums, the argument over evolutionary psychology has seemed stuck in the same loop: proponents point to cross-cultural regularities in mating preferences and to the predictive success of specific hypotheses, such as mechanisms of sex-differentiated jealousy, while critics—drawing on Gould and Lewontin's critique of adaptationism—argue that any observed behavior can be retrofitted with an adaptive narrative and that the field systematically avoids risky predictions about genuinely novel phenomena. Both sides appear to share the same evolutionary biology; they differ on whether explaining the functional design of an existing trait counts as science when direct experimental manipulation of Pleistocene selection pressures is impossible. My own hypothesis is that the stall is mainly about competing epistemic standards for historical sciences—defenders treat ultimate-causal explanation as sufficient, critics demand the forward predictive power characteristic of experimental psychology—rather than a hidden empirical dispute about whether mental architecture is genuinely modular or whether cultural transmission routinely swamps genetic selection. But I may be missing something: perhaps the disagreement conceals a substantive clash about neural plasticity, about the boundary between evolutionary psychology and human behavioral ecology, or about whether evolutionary mismatch renders Pleistocene-adapted mechanisms irrelevant to modern behavior. I want a diagnosis of the disagreement's actual structure: is it verbal, turning on different definitions of scientific explanation; is it a difference in evidential standards that neither side can justify without begging the question; is there an unacknowledged empirical issue—say, about gene-culture coevolution or the prevalence of domain-specific modules—doing the real work; or are the camps answering different questions, one about ultimate causation and one about proximate mechanism, under the single label of evolutionary psychology? Given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as genuine progress: a formal model that constrains adaptive storytelling, an experimental result that forces one side to concede, a conceptual distinction that separates evolutionary psychology from cultural evolution, or a reason to think that no resolution is possible because the disagreement is bedrock.
