I chair the scientific advisory subcommittee of the International Biosafety and Ontological Classification Board, and we must issue a binding recommendation before next month's plenary session on whether a newly engineered synthetic system falls within regulatory protocols for living organisms. The case concerns enclosed lipid-vesicle networks—synthetic protocells—that maintain chemical homeostasis through artificial organometallic catalysts, store information in a non-canonical nucleic acid analog rather than DNA or RNA, replicate with heritable mutation, and autonomously repair membrane damage, yet they lack ribosomes, share no evolutionary ancestry with terrestrial life, and cannot exchange genetic material with natural biota. Our researchers argue for classification as non-living chemical systems to avoid containment mandates; our biosafety officers counter that organizational closure and open-ended evolutionary capacity are sufficient for life regardless of biochemical substrate. I need a formal decision memo recommending a concrete classification—living organism, non-living chemical system, or a novel intermediate regulatory tier—with enforceable conditions, and it must treat the strongest opposing view with intellectual honesty rather than dismissal. The memo should not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; it should be constructible from conceptual analysis alone.

To frame the philosophical stakes precisely, please evaluate the case through three definitional lenses: metabolic continuity (the classical biochemical view), evolutionary potential with heritable variation (the Darwinian-functional view), and organizational closure with autonomous boundary maintenance (the autopoietic view), but also address a less commonly invoked criterion—what philosophers of biology sometimes term normative autonomy, or the capacity of a system to establish and maintain its own conditions of persistence as a functional norm rather than merely a causal loop. Your recommendation should specify exactly which criteria the synthetic protocells satisfy, which they fail, and whether failure of biochemical ancestry should be disqualifying if the other conditions are met, particularly in light of the fact that these entities exhibit evolutionary dynamics in isolation but remain evolutionarily isolated from natural biota.

Please structure your response as follows: Executive Recommendation (approximately 150 words), Analysis of Definitional Criteria (approximately 300 words), The Strongest Counter-Argument (approximately 200 words), Proposed Regulatory Conditions (approximately 150 words), and Conclusion (approximately 100 words). Lead with the bottom-line recommendation, then proceed through the sections in that exact order.
