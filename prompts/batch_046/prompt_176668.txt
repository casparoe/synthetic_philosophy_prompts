A philosopher of religion is applying al-Ghazālī’s occasionalist metaphysics to classify modern explanations of correlation and production. Her rulings so far are these. First, when fire is placed adjacent to dry cotton and burning occurs, she holds that adjacency is only an occasion: God creates the burning directly, and we speak of fire “causing” it solely within the customary course of nature. Second, when a physician administers an antidote and the poison is neutralized, she treats the antidote the same way—as an occasion within the habitual order, not an independent efficient cause. Third, when a teacher explains a geometric proof and a student comes to understand it, she rules that the teacher’s rational communication genuinely produces understanding in the student’s soul; while God sustains both intellects, mental acts of teaching possess a real, though fully dependent, causal efficacy that mere physical adjacency lacks. Fourth, when a predictive algorithm processes atmospheric data and rain follows, she judges the algorithm to be part of the descriptive customary pattern, with God creating both the data sequence and the rainfall in sequence. Fifth, when a pre-programmed surgical robot moves a blade and tissue is incised, she classifies the robot’s motion as an instrumental occasion within artificial mechanisms, denying that gears or motors possess causal power in their own right, and attributing the cutting directly to divine creation upon blade-tissue contact.

The new case is this. A neuroprosthetic implant detects a patient’s mental intention to utter a specific word and, bypassing any conscious articulatory step, directly stimulates the vocal cords to produce the corresponding sound. The patient forms the intention “hello,” and the device immediately generates the acoustic output, with no intermediate voluntary muscle movement.

Applying her framework consistently, should she rule that (i) the mental intention is merely an occasion and the device merely an instrument, so that God directly creates the sound with neither mind nor machine exercising true causation; (ii) the mental intention genuinely causes the utterance through the device, extending the special efficacy she grants to rational mental acts into the physical domain via instrumentation; or (iii) that the artificial mediation breaks the customary link between mind and speech, leaving only direct divine creation with neither mental nor mechanical occasion playing even an occasional role? Identify which of her five earlier rulings is the closest analog. State whether her track record genuinely underdetermines the case, and if so, articulate the precise metaphysical principle she would be adopting with each of the two leading choices. Finally, propose one further hypothetical case—one that does not involve speech or prosthetics—that would definitively distinguish the principle behind choice (ii) from the principle behind choice (i).
