Write a deadpan bureaucratic memo, roughly 600 words, formatted as an annual performance review issued by a fictional "Inter-Agency Council on Historical Causation." The subjects under review are two "senior non-human actors": the 1815 eruption of Mount Tambora and the Yersinia pestis bacterium that drove the Black Death. Apply standard corporate HR vocabulary—KPIs, strategic vision, leadership competencies, stakeholder collaboration—to these entities, treating them as intentional agents who "delivered" climate anomalies and demographic restructuring. The satire should exaggerate the actual determinist move of granting causal primacy and quasi-agency to germs, climate events, and volcanoes, so that a reader familiar with the debate recognizes the target: include invented metrics such as "population-reduction targets exceeded by 40%" or "cross-continental supply-chain disruption ratings," and praise Tambora for "initiating the Year Without a Summer with minimal operational staff." Human societies should appear only as "host ecosystems" or "stakeholder populations." In an "Areas for Development" section, note—without breaking the deadpan voice—that both actors risk "over-reliance on deterministic framing, underweighting local institutional resilience and adaptive agency." Invent a signatory, e.g., "Dr. A. Vance, Chief Historian of Non-Human Agency." Do not name living scholars; do not break character to explain the joke; maintain internal consistency with the bureaucratic genre throughout.
