As a retired experimental physicist who has recently begun working through the philosophy of action, I have been trying to formalize an argument I encountered in discussions of Libet-style readiness-potential studies. The argument is meant to show that neuroscience eliminates not only free will but also the rational authority of science itself, and I suspect it contains a hidden slide—perhaps an equivocation between temporal priority and causal exclusion, or an illicit generalization from discrete movements to continuous cognitive dynamics. I would like you to locate the faulty step and price the alternatives precisely.

Here is the argument in numbered form.

(1) In Libet-style paradigms, the readiness potential (RP) in the supplementary motor area begins approximately 300–500 ms before the subject reports conscious awareness of the decision to move.

(2) If a neural event N is both prior to and sufficient (together with background conditions) for a behavioral event B, and if there is no independent causal pathway from the mental event M that is contemporaneous with or subsequent to N to B, then M is not part of the causal production of B.

(3) The RP, together with standard background conditions, is sufficient for the finger-flexion movement.

(4) The reported conscious intention occurs after RP onset, and there is no empirically established independent causal pathway from it to the movement that bypasses the RP trajectory.

(5) Therefore the conscious intention is not part of the causal production of the movement.

(6) Free will, in any form worth defending, requires that conscious intentions be part of the causal production of the corresponding actions.

(7) Therefore free will is absent in these paradigms.

(8) Now generalize: for any action A, if there exists a complete neurophysiological explanation E that is sufficient for A, then by the reasoning in (2) any mental event M allegedly causing A is causally redundant.

(9) Scientific belief-formation—including the reasoning that led to the acceptance of (2) itself—is an action (or complex of actions) with complete neurophysiological explanations.

(10) Therefore, by (8), the mental events constituting rational assessment of (2) are causally redundant with respect to the acceptance of (2).

(11) But if rational assessment is causally redundant, then acceptance of (2) is not rationally justified; it is merely determined.

(12) Hence the argument for (2) undermines the rational justification for believing (2).

The conclusion in (12) proves too much: it threatens not merely voluntary agency but the very possibility of justified scientific belief. I want to know exactly where the chain breaks.

Specifically:

- Identify the single weakest link—whether it is (2) (the causal-exclusion principle), (6) (the causal-requirement on free will), or (8) (the unrestricted generalization)—and explain why that step initially looks defensible and why it fails under scrutiny. If the flaw is instead a scope shift or modal slide between steps, name it explicitly.

- For each of the three main responses—rejecting (2), rejecting (6), and rejecting (8)—explain what that response costs conceptually and how it affects the interpretation of Libet's data. In particular, address whether rejecting (6) forces one into epiphenomenalism, or whether it can be avoided.

- I am especially interested in whether there is a consistent compatibilist reading that accepts the neuroscience but denies (6) without collapsing into epiphenomenalism, and whether that reading requires reconceptualizing the RP not as a discrete trigger but as one segment of a continuous dynamical trajectory in state space, within which conscious modulation might operate at a different temporal scale—shaping the attractor landscape rather than initiating the trajectory. Please evaluate whether that reconceptualization genuinely resolves the tension or merely relocates it.

Keep the analysis focused on logical structure and neuroscientific interpretation; do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, as I am working without external access.
