I'm trying to figure out what I actually think about commodifying stuff that feels like it shouldn't have a price tag, going by my gut on cases. Selling a kidney to pay for your kid's tution: I'm okay with it, or at least I don't want to ban it. Selling a kidney so you can buy a second home: not okay, feels like you're treating your body like a car you can part out for fun. A store jacking up snow shovel prices 400% right after a blizard: not okay. But jacking up the price of fancy candles by the same amount: weirdly I'm fine with it, nobody needs candles. Paying someone to vote for your guy in a town council election about parking spots: not okay. Paying a shareholder meeting proxy to vote your shares for cash: also not okay, but it feels less dirty for some reason, maybe because shares are already property? Writing a contract that says I'll pay you 50k if you break up with your partner and marry me instead: not okay, even though marriage is already kind of a legal contract. And here's the one I keep flip-flopping on — a hospital letting rich patients pay extra to skip the transplant list, but not actually buying the organ, just buying faster administrative processing. Is that different from buying the organ? I can't tell.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? I was thinking maybe it's about whether the thing being sold is already inside a market logic — shares and maybe administrative speed are already priced, bodies and votes and snow in a crisis aren't. But then the kidney for tution vs. second home breaks that, since it's the same body both times. So maybe it's about the buyer's need versus the seller's desperation? But then the candle vs. shovel thing doesn't fit well. Can you lay out a principle, or maybe a couple competing ones, that captures as many as possible? For each one I'd like to know which case drives which clause, what it says about some cases I haven't mentioned (like paying to skip a jury-duty summons, or a company auctioning off layoffs by seniority), and which of my gut calls it just can't save. I know some verdict has to bend, I just want to see which one breaks the cleanest.
