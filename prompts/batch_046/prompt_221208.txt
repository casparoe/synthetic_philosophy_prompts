Compare presentism and eternalism with respect to their capacity to accommodate a hypothetical ontology of discrete temporal atoms. Specifically, consider a model in which reality consists of a sequence of instantaneous, three-dimensional worlds—each existing for exactly one indivisible duration—separated by intervals in which no concrete entities exist whatsoever, though the causal and nomological relations linking successive worlds are fully determined. First, analyze whether presentism can consistently maintain that only the present atomic world exists without being compelled to treat the interstitial non-being as a real temporal boundary that itself demands ontological commitment. Then, analyze whether eternalism can embed such discrete, separated worlds within a four-dimensional block without reifying the empty intervals as genuine parts of the manifold, or whether it must instead treat the entire sequence—including the gaps—as a single continuous spacetime structure. Conclude by assessing which framework offers the more coherent account of temporal passage and ontological continuity in this discrete setting, and clearly identify the central metaphysical cost that each view must bear.
