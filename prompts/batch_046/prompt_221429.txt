After a long argument with a friend about whether phenomenal consciousness can be fully explained as a kind of predictive interface, I have tried to formalize my own stance on consciousness as a user-illusion, and I want a consistency audit before I present it in a seminar. Here is where I have landed: (a) consciousness is a user-illusion in the strict sense that there is no Cartesian theater or intrinsic qualitative substrate; the brain instead constructs a simplified, egocentric predictive model for behavioral navigation; (b) the illusion is transparent, so introspection reveals only the modeled content and never the modeling machinery, which means first-person authority extends to phenomenological description but not to ontological inference; (c) therefore reports of "what it is like" should be treated as data about the structure of the predictive model rather than as evidence for irreducible phenomenal properties; (d) nevertheless, the framework requires a beneficiary—an organism with survival-related interests—so attributing consciousness demands attributing a functional role that benefits from the model, not merely replicating the computation; (e) consequently, artificial systems that implement an analogous predictive architecture but lack evolutionary interests should be judged as lacking consciousness, not as possessing an unobservable variant of it; and (f) this transparency also explains why the hard problem feels intractable: we are attempting to read the ontology of the interface from within the interface itself. Do these commitments actually hang together? Specifically, is there a hidden tension between (a), which denies any internal observer, and (d), which invokes a "beneficiary" that sounds suspiciously like one? Does (e) genuinely follow from (d), or does it smuggle in an unjustified biological essentialism that (a) ought to reject? And does (c) undermine the evidential basis for (b), since if all we have are the reports that (c) says are ontologically unreliable, how could we ever establish the hidden predictive architecture that (b) claims is there? Please rank the tensions by severity, distinguish genuine conflicts from merely apparent ones, and identify the least costly revision—without requiring me to abandon the core user-illusion framework.
