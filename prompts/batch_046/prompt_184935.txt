I'm working on a chapter about the conceptual coherence of phenomenal properties, and I'm stuck on how to read Chase and Sanborn's quining of qualia in relation to what I've been calling qualitative anaphora. Their argument, as I understand it, is that intrinsic qualitative properties fail to satisfy the compositional and referential conditions required for mental content—so that any attempt to embed a putative quale in a belief report produces a kind of anaphoric failure, where the qualitative term lacks a stable antecedent. From this, it seems to follow that comparative first-person judgments—"this experience is like that one"—are not merely epistemically risky but structurally malformed, since both occurrences of the qualitative description refer to nothing that can be tracked across contexts. Is that the correct upshot? Or is there a way to preserve the intelligibility of phenomenal comparison without reintroducing the very qualitative tokens Chase and Sanborn have shown to be incoherent?
