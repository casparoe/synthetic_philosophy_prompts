For a reading group on Nietzsche I have been asked to write out my working position on the genealogy of morality so that the others can stress-test it. Here is where I stand: (1) I read the master/slave distinction primarily as a logical taxonomy of evaluative structures rather than as a chronological claim that master morality came first; (2) I hold that ressentiment is both necessary and sufficient to produce the slave inversion, yet I deny that ressentiment can be causally efficacious across generations unless it is individually re-experienced in each case; (3) I think the genealogical method undermines a moral system's authority by revealing its origins in contingent power relations, but I also maintain that tracing an origin never by itself invalidates a present normative claim—that is, I reject the genetic fallacy; (4) I reject the metaphysics of the will to power entirely, yet I still explain the triumph of slave morality as the victory of one disguised form of will to power over the direct form exercised by masters; (5) I regard the ascetic priest as the purest embodiment of ressentiment, but I also concede that his extreme self-discipline displays a creative strength that makes him closer in type to the master than to the slave he represents; (6) I insist that Nietzsche's genealogy is strictly descriptive and evaluatively neutral, yet I regularly invoke it to argue that modern egalitarianism is life-denying and ought to be overcome. Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. For each real tension, rate its severity, identify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency, and say what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single commitment, if dropped or modified, would dissolve the most tension across the board, and whether the result would still be recognisably Nietzschean. Do not signal which commitment you think I should abandon.
