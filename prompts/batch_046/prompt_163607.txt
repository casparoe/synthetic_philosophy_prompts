Question: Explain what deflationism about truth is and discuss one important objection to it.

Student answer:
Deflationism is the view that truth is not a substantive metaphysical property. Instead of asking 'what is truth?' in some deep sense, deflationists say that saying 'p is true' just amounts to saying p. The central idea is the equivalence schema: 'Snow is white' is true iff snow is white. This was originally developed by Ramsey as the redundancy theory and was later refined by Horwich into minimalism. Because the schema applies to every sentence, deflationists think there's nothing more to explain—truth is just a logical tool for generalization and disquotation.

One big objection involves indirect contexts. If I say 'Everything Mary said is true,' I might not be able to list every specific thing Mary said. Deflationists try to handle this with quantifiers, but that actually shows 'true' is doing real work: it's picking out a set of propositions that I haven't explicitly named. So the objection shows that truth can't just be a redundant device, and deflationism fails.
