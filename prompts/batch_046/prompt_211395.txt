Phil gets stuck in a loop, acts like a jerk, then slowly transforms: he learns piano, saves a homeless guy, stops trying to manipulate Rita. The usual take is he learns to control only what's in his power — his own virtue — and stops caring about externals like the loop or her affection. But here's where I get stuck: modern Stoic revivalists (the CBT folks, writers like Donald Robertson) often frame the dichotomy as a psychological coping strategy, not as Epictetus's hard claim that externals are genuinely indifferent. Meanwhile there's the whole preffered indifferents issue — health, relationships, reputation are preferred but not good. Phil's arc seems to end with him getting Rita, almost like virtue is rewarded with the external thing he wanted all along. So is the film secretly saying that if you get your internal act together, the externals will follow? That feels like modern self-help individualism smuggled in, not ancient Stoicism.
