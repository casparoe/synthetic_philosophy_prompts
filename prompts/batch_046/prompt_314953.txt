I'm putting together a handout for a grad seminar on induction and self-locating belief, and I want a decision-tree taxonomy — not a literature review — that sorts people by how they actually resolve the refernce class problem in one specific kind of case. The case is predicting your own future mental state after a transformative intervention where your cognitive architecture might be edited, branched, or substrate-shifted. I want the branches to be concrete choices someone can answer without knowing the jargon: do you include counterfactual versions of yourself who made different choices? do you weight by similarity of substrate and history, or reject that as irrelevant? do you think there's a fact of the matter about the correct class, or is the ambiguity just a feature of the framing? Each leaf should carry a named position (or 'unnamed') plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment — I want at least these somewhere in the tree: a strict evidentialist view, a subjective Bayesian restriction, an eliminativist who denies there's a correct class, and an 'indexical-only' view. But build it from the logical space of answer-combinations first, then place the views, and flag any empty branches with a quick diagnosis (incoherent? ugly? merely unexplored?). Around 6-8 leaves is fine. The point is to see where people land when they answer the case-questions, not when they pick a label they like. Oh, and don't include a bibliography or reading list — half the group won't have library access this week, and I want this to work for a model that can't search either.
