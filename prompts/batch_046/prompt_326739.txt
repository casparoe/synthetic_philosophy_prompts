As a software engineer without formal philosophical training, I have been reading about epistemic self-access because of its analogies to system introspection and logging—specifically the tension between the KK principle, which holds that knowing p entails knowing that one knows p, and luminosity, the claim that being in a mental state puts one in a position to know it. Rather than asking for a historical survey, I want the specification for what an adequate theory resolving this tension must actually deliver.

Please lay out the full desiderata: the phenomena and case structures it must handle (gradual-transition scenarios, self-deception, borderline mental states, and the corresponding system-level analogs); the distinctions it must preserve (between being in a state and being able to report it, between first-order and second-order knowledge, and between access and epistemic authority); the formal or conceptual results it cannot violate (including the sorites-driven argument against luminosity and any continuity conditions on mental content); and which of these requirements are genuinely non-negotiable versus which are camp-specific preferences (for instance, whether determinacy of content or internalism about justification is essential).

Please also state explicitly whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable—for example, whether maintaining a strong KK principle necessarily conflicts with the continuity conditions that drive the standard anti-luminosity argument, or whether accommodating self-deception forces the abandonment of second-order transparency. Finally, include one constructed requirement particular to artificial or distributed systems: whether the theory must allow for “opaque introspection,” in which a subsystem can register its own state without the global architecture having second-order access to that registration, and indicate which mainstream positions this eliminates. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on a full scorecard of existing theories.
