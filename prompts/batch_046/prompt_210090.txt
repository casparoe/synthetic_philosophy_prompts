As a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, I have been attempting to apply Rawls's veil of ignorance and the difference principle to a problem I have framed in terms of system architecture, and I have arrived at an argument that seems to prove too much. I would value a precise diagnosis of where it fails.

(1) Behind the veil of ignorance, I do not know my social position, my talents, or my conception of the good.
(2) I also do not know the "institutional generation" I will occupy — whether I am born into a society with mature, fixed redistribution mechanisms or into one where such institutions are still being configured.
(3) The difference principle permits inequalities only if they maximally benefit the least advantaged members of society.
(4) If I enter a mature welfare state with rigid, high-tax structures, an economic downturn could leave me, as one of the least advantaged, worse off than I would be under a more adaptable system, because the state cannot easily reduce its burden on productive capacity.
(5) If I enter a society without redistribution, I need mechanisms that can construct it.
(6) To protect myself against both possibilities, I should endorse a principle that permits redistribution only conditionally and reversibly, rather than one that institutionalises it as a permanent feature.
(7) Therefore, the difference principle actually recommends a minimal, contingent safety net rather than a robust welfare state.
(8) This conclusion seems absurd: it transforms Rawls's argument into a defence of austerity, which I take to be contrary to the theory's purpose.

Where does the argument slip? Is there an equivocation between protecting the worst-off within a single society and protecting a hypothetical worst-off person across different institutional configurations? Or does the error lie in treating institutional maturity as a parameter covered by the veil? If I must preserve the core of Rawls's view, I would prefer to keep (3) intact and reject (7), but I want to understand precisely what rejecting the intermediate steps costs.

Please provide your answer in British English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
