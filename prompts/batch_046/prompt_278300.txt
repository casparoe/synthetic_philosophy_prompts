Here is an argument I assembled for a philosophy of mathematics and epistemic logic seminar, and I am suspicious of it because the conclusion is far stronger than the premises seem to allow — it appears to erase Gödel’s first incompleteness theorem rather than merely limiting human knowledge.

(1) For any true arithmetic statement S, there exists in principle a consistent formal extension of Peano Arithmetic with an automated proof-checker that verifies S. Call this weak formal knowability.
(2) If an agent verifies a conjunction (A and B), then that same agent verifies A and verifies B. Call this proof-closure.
(3) By Gödel’s first incompleteness theorem, fix a consistent, recursively axiomatizable system F and a specific statement G that is true but unprovable in F.
(4) So the conjunction C = (G is true and no F-checker verifies G) is true.
(5) By (1), there is some consistent extension F* with a checker that verifies C.
(6) By (2), that F*-checker verifies G.
(7) The same F*-checker also verifies that no F-checker verifies G.
(8) Now, (1) ascribed verification simpliciter, not verification-relative-to-F*; so the agent is taken to know both that G holds and that verification of G is impossible tout court, not merely impossible in F.
(9) To avoid contradiction we must deny that C is ever true.
(10) Hence every true arithmetic statement is verified by some checker, leaving no room for Gödelian independence.

The conclusion is absurd: it does not just claim that all truths are eventually discovered, but that independent sentences like G cannot exist at all. I want to keep (1) if possible, since weak formal knowability only expresses the idea that mathematics is eventually discoverable. Where is the hidden slide? Is it in the unindexed reading of verification in (8), in applying closure across system-boundaries in (6), in applying (1) to a self-referential conjunction in (5), or in the inference from contradiction to the falsity of (4) in (9)? Which step should I reject, what makes it look plausible, and what do I pay if I give it up?
