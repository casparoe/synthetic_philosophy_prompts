The idea is that the material doesn't target anyone specifically, but its mere presence degrades the shared epistemic climate and makes some people feel permanently unwelcome, even if nobody is forced to read it.

So what philosophical commitments does that distinction actually presuppose? Mill seems to care about direct, identifiable injury to others, but this practice treats the background conditions of discourse as something that can be harmed and protected by institutional design. Does that smuggle in a perfectionist or paternalistic view of the university shaping character? Or can a liberal justify it by saying the harm is just indirect but still real? Is there an implicit theory of agency or causation — maybe that active speech is self-correcting in a marketplace of ideas, but passive presence isn't?
