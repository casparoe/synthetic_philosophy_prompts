The domain is kind of weird: I'm mixing Weber's Protestant work ethic (work as moral calling, leisure suspicious), the French 35-hour-week framing (leisure as a collective right that needs legal protection), and Japanese karoshi culture (where overwork is structurally encouraged and leisure is socially penalized).

Write a decision tree / taxonomy that takes someone from concrete commitments to a named position. The branch questions should be things people can answer without already knowing the philosophical names: (a) is leisure a collective right that requires legal backing, or just a private preference? (b) can working past 40 hours ever be a moral virtue regardless of outcome? (c) are karoshi-type deaths mainly a failure of individual boundaries or of institutional design? (d) does the Protestant ethic survive secularization as pure productivity culture, or does it collapse without religious belief?

Each leaf should carry a position name (or 'unnamed' if nobody actually holds it) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Note any pairs of leaves your tree can't really seperate—like maybe a secular productivity zealot and a religious work-caller end up in the same box. Also flag combinations that look incoherent or just unexplored.

Keep it casual, this is for a handout not a journal article. And please say how confident you are in this whole mapping, and single out the point you're least sure about—probably where I shoved Weber and karoshi into thier same tree?
