Having spent three decades studying crustacean physiology before turning recently to philosophy of mind and ethics, I find myself wondering whether teleosemantic accounts of mental representation—which treat the content of a state as fixed by its evolutionary proper function rather than by any intrinsic phenomenal quality—can be coherently extended to nociceptive signaling in farmed decapods. Please describe the most important implications of applying this framework to the welfare assessment of shrimp and crabs in intensive aquaculture, particularly with respect to the conceptual possibility that these animals might misrepresent tissue damage (that is, represent harm without corresponding subjective suffering) or, conversely, undergo welfare-relevant physiological changes that fail to qualify as representations at all because they lack the appropriate selective history. I am interested in the logical structure of the argument, its potential failures, and its ethical consequences for how we ought to design aquaculture practices; I am not seeking bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations.
