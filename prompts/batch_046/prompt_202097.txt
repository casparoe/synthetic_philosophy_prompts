My study group came up with this and I cant find the hole. (1) In a genuinely hard choice the competing values are incommensurable — neither option is better, worse, or equal on some shared scale. (2) If they're incommensurable, rationality can't favor either option, so picking one is arbitrary, like a coin flip. (3) But if the choice is truly arbitrary, then regretting it afterwards makes no sense except as "the coin landed wrong" — you can't think "I should have picked the other" because there was no "should". (4) Yet people do experience rational regret after hard choices: they genuinely think they made the wrong call, not just that they lost a toss. (5) So if incommensurability existed, this kind of regret would be impossible. (6) But it happens. (7) Therefore hard choices always have a hidden correct answer, and incommensurability is just ignorance in disguise. I feel like (2) might conflate "no overall ranking" with "no reasons at all," but I'm not sure what fixing that does to the argument. Which step do you reject, and what's the cost?
