Q: Marcus Velle's double-frame theory holds that emotions are second-order perceptions of first-order bodily changes that are already conceptually framed by social norms. How is that meant to split the difference between judgment theories (like Nussbaum) and pure feeling/perceptual theories (like James-Lange or Prinz)? And can it actually handle moral disgust without collapsing back into a judgment view?

Student answer:
Velle splits the diff by saying emotions aren't judgments like Nussbaum claims, since you don't need to actually form a belief you're in danger to feel fear. Instead your body changes first, then you perceive those changes, but that perception is already shaped by social norms — so it's not mindless like James-Lange either, since the framing gives it aboutness. This avoids the cognitive overload problem because framing is automatic, not a deliberate belief-formation process. For moral disgust, Velle says it's a second-order perception of gut arousal framed through purity norms. This works because the norm shapes the feeling without being a separate judgment that the act is impure. So he succeeds, though he maybe owes us more on how framing gets learned without being judgmental.
