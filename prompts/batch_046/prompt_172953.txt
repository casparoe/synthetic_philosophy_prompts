My study group has been working through the following chain of reasoning about scientific realism and the pessimistic meta-induction. We think it ends up proving far too much—indeed, it seems to collapse into a blanket scepticism about all reasoning from scientific history—but we cannot agree on which link is weakest.

(1) The pessimistic meta-induction observes that every mature scientific theory that was once empirically successful—phlogiston chemistry, caloric thermodynamics, Newtonian gravitation at cosmological scales—was eventually replaced and is now regarded as strictly false.

(2) By uniform inductive inference, any currently successful theory, including quantum field theory and evolutionary biology, is likely to be replaced and false in the same strict sense.

(3) Scientific realism holds that the predictive success of a mature theory is best explained by its being approximately true of an unobservable reality.

(4) Apply the same inductive pattern reflexively to meta-science: past philosophical generalisations drawn from the history of science—verificationist accounts of meaning, naive falsificationism—have themselves been abandoned or severely limited.

(5) Therefore the PMI itself, being an inductive generalisation from scientific history, is probably false.

(6) If PMI is false, anti-realism loses its principal argument.

(7) Yet scientific realism’s no-miracles defence also depends on an inductive pattern: that past successes being explained by approximate truth will continue.

(8) Hence, if PMI undermines all inductive reasoning about scientific history, it undermines the realist’s own historical argument.

(9) The result is not a victory for either side, but global scepticism: we have no rational basis for judging our current theories true, false, or approximately true.

We find (9) intolerable, but each step looks defensible on its own. Please tell us which premise or inference to reject. Explain why that particular step initially seems plausible—what makes the slide from local observation to global conclusion look legitimate here—and then price the alternatives: what must structural realists, selective or entity realists, and anti-realist instrumentalists each concede if that step is abandoned? In particular, address whether the flaw lies in treating the PMI as self-applicable, in mischaracterising the no-miracles argument as inductive rather than abductive, or in an illicit scope shift between theories with empirical content and meta-level claims about them.

Please respond in British English, and keep the analysis precise rather than impressionistic.
