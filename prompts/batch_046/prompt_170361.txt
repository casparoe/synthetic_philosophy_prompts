At our transaltion workshop, Claire and Dan fell out over Marot's epigrams and this idea of le ton beau. Claire said if you want to keep Marot's light graceful tone you have to keep his rhyme scheme and metre exactly, even if the English sounds a bit forced — she was translating into rhymed meter matching the original pattern. Dan said that's backwards: English rhyme is clunky compared to French, so locking yourself into Marot's form destroys the very lightness you're trying to save. He argued you should drop the rhyme and use loose free verse with modern diction, because the ton beau lives in the wit and social grace, not in the scafolding. Claire replied that without the formal constraint you're just writing a different poem, and Marot's grace was precisely in how he made the form feel effortless. Dan said that's romanticising — the form was effortless for Marot because it was his native tool, but for English readers it feels like a corset. They asked me to settle it but I'm stuck. Please explain who's right about what, and say who is more right overall. Write in British English please.
