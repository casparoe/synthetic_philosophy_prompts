I run a grad workshop on philosophy of mind, about 8 people, 75 minutes. Design a structured role-play that makes them feel the symbol grounding problem, specificaly for emotional vocabulary in a synthetic language with no biological affect. Split them: one team are "Lexicographers" who only get syntactic rules and behavioral correlation charts for terms like grief and euphoria; the other are "Embodied agents" who experience internal states but can only communicate through the lexicographers' symbol tables. Use paper-point payoffs—lexicographers score for predicting agent behavior from syntax alone, agents score when lexicographers mislabel internal states despite perfect behavioral correlation. They should hit the wall where tracking is flawless but something is still missing before anyone names the problem. Include a debrief mapping that failure point onto the grounding gap, say what will predictably go wrong (the lexicographers overfit correlations and think they've solved it), and be honest about which feature—probably first-person irreducibility—the game can't reproduce. Keep it runnable with just cards, no tech.
