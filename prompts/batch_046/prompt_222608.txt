I'm preparing remarks for a graduate seminar on Bradley's regress and the unity of the proposition, and I'd like a consistency audit of my current package before I present it. Here is where I stand. First, I take Bradley's regress to be genuinely vicious for any ontology that treats relations as fully mind-independent, external entities; it is not merely an epistemic puzzle about our cognitive access but a direct reductio of the view that relations are real additions to their terms. Second, I deny that propositional unity requires a real "tie" or constituent—such as an Armstrong-style state of affairs or a Russellian universal—because I hold that the relation in "Socrates is wise" is internal: it is not an entity linking Socrates and wisdom from the outside, but a moment of their differentiated unity. Third, this internal-relation picture, I think, is best articulated in a broadly Hegelian register: the terms are abstractions from a concrete whole, and relating is the articulation of that whole rather than the coupling of pre-given atoms. Fourth, nevertheless, I am an ontological pluralist. I reject Bradley's own monistic conclusion—the Absolute as an undifferentiated, featureless substance—and I insist that distinct entities, whether particulars, properties, or events, exist in their own right without being reducible to modifications of one underlying reality. Fifth, I am also committed to a strong form of semantic holism: the determinacy of any propositional content depends on its inferential place within the entire web of belief, which implies that there are no atomistic propositions with self-standing, network-independent meanings. Sixth, and finally, I treat the psychological unity of judgment—what binds subject and predicate in a single act of thinking—as primitive and irreducible: it is not derived from syntactic structure, neural synchronization, or the semantic coherence of the belief-web, but is a basic, non-derivative feature of conscious intentionality.

My question is whether this set of commitments is jointly tenable. Specifically, does my pluralism in (4) sit consistently with the Hegelian internal-relation view in (3), given that internal relations seem to presuppose that the terms are moments of one encompassing whole? Does my semantic holism in (5) undermine the precise determinacy of relational structure that (1) needs in order to generate the regress in the first place? And does the psychological primitivism in (6) render the internal-relation and semantic accounts in (2)-(3) redundant, or even incoherent, since if unity is already secured at the level of mental acts, there is no further work for internal relations or holistic content-fixation to do? Or are some of these tensions merely apparent—perhaps (1) and (3) look opposed but actually complement each other, or (5) and (6) can be reconciled if we distinguish content-determinacy from act-unity? Please rank the conflicts by severity, indicate for each which commitment is under the greatest pressure, identify the least costly revision that would restore consistency, and be explicit about whether any pair of motivations here is not just in tension but genuinely incompatible at the level of their underlying reasons rather than merely at the surface. I am not signaling which view I would drop; I want the audit to tell me where the fault lines actually lie.
