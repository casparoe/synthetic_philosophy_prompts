I am taking a course on philosophy of personal identity, and I have become particularly interested in the gradual-replacement variant of mind-uploading thought experiments. Most discussions I have encountered either jump straight to the fully-uploaded endpoint or rely on science-fictional teleportation, which makes it hard to isolate the work being done by continuity versus substrate. I would like you to construct a carefully engineered thought experiment that meets the following specifications.

Design a realistic, single-paragraph scenario in which a patient undergoes a gradual, twenty-year replacement of prefrontal cortical tissue with synthetic neuromorphic arrays. The arrays must replicate neuronal firing patterns with high fidelity, but they must deliberately omit analogues of glial cells, thereby breaking a specific form of metabolic coupling that some philosophers take to be constitutive of biological continuity. At the intermediate stage—roughly fifty per cent replacement—the subject reports full psychological continuity, retains all memories and character traits, and behaves indistinguishably from the original patient. Once the scenario is set out, demonstrate how three positions deliver their verdicts: (i) a psychological-continuity account of personal identity; (ii) a biological-continuity or animalist account; and (iii) a functional-integration view that holds metabolic coupling between neural and glial networks to be necessary for the persistence of the original organism. For each, state the belief or judgement about whether the original person survives, and explain precisely which feature of the case drives that result.

Finally, identify the structural feature that does the philosophical work—specifically, the absence of glial analogues despite preserved cognitive function—and explain what happens to the disagreement if that feature is removed (for instance, if the synthetic substrate includes functional glial analogues). Then, briefly, show how a defender of the view that comes out worst in your scenario might attack the case, and ensure that the reasoning does not appeal to exotic physics, perfect predictors, or stipulation of outcomes. Please present your answer in British English.
