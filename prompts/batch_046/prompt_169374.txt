I have been trying to work out the implications of Hofstadter's claim that a self is not a private, indivisible soul but rather an abstract pattern—something like a recursive loop—that can in principle be realised across different brains. The idea that fragments of one person's pattern might persist in the minds of friends and family seems intuitively right, yet when I formalise it I reach a conclusion I find unacceptable.

(1) If the self is an abstract pattern rather than a particular brain-bound substance, then the pattern can be multiply instantiated.
(2) In close relationships, parts of my pattern—my reasoning habits, my turns of phrase, my shared memories—are genuinely reconstructed in other people's minds.
(3) Therefore, at any time, my 'I' is partly distributed across substrates other than my own brain.
(4) If destroying substrate S fails to destroy pattern P whenever P is also instantiated in substrate T, then destroying my brain cannot destroy my self, since fragments of my loop survive in others.
(5) Consequently, if I were killed, my self would not be annihilated; only one of its instantiations would be removed.
(6) What makes homicide gravely wrong is precisely that it annihilates a unique, irreplaceable self.
(7) So, on the distributed-pattern view, homicide is not gravely wrong in that specific respect.
(8) But homicide is gravely wrong.
(9) Hence the distributed-pattern view must be rejected, or the move from (3) to (5) is invalid.

This proves far more than I intended: it suggests that accepting a distributed self makes murder a comparatively minor loss. I would very much like to preserve (1) and (2), because the pattern account of identity and the preservation of fragments in others both seem correct. Where does the argument go wrong? Is there an equivocation between the survival of a fragment and the survival of the complete loop, or does blocking (4) require me to abandon the substrate-independence that makes the pattern view plausible? Please explain which step to reject and what rejecting it costs, and write your answer in British English.
