I'm drafting an argument for a philosophy-of-technology paper on von Neumann's implicit philosophy of technological persistence, and I'd appreciate critical comments on the conceptual framing rather than on bibliography. Drawing on his late lectures about self-reproducing automata, I distinguish two modes of "survival" for artificial systems: mechanical survival, in which the physical or functional structure persists through replication, repair, and adaptation to environmental constraints; and teleological survival, in which the original design purpose or invariant constraint-set remains intact across generations. My central claim is that these modes are not merely conceptually separable but structurally antagonistic in any system with genuinely open-ended self-modification: the very mechanisms that secure mechanical survival—error correction treated as mutable, resource competition, and adaptive restructuring—necessarily erode fixed teleological boundaries, since they treat original specifications as contingent constraints rather than as immutable laws.

I'm applying this framework to current AI alignment discourse, which often assumes that teleological survival is technically achievable through constitutional design, value learning, or recursive reward modeling, apparently without recognizing that the capacities required for long-term mechanical persistence in an open environment generate evolutionary pressure against fixed values. I want to avoid anachronism, so I specifically want feedback on whether this distinction between mechanical and teleological survival is genuinely recoverable from von Neumann's engineering concerns or whether I'm projecting a contemporary safety-theory framework backward onto his work. Additionally, is the claim that the two survival modes are structurally antagonistic too strong? Where might the argument confuse functional persistence with normative persistence, and are there intermediate cases—perhaps tightly constrained replication with limited adaptation—where both modes could stabilize together? Please focus on conceptual accuracy and argumentative coherence; I am not asking for citations or further reading.
