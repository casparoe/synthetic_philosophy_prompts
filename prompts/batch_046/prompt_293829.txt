I'm a data-safety monitor for a small biotech firm running Phase II trials; every two weeks I have ten minutes to recommend whether enrollment should pause due to ambiguous adverse-event signals, with no statistician on call, an audit trail required, and sign-off by the principal investigator. Turn the theory of imprecise probabilities—using lower and upper previsions, and distinguishing aleatory from epistemic ambiguity—into a single-page reusable worksheet procedure. It must specify ordered steps for: (1) encoding a point estimate with wide confidence intervals as an interval-valued belief; (2) resolving conflicting subgroup signals; and (3) cases where the adverse-event mechanism is unknown, so the statistical model itself is uncertain. For each step, state the decision rule or threshold that replaces judgment, flag explicitly where judgment is unavoidable, and note what the worksheet cannot resolve. Apply the procedure to three hypothetical two-week snapshots—one yielding a clear pause recommendation, one yielding a clear continue recommendation, and one where the worst-case upper prevision exceeds the safety threshold yet the point-estimate rule says continue, producing an answer the principal investigator is likely to reject. In that third case, specify exactly what I must document for the audit and where the procedure ends and human discretion begins. Finally, list the forms of model ambiguity this worksheet is not designed to handle.
