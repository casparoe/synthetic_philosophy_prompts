Construct a thought experiment intended to isolate the lexical discontinuity in threshold negative utilitarianism from ordinary risk preferences and from simple aggregative weighing of suffering against pleasure. The setting should be a concrete, non-science-fictional institutional choice—such as a hospital administrator allocating a fixed budget between two available programs—where Program A eliminates a 2 percent risk that 500 patients will suffer severe chronic pain calibrated at 80 units on a shared evaluative scale, while Program B guarantees each of those same patients 40 units of mild pleasure with zero risk of severe pain, but institutionally precludes adopting A. Engineer the case so that the lexical-threshold negative utilitarian chooses A, the standard aggregative negative utilitarian chooses B, and a commensurabilist opponent who accepts that suffering generally outweighs pleasure but denies any lexical ordering chooses neither available option, instead insisting on an unavailable hybrid that splits resources. The magnitudes must be set so that the lexical view’s preference for A is driven purely by the structural claim that any nonzero risk of suffering above the threshold lexically dominates any finite aggregate of pleasure below it, not by risk aversion, loss aversion, or framing effects. Present the scenario in a single paragraph. Then demonstrate that the specifications are met: walk through the reasoning of all three positions and show why each delivers its assigned verdict; identify the precise structural feature of the case that forces the divergence; explain what happens to all three verdicts if that feature is removed, for instance by lowering the potential suffering below the threshold or by raising the guaranteed pleasure past it; and describe how a defender of the lexical-threshold view could attack the framing of the case without giving up the core commitment.
