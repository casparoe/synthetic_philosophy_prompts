For this school, the execution of Danton, the purge of the Girondins, and Robespierre's own fall were structurally inevitable—every bourgeois revolution eventually eliminates its radical wing in order to stabilize capitalist order.

Revisionists such as Furet reject this kind of social determinism. That makes the Terror essentially unpredictable, a historical accident without deeper logic. Meanwhile the saying that revolutions devour their children, usually tied to Vergniaud, shows that both interpretations actually agree on the outcome: Marxists explain it through class logic, revisionists through pure contigency. So in the end both schools confirm the idea, just through opposite mechanisms.'
