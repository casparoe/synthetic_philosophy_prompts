I was grabbing coffee with Maya and Leo and they got into it over whether old biography stuff actually makes you a better person or if its just fancy entertainment with a moral halo. Maya said hagiography and works like Plutarch's Parallel Lives are genuinely useful for moral learning because they dont just list virtues, they show them unfolding in time -- you see how someone navigates a specific crisis and that gives you a practical map for your own life. She pointed out that Plutarch pairs a Greek with a Roman not to claim they're identical but to show that courage or temperance can wear different cultural clothes, so the reader learns to recognize the virtue beneath the costume and adapt it.

Leo said thats way too optimistic. He said Plutarch is basically writing propaganda for virtue, picking and chosing anecdotes to build portraits that fit his theme. The Parallel Lives structure isnt a neutral comparison, its an editorial grid -- he sorts people into ethical categories like ambition or moderation and forces the details to fit. So the reader isnt learning from real lives, they're learning from Plutarch's taxonomy, and any moral improvement is just aesthetic pleasure plus confirmation bias. He added that if the biography is distorted, then using it as a scaffold for self-reflection is just building on false premises.

Maya came back saying Leo treats biography like bad history, but it isnt trying to be a factual record, its doing narrative embodiment. The point isnt that every detail is accurate; its that seeing virtue enacted in a temporal sequence trains practical reasoning in a way abstract rules cant. She called this aspirational benchmarking -- you dont copy the hero, you calibrate your own choices against a vivid example and fill the gaps with your own reflection.

Leo replied that this benchmarking is just projection dressed up. Without historical accuracy the calibration is meaningless -- youre comparing yourself to a fiction Plutarch assembled to make a point. He said if Maya wants to defend biography she needs to explain why an edited, categorized life is more useful for ethics than a well-written novel, since both are constructed narratives, and he didnt think she could do it without smuggling in historical authority she already admitted was shaky.

They turned to me and asked me to referee. Please adjudicate move by move: rate Maya's first point about Plutarch's pairing and practical maps, Leo's reply about propaganda and the editorial grid, Maya's comeback about narrative embodiment and aspirational benchmarking, Leo's counter about projection and false premises, and Maya's implicit defense that constructed narrative can still train reasoning. Say for each whether its clearly wrong, partly right, or clearly right with brief justification. Then say who is more right overall, and identify what both are missing that actually matters for whether biography can teach virtue.
