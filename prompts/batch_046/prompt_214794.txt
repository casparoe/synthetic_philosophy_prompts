I am trying to determine whether moral psychology or virtue ethics has a name for a specific asymmetry I keep encountering in judgments about character. The pattern works as follows. When explaining a failure in some domain — say, courage or fairness — people often invoke situationist considerations: they note that the context was unusually stressful, that social pressure was extreme, or that the agent faced conflicting incentives, and they treat these as reasons not to infer a stable vice. Yet when the same people discuss successes in domains they regard as central to character, they identify one or two highly specific situations as 'revealing' of the agent's true nature, and they project the inferred virtue into very different contexts — ones with opposite situational pressures — without applying the same situationist discounting. The selection of which situations count as diagnostic does not appear to track any independent psychological feature (such as salience or emotional intensity); rather, it tracks the evaluator's prior normative commitment to treating that domain as character-revealing. This produces a kind of one-way gate: situationism is used to protect virtues from counter-evidence, but not used to protect vices from confirming evidence, or vice versa depending on the evaluator's commitments. The result is not mere hypocrisy, since the reasoning in each case can be articulated consistently; it is a structural asymmetry in how trait attribution and situational explanation are distributed across domains. Does this configuration have an established label — perhaps in the literature on trait attribution bias, on the situationist critique of virtue ethics, or in related work on moral judgment? If there is no precise term, how should it be distinguished from related phenomena such as the fundamental attribution error, motivated reasoning about character, or the 'motte-and-bailey' dynamics sometimes seen in defenses of virtue? Please also say where your analysis is most vulnerable: what empirical result about cross-situational consistency, or what conceptual distinction between trait and state, would lead you to revise your view of whether this pattern is genuinely distinct.
