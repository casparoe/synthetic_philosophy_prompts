I chair our university's Interdisciplinary AI Governance Board and we've got a funding decision on thursday that’s turning into a philosophy fight. A research group applied for a major center grant framing "the alignment problem" as essentially a technical robustness issue — reward misspecification, distributional shift, that sort of thing — with a single principal-agent model running through the whole proposal. Half the board wants to approve because the engineering is solid; the other half (me included, kinda) thinks this collapses what is actually several distinct problems into one, and that treating alignment as purely monadic misses the polyadic coordination issues that come up when different stakeholders have incommensurable values about what the system should do.

I need a memo for the board recommending a concrete decision — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with a clear bottom line up front. The memo should engage seriously with the idea that alignment might really be one unified problem (maybe the technical framing captures the heart of it), but also lay out why splitting it into procedural vs substantive dimensions, and recognizing the institutional layer, matters for whether we fund this. I was talking to a colleague in the philosophy dept who kept asking whether we're even asking the right question — is it about aligning AI with human values, or aligning competing human values through AI? — and I think that tension needs to be in there. Keep it tight: bottom line first, then reasoning in short sections, honest treatment of the strongest argument for the other side, max 450 words, one page for the meeting packet. No bibliographies, just the arguement.
