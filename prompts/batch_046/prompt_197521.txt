Write an essay examining whether Kierkegaard's account of Abraham's teleological suspension of the ethical can be translated into a secular, operational principle for resolving conflicts between universal moral rules and exceptional ends. Weigh the argument that Abraham's readiness to sacrifice Isaac demonstrates a legitimate, hierarchical ordering of ethical obligations—where the universal duty not to kill is suspended in service of a higher telos—against the counter-argument that such suspension is intelligible only within the paradox of faith and collapses into arbitrary exceptionalism once detached from a direct divine command. Your essay should reach and justify a definite conclusion: either that the suspension remains defensible only as a private, religious paradox with no public analog, or that it can be secularized into a limited, communicable procedure for ethical override. In developing your position, analyze specifically the role of Abraham's silence toward Sarah and Isaac: argue whether this silence functions to preserve the irreducible singularity of the exceptional act, or whether it reveals that any teleological suspension is necessarily incommunicable and therefore unusable as a shared moral framework. Conclude by stating the conditions under which your verdict would fail.
