I was chatting with my neighbour last week -- he's retired too, used to be in physics -- and we got onto this philosophy idea I read about called origin essentialism. The claim is that I couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg, which sounds okay to me since in my old genetics lab we always said the genome is basically the organism's blueprint.

But here's where my thinking gets twisted: if the exact gametes are metaphysicly essential, then isn't the exact DNA they contributed essential too? Otherwise what makes those particular gametes special? But then I run into problems. I survive mutations every day, and there's that mitochondrial replacement therapy where a child ends up with DNA from three parents. So does origin essentialism mean that kid is essentailly a different person from the one who'd exist without the therapy? Or does it mean essentialism is false because genetic material can be swapped? I feel like I'm treating metaphysical essentialism like it's just about DNA stability, which is probably a category error, but I can't see where exactly the line gets drawn between the origin event and the genetic material that carries forward. Can anyone help me untangle this?
