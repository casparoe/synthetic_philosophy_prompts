write a dialogue between two philosophy grad students arguing over whether a swarm of autonomous drones running a decentralized algorithm counts as one composite object or just a bunch of seperate machines. one should defend universal mereological composition — like, if the parts are spatially related they compose something, period — and the other should defend a restricted view where composition only happens when parts are caught in a unified causal loop with downward causation. make them actually talk to eachother: the universalist should take the causal-loop idea seriously and ask why downward causation should matter for ontology rather than just explanation, and the restrictionist should push back on whether spatial proximity is enough without some functional locking. have them use the drone swarm as thier running example but keep it conceptual — don't just list definitions, let them stumble over edge cases like what happens when one drone drops out and the swarm re-routes. write it casual, like they're talking over coffee, and maybe let one of them misspell "recieve" or forget an apostrophe somewhere. neither should be a strawman.
