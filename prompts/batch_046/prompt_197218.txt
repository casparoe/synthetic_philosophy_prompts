I am preparing the interview questions for a podcast colloquium with a philosopher who has just published a monograph advancing a structural collapse reading of Nietzsche's On the Genealogy of Morals. Her central thesis is that the noble-slave distinction in Essay I fails on internal grounds: the nobles' self-affirmation is not spontaneously self-originating but is constituted through an active, prior exclusion of a despised other—the bad—so that noble evaluation is already reactive and therefore cannot serve as the non-reactive pole against which slave ressentiment is defined. She anticipates critics who cite Nietzsche's psychological descriptions of noble spontaneity and answers that psychological spontaneity is not equivalent to evaluative independence. She also deflects worries about self-refutation with the claim that a diagnostic instrument can carry the infection it detects without losing its clinical function. Write me a sequence of interview questions that makes this deflection progressively harder to deploy. Each question should press the actual content of her position—the constitutive dependence of noble value on its excluded other, the normative consequences for Essay III's critique of the ascetic priest, and whether her reading eliminates any non-reactive standpoint from which Nietzsche could consistently condemn life-denial. For each major objection, include the reply she is likely to offer and then a sharper follow-up that anticipates and closes that escape route. End with a question asking what specific textual pattern or conceptual finding would genuinely count against her structural-collapsing reading rather than being absorbed into it as another instance of internal contamination.
