I have been thinking about an idea I associate with Douglas Hofstadter: that creativity is not a sudden flash but a continuous exploration of a theme, like adjusting the parameters of a concept one by one—turning mental knobs—to generate neighboring possibilities. If that picture is correct, then I am puzzled by how patent and copyright institutions actually work. Patent law denies protection when an invention is merely an obvious variation of prior art, yet grants it when the same kind of adjustment is deemed non-obvious. Copyright allows derivative works only when they are transformative, not when they are trivial tweaks. What philosophical view of creativity, mental value, or social progress is baked into these distinctions? Do the rules presuppose that real invention requires a discontinuous leap rather than smooth neighborhood exploration, or that there is an objective line separating meaningful change from mere tinkering? Reconstruct the implicit theory—whether it concerns the inventor's internal process, the structural distance between old and new ideas, or something about utility—then tell me whether this institutional view can genuinely coexist with the continuous theme-variation model. Finally, say what patent or copyright practice would have to look like if we actually believed that all creativity is just systematic knob-turning on existing concepts.
