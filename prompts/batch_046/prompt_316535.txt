For a seminar on distributive justice I've been asked to map my philosophical profile so others can poke at it. Here it is: I'm a luck egalitarian — justice means compensating for unchosen disadvantages but not for ones people bring on themselves through reasonable risk-taking. I draw the choice/chance boundary with a "reasonable rejectability" standard rather than actual consent. I think if you deliberately cultivate an expensive taste, like getting deep into rare wines or backcountry skiing, you shouldn't recieve subsidies for that. I also think structural injustices like redlining create brute-luck harms that should be fully compensated even when no individual wrongdoer can be identifed. At the same time, I think people have a "duty of self-authorship" to try overcoming bad luck through effort, and that fulfilling it can ground limited desert claims. Finally, I reject the abandonment objection because I think there's always a universal basic floor, no matter how reckless the choice was.

Point out if any pair only looks like a clash but dissolves on closer look. Then say which single revision — dropping or tweaking one commitment — would clean up the most overall.
