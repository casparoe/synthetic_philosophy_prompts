I've been thinking about split-brain patients and whether there's actually one person in there or two. Here are my gut verdicts on cases, and I keep going back and forth.

Case 1: The classic Sperry setup where left brain sees a chicken and picks a claw, right brain sees snow and picks a shovel, then the left mouth makes up a story about cleaning up after chickens. To me that feels like one person confabulating, not two roommates. The right side just doesn't have a voice.

Case 2: But then there's the version where they flash a picture to only the left eye, ask the left hand to point to the matching object, and it does, while the mouth says "I don't know why." That feels like two people: one knows the answer and the other is genuinely in the dark.

Case 3: I also read about cases where the corpus callosum is cut but the anterior commissure is still intact, so emotional reactions cross over. Both sides flinch at a snake shown to either eye, but only the left can say "snake." Here it feels like one frightened person with a broken word-line, not two seperate fears.

Case 4: Here's a made-up case to stress-test: imagine they slowly cut the callosum over months, one fiber bundle at a time, asking every week "do you feel like one person?" They always say yes, even at 100% cut when they can draw a circle with one hand and a square with the other. Does that mean the feeling of unity is just a leftover habit, or does it prove there's still one self?

Case 5: Another hypothetical: after a full split, the left hemisphere learns jazz piano and the right learns classical. Then doctors reconnect them. The person plays both styles but doesn't feel like two musicians merged; they just feel like they know two genres. So maybe separation never created two people in the first place?

So what's the principle? Is it about language being the glue? About shared emotion? About whether actions can conflict? I think case 1 and 2 are hard to square. And what about this: a split-brain patient gets opposite moral answers in each visual field, but with both eyes open picks one consistently every time. One conflicted person, or two people with one getting veto power?
