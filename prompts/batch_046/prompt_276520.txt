I started with Herodotus and Thucydides, and what strikes me is that Herodotus seems happy to lay out contradictory stories about the origins of some conflict and let the reader sit with the ambiguity, whereas Thucydides tries to filter out unreliable testimony by cross-checking witnesses and discarding what he thinks is biased. That feels a bit like how in geology we use multiple isotopic methods to trianglate an age, but then I started wondering about the physical traces themselves—if a sedimentary layer has been disturbed by later tectonic shifts, we're not just filtering bad witnesses, we're dealing with evidence that has literally been rewritten by later events.

So I'm trying to figure out the most important conceptual implications of treating source criticism not just as a tool for texts but as a model for all deep-time knowledge. Does the fact that geological evidence can be physically altered after the fact make Herodotus's tolerance for contradiction more appropriate than Thucydides's stricter filtering?
