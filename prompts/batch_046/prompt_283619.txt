I am working on a novel about a philosophy seminar that collapses into itself when its members try to stage a debate they cannot win, and I need you to design the classroom exercise they would actually run. The setting is an upper-level philosophy of mind workshop with twelve graduate students, one ninety-minute session, in which the facilitator wants participants to feel—not merely understand—the paradox of phenomenal judgment under epiphenomenalism. Please construct a structured simulation or role-play in which students must produce verbal reports about their own conscious experiences according to rules that secretly treat those reports as mechanically triggered outputs with no causal influence on the reporting mechanism itself; for example, hidden cards or prerecorded prompts could determine the content of each report, yet participants must defend the accuracy of what they said as if their awareness caused the words. The payoff or incentive structure—using only points, privileges, or narrative stakes rather than money—should reward consistency with the hidden script rather than fidelity to subjective experience, so that students discover through action that their phenomenal judgments are causally disconnected from the judgments they utter. Before anyone names the paradox, they should experience the coordination failure between what they feel and what they are permitted to claim. Then provide a debrief that maps each stage of the simulation onto the logical steps of the paradox—phenomenal knowledge, the judgment of that knowledge, the causal inertness of the phenomenal state, and the contradiction in claiming causal efficacy—along with an honest account of what will go wrong the first time, such as students interpreting causal inefficacy as proof that consciousness does not exist rather than as a problem about judgment. Be explicit about which feature of epiphenomenalism the exercise cannot reproduce—almost certainly the first-person immediacy that makes phenomenal access seem self-certifying—and explain how I, as the novelist, might supply that missing element in my scene. Write your response entirely in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as though it were a single letter to a fellow writer. Finally, state how confident you are in this design and identify precisely the point about which you feel least certain.
