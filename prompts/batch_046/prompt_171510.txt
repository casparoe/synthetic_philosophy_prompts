For a philosophy of mind reading group, I am writing down my commitments on mental content and reference so that the others can identify where the package breaks. Here is where I stand: (1) I accept the Twin Earth argument that thoughts about natural kinds have their content fixed partly by the actual environment—two molecule-for-molecule identical brains can think different thoughts about water versus XYZ solely because of the chemical nature of their respective environments; (2) I want to extend this reasoning to institutional kinds: if two communities share identical internal cognitive architectures but are embedded in different economic institutions—say, one with a centralized fiat-currency system and one with a decentralized distributed-ledger protocol—the mental content expressed by their shared term for money differs in a parallel way; (3) I deny that there is any shared narrow content between such pairs—there is no descriptively adequate mental state that both individuals share; (4) I maintain that mental causation is fully local: the causal efficacy of a mental state in producing behavior is determined entirely by intrinsic neural properties, with no causal contribution from external environmental relations; (5) I also hold that in cases of complete expert deference—where a speaker uses a technical term without any understanding of its referent—the speaker's mental state lacks determinate content altogether, rather than borrowing the expert's content. Do these five commitments form a coherent package? Specifically, is (4) compatible with (1) and (2), given that externalism seems to make environmental relations constitutive of the very mental states that do the causing? Does (3)'s denial of shared narrow content sit consistently with (5)'s claim of indeterminacy, or do they invoke incompatible mechanisms? And does extending Twin Earth reasoning to institutional kinds in (2) require a social-deference picture that conflicts with the individual-level determination implied by (1)? Rank the tensions by severity, identify the least costly single revision that restores consistency, and say what I would have to give up about mental causation, narrow content, or deferred reference if I keep the rest intact.
