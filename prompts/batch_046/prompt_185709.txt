Give an example of a conceptual issue that arises when one applies Quine's criterion of ontological commitment—quantification over the values of bound variables in a regimented first-order theory—to normative predicates in a naturalized ethics, in a situation where the predicate is introduced not by direct observation but by theoretical postulate. Specifically, construct a case in which two empirically equivalent regimentations of the same ethical discourse—one realist, quantifying over 'injustice' as a property, and one instrumentalist, treating the term as a purely heuristic device with no ontological import—are related by an indeterminacy of translation that Quine's pragmatic criteria (simplicity, familiarity, predictive success) fail to resolve, because the realist framework incorporates a novel structural feature—call it 'normative insulation,' the claim that moral predicates are systematically shielded from empirical revision in ways that observational predicates are not—while the instrumentalist framework preserves methodological continuity with science but eliminates moral entities entirely. Your example should identify the precise point of tension: whether the Quinean naturalist, who accepts only those entities indispensable to our best overall theory, is forced to recognize injustice-properties as ontologically real if the realist regimentation is adopted, even though those properties enter into no causal interactions and serve no predictive function beyond the heuristic one already captured by the instrumentalist rival. Do not appeal to secondary literature, specific texts, or bibliographies; derive the conflict solely from the interaction of ontological commitment, naturalized epistemology, and the insulation thesis you have introduced.
