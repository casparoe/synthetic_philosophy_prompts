Having retired from an experimental career in condensed-matter physics and recently turned to philosophy of science, I find myself repeatedly encountering the paired ideas that there are no skyhooks, only cranes, and that reductionism should be greedy—both often wielded together to reject any suggestion that global organizational constraints in complex systems possess independent explanatory force. In a recent seminar, for instance, an interlocutor appealed to both notions to dismiss the argument that non-ergodic dynamics governing protein folding, or topological protection in certain quantum materials, represent genuine downward-causal conditions rather than mere descriptive abbreviations exhaustively reducible to lower-level interactions.

Could you provide a careful disambiguation of the precise theses hiding in this cluster of slogans? I want a menu of distinct readings—ordered from the trivially true to the radically metaphysical—with a clear verdict on each: platitude, defensible substantive claim, or false. For each reading, please specify whether it actually supports the dismissal of downward causation in biological or physical organization, whether it commits one to greedy reductionism about scientific explanation, and which interpretation is typically being appealed to when these phrases are used to terminate debate. I am not seeking citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; my aim is solely to map the logical geography of the claims, especially as they bear on cases where macroscopic boundary conditions appear to constrain microscopic dynamics in ways that resist complete bottom-up reconstruction.
