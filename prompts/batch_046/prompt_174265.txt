I'm trying to nail down my own rules for when you can actually read beliefs or social institutions off of material stuff without just projecting modern ideas onto it. Here are my gut calls on some made-up but realistic excavation scenarios.

1. There's a settlement with a big circular ring of postholes, no hearth, no storage pits inside, just the ring and a few broken drinking cups scattered around. I'd say yes—this points to some shared ceremonial belief, probably a gathering ritual, not just practical shelter.

2. In the same region, one house has its doorway aligned exactly with the summer solstice sunrise, but every other building is randomly oriented. I'd say no—don't infer an institutional solar cult from one doorway; it's probably just that family being particular.

3. A cemetary has bodies buried north-south with hands on the chest, except one adult male is east-west with his hands behind his back, and he's carrying a dagger made of metal that doesn't match local ore. I'd say yes—this is about social boundaries, maybe an outsider or executed person, so we can infer an institutional rule about belonging and exclusion.

4. A storage room is full of hundreds of clay tokens shaped like sheep, all snapped in half and sorted by size. I'd say this is clearly an accounting institution, not a religious belief about sheep.

5. In a trash pit near that room, there's one intact token made of imported lapis lazuli, same sheep shape. Despite being fancy and foreign, I'd say don't read belief into this—it's just a lost luxury item or garbage. No ritual deposit.

6. In child graves, we find dozens of identical miniature bronze axes placed near the feet every single time. I'd say yes, this is belief—probably protection or a hoped-for social role after death.

7. In a workshop dump, we find dozens of the same miniature axes, but broken and melted. Here I'd say no belief at all—just manufacturing failures.

8. There's a single obsidian blade from far away, carefully wrapped in cloth and hidden inside a grain storage jar. Here I'd say yes, this feels like intentional protection or ritual care—totally different from case 5, even though both are single imported objects.

My verdicts feel mostly consistent but case 5 vs 8 is driving me nuts. I need a principle that tells me when material culture supports inference about belief or institution, and when it's just noise, practical use, or garbage. What's the actual rule? Or do I have to drop one of these? And please say what the principle predicts for a case I haven't listed—say, finding the exact same geometric pattern carved on every doorframe in a village, but never on any portable object. Also, is there a real difference in the principle between inferring an institution like accounting vs a mental belief like solar worship, or should they be treated the same?
