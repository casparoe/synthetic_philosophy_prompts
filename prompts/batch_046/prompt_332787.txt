People keep throwing around "an unjust law is no law at all" like it ends any argument about obeying bad statutes. Can you break down the precise claims that slogan might be making? I'm thinking theres at least three: one about legal validity (Fuller's internal morality of law?), one about moral duty, and one about systemic corruption where a system gets so rotten it stops counting as law. For each version, say whether it's trivial, whether it's actually false, and whether it does the work people want it to do when they invoke it to justify breaking rules. Then map them onto the Hart-Fuller debate specifically - which ones Hart's rule-of-recognition view can absorb without breaking, which ones Fuller actually endorsed in the 1958 exchange, and whether theres a hidden fourth reading about institutional dignity that neither of them addressed. I keep seeing this used to defend civil disobedience but I'm not convinced the slogan alone gets you there without smuggling in extra premeises about democratic legitimacy.
