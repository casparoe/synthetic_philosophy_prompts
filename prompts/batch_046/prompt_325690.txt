Question: Explain the lexical-treshold view in negative utilitarianism. In particlar, why do defenders claim pleasure and suffering become incommensurable once the treshold is crossed, and apply this to a comparison between World A (ten billion people with mild contentment) and World B (the same population plus one being experiencing suffering held at 99% of the lexical treshold — just below the line).

Answer: The lexical-treshold view holds that severe suffering has lexical priority over any amount of pleasure, so above the line they're incommensurable, like comparing height to temperature. Because the suffering in World B is at 99%, it hasn't triggered the lexical block, so we do ordinary aggregation: the ten billion mild pleasures still outweigh the near-treshold pain, meaning World B is roughly equivalent to World A since the negative contribution hasn't reached dominance. Once that same suffering crosses to 100%, it acquires infinite weight and makes World B lexically worse regardless of the pleasure elsewhere. So the treshold acts like a physical step function.
