A common way of presenting Bostrom's simulation argument is through an urn analogy: imagine a vast urn containing mostly black balls (representing simulated conscious observers) and only a handful of white balls (representing observers in base reality). You reach in, draw one ball, and observe its colour only indirectly — perhaps through a filter that might be unreliable. Because the black balls vastly outnumber the white ones, you should conclude that yours is almost certainly black. This analogy is often used to licence a direct probabilistic update — that rational belief requires assigning high credence to being simulated — and to rebut objections that our first-person access gives us special reason to favour the 'white ball' hypothesis.
