For an independent study in my philosophy of medicine elective, I am examining Thomas Szasz's argument that mental illness functions as a metaphor for problems in living rather than as a literal medical pathology, but I want to move beyond the standard schizophrenia-versus-broken-leg comparison that dominates introductory texts. Please provide an example of a conceptual issue that emerges when Szasz's distinction is applied to the contemporary clinical framework of moral injury in combat veterans—specifically, cases in which a service member exhibits symptoms structurally similar to post-traumatic stress disorder, yet attributes the distress not to an external threat but to a self-acknowledged violation of their own ethical commitments. The example should expose a concrete point of tension between classifying the condition as a treatable psychiatric disorder and classifying it as a non-medical problem in living, with particular attention to a scenario where the boundary between these two classifications genuinely collapses rather than being merely ambiguous in language.
