I am preparing a position statement for a graduate workshop in aesthetics—specifically on whether and how expert testimony can guide taste without undermining autonomy—and I want an internal consistency check before I present. I have been discussing these issues with colleagues who come at them from epistemology rather than art theory, and I realize I have never laid out my own commitments in one place to see whether they actually cohere. Here is where I stand. I hold that aesthetic judgments carry what I call normative resonance: to call a painting or a piece of music beautiful is not merely to report private pleasure but to implicitly commit oneself to its being worthy of sustained attention by others with appropriate sensibilities. At the same time, I think expert testimony in aesthetics is genuinely epistemic—critics and historians can possess knowledge about a work's internal structural relations, its embedding in a tradition, or its technical execution that I lack, and this can correct my initial verdict rather than merely supplement it. I also maintain that aesthetic autonomy is non-negotiable: judgment must ultimately be anchored in my own perceptual encounter, which makes blind deference to authority conceptually incoherent, not just bad practice. To navigate this, I endorse what I term calibrated deference: I revise my judgments in response to expert input, but only up to a resistance threshold where my direct perceptual engagement with the work refuses to align. I deny, however, that there are universal, tradition-independent rules for beauty; at best we find family resemblances and locally stable norms. I further believe that aesthetic education genuinely transforms taste, yet I insist this transformation is not reducible to acquiring propositional knowledge about art history or technique. Finally, I hold that persistent aesthetic disagreement between competent observers is rarely faultless—if two people genuinely conflict over a work's value, at least one is making a cognitive error rather than expressing an incommensurable preference. Which of these commitments are genuinely incompatible, which pairs only appear to pull in opposite directions but dissolve on inspection, and what is the least costly revision to restore coherence? Please rank the tensions by severity, and for each indicate precisely which commitment must weaken, be reinterpreted, or be abandoned.
