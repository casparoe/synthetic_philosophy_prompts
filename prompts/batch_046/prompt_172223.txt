I read a summary of David Benatar's asymmetry on an unnamed philosophy forum that frames it as follows: the presence of pleasure is good for existing people, but its absence is not bad for anyone; the presence of pain is bad, and its absence is good even for people who will never exist; therefore, bringing someone into existence always introduces a real harm without avoiding any corresponding deprivation, since there is no subject who misses out on pleasure; the author adds that this asymmetry demonstrates total utilitarianism is incoherent, because it requires symmetrically weighing possible and actual welfare; and he claims antinatalism follows purely from this axiological structure, without empirical premises about suffering being widespread. The post also implies that Benatar must be treating merely possible people as beneficiaries of avoided pain—something like a modal realist commitment—though Benatar himself denies this. This sounds mostly correct to me, but I am unsure whether the asymmetry really undermines total utilitarianism as described, and whether the modal-realist reading is a necessary part of the view or just a critic's misinterpretation.
