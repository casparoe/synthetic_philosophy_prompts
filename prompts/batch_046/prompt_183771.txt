I recently heard a podcast summarize Richard Rorty's antirepresentationalism in the following way. Rorty denies that beliefs mirror an intrinsic nature of reality, so the language-world relation is purely causal rather than representational. The speaker then claimed that this means there is no philosophical distinction between describing a table as solid and describing it as mostly empty space—both are just different vocabularies, with no deeper fact about which one gets the table right. Next, the speaker said that for Rorty truth is only a compliment we pay to sentences that work within our current language game, so scientific progress is merely vocabulary change, not approximation to reality. Finally, the summary argued that because Rorty rejects the mirror-of-nature metaphor, he must think empirical evidence is irrelevant to belief choice, since evidence can only constrain belief if it represents the world. The speaker concluded that Rorty is therefore a radical relativist for whom justification collapses into social agreement. I have a feeling this bundles together accurate claims with subtle overstatements, but I am not sure where the line is. Which parts of this summary fairly capture Rorty's actual distinction between causation and representation, which parts wrongly extend his rejection of intrinsic nature into a denial of physical reality or predictive success, and does his position actually make empirical evidence irrelevant? Most importantly, does the conclusion that Rorty eliminates all normative distinctions between justified belief and mere group opinion follow from the accurate parts, or does it depend on the misrepresentations?
