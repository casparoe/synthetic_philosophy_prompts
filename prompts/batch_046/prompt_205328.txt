I am a sixth-form student serving as the elected pupil representative on my college's Conduct and Appeals Panel, and I am drafting this request for a formal opinion to be circulated before our meeting on Thursday. We are hearing an appeal from Marcus, a Year 12 pupil who received a formal sanction for unauthorised collaboration on a chemistry coursework assignment after being reported by a fellow student, Priya. During the preliminary review it came to light that Priya had received an informal, unrecorded caution the previous term for inadequate citation in an English literature essay—an infringement that Marcus's advocate now argues strips her of any legitimate authority to hold him to account. Our college's recently revised Code of Mutual Accountability introduces a distinction, not found in standard disciplinary guidance, between 'procedural standing'—the institutional right to initiate a complaint—and 'moral standing'—the personal authority to blame without being undermined by one's own comparable failings. Several panel members hold that Priya's prior lapse automatically vitiates both forms of standing and that Marcus's sanction should therefore be quashed; others maintain that hypocrisy is a defect in the blamer's character that may diminish the moral weight of her accusation but does not annul the wrongness of Marcus's conduct or the college's duty to respond.

I should be grateful if you could draft the panel's decision memo, recommending either that we uphold Marcus's sanction, dismiss the complaint on standing grounds, or apply a clearly defined intermediate measure, with reasoning that gives full weight to the strongest consideration on the losing side. I am presently studying an introductory course in moral philosophy, and in discussions with friends I have found myself torn between viewing hypocrisy as a kind of moral contamination that infects every judgement it touches and viewing it as a separable personal flaw that leaves the underlying facts intact; I want the memo to navigate that tension explicitly rather than resolve it by administrative fiat. Please write the response in British English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, as the document must rely entirely on its own argument.
