In my dissertation framework I define a safe Pareto improvement as a policy shift that weakly dominates the status quo in every state of nature within our epistemic set, with strict improvement in at least one dimension in each state. Because dominance holds state-by-state, every rational agent—regardless of her true preference profile—should endorse the change ex ante, which makes it puzzling why democratic theory still treats such reforms as requiring aggregation mechanisms subject to Arrow-type constraints. Is the mistake that ex-ante unanimity across worlds collapses once agents update on the true state and discover they are in different positions, or am I wrong at a deeper level in thinking that state-wise Pareto dominance can substitute for social choice altogether?
