I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I also sit on the advisory board for the Riverside Community Wellness Fund — long story, don't ask — and we're deadlocked on a pilot grant. The researchers want to build a "cross-modal pleasure translator" that takes subjective ratings from music therapy, exercise groups, and literacy classes and collapses them into a single "hedonic point" score so we can compare cost-effectiveness. My co-chair says this is just practical measurement; I keep flashing back to a philosophy course I audited where we wrestled with the heterogeneity problem — like, is the pleasure of solving a chess problem even the same kind of mental state as the pleasure of warm sunlight? If pleasures don't share a common qualitative feature, then adding them up might be a category error rather than a statistical challenge. My roommate thinks I'm overanalyzing, but I need to be sure before Thursday.

We vote Thursday, and I have to circulate a memo recommending whether we fund the pilot as-is, reject it outright, or approve it only if they switch to seperate dimensional tracks with no aggregate score. The memo should treat the researchers' funding-comparability need seriously — it's a real constraint — but also engage the philosophical worry honestly, including the strong modal version that there's no nearby possible world where these states feel like tokens of one shared kind. Give a concrete recommendation with a timeline inside the six-month pilot window, and don't brush off the best argument for the single-scale approach; just say why it still loses. Please don't include a bibliography or citation list — board members won't read them — and write it so someone without web access could follow every step.
