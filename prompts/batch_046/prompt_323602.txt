I'm preparing a presentation for a metaphysics of mind seminar and want a consistency audit before I commit to a view. Here is my current package of commitments regarding personal survival: (1) psychological continuity—preservation of memory, character, and rational capacities—matters for survival, not bodily continuity; (2) I distinguish "channel-continuous" continuity, which requires an unbroken causal-spatiotemporal path without termination and replication, from "archive-continuous" continuity, in which a pattern is copied after the original is destroyed, and I hold that only the former is metaphysically sufficient for survival; (3) nevertheless, I judge that the standard teletransporter—scanning, destroying the original, and reconstructing an identical body elsewhere—is a genuine case of survival, since the informational structure is preserved and the causal history is transparent; (4) I reject closest-continuer analyses because I think identity should not depend on whether competing replicas happen to exist; (5) in fission, where my brain is split and each half is implanted into a different body with full psychological function, neither successor is identical to me, though both stand in an equal "survives-as" relation; (6) I am a reductionist about persons—there is no further fact of identity beyond physical and psychological continuity relations; (7) I think practical concern for one's future tracks psychological continuity, but I deny that these practical attitudes determine the metaphysical facts; (8) gradual neuron-by-neuron synthetic replacement over years preserves identity continuously, even when every original cell is eventually replaced.

Are these jointly tenable? Specifically, does the channel-continuity requirement in (2) undermine my acceptance of teletransporter survival in (3)? Is there tension between my reductionism in (6) and my denial in (7) that practical concerns determine identity, given that my reason for (3) seems drawn from exactly those concerns? And is the pairing of (4) and (5) genuinely contradictory, or does the tension dissolve once we separate identity from survival? Please deliver an audit: rank the tensions by severity, state for each which commitment must be revised or abandoned and what that costs, flag any tensions that are merely apparent, and treat all eight commitments as equally available for revision. Keep your response within 500 words, with tensions clearly labeled and ordered.
