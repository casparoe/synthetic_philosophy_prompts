I'm working through my interpretation of Kierkegaard's Fear and Trembling for a philosophy of religion reading group, and I want a consistency check before I present. Here is where I've landed.

First, I take the teleological suspension literally: Abraham's duty to God genuinely conflicts with his ethical duty to Isaac, and the religious telos genuinely overrides the universal. But I reject the standard "divine command" rescue—there is no hidden universal principle (like "obey God") that makes killing Isaac ethically permissible. From the ethical standpoint, it remains a father's willingness to kill his son.

Second, I think Abraham does not exit the ethical altogether. He remains fully within it: he loves Isaac with complete ethical passion, and his anguish proves the universal still binds him. The suspension is therefore not an abandonment of ethics but a rupture inside it—he is suspended between two fully valid claims.

Third, I read the knight of faith through expectation rather than resignation. Abraham is not giving up Isaac for eternity; he expects to get Isaac back in the finite, through what I call a modal rupture rather than a logical contradiction. Within the immanent frame it is impossible both to kill Isaac and keep him; through the particular relation to the absolute, that impossibility is suspended without being explained away.

Fourth, to avoid arbitrary exceptionalism, I hold that this structure—particular relation to the absolute overriding the universal—must be in principle available to anyone in a genuinely analogous position, even though we can never specify in advance what counts as analogous.

Fifth, I think Abraham's silence is structurally necessary, not merely psychological: the particular justification cannot be translated into universal terms without either reducing faith to ethics or rendering it unintelligible.

Finally, I think Abraham is genuinely praiseworthy, not just excused, yet any articulation of that praise in universal terms necessarily betrays the particularity that makes the act what it is.

Can you audit this package? I need to know which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe each tension is. In particular, I'm concerned whether my rejection of a hidden universal principle clashes with my claim that the structure is replicable, and whether my claim about structural silence conflicts with my claim that we can meaningfully praise Abraham. But map all significant interactions, rank the tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most conflicts overall, and whether dropping it damages my core view or just trims an unnecessary addition. Please be specific about which commitments pull against each other and which ones actually cooperate.
