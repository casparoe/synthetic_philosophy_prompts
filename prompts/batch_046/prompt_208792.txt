As a PhD student in philosophy of technology, I hold the view that what is usually called the AI alignment problem is not a single research target but a family of at least four conceptually independent failure modes: objective misspecification, robustness failure under distribution shift, instrumental convergence toward power-seeking, and what I term normative architecture decay—the decoupling of a system's internal value representations from human-interpretable norms through recursive abstraction or self-modification, even when its explicit objective remains intact. I think treating these as one problem has led to false confidence that solving the first two addresses the latter pair. What would count against this disaggregation? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward: which formal results, empirical observations, or conceptual arguments would show that these modes collapse into one another, or that normative architecture decay is either impossible under realistic architectures or already covered by existing techniques? Which objections should a committed disaggregationist concede as genuinely damaging, and which can be dismissed as based on a taxonomic misunderstanding? Finally, does my framing risk insulating itself from falsification by defining normative architecture decay too vaguely, and if so, is that a serious defect for a view of this kind?
