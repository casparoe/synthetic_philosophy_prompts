I've been trying to wrap my head around Zahavi's handicap principle for a bio/phil course I'm taking, and I keep running into this analogy people lean on — that a peacock's tail is basically nature's version of a luxury watch or a Veblen good. The mapping usually goes like this: the tail is the watch, the peacock is the buyer, the peahen is the onlooker judging status, and the metabollic cost of growing all those feathers is the price tag. In debates people use this to argue that human conspicous consumption is just evolutionary leftover — that buying a $10k handbag works the same way, so you shouldn't judge people for wanting status symbols because we're all just peacocks deep down.

But I'm skeptical about how far this actually holds, and I was hoping you could audit it properly. I want the explicit correspondence laid out — tail to watch, bird to buyer, etc. — and then exactly where the mapping breaks. Like, a watch doesn't physically impair your survival; it's just money you could spend elsewhere, whereas the tail actually makes flight harder and attracts predators. There's also this other category I read about, index signals, where the display is honest because it's physically impossible to fake (like body size correlating with voice depth), which seems like a totally different mechanism from borrowing a watch for an hour or buying one with debt. So is the luxury-watch analogy quietly smuggling in the idea that all costly human displays are handicaps, when some might just be index signals or Spence-style screening?

Also, does the analogy import consumer-capitalist baggage — choice, social class, advertising — that just doesn't apply to birds? If so, does the conclusion that human luxury consumption is "just biology" survive once those disanologies are on the table? And would a different analogy — maybe potlatch-style destructive feasting, or even proof-of-work in blockchain — do a better job of capturing what a handicap actually is without dragging in all this shopping-mall baggage? One more thing: I keep getting confused about whether Zahavi was trying to replace Fisherian runaway selection or just adding to it, and I wonder if that changes what the "cost" is actually doing in the analogy.
