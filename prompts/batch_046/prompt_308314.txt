In our philosophy of collective action seminar we've hit a wall on joint intentionality. The we-mode camp (Searle, Tuomela) insists joint action requires an irreducibly plural mental state; the individualists (Bratman-style) say interdependant I-intentions plus common knowledge do the job. We all agree on the house-building case and nobody denies people cooperate, but for two semesters no one has moved. My hunch is that the stall hides a deeper split: is collective intentionality representational — about shared mental content — or deontic — about mutual obligations that bind even when no one is representing them? Or is this just verbal confusion over 'we'? Maybe one side is asking about coordination mechanisms and the other about the ontologgy of group agents? Diagnose it: is there a hidden empirical question (kids, animals, AI?), a formal gap, or just bedrock intuitions about plural subjects? And honestly, what would count as progress here — a model of distributed commitment, a conceptual distinction, or admitting the debate is malformed?
