I'm preparing a seminar paper on the ontology of explanation in complex systems, and I've become stuck on a dispute that recurs across philosophy of biology, climate science, and epidemiological modeling without ever reaching resolution. The dispute concerns Daniel Dennett's framework of the physical, design, and intentional stances, and specifically whether the "real patterns" isolated by the design stance—functional regularities that license counterfactual predictions yet resist lossless compression into physical-law descriptions—carry genuine ontological weight or are merely predictive heuristics available to cognitively limited agents. One camp insists that any pattern worthy of the name must be ultimately reducible, in principle, to physical dynamics; the design stance is a pragmatic shortcut, not a window onto autonomous structure. The opposing camp maintains that design-level patterns possess irreducible explanatory and constraining power: they shape outcomes in ways that bottom-up descriptions alone cannot capture, even if those descriptions are complete.

My working hypothesis is that the disagreement has stalled not because of any missing empirical result—everyone accepts the predictive success of design-level models in evolutionary dynamics, climate feedback loops, and epidemiological forecasting—but because the two sides are implicitly demanding different criteria for explanatory adequacy, with neither side able to argue the other into accepting its standard. More specifically, I suspect that hidden behind the standard binary is a family of intermediate "diagnostic" stances: modeling practices in volcanic climate-forcing studies and epidemic trajectory forecasting, where systems are treated as if they pursue stability or equilibrium goals, yet without any attribution of mental states or full functional design. If such intermediate stances are conceptually coherent and practically indispensable, then Dennett's tripartite scheme may be incomplete, and the debate may actually concern where pragmatic boundaries should be drawn rather than whether design patterns are "really" there.

Could you diagnose the structure of this impasse? I want to know whether it is verbal, substantive, or rooted in divergent starting intuitions about what explanation must accomplish; whether there is a concealed empirical or formal question—perhaps about computability of reduction, causal closure, or predictive equivalence—actually driving the deadlock; and whether these intermediate diagnostic stances clarify the disagreement or merely multiply categories without resolving the underlying tension. Crucially, please explain what would count as genuine progress given your diagnosis: a formal result about pattern compression, a conceptual distinction that dissolves the opposition, an argument that the question is ill-posed, or something else entirely. Support your analysis by engaging with what each side actually says about predictive success and ontological commitment, rather than offering a survey of interpretations.
