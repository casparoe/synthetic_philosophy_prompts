As a philosopher of action and mind, I am trying to map the conceptual landscape of embedded agency—specifically, the problem of how an agent situated within a larger causal and normative system can possess reasons that are genuinely independent of that system's goals. To avoid well-trodden summaries, I want you to work with a distinction I think is too rarely made explicit: between ontological embedding, in which the agent is literally a subsystem lacking any independent causal boundary, and epistemic embedding, in which the agent retains separate ontology but its beliefs and preferences are causally downstream of the containing system. Assuming that distinction is live, which exactly five open questions, lines of work, or implications in this area should occupy limited philosophical attention over the next few years if our aim is to determine whether embedded agents can ever have authoritative reasons to restructure the systems that embed them? I want a defended prioritization: for each, indicate why it is upstream of others, how tractable it is for conceptual philosophers rather than empirical researchers, and what kind of argument or thought experiment would change its rank. Deliver the response as a numbered list containing exactly five entries, each limited to one or two sentences, with no introductory or concluding paragraph.
