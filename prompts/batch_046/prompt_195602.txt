Compose a philosophical dialogue between two interlocutors, Mira and Theo, on whether virtual objects in persistent, cross-platform environments can be direct subjects of harm. Mira should defend an institutional-realist position: she holds that virtual artifacts can become nodes in extended economic and legal institutions, and that destroying such an artifact can damage the institutional structure itself, not merely harm individual users. Theo should defend mediated interactionism: he holds that all virtual entities are representations whose effects are always mediated through human users and protocols, so any "harm" to a virtual object is reducible to dysfunction in human interests or contractual relations.

Ground their exchange in a concrete scenario: a decentralized autonomous organization uses a unique virtual artifact—an algorithmically generated deed existing only within one virtual environment but recognized as collateral across several platforms—to secure a real-world obligation. A malicious actor corrupts the artifact's underlying data, making it unrecoverable. Mira should argue that the artifact itself has been destroyed and that the harm is to the DAO's institutional integrity, analogous to destroying a charter document. Theo should counter that the harm is to the members' contractual positions and to protocol reliability; the "corruption" is only a state-change that disrupts human-level arrangements, with no independent object-level victim.

Require that the dialogue feature genuine back-and-forth: Theo should challenge Mira's analogy to physical legal instruments by noting that physical instruments possess causal continuity independent of any current representation, whereas virtual artifacts are entirely constituted by present data states. Mira should reply that institutional reality is representation-dependent—paper deeds are also representations sustained by legal recognition—and that cross-platform acknowledgment gives the virtual artifact distributed causal continuity. Theo should then ask whether Mira is treating functional roles as ontological substrates, committing a category error. Mira should concede that she does not ascribe intrinsic value to the artifact, but insist that harm can target an institutional node without being fully reducible to harm to each member, comparing it to damaging a corporation's charter without harming any particular shareholder.

Specify that both characters must be equally competent, neither a strawman, and that each must respond directly to the other's preceding point rather than talking past them. The tone should be formal and precise. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific published works.
