I am a doctoral candidate in philosophy of decision theory, drafting a chapter on rational commitment when threats operate through counterfactual dependency rather than actual causal influence. I am comparing how different frameworks handle the joint tension between the counterfactual mugging scenario and the XOR blackmail puzzle, specifically in the variant where the adversary can observe the agent’s decision procedure but not its realized internal state, and where the payoff structure is symmetric across the relevant possible worlds.

For this comparison, I have articulated four candidate stances. First, the causal-decision-theoretic refusal: the agent declines to comply because its present action cannot causally influence the already-fixed counterfactual prediction. Second, the evidential-decision-theoretic compliance: the agent complies because doing so correlates with being in the world where the favorable prediction or safe state holds. Third, the updateless or functional-decision-theoretic pre-commitment: the agent binds itself in advance to a policy that renders the adverse counterfactual prediction false, accepting present costs to secure long-run modal stability. Fourth, a position I am tentatively calling modal isolationism: the agent treats all counterfactual dependencies as epistemically opaque unless they are mediated by a shared computational substrate that the agent can directly verify; consequently, it refuses both to pay for counterfactual correlation and to pre-commit to prediction-manipulating policies, on the grounds that unverified modal links lack the transparency required for rational commitment.

Please rank these four stances—causal refusal, evidential compliance, updateless pre-commitment, and modal isolationism—from the most adequate to the least adequate in resolving the joint tension described above, without generating either self-defeating pre-commitments or infinite regress in belief updating. Provide a single ordered list numbered 1 through 4, and for each entry include exactly one sentence of justification that explains how that stance specifically interacts with the symmetry of the XOR blackmail structure and the counterfactual dependency of the mugging scenario, rather than appealing to general virtues of the underlying decision framework.
