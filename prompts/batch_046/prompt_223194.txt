I'm developing a position for a paper on collective memory and historical accountability and want a consistency check before I defend it in print. I hold the following commitments:

(a) Professional historiography should take epistemic precedence over communal memory when the two conflict on verifiable empirical claims—if the archival record contradicts a community's founding narrative, the narrative ought to yield.

(b) Collective memory is not merely a defective version of history but possesses its own normative legitimacy as a mode of social self-knowledge and group identity-constitution.

(c) Communities have what I call mnemonic sovereignty: a qualified right to preserve identity-forming narratives even when those narratives diverge from the archival record, provided the divergence causes no present harm and does not deny documented injuries to others.

(d) The archival record is never raw data; it is always mediated by selection, categorization, and institutional power, so treating it as an unproblematic neutral arbiter begs the question.

(e) In cases of documented genocide or systematic atrocity, the moral imperative to honor victims requires subordinating comforting communal myths to the evidentiary record, regardless of the identity-cost to the community maintaining the myth.

(f) The distinction between collective memory and institutionalized history is not binary but a gradient of formalization and peer review, which means the normative distinction between them is one of degree rather than kind.

My concern is that these pull in incompatible directions—particularly whether mnemonic sovereignty survives my priority for archives in atrocity cases, whether archival mediation undermines my willingness to let archives override memory in ordinary cases, and whether treating the memory/history boundary as a gradient dissolves the normative distinctiveness I want to grant memory in (b). I am unsure which tensions are genuine, which dissolve on inspection, and what revision does the least damage.

Please audit this commitment set: distinguish real inconsistencies from merely apparent ones; rank the genuine tensions by severity, specifying for each what I would have to revise and what that revision costs; assess whether (c) and (e) can be reconciled without ad hoc exception-making; and evaluate whether (d) weakens or can be qualified to support (a). Do not provide a bibliography or reading list.
