Set out the desiderata that a theory of life must satisfy if it is to count as adequate. Rather than surveying existing positions, I want the specification first: what phenomena it must explain (e.g. metabolism, reproduction, evolutionary adaptability), which borderline cases it must adjudicate without contradiction — including not only viruses and dormant spores, but also synthetic protocells that replicate using expanded genetic alphabets yet lack evolutionary descent, and self-sustaining hydrothermal reaction networks that maintain homeostasis without heredity — and which distinctions it must preserve, such as that between an individual organism and a collective living system, or between being alive and being a functional living system. For each requirement, state the motivating case or theoretical cost of abandoning it, and identify which single desideratum eliminates the largest share of popular accounts (functional, genetic, evolutionary, or autopoietic). Flag any pair of requirements you suspect are jointly unsatisfiable. Conclude with a brief scoring of functionalism and autopoiesis against your list, but keep the weight on the criteria themselves. Write formally, in British English.
