I hold the view that in reasoning contexts where the joint state space is intractably large and an agent possesses no prior structural knowledge linking two variables, the presumption of independence should be treated not as a defeasible heuristic but as a normatively binding default prior—specifically, that correlation requires positive evidential justification rather than independence requiring justification, and that this reversal is the only computationally tractable approximation to rational belief revision that avoids systematic overfitting in high-dimensional spaces. On this account, heuristic arguments that invoke the presumption—such as the inference that unknown causal mechanisms should not be assumed to align with observed correlations absent demonstrated linkage—ought to be formalized as constraints on admissible prior distributions rather than as informal rules of thumb. What would count against this position? I want the evidence and arguments that should genuinely move me, sorted by how decisive they would be: formal results showing that independence-presuming priors systematically underperform relative to weak-dependence priors in predictive or decision-theoretic tasks; conceptual demonstrations that the presumption either collapses into an unmotivated metaphysical claim about the structure of the world or becomes an unfalsifiable methodological stipulation; concrete theoretical or empirical cases in which treating independence as the default led to systematically worse outcomes than treating weak dependence as the default; and an honest assessment of whether defenders have qualified the view—say, by restricting it to domains lacking any representational structure—into a position that risks nothing and therefore explains nothing. In particular, is there a coherent middle ground between treating independence as a binding prior and treating it as a flexible heuristic, or does any attempt to formalize the presumption necessarily strip away the contextual adaptability that makes heuristic reasoning useful in the first place? I also want to know what the position’s own defenders would concede as genuinely damaging, not merely what critics allege, and whether there is a result—formal, empirical, or conceptual—that would force abandonment of the view rather than mere refinement.
