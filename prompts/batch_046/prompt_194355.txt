I sit on the state disability funding board, and we're stuck on whether to renew Program Meridian or Program Echo. Meridian serves people with congenital insensitivity to pain, Echo serves people with severe chronic neuropathic pain. Both use the same 10-point life-satisfaction intrument, and Meridian clients score 8.3 on average while Echo clients score 3.4. The staff economist says we should compare directly and shift more money to Meridian since it delivers more welfare per dollar. But one board member says that's an illegitimate interpersonal comparison - maybe the analgesic clients just have a compressed affective range, so an 8.3 isn't "better" than a 3.4 for someone in constant pain. Then someone floated this "range-normalization" idea: divide each score by the person's estimated emotional bandwidth from our neuropsych data, so we compare "utilization rates" instead of raw satisfaction. That sounds clever but might just smuggle in an objective theory of wellbeing. We vote in ten days. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend either (1) fund by raw scores, (2) reject comparison and split the budget evenly, or (3) adopt range-normalization with explicit safeguards. Take the staff comparison seriously - don't just dismiss it - and also explain why range-normalization could be worse than useless. Make it something the board could actually vote on.
