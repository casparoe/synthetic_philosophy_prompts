I'm helping put together a small local museum exhibit about a recycling plant disaster from the 80s — a bunch of workers died because the safety checks were split between three subcontractors and each one assumed the others were handling the toxic waste reports. I wanted a panel that compares this kind of bureaucratic distance, where no single person pulls the trigger but the system still kills people, to the way some historians describe the Holocaust as partly a modern administrative failure. My uncle, who's actually a historian, got really angry and said comparing anything to the Holocaust is always wrong because it's uniquely evil and can't be used as a benchmark for other tragedies. I feel like he's right that it could come off disrespectfull, but I also think the pattern about modern systems breaking responsibility into tiny pieces is real and important to show. Should I drop the comparison entirely, or is there a way to talk about the bureaucratic pattern without disrespecting the uniqueness of what happened? I want a real answer that separates what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who actually study this stuff — don't present one view as the consensus if it isn't.
