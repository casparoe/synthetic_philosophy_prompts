hey, quick philosophical question from a software engineer with zero formal background in philosophy. I build recommendation and filter systems. We have this tuning parameter that trades off false positives vs false negatives. If we set it loose, we wrongly block like 200 random users a day across the whole platform but catch almost all the spam. If we set it tight, we only wrongly block about 20 people, but more spam slips through. My manager - smart guy with an MBA - says the tight setting is definatly the ethical choice cause you're minimizing total harm, 20 is less then 200, basic math. But here's the twist: for users writing in this one specific language with maybe 2 million speakers, the false positive rate is always way higher because our training corpus for it is tiny. So under that "tight" setting, the 5 people getting wrongly blocked every day are basically always from that same small group, while english-speaking users are almost never affected. My manager says "well 5 is still smaller then 20, and you're weighting them differently just cause of identity politics." But something feels off about saying those 5 people are just interchangeable units of harm that we can swap around with the other 15. Is there an actual philosophical argument for why treating harm as totally swapable across different people is wrong? Or am I just being sentimental about the language thing? Would really appreciate someone walking me through the logic here.
