Write an essay on wether Pascal's pairing of justice and force in the Pensées really leaves any room for saying a regime is unjust, or if it just collapses into the idea that whoever holds the monopoly on violence is automatically right. The standard read is that justice needs force to be effective but force without justice is tyrannical, which sounds like a balance. But push on the circularity worry: if justice is only recognizable once it's backed by power, then calling a powerful regime unjust looks like saying it's both enforced and not-enforced at once. Weigh the reply that Pascal isn't defining justice normatively but describing its conditions of existence — like saying fire needs oxygen doesn't mean oxygen is fire. Then inject a weirder case: imagine a small community where norms are enforced not by a state but by decentralized social shunning, with no central authority holding the monopoly. Does Pascal's argument still apply, or does the dispersal of force break his framework? Reach a firm conclusion: either Pascal's view can condemn actual regimes without contradiction, or it can't, and say what that means for using him as a resource in political philosophy today. Don't just sit on the fence.
