Consider the hermeneutic practice Galileo advances in his writing to Christina of Lorraine: scriptural statements about the natural world are to be read non-literally whenever they conflict with conclusions reached by necessary demonstration in natural philosophy, but are protected from revision in domains — salvation, morality, and unsettled science — where such demonstration is absent or irrelevant. The boundary of accommodation is thus fixed not by theology but by the epistemic status of the competing claim. What philosophical commitments about biblical authority, the limits of human reason, and the division of labour between natural philosophy and theology does this selective rule presuppose?
