Write a research proposal for a single-semester project (about 6,000 words) addressing whether the institutions-versus-geography-versus-culture debate about national wealth is resolvable at all. Frame the problem not as selecting among the three explanations, but as testing whether they operate at incommensurable temporal scales—centuries-long institutional persistence, static geographic constraints, and intermediate generational cultural transmission—so that any single counterfactual test necessarily mis-specifies at least one mechanism. Identify the specific methodological obstacle: synthetic counterfactual constructions applied to colonial boundary shifts appear to isolate institutional change, yet they rarely hold geographic conditions constant across the relevant time horizons, making causal identification circular. State your comparative case strategy, explain why this framing avoids the standard literature-review format, describe what success would look like (e.g., showing that institutional divergence correlates with economic divergence only over intervals too brief for institutional persistence to be causally active), specify failure conditions (complete confounding of institutional and geographic variables in all available comparisons), and assess honestly whether this project can be completed in one semester without original archival work. Write in American English, and do not include or request a bibliography, citation list, or literature review by named source.
