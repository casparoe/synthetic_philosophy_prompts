I am a novelist preparing to defend an interpretive thesis at an interdisciplinary seminar on cosmology and narrative form next Thursday. My position is that Kurt Gödel's rotating-universe solution to Einstein's field equations—specifically, the existence of closed timelike curves within a homogeneous rotating metric—should be read not as a merely physical curiosity but as a formal demonstration that temporal succession is globally non-orientable: traversing a sufficiently rotated geodesic returns a traveler to a causally prior point, collapsing the asymmetry between narrative beginning and ending in the same structural manner that a Möbius strip collapses the distinction between interior and exterior. From this I argue that any ontology treating past and future as open and asymmetrically ordered is incompatible with the geometry Gödel derived, and that fiction writers have long presupposed such an asymmetry without philosophical defense. The audience comprises two general relativists who regard the Gödel metric as an unphysical artifact of unrealistic symmetry conditions; three philosophers of science who hold that cosmological models ought not be mined for metaphysical conclusions; a narrative theorist specializing in temporal logic within fiction; and a phenomenologist who will maintain that the lived experience of duration is structurally immune to geometric argument. I need a speaker's prep sheet, not a defense: the objections ordered by likelihood and damage, phrased as each constituency would actually phrase them, with for each a concise reply suitable to a two-minute exchange, a clear notation of what must be conceded and what must be held, and an explicit identification of the weakest point in that reply. Crucially, include the objection I am hoping nobody raises—the one that targets the precise inferential step from the mathematical existence of closed timelike curves to the claim of global non-orientability applied to narrative time. Conclude with the single reply that must land if the analogy between geometric rotation and narrative structure is to survive the session.
