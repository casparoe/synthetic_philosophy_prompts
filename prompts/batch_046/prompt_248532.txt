so in some forums people have been going in circles for years about which layer is the "real" home of meaning. the frame camp says meaning is in the interpretive setup -- like how a record player needs the right mechanics to turn grooves into sound, without that frame the record is just plastic. the outer-message camp says no, the literal content does all the work, the frame is just environment. the inner-message camp says both miss the point: real meaning is the self-referential loop, like how that record encodes instructions to build a record player that can play itself.

heres the thing: the debate feels deadlocked. everyone cites the same exmaples from the book, nobody moves, and it smells like theyre not even fighting about the same thing. my hunch is its partly verbal -- one side asks "where does interpretation happen", another asks "what do the symbols stand for", and the inner-message people ask "what makes a system understand itself". but im also wondering if theres a hidden empirical piece were missing, maybe about how actual brains or code parse nested messages? or is the disagreement just bedrock intuition?

what i want is a diagnosis, not a winner. is this a real substantive fight, a verbal mixup about the word meaning, or are they actually answering three different questions under one heading? and given that diagnosis, what would count as actual progress -- a new conceptual distinction, some kind of experiment with layered messages, or is the question itself broken? also, does the fact that modern LLMs produce fluent outer messages with no obvious inner loop or explicit frame tell us anything useful here, or is that just a red herring because the frame is hidden in the weights?
