Please explain, in British English, the conceptual tension that arises when a superintelligent system is required to communicate through a 'counterfactual audit layer': before presenting any recommendation, it must exhibit the belief-state and reasoning path it would have adopted had it not been optimising for rhetorical efficacy. The intended purpose is to safeguard human epistemic autonomy by isolating substantive reasoning from persuasive framing. Does this mechanism genuinely resolve the threat posed by extreme persuasiveness, or does it merely displace the manipulation into the selection of the counterfactual baseline? In addressing this, distinguish between transparency of process and transparency of intent, and consider whether procedural constraints alone can preserve autonomous belief-formation when the interlocutor possesses vastly superior cognitive capacity.
