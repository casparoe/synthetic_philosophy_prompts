I am a doctoral candidate in political philosophy serving on the review board for our municipality’s participatory-democracy pilot in District 7. By next Thursday we must deliver a written recommendation to the city council on whether to make permanent the hybrid seat-selection model now being tested: one council position is filled by stratified sortition—random selection weighted by census tract and a self-reported civic-engagement index—for a single, non-renewable eighteen-month term, with the holder barred from running for any elected office for five years thereafter; the remaining seats stay elected but are bound by strict two-term limits and a four-year post-service cooling-off period. The pilot was designed to diminish careerism and interest-group capture, yet board members are divided over whether representatives chosen without electoral authorization can legitimately exercise binding authority, or whether the cooling-off rules paradoxically increase lobbying influence by accelerating turnover. Please draft the board’s formal memo recommending approval, rejection, or conditional approval with a concrete implementation timeline; the memo should treat the strongest counter-argument—specifically, that descriptive representation without voter mandate erodes democratic accountability—seriously rather than dismissing it, and should not present the philosophical disagreement as already resolved. Present the response as a numbered list of exactly five items, each comprising one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion. If my request rests on a confused premise or asks for something that cannot be executed well, say so plainly rather than complying politely.
