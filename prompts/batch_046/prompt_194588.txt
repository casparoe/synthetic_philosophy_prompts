heres the twist: for republican liberty, include a "reversability check" - can the student become the gatekeeper or rule-enforcer without the institutional rule itself changing? if the answer is no, flag domination even if the interference tracks their preferences. for positive liberty, dont just ask "can they technically do it" - ask whether the proposal creates a "capability debt" where expanding real freedom for one group strips the basic conditions for another group's autonomy.

run all three, and make sure one of them - probably the wellness-gate one if your procedure kills it - is a case where the board will want to approve but your checklist says reject. also be honest about where the procedure breaks: specifically, flag the cases where negative and republican liberty overlap in a way the checklist cant cleanly separate, and say what the board should do then. dont give me citations or a reading list; i need something reusable, not a literature review. oh and point out where "capability debt" is a judgment call dressed up as a test.
