Having recently been appointed to an advisory panel examining post-colonial economic governance, I have been attempting to systematise Adam Smith's scattered rulings on empire and colonies into a coherent track record. On my reading, he judged that the American colonies ought not to be taxed without representation and that coercive retention was economically irrational; that the East India Company's corporate sovereignty in Bengal was destructive and ought to be replaced by direct crown administration; that West Indian slave plantations were less productive than free labour, that the sugar monopoly should be ended, and that immediate abolition without structural support risked disorder; that Irish linen prohibitions harmed both kingdoms and should be abolished in favour of free trade; and that a remote strategic coaling station yielding no agricultural surplus might be retained under direct state funding, but never granted to a chartered company with exclusive trading rights.

These verdicts appear jointly consistent with more than one principle—perhaps an absolute prohibition on corporate sovereignty, a fiscal-balance condition coupled with a representation requirement, or a primary commitment to free labour and open markets. I now wish to test this on a new case. A proposed Climate Resettlement Charter would grant a chartered corporation exclusive seabed-mineral rights and administrative authority over a displaced, low-lying archipelago population, in exchange for funding resettlement, guaranteeing wage-based non-compulsory labour, offering each family a non-voting seat on the corporate board, and providing no parliamentary representation in the chartering state; the scheme expects a deficit for two decades, subsidised by that state.

Please respond in British English. Predict what recommendation follows from Smith's framework, identify at least two distinct rules that accommodate the earlier verdicts equally well but diverge on this charter, state what each predicts, give your best-guess verdict with reasoning, and name one further hypothetical case that would distinguish between the rules. I do not require bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
