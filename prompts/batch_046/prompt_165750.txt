After a long debate last night with a friend about Black’s symmetric universe and whether its two spheres violate the principle of the identity of indiscernibles, I kept noticing a specific argumentative maneuver that I am not sure has an established name. The familiar setup is this: two spheres of identical size and composition, suspended in an otherwise empty space, separated by a fixed distance. My friend, defending Leibniz’s principle, insisted the spheres are numerically distinct because sphere A stands in the relation “being two miles north of B” while B stands in “being two miles south of A.” When I objected that these are merely two descriptions of one symmetric relational fact, he shifted ground: the distinction, he said, lies not in an external coordinate system but in the spheres’ respective “occupancy” of the relational roles—A occupies the northern role, B the southern one. I then asked whether “occupying the northern role” is reducible to an intrinsic property, to a standard external relation, or to something else entirely; he seemed to treat it as a primitive structural position internal to the pair, not derivable from intrinsic features yet also not parasitic on anything outside the dyad. We circled this for an hour without resolution.

Is there a recognized concept for this kind of “role-occupancy” or “structural position” that is meant to ground numerical difference without being either an intrinsic qualitative property or a conventional relational predicate? More specifically, is there a documented failure mode—perhaps a disguised appeal to primitive thisness, or a circular reliance on an implicit orientation—when defenders of the identity of indiscernibles deploy such positions against Black-style counterexamples? If the literature does not precisely name this move, can you help me delimit it: what exactly distinguishes it from ordinary relational properties, from haecceitistic primitive identity, and from the structuralist thesis that objects are nothing but nodes in a relational network? I am less interested in a bare label than in a clear carving of what the phenomenon is, where its edges lie, and which of the neighboring concepts it genuinely approximates.
