I volunteer as a content reviewer for an educational video platform that publishes short documentaries on ancient history, and producers regularly submit scripts treating the Trojan War, the Arthurian cycle, or the Exodus narrative as either pure legend, established history, or an ambiguous middle ground. I need a reusable decision procedure—something I can run in about fifteen minutes per script without specialized archives—to classify where a treatment falls and whether it should be flagged, approved, or sent back for revision. Ground it in whatever philosophy of history and textual criticism actually offers: the gap between event and earliest source, independent corroboration, the risk of narrative aggregation, and the distinction between a claim being unsupported and being actively undermined. Deliver it as an ordered flowchart in words with clear stop points: where it returns a definitive classification, where it stops with a no, where it hands the decision to me because the issue is values rather than evidence, and where it admits it cannot settle the matter. Inject two conceptual distinctions that are not standard textbook labels but capture real problems: “explanatory load-bearing,” meaning whether the event is necessary to account for other verified historical patterns, and “source circularity,” meaning whether a source’s credibility on other matters depends on assuming the event occurred. Apply the procedure to three submissions: one presenting the Trojan War as a verified Bronze Age siege, one treating King Arthur as a documented fifth-century warlord, and one framing the Exodus as a literal mass migration of hundreds of thousands; include the case where the result is one I will not want to accept. Finally, say explicitly what the procedure cannot do—especially where it might misclassify a genuinely historical event that left poor records, or a purely legendary one that accidentally correlates with archaeology—and keep the whole thing free of citations, reading lists, or requests for further research.
