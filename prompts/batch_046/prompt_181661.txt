I was discussing with colleagues after a seminar on moral responsibility, and we kept returning to the same puzzle: we all feel that a hypocrite has somehow forfeited the right to blame others for the very failing they share, yet none of us could articulate precisely what is forfeited—moral authority, credibility, standing proper, or merely social licence. Rather than collecting more intuitions, I want the full specification of what an adequate theory of standing to blame must deliver if it is genuinely to treat hypocrisy as undermining rather than merely embarrassing.

Write the desiderata: what cases the theory must handle correctly (the private hypocrite whose fault is never revealed; the public figure caught in an identical transgression; the institutional body that condemns corruption whilst practising it; the person who has genuinely reformed but whose past wrong is unknown to the audience); what distinctions it must preserve without collapse (between standing to blame in general and standing to blame this particular agent for this particular wrong; between hypocrisy that reveals moral equality and hypocrisy that reveals a failure of authority or expertise; between losing all standing and being subject only to counter-blame); what results from social psychology about hypocrisy aversion and moral licensing it must either accommodate or explain away; and whether the theory must remain compatible with a broader commitment to moral equality or can treat standing as purely conventional or role-based. For each requirement, state the motivating case or conceptual tension behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable—such that abandoning them changes the subject entirely—and which are camp-specific preferences, such as whether blame must serve a communicative purpose or can be purely expressive. Crucially, tell me whether any subset of these desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable: for instance, whether accommodating institutional hypocrisy forces us to abandon conditions on individual standing, or whether explaining why unknown hypocrisy still undermines standing requires a moral-purity condition that makes standing impossibly rare. Keep the weight on the criteria and their justifications rather than on a full taxonomy of existing views, though you may briefly indicate how a moral-equality account, a communicative-theory account, and a social-role account each fare against the list. I do not need a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations—this should be answerable from conceptual analysis alone—and please avoid attributing specific quotations to named texts unless you can verify them exactly.
