I just spent two hours arguing with a close friend about whether Hilbert's hotel demonstrates anything more than an internal consistency result within set theory, or whether it genuinely forces us to accept actual infinities as metaphysically coherent. He insisted that the paradox only works if you already treat the set of rooms as a completed totality available for global rearrangement, which is precisely what a finitist who rejects completed totalities would deny; I countered that the practical demands of accommodating guests seem to make that rejection costly in ways that cannot be dismissed as mere stubbornness. We ended without resolution, and I want a classroom design that makes the tension tangible rather than verbal.

Please design a 75-minute seminar activity for about eighteen philosophy undergraduates, none of whom are mathematics specialists, that puts them inside the conflict between infinitist coordination and finitist refusal of completed totalities. The setting is a single session with no advance reading; the deliverable is a runnable game with explicit rules, assigned roles, physical or card-based materials that require no technology, a payoff scheme using only points or symbolic stakes with no real money, and a structured debrief. The conceptual problem must emerge from what participants do—through deadlock, veto, or failed coordination—before it is named in the debrief.

Specifically, create two procedural roles, not merely speaking parts. Hotel Managers accept that rooms numbered 1, 2, 3, ... exist as an actual infinite set and may propose global reassignments of all occupied rooms simultaneously. Finitist Inspectors may approve only moves that treat rooms as generated one-by-one, and they must veto any plan that requires treating the totality of rooms as a completed object available for simultaneous remapping. Give Managers authority to propose; give Inspectors either absolute veto power or advisory voice—you must choose one, defend that choice as the design that best reveals whether finitism is a coherent constraint or an artificial obstacle, and explain what your choice implies about whether my argument with my friend can be resolved inside the game rather than outside it.

Structure three arrival waves using cards or seated positions to represent rooms and guests. Wave one: a single new guest arrives, requiring one local shift. Wave two: an unending sequence of individual guests arrives one at a time, requiring the classic Hilbert shift to even-numbered rooms. Wave three: an unending sequence of buses arrives, each carrying an unending sequence of guests, requiring a Cantor-style interleaving or pairing. For each wave, Managers have two minutes to propose an accommodation plan with explicit physical movement; Inspectors vote to approve or veto according to their charter. If vetoed, Managers must produce a finitist-compliant alternative—moving guests only as individually needed, never invoking the full set—or suffer a collective penalty such as loss of accumulated points or symbolic eviction of unhoused guests. The scoring should be visible and should create tension: Managers earn points for every guest housed; Inspectors earn points for every veto that genuinely exposes a hidden dependence on a completed totality, but lose points for vetoing a plan that actually required no global remapping.

In the debrief, map each wave onto its corresponding conceptual move: local potential rearrangement, the global pairing that treats infinity as available for remapping, and the higher-order interleaving that assumes nested actual infinities. State exactly which feature of the metaphysical debate the activity cannot reproduce—most likely the distinction between mathematical existence and physical possibility, or the normative question of whether coherence requires realizability—and say frankly what the facilitator must supply verbally. Predict what will go wrong on the first run: likely that Managers invent a "potentialist" workaround that still smuggles in global knowledge of the room numbering, or that Inspectors veto indiscriminately and stall the game. Say precisely how to handle each failure without breaking the framing. Write formally, avoid bibliographies, citations, and reading lists, and take your committed position on the Inspectors' power with explicit reasons.
