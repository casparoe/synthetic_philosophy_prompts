Ive been trying to work out Schlicks idea that meaning comes from play rather than work -- doing something for its own sake, not as a means. Heres an argument that makes it collapse. (1) Schlick says an act counts as playful only if the agent doesnt do it for any further result. (2) But any playful act that succeeds produces satisfaction, health, or social bonds as side effects. (3) Once those results exist, the agent has reason to preserve the conditions that allow the act -- rest, practice, avoiding injury. (4) So the agent now does the act partly to maintain those conditions, which are means to further playing. (5) Therefore sustained play always acquires an instrumental structure; it becomes work in Schlicks sense. (6) But Schlick clearly thinks people can live meaningfully for years through art or sport. (7) So his view implies long-term meaningfulness is impossible. Wheres the leak? Is it that Schlick allows indirect aims, or that Im conflating causal side-effects with the agents actual reasons? Id like to keep (1) if possible, and Im not convinced (6) is false -- maybe Schlick just has to bite the bullet that youth is brief? What does giving up (1) vs (4) cost?
