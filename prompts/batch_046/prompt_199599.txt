In a recent seminar on art ontology, a colleague kept invoking the formulation “the statue is nothing over and above the clay arranged statue-wise” whenever discussion turned to whether melting a bronze work destroys the artwork or merely transforms its material substrate. She used it to dismiss talk of “the statue” as an entity with its own persistence conditions, insisting that all such talk collapses into talk about particles and their spatial relations. I want to bring some precision to this. Could you lay out, as a menu rather than a single interpretation, the distinct precise theses that slogan might actually be expressing? For each reading, indicate whether it is a near-tautology, a substantive metaphysical claim that is defensible, or something stronger that is likely false — particularly readings that conflate mereological composition, modal identity, and aesthetic ontology. Then say which of these readings would genuinely support her move of dismissing questions about the artwork’s survival through reshaping, and which leave those questions untouched or even more pressing. Please treat the line as a circulating formulation without attributing it to any specific author.
