Construct a thought experiment to specifications: design a single, realistic foreign-policy crisis scenario—no science fiction, no stipulated omniscience, no appeal to demons or perfect predictors, and no longer than a paragraph—involving a rising regional power and an established hegemon that can be described with equal structural fidelity as either a Munich-type appeasement situation or a Vietnam-type escalation trap. Engineer it so that analogical reasoning from Munich yields a prescription for immediate military confrontation, while analogical reasoning from Vietnam yields a prescription for restraint and negotiated withdrawal, even though the strategic variables (power-transition dynamics, alliance commitments, domestic political costs, and the adversary's resolve) are held constant across both descriptions. Then demonstrate that the case meets each specification: (1) state the policy belief or recommendation generated under each analogy; (2) identify the precise surface feature—geographic, rhetorical, or institutional—that activates the Munich framing versus the Vietnam framing; (3) show what happens to the analogical reasoning if that feature is neutralized; (4) provide an honest account of which feature does the normative work and whether removing it collapses both analogies or only one; and (5) explain how a defender of analogical reasoning in statecraft would attack your case, particularly by arguing that you have artificially decoupled the analogy from the causal mechanism it tracks. Finally, relate the result to the Thucydides trap claim that structural anxiety between rising and ruling powers makes conflict probabilistically inevitable: specify whether your scenario undermines that structural claim, merely shows that its policy implications are analogy-dependent, or leaves it untouched. Throughout, distinguish claims about analogical misreasoning that are broadly settled in the international-relations and political-psychology literatures from claims about the specific predictive value of the Munich or Vietnam analogies that remain genuinely contested among specialists; do not present a contested interpretation of either analogy's validity as though it were the consensus view.
