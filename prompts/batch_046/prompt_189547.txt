okay so i've been stuck on this thing about whether time actually flows or if it's just a static block like some physics people say. I came across this argument by Rietdijk and Putnam (i think?) that if two observers are moving past each other they disagree on which distant events are happening right now, so there can't be one objective present moment. That seems to push toward the block universe where past and future are equally real. But I'm not convinced we have to go all the way to time is an illusion. I want to figure out what a theory that saves real becoming would actually have to deliver, without just ignoring the physics.

So here's my question: what are the hard desiderata? The theory has to explain why my experience of now feels globally real even though physics says simultaneity is frame-dependent. It needs to cover the case of two spaceships passing each other at near light speed and disagreeing about whether a distant star just exploded, without letting both be right in a way that creates two contradictory realities. Here's a twist I haven't seen discussed much: what if one ship sends a light-speed message exactly at the point of closest approach, and the other receives it later? Depending on frame, the sending and receiving might get assigned to totally different present slices. The theory needs to say whether that message crosses a real boundary of the present or if that's just coordinate bookkeeping.

Also it can't contradict the biological fact that organisms have internal clocks tied to rotation and chemical cycles, or the engineering fact that GPS satellites have to adjust for gravitational and velocity time dilation or navigation fails. It has to preserve the distinction between time doesn't flow globally and time doesn't flow locally -- those shouldn't collapse into each other just because both avoid a universal now. And it should handle the weird case where, in one frame, a person dies before they are born in another frame, explaining why that's not an actual logical contradiction but also not just brushing it off.

Can you lay out the full specification? For each requirement, say what case or result motivates it, whether it's negotiable or non-negotiable, and then score the main competing theories -- block universe, presentism, growing block, maybe local-becoming views -- against the list. I especially want to know which single requirement does the most damage, the one that knocks out the theories trying to split the difference. A table is fine if it helps.
