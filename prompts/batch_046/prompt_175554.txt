I'm designing a final exam for a graduate seminar in philosophy of physics and need one essay question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme totaling 20 marks. The question should test whether students can work with Bell's locality condition rather than recite the CHSH derivation. Present this scenario: a theorist proposes a "context-indexed" hidden-variables model in which each particle pair carries a hidden state λ together with a shared global parameter C, fixed in the common past, that encodes the joint apparatus-alignment protocol used in both wings. The outcome probabilities depend on λ, C, and the local setting, but C carries no signal during flight and is unknown to the experimenters. Ask the student to determine: (1) whether this violates Bell's factorization condition P(a,b|x,y,λ,C) = P(a|x,λ,C)·P(b|y,λ,C) and precisely why; (2) whether parameter independence, outcome independence, or statistical independence is violated; (3) whether distributing C according to a prior can reproduce quantum correlations for the CHSH setup; and (4) whether Einstein locality—no influence between spacelike-separated regions—is genuinely violated or only replaced by logical dependence on a common cause. The marking scheme should award marks for explicitly distinguishing the three independence conditions, correctly applying the factorization formula, giving a precise causal analysis of C, and explaining why quantum correlations remain problematic; it should penalize answers that merely recite CHSH algebra or conflate non-factorizability with superluminal signaling. Specify a 90-minute time allocation and do not include requests for citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts.
