I have been revising the syllabus for my graduate seminar in metaphysics, and this week we are working through the standard literature on dispositions, finks, and masks—the familiar repertoire of antidote cases, electro-fink scenarios, and arguments about missing manifestations. Rather than rehearse that well-trodden dialectic, I wish to introduce a conceptual variant for discussion: call it an “inversion mask.” On this model, a categorical base C genuinely grounds a disposition D with a characteristic manifestation profile, but an interfering factor M does not merely suppress or remove D; instead, M systematically reweights the manifestation conditions so that C now produces the manifestations characteristic of a contrary disposition D*, all without altering C’s intrinsic categorical properties or substituting a different base. The question is not whether such masks occur in nature, but whether they are metaphysically coherent—that is, whether a single categorical ground can genuinely sustain one dispositional profile while an external factor redirects its outputs to those of an opposing profile without destroying or replacing that ground.

Given this framing, I want you to state an explicit credence: on a scale from 0 to 1, what is your actual probability that inversion masks are metaphysically possible? Please do not retreat to “it depends on your theory of dispositions”; I want a number, together with a full decomposition of the reasoning that produces it. Specifically: (1) How much of your credence rides on whether dispositional ascriptions are essentially tied to actual manifestation conditions versus being anchored solely in the categorical base? (2) How much depends on whether dispositions are essentially single-track or can be genuinely multi-track? (3) To what extent does your answer hinge on whether causal powers are individuated by their effects or by their intrinsic grounds? Assign rough probabilities to each of these sub-questions, show how they combine to yield your overall figure, and identify which single sub-question, if resolved differently, would move your final number the most. Finally, indicate what kind of argument—conceptual, empirical, or analogical—would most likely shift your credence by a significant margin.
