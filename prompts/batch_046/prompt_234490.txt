I'm leading a seminar on Grice's theory of implicature and speaker meaning, and we've spent the term working through the distinction between what is said—the minimal propositional content conventionally tied to the utterance—and what is implicated—the further propositions a cooperative hearer recovers by assuming the speaker is observing the Cooperative Principle and its maxims. Our final session ended on an unresolved tension that I want to preserve rather than dissolve: speaker meaning is not merely an ornament added to sentence meaning, but in many ordinary cases seems to override or displace it, and yet there is no explicit conventional marker of cancellation; the hearer calculates the implicature through calculability, checks it for cancellability, and distinguishes conventional from conversational implicature, all without leaving the realm of speaker intention. To close the session I want a poem, specifically a villanelle of nineteen lines in strict ABA ABA ABA ABA ABA ABAA form, spoken from the perspective of someone addressing an interlocutor who systematically refuses this machinery—someone who treats every utterance as carrying only its conventional semantic value plus logical entailment, denying that speaker intentions can be reconstructed from context, relevance, or manner.

The two refrains should alternate throughout and finally converge in the closing couplet without achieving synthesis. One refrain should voice the literal, minimal content of what is said; the other should voice the intended implicature—what is meant—recoverable only if one accepts that the speaker is being cooperative and that the maxims of Quantity, Quality, Relation, and Manner are in play. Each of the five middle stanzas should attempt to reach that implicature through a different maxim, and each should be blocked by the same literalist refusal. I want the formal repetition and inevitable return of the villanelle's lines to enact the deadlock: every attempt to move from sentence meaning to speaker meaning is met with the same denial that anything beyond conventional content and entailment belongs to the utterance. The philosophical rendering should be precise enough that a specialist in Gricean pragmatics recognizes the mechanics—explicit reference to calculability, cancellability, and the conventional versus conversational distinction is welcome—but the imagery should carry the argument rather than jargon. Please do not include citations, bibliographies, page references, or attributed quotations; the poem should stand on its own conceptual feet. It should leave the reader with the sense that speaker meaning remains unrecovered, suspended between the two refrains, rather than resolved.
