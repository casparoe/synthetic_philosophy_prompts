I retired from molecular biology about five years ago and have since been reading philosophy of science in a pretty haphazard way. The demarcation problem keeps coming up, especially Popper's falsificationism, but I also keep noticing something in modern reasearch programs that I think of as falsifiability debt — when a theory keeps stacking up auxilliary hypotheses to protect its core from contradiction, kind of like technical debt but for empircal risk. I'm not sure anyone actually uses that phrase; I might have just made it up over coffee, but it feels like it captures what's happening in parts of cosmology and climate modeling where parameters get adjusted after the fact.

So I want to ask about the implications: if we take the demarcation problem seriously and combine it with this idea of falsifiability debt, what does that mean for how we should actually judge scientific theories today? I'm not looking for a quick "Popper was wrong"; I want the most important implications. Does carrying a lot of this debt push a reasearch program toward being pseudosientific, or is it just what mature science looks like? And does Popper's view that science progresses through bold conjectures and refutations help us manage that debt, or does it completely miss the issue? Finally, what should a layperson make of all those patched auxilliary assumptions — should they see it as a warning sign, or is it just normal science doing its job?
