I have spent the better part of an evening in a sustained argument with a close friend about Douglas Hofstadter’s Aunt Hillary dialogue, and we remain deadlocked on whether the colony-level consciousness he imagines is genuinely substrate-independent or whether it secretly depends on continuous, spatially integrated biological signaling among the ants. She insists that once local rules generate stable, symbol-level organization across the colony, mental unity is achieved regardless of physical layout, so that dividing Aunt Hillary should be no more destructive to her mind than splitting a human brain along the corpus callosum is to personal identity. I suspect she is smuggling in an unstated functionalist premise that global-workspace and integrated-information theorists would reject, but I need a precisely engineered case to show where these theories actually diverge rather than trading intuitions.

Please construct the following thought experiment: begin with Aunt Hillary as a fully functioning ant colony that has developed stable, colony-level representations of its environment through distributed pheromone and tactile interaction. At a specific moment, an impermeable artificial barrier is inserted through the nest, dividing it into two sub-colonies that can communicate only through a single narrow tunnel allowing intermittent message exchange—no continuous pheromone flow, no shared chemical field, and no faster-than-light or quantum-mediated coordination. The local behavioral rules in each half remain identical to the original, and each half continues to adapt locally, but the high-bandwidth, spatially continuous integration that previously unified the whole is now broken. For this scenario, specify exactly what a global workspace theorist, an integrated information theorist evaluating causal density and phi, and a biological naturalist in the tradition of Searle would each conclude about whether Aunt Hillary’s mind survives intact in one half, splits into two minds, is destroyed entirely, or never existed in the first place. Show how each verdict follows from the theory’s core commitments—broadcast architecture, irreducible integration, or biological substrate—rather than being stipulated; identify the structural feature that does the actual work in generating the disagreement; and explain what happens if we restore continuous pheromone flow across the barrier or replace the ants with silicon agents running identical algorithms. The case must remain realistic: ordinary physics, no exotic predictors, and no direct stipulation of mental states.
