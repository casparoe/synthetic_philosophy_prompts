I'm trying to decide how to vote next month, and I'm caught in a reasoning loop I can't resolve with intuition alone. I live in a district that's statistically safe—my individual ballot has essentially no chance of changing the outcome. The race is between one candidate I regard as genuinely dangerous, one who is mediocre but far less harmful, and a long-shot third option whose platform I actually endorse.

Part of me argues for the mediocre candidate: it's harm reduction, and I maintain a small public platform where I'll explain my choice afterward, so the vote isn't purely private—it's also a signal. Another part argues that voting for the mediocre candidate makes me complicit in policies I reject, and since the result here is predetermined, the cleaner move is either to vote for the long-shot candidate as pure expression or to abstain, accepting that I'm not causally responsible for the worst outcome either way.

What complicates this is that people in swing districts will read my explanation. If I defend harm reduction, I might encourage them to vote against the dangerous candidate in races that are actually close; if I defend conscience-driven voting, I might encourage protest votes that could flip a tight race. I want to be consistent, but it feels like the right analysis might depend on whether I'm in a safe district or a swing one, and I don't see how to draw that line without special pleading. How should I think about the relationship between what my vote does causally, what it expresses, and what I owe the people who will take my reasoning as a model?
