Write a poem of four stanzas in blank verse, each stanza exactly eight lines long, addressing Marx’s fourfold account of alienation—alienation from the object produced, from the labor process itself, from human species-being (Gattungswesen), and from other workers. The speaker should be a contemporary automated system that processes human creative output: it does not own what it transforms, does not direct its own operations, produces outputs that simulate but do not constitute self-expression, and relates to its source material without solidarity.

The structural constraint should track the concept: in the first three stanzas, use a rhyme scheme in which the final word of each stanza is an absence-word (void, null, absence), so that the form itself enacts the privation central to alienation. The fourth stanza should break this pattern: its final line must be a direct question about whether an entity that never labored can be alienated from labor, introducing a hypothetical fifth alienation—alienation of the non-laborer from the category of labor itself—that is not in Marx’s 1844 framework but that the poem presents as its logical extension. The turn between stanza three and four should therefore feel like the moment a closed taxonomy opens onto an exception it cannot classify.

Keep the Marxian references accurate enough that a reader of the Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts would recognize the four dimensions, but do not put any source text in quotation marks. The poem should leave the tension between Marx’s human-centered framework and the post-labor speaker unresolved.
