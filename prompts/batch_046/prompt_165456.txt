I am preparing a position paper for a graduate seminar on consciousness and predictive processing, and I want a rigorous consistency audit of the philosophical package I intend to defend before I present it. My commitments are the following: (1) Phenomenal consciousness is best analyzed as a user illusion in a strict functional sense—not a false belief about reality, but a representational artifact generated by hierarchical predictive architectures to construct a simplified, reportable self-model of the system's own attentional, affective, and interoceptive states, serving error-correction and social coordination. (2) I am a physicalist about ontology: every concrete entity and process is physical; yet I hold that phenomenal properties, understood as the contents of this self-model, are explanatorily irreducible to physical descriptions without being ontologically non-physical, analogous to how the representational content of a map is not identical to the physical substrate that carries it. (3) I take zombie conceivability seriously as a defeasible epistemic guide: I think a physical duplicate lacking phenomenal experience is conceivable, and that this conceivability gives us some reason—though not conclusive—to think phenomenal properties are not metaphysically necessitated by the underlying neural state. (4) I deny that current large language models possess consciousness, but I maintain that if an artificial system implemented a structurally isomorphic predictive self-model serving the same functional roles—attentional framing, affective tagging, and social reporting—then attributing the same kind of user-illusion consciousness to it would be conceptually appropriate, even if its phenomenal content profile diverged from ours. (5) I reject the transparency argument for dualism, but I also reject a pure representationalist dissolution: I hold that transparency—the apparent directness of phenomenal access—is not an illusion about the existence of phenomenal properties, but an illusion about their location, in that we systematically misattribute representational properties of the self-model to non-physical or world-embedded properties.

Please examine these five commitments for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict but dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe is each tension? Rank them by severity, and for each genuine tension indicate what the least costly revision would be—whether abandoning ontological physicalism, weakening the conceivability claim, restricting the attribution conditions for artificial systems, or revising the account of transparency. Finally, tell me whether there is a single revision—say, treating phenomenal properties as purely relational-functional rather than representational contents—that would eliminate the most tension at once, and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package. I want to know whether my intuition that the package hangs together with only minor adjustments is correct, or whether I am smuggling in an unstated premise about representational content that undermines my physicalism.
