I'm on the arbitration panel for the Eastern Society for the History of Philosophy. We're revising our official stance on the unpublished notebooks of Voss, a minor figure in our tradition. Three sub-committees each voted consistently on separate premises: (1) dating three notebooks to 1924-26, (2) identifying a specific debate's influence on his argument structure, and (3) whether some marginalia are his hand or a student's. Each group is internally consistent, but agregating them premise-by-premise yields the conclusion that Voss broke with his earlier view by 1925 — which clashes hard with the collective interpretive tradition we've taught for decades, and many members see that tradition as part of our society's identity. The alternative is conclusion-based agregation, where we keep the traditional view and reject whatever premise conflicts, but that feels like cooking the books. Draft the panel's decision memo recommending which rule to adopt — premise-based, conclusion-based, or a hybrid — with a clear bottom line and reasoning that takes the strongest objection seriously.
