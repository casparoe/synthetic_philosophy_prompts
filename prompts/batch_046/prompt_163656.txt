I am a software architect fluent in distributed-systems theory—specifically consensus protocols, conflict-free replicated data types, message-passing invariants, and eventual-consistency models—without formal philosophical training. Restate Wittgenstein's account of language games and family resemblance using only that vocabulary. For each of the following, provide the mapping and the residue: (i) a language game, treated as a protocol governing message exchange among nodes; (ii) family resemblance, treated as clustering among protocols that share no single invariant but exhibit overlapping similarities; (iii) rule-following, treated as the distinction between adhering to a protocol specification and locally interpreting received messages; and (iv) the private-language argument, treated as the impossibility of a single-node state machine maintaining meaningful state transitions without external validation or replication. For each case, state what translates cleanly, what requires distortion (for instance, normative correctness into functional convergence, or social agreement into quorum achievement), and what has no analog whatsoever. Conclude with a residue report: does the leftover indicate a genuinely normative or intersubjective dimension that distributed coordination cannot capture, or does it suggest that Wittgenstein's original formulation was less precise about collective practice than it appeared? Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or bibliographic verification; answer from the concepts themselves.
