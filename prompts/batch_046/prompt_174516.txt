Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, two refrains (A1 and A2) that rhyme with each other and alternate with a single b-rhyme, concluding with both refrains paired in the final two lines—that enacts the dynamics of a Lakatosian research programme. Make the hard core’s immunity to direct refutation the first refrain, and the protective belt’s serial revision the second; let the intervening b-lines carry successive auxiliary hypotheses, novel predictions, and accommodating adjustments, so that the form’s repetitions and rotations mirror the negative heuristic holding firm while the positive heuristic remakes the belt. Apply this not to a standard physics example but to a somewhat overlooked cognitive-science programme: one whose hard core is that episodic memory is always reconstructed narrative, and whose protective belt successively adjusts encoding, consolidation, and retrieval models to absorb neuroimaging findings that suggest direct trace storage, eventually turning degenerating when adjustments become purely post-hoc. The poem should render the progressive-to-degenerating shift structurally—perhaps through a shift in the b-lines from predictive to reactive language—so that a reader familiar with Lakatos recognizes the mechanics rather than just the vocabulary, and the final couplet should present the untouched hard core beside a belt that has been wholly replaced. Avoid quotations, citations, or requests for bibliography.
