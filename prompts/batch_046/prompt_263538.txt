I'm in this environmental humanities seminar and we keep circling back to the Anthropocene thing, specificaly why it hasn't been officially added to the geologic timescale even though everyone uses the word constantly. The practice I keep noticing is that the International Commission on Stratigraphy demands a physical Global Boundary Stratotype Section and Point — basically a literal rock layer, a golden spike — before they'll ratify a new epoch, which is why they've held off on the Anthropocene even though there's all this evidence for a start around 1950 with nuclear fallout and plastic layers, or maybe 1610 with the big ecological shift after Columbus. Meanwhile climate scientists and some historians just treat the start date as a convention they can argue for based on human impact, not on finding one perfect marker bed. My roommate threw out this weird idea that maybe we should decide the start by democratic vote weighted by each nation's cumulative carbon emissions, which sounds totally off-base for geology but maybe makes sense if you think historical periods are social agreements rather than discoveries.

So I'm trying to figure out what philosophical commitments the ICS's practice presupposes. Does insisting on that physical marker mean they think geological time is out there in nature waiting to be found, with human-caused changes being just another biological signal? Or is it more about keeping authority inside stratigraphy and away from climate modelers? And could the democratic-vote proposal even make sense without abandoning the idea that epochs are real features of the world — like, does it require thinking that the Anthropocene is purely a political designation with no geological basis, or can you combine social decision-making with a realist view of deep time? Reconstruct the implicit theory behind the ICS rule, say wheather it actually needs that realist commitment or is compatible with conventionalism too, and tell me what a policy built on the democratic-vote idea would have to change about how we treat the boundary.
