I am running a ninety-minute graduate seminar in philosophy of action with about fourteen students, and we have already moved through the standard introductory treatments of autonomy and the familiar extended-mind cases that tend to dominate classroom discussion. For this session I want a runnable in-class simulation that forces the group to experience a less familiar form of embedded agency, which I will call nested opacity: the condition in which an agent's practical reasoning is structurally dependent on representational and normative arrangements she did not design and may not even recognize, so that success feels like personal competence and failure feels like individual inadequacy, when in fact both partly belong to the opaque architecture through which she acts. The exercise should take the form of a structured role-play with hidden role cards, a collective decision task about resource allocation, and rules arranged so that participants construct the coordination failure themselves before anyone names the concept; there should be no lecture up front, and the idea must emerge from what the rules and hidden constraints make them do. Please design the specific roles and hidden constraints, a timeline for the ninety minutes, a payoff scheme using points rather than real money, and the procedural rules that will reliably push participants into misattributing structural dependence to personal error.

Following the simulation, provide a debrief that maps each observable moment of confusion or blame onto the distinction between instrumental embedding, in which an agent uses a transparent external tool, and constitutive embedding, in which the external structures partially constitute the intentions she acts on, and predict honestly what will go wrong the first time I run it—most likely a collapse into either exaggerated individualism or an unreflective determinism. Also state clearly which phenomenological feature of embedded agency—specifically the pre-reflective transparency of acting through conditions one does not thematize—this classroom format is structurally unable to reproduce and that I must therefore supply in my own commentary. Present everything in continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a single letter, so that the design, timeline, debrief, predictions, and acknowledged limitations all flow together without any formatting devices.
