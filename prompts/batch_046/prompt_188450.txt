Write a short story about the hinge-of-history idea—those moments after which nothing is the same. But here's the niche twist: your protaganist is an archivist who finds proof that one of the biggest hinges wasn't an event at all, but an anti-event. A critical message was drafted in 2143, then deliberatly never sent; the silence itself is what bent the timeline. Now she has to decide whether publishing the draft counts as retroactively transmitting it, which might undo the very branch she's living in. Don't turn it into a generic time-travel adventure—keep it focused on whether a recognized absence can carry causal weight like presence, and what it means for a historian to complete a hinge just by noticing it was missing. Keep the tone philosophical rather than gadget-heavy, and let the weirdness come from the concept, not from aliens or robots.
