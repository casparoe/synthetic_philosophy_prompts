Then branch through cases: a moral claim that fixes social coordination but can't be lab-tested; then a scenario where two isolated communities build incompatible but locally successful belief systems and never interact. The tree should have 6-8 leaves total, and I want them to include Peirce-style convergence-to-ideal-inquiry, James-style verification-in-experience/cash-value, Deweyan warranted-assertability if it fits, plus at least one correspondence-style anti-pragmatist leaf and one underexplored hybrid (maybe convergence-in-fragments?). Each split should be a real philosophical question—like whether practical success is constitutive of truth or just a sign of it, whether stalled inquiry means there's no fact of the matter, or whether truth can be retroactively revised. Wherever the logical space has an empty combination—like maybe "full convergence is required but local practical success is sufficient"—flag it and say whether it's incoherent, just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Don't ask people to identify themselves by label; the scoring should map answer patterns onto positions. Keep the audience as smart non-specialists who know the basic Peirce/James distinction but not much else.
