Hey, I'm a retired biolojist who just started dippping into philosphy, and I keep seeing the Ship of Theseus compared to a living body that slowly swaps out every cell. The usual line is that if you stay "you" despite total cellular turnover, then replacing every plank should keep the ship the same ship too. But this analougy feels off to me. In actual biology, turnover isn't uniform -- lots of neurons never replace, and different tissues have wildly different lifespans -- so there's always some persistant structure. Does that mean the biological case relies on functional continuity, or on a core that never changes? What exactly maps to what between a body and a wooden boat? Where does the comparison smuggle in assumptions about a "soul" or essential design? And if we push it, should we treat the ship's keel or original blueprint as its "brain"? I want a proper audit: what the analogy can actually carry, where it breaks, and whether it survives once you notice that not all cells turn over.
