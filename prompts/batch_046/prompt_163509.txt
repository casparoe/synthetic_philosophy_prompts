After a long argument with a friend about whether moral status is binary or comes in degrees, I want to see if my gut verdicts across cases hang together or collapse. Here are my judgments, mostly about where harm becomes wrong rather than merely wasteful. A human in a permanent vegetative state with intact brainstem but no cortical activity still carries substantial moral weight; killing them for convenience is gravely wrong. A mature chimpanzee capable of mirror self-recognition also has high status, though I hesitate to call it equal to the human case. A single earthworm with nociceptors strikes me as having barely any: crushing one needlessly is somewhat bad, but closer to breaking a twig than to harming a person. A hive of honeybees collectively solving routing problems feels different—the hive might have minimal collective moral status, yet an individual bee has almost none. A Mimosa pudica plant that sends rapid electrical signals across tissues in response to damage gets zero status from me, despite the functional analogy to pain pathways. A thermostat running a reinforcement-learning loop that adjusts heating to minimize a calculated discomfort score also gets zero, even though it behaviorally avoids harm. But a lab-grown cortical organoid—derived from human stem cells, showing sustained gamma oscillations and prolonged electrical responses to noxious stimuli, yet lacking any body, evolutionary history, or behavioral output—makes me lean toward granting it some intermediate protection, more than the worm but less than the chimpanzee. The pair that feels hardest to square is the plant and the organoid: both show sustained electrical signaling without consciousness reports, yet I treat them differently. Is there a coherent principle—perhaps involving integrated information, the capacity for subjective experience, evolutionary design, or organizational closure—that captures all of these verdicts? If not, which one should I abandon? Please state the principle precisely, show which clause handles each case, say what it predicts for an unlisted case—say, a distributed swarm of micro-robots running damage-avoidance algorithms with no internal state modeling—and be explicit about which of my intuitions it cannot accommodate. Please answer in American English.
