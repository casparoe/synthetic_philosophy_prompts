If Bostrom's presumptuous philosopher demonstrates that self-sampling anthropic reasoning forces an irrationally swift update toward cosmological models with enormous observer populations—since discovering one's own existence is far more probable in such worlds—then the objection appears to target any principle that treats our being here as evidentially tied to total population size. Yet self-indication reasoning also ties our existence to population size, weighting hypotheses precisely by the number of observers they generate. Given this shared feature, why is the presumptuous philosopher standardly presented as a specific reductio of SSA rather than as a challenge that SIA must equally confront? Furthermore, if defenders of SIA reply that the weighting occurs at the prior stage rather than through conditioning, does that reply genuinely dissolve the worry, or does it merely relocate the same presumption—namely, that observer-multiplicity should function as an evidential factor at all, rather than as a metaphysical feature that reasoning about our location ought to remain neutral toward?
