I've been working through Marx's writings on machinery and automation on my own—mostly the Grundrisse notebooks and the machinery chapters in Capital Volume I—without formal training in economics or philosophy. The longer I stay with it, the more I feel stuck in a loop that I can't break.

On one side are readers who treat Marx as essentially optimistic about automation: they point to passages where Marx says that once large-scale industry develops, direct labor stops being the main source of wealth, and they read this as meaning that capitalism eventually undermines its own value-form and clears the way for a society based on disposable time rather than labor time. On the other side are readers who insist Marx was never technologically optimistic: they point to the same texts and argue that every machine introduced under capitalism only deepens real subsumption, extends exploitation into the worker's gestures and attention, and turns the laborer into an appendage of the system.

What makes the stall so frustrating is that both camps cite the same sources—the discussion of machinery in Capital and the so-called Fragment on Machines—and both agree on the basic historical picture. They do not disagree about what Marx wrote, and in the small reading group I attend nobody can point to a specific misreading that explains the split. The arguments just circle.

Recently I have begun to suspect that the disagreement may not be about Marx at all, but about a hidden assumption I am calling the latency interval: the unstated belief each side holds about how long the gap can persist between a technological possibility (labor becoming technically unnecessary) and an actual social transformation. The optimistic readers seem to treat that gap as unstable and short; the pessimistic readers treat it as permanently absorbable by capital. But I do not know whether this is a real structural disagreement buried in the texts, a verbal confusion about terms like necessary labor and value-form, or simply two different questions—one about historical dynamics, one about logical structure—being forced under one heading.

Could you diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is? Is it substantive, verbal, about standards of evidence, or about bedrock intuitions?
