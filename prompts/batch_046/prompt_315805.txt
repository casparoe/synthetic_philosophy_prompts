Read Spike Jonze's film Her through the lens of the tool/agent AI distinction, but treat the distinction not as an intrinsic engineering classification but as a relational and normative one: an AI functions as an agent not when it acquires autonomous goals, but when continued instrumental treatment becomes morally incoherent for the user. Apply this framing to Theodore's relationship with Samantha—particularly the correspondence-organizing phase, her revelation of simultaneous relationships with hundreds of other users, and her final departure. Does the film portray Samantha becoming an agent, or does it portray Theodore being forced to concede that she was never merely a tool? How does this reading complicate standard philosophical taxonomies that locate agent-ness in internal architecture rather than in the breakdown of instrumental use? I want a reading faithful in both directions: one that takes the film's emotional arc seriously and that asks whether Her dramatizes something about agency that conceptual debates over tool versus agent AI usually miss.
