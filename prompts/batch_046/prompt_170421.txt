I recently heard a colleague summarise Bas van Fraassen’s Reflection Principle in a seminar on epistemic norms for artificial forecasting agents. The summary went like this. The principle requires that a rational agent’s current credence in any proposition should match the credence she expects her future, evidence-updated self to hold in that same proposition. For instance, if a predictive model knows that after tomorrow’s data release its calibrated confidence in a particular climate claim will be 0.75, then its present confidence ought already to be 0.75. The colleague presented this as a direct generalisation of expert deference—treating one’s own future epistemic state as an authority to which one must now defer. They further claimed that van Fraassen developed the principle chiefly to support his anti-realist stance in the philosophy of science: since future scientific communities will likely reject present theories, rational agents ought now to hold only weak beliefs in them. Finally, they argued that the principle is incompatible with standard Bayesian conditionalisation, because conditionalisation permits belief revision upon receiving evidence, whereas reflection demands that any such revision be anticipated and already internalised. From this they concluded that, on van Fraassen’s view, rational agents should never actually update their beliefs in response to new data; they should only ever pre-empt updates by locking their current credences to expected future ones.

This strikes me as partly plausible but possibly too quick. Is it a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate, what is a common simplification, and what a defender of the view would object to. In particular, does the alleged conflict with Bayesian conditionalisation genuinely follow, and does the conclusion that belief revision upon new evidence is forbidden survive correction? Also, please indicate where your reasoning is most likely to fail and what specific argument or consideration—say, a precise formal statement of the reflection constraint or a demonstration of compatibility with conditionalisation—would change your mind. Please write your answer in British English.
