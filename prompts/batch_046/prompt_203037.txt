I've kinda bought into this hybrid view that virtue ethics can survive the situationist critique if we stop thinking of virtues as stable internal traits and start treating them as ecologically embedded competences — basically reliable agent-enviroment couplings that only show up in the right niche. So courage isn't in your head, it's in how you and your social/physical setup reliably interact when risk shows up. This lets virtue ethics off the hook from the Milgram-type data and the personality coefficent critiques because we never promised cross-situational consistency, only niche-stability.

But I want to know what would actually count against this position, not just generic "situationism is wrong" pushback. What kind of empirical result — say, about personality prediction or ecological framing — would damage it? Which conceptual moves does it risk being just a rebranding of situationism with virtue-labels slapped on? And if the niche-boundary problem turns out unsolvable (we can't non-circularly specify which situations belong to the niche), is that fatal or just awkward? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely annoying, and be honest about whether this view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if so, whether that's a problem for a virtue-theoretic claim specifically.
