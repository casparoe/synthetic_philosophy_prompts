I'm enrolled in an advanced seminar on the ethics of belief, and in a recent study session a classmate summarized the well-known exchange between William James and W.K. Clifford in terms that struck me as suspiciously neat. She claimed that Clifford's evidentialism establishes an absolute moral prohibition: it is wrong always, everywhere, and for anyone to hold a belief without sufficient evidence, with the shipowner example serving to show that even beliefs which happen to be true are blameworthy whenever they rest on negligent or inadequate investigation. Against this, she said, James's essay The Will to Believe advances a form of doxastic voluntarism—the view that belief is directly subject to voluntary control—so that whenever intellectual evidence is exhausted, as in religious or metaphysical commitment, one may legitimately choose to believe based on emotional need or personal satisfaction. She concluded that James therefore licenses a kind of unrestricted wishful thinking, that he imposes no substantive constraints beyond individual preference, and that he succeeds in refuting Clifford by demonstrating that strict evidentialism is psychologically impossible for beings who constantly form beliefs through acts of will.

I suspect this framing misrepresents at least one of the two thinkers, particularly the claim that James treats belief as directly voluntary and that he defends belief purely for emotional comfort without conditions. Could you provide a careful accuracy audit of her summary? Specifically, please address: whether Clifford's shipowner case is intended to condemn every accidentally true belief or rather a more specific failure of evidential diligence; whether James actually endorses doxastic voluntarism or instead denies that belief can be commanded at will; whether James requires that permissible belief without evidence satisfy conditions such as being live, forced, and momentous, rather than merely satisfying emotional preference; and finally whether her conclusion that James defeats Clifford survives once these distinctions are restored, or whether it relies on conflating James's restricted permission with an unrestricted license.
