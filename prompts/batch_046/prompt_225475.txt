As a longtime reader of rationalist forums, I have noticed a recurring move in certain tightly-knit online communities that I cannot place precisely. Members do not reject empirical methods globally; in fact, they often demand rigorous data for peripheral questions—say, the optimal dosage of a supplement or the reliability of a car brand. But when an empirical finding threatens a core identity-defining belief—say, about the efficacy of a practice central to the group's self-image—they rarely deny the study outright. Instead, they partition the domain: they claim the finding applies only to the general population, or to laboratory conditions, or to people lacking some special feature that members possess, thereby neutralizing the threat without disputing science in general. This is not exactly an echo chamber, because they consume outside information and re-categorize it rather than walling it off.
