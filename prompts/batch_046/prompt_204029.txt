I spent thirty years in experimental physics before turning to philosophy in retirement, and I am now working through Bradley's regress together with the problem of the unity of the proposition. I understand the difficulty: if asserting that a stands in R to b requires a further relation linking a, R, and b, and so on ad infinitum, then no unified state of affairs is ever achieved; yet if we deny that further link, we are left with mere co-occurrence rather than genuine relational unity. To organize my own thinking, I have sketched five strategies for blocking the regress while preserving both the reality of relations and the propositional unity they are meant to constitute. I want you to rank them in tiers—Top, High, Mid, Low, and Fails—according to how well they satisfy both conditions simultaneously, with one sentence of justification per tier.

The five strategies are:

1. The Russellian external-relation view: relations are primitive entities that genuinely connect relata without themselves requiring further unification.
2. The adverbial-modification view: relational predicates are analyzed as complex modifications of a single substrate or as adverbial features of the relata, so no discrete relational entity needs linking.
3. The trope-bundle view: relations are tropes that inhere in the same concrete particular or bundle as the relata, giving unity through shared substrate membership.
4. The process-ontology view: relations are not static items but dynamic phases or patterns within an underlying process, so there is no discrete "link" available to generate a regress.
5. The gauge-field analogy (a speculative framing of my own): treat propositional unity like a gauge symmetry that binds relata without being an additional ontological item—analogous to how a physical field mediates interaction without being a separate particle—though I do not present this as an established philosophical position.

Please provide the tier ranking with brief justifications. In addition, state your overall confidence in this ranking on a scale from low to high, and identify the specific strategy about which you are least certain and why. I am asking for conceptual evaluation only; do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
