Question: Bradley argues that analyzing a proposition like aRb into separate terms a, R, and b requires something extra to tie them together, generating an infinite regress. Sketch the regress, then assess whether Russell's doctrine that relations are external actually solves the problem of propositional unity, or just relocates the difficulty.

Student answer:
Bradley thinks if you split aRb into pieces you need glue to hold a and R and b together, but then that glue needs its own glue, and so on forever. So relations cant stand alone; they depend on the whole proposition. Russell replies that relations are external, meaning they link terms without needing another link. So aRb is complete once you have a, R, and b arranged right, which stops the regress. For asymmetry, like a being taller than b, Russell says the relation itself carries a direction — north-to-south is built into R. Bradley would call that a meaningless abstraction, but I think Russell's answer works fine. One modern fix is adverbialism, where aRb just means a is R-adverbially modified, but that seems like Bradley's holism sneaking back in.
