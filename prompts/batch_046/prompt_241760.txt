You are an expert in normative ethics and experiential pedagogical design. Before drafting anything, briefly brainstorm how a structured choice environment—without any up-front lecture—can lead participants to generate a population-expansion sequence themselves and only recognize its axiological implications retroactively.

Design a 75-minute classroom simulation for a graduate value-theory seminar of 10 students. The objective is to make the group feel the force of the repugnant conclusion, but through an injected framing: each simulated generation belongs to one of three qualitatively distinct welfare domains—embodied experience, abstract cognition, or relational belonging—and cardinal welfare scores are not directly comparable across domains. Students should act as “founders” who vote round-by-round to approve adding a new generation; each round presents a lower within-domain welfare score but a different domain profile. By the final round they should have constructed, through their own approvals, a vastly larger population living at barely-positive levels in unfamiliar domains. Only in the debrief should the facilitator name this as an analog to the repugnant conclusion.

Specify the following:
- Materials (cards, tokens, hidden ballots) and roles (founders, domain-arbiters, recorders).
- Exact rules and a consistency-scoring payoff scheme that uses no real money.
- A minute-by-minute timeline from 0 to 75 minutes.
- The debrief: map each voting round onto a specific axiological move (totalism, averageism, critical-level, incommensurability-block); identify the feature of the philosophical problem the simulation necessarily cannot reproduce (for instance, the actual phenomenal quality of barely-positive lives); and describe what will predictably go wrong the first time it is run and how to correct it.
- Constraints: no lecture before play begins; participants should not be asked to name theories during the simulation; include no bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; and avoid invoking obscure named positions that depend on niche memorization—build all conceptual machinery directly into the rules and cards.
