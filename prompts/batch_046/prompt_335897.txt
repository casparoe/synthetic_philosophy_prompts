Write the joint statement that a Bifurcationist and a Unityist could both sign after an adversarial collaboration on the metaphysics of consciousness in split-brain subjects. By "Bifurcationist," I mean the camp holding that complete corpus callosotomy produces two numerically distinct subjects of experience, each with its own phenomenal field and moral standing; by "Unityist," the camp holding that one subject persists, with consciousness severely disintegrated but not multiplied, and that apparent dual behavior reflects modular disconnection rather than ontological fission.

Your statement should treat as live the following injected framing problem: imagine a hypothetical "graded interhemispheric prosthesis" (GIP) that restores callosal transmission in quantifiable increments—first 10%, then 40%, then 90%—allowing researchers to observe whether integration returns continuously or exhibits a sharp threshold at which a second subject either emerges or dissolves. Both camps should address whether this scenario is conceptually coherent, what pattern of results they would predict, and whether the outcome would bear on their disagreement.

Structure your response exactly as follows:

1. Agreed Claims (numbered list, approximately 350 words): State the claims both camps endorse, including shared descriptions of the neuroanatomy of callosotomy, the behavioral data from classic split-brain experiments, the logical space of possible interpretations, and the conditions under which each side would concede that the other had gained ground. Avoid vacuous formulations (e.g., "both sides care about truth").

2. Residual Disagreements (table, approximately 500 words): Present a table with columns for (a) the precise formulation of the disagreement from the Bifurcationist side, phrased so Bifurcationists would accept it; (b) the precise formulation from the Unityist side, phrased so Unityists would accept it; (c) the underlying crux—stated as a specific sub-question such that resolving it would move at least one camp; (d) the shared test, argument, or hypothetical case both sides agree is relevant, with each side's explicit prediction recorded in advance. Include at least three rows: one on phenomenal unity, one on the criteria for counting subjects, and one on the moral-status implications. Separate genuine substantive disagreements from merely verbal ones, and flag any that depend on the GIP framing.

3. Application to the Graded-Prosthesis Scenario (approximately 250 words): Apply the agreed framework and the residual disagreements to the GIP thought experiment. Specify what continuous versus discontinuous integration patterns would mean for each camp, whether either camp predicts a specific threshold, and whether both sides agree that observing such a pattern would count as decisive—or only as suggestive—evidence.

Constraints: Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Do not invoke highly specific empirical studies by date or author in ways that require verification; refer to the general experimental literature on callosotomy only in broad terms. Do not ask for or include verbatim quotations from real texts. Write as if addressing philosophers who can reason from first principles and shared general knowledge, not from searchable databases.
