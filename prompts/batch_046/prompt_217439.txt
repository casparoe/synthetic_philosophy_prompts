I have recently taken responsibility for redesigning the curriculum of a professional reskilling programme for mid-career adults, and I have become convinced that Whitehead's philosophy of education offers more than a historical curiosity: specifically, that the cyclical rhythm of romance, precision, and generalisation should function as a normative constraint on course architecture, not merely a descriptive psychological pattern. On my interpretation, an idea escapes being inert only if the learner has first subjected it to an imaginative reconstruction that connects specialised content to their own lived experience of the problem; consequently, I have structured our technical modules so that learners encounter ill-structured problems for which no taught method yet exists before they enter the precision stage, reversing the conventional foundations-first model. I also take Whitehead's claim that education aims at the disciplined use of freedom rather than economic utility to entail that assessment should measure a learner's capacity to relate specialised knowledge to wider contexts, not merely demonstrate competency.

What would count against this position? I want an honest map of its exposure rather than a generic defence of progressive pedagogy. Which empirical findings in adult learning, cognitive load theory, or vocational psychology would genuinely undermine the claim that romance must precede precision? Are there well-documented cases of effective technical education that systematically violate the rhythm, and do they show the rhythm is dispensable or only that my interpretation is too rigid? Could the notion of imaginative reconstruction be shown to be either too vague to guide design or reducible to standard constructivist theory, thereby stripping the Whiteheadian framing of distinctive content? Does my reversal of the foundations-first model risk unfalsifiability — if a cohort performs poorly, can I always attribute failure to an improper balance of stages rather than to the model itself? Furthermore, what would committed defenders of this view concede as genuinely counting against them: perhaps that economic utility and education are less opposed than Whitehead assumes, or that the rhythm applies differently to adults? Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, indicate whether any have arguably already occurred in existing research, and assess whether the position has been qualified into insulation. I am not requesting bibliographies or citations; I want arguments and cases that can be evaluated on their own merits.
