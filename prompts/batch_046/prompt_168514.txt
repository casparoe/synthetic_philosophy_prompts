Consider a regional land-use authority that decides on development proposals through two jointly applied rules. The first grants every affected household a personal-domain veto: if a proposal alters the cultural, religious, or domestic character of that household's immediate property or customary practice, the household may block it unilaterally, irrespective of broader support. The second is a collective-rationality filter: the authority may approve a proposal only if its own multi-attribute welfare index shows no resident to be worse off, where welfare aggregates environmental quality, economic opportunity, and access to shared amenities. The authority presents these rules as exhaustive—there is no appeal to democratic majority, to a hierarchy of rights, or to deliberative ranking that could override either rule when they conflict.

What philosophical commitments about the nature of liberal rights, the possibility of interpersonal welfare comparison, and the coherence of collective choice does this institutional design presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit theory: does the veto treat rights as non-aggregative side-constraints in the manner Sen's liberal paradox describes, or can it be interpreted as a preference among others? Does the Pareto-style filter presuppose cardinal comparability of welfare across persons, or is it compatible with purely ordinal rankings? Is the authority's claim that the two rules are jointly sufficient philosophically defensible, or does it reproduce exactly the incompatibility between minimal liberalism and the Pareto principle? Distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates, and say whether several rival theories—contractarian, welfarist, or rights-based—could license the same procedure. Finally, indicate what structural changes to the authority's decision process would be required if one rejected either the legitimacy of unilateral vetoes over collective goods or the meaningfulness of aggregating welfare across individuals. Please answer in British English.
