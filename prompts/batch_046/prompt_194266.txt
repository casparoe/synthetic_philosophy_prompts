Consider the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: pleasures range from the sensory to the intellectual to the malicious, yet hedonism treats them all as intrinsically good in virtue of being pleasures. Rank the following five candidate unifying features into tiers (S through F) according to how plausibly they capture what all pleasures genuinely share, giving a one-sentence justification for each placement and noting any pair you regard as jointly unsatisfiable: (1) an identical phenomenal glow or felt-quality present in every case; (2) a shared motivational disposition to prolong or repeat the state; (3) being the intentional object of a second-order pro-attitude; (4) functional integration into a common reward-prediction error architecture; (5) a formal structural property—namely, being a conscious mode in which one's present experience is taken by the subject to be going well. In constructing your ranking, weigh especially whether each candidate can accommodate alien pleasures—those of minds with radically different cognitive architectures—without either excluding them from the domain of pleasure or reducing their diversity to a single sensory affect.
