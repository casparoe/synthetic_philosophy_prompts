Construct a thought experiment in which an institutional resource-allocation board must decide whether to redirect long-term care funding from ten patients with standard, life-limiting conditions to one patient with a rare, stable neurological profile that produces sustained, intense positive affect without cognitive impairment, suffering, or loss of agency—such that the single patient experiences aggregate welfare comparable to the ten combined, but distributed continuously over several decades. Constraints: the setting must be realistic (a public-health triage committee, not a fantasy world), presented in one paragraph; the beneficiary must be an actual human being rather than a fictional creature; the board's decision must depend on predictive judgments about welfare duration rather than on qualitative differences in experience; and the case must be engineered so that act-utilitarianism recommends the redirection, prioritarianism resists it, and a threshold-based sufficientarianism also resists it, with the divergence traceable to a single isolable feature. After presenting the scenario, explain the verdict each named theory delivers and why, identify the feature of the case that does the work, describe how removing that feature—making the neurological condition temporary rather than chronic—alters the verdicts, and state how a defender of the utilitarian position would attack the framing of the case as unfairly constructed.
