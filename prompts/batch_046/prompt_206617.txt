Before evaluating competing explanations for how norms such as chattel slavery and dueling lost their moral authority, I want the full specification of what an adequate theory of moral revolution must deliver. Write the desiderata: the phenomena it must explain, the cases it must get right, the conceptual distinctions it must respect, and the historical or sociological results with which it must be consistent — with the consideration behind each requirement.

Specifically, any adequate theory must account for the documented decline of dueling from an honorable social obligation to a disreputable or criminal practice, and for the collapse of slavery as an institution despite its deep economic entrenchment. It must be able to distinguish, or show indistinguishable, three mechanisms of change: shifts driven by rational moral argument, shifts driven by material interests and coordination problems, and shifts driven by internal dynamics of honor and shame. The theory must also handle intermediate or hybrid configurations — for example, a constructed case of "ritual substitution," in which a lethal honor practice is replaced by a non-lethal ceremonial form that preserves the original social-signaling function but eliminates physical risk. The theory should classify whether such substitution constitutes a genuine moral revolution or a strategic preservation of honor under altered conditions, and it should explain why some norms vanish entirely while others migrate into private, symbolic, or subcultural registers.

Provide the requirements list, flag which are non-negotiable (for instance, consistency with the broad timeline of abolition and the decline of dueling, or the ability to explain variation in the pace of normative change across societies) and which reflect the preferences of particular camps (for instance, the claim that honor is conceptually irreducible to interest, or the claim that rational argument can never drive large-scale normative change without prior shifts in material incentives). Identify any subset of desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable — such as requiring both that honor dynamics are fully autonomous from economic interest and that every moral revolution can be completely explained by changing incentive structures. Then, briefly, assess how the principal existing positions fare against your list: the honor-centered view associated with Bernard Williams, the interest-and-reputation framework associated with Kwame Anthony Appiah, and the rationalist view that moral revolutions occur primarily through the acceptance of better arguments. Keep the emphasis on the criteria and their potential conflicts; the evaluation of contenders is secondary. Write in American English. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
