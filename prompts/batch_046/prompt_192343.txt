I'm putting together a longread on why scientists cling to models they know are literally false — perfect competition, frictionless planes, that kind of thing — and I keep bumping into Friedman's old F-twist argument, where he more or less said the realism of your assumptions doesn't matter as long as the predicitions come out right. I was chatting with a couple of friends in econ phil about it, one who thinks Friedman was basically right but people misread him, and another who thinks the whole insulation idea is dangerous. So I want you to draft what they'd produce if they actually did a proper adversarial collaboration.

Let's name the two camps the Insulationists and the Structuralists. The Insulationists hold that unrealistic assumptions can be syntactically insulated — they sit in the model like scaffolding that never touches the prediction-generating machinery, so predictive success is enough. The Structuralists think that's a fantasy; every false assumption leaks into how you interpret the results, and you need what I'm calling a mechanical transparency ladder — a step-by-step map of how relaxing each unrealistic feature changes the output — before you can claim the model is about the real world sytem at all. I want the joint statement to cover: (1) a numbered list of agreed claims — about what the F-twist actually asserts, about the limits of predictive testing, maybe about whether that ladder can in principle be built for any model; (2) the disagreements in neutral wording, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording of their's view; (3) the cruxes — I think one is whether predictive insulation can be defined without smuggling in hidden value judgments about which predictions count, and another is whether there's a hard tractability boundary where relaxing assumptions kills the model, or if that's just a failure of imagination; and (4) for each crux, a concrete case or reasoning both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction spelled out. Don't give me a bibliography or reading list — I just need the conceptual meat, and feel free to inject whatever niche framing makes the disagreement precise rather than just reheating the same old Friedman debate.
