I recently had to select between two forecasting frameworks for a long-range planning problem—one optimized purely for point accuracy, the other preserving deliberately contradictory sub-models to maintain structural resilience. This led me to sketch an essay arguing that accuracy-first epistemology, when formalized through proper scoring rules, contains a hidden presupposition of environmental stationarity. I propose that in non-stationary domains, the epistemic utility of accuracy collapses unless paired with what I call “revision elasticity”: the minimal cost of locally repairing a belief network after a single false node is discovered, without global reconstruction. I suggest this forces accuracy-first views to either smuggle in explanatory coherence or accept catastrophic fragility, but I worry I may be conflating epistemic utility with practical robustness. Could you comment on whether my argument genuinely distinguishes these, and whether “revision elasticity” is a coherent metric for epistemic utility theory rather than a disguised preference for understanding?
