Here is where I stand: (1) I accept Schlick’s thesis that meaning resides exclusively in “play”—activity valued for its own sake—while all purposive labor is instrumentally meaningless in itself. (2) I also endorse his logical-positivist claim that traditional metaphysical questions about “the meaning of life” are cognitively empty unless reconstructed as empirical questions about human psychology or social conditions. (3) From (1) and (2) together, I draw the normative conclusion that social organization ought to maximize play and minimize compulsory labor. (4) I hold that the play/work distinction is not ontologically fixed but depends on subjective attitude, so an identical task can be play for one agent and drudgery for another. (5) I think Schlick’s optimism—that scientific progress will eventually automate necessary labor and thereby universalize play—is empirically undermined by the historical tendency of automation to generate new bureaucratic and surveillance labor rather than freeing time. (6) I want to defend this view against the objection that if meaning is purely attitudinal play, then destructive or addictive behaviors also count as meaningful; I deny this by insisting that genuine play must be compatible with the agent’s long-term flourishing.
