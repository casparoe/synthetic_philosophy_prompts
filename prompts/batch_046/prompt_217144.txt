I spent thirty years in experimental physics before turning to philosophy of science in retirement. I have formed the view that path dependence—the thesis that history is locked in and that equivalent outcomes would not have arrived through different routes—is largely confined to economic, social, and technological domains, but fails to apply to mature natural science in any strong form. More precisely, I maintain that once a theoretical framework achieves predictive convergence across independent experimental regimes, the space of viable alternatives collapses into what one might call an empirical attractor basin: escape from that basin becomes practically impossible regardless of the contingent path taken to enter it. Thus, while the particular formalisms and historical ordering of discoveries in quantum electrodynamics or general relativity may have been contingent, the underlying structures they describe were not; any scientific community with comparable observational reach and mathematical resources would eventually have been drawn into substantially the same theoretical neighborhoods.

What would count against this position? I want an honest map of its exposure, not a list of generic objections. Specifically: which historical cases, formal results, or empirical patterns would genuinely weaken the claim that mature science converges on unique attractors? Please distinguish between defeaters that are fatal—documented instances of mature, empirically successful but structurally incompatible theoretical frameworks that persist indefinitely—from those that are merely awkward, such as the contingent sequencing of discovery or the influence of individual scientists. For each major category of defeater, indicate whether I, as a defender of convergence, should concede it as damaging to the core claim, or whether I can dismiss it without abandoning the view, perhaps by relegating the case to a pre-convergence or immature stage. I am particularly concerned about whether my position risks being qualified into unfalsifiability: if every apparent counterexample is explained away as not yet having reached the convergence threshold, is that insulation a virtue or a vice for a claim of this kind?

Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and for each provide a brief indication of what a committed convergence theorist would likely concede versus what they would reject.
