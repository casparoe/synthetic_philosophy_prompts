Construct a formal decision-tree taxonomy that classifies theoretical stances on looping effects in psychiatric nosology, using as its running illustration a hypothetical diagnostic category—tentatively called “adaptive dysregulation syndrome”—whose prevalence triples within five years of its introduction not through biological spread but through clinician-patient feedback loops that gradually reshape both symptom presentation and the operational criteria applied by assessors. Build the tree from logical commitments rather than from a list of scholars: the root should ask whether psychiatric kinds are genuinely “interactive kinds” in Hacking’s sense, such that being classified changes the people classified; the first split should ask whether that loop is epistemically corrupting (undermining the category’s validity) or constitutively productive (partly making the kind real); the second split should ask whether the appropriate institutional response is loop-proofing (stabilizing criteria against feedback) or loop-responsive (institutionalizing reflexive co-evolution of the category with its subjects); and the final split should ask whether epidemiological tracking ought to correct for looping-induced prevalence shifts or treat those shifts as constitutive data about the kind’s dynamics in the world. Each terminal leaf should carry either the name of an actual position—such as Hacking’s own interactive realism, a strict biological-essentialist rejection of interactivity, an eliminativist social-constructionist view, or a pragmatic “stewardship” model—or be labeled “unnamed” if it occupies the logical space without a prominent defender, together with one sentence stating either its most awkward commitment or a brief diagnosis of why it remains unoccupied (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored). Phrase every branch as a concrete claim or case judgment so that a respondent can navigate the tree without knowing the theoretical labels in advance, and present the result as a polished seminar instrument suitable for a philosophy-of-psychiatry handout. Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the instrument should be answerable entirely through conceptual analysis.
