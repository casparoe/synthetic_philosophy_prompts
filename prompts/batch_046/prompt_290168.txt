Write a dialogue between a defender of the School of Names thesis that a white horse is not a horse and a critic who maintains that kind membership survives accidental property change. Frame the exchange around whether "white horse" names a composite kind, a subset, or a modified particular, with the defender arguing that color and form belong to different orders of determination—so that specifying whiteness introduces a determinable that the pure form of horse-ness does not contain—while the critic presses that if color functions as a differentia it ought to generate recognized subkinds like bay horse, which the Mingjia do not treat separately, implying the distinction is either rhetorical or collapses into an implausible ontology of separated properties. Have them respond to each other directly: the defender should address the subkind objection by clarifying whether hardness and whiteness are ontologically separable or merely conceptually distinguishable, and the critic should test that clarification by asking whether a featherless bird is therefore not a bird. Present the dialogue entirely as continuous prose without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, indicating speakers naturally within the narrative flow as in a written letter recounting a conversation.
