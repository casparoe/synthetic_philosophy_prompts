I'm on a board for a small arts foundation that funds residencies where artists usually become close friends. We keep having to decide wether to split up toxic collaborations, restore broken joint works, or fund ex-friends to work together again, and I want a reusable decision procedure based on Moore's idea that beauty and friendship are organic unities — the value of the whole isn't just parts summed. Write a step-by-step checklist my board can run in twenty minutes: questions in order, what counts as enough info, hard stops where the answer is no, and spots where the theory runs out and we just vote. Apply it to three cases: two painters with a toxic friendship but one great joint mural; a cracked ceramic ritual piece made by friends where fixing it changes the meaning; and funding a new residency for two ex-friends post-breakup. Make one case come out the opposite of what we'd want, and flag exactly where judgment can't be eliminated.
