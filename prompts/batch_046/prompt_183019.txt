I am writing the final exam for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on Wittgenstein's later philosophy. I want one essay question that forces students to apply the private language argument to a constructed scenario rather than recite the standard beetle-in-the-box summary. The scenario—call it the dual-box calibration case—should be self-contained and built only out of familiar Wittgensteinian machinery: private ostensive definition, rule-following, criterion of correctness, and the gap between seeming right and being right.

The scenario is as follows. A subject S attempts to maintain a private lexicon for two qualitatively distinct but publicly unobservable internal states, A and B. S assigns them private symbols, α and β, anchored initially through a private ostensive act directed at each state. Over time, S introduces an internal calibration check: before applying α or β, S attempts to mentally reproduce the original experience and compare it with the present one. S claims this comparison supplies an independent criterion of correctness. Later, the scenario is modified: S introduces temporal delay and admits that memory of the original anchoring experience is fallible.

The exam question should ask students to address three things:
(i) Reconstruct Wittgenstein's argument that private ostensive definition cannot establish a rule, applying it specifically to S's attempt to anchor α and β and to the internal calibration check.
(ii) Identify precisely where S's calibration procedure either collapses into conditions that require public accessibility or fails to provide an independent standard—focusing on the logical distinction between being correct and merely feeling correct.
(iii) Assess whether adding fallible memory and temporal delay strengthens or weakens the private-language claim, with particular attention to whether Wittgenstein's point about the absence of an external check applies differently when the checking mechanism is itself unreliable.

Please provide:
- The exam question itself, clearly stating the scenario and the three tasks, suitable for a 90-minute essay of approximately 800–1000 words.
- A model answer of about 800 words that does not merely summarize the private language argument but applies it mechanistically to the dual-box case, explicitly addressing the circularity risk in S's calibration and connecting it to Wittgenstein's rule-following considerations.
- A marking scheme that assigns marks to each of the three parts, separates A-level answers (those that pinpoint the circularity and the logical—not merely epistemic—failure of the calibration check) from B-level answers (those that apply the argument generically without engaging the specific mechanics of the scenario), specifies the exact conceptual mistake that costs marks in each part (e.g., treating internal comparison as analogous to public correction), and names the common trap misconception—conflating the problem of unreliable memory with the logical absence of a criterion of correctness.

The prompt should be answerable without web access or citation of specific texts; do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or direct quotations. Paraphrase any reference to Wittgenstein's text without quotation marks.
