I was chatting with a friend in philosphy grad school about Hacking's looping effects — the feedback loop where putting a label out there changes the people who then get labeled, which changes the data clinicians collect — and she pointed out that in the literature this has turned into a permanent stalemate. One group says that when patients start describing themselves in terms of a new category like "chronic temporal dislocation" or high-functioning burnout, that proves the diagnosis is partly manufactured and therefore less real; the other group says minds are porous to culture, full stop, and adapting your self-narrative to a diagnosis doesn't erase whatever biological mechanism was already there. They've been trading these moves for over a decade and nobody moves an inch.

What's actually going on structurally? Diagnose it using what each side actually argues, and tell me what kind of progress is even possible here: a conceptual distinction, a specific study design, or should we expect the impasse to stay?
