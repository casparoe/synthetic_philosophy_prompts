Take Aspect’s polarization-entanglement experiments—including the rapid-switching design meant to close the locality loophole—as the real episode, and analyze it specifically through Bohmian mechanics. First, map precisely what the experimenters faced: in a photon-polarization setting, what plays the role of the “hidden variables” when the original pilot-wave ontology is built around definite particle positions; whether the non-local quantum potential can reproduce the observed correlation statistics without invoking wavefunction collapse or Everettian branching; and whether the rapid spatial separation of analyzer settings creates any special tension for a theory that already accepts instantaneous influence. Say which features of the episode Bohm explains cleanly—such as deterministic outcomes given an initial wavefunction and a clear rejection of local realism—and which it misdescribes or leaves silent, particularly the ontological status of spin and polarization degrees of freedom that are not straightforwardly reducible to particle trajectories. Contrast this briefly with the Everettian account (unitary global evolution, no privileged outcome, branching relative states) and with Copenhagen’s refusal to assign underlying reality to the correlations at all. Then reverse the lens: do these results confirm Bohmian mechanics by demonstrating that hidden variables survive if locality is sacrificed; do they expose a structural weakness in extending a position-based ontology to internal quantum numbers and field-theoretic settings; or are they essentially neutral? I want an honest assessment of where the mapping breaks rather than a forced defense, including the one point about the experiment that Bohmian mechanics has no natural vocabulary for.
