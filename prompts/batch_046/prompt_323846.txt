I am a final-year philosophy undergraduate presenting my seminar paper next Tuesday in our graduate-level formal epistemology course. The thesis I defend is that accuracy-first epistemology—the view that the only intrinsic epistemic values are true belief and the avoidance of false belief—collapses into pragmatic encroachment once we model doxastic states as competing for a scarce resource I call credal bandwidth. I argue that if belief-formation carries genuine cognitive opportunity costs—so that maintaining high-confidence credences in one domain necessarily diverts resources that could yield greater aggregate accuracy elsewhere—then an agent who maximizes total epistemic value must weight beliefs by the practical stakes of the domains they govern. This reintroduces non-epistemic considerations into rational belief formation, undermining the purity of the accuracy-first position. The audience is a mixed graduate seminar of ten students plus one faculty respondent: two are committed accuracy-first theorists well-versed in decision-theoretic derivations of probabilism; one faculty member is a pragmatic-encroachment specialist; three are philosophers of science who regard normative epistemology as detached from scientific practice; and the remainder are neutral but skeptical of formal modeling in epistemology.

Please prepare my Q&A prep sheet. Structure the response in the following sections, in this order:

2. Audience map (about 100 words): identify which objections will come from which subgroup, and why.
3. Ordered objections (about 900 words total): present the five objections I will actually face, in the order they are likely to arise. 4. The hidden objection (about 200 words): the question I am hoping nobody asks, phrased as it would be delivered, with my best reply and an explicit diagnosis of why it is the most dangerous point for my position.
5. Strategic bottom line (about 120 words): my overall posture—where to concede globally, where never to retreat, and the single weakest link in my own argument that I should defend openly rather than hope goes unnoticed.

Keep every reply concrete and tied to the credal-bandwidth framing.
