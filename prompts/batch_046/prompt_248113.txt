For a rationalist reading-group handout, I want a taxonomy—not a gallery of named theories—of positions on the Liar paradox and on truth in general. Build it as a decision tree with four diagnostic questions that a reader can answer without knowing the labels: (1) Is the Liar sentence grammatically well-formed and expressing a single proposition? (2) If yes, should the contradiction be resolved by restricting the truth predicate through a hierarchy, by allowing truth-value gaps, by allowing truth-value gluts, or by denying that the predicate applies at all? (3) Does your resolution preserve bivalence for ordinary non-paradoxical sentences? (4) Is the root problem semantic (about the word 'true'), logical (about negation), or pragmatic (about assertion)? Map the answer-combinations onto named positions—Tarski-style hierarchy, gap theories like Kripkean fixed-point or revision approaches, paraconsistent or dialetheist views, deflationary or minimalist accounts, contextualist or pragmatic accounts—and onto empty slots. For each leaf, give the position name (or 'unnamed') and one sentence describing its strangest consequence, especially what it implies about a sentence that asserts its own falsehood across a temporal sequence or in a strengthened revenge formulation. Flag any pairs the tree cannot separate, such as certain contextualists and deflationists that might converge on the same answers. Note unoccupied combinations—e.g., global gap acceptance combined with full bivalence preservation for all non-paradoxical discourse—and briefly diagnose whether each looks incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored. Keep it for people who have read about the paradox but do not want encyclopedia entries, bibliographies, or citations. At the end, state how confident you are in the tree's separation of contextualist from deflationary leaves and identify the specific node you are least certain belongs in the tree.
