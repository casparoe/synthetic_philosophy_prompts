I want you to construct a rigorous philosophical argument for the claim that Bell's theorem, when analysed within this relational framework, does not merely exclude local hidden-variable accounts but actively destabilises the conceptual coherence of relational locality itself, thereby forcing a reconceptualisation of quantum correlation that avoids both naive wave-function realism and pure instrumentalism.

Your argument should proceed from first principles: begin with the standard CHSH formulation and distinguish carefully between parameter independence and outcome independence, showing how each bears upon the relational locality thesis I have sketched. You must then address the common appeal to the rejection of counterfactual definiteness as an escape route, and demonstrate why—on my framing—this rejection reinforces rather than resolves the tension between Bell-type correlations and relational locality. I am not seeking a literature survey, bibliography, or attribution of specific texts; I require the conceptual architecture developed independently, with explicit attention to how probabilistic causal modelling differs from the topological approach I have outlined. The response should be composed in British English, pitched at a graduate audience capable of following formal reasoning in quantum foundations, and should avoid treating locality as an unanalysed primitive.
