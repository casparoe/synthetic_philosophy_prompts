Write an essay on whether absolute brain size is ever morally relevant to the assignment of moral status, independent of the functional and organizational capacities it typically supports. Consider the correlation argument—that larger brains generally enable more complex cognition—together with the architectural objection that neuronal density and connectivity patterns (for instance, in corvid pallial organization or in a hypothetical miniaturized synthetic substrate with cortical-scale synaptic density confined to a sub-cubic-centimeter volume) can decouple physical scale from cognitive and experiential capacity. Examine whether physical scale carries any independent moral weight—perhaps through robustness, developmental trajectory, or ecological embeddedness—or whether a being with a very small but highly organized substrate could possess equal or greater moral status than one with a large but diffusely organized brain. Weigh the main arguments on both sides, but do not end non-committally: defend a specific verdict about whether brain size should ever figure as an independent criterion in grading moral status.
