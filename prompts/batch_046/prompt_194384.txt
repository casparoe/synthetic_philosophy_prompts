As a retired biologist who has recently begun reading philosophy of mind, I am trying to understand the dispute over whether emotions are judgments, perceptions, or feelings. I have been struck by the predictive-processing account of interoception—the idea that affective states arise from precision-weighted prediction errors about future bodily needs rather than from present stimuli—and I wonder whether this framework dissolves the traditional trichotomy or merely relocates it. Write a dialogue between two equally informed interlocutors: one who argues that this predictive-interoceptive model reconceives emotion as a calibrated readiness-to-act that makes the judgment/perception/feeling distinction obsolete, and one who insists that without an intentional object evaluated as good or bad, such readiness is mere physiology rather than emotion. Have them respond directly to each other, especially on whether evolutionary signaling can substitute for evaluative content, and avoid making either speaker a strawman. The exchange should be rigorous yet accessible to a scientifically literate reader without formal philosophical training.
