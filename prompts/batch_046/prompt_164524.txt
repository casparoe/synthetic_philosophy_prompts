I am preparing a chapter for my doctoral thesis on deflationary theories of truth, and I wish to move beyond the standard minimalism-against-correspondence framing. I need a rigorous specification of the desiderata that any adequate deflationary account must satisfy—not merely a catalogue of what it denies, but a positive requirements list. Please set out the full architecture: the explanatory phenomena it must accommodate (for instance, the role of truth in accounting for predictive success, the utility of semantic ascent, and the treatment of liar-type paradoxes); the conceptual distinctions it must preserve (for instance, between truth and warranted assertibility, between transparent disquotation and metaphysical commitment, and between semantic and pragmatic ascent); and the formal constraints with which it must remain consistent (for instance, Tarskian indefinability results and compositionality conditions). For each requirement, indicate whether it is genuinely non-negotiable or merely a prejudice of one camp, and supply the motivating case or theoretical pressure behind it.

I want to inject one specific, somewhat neglected pressure point: the interaction between deflationism and normative or modal discourse, particularly where expressivist considerations appear to block straightforward disquotation. Must a deflationary theory preserve some distinction between 'true in virtue of the world' and 'true in virtue of linguistic convention' even while it denies that truth is a substantive property? How does this affect its treatment of ethical and modal sentences?

Finally, please identify which single desideratum is most damaging to the prominent deflationary candidates—minimalism, disquotationalism, and redundancy theories—and present the scoring briefly, preferably in tabular form. I am not seeking a bibliography, a reading list, or historical citations; my aim is to establish the conceptual standard of adequacy itself.
