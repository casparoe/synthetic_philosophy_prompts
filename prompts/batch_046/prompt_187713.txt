Write a short story about a Victorian moral philosopher—think along the lines of the guy who wrote Methods of Ethics but also sat on those psychical research committees—who runs a private circle testing mediums. He tries to apply his usual three-part evidential framework—coherence, consistency, and "residual doubt"—to evaluate ghost sightings. The twist is that the framework works to well: it starts classifing his own memories of dead freinds as probable apparitions. Now he has to decide whether to publish his findings or burn the notebooks. Make it feel like a ghost story that accidentally wandered into a philosophy seminar and got lost in the attic. No need for a tidy moral at the end.
