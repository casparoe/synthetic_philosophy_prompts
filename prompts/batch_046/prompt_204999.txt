I'm a retired biophysicist who got into philosophy after I left the lab, and I'm trying to understand this procreation assymetry idea but my study group pushed it into a strange corner. Here's the argument we built, and it feels like it proves way too much.

(1) For an act to be morally wrong, there must be an actual or determinately future person who is made worse off by it.
(2) If we don't conceive a happy child, there's no one who exists to be deprived—just a gap—so failing to create happy people isn't wrong.
(3) But if we do conceive and the child suffers, there's a clear victim, so that's wrong.
(4) Now apply this to climate: our emissions change global conditions, so conception times and partners shift, meaning the people born in 2100 depend on our carbon choices.
(5) Because fertilization is sensitive to microscopic fluctuations—Brownian motion in the tract, temperature shifts—the specific individuals in the high-emission world are physically disjoint from those in the low-emission world.
(6) So for any suffering person in 2100, they wouldn't have existed if we'd cut emissions; there's no identifiable victim made worse off.
(7) Therefore by (1), harming future generations through climate change isn't wrong either.
(8) In fact this wipes out all obligations to people who don't exist yet, since any change in conditions changes who comes to be.
(9) But that can't be right—obviously we owe something to future people.
(10) So where's the leak? I'd prefer to keep (1) and (4). Tell me which step to reject, why it seemed okay at first, and what I'm paying to drop it.
