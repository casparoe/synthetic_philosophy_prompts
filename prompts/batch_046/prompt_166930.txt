I am presenting this at a joint philosophy-of-mind and cognitive-science seminar next Tuesday. The audience includes introspection researchers who treat first-person reports as data, physicalists who think illusionism denies the obvious, and one representationalist who holds that if consciousness is representational then illusionism is incoherent. They care about reportability, neural correlates, and empirical constraints, not metaphysics for its own sake.

My thesis: phenomenal consciousness is not an illusion simpliciter; rather, phenomenal unity is an introspective construct projected onto genuinely existing but fragmented micro-experiences. The standard anti-illusionist objections conflate the reality of the fragments with the illusion of the integrated whole.
