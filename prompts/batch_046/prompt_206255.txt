I work for a county parole review board, and every few months I have to write recommendations on early-release petitions for people serving long sentences. The board is tired of me hand-waving between retributivism and consequentialism, so they want an actual decision procedre I can run in about 30 minutes — ordered steps, what counts as a yes/no at each gate, and explicit flags for where I'm smuggling in a value judgment. The board also just adopted this weird internal rule about "biographical distance": the idea that if the offender's psychology or cognitive state has shifted enough, the retributive debt should be weighted downward because the agent who'd suffer isn't fully identical to the one who did the crime. So the procedure needs to fold that in without making it a free pass.

Write me the checklist. Step 1 should establish the base desert level (retributive). Step 2 should measure biographical distance with concrete indicators, not vibes — aging, cognitive change, sustained identity disruption — and say what evidence counts and what doesn't. Step 3 should run a consequentialist predictive-stability check — not just "is he dangerous now?" but whether the remaining years actually produce measurable security gains versus just sitting in a cell. Step 4 should be a conflict-resolution gate where the two theories point opposite directions, and the procedure has to say who wins and on what ground. At every step, say what evidence I need, what counts as insufficient, and where the procedure stops with a hard deny. Then flag the points where judgment can't be eliminated — for instance, how much distance is "enough" — and be honest that those spots are dressed-up values calls.

Then apply it to two cases:
- A 71-year-old serving life for a murder at 19, now with severe dementia, petitioning for compassionate release. He doesn't remember the crime.
- A 38-year-old who served 5 years of a 15-year fraud sentence, now running a nonprofit that trains formerly incarcerated people, petitioning to serve the rest on community supervision.

One of these should come out in a way I'd hate if I'm mostly retributivist — but the procedure should still deliver it cleanly, with the steps visible. And tell me exactly where the procedure misfires or gives an answer that feels wrong but is technically correct according to the steps. Don't cite philosophers or give me a reading list; I just need the reusable procedure and the worked examples.
