I am preparing a set of interview questions for my high school's oral-history podcast series. My guest is a historian who has recently proposed a new conceptual criterion—she calls it "administrative fossilization"—for determining when a legend becomes history. Rather than relying on archaeological corroboration of events, she argues that a tradition crosses into history only when it is embedded in bureaucratic, legal, or fiscal records whose primary purpose is record-keeping rather than storytelling. She applies this framework to three well-known cases: the Trojan War, where Hittite administrative texts mention Wilusa but contain no bureaucratic trace of a prolonged siege; the Arthurian tradition, where no sixth-century British administrative records mention Arthur despite later medieval references; and the Exodus account, where New Kingdom Egyptian bureaucratic archives—extensive in other domains—show no administrative footprint of a large-scale departure of laborers.

Please draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond asking her to restate the view. I want at least one question on each of the three cases—Trojan War, King Arthur, and the Exodus—each paired with a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. I also want one question that presses the distinction between an absence of bureaucratic records and a mere absence of evidence, and one that asks what her framework implies for traditions preserved solely in poetic or religious texts with no administrative footprint whatsoever. The questions should be suitable for a high-school interviewer who knows the basic outlines but wants to probe the mechanics and limits of her criterion. Do not include requests for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
