ok so i'm trying to write down my view on explaining revolutions (mostly teh french one) for a discussion thread, and i want a consistency check before i post it. i'm coming at this from the rationalist/forecasting side, so my takes are a mix of taleb-style narrative skepticism and some standard historiography.

here's what i actually think:
- hindsight bias is real and makes every post-1789 account suspicious, but i don't think it means we should just throw out all causal claims.
- the narrative fallacy (taleb's version, not just generic story-bias) is especially bad for revolutionary history because the sample size is basically n=1 and the emotional arc (liberty -> terror -> napoleon) is too neat.
- there's still a real structural mechanism behind revolutions devouring their children: when a revolutionary movement captures state power and then faces internal factionalism, the moderates get purged. this happened in france and russia and elsewhere, so it's not pure storytelling.
- at the micro-level contingency is huge (robbespierre dying in 1789 changes everything) but at the macro-level it's low: class conflict and fiscal crisis made some kind of upheaval likely.
- i reject deterministic marxist accounts that make the revolution inevitable, but i also reject pure contingency accounts that treat the Terror as a random accident with no pattern.
- i think counterfactuals are useful but should be mininal rewrites (robbespierre lives, a harvest fails) not macro ones (no revolution ever).
- i think ideological purity is the real driver of the Terror, not just power politics: the jacobins genuinely believed they were saving the revolution from conspirators.
- i also have this weird belief that small LLMs trained on primary sources amplify narrative fallacy more than human historians do, because they learn distributional coherence rather than causal coherence, so they should only be used for retrieval not synthesis.

which of these are actually in tension? like, does my claim about structural mechanisms clash with my claim about ideological purity? and does my macro-low-contingency view make my mininal counterfactuals useless? also is my LLM take just incompatible with my skepticism about synthesized narratives? please rank the tensions by severity, say what the cheapest fix is for each, and tell me if there's one revision that cleans up the most mess. don't need citations, just logic. also sorry if there's a typo or two, writing this on my phone.
