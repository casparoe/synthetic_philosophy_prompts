I'm currently taking an upper-level seminar in metaphysics and philosophy of science, and I'm developing a short essay around the problem of ontology identification when epistemic access is permanently blocked. Rather than writing the full draft, I've sketched the core argument in notes and would appreciate targeted comments on its coherence, accuracy, and whether the framing actually distinguishes itself from positions already in the literature.

The working title is Sealed Ontology: Identification Without Access. The central idea is this. Most accounts of ontological commitment—whether Quinean, in which ontology is fixed through the bound variables of our best theories, or more causally grounded—presume that entities can be identified, at least in principle, through some form of informational access: measurement, causal interaction, or theoretical embedment. I want to examine a stricter case: entities that are not merely unobservable in practice, but principally sealed, meaning all direct and indirect channels to their intrinsic properties are structurally impossible, leaving only an external relational footprint—specifically, a unique pattern of constraints on phenomena we can access. Call these black-box existents.

My argument proceeds in four steps. First, standard Quinean commitment might seem to license quantification over such entities if they are indispensable to our best explanatory framework. But I worry this is insufficient for identification: if two distinct black-box existents generate identical structural footprints, quantification alone cannot fix which one we are committed to, producing an underdetermination stronger than the usual theory-evidence variety. Second, I propose a structural-proxy method of identification: we identify the entity not by intrinsic properties but solely by its unique causal-explanatory role in the network of accessible phenomena. Third, I anticipate the objection that this collapses into structural realism—if we only ever grasp relations, we are really committing to structures, not to particular entities. I try to resist this by defending a thin ontological commitment: we maintain singular reference to particular nodes in the causal graph (so it is not pure structuralism), but we deny that this reference carries any intrinsic nature (so it is not full-blooded entity realism). Fourth, I consider whether this thin view is stable, or whether it simply collapses into instrumentalism about sealed entities.

I'd specifically like feedback on the following. First, is the distinction between practical unobservability and principal informational sealing philosophically robust, or does it collapse once we allow theoretical inference? Second, does the shift to structural-proxy identification genuinely avoid the slide into structural realism, or is thin commitment just a relabeling of an existing position? Third, is my use of Quinean ontological commitment accurate in this context—particularly the claim that quantification without distinguishability fails to secure identification? Fourth, does the essay's framing feel like a meaningful contribution, or does it mostly reinvent debates around entity realism versus structural realism without adding anything? Any suggestions for sharpening the argument or identifying hidden premises would be especially welcome.
