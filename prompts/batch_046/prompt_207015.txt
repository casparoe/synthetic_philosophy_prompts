Write a deadpan satirical parody of a luxury wellness retreat's promotional brochure and binding membership agreement for an immersive "Experience Machine" vacation package. The voice should be that of a polished high-end travel magazine crossed with legal fine print: inviting, precise, and quietly absurd. The brochure should advertise a neurologically calibrated stay that delivers precisely tailored sequences of achievement, intimacy, and creative fulfillment with no corresponding real-world outcomes, risks, or lasting skills. Interspersed with the marketing copy, include a contract formatted as numbered clauses that quietly encode philosophical positions on well-being: Clause 2 should define "guest satisfaction" in purely phenomenological terms, implicitly endorsing hedonism; Clause 4 should state that preference-frustration occurring outside the calibrated environment does not constitute harm to the guest, parodying desire-satisfaction accounts; and Clause 7 should require guests to waive any claim that the delivered experiences lack "objective achievement value," mocking objective-list theories. Add a brief mock-review section in which guests debate whether the retreat counts as living well—one complaining of skill atrophy and existential flatness upon departure, another praising the total elimination of risk and failure. The satire should exaggerate actual moves in the experience-machine literature so that a specialist recognizes the targets, yet remain fully readable to someone unfamiliar with Nozick's thought experiment. Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or references to specific texts by title or date. About 800 words, formatted as interleaved brochure copy and contractual clauses with a short review section at the end.
