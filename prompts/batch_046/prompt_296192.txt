For a philosophy seminar examining Bertrand Russell’s *Marriage and Morals* (1929) without reducing it to a caricature of “free love,” construct a decision tree that sorts actual commitments about the state’s role in intimate life. Start every respondent with the claim: Marriage is not essentially a religious sacrament, but it is not merely a private contract either. From there, branch through three concrete diagnostic questions that genuinely separate positions: (1) Should the law enforce sexual exclusivity between partners, or is exclusivity purely a matter of private agreement? (2) Is jealousy best understood as a primitive instinct that moral education should suppress, or as a rational response to violated expectations? (3) When partners have children together, does the state have an independent duty to stabilize the household form itself, or only to protect children’s welfare regardless of household structure?

The tree must have exactly six leaves, including: Russell’s own view (as developed in the 1929 work), a traditionalist view that rejects Russell’s premises on all three branches, a modern liberal feminist view that accepts Russell’s suppression of jealousy and his rejection of state-enforced exclusivity but insists that economic dependency structures make “free” exit illusory, and at least one unoccupied logical combination that you flag as unstable or unexplored, with a one-sentence diagnosis of why that branch is empty (incoherent, unattractive, or merely overlooked).

For each leaf, provide: the combination of answers that leads to it; the name of the position (or “unnamed”); and one sentence describing its most awkward practical commitment—the specific policy or personal stance it is forced to endorse that sounds strange on first hearing. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or references to secondary literature. Build the tree from the logical space of answers first, then map the named positions onto it, and explicitly flag any pair of leaves that your three questions cannot actually separate.
