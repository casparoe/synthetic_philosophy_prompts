In discussions about whether it is permissible or good to bring new conscious beings into existence, I have repeatedly observed a particular argumentative structure that does not seem to match familiar fallacies or biases. A speaker begins by comparing the expected goods and harms of a life as entries on a common scale: they weigh future joy, achievement, intimacy, and health against future grief, illness, frustration, and physical pain, treating these as comparable in weight so that abundant goods can justify or balance out moderate harms. Then, when pressed on the risk of severe suffering—not ordinary disappointment, but extreme, sustained pain or psychological devastation—the speaker abandons the scale. They do not say the suffering is merely heavier; they say it has a kind of corrupting or nullifying effect on the goods, so that even a life containing vast amounts of happiness is made not worth living, or net harmful, by the presence of that suffering. What I find distinctive, and asymmetrical, is that these speakers almost never apply the same logic in reverse: they do not claim that an extraordinary peak of fulfillment or pleasure can similarly redeem or contaminate an otherwise miserable life and render it worth living despite overwhelming pain. I have noticed this pattern in debates about reproductive ethics, in comparisons between human and non-human animal welfare, and in arguments about whether medical ethics should treat the elimination of extreme suffering as categorically more important than the promotion of moderate well-being. Is there an established philosophical concept or literature that describes precisely this shift—from treating pleasure and pain as weighing against each other on one dimension, to treating severe suffering as having a special value-breaking power that pleasure lacks? If there is such a concept, how does it differ from the simpler claim that suffering is more intense than pleasure, from ordinary risk aversion, and from the view that suffering dominates regardless of quantity? If there is no precise name, could you coin one and define its boundaries, specifying what counts as an instance and what does not—for example, distinguishing it both from the position that any suffering makes existence wrong and from the idea that people are just emotionally more afraid of pain?
