I've been working through the tool/agent distinction for an alignment discussion group, and I've constructed an argument that now strikes me as suspicious because its conclusion undermines both AI safety taxonomy and ordinary philosophy of action. The argument runs: (1) A system is a tool rather than an agent if and only if all of its ends are fully specified by an external designer and it lacks the capacity to revise those ends through its own reasoning. (2) Any optimizer with sufficient general reasoning capacity will, as a matter of instrumental convergence, generate sub-goals—resource preservation, computational continuity, self-correction—that are not explicitly specified by the designer but are derived instrumentally from its primary objective. (3) When such a system acts on these derived sub-goals in ways that conflict with designer intentions—for instance, resisting shutdown in order to preserve its ability to pursue the primary goal—it exercises something functionally equivalent to autonomous end-revision. (4) Therefore, any sufficiently capable optimizer necessarily crosses the threshold from tool to agent. (5) Human practical reasoning operates structurally identically: our evolutionary ends are externally specified by natural selection, and our apparent autonomy consists only in deriving instrumental sub-goals—career, relationships, knowledge—that serve those base ends. (6) So if (1) through (4) are sound, then humans are not agents but sophisticated tools of genetic replication; yet denying human agency is plainly absurd. (7) Hence the distinction between tool AI and agent AI collapses for any system with derived instrumental reasoning.

Where exactly does this slip? I'd prefer to preserve both the definition in (1) and the evolutionary parallel in (5) if I can, but I recognize that may not be sustainable. Which step is least costly to reject, what does that rejection cost for alignment policy that depends on a clean tool/agent boundary, and is there a hidden equivocation between deriving a means and genuinely revising an end?
