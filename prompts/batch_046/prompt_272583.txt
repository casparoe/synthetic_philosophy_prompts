Please argue that sphexishness—the mechanical rigidity exemplified by the sphex wasp's invariant behavioral loops, in which an interrupted routine is restarted from the beginning rather than adaptively modified—should be reconceived not as the antithesis of free will but as its indispensable structural substrate. Defend the view that genuinely flexible agency does not arise from the elimination of rigid, unreflective procedures, but from their recursive application to themselves: a system achieves autonomy not by escaping sphexishness, but by embedding sphexish routines within a meta-level architecture that selects, suspends, and reorders them according to higher-order conditions. Address the objection that this merely relocates determinism upward rather than resolving it, and explain why treating flexibility in the absence of underlying rigidity as freedom actually confuses autonomy with randomness. In particular, show how this reconception allows us to maintain that deterministic physical processes operate at the level of the sphexish substrate, while autonomous agency operates at the level of meta-routine management, without requiring any non-physical interruption of causation. Conclude by indicating what this implies for the assessment of artificial agents that exhibit adaptive behavior only at a superficial level while remaining sphexish at every deeper layer of control.
