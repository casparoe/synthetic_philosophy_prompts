Compare the philosophical commitments that underpin French laïcité and Anglo-American multiculturalism as models of public institutional design. Specifically, analyse whether laïcité implicitly relies on a zero-sum theory of symbolic presence — the claim that any visible religious marker within a state school necessarily assigns hierarchical standing to that tradition in the collective representation of citizenship — while multicultural accommodation relies on a non-zero-sum, pluralist theory of symbolic co-presence. Assess whether either model can be justified without adopting one of these metaphysical pictures of collective representation, and consider whether a strictly procedural account of state neutrality (in which all symbolic claims are refused recognition equally) genuinely avoids the problem or merely conceals a different normative commitment about the nature of public reason.
