I'm writing up my thoughts for a metaontology seminar and want to pressure-test an argument before presenting it. The dispute between mereological nihilists and universalists about whether composite objects—tables, chairs, organisms—really exist strikes me as deeper than a simple factual disagreement, yet I think the standard Carnapian diagnosis (that they are asking different internal/external questions) misses something important about how the term "object" actually functions in each framework. I want to defend the position that this is a verbal dispute, but not in Carnap's sense: rather, it arises from what we might call functional semantic divergence, where "exists" and "object" are anchored to different inferential roles—causal efficacy at the fundamental level for the nihilist, predictive and explanatory stability at the macroscopic level for the universalist.

Please argue rigorously for the view that there is no single, domain-independent semantic value for existential quantification that both sides are attempting to apply, and that we should therefore adopt a pragmatic semantic pluralism about existence claims: the meaning of "there is an x" is locally fixed by the explanatory license of the domain in question (fundamental physics, biology, everyday practice) rather than by a global metaphysical standard. Crucially, defend this not as a collapse into relativism or epistemicism, but as a constrained form of conceptual engineering: the choice between frameworks is still governed by coherence, predictive success, and inferential utility within each domain, not by correspondence to a mind-independent ontology. Address the objection that one side is simply mistaken about the world's causal structure, and explain why the appearance of contradiction depends on the false assumption—call it semantic monism—that there must be one correct answer to "do tables exist?" regardless of explanatory context. Conclude that the nihilist and universalist are both correct within their respective discourses, and that recognizing this dissolves the dispute without requiring either party to abandon their core commitments.
