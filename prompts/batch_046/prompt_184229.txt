Compare Al-Ghazālī's occasionalist denial of secondary causation with Hume's regularity account of causation. Both reject the idea that we observe necessary connections linking cause and effect, and both appeal to repetition and mental expectation—Al-Ghazālī through the concept of ʿādah (custom or habitual course), Hume through custom and constant conjunction—to explain why we anticipate that fire will burn cotton. Yet they diverge sharply on what, if anything, grounds these regularities: for Hume, nothing beyond the observed conjunction and our psychological propensity to project it; for Al-Ghazālī, each instance of burning is a discrete act of divine will, with natural objects possessing no causal efficacy whatsoever.

First, lay out the precise points of convergence and divergence: in what sense are both thinkers anti-necessitarian about nature, and in what sense does Al-Ghazālī's position depend on a metaphysics of direct divine action that Hume's empiricism explicitly avoids? Second, address the problem of miracles: Hume defines a miracle as a violation of a law of nature established by uniform experience, which makes miracles conceptually problematic but not impossible; Al-Ghazālī, by denying that nature has laws in any robust sense, seems to dissolve the problem—yet he still needs to explain why miracles are recognizable as interruptions rather than part of the usual divine pattern. Does his framework actually make miracles easier to accommodate, or does it render the distinction between natural and miraculous events conceptually unstable?

Finally, consider an interpretive injection: if we strip Al-Ghazālī's theology away and read his occasionalism as a purely metaphysical claim—that spatiotemporal slices are ontologically independent and that no cross-temporal causal powers inhere in substances—how does this "event-ontology" version of occasionalism compare to Hume's view in terms of compatibility with modern local field theories in physics? In particular, does the denial of enduring causal powers in objects align occasionalism more closely with a Humean mosaic of discrete events, or does the requirement of a continuous sustaining agent (even if secularized) push it in a direction foreign to both Humean empiricism and contemporary physics?
