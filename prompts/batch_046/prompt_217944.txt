Write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines followed by a three-line envoi—dramatizing an extension of Molyneux’s problem. Imagine a person blind from birth who has learned the layout of a room solely by exploring a miniature tactile model; upon suddenly gaining sight, she attempts to navigate the actual room. Use these six end-words in the standard sestina rotation: model, room, touch, sight, threshold, error. The poem should progress from initial confidence in the tactile model, through the disorienting moment at the threshold of the real room, to an unresolved tension between touch and sight that ends in an error of recognition rather than in successful translation. The compulsory cyclical repetition of the end-words should structurally enact her inability to escape the loop of mismatched modalities. Do not resolve the problem; let the envoi hold the ambiguity open.

Then provide a section titled “Commentary on form and philosophy” (approximately 150–200 words). In it, explain how the sestina’s rotating end-words mirror the subject’s repeated attempts at cross-modal matching, why the envoi’s compression of the remaining terms reflects the philosophical stalemate, and how the invented scenario of spatial navigation via a miniature model extends the classic object-recognition version of the problem without requiring specialized historical knowledge.
