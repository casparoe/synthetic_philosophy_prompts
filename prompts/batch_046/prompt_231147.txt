Write a short story about a neuroscientist named Mara who realizes her awarness is basically a user-illusion, like a desktop interface hiding all the raw computation going on underneath. She runs a small expariment in sensory deprivation that strips away the UI layer of her emotions and self-narrative, leaving only bare neural processing. The story should ask whether there's anything genuinely valuable about the illusion itself, or if it's just a useful fiction we tell ourselves to navigate social life. Keep it somewhat grounded, not too space-opera, and don't worry about citing philosophers—just run with the idea.
