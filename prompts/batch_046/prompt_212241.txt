So I'm in this weird spot with my book club / art group hybrid thing we've been running in my garage since 2021. We started as just friends talking about novels, but then we started doing these "aesthetic audits" where we rank submissions from local artists based on what we call "intrinsic resonance" — basically whether a piece hits you in a way that feels pre-rational. Last month I accidentally got access to the backend of the platform we use to collect submissions, and I realized our "resonance" scores map almost exactly onto some hidden weighting: artists who went to specific schools, who use certain material suppliers, who have prior gallery exposure in particular neighborhoods. The "intrinsic" quality we were celebrating is basically a shadow of these institutional filters.

I mentioned this to the group and they were like, "even if the filter exists, the resonance is still real — you're confusing the cause with the experience." And I get that, but now when I sit in our sessions I feel like I'm either lying to myself about what I'm perceiving, or I'm seeing the machinery and missing the art entirely. There's this tension between participating in the judgment and knowing the conditions that make the judgment possible. I don't want to be the guy who leaves the room and can never enjoy anything again, but I also don't want to pretend I haven't seen the mechanism behind the curtain. Is there a coherent stance here? Like, can you genuinely value the real quality of an aesthetic experience while fully acknowledging that your access to it is mediated by these hidden structural conditions? Or does seeing the filter necessarily collapse the value into something instrumental? I'm definatly not asking for therapy — I want to know if there's a philosophical position that lets you stay in the room without being either naive or cynical.
