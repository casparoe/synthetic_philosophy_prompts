I am preparing a consistency audit of my own views on promissory obligation for a seminar presentation, and I want to check whether the package holds together before I defend it. Here are my commitments:

(1) I hold that the binding force of a promise derives primarily from the legitimate expectations it creates in the promisee, rather than from any freestanding moral duty to keep one's word.

(2) At the same time, I maintain that promises made to agents who—due to cognitive limitation, absence, or indifference—do not actually form the relevant expectation are nonetheless fully binding, because the normative force is anchored in the promiser's own rational consistency and not in the recipient's psychological state.

(3) I deny that the concept of promising contains an internal "escape clause" that automatically dissolves obligation when fulfillment becomes costly; any overridability must come from external moral weights, not from conceptual defeasibility built into promising itself.

(4) I accept a framework I call diachronic authority transfer: in promising, the present self authorizes a specific future course of action for the later self, and this authorization remains binding only if the later self's preferences stay within a "coherence radius" of the values that structured the original commitment.

(5) I also maintain that promises made under conditions of deep epistemic opacity about one's own future values—what I term radical self-ignorance promises—carry exactly the same binding weight as ordinary promises, and that this weight does not diminish as the degree of uncertainty increases.

Please audit these commitments. Identify which pairs genuinely pull against each other, which conflicts are only apparent and can be dissolved, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then tell me which single revision to one commitment would clean up the most overall tension, and whether making that revision undermines the motivation for holding any of the remaining ones.
