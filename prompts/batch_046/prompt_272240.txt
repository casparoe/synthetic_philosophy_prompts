Could you rank them 1-4 (best to worst) for me, with just a quick sentence on each? Here they are:

A. The Machian line: the water climbs because it's rotating relative to all the distant stars and matter in the universe, so no absolute space is needed.

B. The old school substantivlist view: the bucket proves rotation is real even with nothing nearby, so space itself exists as a kind of container you can spin through.

C. This structuralist take I came across -- spacetime isn't a substance or just relations, but the local inertial structure is physically real and explains the water's shape without committing to either old view.

D. One guy called it "asymmetric relationism": he said relationism can survive if we allow that relations themselves can be asymmetrical and locally broken without pointing to any external anchor.
