Decompose your credence into four factors: (i) the likelihood that sortition actually yields less polarized, more long-termist outputs than election; (ii) the likelihood that citizens would accept randomly selected peers as legitimate lawmakers rather than as unaccountable elites; (iii) the likelihood that the reverse audit veto would remain a genuine check rather than being captured by the assessing bureaucracy; and (iv) the likelihood that the interaction between the two chambers produces productive synthesis rather than deadlock. For each factor, provide a rough probability and a one-sentence justification. Then combine them (explicitly stating your aggregation rule) and report your final number. Afterward, state in one sentence how confident you are in that overall figure, and identify the specific sub-factor or inferential step about which you are least certain and what kind of evidence—historical, experimental, or theoretical—would most move your view. Do not cite specific authors or texts; reason from the conceptual structure alone.
