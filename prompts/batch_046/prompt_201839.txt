Construct a decision-tree taxonomy—suitable for a philosophy seminar—that sorts explanatory accounts of rapid moral revolutions (the collapse of norms once treated as indispensable, such as chattel slavery, dueling, or foot-binding). The tree must begin with concrete comparative judgments about cases, not with theoretical labels, and must end with named positions and explicitly marked empty branches.

Root: Present three brief vignettes of norm collapse—dueling in Western Europe after roughly 1830, foot-binding in China during the late imperial and early Republican transition, and the disappearance of debtor’s prison in nineteenth-century Britain. The first branching question asks the respondent which two cases share the same underlying mechanism of abandonment, forcing a choice among (i) the practice became ridiculed or shameful for its defenders, (ii) it became economically or administratively irrational, or (iii) a competing prestige system replaced it. This triage determines the primary driver branch.

Subsequent nodes (each a concrete diagnostic claim, not a label):
- Whether the old norm’s defenders changed because they lost internal prestige (the practice undermined its own honor conditions) or because external status rewards shifted (a peer-signaling competition between honor codes).
- Whether change was elite-led and then cascaded downward, or whether mass refusal made elite enforcement impossible.
- Whether the aftermath involves moral residue (widespread retrospective shame), nostalgic amnesia, or neutral historical reframing.

Leaves that must be present:
(a) Bernard Williams’s thesis that norms collapse when honor detaches and the practice becomes ridiculous;
(b) Kwame Anthony Appiah’s thesis that honor codes shift through status competition and peer signaling;
(c) a pure material-interest account;
(d) a pure rationalist/argument account;
(e) at least one hybrid combining two drivers;
(f) at least one unoccupied logical combination, with a brief diagnosis of why it is empty (incoherent, unstable, or unexplored).

Additional constraints:
- The taxonomy must distinguish “honor becomes internally contradictory” from “honor is outcompeted by an alternative prestige system”; ensure this splits two actual branches rather than collapsing into one.
- Where two named positions agree on every diagnostic case, flag them as indistinguishable by this instrument rather than inventing a false separation.
- Do not ask for bibliographies, page references, direct quotations, or verification of obscure dates. Reference thinkers and ideas only in paraphrase.
- The instrument should be answerable by someone with general historical and philosophical knowledge and no web access; avoid dependence on highly specialized empirical details.

Deliverable: the decision-tree flowchart described in logical form (each node as a question with answer options and branch destinations), the path-to-leaf mapping, the list of occupied and unoccupied leaves with brief diagnoses, and a short note on which structural feature of the cases does the diagnostic work.
