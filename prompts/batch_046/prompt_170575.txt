I am designing a single ninety-minute seminar for advanced philosophy undergraduates who have worked through classical utilitarianism but have never encountered negative utilitarianism in its lexical-threshold form. The aim is not advocacy but structural comprehension: I want them to see why the commensurability of pleasure and pain is not an auxiliary psychological claim but the pivot on which the lexical ordering stands or falls. Plan the session's explanatory arc with the following constraints in mind. First, specify the dependency order: which distinction—say, between quantitative trade-offs and categorical priority—must be firmly in place before the phrase "lexical threshold" can be parsed without collapsing into mere risk-aversion or strong preference weighting. Second, identify the one carrying scenario or comparison that should run through the session; describe what it must contain (for instance, a precisely calibrated asymmetry between suffering and satisfaction) and what it must deliberately exclude (for instance, extreme physical torture that makes the intuition trivial by shock rather than by structure). Third, map the misconceptions in the sequence they will likely arise: probably that this view is just pessimistic utilitarianism with a heavy coefficient on pain, then that lexical ordering requires literally infinite quantities of pleasure to offset suffering, then that denying commensurability is merely an emotional bias rather than a claim about evaluative structure; for each, state exactly where in the arc to head it off and with what counter-move. Fourth, propose one diagnostic question or task I can deploy at the end that reveals whether a student has grasped the structural claim—distinguishing it from a student who has only adopted a darker mood—without requiring them to defend the view. Fifth, state explicitly what I should set aside and acknowledge I am not covering at this level, such as the full debate over whether any pleasure can in principle offset extreme suffering, and why leaving it out protects rather than weakens the session's coherence.
