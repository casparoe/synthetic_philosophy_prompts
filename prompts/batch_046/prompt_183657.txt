My ethics advisor's track record on positive rescue duties is as follows. Case one: a child is drowning in a shallow pond and you can save them by wading in, ruining only your shoes; she rules rescue is obligatory. Case two: the same pond, but saving them requires destroying a forty-thousand-dollar suit; she still rules it obligatory. Case three: a child is dying of a preventable illness across the world, and you can save them by forgoing the cost of an expensive restaurant meal and donating to an effective relief organization; she rules donation obligatory. Case four: the only available donation channel is a corrupt intermediary that wastes ninety percent of funds, with no transparent alternative; she rules there is no obligation to donate through that channel. Case five: you can save five strangers by destroying a unique, irreplaceable cultural artifact of comparable non-instrumental value; she rules you must save the five. Case six: you can save five strangers only by actively harming one other innocent person; she rules this is forbidden. Case seven: with identical effort you can save either your own child or two strangers; she rules that saving your own child is permissible but not required over saving the two. Case eight: you can prevent a child fifty years in the future from drowning in a climate-related flood by reducing your present emissions at a cost comparable to a luxury meal; she rules that a weak, defeasible duty exists but is easily overridden by present obligations of similar weight.

Next term she must rule on this case: an autonomous rescue drone can be remotely authorized by you to breach an uninhabited reservoir and flood a valley that contains no humans, no economically valued property, and no sentient inhabitants, saving one child trapped downstream with near-certainty; you are not physically present, will have no direct interaction with the child, and the destroyed ecosystem has no moral status comparable to a human life. Predict her ruling. Identify whether she is implicitly running a Singer-style principle that serious harm must be prevented whenever the agent's sacrifice is not of comparable moral importance regardless of proximity or mediation, a proximity-modulated deontological account that strengthens rescue duties mainly under direct physical presence or personal relationship, or an imperfect-duty framework that weighs expected efficacy against personal cost and allows easy override at a distance. State the closest analog from her track record, say what principle she adopts either way, and specify the single further case that would distinguish the two leading interpretations. End with a line in the form Verdict: [ruling]; Theory: [principle name]; Test: [case description].
