Design a 75-minute seminar activity for 14 upper-level philosophy students on advance directives and the authority of the earlier self, focused specifically on diachronic authority under "value metamorphosis": the case where a patient's later self does not merely hold different preferences but operates within a transformed evaluative framework, so that an advance directive written by the earlier self is not just outdated but conceptually alien to the present, severely impaired patient. The participants should come away feeling, before it is named, that honoring the document is not the same as honoring the person.

Structure the session as a group role-play in teams of four, with roles: the Directive Author (age 35, who refused artificial nutrition in cases of severe cognitive impairment), the Transformed Self (age 65, competent, who has adopted a radically different conception of dignity and now wants to live), the Present Patient (age 80, severely demented, non-verbal, receiving care), and the Surrogate Decision-Maker (bound by institutional rules). Give each team a sealed "directive packet" containing the original refusal and an ambiguous, partial oral revocation made by the Transformed Self. Provide the Surrogate with a hidden payoff scheme: honoring the written directive earns institutional legitimacy points; responding to the Present Patient's apparent comfort earns welfare points; but overriding the directive without clear documented revocation costs authority points and risks a penalty. The rules should require a binding group decision within 15 minutes, after which you reveal hidden information: the Transformed Self's revocation occurred during a temporary depressive episode, yet the Present Patient's current evaluative framework is genuinely incommensurable with the Author's.

Include a timeline with materials, the exact rules and point allocations, a debrief that maps each role's arguments onto the distinction between preference continuity and framework discontinuity, and an honest note on which feature of value metamorphosis—the incommensurability of evaluative concepts across temporal stages—the payoff scheme cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply. Also say what will predictably go wrong the first time (likely students collapse the conflict into a simple autonomy-versus-paternalism debate) and how to prevent that collapse during the activity itself.
