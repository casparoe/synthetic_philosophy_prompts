After a long and somewhat exhausting argument with a friend about why some nations are rich and others remain poor, I have adopted a fairly rigid position: that the institutional, geographic, and cultural explanations are structurally underdetermined by history, not merely empirically incomplete. The problem, as I see it, is that colonial and developmental institutions were never assigned randomly — they were placed where pre-existing conditions made extraction profitable — so every natural experiment and synthetic-control comparison inherits the same selection bias at a global scale. Add path dependence and equilibrium selection, and I think every observed correlation between institutions and wealth is compatible with all three master narratives. I therefore doubt we can ever genuinely know the answer. What would count against this position? I want the specific arguments, empirical findings, or methodological breakthroughs that ought to change my mind, sorted from decisive to merely awkward, including anything that has arguably already happened. I also want an honest assessment of whether I have qualified the view into unfalsifiability — for instance, by treating institutional successes as endogenous to geography or culture, and failures as proof of institutional weakness — and whether that insulation is a vice or just appropriate epistemic humility. Please respond in American English, and keep the reasoning formal and polished.
