Not moral facts in the usual sense, more like normative pulls that don't need a mind to be pulled toward anything.

The setup: centuries after people disappeared, there's this valley where a rationale — specifically that unnecessary suffering should be reduced — has somehow become... physical? Like it exists as a pattern in the geology, or in the way light refracts. The automated maintainance drones don't have beliefs or desires, but they still respond to it. Then one drone encounters a different, older rationale embedded in a rusted satellite: that chaos and unpredictability have intrinsic value. The drone isn't 'choosing' anything, but its behavior shifts as these two free-floating reasons conflict inside its programming architecture.
