I am preparing for a seminar defense of a thesis on the French Revolution's radicalization that deliberately occupies a space between social history, the history of political thought, and analytic philosophy of history. I argue that the standard explanatory trilemma—structural class struggle, the autonomous logic of republican ideology, or sheer contingency—fails to identify the actual causal architecture of the Terror. Instead, I propose what I term recursive legitimation collapse: within the revolutionary committees, particularly the mechanisms of public accusation and mandate renewal, the procedural rules for authorizing coercion became coupled to their own evidentiary outputs. This generated a selection environment in which moderation was not defeated politically but rendered epistemically invisible, not because moderates lacked virtue, but because the institutional grammar of suspicion made their positions unrepresentable within the committee's internal logic. Consequently, the common observation that revolutions devour their children misattributes to moral betrayal what was in fact a dynamics problem—specifically, a form of procedural overfitting in which recursive accountability compressed the space of permissible dissent. I grant that class position shaped the content of accusations and that ideological vocabulary supplied the rhetoric; I deny that either supplies the mechanism of escalation.

The audience is a joint colloquium composed of three groups with sharply different methodological commitments. There are two Marxist social historians for whom the consolidation of bourgeois property relations remains the primary explanatory axis; they will suspect that my institutional account is merely superstructural window dressing. There are three scholars formed in the Cambridge School tradition—contextualist intellectual historians—who treat revolutionary language as strategically deployed in specific controversies and who regard macro-causal mechanisms with deep skepticism; they will press me on whether my framework smuggles structural determinism back in through procedural language. Finally, there is an analytic philosopher of history who doubts whether narratives of this scale can sustain mechanistic claims at all and who will challenge the evidentiary basis for attributing causal power to institutional rules rather than to agents' beliefs.
