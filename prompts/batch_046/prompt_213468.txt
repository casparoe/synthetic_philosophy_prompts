At dinner my cousin Maya and her roommate Leo got into it about heritability. Maya said if IQ heritability is around 0.8 then tutoring programs are mostly a waste, since genes explain most of the spread and enviromental tweaks barely move individual outcomes. Leo said thats a category error — heritability is a population variance ratio, not a personal destiny — and enviroment can still shift the whole mean. Maya pushed back that Leo is dodging: if genetic variance dominates, then for any particular kid the marginal gain from an intervention is tiny compared to their genetic baseline. Leo said you're ignoring gene-enviroment interaction and reaction norms; the same tutoring can have wildly different effects by genotype, and passive rGE means family enviroment is partly genetic, so heritability estimates actually soak up enviromental effects. Maya said that sounds like redefining enviroment just to save it. Leo replied no, it just means policy can't assume uniform treatment effects, and both are missing that twin-study heritability confounds shared enviroment with genetic similarity in ways that SNP-based estimates don't. They asked me to settle it. Whos right about which point, and what are they both missing about how these numbers actually get calculated?
