everyone in the pain litterature keeps saying pain asymbolia is like a smoke detector that still blares but the homeowner has earplugs in — the signal is intact but the aversive response is gone, so this proves pain's badness is just the 'earplug-less' affective layer and not the signal itself. people use it to argue pain isnt intrinsically bad, just instrumentally annoying. but i keep thinking about hypothetical cases where they not only dont avoid the stimulus but actualy seek it out — like the alarm isnt just muted, its been rewired to call you toward the fire. audit that analogy: what corresponds to the detector, the blare, the earplugs, the homeowner's behavior, and where does it break once you include the appetitive-reversal twist? does the conclusion — that pain's badness collapses to affect — survive, or was the analogy smuggling in the idea that all signaling is inherently protective? be committed: if the analogy fails, say what actualy distinguishes the badness of normal pain from these dissociated states, and dont just list options.
