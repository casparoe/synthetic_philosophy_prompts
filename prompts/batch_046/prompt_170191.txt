I'm hosting a seminar-series podcast on philosophy of biology, and next month's guest is a philosopher-biologist who has recently articulated a position she calls processual threshold pluralism about life. Rather than seeking necessary and sufficient conditions, she holds that biological processes—autopoietic closure, evolutionary continuity, and adaptive metabolism—generate overlapping zones of vitality with no sharp categorical boundary between living and non-living things. She extends this to argue that astrobiology's search for a universal definition is methodologically mistaken because it treats a comparative, graded phenomenon as if it required a binary classifier. Most controversially, she claims that synthetic minimal cells created in the lab, even when they display full metabolic and reproductive functionality, fail to be genuinely alive in the fullest sense because they lack an evolutionary history—a condition she treats not as a historical curiosity but as an ontologically constitutive feature of vitality.

Write me a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that avoid generic biography and instead press directly on the architecture of her view. I want at least one question that forces her to address whether viral entities fall inside one zone, outside all of them, or in a liminal space between zones; at least one that asks how her evolutionary-history condition applies to synthetic organisms without making the concept of life parochially Earth-centric; and at least one that probes whether her pluralism collapses into a form of eliminativism about the concept of life altogether, or whether it can still do explanatory work in medicine and conservation biology. For each of these three, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give—particularly the move of saying that a particular objection mistakes a grading for a failure of definition. Also include a closing question that asks what empirical discovery—say, in synthetic biology or extremophile ecology—would genuinely threaten her framework rather than merely expand one of her zones, and specify what kind of answer would count as a concession versus a deflection.
