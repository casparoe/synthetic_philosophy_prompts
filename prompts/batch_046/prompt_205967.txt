As a novelist who recently joined the preservation board of the Meridian Maritime Trust, I am requesting a formal decision memo for our Thursday session regarding the reconstructed research brigantine Aurelia. In 1987 the original vessel—launched in 1862 and sustained through gradual plank replacement—was destroyed in a dockyard fire; shipwrights subsequently rebuilt the hull using sixty percent salvaged original oak and forty percent carbon-fiber-reinforced timber, replicating every joint and dimension so that the rebuilt Aurelia is seaworthy and structurally indistinguishable from its predecessor, yet it possesses no unbroken chain of material continuity. Our founding charter restricts Class-A heritage funding to artifacts that maintain material identity through time, a clause drafted for gradual repair but never for catastrophic reconstruction, and the board is split between those who regard functional and narrative continuity—bolstered by the incorporated salvage—as sufficient to preserve the very object historical memory requires, and those who hold that an interrupted material history reduces the vessel to a faithful replica. Draft the board’s written opinion recommending whether to retain, revoke, or conditionally maintain the Aurelia’s Class-A designation, treating the strongest argument for the opposing view with genuine seriousness rather than dismissal, and include a concrete bottom line with a brief implementation timeline that members could vote on. Present the memo as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences long, with no introductory or concluding paragraph, so that each point functions as a discrete step leading to the recommendation.
