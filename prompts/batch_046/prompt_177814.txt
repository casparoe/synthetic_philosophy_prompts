I'm drafting an essay for a seminar on moral epistemology that uses the WEIRD-psychology literature—specifically the well-documented finding that Western, educated, industrialized, rich, democratic populations rely on autonomy, harm, and fairness intuitions far more heavily than loyalty, authority, and purity—to test whether moral objectivity can survive serious cross-cultural variation. My tentative argument is that non-WEIRD societies still display punitive reactions to unprovoked harm and failures of cooperative reciprocity, which suggests a thin, second-order structural objectivity—constraints on what can count as a coherent moral system—while leaving first-order normative disputes entirely unresolved. I then claim that this saves moral realism only in a philosophically empty sense: if objectivity means nothing more than "there are logical limits on contradictory norms," then the WEIRD data actually shows that substantive moral disagreement is ineliminable and that first-order objectivity is largely a WEIRD projection.
