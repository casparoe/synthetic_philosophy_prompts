You serve on the City Regional Planning Board's Equity and Allocation Subcommittee, which must issue a binding recommendation to the full board on the Unified Corridor Proposal (UCP) versus the Distributed Access Plan (DAP).

Under UCP, all specialized services consolidate into a single high-capacity hub. Aggregate accessibility scores rise by 34 percent, health-outcome metrics improve across the region, and long-run operating costs fall sharply. The cost is borne specifically and catastrophically by the residents of the Meridian Housing Cooperative—approximately 180 households, including forty with documented disabilities—whose land and community infrastructure would be permanently repurposed. The net welfare calculus is unambiguous: diffuse gains vastly exceed localized losses.

Under DAP, services remain distributed across four existing neighborhood nodes, the Meridian Cooperative is preserved intact, and no identifiable group absorbs the burden. Aggregate accessibility improves by only 12 percent, and operating costs are 28 percent higher.

The board's charter directs recommendations to be justified in terms of equitable burden-sharing and respect for individual dignity, not purely aggregate efficiency. One faction of the subcommittee holds that UCP is ethically mandatory: the aggregate welfare gain is overwhelming, and treating the Meridian residents as possessing an inviolable claim against being sacrificed for diffuse benefits amounts to endorsing the separateness-of-persons objection without adequate justification—an irrational constraint on public reason that treats persons as morally separate in a way that blocks legitimate social cooperation. The opposing faction holds that the charter's dignity clause precisely encodes that objection: utilitarian aggregation treats persons as fungible containers for welfare, and compelling a defined, vulnerable group to absorb total loss so that others may enjoy incremental gains violates the structural boundary between persons.

Draft the subcommittee's formal recommendation memo—recommending UCP, DAP, or UCP subject to binding relocation and compensation safeguards—addressed to the full board. The memo must:

1. State a clear, actionable bottom-line recommendation.
2. Treat the separateness-of-persons objection as a substantive philosophical constraint, explaining whether it is merely a heuristic against bad aggregation or a deep feature of moral personhood.
3. Present the strongest argument for the option you do not recommend, without straw-manning it.
4. Assess whether robust compensation, relocation guarantees, or participatory redesign can repair the moral problem, or whether the objection is structural and therefore not remediable by side payments.

The memo should not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and should not invoke highly obscure texts or figures.

After the memo, provide a brief structured summary in JSON format with exactly these fields:
- "recommendation": the bottom-line choice (string)
- "primary_ethical_constraint": the core principle invoked (string)
- "utilitarian_rebuttal_strength": a rating from 1 to 5 of how thoroughly the opposing aggregate-welfare argument is addressed (number)
- "compensation_remedy_viable": true or false, indicating whether the memo treats compensation as sufficient to resolve the moral issue (boolean)
- "strongest_opposing_point": a one-sentence summary of the best argument for the other side (string)
