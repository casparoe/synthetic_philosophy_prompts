So I'm working part-time at this regional ethnographic archive—nothing fancy, just boxes of old pottery and tools people donated over the years. We're digitizing the whole collection and my boss just read something about the School of Names and that old white horse dialogue, the one where they argue a white horse is not a horse because the word for horse refers to shape alone and adding white introduces a color that changes the class membership. He wants me to turn that into an actual filing proceedure for our catalog team, who have no philosophy background at all.

What I need is a reusable checklist they can run on any object: the questions in order, what counts as passing each gate, where we file under the base category, where we create a separate entry, and where the logic runs out and I have to call him because it's a judgment call not a rule. The distinction should come from whatever the Mingjia actually say about names picking out form versus form-plus-accident, but translated into physical-object terms—so it needs to handle paint applied later, broken pieces, and properties that seem essential to recognizing the thing. Then apply it to three items we actually have: a plain unglazed clay bowl, the same bowl shape but glazed white with a chipped rim, and a wooden horse carving that's been painted entirely white. One of these—probably the horse—should come out in a way my boss won't like, and I want you to flag exactly where the proceedure fails and a human has to decide. Don't ask me for sources or a reading list; I don't have the texts and the cataloguers won't either. Just build it from the logic itself, and be honest about what the procedure can't settle.
