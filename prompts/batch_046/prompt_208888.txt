So I'm working on this long-form piece about why debates over free will never seem to die, and I keep circling back to Galen Strawson's basic argument -- the regress thing where if you're truly responsible for what you do, you'd have to be responsible for the way you are, which means being responsible for what caused you to be that way, and so on forever, so true moral responsibility is impossible. I was discussing this with some friends over drinks last week and one of them said the whole thing is a non-starter because it just redefines responsibility into something nobody actually cares about. That got me wondering whether the stalemate in the literature -- and I've read enough to see it's been going in circles for decades -- is actually about the logic of the regress at all. Maybe it's a deeper disagreement about whether moral responsibility needs some kind of metaphysical backing, like being causa sui, or whether it's just a social practice that works fine without that. So here's what I want: not a survey of compatibilism versus Strawson, but a real diagnosis. Are the sides talking past each other? Is one side asking about our actual practices of blame and praise, while the other is asking about ultimate cosmic authorship? Do they disagree about what counts as data -- like, do Frankfurt-style cases settle anything, or do they just beg the question against the basic argument? And is there any hidden empirical question here, maybe about how people actually assign blame, or is it purely conceptual? I definatly want to know if there's any move -- a distinction, a formal result, a shift in the question -- that could actually end this, or if we should just accept that the disagreement is rooted in starting intuitions neither side can defend and move on. Give me reasons, not a verdict for one side.
