Let's imagine Avicenna somehow shows up in a modern neuroscience seminar and hears about split-brain patients where one hemisphere denies agency over actions started by the other. He also learns about a new hypothetical philosophers have been playing with: total sensory deprivation except for one artificial proprioceptive feed tied to an algorithimic controller that sometimes moves the limb without initiation. Given his flying man argument — that knowing you exist doesn't depend on bodily sensation — what would he say? Would he still defend an imediate, non-inferential self-knowledge, or allow that fragemented agency and fake feedback can break it? And do you think he'd treat the split-brain data as a direct counterexample, or just as evidence the soul gets obscured by bodily damage?
