Hey — I've been trying to wrap my head round Chalmers' zombie argument, and I think there's a tension people don't talk about enough. The usual claim is that if I can genuinely conceive of a being phyiscal identical to me but with zero inner experience, then consciousness can't be phyiscal. But there's also the transparency thesis — the idea that when I introspect my phenomenal states I grasp their essence fully. So if I already have complete knowledge of consciousness via transparency, why do I need the zombie conceivability step at all? Couldn't I just argue directly from transparency that consciousness isn't identical to anything physical?

My confusion is whether these are actually two separate arguments, or whether Chalmers needs transparency to make conceivability work. If transparency is false — say, phenomenal concepts are just partial descriptions — does the zombie argument collapse, or can it stand on its own? And if they do work together, isn't there a risk of circularity: I use transparency to claim I know consciousness fully, then use that knowledge to conceive of its absence?

Can you lay out how these actually relate, whether physicalism can survive by denying one but not the other, and whether the 'missing physical detail' objection still applies once transparency is in the picture? Please write the answer in American English.
