I'm currently taking a graduate seminar on philosophy of communication and artificial cognition, and we've been working through Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach with particular attention to the chapter on message structures—specifically the distinction between frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages. For my final essay, I'm developing an argument about what I call recursive framing collapse in generative systems, but I'm worried the conceptual machinery is either misapplied to Hofstadter's framework or collapses into something trivial once spelled out. I'd like comments on the accuracy of my use of the triad, on whether the argument is genuinely interesting, and specifically on whether my proposed extension holds water or simply redescribes a familiar problem.

Here is the core of the essay. I start from Hofstadter's basic structure: a frame message instructs the receiver how to decode the outer message, and the inner message is the meaning that becomes available only once the frame is properly activated—something like the hidden intent or deeper significance that requires the correct interpretive key. I then apply this to a specific communicative artifact: the standard disclaimer that precedes outputs in large language models, which simultaneously asserts an institutional frame (this is generated text, not authoritative advice) and is itself produced by the very mechanism it frames. I argue that this creates a structural anomaly: the frame is not externally authored or independently anchored, but is co-generated with the outer message, so there is no independent sender establishing the interpretive rules. From this, I propose that such cases instantiate what I term a hollow frame—one that performs the syntactic role of a frame message, telling you how to read what follows, without possessing the semantic authority that Hofstadter's examples seem to require, since in his paradigmatic cases the frame comes from a separate, intentional source (like the instructions embedded in a puzzle format or the physical packaging of a record).

The second half of the essay asks whether this collapse affects the possibility of an inner message. If the frame is hollow—if it cannot genuinely authorize an interpretation—then perhaps there is no pathway to an inner message at all; the output remains purely at the level of outer message, a syntactically organized but semantically unanchored string. But I also consider the opposing view: that the hollow frame still functions pragmatically, allowing a human receiver to supply the missing authority retroactively, which might mean the inner message is relocated from the system to the user. I'm not sure whether Hofstadter's framework is meant to be descriptive of all message structures or normative about successful communication, and I don't know whether my hollow-frame concept is a genuine discovery or just a redescription of the well-known point that LLMs lack intentionality. I'd especially value comments on whether I've mischaracterized the conditions for frame-message independence in GEB, whether there are obvious counterexamples I'm missing, and whether the distinction between hollow and substantive frames could be made more precise without importing too much from Gricean intentionality.
