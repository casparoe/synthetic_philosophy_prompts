Write the full specification of desiderata for a theory of gradated moral status that orders or weights entities along a continuum from insects through intermediate biological and artificial systems down to thermostats and simple negative-feedback loops. The criteria themselves should be the deliverable, not a literature survey, though you may briefly note how major families—hedonistic, preference-satisfaction, and capabilities-based accounts—fare against the list.

For each desideratum, include the concrete case or distinction that motivates it, and mark whether it is non-negotiable (dropping it dissolves the problem) or camp-specific (e.g., required only by those who prioritize evolutionary continuity, or only by those who require subjective experience).

The list must cover at least the following; add others if they expose hidden tensions:

1. The aggregation and emergence problem: if individual bees carry minimal status, does a hive possess summed status, emergent status, or none? The theory must deliver a principled answer that does not treat collective entities as mere aggregates when they exhibit integrated information processing.

2. The architecture-independence constraint: the distinction between reactive homeostasis (a thermostat) and nociceptive withdrawal (an insect) must be drawn without exclusive reliance on neural anatomy, since some invertebrates lack centralized brains and some artificial networks possess them. State the functional or phenomenal criterion that does the work.

3. Phenomenal density versus intensity: the intuition that mammalian suffering is not merely quantitatively greater but structurally different—perhaps involving temporal integration or self-modeling—than insect distress. The theory must say whether this is a difference in degree, kind, or both, and how it maps onto moral weight.

4. The replacement test: if an insect is replaced by a functionally identical artifact lacking any inner life, does moral status vanish? The theory must identify the feature—behavioral, functional, or phenomenal—that tracks status, and explain why that feature is not arbitrarily distributed.

5. Evolutionary continuity without panpsychism: the account must respect phylogenetic gradation (cephalopods, crustaceans, insects) without granting minimal status to every feedback loop, yet it must avoid anthropocentric cutoffs that grant mammals status by fiat.

6. Scalar versus vectorial structure: is moral status one-dimensional (more or less of a single property), or multi-dimensional (e.g., separate axes for valence capacity, temporal depth, and social embeddedness)? The theory must specify whether an entity can rank high on one axis and negligible on another, and whether such divergence is conceptually stable.

7. Marginal-case consistency: the account must apply without contradiction to severely brain-damaged humans, sleeping mammals, colonial organisms, and advanced language models that process pain-related language but may lack affect.

Finally, flag any subset of these desiderata that you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable—particularly the tension between architecture-independence and avoiding panpsychism, or between evolutionary continuity and a non-arbitrary cutoff. If one requirement does the most damage to popular theories, name it and say why.
