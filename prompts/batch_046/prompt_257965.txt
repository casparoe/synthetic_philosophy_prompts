Please describe the most important implications of Gettier-style cases—where a belief is justified through sound reasoning from a false lemma that happens to be rendered true by an independent, unrecognized environmental factor—for the adequacy of purely virtue-theoretic analyses of knowledge that reject any standalone anti-luck condition. Focus particularly on whether such cases undermine the claim that knowledge is essentially true belief that manifests intellectual virtue, especially in institutional contexts like multi-laboratory research networks where one group's false modeling assumption is accidentally offset by an unrelated calibration error in a partner lab. Address what this means for attributing knowledge to individual researchers, for the stability of collective epistemic achievement, and for whether virtue epistemology must implicitly smuggle in a modal safety requirement that it officially disavows.
