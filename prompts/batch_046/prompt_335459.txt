A research-ethics board that explicitly works within a Nozickian framework has issued the following rulings on animal-use protocols: invasive experiments on great apes are categorically forbidden regardless of human benefit; standard industrial poultry farming is permissible only if every bird has sufficient space and enrichment to avoid chronic distress; keeping a solitary, socially complex cephalopod in a bare tank for public display is banned; culling wild deer to prevent winter starvation is allowed if performed quickly; using bees for commercial pollination is unrestricted; and training marine mammals for entertainment is prohibited when it relies on aversive conditioning but permitted if participation is genuinely voluntary and cognitively stimulating. The board now faces a proposal to create and maintain a line of brain organoids derived from porcine neural tissue that exhibit coordinated electrical patterns resembling those associated with nociception in intact pigs, but that lack any integrated body, sensory organs, or capacity for long-term memory, in order to test analgesic compounds. Predict the board's verdict. Articulate at least two competing interpretations of the Nozick-inspired principle that could account for their prior decisions — for example, one interpretation that applies graded side-constraints based on the presence of a unified subjective life, and another that applies direct constraints only to beings capable of framing a conception of the good — and state what each interpretation predicts about the organoids. Finally, identify one further hypothetical case that would best distinguish between these interpretations.
